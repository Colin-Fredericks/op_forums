    Character sheet in progress!
    It just clicked that Alichino is one of the Malebranche. If I were the jester and my creator left me with a copy of the Divine Comedy, I'd be really worried that there's a Malacoda out there somewhere looking for me.

    I finally got a copy of the Decameron to read today, and I might write some fiction stuff around it to play around with the puppet's voice and personality.

    I'm leaning towards Empathy or Joy or Love for an Elpis, and Logical for a Torment. Something to do with isolation/fear/loss of self might fit, too.

    Dark Eras suggests that medieval European characters with a few dots in Academics probably know Latin or Greek without needing to buy Language. Does that sound about right for this game?

    I think I get what you're going for, Cinder, but how are you picturing the frame story of tales told about the throng working? Is that just you as a narrator, or are we involved somehow? It could be cool to set up an adventure by asking "the crowd" a few questions and having us talk about them and suggest answers and elements that might come up in this story. "I wanna hear a love story! Tell us about the war! Do one with magic in it!" Maybe there's some overall topic or question for the Chapter that acts as a shared Aspiration, like "are nobles more virtuous than commoners?" and there's an incentive for us to do things play up that theme in addition to whatever is primarily going on.

Maybe there's some overall topic or question for the Chapter that acts as a shared Aspiration, like "are nobles more virtuous than commoners?" and there's an incentive for us to do things play up that theme in addition to whatever is primarily going on.
        I'm still thinking on how to implement it as something more than just a high-concept narrative device, but I was indeed leaning towards something like the latter, where the NPCs that "narrate" the story state a topic and you as player get something if your character explores it. Shared Aspirations are one of the ideas, but I got a few other too I'll ponder about in due time.

        Overall, most episodes will (if I manage to do things right) highlight some aspects of the human or Promethean condition and the search for soul and salvation, so giving an incentive for the characters to explore them sounds cool. Chapters that come with questions for the Throng to answer with their actions and that they're free to do so as they see appropriate or things like that. As long as the characters confront with it it, no matter the lesson a specific one sees in a story, it's fine. The character makes its experiences, Virgil nods in approval, the people telling the stories are entertained and the Pilgrimage keeps going forward. But yeah, odds are there's going to be some practical implementations of it for us to play with.

        To say more would spoil some of my intentions for the conclusion and the moment where the short chronicle wraps up, so I'll try to shut up for now.
