    Luck isn't on Strife's side. Even with Fate guiding his hands to the most likely places, he finds nothing hidden. The chanting and singing continues outside.

    How many shadows should be moving on the floor? There's five of you, right?

    Unbound, hopelessly tracing the stone, nearly trips as his foot catches something cut into the floor. Everyone feels something like a chime in their Periphery- magic was at play just now.

    One of the stone slabs fits loosely into the floor, and it slides easily along a carved track once you know where to look. A stroke of luck from Khvarenah's magic. Unbound, and anyone who follows him, climbs down into the passageway. The priests will certainly notice the open trapdoor if one of them enters the archive. Does someone stay outside to stand guard? Urbi could, although she doesn't have thumbs to reopen the trapdoor if you close it. She cautions you: "Be very careful. You're entering a dangerous situation, even with magic to hide you."

    The passageway is mud brick walls and a packed earth floor, not the fine stone of the temple. Maybe it wasn't included in the floorplan. It's cold as soon as you drop down into it, and cramped- you almost need to crouch. The only light is a hazy square from the open tile. From where you stand, it looks like the passage goes westward about 15 yards before opening up into a larger space.

    Yep and yep. +1 AB for Charmed, 11 in the pot.

    Woland roll a Clash of Wills with Mind, +1 for your spell's Duration. Something is pressing up against your Incognito Presence on the group.

    Everyone, be sure to state whether you're in the passageway or outside. There's still a -2 Poor Light penalty in the archive, which increases to -3 in the passageway.

                            Imhotep sees truly. His enhanced senses alone can't overcome how dark this place is, but he picks some details out in the light of the trapdoor.

                            With Life, he sees that the only living things in this place are the sorcerers. Points of warm, pulsing light in the darkness.

                            With Death, he recognizes the curse of those who walk after death, sometimes called "the ones who can't stop." This wretched creature has no soul, but the shattered remains of his sheut have clotted and spread to take its place. The shadows that surround this thing are moving like flowing blood. There is an animating intelligence behind the darkness circling their feet. The shadows are part of him. His corpse cannot contain his sheut.

                            There's a separate phenomenon here. He can't see it clearly, but all along the walls and deeper down the tunnel, he feels something heavy pulling down into the earth. It's akin to the Anchors that keep a ghost from passing on, but different in kind. As a Moros, he sees Death and Matter at once, and he sees that the heaviest points are marked by carved notches.

                            The Shadow listens to Imhotep's appeal, possibly with interest, but his face doesn't react like a human's would. But apparently there's enough humanity there to rise to Unbound's taunt.

                            "No, Greek."

                            Everyone feels magic in their Periphery. Down in the tunnel, the Shadow bends backward and clutches the ceiling like a spider as the shaft of light grows as thin and faint as a pinhole. You can see nothing, but you feel the darkness itself become cold and heavy, like thick fog. Its voice is not coming from anywhere in particular.

                            "The master lies in henet, as he has since your soldiers came to Hibis. And yet, true to his word, the black blood on the sphinx is still fresh. Remember, weret-hekau, that we are as faithful as Sutekh even in death. We can bargain if you come as guests, but not as burglars."

                            "We know the Red Land well. The western desert is the road to Duat, and we have wandered back and forth between the worlds with Sutekh's blessing. We can stand on your bow, chosen of Djehuty, and spear Apep when he swims close. If you know your destination, we can help you reach it and return to the world of life."

                            After some preliminary negotiation, he offers these terms:
                            • Greater Request: Retainer 3 for the Term. Will assist in combat and act as secondary actor in travel rolls.
                            • 2 Medial Costs: Someone in the caravan must offer blood freely at the end of each night, 2 Lethal damage. Each sorcerer must maintain a blessing on the Shadow for the Term.
                            • Medial Term: You must return to Hibis by Day 28, the end of the Aquarius cycle.
                            • Medial Forfeiture: If you do not meet the terms, the Shadow will capture one of the caravan for sufficient blood as they return to Hibis alone.


                            Instead of Poor Light, everyone in the tunnel has Blinded (-5 to rolls requiring vision, including Scrutiny/Revelation.)

                            The Death/Matter mystery is Opacity 3. Poor vision penalties apply to Scrutiny/Revelation.

                            With his x-success, Imhotep has an insight into negotiation: you could reduce the blood cost to 1 Lethal damage per night if one of you casts an appealing permanent spell on the Shadow before leaving for the Red Land, and a second permanent spell when you return.

Offering these dark creatures a Time working in place of a longer blessing (from him) would lead to good things. Of course, it's anyone's guess whether they would like the results.                                                 Unbound opens his true eyes. Cruelty lurks in his Mind's eye, a mote behind his brow, chastised for now. He can't see the whole area clearly, but the only waking mind he senses besides the three sorcerers is the lone Shadow, which is mere inches away. Nothing else, not even dreamers.

                                                With Space, he sees the same information Khvarenah saw.

                                                With Spirit, he sees that the tunnel is Resonant with darkness itself in a way that mundane shadowy places are not. It doesn't look like any spirits have come from their hidden places, though.

                                                He draws a circle of sage. The Shadow speaks. "Why do you ward yourself, Greek? Do you think we cannot see you in the dark? Why do you come through our door and insult our hospitality? Guests have nothing to fear from us, only thieves."

                                                His flowing mind does not cross the circle. And hearing his voice is enough to sense and read his psyche.

                                                He thirsts. Pervading everything else, you feel a hunger deeper than anything you've ever felt, even the drive for ever-greater Gnosis. All his thoughts and actions are ultimately driven by bloodthirst, even if it's delayed. His reason is enslaved to his appetite. He must feed.

                                                The Shadow isn't good with people (Social tertiary, 6 dots), but he thinks deeply (Mental primary, 9 dots). He is Cautious to a fault, but prone to Zealous outbursts for his gods and masters.

                                                He is dearly devoted to his master. He has been in henet for years now, far too long. He urgently seeks a way to lift the mysterious curse laid on his master.

                                                He has heard rumors of the sha, beast of his patron god Sutekh/Set, seen in the desert lately. He considers it a sign and wants one brought back to lift the curse. In pursuit of this, he has mesmerized the priest Mitry to acquire one from a traveler.

                                                Finally, he wants to hunt and kill some creature that bedevils him. Visions of a shadowy bird with a golden beak and eyes, something like the strigoi of Greek legend. Maybe Alexander's armies brought something with them.
