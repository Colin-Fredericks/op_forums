    "Then listen, and prepare yourself."

    By now they had reached one of the many sheltered asides in the great garden at the heart of the new temple's great cloister.

    Once they were there a look came over Yi's face, one he had long practiced. He had been a teacher, from time to time, and was not entirely unskilled in that art.

    "I speak now from the account of Narak, who wrote the Brazen Book of Hell; of Shes'a'shes, who compiled the Dozakhzi; and from the glass scrolls of forbidden wisdom that only fools and sages seek.

    Five days we shall travel, as the sky turns black. Five days we shall travel as the moon turns to blood. Five days we shall travel until the green sun rises. Then we shall be in hell. As we pass through the black stone gates we shall be judged. Thrones are set up there, some empty and some filled. Some held by their owners, or the proxies of their owners, and some by pretenders. Do not inquire which is which, for it is a dangerous game they all play.

    If we are found weak, or vulnerable, we will be killed. Each alone makes that judgement. What the lone courtesan on the red beast decides is not binding upon the pack of bloody, chattering apes. Each must be impressed, or each will seek our blood; by spear or club or poisoned drink. We must be ready at once to do great violence and must present no illusions about it.

    When the demons of the threshold see not enough gain by force, for they always take pleasure in pain, they will seek to profit from us in other means. They will ply their trades to us, offering things desired and feared. Secret wants, that you would never dare speak for shame, and hateful secrets that will lie rotting at the center of your heart. They will freely offer cups of the Hegra's wine, yet a single taste and you will be drunk forever. The harlots will offer you strong sons and daughters, delivered that very day, and they speak the truth. Yet they will have their price.

    You could learn from them the devil crafts, to smelt blood and voices into treasures, to string the harp of time or beat your fear into armor. They know all these things and could teach them to you, and your craftsmanship would have no equal on Earth. They will offer you everything you want, and they are able to give it to you.

    That is why you must refuse them. Not because they lie, though they do in a thousand truthful ways, but because they do not. They offer you the easy way, the effortless path, and it leads to damnation. They have no wisdom to offer, save the poisonous wisdom of the indolent. To gain these things without your own effort forever prevents you from earning them. Your spirit with wither, your enlightenment will be stunted, and you will be good for nothing but to while away the ages in decadence."

    Yi fell silent and his hands thoughtlessly worked through Immaculate mudras; shuni for patient endurance, surya for confidence and industry, and prithvi to resist temptation. It was a perfectly normal and pious habit, for an immaculate, and it gave him something to do with his hands instead of fidget while he considered things he did not say. Then he continued.

    "The nomes of Malfeas are a place of sensation. They will not constantly assault you with a single offer, for the hearts of men are quick to callus. They will feign they care nothing for you, going about their business as though they cared not at all. For all that their actions are unchanged, know that you are always in their hearts. We are not of Hell. The scent of clean air and the flush of the yellow sun are on us. The pearl of envy and self-loathing accrete around the memories of us until we are hateful to them regardless of our deeds. They may show us every courtesy, call us friend genuinely, even love us unrestrainedly. Yet they will always hate us. For they hate themselves and that hate is ever before their eyes until all they see is degraded enough to deserve them.

    There is no calm there, no rest, no peace. Silence is the enemy, flee it if it finds you. Every shadow watches you, never trust them. Sleep is a snare, practice it lightly or not at all. After a fortnight beneath the green sun your throat will feel as though it is full of sand and hot ashes. Yet do not accept even a cup of water, for it is offered to make you receptive. The wealth of Hell is endless, and they are patient. What is a drink, no matter the cost of its import, or ten, or a hundred, or a thousand. If you say yes but once they have traded a a stone for a mountain. No one does anything without seeing their own gain in it. That is the little law of hell.

    The greater law is your shield, that alone which permits us to enter. 'And they be strong, they may do as they will.' is the whole of the Law. We are mighty, princes of the earth. Yet there are things mightier still and many of them dwell in the infernal city. This is another temptation. There are many weaker than you, and you can work your will upon them without consequence. That is a heady drug. They will come to you, and offer themselves to you. They will offer themselves as slaves and sages and soldiers; recognizing your might. Yet that road does not end. Always in Malfeas' gates are those who want what you have and always there are those with things you can take. It is a plane of conquest that does not end, and no ambition is too great beneath the demon sun."

    He had been a little animate during his lecture, but now he turned toward his fellow Solar. "Please don't dismiss what I just said because it was spoken oddly. I had to memorize much of that verbatim. I haven't been to Malfeas, but I have spoken with those who have. Some of the stories are exaggerated, some fall short of the truth. But all agree, that it is dangerous."

You follow one of the great carts laden with the works of Faen artifice, until it stops suddenly.                         Yi raised his eyebrow as though surprised.

                        "Oh no, that attitude would see you and the rest of us dead inside of an hour. I am a poor instructor if that was what you took away."

                        He had expected it, it had been his own first conclusion as well.

                        "Consider for a moment what behavior like that would mean. You would become hopelessly lost, unable to accept directions from anyone. You would die of thirst, because not even we can carry enough for both crossings and our time there. Worse, you would eventually give insult to someone to whom steel is no threat.

                        No, you must trust. That is the most dangerous part. Rather, you must be cleaver. Remember that there is always an ulterior motive. Always consider what it could be and take steps to protect yourself. You will fail to take all precautions because you cannot anticipate all desires. So you must be prepared for that which you cannot prepare for. Some of these things will be relatively benign. Some, the young or innocent and there are some even in the heart of hell, will only want to spend time in your presence. Some, the lewd and the wanton, will only want to spend time in your presence in the hope that you will seek more. The first will hurt you more than the second, and both may be the chosen weapon of something more devious. In Malfeas, as on the plane of Earth, the best tools are often people. In hell that is always the first recourse."

                        He sighed.

                        "The mistake you will make, as will I and everyone who first ventures there, is that Malfeas is the realm of evil. There is no greater error. When you label a thing evil you shape it to conform to a pattern already known to you. You expect certain things from it, react in a certain way to it, believe certain things about it. Malfeas is a place first of strangeness. No conception of evil, no matter how broad, is vast enough to contain it. If you believe it is evil you will blind yourself to the greater portion of it. It is the weakness the dwellers there are most ready to exploit.

                        To think it evil gives it a power over you. Malfeas has enough power already to destroy us, it needs no more. There is evil there, I would be the worst sort of fool to say otherwise, but there is also goodness. There are acts so selfless they would earn a mortal divinity. There is compassion so great it would shatter all the minds of men to experience such pity. There is generosity, magnanimity, honor, love, hospitality, and every worthy virtue. Yet always there is something foul mixed in.

                        There is a tale... but I have taken up enough time already. I am sorry..."

                        Sitting down heavily he finished wearily,

                        OOC: If he wants to hear the story
                        "Most likely, I am making things more difficult than they need to be. Most of this never matters to people, except in the ephemera of sorcery when it is vital to know the inconsistencies of the demonic character. Perhaps it is all wrong as well, and I am only fooling myself. In truth I have not been to the Demon City, and speak from the same inexperience in which you question me.

                        To answer the easy question, no; there is no metal of special harmfulness to demons. Only sacred arts, such as the Golden Janissaries make use of, or, I suspect, the sun has granted to us are of such use."

                        "The Sage Humble Before Heaven once heard tell of a pack of the troublemakers in the countryside. They slew livestock, defiled shrines, and carried off men and women to cruel fates. Vowing to deliver the people he hunted them until he came across a canyon of bare rock. A river flowed through and the stone was slick with mist day and night. He found the ruffians there and watched them three days and nights.

                        The first night he saw one gnawing on bones until another beat him and stole the cracked and dry morsels. 'Have I not done my share? Did I not slay the fat thing and haul it and its bones here? I have been hungry for fifty days, ever since we came here. Let me eat.' he cried and the one who beat him answered, 'You dog, to speak of fifty days hunger! Speak to me when you have hungered fifty years, since first I crawled from a dirty hole and screamed. I too killed a fat thing and hauled it here, and I had but a mouthful of it. I eat what I will'

                        The second night he saw them huddled against themselves and shivering, for the rock was cold and wet and they had no fire. The one who had taken the bone slunk to a pile who clung together for heat and said, 'I am cold, and the nights here are long. Take this morsel and let me share some of your heat.' Instead they beat him, took the bone, and left him on the ground to shiver. When he had recovered a little he said, 'Why did you do that? I would have given it to you, and had said as much. You could have kept me on the outside and had more heat than you do now.' The leader of the bunch said, 'When I joined the boss he beat me bloody every night so that I could barely stand in the morning. When I asked him why he said he would trust me to stand at his back when I could stand there on my own. Offer warmth when you have some to give. Promises are lies you tell yourself first.'

                        On the third night they were all very hungry and made to sneak out and do more mischief. The sage met them at the entrance to the canyon. He beat them, one by one, in combat until they lay at his feat begging for mercy. Then he offered them a better way, the peace of the five steps and the security of the community of believers. The people of the region found his body floating down the river with a bone stuck through his chest.

                        The troublemakers were demons, some of the Erymanthoi. Do you think the sage a fool to do as he did? I do not need an answer, but think first if they had been humans. Imagine youths raised in violence and poverty, who have never known affection or peace. Would they have been any different? The point is not to feel sorry for demons, but to attempt to understand them. Even the greatest of demon slayers know this, that even the sins of those most deserving of death are balanced, if only the smallest amount, by circumstance. None are wholly evil, even if they cannot be good. The little bit of good can destroy you more easily than all of their evil. Yet it is still good, and to call it otherwise is to demean all that is good everywhere.

                        Guard your heart and mind, but do not close them. None can save you then, whether you are in Hell or not."

                            Jensven is unable to hide the fact that he is admonished, yet he says nothing and does not interrupt. Nor does he refuse to listen. He knows that having any and all information available about the demon realm will assist him later. He is unlearned in this regard and he realizes he needs to learn about it and fast.

                            All through the descriptions and advice his gaze is averted, but he is listening. The Dawn does not nod or give any sign of assent. His only movement is that necessary for the intake of breath, the occasional blink, and a fingering of the hilt of his steel blade. What is readily apparent is that his mind is working feverishly to disseminate the information that is being told to him and reconcile it with the information he already has. In effect, he is rationalizing, comparing, and contrasting in an effort to achieve some level of understanding.

                            At the end of the explanation the former smith finally speaks, "You do not need to be sorry, Sun Priest....Sun Brother." He smiles a little at the second title. "I have time for learning. I almost always do. Time spent making sure you know things, is time spent well."

                            "You should rest. I will. I will be up early and I will get Cinder ready." As the Bronze Tiger starts to move, the canine jumps up to move with him, "As you can see, Shadow is always ready when I am."

                                        [Meanwhile, Elsewhere]

                                        A mighty citadel of verdigrised copper and rusted iron floats upon a turbulent sea, beneath an ever-raging raging storm. Nestled in the formless void, the towering citadel is a realm of bizarre geometries and physics as alien as the will of the mad exile-king who wrought it from the formless aether.

                                        The citadel is empty. It has been since its creation. The sole resident of its damned halls are its architect, its king: Baal-Hadad, Sovereign of Sea and Sky.

                                        Hadad sits upon his throne, a tremendous and terrible chair, as gnarled and twisted as the halls it sits within. Within his hand sits a vitreous glass bowl, filled with a liqueur of earthly make. Though it would pride any manufacturer among mankind, the beverage is cheap to the tongue of one who has drunk deep of the wells of dream and prayer. Occult power thrums within his soul, and mere boundaries of space do not stay his hand or vision.

                                        Now, he gazes upon a throng of witless manlings as they follow in lock-step behind a child who plays at being a god. He idly runs his hand through his beard, as Yi's warning and parable reach his ear.

                                        And he laughs. Soon he shall teach this upstart the true shape of the world.
