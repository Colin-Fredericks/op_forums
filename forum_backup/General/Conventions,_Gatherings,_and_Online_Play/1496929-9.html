                                        The Marquesa sings, “Never in Summer, never in Spring/Never in Autumn til it’s last wing/Come Winter come snow/Come despair and sorrow/Open the door now for me.” Then she touches the mirror, and it ripples like water. All three pass through.

                                        The Hedge: a psychoreactive place, only somewhat ‘real’ in a traditional sense, it delineated the wilderness separating Earth from Arcadia. Here, Earthly stability and Arcadian madness met and mingled, a thorny maze designed to protect both sides of the equation.

                                        It was Winter’s time, and so the Hedge was covered in ice and snow, like an Arctic forest. It seemed as if warmth was impossible, here. The Hedge was framed as a series of rows of thickly entangled trees, while small hobs the size of very tiny birds watched from all around.

                                        The Marquesa took a moment. “We’re going to Bucketown. It’s a hob township, not a Market. Still, be careful. Hobs aren’t as lethal as the Others, but never forget they’re not human, either.”

                                        And the three set off.

                                                        11-27-2022, 02:58 PM
                                                        “Then let’s go,” she says, and the three of them begin walking.

                                                        The Hedge is strangely claustrophobic, with a canopy so thick it only barely allows in cracks of sunlight here and there. The Marquesa knows where she’s going, and leads the two men down a series of forested ‘corridors’. It appears she’s leading them in a circle, before the Hedge unexpected gives way to a thick, long, high wall made of oak boards, with sharpened sticks with pigs’ heads impaled by them here and there.

                                                        There is a single visible gate in, and manning that gate is a single hob, a meter tall and weighing as much as a small child, wearing Mad Max-style armor and the helmet of an American-style football player. It’s skin is bright, neon green, and it looks asleep. The three walk right up to it. It is still asleep. The Marquesa audibly clears her throat, and the hob jumps awake.

                                                        It looks about warily, then is obviously morbidly embarrassed at having being caught napping. It looks ashamedly at the ground. “Ma’kessa,” it mumbles. “Ya won’t...”

                                                        “I won’t tell anyone what Hodge? That you were asleep on the job again?” The Marquesa snaps. “Let us in, you imbecile, and I’ll keep quiet.”

                                                        The hob mumbles an apology then knocks on the gate, which begins to crank open via some winching mechanism.
