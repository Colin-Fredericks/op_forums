            "Nothing," was the Bearer's answer, and Pakari mentally kicked himself for not coming up with a better question. What had he expected? That a homeless wanderer would constantly be occupied with work or lead an active social life? At least she wasn't staring at him anymore - that was a relief.

            "So, you are a Guardian. Surely the girls of your village are displeased."

            Pakari's feeling of relief left him immediately as his face flushed and he struggled to regain his mental balance.

            The truth was, Pakari didn't really know what to do with women. When he was much younger, the girls in his village had teased him for being so large. When they'd all grown up a bit, they still teased him, but their tenor had changed and he felt ambivalent about that. Nothing his aunt or any other adult figure in his life had taught included how men should relate to women. The girls' attention was flattering, but he didn't know how to respond to it, and when Pakari didn't know what to do he tended to act defensively. Trying to be respectful and considerate to everyone failed miserably in the face of pre-adult drama. Other boys his age were no help. Most of them were also teasing him or openly jealous of the unwanted attention he got. The rest usually gave him advice to the effect of "you're big enough to take whatever you want, so why not do that?" - which went against the core of Pakari's being.

            Things had gotten far worse after his coming-of-age flight. He drifted, he dove, he lost control, he crashed, and the next thing he remembered was when the fishermen dragged him up out of the water, half-conscious and gripping a giant eel hard enough to crack its bones... and somehow that made him a magnet for every woman of marriageable age in Dreaming River. After some weeks of dodging proposals and trying not to seriously offend anyone, Hasya's arrival and announcement that he wanted an apprentice had seemed unbelievably lucky. It wasn't his only reason, but Pakari jumped at the chance to become a Guardian partly so he could escape without breaking anyone's heart.

            The girls were indeed displeased, of course. One or two had even tried to talk him out of it, but what was done was done.

The quake wouldn't be doing Hasya's improvised wards any favors, and at this point Pakari would help best by retrieving the goods he had been tasked to get, and bringing them back as quickly as he could. If he had survived the quake, and Pakari had no reason to think that he wouldn't, Hasya could certainly take care of himself for a little longer.
