Some of the information will be filtered by their own interpretation and frame of reference. And even mentors lie sometimes, if they feel the need. It's up to all of you to decide what your characters believe (especially when the information from these posts contradicts the general knowledge post). We can go over them as needed, in case anything is unclear, and it is my hope and intention to have character sheet templates and character creation rules posted by tomorrow (they just need a little tweaking).

                                However, first I want to take a minute and address Haleem's character concept.

                                Originally posted by Al-Haleem View Post
                                Oh, awesome, I actually wanted to play a Ragabash character! I can write out a little biography if that helps, apologies if you weren't looking for that:

                                Narasimha-Vallabha (Or Val as he prefers to be called) was born in New York to an eccentric named Natasha Narula, a screenwriting lecturer at New York University. His father died of heart complications when he was only seven, so he was left to his mother's rather lax child-rearing methods, which consisted of letting him do whatever the hell he liked. Always interested in his Indian heritage, Val studied the Vedas as well as Sanskrit, and can rattle off shlokas with surprising ease. Sometimes they're even accurate.

                                Just before entering university, his mother received a letter inviting her and her son to a reunion at the Narula clan's estate in Utter Pradesh. Natasha had no interest in going, but she was unusually insistent that her son attend. Ever-eager for new experiences, Val agreed.

                                He arrived in a country unlike anything he'd imagined. No amount of Bollywood had prepared him for how different life in Hindustan was. Reaching his family's serene hawaili was something of a relief, and his relatives were infinitely gracious despite his awful Hindi. They arranged a magnificent dinner in his honor, and were positively obsequious. Rather an ostentatious affair for the prodigal son of the family's black sheep. In fact, everything was a bit suspicious...No one would stop smiling. And then there was this damn ringing in his ears, and then...

                                He awoke to darkness, and an unholy thirst. A gentle, bearded face appeared at his side and offered him a glass of...something. Val drank it without thinking and returned to oblivion.

                                Val discovered the gravity of his predicament when next he woke. He'd been left at the bottom of a deep, dry well. During the day, the well was sealed, but at night, he could gaze up at the magnificent star-studded heavens, and the moon, brighter then he'd ever seen it. His only companion was the bearded man, who appeared when he awoke, and offered him his only sustenance, something he called 'soma'.

                                And Val dreamt, of days past, of gods and heroes, and wolves, always wolves. Moons slipped by alongside time, until existence itself seemed so little and insignificant. On a night of perfect, unbroken darkness, he stared into a beautiful abyss, and was filled with the very essence of life. Howling out in ecstasy, Val knew nothing but the rush of blood in his veins, and the frantic beat of his heart. Claws tore at stone walls, terrible teeth gnashed, and Val wanted to kill...And the bearded man appeared again, and Val flexed his talons, hungry for blood, but then the bearded man became something...more.

                                Val entered a hidden world, he was one of the Chosen, or so he was told. The bearded man, named Dehazku, instructed him in the ancient arts of his tribe, stripping Val of his old name, his very identity, and preparing him for a life dedicated to his new family, Dehazku called them the 'Vrikodara Jata'. Dehazku, following some arcane series of astrological predictions had determined Val would be born with the mark of the moon, and was destined for great things. With manic eyes, Dehazku told Val told he was pure and fit for breeding, that he would lead his tribe to a new golden age, that he would help retake Hindustan from blood-drinking vetala and wicked demons who took the form of tigers. Quickly determining that Dehazku was slightly nuts, Val thought only of escape, and his poor mother, who'd given birth to a monster.

                                So he fled, making for New York, but Val is deeply worried that Dehazku simply let him go, as a sort of test, it'd all been too easy, and any day, Dehazku could be coming for him...The thought of meeting the elder again fills Val with fear. No doubt Dehazku would't hesitate to use Val like a cog in some greater machine...But screw that, he'd figure this werewolf stuff out on his own. Besides, his mom's a pretty chill lady, she'd understand...


                                Tribe: Vrikodara Jata (Get of Fenris)
                                Auspice: Ragabash
                                Breed : Homid
                                First off, I like the concept. It's fairly unique. :-) I have to warn you, though, that I have limited knowledge of Indian culture. Well, I know more on the subject than an average American, to be sure, but I'm hardly an expert. For example, I know what the Vedas are, and what Sanskrit is, and what Soma was (as much as anyone knows what Soma was, since I don't think historians agree on its composition), and where Uttar Pradesh is, but I had no idea until I went googling what a shloka is. This means that I may occasionally need to ask you for clarification of certain points. The nice part about a PbP game, as opposed to a straight tabletop game, however, is that I can take a little time for research as needed between posts.

                                Secondly, this game is set in the Delaware Valley (which includes the Philadelphia Metropolitan Area), not New York City. Take a look at the setting post, way at the beginning of this thread, for more information. So, if your character is heading back home to NYC, he'll be well out of our area. There are a few simple solutions. First off, Val and his mom could easily be from Philadelphia rather than NYC. If not, however, we need a reason for Val to be in the Delaware Valley rather than New York City. Perhaps he feared leading Dehazku back to his mother (or feared going back to her himself as a monster), and wanted to go somewhere else for her protection? Or perhaps he was afraid for himself, and chose not to go back to NYC where he would be more easily found?

                                Thirdly, I am unfamiliar with the Vrikodara Jata; I have never heard of a Get of Fenris off-shoot in India. Are they from a book that I am not familiar with, or are they your own creation? If they're your own creation, that's fine, but we'll need to discuss some details so I know where to go with them in the story. Also, if you could translate the term for me...

                                -Jim

                                                        OK, cool. Well, let's start with some basics. Has Val been in contact with his mother at all since his escape? Even if he hasn't seen her, has he called her to say that he was alright, or sent a text, or an email, or a letter or some such? Or has he completely avoided any kind of contact with her? Whichever he did, what was the reasoning behind that decision?

                                                        How old is Val, exactly? The initial post was a little ambiguous on that point.

                                                        Now, the game is going to begin with the characters going through their Rite of Passage together. This means that, at some point, Val is going to have to come into contact with some of the local Garou prior to the game beginning. There are a million and one ways this can have happened. Perhaps Val got caught up in a fight with or between one of the many supernatural factions in the Delaware Valley. Or perhaps a kinfolk met Val, realized what he was and guided him to the local garou (or the other way around, putting the local garou onto Val's trail). Or the same role could have been filled by a spirit. Like I said, a million possibilities. Have you given any thought to what Val's introduction to the local Garou would be?

                                                        Now, most Garou in this setting are attached, at least loosely, to one particular Sept. The pack, once formed, will represent the region as a whole and include members from several individual septs, but the individual pack members still have ties to individual septs, for the most part. This is partly for story reasons, as it gives them a certain set of NPCs to interact with regularly and gives me a chance to expound on the setting. It is also for practical in character reasons- having a home base and a sept structure you can turn to at need is very valuable for garou, especially young garou. If you haven't already, read the setting information at the beginning of this thread (it's essential reading for this game, actually, so if you haven't done so yet, you need to do so now before we can move any further with character creation; I know it's long, sorry).

                                                        Now, as you can see, there is one Get of Fenris dominated caern in the region, the Germantown Volunteers Sept, so that is a possibility. However, that's not the only option; there are several open and multi-tribal septs where Val could have found acceptance, too. For example, if Val was living on the streets when he first arrived in the region, the Bone Gnawers of the Sept of Dockyard Miracles might have sheltered him. Or, perhaps he went to a charitable organization looking for help with the basic necessities and ended up being put in touch with the Children of Gaia at the Sept of Friends. If he had a little money, he might have been looking for a discreet place to rent a room for a while and blundered into the Glass Walker Society Hill Sept. Or whatever- plenty of options. Do any of these jump out at you?

                                                        As for the Vrikodara Jata, there are two ways we can go. They kidnapped him, drugged him, induced his first change, and then he escaped. Not a lot of time in there for deep cultural learning. Which method would you prefer?

                                                            Hi Jim! Many apologies for not replying sooner, I’ve been travelling and my schedule’s a bit disordered. Anyway, to answer your questions:

                                                            Val’s Mom: Val returned to New York and met up with his mother. However he couldn’t quite work out how to say ‘I think I’m a werewolf’ in a tactful manner. Furthermore, he was filled with a creeping dread that Dehazku was closing in on him. The thought of endangering his mother terrified Val, so he stole some of her money and used it to get as far away from home as he could. He knew it’d leave his mom distraught, but it was better than the alternative. Val has a vivid imagination, if Dehazku confronts his mother, things will get messy, and he’ll never forgive himself if she’s harmed.

                                                            Val’s Age: Val is 17 going on 18.

                                                            Val’s Connection with Delaware Valley: Val arrives quite confused, he still doesn’t understand his new ‘condition’ and apart from avoiding Dehazku, his greatest desire is to learn more of his furry alter-ego. Val scours the seedier side of town, hoping to encounter a fellow supernatural. He has an instinctual feeling that he’ll no one when he sees them. At a dive bar, Val is approached by a mysterious man who claims he knows what he is. This man, Malakai, offers to explain the secrets of Val’s new existence. Although he’s nervous, Val is excited at the prospect of knowledge, and meeting a werewolf. It helps that Malakai is handsome, in a hard-bitten sort of way. Over several weeks, Malakai falls into the role of mentor, weaving a complex cosmology that boggles Val’s mind. Unfortunately, Malakai is a Black Spiral Dancer, and Val soon believes he exists to serve the Wyrm, a benevolent entity that seeks to annihilate the current corrupt world, and create a paradise in its place. A bit out there, but a more palatable philosophy than Dehazku’s fevered ranting’s. After some time, Malakai decides Val is ready to be inducted into the ‘family’.

                                                            He takes Val to a deserted location, where he introduces him to other servants of the Wyrm. Val’s slightly unsure of things at this point, but he’s come too far to give up his search for answers. As the Black Spirals begin a ceremony to enfold Val in the Wyrm’s embrace, a pack of Garou attack. They slay some of the Dancers, but the rest, along with Malakai, flee. Val, too slow to run for it, is soon the awkward centre of attention. The Garou are divided. Some think Val has been tainted and should be destroyed; others are less convinced of his corruption. The deciding vote comes from a Child of Gaia, Sunchaser. She vouches for Val, and offers to take him under her wing. Confused, and with no one else to turn to, Val reluctantly agrees.

                                                            Val’s Sept: Sunchaser is a member of the Sept of Friends, and welcomes Val to their number. She’s fascinated by the lost cub, and tries to coax out information of his past, to no avail. Val spins increasingly elaborate lies, and while Sunchaser doesn’t buy them, she accepts Val’s desire for privacy. Sunchaser teaches Val Gaian philosophy, but at this point, he’s sceptical. After getting versions of the truth from Dehazku, Malakai and now Sunchaser, Val doesn’t know what to believe. Gaia? How do you know she’s real? The Triat? It sounds like a rip-off of the Hindu Trimurti. And now the Wyrm’s bad? Can’t anyone make up their mind? Through it all, Val keeps his silence, planning his next move, dreaming of escape.

                                                            Vrikodara Jata: I had some vague ideas on the sub-tribe’s nature, but it sounds more fun if you work out the details, if you’re willing. Go wild! My only request is you make their philosophy quite alien to the average Western Garou. I can’t wait to see what you come up with.

                                                            So there it is. I hope it wasn’t too long-winded, but you inspired me. No questions yet, you’ve been very thorough with your information. And I’d also like to thank you for putting so much effort into this whole deal, I’m truly impressed.

                                                            PS: If you don’t think Val’s a viable character, I had a second option. An Abomination named Ebon Moondark, doomed to walk the earth, lamenting his cruel fate. He excels at soliloquys expounding on his bleak existence, contemplating the nature of life, waxing lyrical on the night’s beauty etc. etc. Possesses several level six Gifts, which he learnt on the internet (vampiric werewolves have lots of free time) and has a Grand Klaive with a ten page backstory I’m more than willing to share. He has purple eyes, and is terribly attractive (but thinks he’s hideous) and sparkles under electric light (so he doesn’t go out much). Spoiler: He’s actually Caine in disguise. Shhh. I don’t like to brag, but he’s a pretty darn amazing character. I wouldn’t be surprised if he starred in his own Tribe-novel. (Those are still a thing right?)
