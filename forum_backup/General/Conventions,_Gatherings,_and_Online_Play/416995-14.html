                    Marian's internal monologue was a buzz of activity. It seemed that he was surrounded by those that are more adept at combat than he. Of course, those more adept at combat were exactly what he would need if he were ever to accomplish his goals. It wasn't that he would manipulate them or trick them into doing things that would benefit him, but if he could get the forces of this other Deathlord to fight with those of the Bishop by playing his origins as a Deathknight true, then he could have the both of them pound each other into the ground. Picking up the pieces wouldn't be a simple matter, but that was a bridge he would have to cross when he got there. For the meantime, though...

                    The Captain's flame pieces were not quite as deadly as he had feared, though they were still deadly enough to end his existence should the Captain will it. The metal man was surprisingly capable of turning his arm into a weapon of some sort. That would come in handy if turned to the purpose The Prodigal now found himself pursuing. Finally the Immaculate was just as deadly as he could have hoped. He would have to tread carefully with all of them because any of them could defeat him almost as easily as they were defeating these creatures.

                    As his thoughts whir through his head, the Daybreak will back slowly away from the fight with his staff at the ready. He is prepared to attempt to deflect blows that come his way, but he is not going to attempt to attack the creatures. He was first and foremost a scholar and scholars were unsuited to combat. That was something he might have to change...

                    Yeah, I really didn't do anything for combat with this guy. I have Melee Essence Triumphant and Elegant Flowing Deflection only for the purpose of making sure he doesn't get wrecked. He could use the excellency to attack, but I do not think that is his style. Guard and Move, or Move and Guard. Whatever order works best at this point.

                                                Alma sighs, then in a flash, a giant appears where she stood. Easily nine feet tall, with red-bronze skin, several horns jutting from her temples, as reddish-brown fur rustles here and there on her figure. She flexes taloned fingers, and he looks down at Zarah for a minute, before the smaller armored woman returns to view. No one else seems to have seen this terrifying vision, with the pub owner puttering around with his glasses and Zan still sleeping soundly.

                                                "What you hear is correct. By your reckoning, I am "Anathema." What I am, though, I cannot say. I bear the memories of Alma, a princess in the line of Mereving in the land of Tarkh, in the region called the Hundred Kingdoms. She was betrothed to a man in the kingdom of Zell, to the east of Tarkh, and their union was supposed to unite the two neighbors in harmony, cementing friendships that had been built up over generations. However, then they appeared. Two men, sowing the seeds of strife and war. Kharas, the Lord of Blades, and the Sun-King of Flame, himself, Oda Noodlnaga. The two had armies of beastmen armed with unusual weapons that blew fire like the breath of the gods themselves. They sowed mistrust between Tarkh and Zell, and the princess was slain in the final battle for her home of Asterberg. However, her beloved still loved her, and sought any way to bring her back, employing even dark magicks and the shrines deep beneath Asterberg, baptized by an fell god at the end of time, whose Ichor is said to have stained the earth itself black. It was this that I recall, for I awoke to these two. I was not Alma, and yet, I am, and for this reason, I use her name, for it is the name in the memories in my head. However, when my birth was sussed out, I was called an evil spirit and fled, hounded by the Wyld Hunts of several nations. Which is why I am here. I have an unnatural hunger for blood and flesh, I know this, but I do not wish to prey on innocent humans if I can help it. The creatures of this place share my taste for blood, so I see it as a fair trade. Does that answer your question?" Alma asks, lazily.
