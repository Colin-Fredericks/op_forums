            Earth's Inscription is a tattoo wrapped around her bicep which reads "A MAN OF WORDS AND NOT OF DEEDS IS LIKE A GARDEN FULL OF WEEDS." She knows that this phrase was important to her creator, but doesn't know its origin. As her creator explained it, it means that she was built to work and not to speak, the idea being that a garden full of weeds is a bad thing to be. After everything she's been through so far, she's decided she doesn't want to read the tattoo as a constant reminder of her failure, and instead interprets it backwards. Words are a valuable kind of deed, and a weed-choked garden could also be called a place teeming with life, even if it's not the life the gardener expected. It currently gives bonuses to Expression, Intimidate, and Survival.

            Related: her creator intended for her to fight and grow physically strong as her purpose, but after so much failure and disappointment, Earth doesn't really want that. She does have an affinity for gardening, raising animals (or Pandorans at least), and generally working in the dirt and training and cultivating and being responsible for other life.

            She remembers a time before she broke out of the Arizona desert, but doesn't have words to describe it. The general sense of it is a creator greater than the one who physically made her. She believes this space or time or creator or feeling is what she glimpses in her Vivid Dreams sometimes.

            ---

            What sort of human do you want to be?
            "Handsome, masculine, tall, I guess. Mostly I don't wanna need people to like me or care whether they respect me or not, because I'm so sure of who I am and where I'm supposed to be that I've got everything I need already. I want there to be no one I'd rather be than me."

            How have humans taught you to fear and hate?
            "I ain't afraid of humans, they're afraid of me. What would I be afraid of? Anyone who tries to fuck with me is gonna end up dead.

            I hate humans because they're beautiful to me. It's not even anger, they're just...there, in my brain, dancing and laughing and holding hands and shit. Not laughing at me, they don't care about me, I know that. Just being happy being themselves. Even when I was a Freak, they were still in my head, still beautiful. That's what got me out.

            I think the Principle created humans to show me what I can't have. What I can never have."

            How did you split with your creator?
            "Heartsblood was this big scary Tammuz, 7 foot tall, hard as granite with a big leather apron and an Azothic sledgehammer, and she lurched around the Southwest looking for stuff strong enough to fight. Eventually she made me. She needed me to become strong enough to beat her in a fair fight, but I came out wrong, and I was never good enough- and that doesn't feel right, being shit at the one thing you were made for. I kept trying to leave and do my own thing, but she brought me back, usually because I was gonna get myself killed by Disquiet.

            Eventually she figured she should just kill me and try again, and that was the real Milestone. Like I was just a bump in her road. I didn't like that. I fell into Stannum, and now I could put up a good fight, on my terms. Don't need to be tough to throw lightning bolts. We fought a lot, I'd run for a few days and she'd catch me and we'd fight again, or I'd find her 'cause I wasn't done being angry yet. But eventually I limped away from a fight that wrecked her Hovel, and she didn't catch me.

            That wasn't the end though. When I was a Freak, me and Dead Girl tracked her down again, more than once. Like, even when she started trying to run away from me, get a new throng, learn from some humans- we would find them and break them, because we liked hurting her. It was bad.

            Finally we had this big ugly throwdown at the Grand Canyon during a thunderstorm last summer, Heartsblood versus me and Dead Girl and our pack. It went on for days, because we kept getting recharged. Finally she took a tumble, and we walked away alive. That one made it into the Ramble: 'The Battle of the Canyon,' they call it. I heard that story a few weeks back, sound effects and all, and realized we were the bad guys. Makes me wonder who they heard the story from.

            That's the last time I saw my creator. So far, anyway. I'd be surprised if a fall killed a Ferrum that tough."

            What keeps you on the Pilgrimage?
            "Not hope, really. Hope is a mistake.

            More like knowing the alternative. I've been off the road, and it fucking sucked. You imagine there's a point where things get so bad that they break and you magically push through into something different and better, but that point doesn't exist. You just keep being a monster. It never ends.

            Gotta keep pushing this stupid boulder, no matter how many times it rolls back down the hill. Sisyphus is in Hell, but at least he has a purpose."

            What would you give up to become human?
            "My memories. My footprint. My past. Heartsblood. Dead Girl. All of it, the good and the bad. I don't want the stories other monsters tell about me, or the nightmares, or the memories of what I've done and stolen and killed. I want none of this to have ever happened, and I want a blank slate.

            Maybe that's cowardly. But I know I'm not strong enough to face the wreckage. The people I left behind have to sift meaning out of what I did, and I can't help them with that. I can't make this better. The most I can do is run away and hope if I become human, I'll never have existed at all."

            • Throw down some lightning in combat.
            • Meet someone who knows her through Repute.
            • Learn what's become of Dead Girl. (Long-term)

                            There was a moment when she consciously decided to come back to the Pilgrimage, but the actual transition was very gradual. She became a Savage, a Role she had attempted before leaving the path, but hadn't really understood before. She was still stalking prey and lurking in the wilderness and breaking shit and driving fear into human hearts, but now she stayed far away from Pandorans and no longer warped her flesh or invoked Flux.

                            Recently she blew into a real small town in Manitoba called Powerview-Pine Falls, maybe a thousand people, used to be a papermill town until the mill shut down after the Great Recession. It reminded her of something she'd seen with Dead Girl, a dying lumber town in Oregon where the ghost of the foreclosed mill was demanding human sacrifices from the people too poor or stubborn to move on. The memories brought her rage. So she busted into the old mill, caused a stir, beat down some Disquieted squatters, broke a bunch of equipment, burned a bunch of Pyros, and within a few days turned the whole building into a Wasteland. Eventually she climbed up to the roof like King Kong and rained lightning down on the mill and surrounding buildings, expecting to revel in the screams.

                            The thing that stuck in her mind was looking down from her perch and seeing a cop staring back up at her from the small crowd below. There was a woman with him. He had a gun, but he wasn't pointing it at her, he was just holding the woman painfully tight.

                            Earth thought: This hurts you. What I'm doing is hurting you. You are going to remember this.

                            Earth-Dies-Screaming had done this routine many times before, but now Earth was looking at it through the eyes of the humans, and grasping the idea that she had now become a thing that happened to this community, something they would pull together or break apart over, just like the papermill shutting down.

                            She climbed down from the roof and moved on. That was just a few weeks ago, the papermill Wasteland is probably still there.

                                        She completed Savage, now she's Vigilante.

                                        If Earth stops working to think about herself in depth, it's melancholy-infused variations on "you're not fooling anyone, you're worthless without Dead Girl, what's the point of looking for something you can't have, you never had a chance, two wrongs don't make a right, you're just one big super-wrong," etc etc. So instead, she usually locks up all those thoughts in Pandora's Box and focuses on the present!

                                        The Vigilante is kinda like that familiar story in the Azothic Memory about the people that put all their sins inside of a scapegoat and run the goat outside the village walls. They drive it out, but they also need it. The Vigilante is outside the community, but they also regulate it, they're not completely separate. The Vigilante does the things that people want to happen, but are afraid to do themselves. So she helps them. This is how she displays her heroism.

                                        Her usual MO so far is to hang around people whom the neighborhood fears and despises, then use Disquietism to build up her own Disquiet and focus it on the people who need to be punished. She helps the community fulfill their longing by turning their hatred of her onto the people they're afraid to act against.

                                        ---

                                        Earth's Elpis is Faith: not necessarily human religious belief, not necessarily benevolent or malignant, although it can be any of these. It's the uniquely human attitude that despite all evidence to the contrary, there is something greater at play, some unseeable hope that will carry the day. Standing against the mundane world with the power of the irrational. Challenging someone to a fight that you have no chance of winning, refusing to follow human laws because you serve a non-empirical higher power, finding meaning in a senseless tragedy. Somewhere between Hope and Courage and Drive and simple stubbornness. Incidentally, in her time in Canada she's become very interested in Louis Riel for this reason, and wants to learn whether the Divine Fire inspired his rebellion somehow. [for the record, her Torment is Alienated]

                                        With that in mind: for her, the Principle is the desperate hope that's too fragile to mention out loud. She knows that Dead Girl would laugh if she heard her say it, but she badly wants to believe that there's a greater meaning behind all of this, that somewhere in the heart of the Divine Fire there's a Principle that knows her by name and says "yes, spark of my flame, you are seen and you are exactly where you are expected to be."

                                        When she's closer to Torment, the Principle becomes the cruel animating force of the cosmos, the architect of all her suffering, mocking her with the impossible hope that she could ever turn lead into gold. The victories of other Prometheans are engineered to prove just how hopeless she really is.

                                        Earth-Dies-Screaming had a Vivid Dream about the qashmallim once, after that last Firestorm shook her out of her complacency. She still remembers it. She was laying broken at the bottom of the Grand Canyon in the dead of night, twisted and mutated and afraid, and she cried out and begged for help until finally an angel in the shape of a swarm of butterflies floated down and alighted on her.

                                        She said: "I am in pain." The angel said: "I know."

                                        She said: "Will you make it stop?" The angel said: "No. But we are in pain with you."

                                        She wept. The angel waited.

                                        She said: "Will the pain ever end?" The angel said: "Yes. One day, the pain will end."

                                        That's about as useful as the qashmallim usually are for her.

                                                        An abandoned military base is a good place to camp: people usually stay away, there is a lot of space and abandoned equipment (the soldiers would've said 'junk', but if you know what you're doing, it was good working material) and you have at least one working roof above your head. This is where Mark 5 opened his eyes for the very first time.
                                                        His genitor had worked carefully, making sure to have a resilient, strong body, and it showed: Mark 5 was durable from the beginning and grew even more so with Mudbreaker's teachings. At stern teacher, she didn't pull punches, nor did she slow down when her progeny could hardly keep up. They exchanged few words; in fact, despite only having met a couple of humans within a few months, Mark 5 had exchanged more words with humans than his genitor. Then, one night, he woke up, and Mudbreaker was gone. Thinking this was another exercise, he started searching for her, but when he finally found traces, they'd gone cold hours ago.
                                                        He'd never catch up. She had abandoned him.
                                                        Mark 5 did not roar his rage out into the night. Instead, he carved a figure of wood, it's small, curvy figure a crude semblance of Mudbreaker's own - then, he smashed it. He did so again, 57 times (he liked counting).
                                                        Inhabiting the base soon started to manifest a Wasteland, and his occasional contact with humans proved fruitless: he had hoped to make them like him by helping with cutting woods and similar tasks, but apparently some woods shouldn't be cut, and cutting too much wood made them angry because it made them look lazy and so on. It didn't take long for the first attacks, but Mark 5 weathered them. When the first Molotov hit his hut, though, he knew it was time to leave.
                                                        He started moving and did not stop until the sun had set 3 times.For a short time, he lived in the woods, lost as he was. Then, he met the frozen Quashmallim.
                                                        She lead him to a city. He took it to mean this was where he was going to live, now. At first, he simply wandered the city as a beggar, thinking about his life.
                                                        Mudbreaker had cared for him, but she always seemed distant, disappointed. It was only later, when he learned to understand the pilgrimage that Mark 5 realized that making him had not given Mudbreaker what she had hoped for.
                                                        Angry about how he was forced to flee, he decided to pick up where Mudbreaker had left him: forging a strong body, to weather the Pilgramage - and to make a nice home for his future soul. He picked up running again. He liked the sort-of-companionship you could enjoy by running the same route as others. Once, a car hit him, the owner panicked, people screamed, but when the driver was on his phone to call for help, Mark 5 managed to get up and get out. He knew then how much more abuse than humans he could take. He had to change his running route then, which was just as well: Disquiet was sure to set in sooner or later if he kept running the same ways. He climbed and even entered a gym (paid in cash) to get to lift weights (it looked so fascinating). However, the breakthrough took place elsewhere. He remembered how he was to sit motionless in the cold rain, and repeated that atop the buildings. There, he saw a small group of people, jumping from building to building. He followed. They saw him, ran faster, took ever increasing risks, and at one point, Mark 5 lost balance and plummeted to what could have been his (first) death. He pulled through, with a generous helping of electricity and blowing out a lot of Pyros to strengthen himself against the impact. He started running at the top of things more often, jumping over chasms, climbing up walls and water pipes, pulling himself up when he nearly failed a jump. He grew nimble and manoeuvrable. Then, one day, he saw the people, and took pursuit again. This time, he unfailingly gained: every time they jumped, he jumped a bit further, everytime they suddenly changed direction, he had already taken up the new direction. They dreaded the moment when he would reach them. He did... and overtook them, leaving them behind. A small band of perplexed thieves to his back, Mark 5 basked in the feeling of having overcome a tremendous challenge. There, he realized it had been his choices to train, to build himself up that enabled him to achieve this. He had endured what the world threw at him, and when he had been bested, he had grown to overcome it. He was no failure - he could CHANGE. The surge of hope near gave him wings as Mark 5 took the first step forward on his Pilgramage.
