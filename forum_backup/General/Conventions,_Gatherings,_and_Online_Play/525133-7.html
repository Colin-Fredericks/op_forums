            Alright then, after losing all that I had written down to a sudden reboot of my pc, I've mostly given up on the Hunting grounds style writing. This is mostly a write up on the city itself, the Uratha/criminal/supernatural part of it is upcoming.

            Willipa Bay is centered on a sheltered peninsula jutting into the mouth of the Albian River, with access to eponimous Bay and its islands, built mainly three hills, modest in size if compared to the surrounding mountains, but still quite steep. The city has over time spread to the western outer peninsula and along the south of the riverbank eastward as well as pushing south towards the mountains.
            The Downtown area of the city is towards the waterfront, the early center of Willipa's livelihood and activity. Royal Hill, the northernmost of the city’s three main hills, lives up to its name with some of the most expensive real estate in Willipa. To the south rise University Hill, home of Willipa Bay University (WBU), and Sunset Hill, bordering the Red River. As the hills level out into a broad valley, the neighborhoods give way to the Eastern District and Lakeside, curving around Lake Vallee, an artificial lake formed by the Red River Dam.
            For many years, the major businesses in Willipa Bay were the fishing and lumber industries, alongside the various various mining companies working in the gem-rich Atlas Mountains. Recently however, Willipa has gone through a long economic slump as the local paper and forestry industries cut back on their operations and closed mills, while at the same time the fishing industry declined. This has caused an increase in the unemployment and crime rates. Efforts have been made in the last few years to attract new businesses to Willipa, in particular high-tech companies, While there has been some success, specifically with including computers, software, and aviation companies, it has been slow going.

            Neighborhoods (To be expanded upon) Here are a few bullet points:
            Lakeside: the "new money" neighboorhood, eccentric people live here
            Crown Heights: on Royal Hill, expensive, the "old money" neighborhood.
            Eastern Distric: actually south of downtown, Asian part of down, mainly Chinese and Japanese, with others in minor numbers (Korean, Vietnamese, etc)
            Concord Row: downtown area, shopping district.
            Southern Shore: suburban area, ethnically diverse, but slowly turning bad.

            Locations of Interest (to be expande as well):
            Willipa Bay suffered early flooding problems, which led to the city administration literally covering over and building on top of the old central part of the city in the lowlands. This created an “Undercity" where buildings and even streets from the 1800s are preserved beneath the current downtown area. A small part of it, known as The Maze has been properly mapped and turned into something of a tourist attraction. Beyond the lighted, well-trodden areas of the Maze, the Undercity conceals some hazards. Rotted floors, collapsing ceilings, fetid water, vermin packs, fugitive criminals, violent lunatics, and more dangers all lie just past the souvenir stands.

            Willipa Bay's Riverfront is still quite active as a port; much of the shipping has shifted eastward along the river and westward towards the outer peninsula, leaving much of the downtown waterfront area to piers dedicated to shopping, pleasure craft, and tourism. The more unsavory sorts trade is just as active, and if one knows where to look and who to talk to, they can find that money can buy many things here.

            The Lakeside area was originally—in addition to being nowhere near a lake—sparsely populated, save for a few gem miners working a few smaller claims. When those finally petered out in early 20th century, there was some talk of what to make of the area, but no real action was taken until the Great Depression. Then in 1933, under the Works Progress Administration, work began on the Red River Dam Project. When it was completed, the present Lake Vallee was formed. Today, Lakeside remains the place in Willipa where people who’ve just gotten the means to realize their dreams congregate. Its residents tend to be outside-the-box thinkers whose lack of patience with rules and convention got them where they are, and thus refuse to conform now.

            Questions are welcomed, question help me flesh out stuff.
