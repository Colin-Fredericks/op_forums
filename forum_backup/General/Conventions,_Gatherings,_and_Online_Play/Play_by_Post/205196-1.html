    Max was too swept up in a world suddenly free again of pain -- well, aside from the hook still in his back. There were no especially deep thoughts; something like a protective catatonia had set in. Even so, there was an underlying dread that mirrored Jessica's own: their so-called "Keeper" might well be waiting, perhaps thinking up another torment the next time they displeased him. Though that gave the Keeper too much credit. He would likely give them no thought at all until he wished to admire his collection or they stepped "out of line," and then might casually resign them to suffer without batting an eye.

    The ox's time in the slaughterhouse had wrenched away some of her human identity. The pig's had done likewise to his sense of personhood. "Ribs... Tenderloin... Tusks... Oink... Bacon... Ham..." he mumbled, mindlessly tracing out the proper lines of each cut on his body. Pain was a powerful teacher, and had on some level buried the cruel diagram in his self-image.

        The hooks were no more tender in releasing them than they had been catching them. Like a child on a swing set, they came to a very abrupt halt, practically flinging the two off their blades and onto the floor. They were back in the cage room, and the two cages that had melted before were replaced with shinier new models. It made them stand out among the older and worn out cages the rest of the Zodiac were trapped in. Apparently the Keeper had fulfilled his intention of having them reforged, instead of simply discarding them.


        "Oh good, I just finished installing the new cages." said the Keeper's voice as he faded into view with his back facing towards them. He turned around smiling as he watched the last remains of the hook wounds mending themselves.


        "Slaughter House is where I place my disobedient Beasts." he said, "I have other punishments for other types of servants. These two just got back from The Foundry where I break down and remold my Elementals in the searing heat, pounding hammers, and boring drills." he smiled then as a far off expression came to his eyes. He seemed actually proud at having designed such a torment "I don't believe they will be letting you two back out anytime soon. But just to be sure, I've added 2 additional locks on the door." the Keeper mused as the doors opened.

                Max realized he'd been staring at the ox when she gave a nod. He guessed he hoped she had come up with some idea or plan to throw into action, but alas, if she had, it was something less immediate. He sunk a bit and began to slink back toward his freshly minted cage. As he neared the open door, he paused. Maybe it was just the endorphins roaring through him with no more pain to mend (other than the phantom variety), but a fancy took its hold. He took a long, deep breath. He would just enter immediately or shut up if commanded.

                "Th-These are... very nice cages you've made for us," he said, laying a hand on his own, and trying to sound impressed. "I-I mean, I'm no expert, but the... the craftsmanship speaks for itself."
                "The 'craft' speaks for itself, too," he heard the cage mutter, caked through with bitterness.
                Max swallowed and forged on. "Yeah, just... You spared no expense. N-Nothing but the best for us, right? Th-Th... Thank you." He couldn't express how much he wanted to break in those slightly hoof-like hands of his by punching himself in the face.

                "But-- A-And I'm n-not meaning any disrespect here!" he said, turning toward the Keeper with what he hoped was a winning smile. And looked down near the man's feet. Even the turn to face him had brought the twinge of the hook in his back. His porcine body locked in a cringe, Max pressed on, "But I-I was thinking about that 'one a year' thing you said. I've heard people and... well... animals locked in cages all the time just get tired and stringy. Haggard. Just... Just not presentable at all, y'know? With all the trouble you went through, I'd... hate you to be disappointed when you came to... to admire us, or... show us off."

                He swallowed. This was the tricky bit. "So... maybe there could be times when we're... y'know, out and about. Just a little!" A bit of a squeal slipped into that last bit. "Just... Just for exercise and, and... some recreation and things to do. That stuff. Like, uh... Free-range! I-I mean, you said it yourself: live models are more fun. Wouldn't you get more... more fun out of us moving around like that? I-I-If you think you could find some way to do it." Max raised his arms, as though he feared being struck. He dared to lift his eyes to the man's shins, and began to laugh nervously. "A-Again, you know best! I'm... I'm just a... a... just a Beast." His voice cracked at the word. Max's world spun a moment; there was something powerful about actually saying it. But he'd come too far.

                He coughed to get his bearings and forced himself to say the last word. He needed it. But could not imagine having anything more vile in his mouth. "M-M-Master..."

                    The Keeper looked down at the pig as he continued to plead. Indeed, the man's height seemed to have shifted without them noticing before, he gently placed a hand upon Max's cheek and smiled at him as if he were adorable in some fashion.


                    "Oh you still don't get it, do you?" he asked, smiling cheerfully. "You're not in that boring old human world anymore. You're in my realm, and whatever I say goes." he patted his cheek before continuing. "I could lock you up in a 5X5 sized box for 50 years, and you'd still be just as pretty and strong as I want you to be." he boasted. "Everything you see in this realm belongs to me. The furniture, the animals, the people, the monsters, the very landscape, I am lord of this domain and the laws of reality are subject to my will." he moved his hand away and quickly struck the pig in the face with an open palm.


                    "That's for not following orders immediately." he said, "But you bring up an interesting point, it's not really very entertaining to just have you sit around all day. Doesn't really capture the essence of the Zodiac unless you get to show off a bit." He thumbed his chin a bit in thought, "I could have a ranch constructed, I suppose." he muttered, tilting his head at the idea. "And since you enjoy my work so much, I'll let you in on a little secret." he said, leaning in and pointing around the room. "Everything in this room that can talk... used to be a person. I can turn you into more than just animals. I want you to remember that." he finished.

This time, she dreamed that Curtis was sleeping with a woman who thought was her. Her cats knew the truth, but had no way of alerting Curtis. This time when Jessica awakened, she gave up the idea of escaping through sleep.

                            Max squealed and staggered back from the slap. The chilling picture the Keeper had painted had left the student spellbound, and the gentle touch on his cheek (swifter than he could flinch from) had lowered his guard. Needless to say, hand on his cheek, he got into the cage as quick as his hooves allowed, the door slamming behind him. The slap was hardly the most painful event of even the last five minutes, but the surprise gave it extra heft. As did the confirmation that his little effort to improve things had failed. Worse, the seed he had planted might actually be growing into something horrible in the alien soil of their Keeper's mind. And given what the shopkeeper had already done...

                            The pig felt his legs buckling and managed to sit before they gave way altogether. "I... I will..." he told the terrifying being before him, barely managing a whisper. The conversation seemingly over, he simply sat there, decompressing.

                            Then all the insanity hit Max in a rush. That this was his life now, this was the new normal. And with no sign that things were going to get better. And it might very well get a lot worse. That he would never see any of his friends and loved ones ever again, and they might never know. He had no more luck than Jessica in holding back the tears and sobs. He cried himself half to sleep.

                            He looked up when his tears had run dry, some time later. He saw his 'partner in crime' curled up, somehow managing to sleep. He still didn't even know her name, but didn't want to disturb her. The sleep didn't look restful, on the floor of her cage. The cage he now knew to be as much a person as they were. Like his own.

                            At which point Max decided on something he could do, at least. Something decent. And something to get out of his own head. Unsure how to address it, he set a hand on the door of his cage and said to it, "I'm... I'm sorry. You shouldn't have gone through that. Or be like this. If it was anything like the..." He trembled just to think of the slaughterhouse. "... like what we went through then I... I just..." He gave up trying to articulate the thoughts around it. "I'm just so sorry."

                            A few moments passed in silence, the cage not responding, for whatever reason of its own. Max respected that. Perhaps it was asleep, too; how would he know? Still, on the off-chance it wasn't, he asked it a question he suspected it hadn't been asked in a while. "So... what's your name?"

                            With or without an answer, it wasn't long after that his new ears told him the ox was waking. "Ah, you're up, too? Didn't catch your name either, earlier."

                                                Time began to pass, though it was impossible to tell how long in... in wherever the hell they were. The two prisoners would find themselves frequently losing track of time, having conversations that seemed to last only a few minutes, and then discovering several hours had passed by. When their Keeper finally announced the ranch had been completed, they had no way of knowing if it took a few days, or several years, such was the maddening power of this realm.


                                                As the cages were wheeled out to the ranch, it was a shocking sight to behold. It did not resemble any ranch the human world had ever known. It actually looked beautiful compared to everything else they had experienced so far. Fluorescent plants illuminated the landscape in hues of purple, blue, pink, and turquoise, the sky was perfectly cloudless and displayed the image of a starry nebula expanding into the vastness of outer space, complete with planetary bodies and swirling galaxies. There was little room to wonder how they were able to see entire galaxies in the distance without a telescope, it certainly wouldn't be the strangest thing they'd come across here.


                                                It was clear their Keeper had gone to a great deal of trouble in order to create the majestic beauty of celestial beings like the Chinese Zodiac. Although just how many bushes, trees, and blades of grass were actual plants, and how many were fellow prisoners? For that matter, how many stars were actual stars, and not just somebody he had pinned to the sky? What were the limits of their Keeper's power; did he even have any? Was he really just some shop keeper, or some kind of forgotten pagan god? So many questions for Max and Jessica to ponder in their time here.


                                                "Release your contents, slaves." the Keeper ordered, and the doors to the cages popped right open. The other members of the Zodiac crawled out and took their first steps on the illuminating grass beneath their feet. "Welcome to your new habitat!" the Keeper said to everyone.

                                                        Max had discussed with Jessica concerns and trepidations about the "ranch." But this? Frankly? The sight of it all took his breath away. He had seen fantastic locales in movies and games, and imagined them from books. The luminous beauty around put them all to shame. Unselfconscious, he got some mileage out of his new snout, snuffling at the aromas of the place, trying to drink in as much of it all as he could. Just being there made him feel good. For a moment, he forget it all: the abduction, the transformation, the torture... None of it mattered. But just for a moment.

                                                        He shook his head to clear it, ears flopping about comically. 'This is the most amazing place I've ever seen, but it's still a prison.' Max kept telling himself that. He tried to distract himself by stretching his legs. Like Jessica, he turned his focus to the other zodiac members. Despite knowing it was horrible what had happened to everyone, he found it fascinating to see these living blends of human and animal. Still, it did feel a little... unseemly. Not just because clothing consisted of whatever few scraps and rags remained after the change, but looking at people who were -- if anything like the Pig -- still very uncomfortable in their own skins. Indeed, he still felt embarrassed just to be seen as he was. And he couldn't be alone in that. All of this kept him from going over to say 'hello,' or staring too much.

                                                        Still, there were some he couldn't help keeping an eye on: chiefly the Tiger and the Dragon, a bit less so the Snake and Dog. 'I don't need to worry. It's normal for a human to feel like this when he sees a wild animal,' he told himself, 'even if I know they're still people, whatever they look like. They won't hurt any of us. How I feel is normal, and I'll calm down. Yeah. Normal.' Max tried not to wonder whether there were claws or jaws like those that had taken turns stripping meals off him. Or whether he was picking up their scents, aware they were picking up his, and some part of him that was now prey was having a very natural reaction to that.
