    Tristan da Cunha: Isla

    Full fathom five thy father lies;
    Of his bones are coral made;
    Those are pearls that were his eyes:
    Nothing of him that doth fade,
    But doth suffer a sea-change
    Into something rich and strange.
    Sea-nymphs hourly ring his knell:
    Ding-dong.
    Hark! now I hear them,—ding-dong, bell.

    -William Shakespeare, from The Tempest


    Isla came into her fathers room with a mug of milk tea. Her skin was about the same color, and her young face was framed by long, dark, tight curls that seemed to form a halo all around. She set the mug of tea on his night stand.
    He wasn’t moving. His body wasn’t going up and down under the covers the way it usually did while he slept.

    “Da?”

    She knelt down by his bed and tried to shake him. He did not stir. She held her face close to his lips and nostrils, trying to feel even the faintest breath. His skin felt cold. He was cold. His hands clutched a small photograph in a frame: a picture of her mother when she was young. She wept, sea salt tears streaming down her face.

    The small Anglican church on the Island of Tristan da Cunha was more or less full. Isla’s father had been well liked, even though a strangeness had hung about him that many in the small community did not understand. He had come from off the island, no one could say where, and swept that young woman off her feet. And then the child. And the wife had died. But he stayed on the Island, with the little girl.

    She looked at the coffin where he laid. Briars and roses intertwined in a bouquet on top of the casket. Her father’s hand still clutched the photograph.

    Across the aisle the MacAedan boys sat silently in their best clothes. The youngest one, Artuir, 17, about her age, looked over at her. And then quickly popped his head into line with his brothers when she returned his gaze.

    That night she went to the cemetery. She tried to be silent and not be seen by anyone. She approached her father’s grave. It had a headstone and border around the plot made by white painted bricks. Beside his was her mother’s headstone. She bent down and kissed it. She took the shovel that she brought with her and planted it in with her foot. The words of the funeral rang in her ears

    May God our Father forgive us our sins and bring us to the eternal joy of his kingdom,
    where dust and ashes have no dominion.

    She looked out at the green field surrounding the cemetery, and she could hear the ocean waves.

    He makes me lie down in green pastures and leads me beside still waters.

    It took some time, but eventually she hit coffin. She cleared enough dirt away to open the lid. With all her strength, and several tries, she lifted her father out of the grave and placed him in a body bag. Then she filled the grave back in. They would be planting flowers soon.

    The graveyard was surrounded by a low wall made of rough unmortared stones, near the sea shore. Isla put the bag in a small cart and pulled it down towards the beach.

    She took the body of her father out of the bag, removed the clothes, and dragged it along the beach till she reached the waves.

    Naked came I out of my mother's womb, and naked shall I return thither: the Lord gave, and the Lord hath taken away; blessed be the name of the Lord.

    She removed her own clothes to the skin. She took hers and her fathers clothes and stowed them in the cart. She retraced her steps, careful that there were no tracks leading to the water.

    She began to swim out into the waves, continuing to drag the body with her into the sea.

    Behold, I tell you a mystery: We shall not all sleep, but we shall all be changed— in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye…

      ​Katab

      Despite the seasonably cool weather, many had chosen to stay inside tonight. Perhaps it was the grumbling sky, perhaps it had been a long work day for most. The usual hustle and bustle had quickly died down, and only a few cars now drove past a small café. Inside, a somewhat rough figure sits, sipping on some hot tea, and regarding the park just across the street. They wear a slightly worn and stained waterproof jacket and pants, cutting a sharp contrast between the soft wood design of the store. Presently, an employee come up to them, seemingly hoping for an early night.

      “Uh, I’m sorry, but the store is about to close.”

      The figure takes a moment and nods to the employee. They finish their drink and look around for a moment before rising.

      “Alright, last check. Wallet? Check. Key? Check. Passport and id? Check. Payment? Yep, still whole and full. Are the enchantments on my stuff still holding?...it’s been like a week, I’m sure they haven’t they gone bad yet. They’ll hold for another night. Probably. Phone? No mail. Should just be the night guards by now. Guess it’s go time.”

      The figure gets up and exits, turning towards the ally just next to the café. They furtively look back and forth before entering, pulling out a small 12-sided coin and look downward at the sidewalk. They begin to focus on an old memory, of a shadowy gate. They roll the coin in-between their fingers for a moment, before flicking it onto the street. As they do so, the imaginary gate opens, and before them appears a discoloration appears, as if the world before them was rendered into a grainy photograph. They regard it for a moment before striding forward, and into the discoloration. They seem to vanish from the ally, and the discoloration stands seemingly alone for a moment before vanishing as suddenly as it appeared.

          Katab

          Katab looks back out from the alley towards the park. The world around seems much dimmer than before, like a desaturated photo. Mist covers the ground around them, lightly obscuring their feet. The buildings and cars have become slight see-through. They begin striding towards their destination, across the street with little regard for the see-through cars. They spot a muttering figure through the mist that seemed to be all too physical, whom they carefully avoid. Even without checking, several such figures were all around. The park, from Katab’s Twilit perspective, seemed ominous. It seemed to Katab that there was much motion just out of their sight. They simply hurry their pace.

          After a few minutes, they made it out of the park, standing before a building of glass and stone, much more substantial that its surroundings. Letters and the building’s logo pronounced it to be the Australian Museum as if it was the only one with a claim to that title. Katab shakes their head, not for the first time, and eyes the glass doors. They were quite as solid as the rest of the building, even to this state of Twilight, perhaps due to the Gate residing inside the building. Katab sighed and made their way to the far back of the building. There was an employee-only door, leading to the various offices that formed the back part of the museum. Katab pulled out a small key, shaped out of ephemera to perfectly fit the lock of the back door. However, they hesitated before unlocking the door. One of the nightly security guards was illuminated just beyond the door, sitting in a small nook, reading a magazine. Katab looks at a light directly behind guard and aims a finger gun at it. A moment later, the light flickers and goes out. The guard starts, and turn to examine the light. Katab quickly and quietly unlocks the door, before slipping inside, and relocking it. The guard shakes his head at the light, before returning to his magazine, seemingly none the wiser.​

          Katab quickly made their way up to the second floor, passing several unaware security guards on their way up. They quickly spotted what they were looking for, an exhibit simply titled Dinosaurs. Achient stone and bone litter the exhibit, some sealed in glass containers, while others are exposed to air and the public. Katab takes a moment to admire the persevered bone before looking up and down the various aisle. They search around for any security guards who may be lingering in the exhibit. After determining the area to be clear, they make their way to a particular Tyrannosaurus skull, far bigger than any of the old bones. The mouth of the great predator was big enough to comfortably swallow a person whole. Dark stains cover large sections of its teeth, giving off the uncomfortable impression the skull had just eaten a meal. Katab makes one last check that nobody is around and presses the ephemeral key against the skull. The bones creak for a moment, before opening wide to reveal…another set of exhibits beyond, much like the room Katab was currently in. Katab shudders a little, but passes through the open maw into the room beyond.

                                  Bell Family Farm, Marin County, California, USA

                                  Nadeshiko Bell was in the shed preparing an arrangement of flowers. She was a skilled disciple of Ikebana, and focused on traditional designs. There was nothing traditional about this arrangement, but the girls had insisted. She had constructed a hanawa of yellow chrysanthemums and lilies.

                                  She looked out the door of the shed into a grassy field and the rows of flowers beyond. It was summertime, and the summer flowers were in bloom filling the air with sweet fragrance. There was a faint breeze and she could also smell the sea. The summers were cooler here than in Osaka. Over on the other side of the yard was the farm house, and the two little girls Keiko and Rei sat in their best dresses on the porch looking solemnly at a cardboard box, their little legs dangling off the porch swing.

                                  A little farther off , under an old tree, was her husband, Clint, and an old family friend, Richard, one of Clint's former kendo students. They were digging a hole big enough for the cardboard box and discussing something. Nadeshiko felt her anxiety quicken. She knew it had nothing to do with this sad day, but was some sort of mission that Clint was sending Richard on, filled with their secret names for things. She respected her husband and understood that his work was important, but she did not like these "missions". She thought about her father, the Air Defense General back in Japan. And her sister Patricia, traveling all over the world as a reporter. This reminded her of what she had been dreading. She needed to call Torisshuchan and tell her the bad news about Michi her cat, who had been with Trish since she was a girl and whom she had left here at the farm when she went to Europe.

                                      Paris, France, just before Midnight

                                      "I need to speak with you when you are able" said the text from Nadeshiko on Trish's phone. Trish Takehashi was on the train, in the quiet section, ridding from Istanbul to Paris. She had had quiet the adventure while she was in Istanbul and she was still trying to figure out how to describe some of it to her newspaper readers. High Tech corporate espionage and hackers that seemed to be able to hack even the laws of physics. How did she always end up in these situations?

                                      She got up to go to the Nonquiet section of the train. She was dressed in dark raw denim jeans, a tweed jacket, and sensible shoes for traveling. She pulled her dark, wavy black hair from her eyes . She made her way there and dialed Nadeshiko. She looked at her watch. Still afternoon there. "Konnichiwa Oneesan".

                                      The sky outside the window began to darken as Trish's older sister told her the news about her cat, Michi. He had been a very brave cat, and had even helped her to defeat some unusually aggressive spiders back when she lived in London. "I'm glad the girls are taking it so well, and that Clint's student was able to help out. He seems like a good friend," she said continuing in Japanese. "It is very sad. Shikata ga nai." Trish looked out the window as she disconnected the call. It had begun to rain, and drops were streaking down the glass. A few tears ran down her cheek as well. This always seemed to happen. Like the sky knew what she was feeling. Just luck she guessed.

                                      She went back to her seat in the quiet section. There, sitting across from her seat was a little girl. She was dressed oddly, in old fashioned clothes. As Trish sat down, she felt like that part of the train had gotten colder. The little girl coughed. She looked at the girls eyes. They seemed as sad as hers had been. They were feeling twins.

                                      ...

                                      Trish and the little girl, whom she had learned spoke German, got off the train when it arrived in Paris. As they went to go get Trish's luggage they saw a tall man with blond hair holding a sign that said "Trish Takehashi". He seemed a little lost, as if uncertain what he was doing. Trish took an instant liking to the man. And not just because he was tall and she had always felt a little subconscious about her own height growing up in Japan. He seemed, despite his overwhelmed and bewildered look, to have a very trustworthy face and Trish had a sense of these things, who to trust and who not to. He also, as she gave him a closer look, to have an oddness about him. Like little sparkles that seemed to dance about him. She had seen this a few times before, and it forebode some interesting times ahead.

                                      The little girl, Rahel, began a coughing fit. Trish got a cough drop from her bag and handed it to the child. It seemed to pass through her hand and drop to the floor. "Oops. Clumsy of me, " Trish said in German. She tried to give Rahel another one, but the same thing happened and she gave up.

                                      She and Rahel approached the tall, blond man. "Bonsoir monsieur. Je m'appelle Trish Takehashi, la journaliste. Ca va?" And stuck out her hand American style.

                                                          Gilad

                                                          Gilad made his way through the streets of Paris, slowly taking in the illuminated buildings and the buzz of night life. The night was a bit colder than usual, but nothing he couldn't handle. He was wearing a gray shirt and a brown leather jacket, a brown belt and some jeans. Reasonable enough for a bard, but durable for Arrow work, although he often wondered what kind of grudge inspired the design of those pockets.

                                                          "I think that is the Saint Chapelle, I might have gone too far"

                                                          He took some hand drawn directions out of his pocket and studied them for a moment, seemed he was still on the right track. He checked his watch, and hastened his pace. He arrived at the Gare Est Train station, pausing from a second to contemplate it from the entrance plaza. A structure the size of a palace, built to withstand countless winters, emitting an inner light that defied the lethargy of night. Inside, domes of glass shielded from the rain and made the sky itself into the ceiling mural. Day or night, countless good for sale filled the shelves. He made his way through it all.

                                                          "Sometimes I think these cosmic tyrants have been using lathunds, you could never build this if they didn't"

                                                          He made his way to the station's map and looked for the correct terminal, making his way there. He arrived just in time, but it seemed the train had yet to arrive. He took the opportunity to buy a beer and find a place to sit while waiting, enjoying people watching for a while. He heard many languages and sounds, sometimes the occasional Swedish, which brought a smile to his face. This was a good place to visit, especially when he missed the sounds of home.

                                                          The train pulled into the station, a colossal snake of iron and glass, moving faster than the greatest horse. He made his way to the disembarking point and lifted the sign with Trish's name. Suddenly Gilad heard the sound of a willow flute, the sylvan tune he heard in his Årsgång clear as day. He listened more attentively, no longer focusing on the melody, but on the intent, the heartbeat, opening all his senses to the indescribable meaning only it can convey. Waves of sound gained color, then forming as his eyes adjusted. Searching around, he saw a woman accompanied by a little girl humming with the song of the dead. She saw her speak to her, untroubled by the Sleepers nearby.

                                                          "She can see ghosts ?"

                                                          Before he could think further on it, the woman noticed him and made her way over. Before she could say anything, the little girl started coughing and the woman offered her some type of...medicinal pellets ? Whatever the case, it phased through the ghost and fell on the floor, but the woman didn't seem to acknowledge the fact.

                                                          "She can see them, but doesn't know they are depart..." She started speaking something in French, Gilad had no idea what it all meant, he heard Trish Takenashi somewhere in there.

                                                          "Okay, its the right person. They told me she can speak Swedish, lets just hope for the best"

                                                          "Hej, miss Takenashi and companion. I am Erik Andersen from Utopia, welcome to the City of Lights !" He turned his attention to the fallen medicine, then to the ghost and back to Trish "I'm afraid its too late for the medicine to work, your friend has already tossed the spoon in the corner."
