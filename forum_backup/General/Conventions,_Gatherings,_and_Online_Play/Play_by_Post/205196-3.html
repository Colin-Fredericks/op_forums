            Max understood that bit about 'more influence.' He'd had some strange and distressing thoughts every now and then, so knowing it wasn't just him was a help. Also a harm. "S-so... We're just going to think more and more like our animals the longer we stay here?" It was bad enough looking like a pig. Just earlier that day he'd found a little mud pool and had been tempted to go roll around in it. That these inclinations might get worse was very unwelcome news. To say nothing of the other animals: the lion was clearly holding himself back, and this was just a few minutes for a chat! Even if, say, the tiger had an iron will, it would only take a moment's slip-up. And they'd be in close quarters a lot longer than just a few minutes.

            So Max took the lion-man's advice and listened to every word. 'Arcadia... So that's where we are?' He rolled the words around in his head, hoping to turn something up as he nodded. "Thanks for the help. And the risk." He didn't know what sort of tortures the guy had gone through, but didn't need more than guesses like "becoming countless lion skin rugs" or "spending ages as a head mounted on a wall." Still, while more than he'd like to admit was glad to see the maned predator leave, he had another question. He took a deep breath and tried to calm himself as he stepped forward. 'Oh, quit shaking! It's not... not that scary...' he told himself, completely unconvinced before the terrified squealing in his heart. The snoutful of the carnivore's scent would do that to the poor little piggy.

            "Just uh... Just a moment more. How'd you get here, anyway?" It had puzzled Max. "I tried looking for the road that brought us here and the next thing I knew I was on the other side of the ranch. Is there some kind of trick to it?" Another thought then struck him, ears visibly perking. He wasn't actually sure he wanted to know the answer, but, well... Knowledge was power, and they needed every bit they could get.

            "A-And... One last thing. It was the year of the Horse when we were taken and..." He didn't want to refer to any of them as animals, but he had no other name. "... and we haven't seen the Horse in a long time. You guys are on a shorter cycle, right? So..." The pig's eyes drifted toward Nate's for a moment. "... So what happens when it's time for your sign?"

                        Nate eased up at Leo's departure, but wondered about his words. What did he mean by wandering the realm? What realm? Arcadia? Suddenly, he wasn't sure about wanting to go next. Leo was right : his urges were getting stronger and stronger; and he didn't want to risk losing himself to the brain-washing magic of this world. Although, the little bleating voice in his head tried to reassure him. That's right, he thought. For all the madness and horror in this place, there is also wonder. He just had to look around him : this ranch was gorgeous. He wouldn't mind staying here. He didn't have to chance to explore it yet! They should lay low for a while, and wait for a window of opportunity, but for now, they should enjoy it. They were seeing things an human wouldn't ever dream of!

                        "I still don't trust him", he said to his two hoofed colleagues. "He's clearly getting desperate to get the Keeper's attention again, just like a cat wanting to get petted. If he's losing interest in them, that means we're safe as long as we behave, and there's no need to rush ourselves, we'd only run into more troubles and tortures. We should play along for now, and be the nice Beasts the Keeper wants us to be." Yeah. This plan felt right. Just play along for now. "Of course, we'll have to focus on keeping our humanity. But as long as we're aware of this place's influence on our mind, that shouldn't be a problem."

                            Max likewise relaxed with the Leo's exit; he hadn't realized he'd had his legs tight as a coiled spring, ready to bolt. Perhaps it was the new feeling of calm that made Nate's suggestion seem more palatable than it otherwise might. He certainly bristled at the thought of doing what the Keeper wanted and just being good little animals, but well... Things had been so hard, and it would be so easy not to rock the boat, now that it was out of stormy waters. They'd suffered so much; didn't they deserve a little happiness? Just for a bit? "I..." he began uncomfortably, "I... don't know if I agree with everything, but I see a point. I mean, after all we've been through, we're all really stressed out. I think we should grab some R&R while we can. It'll clear our heads, make it easier to think of a way out. It's... it's just for a while, right?"

                            The pig visibly relaxed further. He didn't quite pick up on how "laze about and indulge" was the basic plan in question, nor how it perfectly lined up with his subtle swinish inclinations. "But I think we can trust the guy. It sounds a lot like what the Keeper was saying before. The way out might be something like Jessica's idea -- pretty sure you caught that one." Max looked at Nate's large ears, and flopped his own pair for emphasis. "If we're waiting, we can just mull it all over."

                            At this point, he took a deep breath. The air smelled just as good here as it had when he'd arrived, and he was actually glad to have such a keen nose. As Jessica quietly suspected, he could even smell generally where the lion had gone off, but was mostly focused on how much nicer it smelled without him. Actually, around here, it was even more appealing than before. He worked his snout a moment more, and managed to zero in on the particular. Distracted and unthinking, Max turned his head away from the other two farm animals and began sniffing at the air, toward a tree. Or, more precisely, something growing near its roots. Jessica had also been quite right about pigs and truffles. Though this little piggy wasn't finding it so much of a blessing, as a trickle started to drip down from the side of his mouth.

                            Catching himself with a grunt, Max shook his head and swallowed the excess saliva. He blushed at this embarrassing display. "Aw, man..." he groaned. "I... I don't know how easy keeping our humanity is gonna be here..." He tried not to think about the fungi, but his snout still sang its treacherous siren song. "I'm... really scared about that." He looked between the Ox and Sheep, as though for support. "H-Have you guys gone through anything like... like that?"

                                                Nate openly laughed at Jessica's exit, seems like he had struck a nerve. He was cut short by the bell, and gulped out of nervousness when their respective meals appeared. If we want to survive and not get punished, we have to force ourselves. Besides, if we don't eat, we'll likely starve or be too weak to escape potential danger."

                                                He looked around, and instinctively lowered his head, feeling as if the Collector was watching them. "Look, just think about something typically human to focus on while eating. Like...I don't know, maths, grammar, law, or a tv show or whatever." He tried to remember his favourite song from his band, the Wild Exhibit --Oh, the irony--, and began to mentally rehearse it.

                                                    Jessica's revealed attraction surprised him a bit. 'How... How do you think about having the hots for someone at a time like this?' The idea also made Max wonder, just a bit, whether she wasn't being as honest with herself about her bestial side affecting her. Animals weren't exactly shy about making their affections known. Which he felt, on second thought, was maaaaaybe a bit sexist. ' "Oh, a woman getting attracted quickly? Clearly animal urges at work!" Ugh, the hell am I thinking...' He did feel better about rolling his eyes at the Sheep, though. "Oh, for crying out loud, Nate, just--"

                                                    But that was as far as Max got before they all heard "the dinner bell." He looked around, curious what was coming. Also concerned; part of him had been waiting for the other shoe to drop. He smelled it before he saw it, and couldn't smell much of anything else. A great trough, full of... Yeah, he really didn't want to think. It looked vile. He could hardly imagine anything more unpleasant trying to pass itself off as food. His food. Which made it particularly uncomfortable that much as it smelled as foul and overpowering as it looked... Part of him also found it fascinating. Comfortable. Right.

                                                    "... No," Max told... well, the universe itself at this point. "No no no. No. Just... No. You have got to be kidding me." The protests were a bit hard to swallow, of course. His snout was plainly intrigued by the contents. Nate's counsel didn't do much for his resolve, either. "... I... I know." He trembled. "And... And that's not bad advice. I'll try that. Thanks. Let's uh, just... get through this." A bubble drifted up from the brown sludge. He shuddered. "Bon appétit..." he said sardonically, taking a seat at the edge of the trough. "Just, uh... Do me a solid, ah? H-Hit me if I look like I'm enjoying this."

                                                    Max looked down at his meal. There was a faint temptation to plunge his face into it, but he held strong, there. Not that he was sure of how to eat it. He glanced at Nate's efforts and felt a twinge of envy. 'It's like he's just eating a bag of chips...' He dipped his hand in, hesitantly, and found some resistance in places. He pulled out what looked like most of a carrot, still dripping brown. 'Okay... Okay... You got this. Just. Think of Black Adder annnnnd one, two, ... three.' Max bit into it. It was the mushiest carrot he'd ever had, likely half-rotten. And significantly tastier than it had any right to be. '... Dammit.' He finished the rest of that handful quickly, nearly licking his fingers. He then cupped an especially liquid section in his hands and awkwardly tipped it into his mouth. Much messier, but again, not half-bad. Enough for him to repeat the maneuver twice more, before the line, "A man with a small, painted, wooden duck on his head," brought him back to himself. He licked his chops and checked his progress. The trough was still quite full. 'This is gonna be a loooooong meal...' he thought with a sigh, picking out what appeared to be a corn husk. He decided it beat out the tamales at his roommate's favorite restaurant.
