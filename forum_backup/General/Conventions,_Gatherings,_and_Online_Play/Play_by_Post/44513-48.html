                                    Pyrrhian Legions

                                    No one knows from whence this ghostly horde came.

                                    Some stories tell of an epic warrior-king whose small nation was famed for the heroic prowess of its troops. But this nation was lead into a war with a much larger realm. Though it won battle after battle, the larger nation could easily replace their raw conscripts, while the smaller had a greatly reduced pool of manpower. Eventually, the tiny nation was ground to dust, even with its string of victories unbroken.

                                    Others tell of an antediluvian race of giants, slain by the gods in the ages before man conquered Creation. The foul shades of these giants use their fell rituals to enslave the minds of ghosts, leading them in vengeance against the favored children of the gods.

                                    Still others tell of bands of mercenaries commissioned into the service of one of the Lords of Death, sent out to build independent legions in their service. But the Lords of Death have waged war on the Legions in the past, so that cannot be true, right?

                                    Regardless, these Legions travel the northlands of the Underworld, seeking souls to join their ranks. Entire underworld nations have fallen before the might of these ghastly armies, and their inhabitants given the option to join their ranks, or to be rendered into Soulsteel and Jade to build and repair the legion’s warmachines. With tales of black necromancies and the snarling visages of the legionnaires, many choose oblivion or eternal torment over service.

                                    Yea, many in the legions are obviously inhuman, though whether this is a relic of their pre-death heritage or a symptom of membership, none know. Something exerts influence over their members, though, transforming the most reluctant recruit over time into a grim legionnaire. This also influence seems to warp the specialist skills of the recruit into the service of war; architects become siege engineers, doctors become skilled in the arts of molition, herdsmen the tamers of plasmic war-beasts. Those without skills so warped simply become the grim linesmen of the legions.

                                    The legions move independently, engaging in their own battles, though is is not uncommon to have two or three join forces. They never fight each other, though. Whomever (or whatever) controls the Legions seems to have some form of prescience; it is not unusual for the Legions to march for months, only to arrive at the site of a large, nation-shaping battle exactly as it begins. As the shades of those fallen warriors descend into the underworld, the Legionnaires press-gang them into their own ranks.

                                    There are eleven known Legions, each one massing between one and six thousand in number (the average size is about four thousand). It seems that once a legion reaches a certain mass, one of the captains creates their own command structure and forms their own legion. Should a legion get too small, it is absorbed into the next one it encounters (if it should still be too small at that time).

                                    With a total number of around 45 thousand, the Pyrrhian Legions rival the military might of even the more martial Lords of Death. At least, they would, if they were to congregate in one point. The last time that this is known to have happened was when the Mask of Winters sought to subjugate three legions into his ranks in RY 421. Even the Battle of Futile Blood only drew five of the dozen or so Legions.
