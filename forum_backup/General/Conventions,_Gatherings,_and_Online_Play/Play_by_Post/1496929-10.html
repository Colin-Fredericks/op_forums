            As the two men enter Bucketown, a grim and morbid sight awaits them. Bucketown is a town built around a single business, and that business is funerals, taxidermy and embalming. A bizarre smell of formaldehyde is pungent to the point of nausea.

            Every single building in Bucketown is death-centric, from comically tiny funeral homes to graveyards where picnics are held every day, to a crimson cathedral made of bird bones. The hobs that move about are universally very small, and resemble upright walking cats. Each cat carries a first aid kit with them, and they sing wordlessly to each other. They wear elaborate little business suits all in white with purple trimming, and don’t seem to notice the three interlopers.

            Buildings are clustered closely together, with narrow alleyways forming paths to follow.

            “Welcome to Bucketown”, the Marquesa said.

                    The Marquesa replies, “Who knows? The minds of hobs might not as alien as the Gentry, but there’s still no predicting what they’ll do.”

                    She takes the two men to another oddity: a candy store, all wrong angles and childish handwriting on the sign above the door reading, Witchypoo’s Candy Store. It looks excessively cramped, especially for two Ogres, but all three squeeze in with surprisingly little trouble.

                    Inside the candy shop is a claustrophobic nightmare, walls filled with boxes, display places showing off new candy, and workers—-little gingerbread men helpers going to and fro. Behind the main counter sits a human-sized, humanoid carp, dripping wet and writing quite dexterously for having no hands.

                    The Marquesa approaches, and says, “Witchypoo. You remember me? I bring two petitioners who desire answers.”

                    Witchypoo adjusts her small spectacles and smiles. “Ahhhh....Your Ladyship. Bring two fine specimens to to my store. Mmmm.”
