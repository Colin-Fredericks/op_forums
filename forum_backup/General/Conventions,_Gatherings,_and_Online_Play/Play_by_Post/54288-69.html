    (Yi)

    "The Weaver of Waters and Wishes answers your call, Exalted One," Ainti Teph says with a small nod. Meeting Yi's eyes she continues, "You are exiled from Lookshy, and have been declared Anathema; your name has been excised from the rolls of those who may call upon the service of me and mine at will. But your contest entreagues me, and I shall permit this minor indiscretion. "

    Yi can see the hunger in her eyes, orbs as green and deep as the inland sea. It is not the contest that has caught her attention.

    It was the prize.

    With the terms of the contest set, the water spirit pulls out her shuttle of blackest jade and sends it soaring unto the heavens. When the shuttle returns, it brings with it a wine-dark thunderhead, crackling with yellow-blue lightning. She uses not her fingers to weave her cloth; tiny jets of water from the river shoot the shuttle through the cloud. The jets knock the jade artifact though the cloud up and down, side to side, fore and aft, and all directions in between with such speed that the heavy weaver's tool becomes little more than a narrow blur. Some of the jets are caught in the tightening cloud. You can tell this is no error, but instead a deliberate part of the construction. The rhythmic sound of the jets, the crackling of the lightning and the whir of the shuttle combine to form an almost musical quality.

    A long bolt of cloth sits before you, woven of still-vapor cloud. Long stands of still-liquid water run through it in slowly swirling patterns, contrasted with the sharp flashing patterns of trapped lightning. Combined, you get the impression that the water-spirit had taken a hurricane and made a scarf from it.

    With both parties finished, Ainti Teph examines the products. Both pieces are subject to all manner of testing its quality. Given the degree of her examination you are half surprised when she places both bolts down without tasting them. With a wicked grin on her face, the water spirit declares, "As the judge of this contest, I declare that my craft is-"

    Ainti Teph is cut off when a short dark figure seems to condense from the mist and places a slender hand upon her shoulder. You hear a soft murmur echo from the new arrival, and the water spirit's face pales and her eyes grow shocked.

    "- is inferior. Definitely inferior. I lost." Ainti Teph blurts out. The figure removes its hand and seems to fade back into the mist with that, but you get the feeling that whomever, or whatever, it is is still watching.

    The water spirit holds up her end of the bargain and places the desired charm upon your cloth. once she has done so, Ainti Teph says, "I will not tell anyone of this meeting, Exalted One. But never call me again if you relish the freedom you have from the Seventh Legion."

    She stands tall and straight as she declares her term of service fulfilled. And with that, she is gone.

                Jensven gives Maia the kind of respectful, professional nod he had seen the Guild smiths give each other during his short stay with them. It wouldn't do to give any impression that something that hadn't happened was happening. Besides, the forge-mistress is at work, and he doesn't want to disturb her. If she is constructing such a weapon, it has to be for someone of import. The smith turned Solar enjoyed watching other smiths work nearly as much as he enjoyed doing the forging himself. He doubts he would ever be able to sculpt fire like that, but if he looks closely there was something he could learn about forge-craft. Observe he does. He knows he could use the new powers he was given by the sun to increase his ability to focus his sight, but he holds that in check. He doesn't want to use such things frivolously. It seems a waste.

                Shadow has other ideas. To the dog, Maia is someone to be pounced on, licked, and greeted with a blur of the tail. He gets as close as he dares whines and makes noises that sound like he is attempting to imitate mortal speech. It is obvious the flames scare him, but he is insistent on getting the attention of the woman in front of him.

                The Dawn turns to the equally hyper forge spirit, "Yes, Cinder. The wood is for you. I thought you could use it as a resting place while we were traveling. That is, if you are able to rest in it and not burn it up. I also bought various other kinds of wood, charcoal, and coal for you to eat when you are away. You do need to eat, right?"

                        (Yi)

                        "You sound like Serapis when he's drunk," whispers a feminine voice from no discernible direction, "And Helios always."

                        Once again, a figure seems to condense from the mist, this time right in front of Yi. The figure is short and cowled, and covered in loose clothing of a dark grey or blue that disguise the lines of their figure, though Yi can notice a slight flair to their hips and chest, hinting at a feminine form beneath.

                        "I have no name in this age, though in the First I was called Zorya Serenitor, 'Who Cleanses The Skies'," the figure says softly, "the Sun has decided to pay attention to this place, in his own way. Where the gaze of the Most High falls, my siblings and I go." You detect a hint of amusement in her voice as she continues," I was in the neighborhood, so your call to Heaven called me here. As is my nature."

                        Zorya begins to fade back into the mist, saying "I will not help you otherwise, for that too is my nature."

                        Zorya disappears into the mist, her form and presence gone, leaving Yi seemingly alone the banks of the river. Then he feels a hand grip onto his shoulder, softly but firmly.

                        "Be careful," Zorya whispers into Yi's ear, "his heart still breaks whenever one of you falls."

                        The hand releases Yi's shoulder, and when he turns around there is nothing there.

                                                (Jensven)

                                                "Oh! Okay," Cinder says. He flits over to Maia, who is putting down the weapon she was working on. Cinder's fire becomes very straight and steady, almost as if he is standing up straight. He leads his throat, saying, "Madame Thaumaturge, as per the details of our contract, I declare my term of service complete."

                                                Maia nods, a small frown on her face. Cinder continues on, saying, "if I don't make it back, I want you to know you've been just like a mother to me. A crabby old mother who needs to get out of the house more often-"

                                                Maia's prosthetic arm slams into the tiny fire-ball, sending him flying across the room. "Your jokes stink," she says, her voice cracking. "Make it back safely, you hear? Both of you. Alright," she amends after a sharp bark from Shadow, "All three of you. Make it back."

                                                Cinder laughs as he flies to the top of the room and releases a massive disk of fire, drawing all eyes in the room to himself. "You her that," he says with a manic laugh in his voice, "You can't rely on me to do your work for you! Good luck dealing with Methis!"

                                                The tiny fire-spirit. Now returned to his normal coloration, lights down on the wood Jensven brought. He spreads over nearly half the wood, but remains a respectful distance from Jensven's hand. "We leaving, or what?"
