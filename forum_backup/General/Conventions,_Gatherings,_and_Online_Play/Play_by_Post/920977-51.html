            Originally posted by Caladriu View Post
            Guest Something I was thinking about given how often Moonstone risks Wisdom loss. The Chronicles of Darkness book has this system:



            Can that be applied to Act of Hubris rolls as well? It seems to make sense given that Wisdom is about control and foresight and thinking about your actions in advance. Maybe it could be used only if you meditate on the act you intend/expect to commit and what its consequences might be.
            Sorry, just realized I never replied to this.

            I'm on the fence about it. On one hand I totally get the case in favor of letting you do this. Seems to make sense that meditating like this would help you stay present in a way that you don't lose sight of reason when casting spells.

            At the same time, it seems like mundane meditation should not sufficiently protect you from the temptations of hubris. Against the very real power that you're drawn to when you use Awakened magic, meditating seems like it would be a paper shield.

            Personally I think the simplest answer is to go with no for now, but I might change my mind after thinking about it more. The CoD core book is really intended for mortals, not mages, so I don't think it makes sense to apply mundane actions to supernatural character rules. The things I'm ok with using from that book are rules on mundane actions and equipment, like Merits.

Please either PM me or comment here with your general thoughts, things you liked/disliked, or any questions or concerns about the game. I'm still a developing Storyteller, and I want you to enjoy the game, so please let me know if you're not. I hope you are, though! I'm certainly enjoying the chance to write and create ideas and bring this universe to life, and I'm really digging all of your characters.

                        Kvitebjørn Kong Valemon - Thanks for the feedback! Glad to hear you're enjoying it. Personally I like to be the one "at the helm" for NPCs, such as leading conversations, etc., but I really like the idea of players being able to introduce NPCs relevant to your characters.                                                         Sure, great question. Here are some possible examples I can think of:

                                                        1) Let's say that Phoenix is disciplined and has a steady routine for when and how he trains at the martial arts studio. Maybe something comes up that unexpectedly prevents him from following that routine, so he gets irritable towards someone that day when he can't go.

                                                        2) Maybe he thinks in more black and white terms about people based on first impression, and someone's mean to him at first encounter. The next time they try to offer him help with something or ask a favor, he shoots them down because of how they treated him before, so he misses out on a chance to improve the relationship or get some help with something.

                                                        3) Phoenix has a rough day and doesn't know how to tackle a problem. He decides to fall back on a certain routine he's done before (e.g., train at the dojo, talk to an old friend) rather than face the actual problem.
