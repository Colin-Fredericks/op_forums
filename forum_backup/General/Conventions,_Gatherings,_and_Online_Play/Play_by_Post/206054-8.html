                                        Ah, I'm glad things seem well-received on that last post. ^_^ Always concerned about overstepping boundaries.

                                        @Cire: Thanks, I'm glad it's not an issue. And aye, like I said, I could be happy either way. I'm enjoying these characters and would like to follow them, but it would also be the first time I've started a game with an intent to tell one story, told that one story, and stopped, and there's an interesting feeling, there. Really, it's a matter of 'I won't be disappointed,' whatever happens.

                                        @Nyrufa: We could always frame it less as sticking strictly to Changeling: The Lost and more as something very heavily inspired by and drawing from that game. That seems like it might lay to rest some of your trepidations. It could also keep those of us better acquainted with C:tL as written on our toes, as we may not know what to expect.

                                        Aye, definitely makes sense that things would be less solidly rooted from the get-go. I've also kinda gotten the impression from reading online Changeling character origin stories that it's pretty common for a character to have... properties in Arcadia that they very definitely don't in-play, which are either explained away or just sort of fade during/after the escape (e.g. a woman turned into a dragon several stories tall ultimately finds a thin crack in her den, and wears and scratches her massive body away into something relatively human-sized in the effort of getting through). It makes sense that the characters would have the potential to explore a full assortment of relevant Kiths. If we go for the "drawing inspiration from" option, settling on a Kith may not be something that needs to happen; the Arcadia-granted properties can come up when they're relevant and fade when they aren't.

                                        But yeah, I don't anticipate the Boar sticking around for more than the next post or two, nor of Max getting a Bruce Banner deal going on. We'll see, though.

                                        @Evilmarmelade: Aww, cheer up. ^_^ Pigs are known to be very intelligent, and I've seen goats and sheep appear on some lists of smart animals, too, so I don't think there's a vast gulf. It's also worth considering that measuring animal intelligence is kinda tricky, and without a universal standard. I mean, with all the different physiologies, there probably can't be a universal standard. And that's before considering that the notion of IQ itself is controversial. There are many different types of intelligence and a given test is by its nature going to have to prioritize some over others, not least because some types of intelligence can be very hard to measure in quantitative terms.

                                        So, really, that bit just says that sheep and cattle are on about the same level just below pigs based on whatever standards of intelligence a particular test selected. There could be ways in which sheep and cattle think circles around the porkers and vice-versa.

                                        ... So, yes, I'm not just long-winded in my IC posts. ^_^o
