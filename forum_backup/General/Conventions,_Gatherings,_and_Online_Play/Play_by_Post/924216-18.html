            Character sheet looks good, thanks for updating! I do have a few thoughts for you:

            1) Resources: Your background says that Dr. Luck is pretty broke, but now you've added Resources 2, suggesting that he has some decent expendable income. So you could say that he's recently inherited some money, maybe had his first breakthrough with a book, or that lotto ticket he found actually won him some money. You also could then feasibly own a nicer car, an actual working cell phone, and so forth. But if you like the idea of him being a "starving artist", you could cut the Resources merit and put that into something else.

            2) Legacy: If interested, you could start with your character inducted into a Legacy. If you aren't familiar with these, I would review that section of the core Mage 2E book. It's a way to give yourself another Ruling arcanum and access to some additional supernatural abilities. I am using a house rule that if your Legacy's primary arcanum is already a Ruling arcanum for your character (so if you pick one that has Fate or Time as its Ruling arcanum), then you get its optional arcanum as a Ruling arcanum instead.

            3) Yes, there will always be a possibility of encountering other supernatural creatures, including vampires, ghosts, goetia, or something else. However, werewolves will be the most likely to encounter because of the setting.

                                                Well Lictorial training usually takes about a year and you wouldn't do it with a complete rookie either way. My char would just have spent more time refining his skills after awakening and dealing with personal issues than the other pcs perhaps. Or he is a late bloomer or something.

                                                The real question I suppose is wether you want to have a PC even be a Lictor. They're forbidden from taking apprentices or holding any sort of other official office, as they can be called in to arbitrate at the drop of a hat. So essentially he could get called in to work a state and a half away on the drop of a hat. Personally I think that'd make for a neat storyhook, but IC it means his cabal would need to be aware that he may leave suddenly now and then and it's rather unconditional.

                                                I have Sanctum and Sigil but I haven't looked through it in a long time. Anything in particular I should review that relates to cabals?

                                                Cabal positions (p. 20) - basically just classical ways for the cabal to organize itself, not really necessary imo but could add some fluff (and as a thearch I guess my char should push for them to be established, they have hard on for all kinds of well established organization afterall).

                                                Protocols (p. 25) - basically another form of inter cabal organization. Essentially the cabal works out what they stand for and swear an oath (can be but is usually not a binding oath via the fate arcanum) to uphold these guidelines, turning the whole thing into an iron law. Can be especially important for thearchs who don't want to screw over their cabal. (SL usually doesn't ask thearchs to activly works against their cabal, but pumps them for information about them. They won't ask them to break the law to do so however....Not widely known outside the order though)

                                                as part of the Protocols - the great Rights (p. 26) - This is arguably the most important sections. Details the 5 great rights of cabals (which are golden laws, so trump any other regulations that have been established by a consilium or something of the like). The cabal has to choose which of these rights they want to upholds. It entails a right of crossing (establishes a way of crossing the cabals territory for other mages), the right of Emeritus (those that have earned the cabals respect are expected get it in return - the most often disregarded right I think, since it kind of gives the cabal a responsibility to act in certain way to people deserving respect... its actually quite complex, best read it for yourself), RIght of Hospitality (the duty to grant hospitality to those who ask it), Right of Nemesis (basically the awakened war option), RIght of Sanctuary (Sancta are involatile and should not be violated).

                                                Cabals can choose not to uphold certain of the rights, but those who choose to ignore more than one, maybe two are usually regarded poorly. You also can't expect people to respect the sanctity of your own sanctum if your creed doesn't involve respecting theirs....

                                                Sigils (p. 34) - not super important, mostly fluff a cabal has a Sigil (or sigils even), mostly fluff
