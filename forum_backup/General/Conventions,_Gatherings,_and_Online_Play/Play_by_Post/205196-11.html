        Well. That certainly met the "very obviously dangerous and very, very quickly" criteria. Max's wagging tail slowed its tempo to a dead stop as the color drained from his mud-smeared face. Understandably, he was too stunned to do much more than gawk, even as his fear built to a fever pitch. It was like a bucket of ice water, returning his rational mind to the fore. A mind that was horrified at how badly that had almost gone... and even more horrified at how a part of him still wanted to wallow in the mud badly enough to brave the danger. The prey animal in him had joined the "Get the hell away from the mud" lobby, but it worried him that, if that wasn't the case, the hedonist's calls of "How bad could it be?" might just get him to commit that bizarre and pathetic form of suicide, after all.

        Of course, it was hard for the fear of being eaten not to win out, especially when the awful thing lunged at Max. The pig started squealing in even more terror than before, throwing himself away from the mud pit and turning to run. In his haste, he turned at an odd angle and lost his balance, ending up on all-fours. It dazed him a moment, but the adrenaline was still flowing and his bestial side seemed to prefer the new posture, starting to charge off. The Pig being nearer the forefront worked against him, though; the mud creature managed to brush a naked leg and oh... It felt as wonderful as he'd hoped and feared. The tingle of relief and pleasure made him pause for a moment, even halting the non-stop squealing. During which the monstrosity managed to move in for a tighter grip. That resumed the frantic struggle to get away from the avatar of death on the mire, but things did not look good. Desperate, Max squealed out to it: "Please! Please! Don't eat me!"

                                    Max had tried making a go for that sign, whether for leverage to pull himself free or -- if poorly secured -- a weapon. The thing caught up with him too quickly. It wrapped what could charitably be called its mouth around him. The little pig just stared at its maw. 'This is it,' he thought bleakly. This was how his story was going to end. Eaten by a monster made of mud. And, as a last kick to his human pride, he was going to die squealing.

                                    But then he didn't. An arrow caught it dead in the "face" and Max's struggles managed to get him somewhere. And that somewhere, as Sagittarius clip-clopped into view, was breaking free and bolting on four legs toward the centaur. And then hiding behind him, of course, looking around the equine legs at the flailing mud-beast. The disparaging quips weren't welcome -- Max took particular issue with the word choice on that last one -- but he was certainly not looking this gift horse in the mouth. He just panted back there, heart still racing, but managed to grunt out a, "Tha... Thank... Thank you... So... So much..." Even with his staggered breaths and bestial voice, the pig's gratitude was obvious.

                                    And Max hadn't looked like much more of a pig than he did at that moment. On all-fours, naked, trouble speaking, coated from the chest down in mud -- and with heavy splotches above that, even... But at least he was alive to feel the humiliation and shame of it. After all, someone had come along to save him. 'Again...' he thought, just a mite bitter. He wondered if he'd ever get a chance to save anyone, or even save himself. It had bothered him a while, how far off this was from expectations he hadn't realized he'd had. Max read a lot of fantasy. Someone from "the real world" winding up in a fantasy land and becoming a hero was a cliche. But that cliche had featured in some daydreams. They were about as far from his present state as one could get.

                                    To underscore this, with the immediate danger over, part of him really wanted to roll that mud over the rest of him while it was still nice and gooey. Max fought back the urge. If all he was good for was getting saved, he could at least make his savior feel he had saved a remotely respectable person, not a filthy animal. He tried to calm himself down as he brought himself back up to two legs. He also tried not to think about how nice and natural four legs had started to feel, there. He tried for conversation to take his mind off that. "So... Just, uh... Just in the neighborhood or... were you... keeping an eye on me?"
