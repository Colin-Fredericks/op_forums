                                        Yi bowed graciously and began.

                                        "To you, Larissa, I begin to sing:
                                        O glorious goddess, flashing-eyed, keen of mind, great of heart, pure in nobility, savior of the people, valorous, beloved.
                                        From their honored midst the Boule beheld you, divine soul in mortal frame, and gazed in awe on your benevolent works.
                                        But Larissa rose mightily from amid the people and stood before Origenes the founder, clad in authority:
                                        great Novalesk began to reel tremendously at the might of the strong-armed goddess, and Creation round about cried fearfully, and Heaven was moved and set to furious activity and wonder:
                                        the bright charioteer of Day stopped her great vessel a long while, until the great Archon drew her blade and all the people shouted for joy.
                                        And great Novalesk was glad. And so hail to you, daughter of Origenes the founder! Now I will remember you and with many songs as well."

                                        His song rippled across the sanctum, their essence laden reverberations causing its structure to dance like the surface of a pond. He caught up his on echo and incorporated it into his melody until at the height he was singing in three part harmony with himself. With the final word, the golden winds of his hymn came together before the goddess, coalescing into ambrosia.

                                        It was something he had pieced together from snippets of conversation and prayer as he had walked with Larissa. It was probably missing sections, and he had been forced to improvise, but he hopped the Ambrosia would be well received.

                                        Prayer roll for no other reason then to benefit the cult of Larissa, provide her with some Ambrosia, (and perhaps get her to think well of me)

                                                    (Tirien)

                                                    Ceryd points Tirien towards an establishment, which she claims is a likely spot to get a nice picture of the Vicruith adults. She excuses herself, saying that something has come up. She transforms into a Crimson Flying Fox (with miniature versions of her horns) and departs, her vulpine form alternating between running and jumping the the trees.

                                                    Tirien follows her advice, finding herself in a Public House. Inside, she finds many a Geherin, including a handful of Darmud's warriors and Dero. Dero appears to be basking in the admiration of young woman of approximate age to him. Ah, to be young.

                                                    The nearly the entire of the public house is listening to a particular warrior (of a clan with Tirien cannot identify), who is telling some tale.

                                                    "That makes no sense, Osin" one of Darmud's warriors calls out, "How'd anyone know what was going on in Heaven while Finn was battling the Six-Speared Knight?"

                                                    "Does I look like a skald, Fer?" The narating warrior, Osin, responds, "Maybe Lady Ceryd was there, eh? Spending some family time with her mum." He takes a large swig from a pitcher-sized mug. "Dammit man, I lost my place." He takes another swig while apparently attempting to recall his place in the story.

                                                    "So, while Finn was battling the Six-Speared Faery Lord, the Sun and the Moon and the Red Star of Battle were all in Heaven looking down at him, right? Each one held a spark of their powers in their hand, and argued over which one would make Finn their champion. The Red Star said that he was a might warrior, especially skilled in fighting the Fae. The Fae are the Stars' special enemies, you see, so the Red Star demanded to make Finn hers. The Sun said that he hadn't seen such a might warrior since the sun-kings of old, so naturally, he wanted him too. And the Moon claimed that he was already hers, with the Vicruith being her daughters' people and all.

                                                    "They argued three days and three nights, while Finn and the Faery knight fought across forest, hill and moor. In the end, they decided to settle the enlargement by casting lots. That's the only game they could play to settle it, since the perfection of the sun and moon make it so they always win any game of skill, and the Stars naturally tilt the field in their favor. But lots is entirely chance, so none of that went into it. But as they was casting their dice, Finn slipped in the moor, and the Faery ran him through.

                                                    "Finn's body sank into the moor as the Fae knight took his prize, Finn's magic spear. And then he rode of, to terrorize parts unknown. The End."

                                                    "Nah, that's not what happened," another warrior shoots out, "The Fae tied Finn's body to his saddle and rode off into Faery, proclaiming him to be the best warrior he'd ever faced in battle."

                                                    "Either way," Osin responds, "The Faery lord killed Finn and his body was not recovered. Satisfied?" A general rumble of agreement rings out.

                                                    "Its a damned shame, too," yet another speaks out, "Finn'd have made a damfine chief in ten of twenty years." Another rumble of agreement.

                                                    With that, the collected warriors toast to fallen Heroes, and then return to their individual conversations.
