                                                    Something Awesome:

                                                    It was an interesting Calibration to say the least.

                                                    Of course, Calibration was always an interesting time, Alamass thought, as he carefully peeled an unsightly strip of bark from the Magnolia.

                                                    The elemental popped the bark in his beak-like maw and chewed as he examined the tree. It stood nearly fifty feet tall, towering over the surrounding estate, and its five trunks had intertwined into a towering braid. Much like the body of the Elemental Dragon crawling on its surface.

                                                    Alamas climbed his way up the tree, thick-thorned vines uncoiling from his body to hold onto the tree. Alamass had carefully tended the tree’s Essence for centuries, and returned to the Cabin every Calibration to help it through this time of transition. One of his masters, some centuries ago, had likened Alamass’s task to pruning the tree. While technically accurate, Alamass preferred to think of it as bonsai, but in reverse. For, unbeknownst even to the sorcerously breed warriors guarding the courtyard, the tree Alamass tended was the source of the Demesne that powered all of the Cabin’s wonders. The dragon jumped slightly as a blossom erupted from the tree trunk inches from his head. In mere moments, it opened into a large white flower, and a sweet scent wafted out.

                                                    Plucking the flower gently, Alamass scurried down the tree. He clutched the flower gently in his taloned hand, marveling at its beauty, its symmetry. At the bottom , he coiled his long mass into a pile; the closest his serpentine body could get to a sitting position. Alamass cupped his hands together and took in the blossom’s scent. Strong, but not overpoweringly so, Alamass thought with a smile, just like her.
                                                    Alamass reached down slowly and placed the blossom at the base of the tree. Thick, sticky tears welled in his eyes. The elemental dragon closed his eyes, keeping his tears from flowing.

                                                    “Cherish your existance, little elemental,” Quick-Sword Esmel had told him after saving his life from a monstrous Fae, “the wheel of reincarnation does not turn for your kind.” She had cared for many, protecting life in all forms. Yet the Dawn-caste dove into battle with almost reckless disregard for her own safety, as if she cared little for her own life. No, that wasn’t it, Alamass thought, she counted on others to protect her as she protected them.

                                                    For her, Alamass had sought the secrets of sorcery and of martial arts. The Solar had no armies, no legions at her command; her military consisted entirely of individual heroes, be they Exalted, mortal, or spirit, drawn in by the Solar’s magnetic charisma. Alamass forged himself into a weapon for her, and he became one of her boon companions. He became her aegis, saving her life time and again in repayment of his eternal debt.

                                                    And then he failed.

                                                    A Primordial, who had fled to the Wyld rather than die to Exalted blades or submit with his king, returned to invade Creation. True to her name, Esmel was one of the first to respond, one of the first to launch a counter-offensive against the hordes of war-daevas. On the spot Alamass now sat she had met her foe in battle, and had died. He had planted this tree in her memory, for the Magnolia blossom was her crest.

                                                    It was her favorite.

                                                    Alamass shook his head to clear out the painful memories of loss and regret. This place alway brought back those painful memories for him; while Esmel was not buried here, he always regarded this place as her Tomb. And so, he returned each Calibration to tend to it. The Elemental Dragon placed each of his hands together, and reached deep into the well of power next to him. And he began to pray.

                                                    “My lady Esmel, your successors grow more distant from you with every incarnation,” Alamass prayed aloud, “I sometimes wonder if they even are the inheritors of your Solar glory. Your newest inheritor has bred artificial heroes with sorcery-”

                                                    A loud grunt from one of the warriors cut off the dragon’s prayer. Enraged at the interruption, Alamass spun around, only to see two of the magical warriors on the ground their heads severed from their bodies. A tall figure, in silvery-blue armor, stood over their corpses. It casually flipped around a slender bloody blade of the same material, wiping the blade on a long cloak. The figure was surrounded by a soft violet corona.

                                                    “You will not find my master here, Reckoner,” Alamass said, drawing himself to his full height.

                                                    “Your master is dead, Alamass,” the figure said in a familiar tenor. It reached up and removed it’s helm, revealing the auburn locks and tanned skin of Peerless Ocelot, Chosen of Endings and master of a dozen Styles.

                                                    “Your master fell outside the gates of Hollow alongside Kestrel the Slayer, Radiant Prism, Ma’He-kono the Younger, and Horadrum,” the Reckoner said with steely eyes, “they fell gloriously in battle, as befit their Exalted station.”

                                                    Alamass drooped slightly, confused, “Hollow? But they were suppose to be in Maru-”

                                                    “I seek my Master, Towering Falcon,” Ocelot said, cutting off the Dragon, “He was not in Maru, nor Tarhoun nor any other place I sought to look for him. Is he here?”

                                                    Alamass looked at the Reckoner’s panoply. His armor was a magitech wonder, his cloak crafted from the Wyld Sorcery of Kardalla the Librarian. At least three hearthstones glistened in the Sidereal’s armor, and two more along the length of his daiklave.

                                                    The Sidereal was outfitted for war.

                                                    “Did you kill them?” Alamass whispered.

                                                    “That’s irrelevant-”

                                                    “DID YOU KILL THEM?” Alamass shouted. Masses of vines uncoiled from his body and lashed about in uncontrollable anger.

                                                    “No, I did not. But I set into motion the events that did,” Ocelot said, his voice pleading, “The Solars were not themselves. They have fallen into habits of madness and destruction. Now, do you know where Towering Falcon is? I must finish what I’ve started.”

                                                    Cherish your existance, little elemental. The wheel of reincarnation does not turn for your kind.

                                                    Some things are more important than that,Alamass thought. He lunged at Ocelot, using his coiled lower section like a spring. A tornado of vines whipped at Ocelot, venom dripping from his fangs and talons. He had to incapacitate the Reckoner immediately, lest he slay Alamass and continue on his murderous rampage.

                                                    Ocelot ducked under the whirling vines, blocked a claw strike with his elbow, and performed a quick side-kick that caused Alamass to fly back into the towering magnolia. Alamass’ vision danced with lights on the impact. A moment later, Alamass’ vision cleared, only to see the Reckoner stalking towards him with blade in hand. He could barely move before Ocelot ran him through, pinning him to the tree. Alamass felt his essence drain from him, flowing from his being into the demesne he had tended.

                                                    “I didn’t think so,” Peerless Ocelot shook his head, “I’m sorry, Alamass. You’ve been nothing but a dutiful servant, albeit to a bad master.” With a swift yank he withdrew his blade, but still Alamass felt his essence draining.

                                                    “We’re products of the Solar’s age; an age that is now over,” Ocelot said as he put his helm back on, “It would be better if we disappeared from history along with them.” Ocelot turned from the fallen spirit and began to walk away. “When you wake, tend to your garden,” Ocelot said over his shoulder, “The world will forget about us and be better for it.”

                                                    The Reckoner leaped into the inky black sky and disappeared from sight but a moment before Alamass faded from consciousness.
