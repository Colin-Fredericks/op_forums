                        The Throng

                        The Titan leads Scout towards an electrified fence guarding an industrial workshop, something a little heavy duty for the area, but a strike of good fortune for the two. Empowered in the wake of the Firestorm, the Osirian finds himself a more potent conductor than he had been before. In seconds, his mummified flesh knit together with impossible speed, his humours invigorate with Pyros. Though Mark 5 seeks to punish himself by avoiding healing for now, stray bolts of electricity, no longer needed and no longer contained, arc out towards the nearest conductor, Mark 5. Snaking up his arm, it immediately repairs bruised and battered flesh, and the Titan, implacable, invincible, flinches backwards, avoiding the healing blessing of the Principle.

                        OOC: Because of the Efficient Conductor Merit, Scout heals all three points of Lethal damage immediately, as well as regaining a point of Pyros. Because Mark 5 is the nearest conductor, the excess electricity spreads to him, healing two points of Bashing damage and regaining a point of Pyros.

                        Earth, lost in thought, uncomfortably trying to offer what insight she has to the perplexed and numbed Muse, latches on to a hardline at a nearby serve-yourself car wash. Her potent humours repair her body, invigorated with Pyros, healing swiftly and surely. A stray arc lances out from the battered Earth and lances towards Adonis, starting the process of healing. The Muse is lured towards the siren song of healing and power, latching on to repair himself. He heals, his humours empowered by life-giving lightning, but two Created drawing on the source shorts it out. Before Adonis can draw on it for more power, the electricity fails, and a grumbling employee stumbles out of the convenience store to flip a breaker.

On Adonis' first turn of healing, he heals four points of Bashing damage and gains a point of Pyros.                                         Interludes

                                        Scout

                                        The Osirian makes his way to the Millennium Library, the central branch of the Winnipeg Public Library system. As Scout ascends the stairs, passing lamplit alcoves adjacent to the wall of windows looking out onto the empty reading garden, he keeps a weather eye out for Tom Barebones, a fellow Tammuz that Earth had directed him to. The fourth floor was where the primary historical section was, as was the local Historical Room, where the most records outside the Manitoba Archives were kept. The reading garden was, in extremely late autumn (should be winter), a destitute wreck. The urban marsh was drained, leaving the brown and black rot of algae baking in the sun against cement, chairs were stacked haphazardly by tables covered in a thick layer of dust and salt, and woven wicker balls holding darkened lights swayed in the empty, skeletal branches of the trees.

                                        Wandering the fourth floor, you seem him, Tom, quietly filing books in the deep shelves. He is rail-thin, wearing a tattered canvas coat and several scarves of riotous colours- you think you can see writing inked onto the back of them. His face is pock-marked and scarred, with raised lumps of acne, warts, and other growths. To your Created eyes, past the Azothic Mask, you see salt crystals and lumps of clay crawling along his skin, breaking up his outline. In fact, it looks like he might be more clay than flesh where you can see the yellowed ivory of bone poking through. When the coat sleeve on one arm rides up, you think you can see a block of text tattooed there. You approach, and the Named seems startled, eyes darting for a place to run to, until your Disfigurements become clear, and then he visibly relaxes. You mention what you're looking for, and why, and that Earth sent you, and Tom just looks at you, as though attempting to memorize something, at which point he pulls out a well-worn and well-cared for sketchbook from a massive pocket he has sewn into his jacket. With an sketch artist's precision, he drafts a portrait onto a page, and then very carefully writes down a sentence before he speaks it.

                                        "Jussst follow me, pleassse. Religion and Esssotericisssm are right over here." He rolls the words around in his mouth like a stone, drawing out certain sounds with a far-away look on his face, as though trying to reach a memory he cannot quite recall. A Word that had been spoken to him once, in the Dreamtime before life, deep in subconscious Memory.

                                        For the next several hours you work side by side, Tom pulling down books, handing them off, and then disappearing for a while, though behind a bookcase you can hear the scratch of pencil on paper, a quiet whisper, the flutter of cloth, the click of a tape recorder, the static of a well-worn recording, and the breathy sounds of someone trying to follow along word for word without being heard. It becomes relatively clear that Tom is not an Originist, but with his subtle guidance your research goes well.

                                        You find that rebis is, etymologically, from the Latin "res bina" meaning dual or double matter, and that it saw the most use amongst a group of European mystics called the Rosicrucians, a fraternal lodge that first pops up in the late 16th and early 17th centuries with the publication of several texts proclaiming the existence of a hitherto unknown esoteric order that is "built on esoteric truths of the ancient past". It had a seminal influence on emerging Freemasonry, and the philosophy merged and flowed with the Masons, the Golden Dawn, and the Theosophists as the years wore on. There was scepticism regarding the claims of the Rosicrucians, but that didn't stop people from joining... or from alchemy becoming the latest fad. Alchemy is built off of three axioms "As Above, So Below", "As Within, So Without", and "Know Thyself". The rebis was a symbol of the Chymical Wedding (one of the core parables of the Rosicrucians), the ultimate merging of Soul and Body into an idealized, perfected being. In the 1750s, a Rosicrucian and Masonic secret society called the Order of the Golden and Rosy Cross rose to prominence among proponents of alchemical philosophy. It espoused alchemy-as-spiritualism, touting the mystical science’s physical processes as a metaphor for the enlightenment of the human soul, and imagined that one day it would accomplish the ultimate “Chymical Wedding” — the unification of the mundane with the divine. Rosicrucianism began among doctors who viewed themselves as mystical philosophers on top of their earthly mandate to heal the body, and the trend continued; many of the Order were physicians as well as spiritualists. Like its predecessor, what the Golden Thorn wants more than anything is to achieve the Chymical Wedding. Unlike its predecessor, it views this ultimate enlightenment as a literal process. Its members believed that only by merging, body and soul, with other human beings can one achieve spiritual perfection. Each strived to find a person with whom he can create the most ideal amalgam, and then to mix an alchemical formula that they can both imbibe to complete the human compound. Not all Chymical Weddings are presumed to have only two components, and some in the brotherhood have spent lifetimes trying to find exactly the right set of people to make the compound work. No successful Chymical Wedding had been confirmed, but rumours circulated of those who have managed it.

It is scrawled with equations and observations and stream of consciousness thought processes connecting books, experience, and philosophy. Scout can see, from where the sketchbook is sitting on the nearby table, cover flipped up, that the page that started with an introductory sentence and a portrait of his face has turned into a snapshot of his time at the library. Further sketches of him pouring over texts, thoughts, hopes, fears, phrases carefully constructed and others scratched out with frustration and shame.

                                            Interludes

                                            Mark 5

                                            Rumour has it that the Breaker is going around the North End again. He's an urban legend, a boogeyman, someone the police can't (or perhaps won't) track down, and the story keeps on growing. They say he's not a man, they say he's not even human, they say if you burn a picture of someone you hate and chant "Breaker, Breaker, Breaker" into a walkie-talkie that person will end up dead. That one's gained some traction, since the source of many a young person's ire is the gangbanger on the corner just a few years older than them, and these days they're turning up dead. They say he can't be killed and that his fists and feet are made of steel. Some people are saying he's an experimental super-soldier from the 'deserted' Kapyong Barracks, but everyone really knows that weird shit goes on in those empty old buildings over on Kennaston Boulevard.

                                            Now, far more fearsome, are the stories of the one that calls themselves Darkstorm. No one even thinks that they're human, but exactly what Darkstorm is, no one is exactly sure. Some people, who have relatives out in British Columbia, say that maybe Darkstorm is a thunderbird who has lost their mask and their feather cloak and is stuck in human form, or is someone with thunderbird blood, but either way Darkstorm is just as wrathful as they are. They say that Darkstorm's voice rolls like thunder, and when they blink that lights flicker. Someone was standing on the Desraeli Bridge one day when someone down below in the trainyards let out a wrathful scream and the doors of nearby train cars and buildings were ripped towards them as if they were a massive electromagnet. They're sure it was Darkstorm, and they believe. After all, aren't thunderbirds supposed to punish the wicked? Hasn't Darkstorm just gone after the local gangs, driving them from the relative safety of the train yards to places where they can be caught?

                                            A local Indian Posse warehouse was found with a message tagged on its door, scorched into the metal itself by a massive current of some kind. Their Hell's Angels patrons are none too pleased with how the gang has been kicked around so badly by two people (there are just two of them, and they are really people, right?) over the last couple of months. They need to fix this, now. When one of the local leaders, a 'Chief', shows up in the trashed parking lot of an industrial park, he has backup. Warriors, their arms tattooed with arrows, guns tucked into waistbands, red bandanas tied across heads, over faces, around legs, leather jackets and leather gloves and steel-toed boots for the intimidation factor. They end up squaring off with just two people. One of them is hanging back under the shadows of the building in a long rain coat, arms folded, one arm thickly corded with muscle, a hat saying MAKE AMERICA NATIVE AGAIN pulled down tightly over their head. Behind them is some kind of weird-ass graffiti, but they can see the stylized tag of Darkstorm in there, and that gets a few of the boys nervously reaching for their guns. The other is on the roof and leaps down to the pavement, rolling forwards effortlessly like it was nothing and looming up in the Chief's face. He's wearing black pants, a black shirt, both of which are stained with... they aren't sure what, and he has a black ski mask on his face, with duct tape around the eyes and mouth. What the fuck is this? A fucking superhero mask?

                                            The Chief laughs in the Titan's face, a face scarred by acne (and muzzle burns, in a few spots) impassive in the face of the flashy parkour shit. You're close enough to see the shield tattooed on his neck and the dull outline of IP beside it, the arrows and knives fading away on his forearms, the spots of shockingly pink skin where frostbite and burns did their dreadful work. This guy has lived hard, and he's not impressed. When he pulls his gun and puts it to the Titan's head, he isn't ready for this crazy bastard to grab him by the throat and just lift, ever so slightly. Now he's dancing on the tips of his toes, choking on his own body weight, and seems to have forgotten he's holding a gun. Would the gun even help? Darkstorm takes a step forwards out of the shadows and makes a show of clenching a fist, sparks flying from fingertips as a haze and the smell of ozone gathers around them. The backup scatters. The Breaker informs him, very clearly, that the Indian Posse is not to do business in the North End anymore, and if they stay here, well, then they're going to have a problem. Because next time, he's not going to go after the Strikers he sees roughing people up or dealing, it's going to be a war. "I'm going to give you a week. Clear out." He lets the Chief go, and the man falls back towards the ground, gasping for breath, and IP scatters.

                                            Over the next week, warehouses empty, safe houses are abandoned, and people either ditch patched jackets and red bandanas or decide to leave town.
