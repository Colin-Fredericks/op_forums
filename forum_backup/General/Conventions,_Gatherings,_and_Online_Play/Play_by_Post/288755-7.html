                                                An intolerance of cheating men/irresponsible fathers is fine. Val's got an intolerance for 'absolutes'. The important thing is you make it part of your character and are ready to role-play it!

As he's written now, I don't think I can make any suggestions without changing his personality.

                                                I do think Geoff could benefit from a clear driving goal or motivation. Or, alternatively, to use Werewolf speak, an Urge*. An Urge is a negative emotional attachment that a character needs to work through. For Hannah, it's coming to terms with falling to the thrall of the Wyrm. For Echoes its confronting and acknowledging the part of herself that revels in others fear. For Jon it's his hatred and resentment of his father. If you don't want an Urge, a personal goal or motivation is also good. Val's goal is to discover Truth in his new existence (and not give into Fenrir ideals). Jame's motivation is to embody his mentor's teachings and become a 'bridge between the people'...at the cost of his own desires. So what's Geoff's personal goal? Does he have an Urge? What's the deal with his mysterious cleithrophobia? Are there any spiritual entities interested in his development?

                                                Perhaps ask Geoff these questions:

                                                1: What does he want?

                                                2: How does he achieve it?

                                                3: What's stopping him from achieving it?

                                                4: And what happens when he gets what he wants?


                                                As he is though, I'm a fan of Geoff. The pack sorely needs someone to play the role of peacekeeper. But sharpening up Geoff's motivations might be a good place to start if you want to develop his character more.

                                                        Bashir

                                                        All right, let me take a read through of your character sheet with the modifications.

                                                        Fatalist and Competitor are a good nature/demeanor combination for this character.

                                                        You should really pick specialties for your attributes over 4 (technically it's optional, but if you don't choose one, you won't get the associated bonus). Manipulation is great; however, I worry about the distribution of mental abilities. Given Jon's skill at debating and fast-talking, it seems like you've skimped on Wits. The ability to think and react quickly is essential for those abilities, after all. It would be better if you took a point out of Intelligence and put it into Wits (unless you wanted to respend some other things to free up freebie points; either way works).

                                                        You should consider putting a point into Streetwise. Given Jon's background, especially his job, a very low level of Streetwise would certainly be justified and could prove useful. Just a suggestion, though.

                                                        You'll need to choose specialties for Academics and Expression. These aren't optional. Remember that custom specialties are fine, pending my approval.

                                                        Robert's Rules of Order (and yes, Josh, many debate teams employ parliamentary procedure, though I'm more familiar with it from my time in Student Government) is not really an appropriate specialty for Law. Not only is it far too narrow, but I wouldn't even classify parliamentary procedure under Law; I'd consider it a function of the Etiquette skill (and I would consider Parliamentary Procedure to be an appropriate Etiquette specialty). Also, given Jon's background, two dots in Law is pretty high. It suggests a level of formal training that isn't implied by his background. Unless it was your intention for his focus to be on Garou law, with his training having been received at the Sept of the Thundering Pines?

                                                        You spent six points on Backgrounds; I'm assuming this is where the freebie point from Dark Secret went?

                                                        Good choices on the gifts; they're exactly the choices I would have made for Jon.

                                                        The Rite of Cleansing is not a bad choice; a very useful rite that many Garou choose to learn, though not usually a focus for Galliards. The Rite of Heritage is a little more... problematic. Remember, rites have to have been learned from another Garou at some point. One would think that a demonstration would be a part of that, and then... bam, there goes his dark secret. You might want to rethink that one. A suggestion- maybe drop both of those and take the Rite of Praise, which is both useful and very appropriate for a Galliard? There are also two level 2 rites from the Shadow Lord revised Tribebook that would be both useful and appropriate for Jon to have been taught- the Murmur Rite (which protects the participants from all forms of supernatural eavesdropping) and Communion with the Storm (which, if enacted during a thunderstorm, allows the ritemaster to commune with Grandfather Thunder). I won't require you to change your choices, but know that if you stick with the Rite of Heritage, whichever Garou (probably Quiet Storm, the Alpha and Ritemaster of the Sept of the Thundering Pines) taught him the rite will be, at the very least, suspicious of Jon's heritage.

                                                        Although, I can see why Jon would want to know the Rite of Heritage. After what happened with his Dad, the desire to be able to verify the... pedigree... of any potential mating partners would certainly be important to him.

                                                        I'm cool with Gaye but, if she knew Jon when they were in school together, we need a reason for her to be in South Jersey now (as opposed to up near Trenton, where Jon grew up). I suggest that she be a Computer Science major at a university down here, like Rowan or Rutgers Camden.

                                                        We'll need to pick a garou astrological sign for Jon. I see that you do not have access to Rage Across the Heavens. Check out this link for details on most of them (unfortunately, it doesn't include Hakahe; I'll have to get you some information on him when I can). The information on each of the Planetary Incarnae from an astrological perspective is on the bottom of each page. Take a look and see if any jump out at you. I suggest special attention to Ruatma and Mitanu.

                                                        I need clarification on something, since the backstory is a little unclear on the issue- is Jon aware of his mother's heritage? If so, was it his Dad who told him? If it wasn;t his Dad, how did he find out?

                                                        An intolerance of cheating men/irresponsible fathers is fine.
                                                        Good thing we have you here to approve these things for me, Josh. I appreciate your help, and I'm glad that we're all working together, but please remember that I need to make the final decisions on questions like this.

                                                        And yes, Bashir, either of those would be fine for the Intolerance flaw. Just make sure you are prepared to roleplay it (as Josh said). I'm also OK with Mitanu's Tongue.

                                                        Flo

                                                        I think Geoff is great as he is. He has an interesting backstory, a playable personality and sufficient connections for me to thread interesting things from his life into the story.

                                                        As for Josh's suggestion of an "urge" or driving motivation, I'm inclined to disagree. It's fine for some characters to have such a clearly defined motivation at the start of play, but it's hardly essential. Most people go through life without such a particular and singular focus. You have the character down, and there's nothing in his history that necessarily defines a major focus of his activities like that. Such a thing should not be forced. It is, however, something that he can come to have during the course of play. If you want the character to have an "urge," rather than forcing it, why not let it happen naturally during the course of play, once we start?
