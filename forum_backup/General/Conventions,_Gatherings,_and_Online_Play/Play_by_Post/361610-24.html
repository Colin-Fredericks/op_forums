                                    Splendor had not always enjoyed getting new clothing. In fact, Agralzo had told some of the most embarrassing stories about that early dislike. Then the great god had been referred to Mistress Ariadne. At first the, still very young, mortal had been afraid of the many armed goddess. Then she had made him a new outfit. Watching her spin the silken garments about him had amazed the boy. She had explained the meaning of every knot and fold, until Splendor had felt like he was wearing a story like his father would tell him before bed. He had never minded going to the tailor then. In fact, there had been more than a few times he had let his clothing get damaged just to have an excuse to visit Ariadne again and hear the story of the new cloths he was getting. Given what he later found out the weaver goddess charged, it was lucky for him that Agralzo was not a poor god.

                                    Smiling at the memory of happier times the Solar spoke to his companion.

                                    "I hope this will not inconvenience you overmuch. After you have made the introductions I am sure you will have some time for your own affairs while my new garments are prepared. Do you have any particular suggestions? I believe the feast of the Maiden Who Once There Was is nearly upon us, though festival clothing might be outside our price range."

                                    That should have been a safe way to show that he was not entirely as disconnected as he seamed. The Feast was a relatively quiet affair in Heaven, where aspirants sought sifus, students demonstrated their accomplish on the lotus path, and onlookers meditated on the lessons on display. Well, that was how Agralzo and he had spent it. There was always someone throwing a party somewhere, but that was just Yu Shan. Surly creation wasn't that different. The clothing had been equally subdued, which would give Flynn the chance to offer his opinion, one of the best ways Splendor knew to make a good impression on someone.

                                            Splendor was quiet for a time as they walked, as though he didn't mean to answer. At last he opened with. "The Wyld, grieving under an alien sky as my father's friends debated weather it would be wiser to abandon me. But that isn't what you were really asking."

                                            "I don't actually know the city's proper name in Fire or Forrest tongue. It translates to 'Jade Mountain'. My father was a bureaucrat, who hated his job and loved his work if that makes sense. He did most of the paperwork in his workshop, which used to drive his assistants mad with the informality of it. They never said anything though, he was far too important. Well, never directly. I think they made a game of how many ways they could disapprove without crossing the line into rudeness. We lived near the center of the city, near his office which he did visit from time to time. My peers all called it 'the city' as though everything past the city center was some sort of outland.

                                            I wasn't my father's natural son. I was nine before I even knew why that might matter. We spent more time at father's manse than anywhere else, but we often went to visit his friends, when they weren't calling on him. Inventors, sorcerers, artisans, authors, it seemed like he knew everything in Creation who made new things. When I picked up on that, is when I started trying to make things. I knew he was important, but when I started to learn how just how important, and how illustrious his guests and friends were, it made me feel like I didn't deserve his time. Don't mistake me, he never even implied it. No matter what he was doing he always made time for me.

                                            I think it made him a little sad when I went away because of that. Maybe he felt that he hadn't done enough to encourage or praise me. He did, but...

                                            Last calibration was the first time I had seen him in five years. Nominally it was to present him with the first work of high artifice I had made. Which was tradition of course, but it is amazing how much less exuberant you feel when you aren't just leaving something in the temple like the other apprentices. We met at my 'aunt's party. She wasn't actually my aunt you understand, just a good friend of my father who I always assumed was more than a friend.

                                            That's why I am alive by the way. The party was in an orchard in the southern parks. It meant I was near enough to the outer wall to make it through a gateway. From there it was south-east to the Wyld and hopefully an escape from persuit, then north-west to the grey river and eventually Swimming Owl."
