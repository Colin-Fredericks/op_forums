There are a lot of foes of the Garou who have a fondness for making their lairs underground, which will usually mean tight spaces. It's not as debilitating as Cynophobia or Nyctophobia would be, but it's pretty close. And remember, if you buy it as a flaw, it's not just a discomfort in small places that can be expressed through role-play or overcome without too much difficulty in an emergency. If you still want Geoff to be claustrophobic, that could be expressed with a flaw like Compulsion (Avoid Enclosed Spaces) or Intolerance (Being In Enclosed Spaces). The Compulsion flaw can be overcome by spending a WP, and the Intolerance flaw adds a difficulty penalty, but will not in and of itself force the character to flee. However, if you fail the willpower roll associated with the Phobia flaw (and remember, you have to get three successes on the roll, not just one), Geoff must flee unless flight is physically impossible or someone has a gift or something that can help him overcome his fear.             Ward. I can see a lot of discussion went into this one. The problem with the Ward flaw is that it has a tendency to define the relationship. Any family member or friend of a character (especially teenagers) has the possibility of getting into trouble and needing rescue, but the Ward flaw guarantees it, and guarantees that it'll happen more than once. It also limits the character growth of the NPC in question- unless you choose to buy off the flaw with experience later (or transfer it to another suitable NPC, if one arises in the story), the Ward NPC will always be needing rescue, and I can't develop them into a character that is capable of caring for themselves. So, I urge you to give this some serious thought.

You don't really need the extra freebie points (I think you haven't spent them all yet, and I just pointed out above that you can get two of them back from the Natural Science ability).                                                 Unless it's in some random book, which it very well may be, the subject of homosexuality and W:tA isn't something nearly as stressed as it is in W:tF. W:tF puts it right in the core book up front, and makes sure it's a theme in the slapts, W:tA not so much. I know there's a few gay Garou couples scattered in some of the fluff (most notable Tribe Book CoG), but I don't even remember how that's handled, I just know it's there. That being said, I think it's left pretty openly vague for the ST to fill in, and it's probably one of those things that's meant to vary based on who you ask. I say that because it's not technically a violation of the litany; let's break it down.

                                                Garou shall not mate with Garou

                                                Mate is in this case a verb, the thing that is being done. Let's use some dictionary powers.

                                                verb verb: mate; 3rd person present: mates; past tense: mated; past participle: mated; gerund or present participle: mating
                                                • 1.
                                                  (of animals or birds) come together for breeding; copulate.
                                                  "successful males may mate with many females"
                                                  synonyms: breed, couple, copulate "pandas rarely mate in captivity"
                                                  • bring (animals or birds) together for breeding.
                                                  • join in marriage or sexual partnership.
                                                    "people tend to mate with others in their own social class"
                                                • 2.
                                                  connect or be connected mechanically.
                                                  "a four-cylinder engine mated to a five-speed gearbox"

                                                The emphasis is mainly on the breeding part. Sure, it's kind of evolved into a way to say married or be partnered with, but that's kind of an afterthought when it comes to the words etiology. It's a derivative of what it means to mate, rather than the meaning itself. Especially when we consider just how damn old the Litany is.

                                                Not compelling enough, let's look at the justification for why Garou shall not mate with Garou: The Law: Werewolves should mate only with humans or wolves. Because metis offspring are deformed, twisted or even insane, Garou are forbidden to mate with their own kind. Of course, this law is enforced largely because of age-old prejudices against metis. This stricture forms the basis for some of the greatest tragedies of Garou culture. Galliards have been known to move listeners to tears by telling ballads of two werewolves who fall in love and can never express their passion… or who do so at the cost of their lives.
                                                The Reality: The number of metis in Garou culture is increasing steadily, showing that this law is not as inviolate as it once was. A handful of tribes claim to actually be kinder to their metis than others. Unfortunately, these claims are often little more than tactics used to recruit and exploit metis for tasks lupus and homids would just as soon avoid.

                                                The problem here is squarely about the Metis. It's the only thing outright mentioned as a justification of a violation of the Litany.

                                                So, I can pretty plainly see what the Litany is trying to say is this.

                                                Garou shall not produce offspring with other Garou.

                                                So where does that leave us. If two Garou get it on, is it a violation of the Litany if there can be no offspring? Well only if you decide that Garou also have a duty to produce healthy offspring, which can only be done with kinfolk really. Such a thing might be seen as a waste of time and effort to the extreme, just as much as mating with a infertile kinfolk. But if that's the argument it's not about homosexuality anymore, and it's still skirting the spirit of the law when it comes to the Litany.

                                                So the only way it's a violation is by the letter of the law, which is probably a stance each Garou has to have individually, as each tribe, breed, auspice, and individual interprets it slightly differently anyway.
                                                So the answer is, there are as many answers as there are Garou, and that all Garou regardless of being gay should do two things, not make Metis, and bare the burden of breeding healthy offspring.
