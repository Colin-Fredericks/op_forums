                                    Wahine looked out her spy glass at the magnificent ship out at sea, studying and admiring it as only a master craftsman could. Her thoughts ran as the maiden spoke behind her. Though her back was turned, she listened intently, making shrewd mental calculations. No doubt a ship like that had a maintenance list a mile long. She should get back to the village and fire up her master's workshop. The semi-retired old bat would definitely want to get a jump on milling some fresh timber. The thought of trade excited her and she knew her carpentry skills alone gave her tremendous bargaining power. Her eyes glittered at the thought of what might be inside that massive hull. She made a mental note to introduce herself to the captain when she had a moment. Cozying up to the captain always had its advantages. But there would be time during the next few weeks as the crew restocked supplies, bartered their wares, and indulged in a little rest from the sea. At the moment, there was work to do and she wanted to get back her village.

                                        Calm Sea Village, RY 767, 23rd of Ascending Fire, Half an Hour After Sunrise:

                                        As Wahine headed towards the river in the hopes of catching a boat, her parrot abruptly fluffed its feathers and doubled in apparent size, squawked wordlessly, and took to the air. As she turned to watch its path, she could see inland through the break in the trees made by the winding of the river. In the inland forests, the trees were beginning to sway as though in a heavy wind...

                                        The Maiden was adorable and friendly, and the crowd was warming to her quickly. They were nodding and the locals were warming to the idea of letting the strangers dock their ship and rest...until the Maiden fell over again. This time she did not get up, and lay facedown in the sand twitching. Her clumsy friend from the boat rushed to her side and turned her over, and the people of Calm Sea Village started back in fear, for her eyes were wide and white and she muttered in a strange language.

                                        Haku was angrily demanding to know what was happening from the foreigners who couldn't understand his language, who were bristling defensively around the girl, when suddenly the beach bucked underneath them like a rolling wave. Seskan was caught off guard and lost his footing as the sand under him suddenly began pouring into a chasm, and he was almost drawn into it before a blast of hot wind from under the earth bodily lifted him and his damaged glider out of harm's way. He slammed into something heavy, and everything went fuzzy. Wahine had spent time at sea, and she had a few seconds of warning, so she kept her feet long enough to see the huts and villages of Calm Sea Village collapsing, trees being uprooted, cracks opening in the earth and black smoke pouring forth, to feel a wind like the air coming off a furnace blowing from somewhere inland and to hear an all-encompassing roar like that of a hurricane before a falling tree pinned her in place and broke several of her ribs.

                                        The watchtowers guarding the coastline from monsters of the southern sea swayed and fell. The Guild ship out at sea swayed amidst the outsized waves.

                                        Wahine was the only one who remained conscious long enough to see the sky go dark as the moon rose to blot out the sun.

                                        Pakari was abruptly awoken by the sound of a woman he did not know screaming in absolute terror. It was the sort of sound that rouses most people, no matter how much they and their aching bodies would like to keep sleeping. It roused Seskan too (who almost fell off of a couch he had been laid upon), and woke Wahine with a sudden start that had her immediately wincing at the pain in her broken ribs.

                                        The hand that had been reaching for the Maiden to wipe he brow pulled back in distress, and a gentle voice spoke. "It's alright, child. It's alright. It was a dream, and your dreams are not the ones that hold power here."

                                        Scattered moans and groans drew attention to the other wounded who had been gathered in this...where was this? It appeared to be a stone grotto, or a cave with a roof open to the sky. Flowering vines hung down into the cave, and the color of the sky meant the sun was either rising or setting...they were surrounded by men and women who were wearing bandages or splints. Pakari recognized a bundle with a child's mummified hand sticking out of it propped in a corner: the bones were here, but he did not see their Bearer. Seskan, sitting up, found that he was wearing a splint on his leg and it hurt to put any weight on it. The Maiden, coming slowly out of her nightmare, recognized Wahine on a wood-and-rushes cot. Her adorable rabbit-eared companion was nowhere to be found.

                                        Several warriors with intricate geometric tattoos in white, black, and red stood silently around the periphery of the chamber, their eyes darting from person to person.                                                         At first, there was just a dull ache as consciousness floated at the edge of wakefulness. Then, as a blood curdling sound of terror jolted her upright, there was only excruciating pain. She yelped silently, hand going to her side reflexively, breathing sharp and shallow through tightly clenched teeth, eyes tearing as the sensation passed . There was no mistaking the pain in her side for anything other than broken ribs. How many she was not sure. She'd always heard breaking a rib was one of the most painful experiences a person could endure, mostly because there wasn't a damn thing anyone could do about it and every jostle, every breath was a painful reminder of injury. She was having a hard time disagreeing. Painful, yes, life threatening... doubtful, as long as she took it easy and didn't get bumped around too much as her bones began stitching themselves back together. She wasn't a doctor but she assumed if she could still breath normally, albeit not without some pain if she inhaled too deeply, then it must have been a clean break. She pawed clumsily for her staff to lean on.

                                                        Settled upright, the pain receded to a tolerable ache. She looked around, utterly bewildered. She recognized some of the others but the strange tattooed individuals where a complete mystery. She'd heard of tattooed warriors that lived on the far side but why would they be here? Her gaze was suddenly drawn to the unusual man in the room, eyes becoming fixated on the golden stranger whose pleasant honeyed words filled her head with the scent of delightful sweetness. For a fleeting second, she felt soothed by his presence, that she could loose herself in the gentle warmth of that honey scented sound. But something deep in the back of her mind, some part of her momentarily forgotten, was deeply unsettled. When he flashed his fingers at a nearby man she'd never seen, she jumped painfully to her to her feet. Wincing through gritted teeth as she leaned on her staff there was a flicker of fear in her eyes as she glared distrustfully. Her hand clutched the iron pendent in her necklace as she breathed in shallow breaths and waited for the pain of another sudden jostle to pass.

                                                        What are you? She mouthed silently through narrowed, apprehensive eyes. She hoped he was a god or spirit that meant no harm but she would not let her guard down on a hope.
