                                                Val rests upon his haunches and stares into the sky. Taking a deep breath, he begins to speak in a soft, quiet voice.

                                                “Once upon a time, in distant and significantly warmer India, there lived a cynical scholar. He was discussing the Goddess Saraswati with his friend, a Brahmin.”

                                                ‘How is it possible the Goddess can know all?’ the scholar said.

                                                ‘She is a goddess,’ replied the Brahmin. ‘Nothing is hidden from her sight.’

                                                ‘Yet how can you be sure?’

                                                The Brahmin sighed heavily. ‘Come to my temple, I shall call on the Goddess to answer any question you desire.’

                                                ‘So be it.’

                                                “Three days later, the scholar journeyed to the temple, carrying a simple black box. The Brahmin asked what it contained, but the scholar simply smiled. Shrugging, the Brahmin began his ritual, beseeching Saraswati to turn her gaze upon him.

                                                “A rush of light, of colour. Before the men rose a radiant being, all flame and white stars.”

                                                'And why do you call upon me, pundit?' Intoned the Goddess, voice shaking the heavens.

                                                The Brahmin fell to his knees, trembling. 'Font of Wisdom, this man doubts your power, he cannot believe you are all-knowing.'

                                                'Indeed?' The Goddess turned her fire upon the scholar. 'Ask, and I shall answer.'

                                                Unafraid, the scholar raised his box. 'Oh Goddess, tell me what this vessel contains, and I shall bow to you.'

                                                Saraswati laughed. 'A trick. The box is empty.'

                                                Now the scholar laughed. Removing the lid, he raised the container to the Goddess’s face…

                                                And her reflection stared back at her.

                                                For the scholar had affixed a mirror to the box’s bottom.

                                                'No, sweet lady,' said the scholar. 'This box is not empty, for it contains everything.'

                                                “Too often do those who believe they know all refuse to look beneath the surface, it is easier to accept the expected, than to peer past the veil, into yourself.”

                                                Val tears his gaze away from the sky, looks at ‘Bridge, eyes lidded.

                                                “You see only the obvious. It is right or wrong, black or white, yes or no. Can I not love and hate you? Do you see only an empty box, or your own reflection? I did not choose to join you, but I can do nothing else. If you think I’m empty, so be it, but whatever you believe, would you kindly get out of my way?”
