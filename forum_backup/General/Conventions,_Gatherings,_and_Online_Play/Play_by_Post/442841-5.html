                                        Originally posted by Barbrarilen View Post
                                        Sorry Okami, your descriptions are just a little erratic.                                         I see how I could have come across that way, and I apologize. I should clarify how I've been going about the discussion.

                                        Ok, So back when I first posted about the spirit and the new location it was more in response to your post on page three that mentioned we should discuss the kinds of spirits we could consider as a totem and what we want out of them. Then the discussion of Indy's church and its spirit came along and while I thought it was cool I also saw it as the first proposed option in a discussion about possible spirits.

                                        As I thought about what kinds of spirits we could go for in a totem I thought having something a little bit nature-y would be cool too, as I like the notion of werewolves being kind of pulled towards nature when they come across it (even in a city) because its natural to them. This led me to peruse through Dusk's posting of the sections of the city and how the territory was carved up and I noticed that as a coastal city the waterfront hadn't been taken up. I thought this would be a cool way to combine the urban and the natural. That led me to wondering about 'ok, if we change settings from in the city with this old church to the waterfront, what might that do to what spirits we could find around?'

                                        My head was already liking the idea of this history you guys really liked with the church, the idea of it being old right? So then I got thinking about cities on the coast a hundred years or so ago, and remembered this documentary I has seen about fishermen who turned to bootlegging alcohol. They used the local sea caves and even made tunnels connecting each other's homes (as all the homes were wall to wall where they lived. Pretty much everybody knew about it from what I could recollect, but even if they didn't my head was aware of how back then sometimes whole towns would be in on it). I began to wonder what kind of spirit might grow out of that, so that's where all of that came from.

                                        Originally posted by Barbrarilen View Post
                                        The caves might be an area full of emotional resonance because of the history (old spot), tradition of secrets (prohibition bootlegging), familiarity (known only by 'the locals')... but a place that feeds off privacy is eventually going to be forgotten, isn't it? I don't know if it would be very strong if people don't know about it anymore. That would make it pretty dangerous, if it is a once-powerful area that's now pretty starved for essence because its nearly forgotten?
                                        That's why I put in the notion of a church being built over it, the spirit got by through eating the essence the new place generated that was the same as the old, then the new place too ran its course. As I wrote all this down I realized that, as I've studied urban environments in college, its amazing how buildings and neighborhoods we just assume have always been around can radically change from decade to decade and what we think is 'traditional' or 'old' really isnt at all, things are built over, taken apart, and forgotten. That made me suddenly remember an article I read once about how during renovations in a restaurant in a city they found an entire speakeasy, complete with furniture and bottles, just behind an old wall. The place had just been covered up and forgotten. So I never really saw the resonance of much of the essence being truly 'secret'. It was more 'open secret' or what we often simply call 'community'.

                                        We could do that, I'm good with what the pack decides. I apologize if I came across as 'We need to do this instead!' Which I realize I likely did.
