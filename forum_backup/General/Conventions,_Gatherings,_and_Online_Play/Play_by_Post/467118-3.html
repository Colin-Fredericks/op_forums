    Indiana looked back at her pack, unsure whether she should go first. "When is fare not fair?" She repeated to herself under her breath. The others seemed to still be thinking. The answer was clear as day to her, but it was almost too simple - leading her to believe she may be misunderstanding somehow. Most of the others hadn't been in Victory for too long, and Violet rode her bike everywhere, so maybe she wouldn't have heard about it. Which left the responsibility to Indiana.

    "I'd say the cost of bus fare has been way too high since the hike last June." She said, confidently. The price increase had been an unpopular move among many of the working class. "I heard they were trying to 'go green' or something, but all they're doing is making it so that people have to walk everywhere."

    She smiled at the bartender and waited for a response.

            When is fear not fear?

            The words of the other Ithaeur mulled around in Joseph's mind. It felt like a riddle, but the possible answer? A play on words perhaps? Again he was grateful for memories of his old mentor. Speaking as little as possible while implying much was something she had turned into an art and Joseph had to quickly get used to such phrases. This was difficult, was the question she was posing to him created by her or was it told to the pack? The speaker- his thoughts stopped as realization dawned on him. A smile curved his mouth before he realized it as he began to answer.

            "Whomever told you that was warning you, not asking a question. Trying to communicate something about this thing that attacked your pack." His mind traveled back into one of his first trips into the Shadow. As a wolf he had expected a hunt, blood and fangs, instead his mentor had contracted a rabbit spirit to ask him a question he had to answer then lead him on a chase through its burrow. A true labyrinth that had taught him in a way no words could what it meant to solve a problem. What he needed was more knowledge. Already his mind was traveling through a burrow as it pursued an answer to this creature.

                An answer came to Aliza's mind, as well, but she let it rest on her tongue while she listened to her packmates. Even if she was to be a voice for them to listen to, she certainly couldn't speak for them. Nor would she want to, when they gave such wonderfully interesting answers! What would prompt Joseph to presume the old Ithaeur had been told that by someone? Crescents dealt in mysteries and whispers as a rule, perhaps he had a reason to have developed such a question himself? What would prompt Indiana to respond to such an oddly sage challenge with non-sequitor and wordplay? Was lateral thinking the answer to a pack disrupted by grief?

                Perhaps she was too literal. To Aliza, fear was not fear when it was wisdom. When, in hindsight, everyone agreed that you were right to be afraid. Fear was not fear only when there was something worth being frightened of. Then it was... prudence. Discretion. Bah. No one sung sagas about cautious heroes. It was not as satisfying an answer, so she waited to see if her peers' responses earned them a hint, or at least another insight into how they themselves approached such a curiosity.

                                In the end it always amazed Joseph how much no words could say. The step toward Aliza, the tensed muscles in the hands, the eyes watching her body for any sign of movement. The wolf in him saw these things and felt the challenge before the words ever left his mouth, their noise adding insult to the physical accusation. His own response was nearly immediate. He didn't want to cause a fight, but this was a test, if they wanted to work with the Behemoth's they had to pass. Backing down would have been a failure. Aliza had been standing next to him, so pivoting, taking two steps forward and shifting to the side, in-between her and the man happened quickly enough. It was odd, in these moments he always felt something. When he needed to put weight behind his words it was always as if a coldness was coming off of him. He didn't think that was actually true, but nonetheless the cold never failed to hover over his skin like a winter breeze.

                                "We're here to make sure that doesn't happen, and have already answered your Ithaeur, perhaps you should let your pack do their jobs."

                                The message was two-fold. Not only had he challenged that the man's desire for a fight was actively working against his pack, Joseph's body had spoken for itself. His eye contact that was fixed but still kept the man's whole body in view, the closing of distance, and the relaxing of his shoulders to react quickly all said simply;

                                'back down, now.'

                                        Reading the situation, Indiana moved instinctively behind the man, silently entering his blind spot. She stands, taut, eyes peeled on Aliza, Joseph and the challenger for any sign that she will be needed.

                                        She was no longer listening to the conversation, she hadn't been for some time. Instead she was focused on the tension in the room, the body language of its occupants and the subtle shifts in the flow of the exchange. They were in control, she could tell. The man was a powder keg, but Aliza's rage was a controlled burn and whatever Joseph said had an enormous impact on the room, vibrating powerfully from him like a shockwave. Still Indiana remained coiled like a snake. Waiting.

                                        She felt the animal inside her rising to the surface. She wouldn't admit this if asked, but she wants it to break bad. She wanted to put him down. He had insulted her pack. Disrespected Aliza. Not only that, but he was fool. He was all bluster and bravado. Indiana stoked the rage within her, knowing that she may need it. She stared at the garish tattoos running down his arms, wondering what would happen when she tears the arms off his body. Would they grow back without the ink?

                                        Yet her face remained cold.

                                                            Indiana takes a deep breath. As always, she is struggling to restrain the urge to shift - but she can read the cues from her pack. Nobody wants this to go south. The rest of Behemoth don't either. This wasn't a pack standoff, this was just an asshole who didn't have the brains to handle the situation. If no one had brought claws out yet, then this all could still be saved.

                                                            She knows she is in a perfect position to take him out. He's so wrapped up in his stare down, she's almost certain he doesn't even know she's there. But instead she shoves her twitching fingers into the pockets of her hoodie. She won't be the first to draw blood. Not if she doesn't have to.

                                                            "Violet is right. Why don't you cut this macho bullshit out before you ruin the reputation your pack and your alpha worked so hard for. It's fucking disrespectful." She says, disdain dripping from every word. "You're still part of a pack."

                                                            She waits a beat and then continues. "And listen. Maybe it plays out like you think it will. You rough up the newbie pack that tried to help you, and you stay alone in this. Forever. Word gets out that the Behemoth don't accept help from nobody, and they don't deserve it either. Nobody here wants that."

                                                            Indiana almost stops there. She knows she should. But the fact that this guy hasn't even considered the fact that her pack members aren't the only ones he's putting in danger - That he doesn't even respect them enough to consider that fighting might be a bad idea, well that just pisses her off.

                                                            "Or maybe it doesn't work out like that at all," She says plainly, "maybe I take your arm off and beat you with it till you spit out enough teeth to make me a necklace..." she continues, a small smile on her face from the obvious lie she's about to tell. "And nobody here wants that either."
