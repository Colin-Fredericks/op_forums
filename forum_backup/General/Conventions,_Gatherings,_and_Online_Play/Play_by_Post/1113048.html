    [Recruitment] New Mage: The Awakening (2e) Player Seeks Game, or to Start One

    07-07-2017, 10:11 PM
    Hello Everyone! Welcome, Welcome, Welcome.
    If you are reading this You are probably interested in either Storytelling or playing in a game of Mage: The Awakening 2nd Edition. I would love to have you join me in my exploration of this game line. I have never run, or played in a game of it and would love to try it out. But I can't do that by myself. Tabletop RPG's are not like that.

    So what do we need to put together a game?
    ST: rwknoll

    Player1: HaplessWithDice
    Player2: rwknoll (Probably)
    Player3: Hardwire99
    ----------Optional--------------------
    Player 4: (Open)
    Player 5: (Open)

    Wow that's a lot of openings! You may notice that players 4 and 5 are optional. Why is that? Well simply put...because 3 feels like the minimum and more than 5 may start to create problems and over crowding. You may also have guessed it but as of the time I am writing this the position for ST is wide open. Yes, we have a game that needs a ST. I haven't run mage before so I don't feel comfortable as the ST. I don't want to half ass the Morality system if I tried to run it on my own that seems some what important to the game.

    Does this mean I don't have Ideas? NO I do, but I don't know mage well enough to run it so...come join in me and let's play mage. Please post your interest and maybe an idea or two of the kind of characters you want to run (maybe a paragraph or two nothing too much). We can fill out those details later.

    -----------------------------For those who want to ST-----------------------------------
    So I hate to put too much demand on you but here is what I need from you.
    • A willingness to deal with 1 or more new player in a Play by Post setting.
      • You don't need to spoon feed me answers when I have a question but politely pointing out a page number or section header or general location in the book to find an answer goes a long way.
    • A sense of hope, and optimistic view of your players.
    • The ability to craft a story that has elements of the mystery and the macabre .
    I look forward to hearing from you and the players soon future ST.

      Here is an example showing the character I would like to play.

      Jason Tayler Shadowname: Charron
      Jason was an ordinary young man you spent his time studying without a real aim. He could not figure out what he wanted to do with his life and where he wanted to end up. Without guidance he ended up living his life without a true aim or direction. He searched for something, anything that would give him a path to walk that would be his. He never found it and walked though life going through the motions. His faith provided one pinning point in his life, the other was the need for education These two under pinnings though provide only a scant framework for the young man. He could not determine anything else. He felt like the best two years of his life were in his past, written and done, when he was a missionary for his church out on the Navajo reservation and surrounding cities in New Mexico.

      It was the death of his sister that stirred something in him, that caused him to remember the oddest things, about life, and death. He flung himself into learning about death, about customs and cultures surrounding it, and how to help people morn. He finally found his direction. His study, his life, became death, endings, separation, and helping people not see death as the end but the beginning of something new. That is how he became a Moros mage, by delving deeper and deeper into death, separation, and ends.

                          ​Oh, that's some of the first things I thought about when coming up with the concept. Here's the gist of it: While there are several groups out there that are panicking and acting out of fear, most of the general public isn't because they've realized that the supernaturals have been around them the whole time in their societies. Ergo, it can be easily discerned that they have societies of their own that keep them under control. So mortal governments worked with supernatural representatives in creating a treaty that essentially gives supernatural protections under the law, so long as they obey it. Should they break any laws of the mortal society they live in, they are to be punished as dictated by their fellow supernaturals.

                          The supernaturals dealing with such criminals have been placed under the obligation to ensure they are punished in a substantial way, as the local mortal society can make a case for invoking the self-defense clause in the treaty. This clause allows a mortal society to make a case that they need to defend themselves against the supernaturals, with an international court. Should the court agree to allow the society to invoke the clause, then the society is allowed to use anything at it's disposal to eliminate the supernaturals under self-defense. Basically, the supernaturals have to play nice, or the mortals get to kill them. Naturally, the clause has been nicknamed the "Stampede Clause".

                          ​While the treaty is certainly functional, it's nothing more than a patchwork job made in a rush to establish some semblance of order as quickly as possible. As a result, it's riddled with legal loopholes that any savvy politician can exploit the hell out of. As for your specific questions:

                          ​"Uncle Lu was murdered and the cops have no leads are they able to pay a Moros mage to raise Lu's corpse and simply ask it for help in finding it's killer?​"

                          ​Here's the thing: there's a difference between doing something unlawful and doing something illegal. An unlawful act is one that is strictly against the law, whereas an illegal act is one that legal authorities will prosecute. Resurrecting Uncle Lu could be considered unlawful due to desecrating the dead, but not illegal because the police are causing minimal harm (if any) and are only doing so for the purposes of investigating a case.

                          ​And now I'm getting an image of Phoenix Wright cross examining a zombie.

                          ​"Does Hank the vampire still have rights as a citizen now that he's a blood sucker?"

                          ​He does, but as a vampire citizen. If he was a mortal at the time the treaty was made and then Embraced at some later point, his status as a citizen would change and therefore, some of his rights change. This includes things like healthcare only giving him blood, and his property is subject to a 99-year lease (considering his indefinite lifespan), he can only feed off the blood of someone who does and can legally give consent, etc..

                          "John the Werewolf, is it a hate crime to kill him with silver, when the only thing wrong he's done is intentionally over charge old man Wilkins for when he acted like a racist prick blaming black gays for all the worlds problems, while drinking in John's bar?"

                          ​Depends on what the mortal society deems is a hate crime. If a hate crime must involve a protected class as a victim, then it wouldn't be considered a hate crime, as supernaturals wouldn't be considered as protected classes. However, if a hate crime is determined by the perpetrator committing the crime specifically out of hate for the victim, then it would. Overcharging old man Wilkins by raising the advertised price isn't a crime (though it is a crime if John didn't give out adequate change for Wilkin's payment).
