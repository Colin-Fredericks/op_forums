                                High Above Whitestone Island, RY 767, 23rd of Ascending Fire, Half An Hour After Sunrise:

                                "The bird will be able to find you again if we land?" Haku seemed impressed. "Clever creature. Too bad they take so long to train, they'd make excellent messengers...Calm yourself, Seskan!"

                                Haku banked to face away from the ship, back towards the island.

                                "Our gliders are much faster than any ship! But you're right, we should warn our allies. I'm going to land first! Follow alongside me and copy what I do exactly! Taking off and landing are the most dangerous parts of flying, so if you're going to get yourself killed today it'll be in about three minutes when we land! We'll be landing in water, and you don't want to get trapped under your glider and drown, so once you've come to a stop pull the cord to release your harness!"

                                Over the next couple of minutes, Haku and Wahine made a long slow descent from the sky over the ocean towards the mouth of the Sunset River. As the river grew rapidly larger, Wahine experienced an instinctive lurch in her stomach and rush of adrenaline simliar to when they'd first taken off: falling slowly and at an angle is, when you get close enough to the ground, still falling. The white sand of the beach swelled in size before her, and the tiny people gathered on it grew bigger and bigger and suddenly they were behind her-Haku tilted the glider upward slightly and ran along the surface of the water, and all she could see was his feet and splashing foam. The glider soared perpendicular to the surface of the river, and slowly Haku's rapidly pumping legs sank deeper into it...

                                "Take a deep breath!" Haku yelled, and suddenly they were underwater and the world was roiling bubbles and her pounding heart and the terrifying realization that it's very hard to swim when strapped to a man twice your size, and then her head broke the surface as that powerful man treaded water for the both of them. The glider floated in the river about a yard away, drifting gently out towards the ocean. Haku grabbed one of its straps and looked upward. Ignoring Wahine for the moment, he bellowed skyward.

                                "Don't get too deep too fast, or you'll flip over and break the wings!"

                                In the Jungle About One Day's Hike from Dreaming River Village, RY 767, 23rd of Ascending Fire, Half an Hour After Sunrise:

                                As Pakari turned to leave, a gentle breeze began to blow from the direction he'd come from. As he watched, the little spirits that he'd seen hide before the earthquake poked their heads out from behind tree branches and looked at one another...and then uniformly dashed off through the branches like frightened monkeys. Something...he wasn't sure what, but it had long legs and far too many of them and it was about the size of a house...scurried through the distant forest in the same direction. The animals didn't seem to notice, the birds were still chirping...but the local spirits were all fleeing something, and they were all fleeing in the same direction.

                                That direction was away from where he'd left Hasya.

                                At the Tomb of the Stone Maw, RY 767, 23rd of Ascending Fire, Half an Hour After Sunrise:

                                The Tomb of the Stone Maw is built into a section of the mountainside that juts out in a large rocky outcropping. The black stone of the mountainside was carved long, long ago into a demonic visage: a face with great bulging eyes and tusks. Its eternally roaring mouth opened in an archway as tall as a man and wide enough for three to walk into it side by side, if they were foolish enough to do so. Its great lolling stone tongue formed a rough ramp leading up into the dark passageway. The whole affair had been draped in vines and coated with moss by the ages, so some of the effect had been lost, but it was still a foreboding sort of place, the sort of place you built explicitly to tell future generations, "Hey. This is a bad scary place that you should not go into."

                                According to the Temple Guardians, the Tomb of the Stone Maw had been built by the gods themselves to house the body of a horrible criminal. The stories varied: records from the Dreaming River's oldest temples indicated the tomb belonged to a dark sorcerer, a sea-wizard who had summoned storms and terrible waves and who had commanded dark spirits to bedevil the people of the island. They said he'd been struck down by the gods for his crimes. The Green River Tribe's priests claimed the tomb was the resting place of one of Keshet's children, a half-god chieftain of their people who had turned into a monster and devoured his own family before vanishing into the jungle. They said he had stalked and devoured young men and women from all over the island, and that Keshet's grief at his son's madness had led to three years of unrelenting rain before the beast was slain. The Sunset Tribe claimed that the tomb housed a god slain in battle against an army of demons, and said that the other gods had built the tomb to honor his memory, and had filled it with curses and surrounded it with warnings to prevent it being defiled by the presence of mortals. The Whispering Valley Tribe claimed the tomb had been built by Konani of the Evening Light to house a terrible witch with whom he had fallen in love but had slain anyway to protect his people from her fell magic.

                                The only thing everybody agreed on was that the gods had summoned a guardian to stop any prying mortals from disturbing the eternal rest of the one who slept in this tomb, and that the guardian was terrifying: it was invincible in battle and enjoyed devouring humans, and it never slept. Its claws could rend stone and its breath was a black cloud that blotted out the light of the sun, and even the gods feared its dread power. This was fine...until several centuries ago a foolish priest had entered the tomb and stolen something. The guardian had retaliated by coming out of the tomb every night of the New Moon after that, devouring anyone it could catch. It had been sealed away again after three bloody years by the personal intercession of the gods on behalf High Priest at the time, and he had given his life in the binding of the monster. For hundreds of years the priests had maintained the wards keeping it locked away in the tomb where it belonged.

                                The wards in question included the annual sacrifice of a white goat at the entrance of the tomb, sigils carved into the trees surrounding the tomb and painted over with pig's blood on the day before each night of the New Moon, and sacred chimes hanging all around the entrance which thrummed in deep wooden tones when the wind brushed them against each other. Unfortunately, the big storm last night had downed two of those sigil-bearing trees, and had torn down about half of the sacred wind chimes along with them, breaking several in the process. Hasya had sent Pakari to gather supplies to replace them. The New Moon was six days away.

                                Hasya was feeling pretty pleased with himself despite the situation. He'd managed to get most of the chimes strung back up, and they were once again thrumming pleasantly. He'd carved sigils into some more trees surrounding the area to match the ones on the fallen trees...he wasn't sure they'd work until Pakari came back with the pig's blood to paint onto them, but he figured it was better than nothing. He was just sitting back and stretching his aching back (he was getting too old to be climbing trees to hang ropes, he decided) when the birds of the forest went abruptly silent.

                                A moment later, the earth rumbled, and the trees shook violently, and the ground rippled like waves at sea, and the stone tongue of the Tomb of the Stone Maw splintered and cracked, and he lost his footing and fell to the earth and banged his hip, and two more trees weakened by the storm creaked and crashed to the ground, and the wind chimes along with them.

                                Then there was another deep, still silence.

                                And from the shadows within the Stone Maw, a scraping sound like an obsidian knife being sharpened on stone, and a deep rumbling chuckle.

                                "Today is really not your day, old priest."

Maybe a warrior or an enforcer of some kind- someone to whom the people turned when they needed help. Logically, he knew that he was suppose to side quietly and let Haku do all the talking- but that wasn't what he had in mind. Those people didn't really appreciated weakness, so showing a little attitude could work for his benefit. Sure, Haku may see him as rude and decide he wasn't acting accordingly- but in that case, he could always pull the "stranger" card and apologies for still trying to learn the ways of their people. It worked for him when he met the High Priestess, after all- a win win situation, which could help him rise his reputation, and maybe even gaining someone in a key position as an ally.
