            @Evilmarmelade: Oh, I'll definitely agree there. ^_^ I tend to play relatively low-key characters, but I've had a lot of fun with arrogant, bombastic sorts who aren't nearly as awesome as they think they are, too. There's a ton of fun to be had with that.

            @Thorbes: Yeah, I can understand how that might put you in a tricky position. ^_^o Part of me is wondering if I should have maybe PMed you with Max's plan. It might have made it easier to work it in (or not), while still leaving room for the tasks. And yeah, the personally-suited tasks sound like really fun and interesting set pieces; I'd hate to lose them.

            Still, here's a thought. Whether the characters fail or succeed at overcoming those tasks (and thus parts of themselves, to some degree)* may well have deep effects on the characters after/if they leave. So even if the tasks' prizes don't wind up counting for any of them, they could still have a definite impact. As for Max, perhaps their Keeper could prepare tasks for all three of them, noting that Max doesn't have to participate, but that it would please him. While also noting that he'd prefer to accept Max's terms after the fun and games, and that doesn't strictly have to do so. The implication would be that the Keeper may find it suspicious if Max is willing to sign away his heart and soul to make the Keeper happy, but is unwilling to amuse him like this. It may impact any decision to accept, or decide he smells a rat (no offense to the Rat).

            * The events themselves may also be kind of traumatic or otherwise deeply affecting.

            So, in that case, all three would (probably) wind up facing the interesting and/or horrible scenarios the Collector's concocted, and they could still leave a mark on their psyches. It's also possible, should Max's plan pay off and the retroactive nature of the new terms come into play, that Jessica and Nate might be entitled to arrange for different compensation if they succeeded.

            Thoughts?
