        "Hm." Swimming Owl's brow furrowed in thought. "Master Splendor, you live up to your name. That plan is brilliant. Lots of moving parts and multiple points-of-failure, which is always risky in a business plan...but brilliant. I like it. That being said..."

        She accepted a drink from one of the automatons.

        "I think Nahuel is right. That complexity is a weakness. Only one or two parts need to fail in order for the whole thing to fail. Also, as the person ponying up a lot of the starting capital for this plan...well, even if building a casino would be highly profitable, you don't build a casino in a city you know is about to be invaded. Good way to lose your whole investment with nothing to show for it. This plan does not account for disasters beyond our control, the most likely of which would be military action by the Mask of Winters or by less dangerous warlords in the region looking to consolidate their power. There is a reason the place is filling up with refugees, after all. It's safe now, but with the Realm's troops gone it's only a matter of time until someone tries to seize this territory.

        I propose an...alteration to the plan. Working the fields just means we have food, something an invading army will want and can take without paying. I propose that instead of putting our slaves to work as field laborers, we train them as soldiers, and offer our services as a mercenary company. We offer the slaves benefits and the ability to rise in the ranks, and we offer them their freedom upon completion of a set term of service, to earn their loyalty. We supplement their ranks gradually with mechanical warriors who don't feel pain or need to sleep. Otherwise the plan remains the same, except that our investment defends itself and can defend our interests in the region. We might want to replace the field-god with Nahuel. He'd make a good warrior-deity, and he can train the troops in one shape and show up for the occasional "miracle" in another. Plus we don't have to involve another outsider in our scheme, we keep it in-house.

        Given the tumultuous nature of regional politics, I expect people will be willing to pay more money for protection than for food these days anyway. If, a few years down the road, we have a small army of battle automata and a larger army of skilled warriors loyal to us...well, then it's a simple matter to seize and consolidate power ourselves, set down whatever local laws about slavery we like, and start flexing our muscles to impact the policies of neighboring nations.

        ...and, of course, I make money on the mercenary contracts, can use our police force to make things a little harder on my competitors in other local markets, and I get rich even if the Realm shows up in three or four years and kills you all. Sound good?"

        So I'm making God-Kicking Boot, an Exalted webcomic, now. Updates on Sundays. Full-color, mediocre but slowly improving art. It's a thing.

                    "While local conditions will ultimately determine the final form of the plan, I must bring up some difficulties with your suggested modification.

                    In the first part, I can think of few activities more likely to bring opposition from local power groups. An army of slaves, and a very public anti-slavery policy will make us look very much like one of those warlords we are concerned over. Our ability, or rather lack thereof, to train and equip them fast enough to be a legitimate fighting force is also a concern. It may even push them into the arms of our enemies.

                    Secondly, you may be overestimating the ability of Master Talos and myself to produce automatons. While I would have to inspect the facilities here before I can make any judgments, it will be insufficient on its own. Artifice is a discipline of cost. Those costs are not measured in silver or jade, though much of those is required, but in such prodigies as retain or renew a vital echo of the Creation. To produce warrior mechanica according to the... old pattern several would be required for each instance. We would need to condense industry to a fluid that we might shame the engine of Carnot, or gather the corners of spheres to shape metals in angles of uncertainty to confound the bloody sword of the gods' swarthy servant time."

                    He stopped mid elaboration and muttered, "That has the ring of verse I did not pen..." Gathering his thoughts again he continued.

                    "Please forgive me, my thoughts at times outpace me.

                    Third, we actually do require labor. Until the devices are constructed and in place there will be a great deal of physical work required. Even once they are functioning they will not totally eliminate labor.

                    Finally, not all of the slaves we receive will have the desire or inclination for soldering. While I take no pleasure in using humans in this way, I think we only affirm their condition, and the sin of it, if we force them to fight. If that is their choice, so be it. Yet if we offer it to them, it is our responsibility to be able to see it done well. We would need trainers, armorers, and additional sources of income to support all of it."

                    He seemed just about finished when he started,

                    "That said, we may have an existing mechanism for this sort of thing. If the stern brotherhood were to accept aspirants we could use that as a vector for training without suspicion and ensure that it is a choice rather than a compulsion. Still, I am not certain our already straining budget can afford it. We need harder numbers before we can say one way or the other."
