        The biggest building is a single tower twice as big around as the others, it's top reaching above the water. The tower isn't round or square like most of the others, but appears to be a hexagram in cross section. Each floor is defined by an arched window, at least twice as tall as a man, located in the center of each section of wall, except for the top floor, where each point of the star is a balcony, each meeting in a wide archway at the hexagonal center of the tower, supporting the spire on delicate-looking arches. A spiral staircase in the center provides access to the interior of the tower. All of the towers windows appear to be intact, with a stained glass insert at the top of each window depicting a sunburst. The bottom of the tower remains shrouded by the darkness.

                    (After a long hiatus, this game has restarted. To accommodate real life better, the game was moved to Discord. Transcribing it here as a story archive.)

                    West Sparrow keeps drawing closer to the oddly intact hexagram tower, headed toward the nearest stained glass window. He’s looking for traps before just going through the archway under the glass.

                    Banfiz, lost in his study of the reliefs, does not notice that Sparrow has left the group. He is hyper focused and unlikely to notice without someone specifically calling him. While this does make him vulnerable, he has forgotten there may be dangers to this mission.

                    The nearest window, looking in on the floor just below the balconies, shows what appears to have once been an office. The room contains waterlogged, decaying cabinets, shelves, and furniture, including what appears to have once been a desk. The sea life encrusting the exterior of the tower is not as thick inside, thinning out further in the darker corners of the room. Two open doorways inside the office show gloomy rooms beyond, but any details of the interior are too difficult to make out from the window. West Sparrow doesn't notice any dangers lurking around the archways on the balcony floor, though he does startle several small fish as he swims closer. Within, you can see that the stairwell goes down as far as you can see, with a circular space in the center allowing you to see landings and a railed walkway at each floor.

                    West Sparrow goes through the archway and starts investigating the office, paying particular attention to the desk.

                    "Don't get too distracted friend," Ethan says as he passes Banfiz, following West Sparrow to the office. "We are here for a purpose, though there will likely be time for study if we're successful." Ethan directs a few of his men to do a brief survey of the floors beneath the waves, before turning to aid West Sparrow's investigation.

                    "Huh? What? Oh I see. Young ones so eager to see the new they miss the old. Well, needs must as they say." He disgruntledly follows the others into the building

                    Descending to the next floor, you see that doors and hallways lead off from the walkway circling the stairwell. Rotted scraps on the floor show that once they were once carpeted. Elegant fixtures mounted high on the walls would have provided light. Finding the hallway that leads to the office is not difficult, proving to be one of the rooms you glimpsed from outside. The other opens to what was once a parlor or sitting room, judging by the wreckage of couches and chairs. Several pedestals arranged along the wall display sculptures: a marble bust, a sinuous green dragon, a tarnished silver tree, and a pair of polished wooden dancers, miraculously preserved.

                    West Sparrow glances into the sitting room. The treasures on the wall are interesting, but not immediately so. None of them are maps. He heads into the office, determined to search every drawer and cabinet for signs of maps and/or this building’s former purpose.

                    Choosing a likely looking cabinet as a place to start, West Sparrow pulls open the door. The sudden disturbance causes the cabinet to break from the wall, falling down on the swashbuckler.

                    Feeling the water churning, West Sparrow throws himself to the side, hoping to avoid being pinned under the cabinet.

                    West Sparrow gets clear of the cabinet before it crashes to the ground, where the cabinet crumbles a bit under its own weight.

                    Seeing at a glance that West Sparrow is fine, Ethan checks to see if the cabinet contained anything worthwhile.

                    The cabinet's wreckage is empty.

                    Banfiz, upon entering the room, did not notice the cabinet but was rather more interested in the sculptures, particularly the dragon, and begins to investigate it. Using his knowledge of crafting to determine the maker and time period and possibly take it with him for inspiration

                    Hearing the large crash from the other room Banfiz just shakes his head and goes back to his study.

                    You believe the dragon, which is made of verdigrised copper, is the work of a renowned sculptor from the pre-Shogunate period, Nifuji the Eight Fingered. That puts the piece as more than two thousand years old.

                    "Excellent, excellent. Must add this to the collection. The things I can learn from this!" He puts it in one of his pockets as he continues to look at the other sculptures.

                    The bust is of a middle-aged woman with long hair wearing a simple tiara. She has high cheekbones and an aquiline nose and her expression is stern. On her brow, lightly engraved, is a full circle. The tree is a replica of a bonsai, each twist of the trunk and each tiny leaf captured in exquisite detail. The pair of dancers are in a flowing, more abstract style, almost seeming to spin about one another as you watch. The wood is a warm, golden brown and seems untouched by time spent underwater, in stark contrast to the furniture around you.

                    Having successfully dodged the cabinet, West Sparrow metaphorically dusts himself off, and starts looking through the office furniture again, albeit with an added measure of caution.

                    Disappointed that the broken cabinet held no valuables, Ethan joins the search in earnest, digging around the office with a bit more abandon than West Sparrow. Notwithstanding his own antics, Ethan gives a few hand signals to a waiting marine to have the rest of the shoal go a bit slower in their search. Hopefully the water will not have consumed anything of note, but haste now after all these years will not help.

                    Banfiz, admiring the dancers, finds the illusion of movement fascinating and somehow manages to store it away without noticing he has done so. He thinks to himself "I should show this tree to the others as this is the pinnacle of art" and picks it up and holds it with his left hand. While calling out to the others to come look at the beautiful tree, his right hand is drawn to the circle on the busts forehead and he slowly traces it counterclockwise with his finger.

                    Nothing happens when you touch the bust.

                    Ethan and West Sparrow's search of the room doesn't turn up much. The wood of the shelves, cabinets, and furniture is just as decayed and waterlogged as the cabinet that fell and any papers that might have been contained within have long since rotted away. The search isn't entirely fruitless. Inside the desk's drawers, you find an intact seal stamp and a writing brush with silver bristles.

                    Not expecting much, as he’s not that well traveled or learned, but hoping his luck is turning, West Sparrow examines the seal stamp to try to identify the former owner.

                    The stamp plate is blank.

                    Having finished his art appreciation with his pockets a little fuller, Banfiz goes to find everyone else.

                    Frustrated, West Sparrow “tosses” the stamp away, and swims out of the office. He heads to the other room, intending to examine the treasures he saw in the other room. He rolls his eyes when he sees that Banfiz has already pocketed nearly all of them. He taps Banfiz on the shoulder and points at the surface, then opens and closes his mouth, trying to get Banfiz to agree to go to the surface so they can talk without resorting to mime.

                    One of Ethan's marines returns, giving a series of hand signals: No enemies spotted, no treasure, proceeding down.

                    Banfiz looks at West Sparrow, looks to inhale a lot, and says "We are breathing under water. This means we are still oxygenating our lungs and can talk like normal, though it will be muffled."

                    West Sparrow pauses, breathes in extra, and says “oh, I see. Ok then.” He takes another big breath and continues, “so what did you find? My search came up with nothing helpful.”

                    "Unless Banfiz has other insights, I think we might try our luck deeper. Perhaps there will be more in the harder to reach areas, or at the bottom of the spire, especially if this ruin sank quickly," Ethan adds.

                    "I have nothing more to see on this floor so, yes, let us proceed"

                    As you proceed downward, searching each floor with Ethan's shoal, you begin to get a general idea of the tower's layout. The uppermost floors seem to be luxury apartments, with offices, sitting rooms, bed chambers, and so on. As with that first office, much of the furnishings are ruined and rotting. The surviving furniture is mostly stone, such as marble display pedestals, vases, or porcelain wash basins. Here and there are pieces that look nearly pristine despite their age, like a wooden dining table and matching chairs. Going further down, the levels are smaller apartments. Here there are fewer pieces of intact furniture and less evidence of decorations. At this depth, the water is noticeably colder and the light is growing dim. Without your new adaptations, it would be very difficult to see. Just when you think you might be exploring an ancient apartment complex, the levels even further down contain large dormitories, public baths, blocks of single rooms, and workspaces of various sizes. Then you find something very interesting. The next room you explore is a large library. Despite being completely flooded, the room is filled with intact shelves, stocked with books. The shelves and the books appear to be completely dry.

                    West Sparrow, having been metaphorically burned earlier, is hesitant to touch the books, but on the other hand, this has been the best chance of a map they’ve had all day, so he swims toward the nearest shelf, draws Breezedancer, and gently pokes at the nearest book with the duller side of the sword tip.

                    Nothing happens

                    West Sparrow sheaths his sword, and then grabs a book with his bare hand, attempting to remove it from the shelf.

                    The book comes away as easily as from any other shelf. As soon as it leaves the shelf, the pages begin soaking up the surrounding seawater.

                    West Sparrow hurriedly puts it back, not wanting to damage anything.

                    The book dries instantly, though the wrinkles of water damage on the visible page edges and a crust of salt scale on the cover remains.
