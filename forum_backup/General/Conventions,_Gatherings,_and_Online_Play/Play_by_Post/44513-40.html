                                    I'm working on making enough awesome ruins and stuff to throw you guys (Read: Hyuga) off of my scent after Diekatu's statements. Have some other stuff instead!

                                    Weapons of Novalesk
                                    Novalesk is the progeny of the Second age, born from the Balorian Crusade and shaped by countless conflicts. Is it any wonder then, that regalia there is solely weaponry? What follows is a cross section of the regalia of certain positions in Novalesk.

                                    The Blade of the Archon: Forged by Myrinne for her own use, and left behind on her final fateful journey, this blade of Starmetal and Gossamer is a long and broad Daikliave with an ornate Orichalcum hilt. It's origin and imposing nature made it an easy choice as the symbol of the Archon on the formalization of the Nation. It is rumored that the blade can imbue the holder with a portion of the fabled founder's strength and intellect.

                                    The Three Swords of the Founders: Nikoloi made these three Short Daikliaves after his mother's death for his brothers and himself. Made of White Jade and Bronze, the sole distinguishing feature between these blades is the respective Mons of their original owners inscribed in the forte. Asside from their construction and origin, there is nothing truly spectacular about them. Regardless, these three blades are the official regalia of the patriarchs of the original three Gentes.

                                    The Pyrtanein Scepter: Given the pattern of weapons as regalia, a Scepter seems discordant, at least until one considers that a scepter is really just an increadibly ornate cudgel. Found in a first-age ruin by a local scavenger lord, it was donated to the state in the fifth century Post-Contagion. The Ruby-encrusted gold cudgel is rumored to have eldritch powers that bind individuals to speak the truth and obey basic rules of etiquette.

                                    Magisterial Axes: Each one of these jade-steel Grimcleavers is supposedly made at the hand of Origenes himself for use by his law-enforcer priests. It is said that the magistrate's Essence is interwoven with that of their weapon, making it impossible for anyone else to use it. The weapons are therefore symbols of the wielder's authority to enforce laws and adjudicate trials of National law. Traditionally, when a magistrate levies the death penalty, they use these weapons to enforce their ruling.

                                    Panoply of Origenes: Historically, Orignenes has owned two pieces of Divine Panolpy; a silvery stave etched with jade, and a straight broad blade of similar construction (how a relatively minor Terrestrial spirit managed this feat, he does not reveal). Accoriding to rumor, these artifacts possess the power to gift and remove Essence, respectively. The blade, however, was granted to Archon Larissa upon her apotheosis, making her own.

                                    Ruins and Tombs will be coming soon, I promise.

                                                            The Kree Monoliths
                                                            In the north of Novalesk, there lies a series of giant monoliths. Seemingly grown from the ground out of yellow stone and garnet crystals, these monoliths predate the Shogunate. They serve no known purpose or function, despite the attempts of Novaleski savants to discover their purpose. Some say that they serve as gateways to the Demon City or the Underworld. Others, that the offer protection from the Wyld. The only thing that is known is found on the smallest stone, only the size of a large desk. The stone bears precise Old Realm Script which says, "Kree Red-Wings raised these stones." Who Kree Red-Wings was, and why they raised the stones, however, is left shrouded in mystery.

                                                            The Singing Statues
                                                            In the marshlands of southern Geherin, there is a large ruin that the Geherin give wide berth. This ruin is a lightly walled complex, some three square miles in area, and it has no known form of traps, save its occupants. Hundreds of living statues reside in the ruins, either pacing through endless loops or fixed in place. These beings, often resembling disproportionate humans or beastfolk, are not aggressive, and will not even defend themselves if attacked. Each one, however, roars out a unique hauntingly sick song, which together forms a cacophonous din that drives men mad, and few who enter leave with their sanity, if ever at all. Those occasional Geherin that enter that manage to leave with a portion of their wits intact often become Skalds, as the experience supposedly grants them the ability to see the future. Such is the fate of Orin Dead-Eyes, a Terrestrial Hero who braved the ruins two decades ago, and lost his sight in the process.
                                                            Even the Fae are not immune to this, as the music of the statues rends asunder their thin venier of story and narrative. Some claim that Fae slain this way calcify into new statues, adding a new song to the horrid cacophony.

                                                            The Lumen Pyramid
                                                            Two weeks travel South-Southeast of Lake Vera, the rolling green Grasslands break way into red-brown desert. At the rough center of the desert, there lies a giant gilded pyramid. Despite being the proving grounds for young, foolhardy Novaleski Scavenger Lords for centuries, the site remains undespoiled. The Pyramid is rife with deadly traps, automaton scarabs that suck all the fluids from their victims, and golden mummified warriors. Most who survive simply encounter the corpes of a previous expedition and leave.

                                                            One man, centuries ago, emerged from the pyramid, half delirious with thirst and mortally wounded. He claimed to have made it to the treasure-room, wherein an Anathema god-king is interred in all his regalia, his heart still blazing with his unholy power. He died not long after.

                                                            The Tomb of Gens Heftes
                                                            The region now encompassing Theros and Novalesk and Ordon was known as Towering Prefecture during the Shogunate. The Prefecture was ruled by Gens Heftes, who claimed to have lived in the area for thousands of years. The northwestern portion of the prefecture bordered into the Metagapalan mountain range, and there the Gens laid its dead. A sprawling underground complex holds hundreds of individual tombs, each one the final resting place of a single Gens member. Though each of the Terrestrial was entombed with a full regalia of artifacts and other goods personally important to them.
                                                            Yet, even a Terrestrial Gens of great history and wealth could not afford to leave such a wealth of magics unused. A decade after death, the Gens would exhume the dead, to remove any items of magical puissance or great material value. Only items with only sentimental value would be left behind with the stripped corpse. Only two dozen or so corpses were left with their artifacts when the Contagion-weakened Gens retreated from the Balorian legions.

                                                            That should be enough to obfuscate matters. Now that that's done with, does anyone have any other requests?
