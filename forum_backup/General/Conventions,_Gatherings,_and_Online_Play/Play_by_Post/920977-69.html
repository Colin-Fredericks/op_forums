                                Originally posted by rwknoll View Post

                                I...actually don't know what you mean. I'm a bit rusty on my Shakespeare references.
                                It's a line from Macbeth. When Macbeth goes to the Weird Sisters for information, they say that "no man born of woman" can kill him. He takes this to mean that he's immortal, but at the end of the play, Macduff comes to get revenge for his wife and son, and reveals that he was "from my mother's womb untimely ripped," which scholars generally interpret to mean that he was delivered via C-section or his mother died in labor and he was removed from her corpse, thus he was not "born." Macduff then kills Macbeth in a duel.

                                Fun fact, this "cop out" was what inspired J.R.R. Tolkien to have Eowyn kill the Witch-King in ​The Return of the King: he had gone to see a production of Macbeth as a child, and had assumed that the twist in the prophecy was going to be the Macbeth would be killed by a woman. Hence the similar terms of the prophecy.

                                The more you know.
