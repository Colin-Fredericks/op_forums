The next, his hand slipped and he almost went down the rest of the world's most harrowing mudslide. The sound of that awful gorging, crunching, and chewing from below had been drowned out only by his terrified squealing, until he hit a muted green obstacle.

            Clutching desperately at the wall, and never more aware of the tactile information his new hooves weren't giving him, Max tried to keep steady. "Really, thank you. But... Where is this? How do we get out?" When he thought he had as decent a grip as he could expect, he tried to take a look around the new environment, mostly centered on the other openings ('Not out through those... Still eager for information, Max's snout worked the air, much as it brought him little other than the overpowering, nauseating ('And unreasonably appetizing...') odors from below.

                                    It was rough going, making the crossing, but Max actually thought he was doing quite well. At least until the point his muddy grip slid and a mud-caked hoof did likewise. His arm flailed as his heart jolted into overtime. It's unclear if he would have made it, had he been in the large gap between tunnels. But the Chinese Zodiac says pigs are lucky, and perhaps there's some truth to that: he was near to one of those tunnels, and managed to wrap his arm around it. Max grunted as his head thumped against the wall from the angle, but he maneuvered his wayward hoof back to solid ground... in time for it to be swept away in the mudflow. Another mad scramble amid a strained squeal brought it back just outside the opening.

                                    The Pig stared, and began to despair. Forget rescuing Nate; at that kind of speed for the mud, there was simply no getting past it. The coursing river was up past his shin! 'Hock,' Max recalled at once, from that grisly butcher's diagram. He trembled for reasons aside from being splattered in cold wet earth. 'Wait... Earth...' He eyed it speculatively. ' Well, worth a shot.' Even in this unnatural place, the mud was natural. And therefore... "W-Wilds. I-I could use some help with all this mud."

                                    Max felt the difference immediately, the small of a fresh breeze in his snout, while his body felt as though he'd been sunning himself for much of an hour. Make no mistake, it was still tricky gripping the wall and balancing on such a narrow strip -- on hooves, no less. But he hadn't realized just how badly the stuff had been slicking his efforts or muddling his sense of touch. Hesitant, the piglet tried again for the raging mud current. His hoof went into it as easily as if it were a still pond. Nor did it slip when it reached the solid surface below. It was odd to feel the mud rushing past his bristly leg, but the Pig would take that over being carried off by it any day of the week.

                                    Pleased, Max turned back to maneuver the rest of him off of the wall and paused on spying Summernickel. The goblin was a ways back, having begun to follow reluctantly -- given the treacherous distance to travail, Max could hardly blame him -- and appeared to have stopped. The long-eared creature stared at his porcine companion. And then grinned, as though an old coin he'd stopped to pick up turned out to be a doubloon. "'Wilds,' the litt-- 'Wilds,' Max says," Summernickel remarked, correcting himself as he began to make double-time across. "A handy Contract, is that. Perhaps Max might... extend its Clause to ol' Summernickel, too?" The goblin's grin turned ingratiating as he neared the piglet. "Summernickel promises to help Max -- and Max's friend -- escape if they help Summernickel. And that Contract is no small help."

                                    Max considered this a moment. 'Wilds, would that even work? Can I ask you to spread your protection around?' He felt a mythic presence stir slightly with a dull wind, not elated, but not disagreeing. 'Hmm... It would be a help. And... Maybe if I ask him to swear to respect and honor the wilds while you protect him?' The wind swirled again with more enthusiasm, its misgivings much allayed. 'And... Can I do the same with Nate?'The wind brushed one hand, and then blew in the Goat's direction; the Pig got the sense that distance was the obstacle. 'Okay. All the more reason to hurry.'

                                    "Well?" Summernickel asked, having reached Max. The piglet looked at the outstretched hand, and nodded, adjusting his grip and extending his own, slowly. "Okay. I-If you help us make it through, w-we'll give you some help. J-Just swear to honor and respect the Wilds, and they'll protect you as long as you do." The two hands clasped, and shook, and Max then felt the warmth and wind from the other side. As well as something else. The promise, perhaps? Regardless, Summernickel seemed pleased. They shared a quick smile, and then Max went back to the matter at hand, traversing the mud. 'Kinda pleasant, actually,' he thought of the flow. 'Might be nice, later, to just lie back and let it flow over--'
                                    "Stop, piglet, stop!" came a screech from behind. Max stopped just in time for a large rock to plow through where he would have been if he hadn't. He gaped, soundless, until he heard the crunch of the rock in the swinish maw below. Summernickel gave him an encouraging tap on the shoulder from behind. Shaken from his daze, Max stammered a, "Th-Th-Thank you," and hurried across. A little optimism was a good thing, but he made sure to mind his caution as well.

                                    The rest of the way was much less eventful. A couple of scares, but with some care and the aid of the Wilds, they made much better time, with Max shouting periodic encouragement to Nate. And, at last, they made it. With little preamble, the Pig crossed over to where the Goat dangled and reached down. Even as he did, though, he realized how exhausting his trip had been; he was giving a grunting pant. Summernickel looked to be doing better, but not by much. Even working together, this was going to be trouble. So Max tried a different track. "N-Nate! I... I need you to-- to s-swear to h-honor and resp-pect the Wilds. J-Just trust me, and we'll make it! N-Now!" He forced his weary, muddy arms to grab one of Nate's, while Summernickel did likewise with the other, cursing in a language neither beast knew. They were going to depend on the Goat and that Contract. Or else all three of them probably wouldn't even manage to be plaque on the massive, unceasing teeth below.

                                                        The goblin nodded his long-eared head in recognition of his achievements, and then to the two new ungulates, perhaps seeming a bit overeager. "Yes, it is Summernickel's pleasure to meet Max's friends. Per Summernickel's agreement with Max, only we three will help each other escape," he noted, gesturing to the Goat, the Pig, and himself. "Certainly Jessica wishes to take part as well...?" The fellow looked hopeful, but also perhaps a bit impatient. "Reunions and greetings are fine things," he added, in a note of diplomacy, "but escape is finer." Summernickel then tensed and gave a shriek as what looked to be a chair came flying down from the tunnel above. Thankfully, it was nowhere near hitting anyone, but it did make his point: this was not the safest of places.

                                                        "Right, right." Max stood there dumbly. "... Um. D-Do we have a plan...?"
