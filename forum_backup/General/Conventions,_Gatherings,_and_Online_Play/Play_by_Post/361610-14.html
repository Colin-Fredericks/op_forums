                                    02-18-2015, 04:22 PM
                                    "Labor saving devices are tools, capable of being turned to more than one end. Moreover, slavery is merely a target we all find agreeable to work against. Even if there is no division among us, we will need to balance our longer term influence with the short term suffering of the enslaved. What would help is if I had more information about local conditions, I cannot..."

                                    Behind his many veils Splendor's eyes opened very wide. To himself he muttered, "...back bricks without clay..." and "The problem is maturation..."

                                    He turned deliberately to Swimming Owl before sweeping his eyes across the table. "Is there precedent for the hypothetical I am about to relate? Suppose I have a prophetic vision that a pack of wyld wolves will sweep in and slay two thirds of all the pigs of Puyo. Therefore, I go to those with herds of hogs and offer to pay them the full value of their pigs now in exchange for a certain number come Autumn. When the time of slaughter comes, the price of ham, pork, and bacon is very great because of the shortage. Yet because of the contracts I have made I have the pigs I purchased at the agree price and sell them for a great profit.

                                    Would the authorities support my right in such a case?"

                                    Before the guildswoman could answer he turned to one of the serving automata and said, "Fetch me paper and writing supplies, I must write this down."

                                    As soon as he had it he began to write, speaking quickly as he did so.

                                    "We would need to establish at least four organizations, at least the convincing facade of them. First, a priesthood of a moderately powerful harvest god, though why would an important god violate the Mandate that way? Nothing to gain by it... pick candidate later, perhaps elevate local field god. Second, an ascetic order committed to extracting the maximum labor from slaves. Best if they appear especially stern. Third, an inventor or group of them to serve as the source of our labor saving devices. Finally, a group of moneylenders and merchants.

                                    We start with a paper transaction, Swimming Owl promises a sum of money over a period of time for services or some such. The content doesn't actually matter. We sell the future revenue for a cash payment now, and use those funds to establish the credibility of our priesthood. Then we sell ecclesiastical ranks, from a nominally finite pool, which provide a say in the operation and a right to draw a portion of the assets for personal use. We invest this capital in land purchases. We negotiate those purchases before hand through multiple intermediaries to keep it from becoming known that we are buying up large parcels.

                                    Then we negotiate a contract with local slaveowners for labor, paying them with a portion of the profit from the fields. We offer to purchase them at the end of the season at inflated prices with the remainder of the harvest. That's where our discipline order comes in. They approach the slave owners and offer to get even more labor as their religious duty in exchange for full reign to practice their faith on them. In reality, they inform the slaves that they will be receiving a fair wage, on paper, which will be applied toward their purchase price. If they have a sufficient output by the end of the season they will have earned their freedom, otherwise the purchase agreement with the owners will not cover them. That should motivate them more than any whip.

                                    While all this is going on the inventors come in. The priesthood will contract them to increase the yields. We use a few demonstrations to build talk around them and use that to boost faith in the sale of priestly ranks and offer the benefits to the slave owners to sweeten the labor deal. Make their compensation publicly outrageous, and paid on delivery, then use that to entice merchants and craftsmen to provide us with the additional labor and materials we need to actually produce them.

                                    At this point, we will gain a great deal of real assets, owe three times their value in debt, and have promised the next ten years of output within two. Essentially, we are minting money from promises and dreams, trading it for real wealth, and financing it by selling pieces of things that if summed would be greater than the whole."

                                    He paused for breath as he looked over what he had been writing, a schematic of the plan that covered several pages and included everything from suggested percentages, estimated rates of return, and proposed details of the false organizations.

                                    "Then we are going to need money to pay installments on all these debts and cover our operating costs. So we cause a panic. We drop rumors about something that will cause people to lose faith in our ability to repay. Once it has fomented the fourth group comes in. They offer essentially the reverse of what we have been. If people pay them small sums now, they will pay out large sums if the priests default on their obligations.

                                    If everything goes right, something that will require all of us to strain our skill and excellence, everyone we scammed will be rich, a huge number of slaves will be free without creating new demand, and we will be unfathomably wealthy and own most of the city-state. If it goes wrong than the house of paper collapses and the probate courts will have a full caseload for years.

                                    It will take someone far more persuasive than I am to convince the relevant parties to agree to all this.

                                    On consideration, I may have been struck by a moment of madness from Dedwen and Mun. What say you?"
