            Originally posted by Dietaku View Post

            Answer to Question 1: Here we have Janus Q. Solar. Janus is a Gunstar character, and an affable enough chap. Let's say he's a Zenith Caste General. Janus wants a distinctive weapon, and one that also could assist in his generalship. He settles on the Third Arm Glaive (From Storyteller's Companion), a potent artifact spear which doubles as a banner for mass combat. However, the Glaive is a five dot artifact; that's pretty hefty, even for the Glaive's impressive benefits. HOWEVER! Because of Janus' Artifact 5 rating, he gets an effective 15(!) points in artifacts he can buy, so he could easily buy a set of power armor or another weapon, or possibly even a warstrider, even WITH his Third Arm Glaive. Y'see, Artifact in Gunstar works as it does for Alchemicals, which get an effective three for one rate of purchasing artifacts for background dots.If you didn't take this into account, it's cool.Just add whatever you want and have points for to your sheet, and everything will be even.

            Answer to Question 2: They often do, but sometimes they don't. The outliers are usually powerful or strange items from the Wyld or are Helltech from Malfeas. Such things are made out of Gossamer (The stuff of Dreams made into a tangible form) or demon flesh and Vitriol (Malfeas' one element, a form of passive consumptive acid which due to Malfeas' mutilation, became liquid hate and anger. Here in Gunstar, however, it is a powerful alchemical reagent, known as Thien-To.)

            It doesn't cost any more to purchase items made out of the Magical Materials at Chargen; after all, that's partly what makes them artifacts in the first place, and so you can just say ," Oh, yeah, my Daiklaive is made out of Orichalcum. No biggie" and most, if not all STs will go, "Okay, yeah."

            Now, MAKING artifacts in-character, on the other hand, is a different matter. To do so, you need to have magical materials, or exotic materials, on hand, and to make the alloys necessary for the magical materials is a long, expensive process in and of itself. Oadnael's Codex goes over it far better than I could.

            As per your last question, let's go back to Janus Q. Solar again. Janus has an artifact Francisa, an axe made for both throwing and for use as a melee weapon, constructed out of Orichalcum, made by hist bestest buddy Golden T. Alchemicalbot. Should Janus decide to toss that sumbitch at a demon, it'd have the Orichalcum bonus for thrown weapons. Should he decide to bury it in the demon's face with a strike, it'd have the melee bonus. Does that mean it has BOTH bonuses? Well, yes, and no. You only get one benefit at a time depending on what you're using it for, and weapons which allow such options are rare (The Vajra spear in Scroll of the Monk comes to mind.)

            Hopefully, that answers all your questions.
            Thanks for the answers, but you didn't answer my questions about Lunar shapeshifting. Does moonsilver shift to fit any alternate form or just the war form? Do smaller sizes provide bonuses to dodge or stealth?

The reasoning being that he commisioned the artifact before begining the infiltration and had them store it on the ship for when he met up with the rest of the team.

            IC reason for artifact:

                Ah, sorry, I appreciate you reminding me. I've always been in groups who allowed Moonsilver armor to benefit a Lunar in any form, regardless. That's kinda what it does, being as Protean as the Lunar is. It may not be overtly apparent (Unless you really find the idea of a mouse in silver chain-mail to be appealing, which I kinda do now that I think about it), it does protect its user. As for whether small forms provide stealth benefits or not, it depends on the form. For the bobcat and rat forms you asked about specifically, I'd say that rat provides a +2 dodge bonus due to being so small, and bobcat is +2 to stealth and athletics, being an ambush hunter an all.

                Also, from one Lunar player to another, here are some tips:

                -Gift charms and Deadly Beastman are your friends. Don't let those Terrible Argent folks tell you otherwise.
                -Dexterity and Wits are bullshit in how good they are, while Manipulation and Appearance are for Trolls who wish to use Troll-fu.
                -Stamina makes you nigh-unkillable, and is always good to dip into.
                -Alternate forms are always an opportunity for stunting.

                Also, your harness is fine, as it reminded me of Breath of Fire, and that is always a good thing. I hope that answers all your concerns, and if you need anything else, don't hesitate to ask.

                                        I assume you mean the regimented and codified styles of fighting, because otherwise, your question would get a ridiculous answer. As you say, there is Titanic Hero, and of course, Black Claw, the style I trained under Peerless Ocelot in. However, there are many, many more.

                                        To wit, there are as many martial arts in the Spiral as there are stars in the Sky-vault, if not more. I have heard of innumerable ones, but my personal favorites are as such:

                                        Impossible Black Quill Style: A Terrestrial style that revolves around manipulating your hair, especially into hardened dagger-style quills. Nasty stuff.

                                        Terrifying Hungry Wolf Style: A Celestial style formed by the many predatory Titans, it involves losing oneself to instinct and using your teeth and fingers to violent effect. I'm not a fan, but I've seen it used to great effect at the Battle of Loarn.

                                        Zeni-Jutsu: A martial art used by the Guild, which involves the weaponizing of the black nachre coins they're fond of, along with using paper scrip as a thrown weapon. It can also transform fallen foes into cash.

                                        Awe-Inspiring Shadowy Cavern Style: A Sidereal style that alloows you to summon objects from your mind, using Ignorance itself as a weapon Supposedly, Cytherea lent this Style to her Exalts, the Philosophers.

                                        And of course, Septimal Aesthete of Wonder style, a style I hope to initiate into one day, that manipulates the Limit of what is and what-is-not. It emulates the Principle of Oramus, my master, you see. I've seen it used drive men, gods and Daeva alike to ruin, or to save entire systems from total destruction. However, I'm not that far along into enlightenemnt.
