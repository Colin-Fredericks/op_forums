                                Philophrosyne
                                It was morning. Philly decided to pack up camp and come down the east side of the mountain towards the ski resort. She’d pick up her truck there and head back to LA. She had been up in the peaks for a few weeks now, since New Years, practicing honing her form to survive in the cold temperatures and thin air. There were not many animals awake up there this time of year. All the bears and wild cats were in hibernation, but she could sense them sleeping, feeling the web of life all around her even under the thick powder of snow.

                                She took long strides, pushing herself to eat up distance while still trying to get used to the snowshoes. She’d never been up here this time of year. She had also packed low rations and was trying to fast with a slower metabolism.

                                Philly wanted more. More of this. Just looking out with mundane and magical sight to take in the world with all richness. Was that all she wanted, or was there also something pulling her towards the invisible, and beyond that to the Supernal? To the Primal Wild, and Ecstasy. She felt like she wanted to become what she saw, all this beauty. But she also feared being swallowed up by it. Was that fear wisdom? She had come up here after the holidays to sort some of it out in her own heart and head. She had told some of the sleeper side of her family that she was going on a “Spiritual Retreat”. They nodded with approval, but also worried about her safety. A young woman way up there on the mountain alone. That didn’t seem safe.

                                Well, it wasn’t. It wasn’t safe. And that’s what she had wanted. To not be safe. To go beyond the limits of safety into something else.

                                She had turned off her phone. Gasp. What would everyone think of that. Not that it mattered that much. Reception sucked up here this time of year.

                                She finally picked up something resembling a park service trail and moved faster down, adjusting her oxygen intake as necessary, moving below the tree line. Finally she got to the resort and started looing for her truck.

                                She looked around at all the resort guests coming and going, and over at the luxurious lodge. Civilization. She wondered if any of her old college friends were here, but she didn’t stop to check. She got on the road in the late afternoon, and adjusted her eyesight for the sun setting.

                                It would be late before she got back to her uncle’s house, where she was currently crashing. She pulled over for a pizza in Pleasant Valley. She had been dreading it all day, but she turned her phone back on, and then set it in the cup holder while it blew up. After a few minutes she picked it up again.

                                Let’s see. Voice Mail full. No duh. Gazillion missed calls from mom. One missed call from dad. Gazillion texts from mom. She had told her where she was going. Lots of Instagram posts from sorority sisters and other assorted friends. Grandma's been busy on Facebook. Indeed, Monster, Linkdn, along with several personal job offers from concerned relatives and family friends.

                                “One word Benjamin. Plastics.” She thought.

                                And then as if her thought had summoned the ghost of Anne Bancroft – a message from Chevalier.
                                She opened it.

                                        Philly snorts. Jaunt. Yeah. That’s what that was. On top of a snow covered dormant volcano in the dead of winter.

                                        Auntie Chev has a wonderful plan. Oh brother. Debutants does not sound promising, especially after looking at all those Instagram pics. Chana should not be giving Starbucks her Shadow name for her peppermint mocha order, even as a joke. And Twix with her new cabal and her new Alchemist boyfriend and blah blah blah. But then Philly has a twinge of guilt. Family and friends are important. You can’t live like a hermit on top of a mountain all your life. Can you?

                                        An old house. That’s intriguing. I wonder which one she means? How old. What kind of condition is it in? How many square feet is it. How many acres? Drainage? Termites? What is the neighborhood, on this side and the other side, like? Some of those decorating pics she scrolled through were kind of cute. What does she mean by “Used to belong”? What’s the story there?

                                        “Tossed the bones.” Its so hard to think about Fate, when there is so much in the Here and Now. Looking forward and backwards does not come easy, but I guess for some mages it does. Everyone means well. Everyone wants what’s best for me. What do I want. If there is a pattern of Fate, do I even have a choice?

                                        Philly looks down at the empty pizza box. Pig. Well I guess I hadn’t eaten anything in like a week.

                                        She pulls out of the parking lot and back on to the highway. She gets to Little A at dusk, just before the shadows have completely turned to dark and there is still a twinge of blue in the sky. The light pollution drowns out the stars and she sighs. As she goes down the main drag she thinks of all the memories of this town that is so much a part of who she is. All the shops and restaurants, the post office and bank, and the elementary school with the fingerprint pictures on the windows. She hears the night train pulling through down at the tracks.
