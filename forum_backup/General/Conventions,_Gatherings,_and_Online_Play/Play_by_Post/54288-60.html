        (Novalesk)
        The adoration of the crowds are loud; it seems as if the whole city are present. Yet, seeing the Boule causes it to die down far faster than Yi could hope. As silence begins to fall, a voice pierces the crowds. It appears to originate from a priest whose stole is of woven orichalcum.

        "By what right does this outlander wish to rule us?" the priest shouts, "No right! He claims mandate from the Lords of the Divine City, but so too did Odal Thousand-Fangs, and Karandros the Reaper! He is naught but words! Do not listen to him, sons of Novalesk, he will lead us to ruin!"

        Some small murmuring arises from the crowds. The priest seems to have hit a Novlaski hot-button. He seems about to launch into another tirade when Holly launches herself into the open area surrounding Yi. Her landing is so hard that the stones crack beneath her feet.

        "I know I did not hear that, Kaiphas," she says, "I know you did not compare my son to two of the worst villians in our history. Otherwise, I would demand satisfaction for bismirching the nobliitiy of my household."

        "So it is true," the priest, Kaiphas says, "You did adopt the Anathema. Very well. If he is your son, though, he is of age. He can speak for himself."

            Sol, but you are generous.

            "Peace mother. Pray give your son a moment to think on what has been said. For surely Kaiphas would not speak words without knowledge."

            He paused as though considering for a moment. When he began to speak his tone was contemplative but firm enough to carry.

            "Anathema... a strange word put to strange purpose. In ancient meaning it meant that which was dedicated to the gods, what we now call sacrifice, and so not put to profane use. Yet if I am a sacrifice, to what am I dedicated? In later use it means that which is in opposition, that which is wholly against the nature of that to which it was compared. It was in that sense that the think it was meant now."

            He turned to the crowd and began to preach.

            "In the immaculate text, those who shine as the lights of heaven are called the anathema. Yet it cannot be their actions which earns them this title, it is given er they do anything for good or ill. Moreover, are not the fey opposed to all shape, the dead to all life, the demons to all that is not hateful, and in like manner are many things in total opposition to the Creation. If the chosen deserved destruction they earned that fate by their own actions. Unless any here would say that Odal or Karandros did not bring their own doom by the things they did.

            What then are they anathema too? Do not the text say that they sought to change their station, to rise above their place, to refuse to accept the order of things? They are anathema then to the world that is. Yet who of conscious does not deserve the same? Did not they very day an infant die in its cradle? Did not children and elders succumb to hunger while all about them was food? Did not friends come to blows? Did not sickness claim those who could have been save had their been doctors to tend them? Did not ten thousand tragedies occur that did not need to? The world that now endures is a thing of horror. Do not the fey even now march against Novalesk and the oceans of the Wyld wear away the shore of Creation? Do not the shadowlands swell vast from their gluttonous feast upon the slain? Have you not heard of the doom of Thorns, that once great nation that fell in a single day to the unliving hosts?

            Who would accept such a world who did not reign in luxury while Creation burns about them? If this is what I am called wholly against I can think of few higher honors. Yet that cannot be all. For I see you O Novalesk, men and women who have made a life amid the vast circles of the earth, and I know you were meant for more than this. I see all mankind and know we were meant for so much more than this. I will not accept that the son of the farmer must toil in the fields until the work has ground him down because there are not other options for him or his family. I will not accept that the daughter of the weaver must marry and bear children until death or old age mean she can offer no more. I will not accept that we are no more than the product of our circumstances.

            If that is what it means to be anathema, to say that everyone, mortal and mighty, deserves the choice to decide their own future then I will proudly wear that title. Who could use it as an insult and not thereby claim to support all the evils that are now upon the earth?'

            He turned to Holly but still spoke so all could hear him. "So you see mother, Kaiphas has done me a great honor."

            Then he seemed to consider again.

            "Yet what of outlander? That seems a very odd thing to say? For none of Novalesk sprung up from this native clay. Nor did the gods fashion them and set this here. Indeed, the weight of scholars concur that all mankind has its origin in the far southeast. Is it not true that those who came here all came from elsewhere, that the twin troubles drove many from their homes to this place that is now Novalesk? I know few save Origenes Protogenos who may have claim to call any truly outlander. What then can he mean?

            By law and custom I have become Novaleski, have chosen this as my country. What more can be said of those ancestors who found no nation and cause one to be? Indeed, the child of novaleski born in distant places is still counted among the body of the people. So place of birth is not what it means to be of Novalesk.

            I confess I can think of nothing he could mean save that I bring new ideas which seems an oddly unspecific thing to say. Unless anyone would claim that my mother by choice is not also Origines daughter?"

            Turning to Kaiphas one last time he said, "As for ruling, I really have no desire for it. Governance is a matter of incredible work for little thanks. It requires accepting responsibility for all that occurs without recourse in heaven or earth. There is no aspect of human flourishing I could not possess far more easily without it. In fact, there are few people who actually rule that enjoy the job. Or did you think that the cushion on the chairs in the Boule were so comfortable as to be worth traipsing to Hell and lugging heavy objects across the endless desert for?"

                                (Novalesk)

                                Kaiphas blanches under Yi's verbal assault. He is about to retaliate as Jensven reaches Yi. Seeing that he has not only been out maneuvered by Yi, but that he is also outnumbered by the three Exalted, he shuts his mouth without saying anything.

                                A clicking sound coming from the Boule draws the attention of the crowds, saving the Heirophant some face. The boule parts, an a gangly man in simple black and red robes passes through a silver rod in his hand. He walks with a heavily limp and is using the rod as a walking stick (which you see is the source of the sound). He has a long narrow face, sunken beady eyes, and his salt-and-pepper hair (more salt than pepper) is oiled back, revealing a harsh widow's peak. Yi remembers Larissa identifying him in the Boule; he is Nikoloi Mari Vellus, Deputy Minister of Trade and Commerce, Nikoloi's candidate for the Archonship.

                                "The name's Vellus. Nikoloi Vellus," he says, handing Yi a scrap of vellum, "I'm an honest man."

                                And lo, looking at the vellum it's a business card which proclaims that Vellus is indeed an honest man.

                                "Now, I like the cut of your jib, kid. I'm willing to deputize you as a official member of my trade envoy corps. What do you say?"

                                        "I have engaged in commerce, and counted myself skilled in the art of the money-maker. I acquired wealth for myself and my masters and found no honor in it, save such honor as may be gained by any who engage in fair dealings and upright practices. There is no kleos to be had in the gaining of riches and in their possession, only in their use may glory be won; and that a poor sort of glory.

                                        Timê is to me a poor thing to strive for, which may be given and taken arbitrarily. We are all held in a single honor, the brave with the weaklings. For my part I will be content with what Heaven sees fit to provide.

                                        As for kleos, such as might be won by he who has received such prizes of honor: I will cultivate virtue and have such kleos aphthiton as I earn by deeds alone. Neither shall I boast to increase it nor accumulate honors so as to be worthy of it.

                                        Therefore I must decline lord Nikoloi. I would not deprive you of timê, for what could I gain that was not at your expense?"
