    For the rest of the month, Adonis spends his time seeing Erwin. He also spends his days as he usually does: people watching. He spends hours sitting in place, watching humans speak to each other. Each person has their own way of moving, way of talking. Sometimes, he invites some of the others to watch. ​"Just follow one person. Where are they coming from, where are they going? Who are they talking too? Notice what they say and don't say to each other. Sometimes they say more with their gestures than they do with words."
    Scout is... strange. Muses are blood and air. Nepri are phlegm and water. They are both fluid, but Scout is cold, where he is hot. Earth mentions Scout's fear of slumbering in the river. He meets Scout and offers to let him stay at his apartment when he can manage. He can't help but think that his apartment is becoming a hotspot for Created in Winnipeg. Maybe Scout could share the apartment as a Hovel. It's supposed to be a one-person apartment, but he could use Eros to convince the land lord to let Scout stay with no problems. The only problem with that plan is it could cause Disquiet, and he doesn't want to cost himself and the others a safe haven.

    The first night Scout stays, Adonis asks, ​"Could I... watch you? Undress? I'm just... curious how we compare. I know we're all made different ways, I just... want to see if it's true." He removes his clothes without prompting, and then allows his true form to shine through. "My creator... she had a thing for some actors. Most of them died young, so she salvaged their body parts, stitched them together." He recites the names of each man who makes him up. "My head... she killed the one she liked the most to get it. She told me so."

            Adonis

            It isn't too difficult for the Muse to get Erwin talking. He has very strong opinions about his job and his employers, and it seems like he hasn't had the chance to vent in a very long time. The Amalgama office in Winnipeg is fairly small, especially compared to the one in Toronto and, ultimately, the home office in London. There's maybe a hundred, two hundred, people working for the company in the city. They only really have offices in the UK and former British colonies, reportedly since Amalgama was once a metallurgy guild, back in the day. Erwin isn't overly fond of his superiors, but he's uncomfortable with sharing any particularly salient details. What he does share is that he had to pay through the nose for the privilege of using certain materials that only Amalgama really has access to, and that taking a job with them was a way to reverse the cash flow. The way Erwin speaks, you think he might even still be indebted to them.

            Erwin still doesn't know, exactly, where they stand. It's far too soon. Of course, he isn't impartial to going on a few more dates to figure it out. There's something different about the way they share information, the back and forth, the exchange. It's very different to what he had with Lily, but it's... similar in some ways too. He can't quite put his finger on it, but his Azoth flares in response. By some small miracle, the Divine Fire hasn't scorched Erwin yet. Disquiet hasn't set in. But Adonis can't dare to hope... can he?

            OOC: Adonis gains a Beat for resolving his Aspiration to go on a date, and he gains three Vitriol Beats from a instinctive Milestone progressing towards Follower. What did Adonis learn in his interactions with Erwin?

            Mark 5 and Regret

            The Titan and the Originist wander the streets of the North End and Point Douglas, one in a rough mask, and the other tallying along with journal and pen never far behind. Regret rarely gets himself involved, but when he does, he handles himself admirably, the Dead Soliders giving him a fighting chance at lives that were cut short. He thinks he was a little too effective, at one point. He came close to acting on his Torment, and the whispers of the Dead Soliders were very loud indeed. Some people got scorched by the Divine Fire, and Disquiet set in.

            Mark 5 was more discerning in how he chose his targets. He didn't just roam the streets and deal out his own brand of roughshod justice on anyone he thought needed it, he spoke to those he had already made connections with, he tried to determine who was an actual risk to the community. He spent several days gathering information with Regret, and then the two struck out. They came upon an abandoned warehouse, a known hideout of a violent, unsophisticated local gang. They are brutes and thugs, interested in armed robbery, arson, drug dealing, and murder. Of course, they were all but helpless against the might of the Titan's Transmutations. They all bore tattoos of the letters "IP" on their arms, and all wore red bandannas. Some had crossed weapons tattooed on their arms as well. Mark 5 earned some repute from this, which has improved his standing in the neighbourhood as the vigilante known as the Breaker, but perhaps he aimed a little too high.

                Scout

                It is exceptionally easy to track down information about Amalgama LTD, and Scout is rather uniquely qualified to be looking into their particular brand of occultism, and what he's found is nothing but endless arrays of exploitative ugliness. Back and back, those in charge of Amalgama have left a trail of fire, blood, and broken bones, all the way back to the 15th Century Netherlands. Rumour has it that they are a triad, the alchemists in charge, and that they found their own Vitriol-fuelled waters of life back then. Since then, they have done nothing but try and control as much of the Divine Fire as they can. They want to own alchemy. The syndicate started simply, but it quickly grew. They seek out alchemists of sufficient talent, regardless of whether they hunt the Created or whether they are on their own curious Pilgrimage, and offer them a position as a "client consultant", meaning that they end up paying Amalgama for the right to practice alchemy without being abused or murdered by Amalgama thugs. However, if you agree to work exclusively for them, as a "partner" they'll pay you. You can rely on a steady paycheque, but it comes at a cost. You'll be expected to perform alchemy for the corporation, crack the heads of fellow alchemists, and even be called upon to hunt the Created. The only way to ensure you are left alone by the corporation is to rise in the ranks, and to rise in the ranks you must capture and gut the Created. But for all that these corporate alchemists are protective of their assets, only the three immortals running the show are invaluable. Everyone else is expendable in their quest for true eternal life.

                Finding information about their corporate dealings is a little more difficult, but it's doable, especially since there's an office in the city. You know that they are one of the top biochemical corporations in the world, up there with the Cheiron Group and Last Dynasty International, but their product can be unstable and have unexpected side effects. People also whisper that Amalgama is a good friend to have in a tight spot, that they are troubleshooters and consultants who can take care of business and unforeseen problems.

                *****

                Meanwhile, it is easy enough to dig up information about the Saint Boniface Cathedral and the fire of 1968. Once upon a time, it was a magnificent feat of neoclassical architecture, a Romanesque building with homages to Byzantine church design. The soaring bell towers rose over a hundred and fifty feet into the air, and the basilica seated a little over two thousand people. The entryway was a Tyndall limestone facade of three archways, surmounted by the towers and the massive stained glass rose window. Historically, the building is vastly significant. It was the church of the Franco-Manitobans and the Metis, in all of its older incarnations and even to this day. It has met with an end in fire several times, but still it stands. It is said that overheating tools from the roof crew handling repairs was responsible for the fire, but rumours and urban legends of cigarettes and alcohol still circulate. What remains of the original building is the limestone facade where the bishops of the Archdiocese were intombed, though the towers have fallen and the beautiful window was melted to slag. Instead of rebuilding the original church, architect Etienne Gaboury made a point of building a new church using the wreckage of the old one. A new building, risen from the ashes of the old one, a holy relic of patchwork architecture much loved and much abused. Perhaps it isn't so strange that the Created are so drawn to it.

                Other rumours circulate in occult circles. That the burning was the result of a small Firestorm sparked by a Promethean attempting the New Dawn. No one knows who that Pilgrim was, or even if they survived, but the rumour persists. Some say that the old walls were crawling with Pandorans drawn to Athanors inscribed into the walls and the facades of the basilica itself. Whatever the case, the first wall still stands, and the dead bishops remain. Some say that the ghosts of the bishops protected their final resting place, the oldest cemetery in Western Canada. They also say that Marie-Anne Gaboury, first white woman in the Canadian West, grandmother of Louis Riel, rumoured witch in life, is guardian of the place, a goddess of death on the prairies, who wouldn't allow the grave of her revolutionary grandson to be defaced by flame or by misfortune. She lived through revolution and bloody trade wars to the ripe old age of 95, and many doubt she'd allow her life's work to go down the tube just because she did a simple thing like die.

                *****

                In between investigating Amalgama and seeking out the history, official and occluded, of the Saint Boniface Cathedral, Scout manages to find time to print off a map of the tunnels beneath the Exchange District, around the Manitoba Legislature, and that are established in a few other locations around the city. It isn't the best map, but it will have to do. It's the only one they've got.

                *****

                Scout also spends time plumbing the depths of Azothic Memory, risking scorching the land with the Divine Fire to find the knowledge that eludes him. His Transmutation whispers to him that there is an Athanor inscribed near, or perhaps even within, the Cathedral, but it doesn't detail where, exactly. The information is indistinct, hazy. There is so much weight to the Azothic Memory in Winnipeg that it might be difficult to tease memories of one specific Athanor from all the memories of Athanors that have come and gone. Perhaps he needs to take another approach. Every Athanor is burned into Azothic Memory itself, so perhaps he needs to find a way to go deeper into the Memory of the Divine Fire.

                He takes up the Stannum Athanor, the journal of the unknown Outcast, and tries to force his way into the Azothic Memory. The Divine Fire could come spilling out of him, burning the land, starting the process of the Wasting of the Peaceful Range, but he needs to at least try. He takes the journal, and begins to meditate on the contents, allowing his own unique understanding of the Azothic Memory as an Originist to guide his path. Scout sees.

                He sees himself as a man containing a thundercloud in a thin bag of human skin, made of separate, angry parts and held together by sloppy stitches. He needed a way to let it out, before it killed him and anyone around him. He bleed out the storm, bit by bit. He let electricity crackle with bitter sneers, he let loose lightning with hateful bile, but nothing ultimately worked. He needed to become the eye of the storm, but he didn't know how. He sees landmarks, a viking in stone, a lake, a serpent in the water. He sees himself with a hammer, with gloves, with a weight lifter's belt. He channels the storm but he flays himself from the inside. He can feel it building, the calcifying acid of Flux, the twisting churning engine of Torment. It is a burning in the gut, the taste of vomit on your tongue, orange juice after toothpaste, the shudder of revulsion, bones cracking under tensed muscles. There is a rift between himself and others, a chasm created by lightning and thunder, tended to by a river of Flux. He turns the storm without, focuses his rage on others, he throws Flux like mud, like hateful words, and people seek to come close anyways.

                He finds the eye of the storm, and he becomes it.

                Within the storm, all is anger and rage, buffeting winds and arcing bolts keeping everyone out, and himself within. A wisp of a face appears in the storm, elongated arms of white lightning stretching wide. Electricity arcs between fingers like tears in the world, a noise loud enough to split the world, until it is silent within the storm, until the storm doesn't matter, until the storm doesn't exist. White and golden flames burn within the clouds, following arcs of lightning, breaking down walls. Vitriol, green and slick, falls like rain, stinging your skin. The storm walls fall. The vision ends.

                    Earth

                    The Fury vents, and cows anyone that crosses her. Divine lightning and Flux-scarred talons leave burns and dreadful tears throughout the train yards at Dufferin Industrial and the Weston Shops. Terrified rumours escalate into urban legend, and then into the occasional police report. They call the dreadful, seemingly protective, terror Darkstorm, a raging storm front of lightning, aggression, and self-hatred. Earth doesn't target anyone in particular, she just vents her wrath on acceptable targets, and the foot soldiers of several local gangs end up on the wrong end of a fist or a thunderbolt. The African Mafia, the Central, the LHS, Earth has upset the activities of them all. They all use the train yards as a place to deal drugs, to traffic illegal weaponry, and to beat the hell out of any dissidents who need their heads cracked. More often than not, the police end up in the train yards because they heard someone was beating the hell out of people, only to find groaning, battered bodies wearing gang colours on the tracks.

                    It is easy to find things about Louis Riel. He is easily one of the most written about early Canadians, and one of the most controversial. Canada was a country born of peace, that was immediately tested in the crucible of resistance and war. The Hudson's Bay Company sought to give away land that wasn't their to give, and the people who lived on that land disagreed rather vehemently. It was Riel who led the resistance, Catholic, French, Metis against Protestant, English, Canadians. The Quebecois, a conquered people unbroken, roaring support a nation away, the Orangemen snarling threats in the ears of Ottawa. His mortal life is fascinating, but the details of the rumours of any potential supernatural influence are hard to find.

                    You have heard that his grandmother was supposed to have supernatural power, and that when he was executed a well-loved statue of a saint that he owned lost its head in his St. Vital homestead. You have heard that he was considered for the priesthood, before he was expelled from the seminary for contrary beliefs and rebellious attitudes. He was already a firebrand in the Red River Resistance, but in his exile he became something more. An absentee member of parliament, an outlaw politician. He was committed to an asylum in Quebec under an assumed name when he suffered a breakdown. Priests sent missives, they whispered in his ear, they persuaded him he was the chosen spiritual leader of his people, a new Moses for a New World. When he suffered outbursts of manic megalomania, he wrote extensively on spiritual matters. He was a heretic who supposedly kept a book of prophecy written in the blood of the last Canadian bison. You've read his writings, you read transcripts of his final statement to the court. Assertions that he wasn't mad, but chosen by God, guided by angels. His last confession shows confusion. Apparently, he had misinterpreted his spiritual mission, as told to him by the Church, and in the end renounced heresy and any breaks he may have made with Rome.

                    The book in buffalo blood is conspicuously missing.

                    The Principle has agents, advocates, really, that work Its will across the world. The qashmallim, the Dominions, the angels, the Messengers, and more names besides. You have heard stories about how it has sent god-like beings to protect a single human life, and sent tiny wisps of flame to alter the fates of nations. Their Missions are unique, unable to be duplicated, and are attempted only once. They exist only for the Mission, no matter what the Mission is. They do not question, but neither are they omniscient or omnipotent, they may only operate with whatever knowledge they possessed when they were born from the Divine Fire. They command obedience, they offer advice, they make earth-shattering revelations. And they lie. As often as they tell the truth, the angels lie. And when they come not with blazing revelation, they come with a fiery sword. They are inscrutable and implacable, but the Messengers always, always, produce change, good or bad. It is the nature of the Divine Fire.

                    It would be trivial for a qashmal to alter the contents of a letter, or impersonate a priest. It would be trivial to nudge a man on a ledge one way or another. There is that lingering book of prophecy that has yet to be uncovered. There is only one place left to look really. Riel's grave at the Saint Boniface Cathedral.

                    *****

                    You see Riel's grave in the churchyard and it seems remarkably modest. The Church itself is a beautiful work of stone, iron, and wood, the new sanctuary built within the burned out husk of the old one. Wouldn't hurt to look inside. The ceilings are lower than you would have thought, and the room is more claustrophobic. Arms outstretched, feet pointed downwards as though suspended. A little placard indicates the piece is called "Christ of the Red River".

She talks about God as if God was a person she knew, a person she trusted and respected, a person who was right here in the church and that Earth was offending by her current state. Whatever it was, it set something on fire inside of Earth, and it triggered an Elpis Vision.

                    The world was made in various shades of flame and ash, burning in a black void. Earth was alone on ground made of packed ash, with a banquet spread out before her. It's not like any human meal she's ever seen in her wandering, but it speaks to something in her, of nights spent wandering and sharing meals with fellow travellers. The raw, coppery taste of blood, the fatty succulence of pork that isn't pork, the citrus-like tang of Vitriol, all rising up in the back of Earth's throat. A body, stretched out on the ground, Pandorans a mass of tentacles and thorns and tusks and other spastic riots of flesh tearing at it. Dead Girl, beautiful white feathered wings spread, viscera caking her full lips, her pianist's hands, moaning as she sucks at sweetmeats carved from someone's back, draining the Vitriol, draining the humours, draining the Pyros. Memories. Unpleasant ones.

                    Earth is brought back to the table and the spread banquet by a sudden crackle of flame. A figure of flames in white and gold, a shining void of a person, sits opposite her. You notice one hand made of darker flames, deep indigo and purple so dark as to appear black, forming a clawed right hand. The figure is broad shouldered and powerful, and they gesture at the food, encouraging the Named to eat. They don't speak, they never speak. Earth wants to lash out. This is more trickery. This is more manipulation. More mOcKERy and she won't stand for it. She beats at the figure, and the figure changes, and splits, and becomes many. She is shattering a veritable army of figures made from calcified ash, and whenever she strikes down a statue, a flame alights on the dirt, and rises as a peculiar flower. A garden grows in Earth's wake. She smashes open furrows into the dirt, and the plants grow of their own accord. The figure with the blackened hand waits in the middle of the garden, a corona of white flames about their head. They beckon to you to come closer.
