            "My adventures?" Maia says, clearly surprised. "I haven't gone on any adventures. Though I can see why you'd think so. Me having all of these," she waves self-consciously towards her heavily scarred left side. "I suppose I can tell you how I got them. Tale for a tale."

            Maia orders a fresh round of drinks and grows pensive, clutching her refreshed drink, "It was about fifteen years ago, back when I was scarcely older than my prodigal student. I was a journeyman at the academy, studying the Art of Warding. Chancellor Feiro was mentoring me, and wanted me to summon a demon for a practical exam."

            "A gethin," she continues, her sole remaining eye growing slightly glassy at the memory, "sneaky little lizards, about the size of a dog. About as harmless as demons come. Chancellor thought it would be a nice, easy practical. And it would have been, if I had done my calculations right. Instead of summoning a gethin, I summoned the Sire of the Gethin, Octavian, the Living Tower."

            "It was a bit of a shock, to put it mildly. He was pretty pissed for some reason, so he swung at me with this giant metal bar. I tried to block it with my left arm. It ... didn't work. Then all I can remember is some sort of blast, and waking up a couple of days later. The Chancellor bound and banished the demon, but I got caught in the blast, burning the entire left side of my body. The physicians were able save my life, but they couldn't save my eye or arm.

            "Feiro looked over my calculations. Turns out that I forgot to carry a one. One tiny math error caused all of this. He was baffled though, said that the ritual should have just failed. He's even tried it a few more times over the years, but its never had an effect," Maia grumbles, "But I'm getting off course. I was determined to get my eye and arm back, but i would never be able to afford sorcerous rejuvenation. A magical prosthetic would be almost as expensive, if I didn't build it myself. So I transferred to the Artificery, and worked like I was possessed," Maia's voice has become distant, lost in the memories, "Then, combing through the archives, I found an ancient codex on golem construction. The proper preparation rituals, construction methods, binding tables. Everything."

            She locks eyes with Hyuga for a brief moment, before looking away, somewhat embarrassed, "I'm part Fire Elemental, and most golems work by binding an elemental into a shell as a power source. So I hatched a plan. I built an arm, and worked for years, modifying the formulae, and refining my alterations. Checking and double-checking. After nearly five years of work, I ritually bound my own elemental essence to this."

            She holds up her left arm displaying the delicate artifice of its construction, "It's just as nimble as my other arm, and has better reflexes, but I can't find a way to make sensory feedback. Otherwise I would have replaced my eye too."

            Maia sits back into her chair, and eyes her now-empty drink, shrugging, "So that's pretty much it. The grand tale of my great fall and rise to the dubious heights of academia."

                (Temple)
                "They are right, in some cases," Origenes laughs, "Such nations exist. They're not exactly common, but not exactly uncommon either, if you catch my meaning. I prefer to avoid such vulgar violations of Celestial law, though."

                "As for the current domestic situation. The Fae caught us in the midst of a ... delicate political situation. The previous Archon, our head of state, left us rather expectantly. Gens Nikoloi and Gens Zophyrus each offered a candidate, with their long-standing political allies immediately throwing in behind them. Each faction has more than enough votes keep the other from ratifying their choice, even with the undecideds."

                "Nikoloi's candidate is Nikoloi Mari Vellius. He's a career bureaucrat, a man with decades of experience dealing with the political situation here in the Capital. He would be a strong candidate under most circumstances, as he has a reputation as an honest man and the experience to get work done. But things aren't quite usual. About two seasons ago, a young member of Gens Althea, one Althea Sanna received the grace of the Danaa'd. Althea is a long-standing ally of Zophyrus, so they threw behind her. She might be a strong candidate as well, were she not less than two decades old, with absolutely no practical experience.

                "Ptreas and Spyrus have neither declared for a side or put forward a candidate of their own. Both are small, though, and lack sufficient numbers, even together, to break the stalemate," Origenes raises a finger and a brow, "but many members of the other Gentes are hoping that one of them might be able to produce a compromise candidate. From their inaction, neither of them feel like they have a good person to do so."

                "Aside from the Gentes, there major institutions of the nation are my Temple, the Academy of Sorcerous Wisdom, and the Strategoi, each serving as the core of culture, learning and the military for the nation. I've already told you of how I factor into the politics. The Academy is nominally non-partisan. However, a significant percentage of the citizenry has spent at least some time studying there, even if only for a season or two. Tacit support from the Chancellor would gain a significant amount of favor from the upper classes for a candidate. Nikoloi Fiero is relentlessly apolitical, though, so good luck with that."

                "The Strategoi serve as the military masters of the nation. They administer to and lead the Capital troops, who are at the beck and call of the state itself, rather than the Gentes. Unfortunately, those forces were the first to meet the Fae and their minions in the field, and suffered from it. The survivors from those battles are still in the field, attempting to regroup. There are currently two Strategoi, Spyrus Eugene and Zophyrus Vasil. Vasil's a fierce house loyalist, but luckily she's leading the Capital troops in the field, and out of the picture."

                Origenes begins rubbing his beard-less chin in thought, "Now, if I wanted to break this stalemate, and I knew the nation was at risk, I would convince the two neutrals to place their troops at the hands of the Strategoi. The Strategoi hold the power to raise levies, independent of the houses or the Boule. With a core of veteran household troops to temper the untrained levies, this new legion rushes to the aid of their comrades in the field and fights off the Fae. A brilliant Ptreas or Spyrus commander distinguishes themselves in the fighting, gaining the respect of the military, the admiration of the masses, and the gratitude of their god."

                Origenes sighs, "But sadly, I'm not allowed to be a political actor. So it would be up to someone else to pull of this feat. Or to convince one of the Gentes to withdraw their candidate. Either or."
