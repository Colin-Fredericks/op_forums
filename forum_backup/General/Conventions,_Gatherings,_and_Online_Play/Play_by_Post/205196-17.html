                Max cringed at the sound of his voice from the Secretary's lips. It was unexpected and Max, just like so many people, hated hearing the way he sounded. Well, probably not 'just like:' most people don't have to hear themselves sobbing, with hints of grunt, snort, and squeal underlying every bit of it. That and the stutter brought him a fact to focus on, one that was bizarrely petty, given the gravity of his situation. 'Oh no... I... It's like I'm Porky Pig...'

                But that thought was quickly forgotten. The unpleasantness felt like a day at the beach after what came next. Max felt his stomach knot with shame as Nate shouted at him. He didn't think the poor Goat could sound more horrified. Or more disappointed. 'Nate... No, I. I have to. I have to...' For a few moments, Max held his breath, and then gave a few sobs, human and animal vocal chords mingling. He turned his head toward Nate, but couldn't meet his eyes. "N-Nate... I... I'm sorry. I'm s-so sorry, but..." Another snuffle. "I have to. Th-This is the only way. I-I-I mean..."

                The Pig took a breath, tears falling from his eyes. And a bit of anger came out with them in his words, anger at the whole desperate situation. "Y-You saw it in his face when y-you and J-Jessica asked. He's th-thinking about how to give you wh-what you asked for, but n-not what you want! Y-You know that! And he'll do it! H-He's pr-probably been at this l-longer than we've been alive, all of us p-put t-together!" Max swept his hand around the other assembled animals. "Y-You'd have to be stupid to tr-try to get one over on him!"

                "Y-You two are in trouble! Big trouble. Just... Just admit it. A-A-And..." The anger had been subsiding, but left him completely. "... And you're both better than me." Th-That's at least something I could finally do for you. Y-You'd get a choice. I-if you don't like what he gives, you could s-switch. M-Maybe he'll even show m-mercy...?" Max had believed most of what he was saying. The thought of the Keeper showing mercy was not among it.

                "Just... P-Please don't hate me for this. And... And..." The Pig trembled. "I-I don't know if I'll still b-be me after, so... Th-Thank you. For being my friends." And then he dipped into his breathing exercises to get more of a grip, and tried to put those friends out of his thoughts.

                Max looked up hesitantly, still not daring to meet the Keeper's eyes. "I-I-I'm s-so s-sorry for the w-wait. ... M-Master." There was that nauseous feeling again. "Uh. Right. M-My terms." He took another deep breath. He tried to clear his thoughts again; he needed all that emotion out. This was going to be hard enough as it was. "I-It's... Just like you said. A-Anyone with a C-Contract like us can sw-switch to the new terms if th-they want. And it's retroactive. A-A-And..." Another breath. "Yes. If you meet the terms, y-you'll own me, j-just like I said. I-If you agree to the n-new terms altogether. So..." The little pig dabbed at his eyes with his sleeve again. Unfortunately, the dried mud on his face was by now wet with tears, and smudged terribly, making him look even more pathetic. A blush was evident, even through it.

                So, with one last breath, Max cast a glance up at the little pig statue near the Keeper that started all this. He took a few shaking, clopping steps toward the center of the court and plunged in. "The new terms. It's still the first person to buy a given figurine as in the old terms. In this case, it's me, so I'll just keep referring to me. So, when you sold it to me, you... W-Well, it's a sale: you agreed it c-couldn't be yours anymore, and, and that sole ownership would be mine. 'cause... Th-That's how sales work." He coughed into his awkward flailing. "A-Anyway. At the same time... You..." A gulp. "Instead of getting permission to bring me here and change me, you... You took ownership of me, m-my heart, my soul, my... my everything, in the broadest sense, and not a bit less. Th-The kind of ownership that would let you do all you've done to me, and... anything else." The Pig fell silent, feeling very ill, and hoping he hadn't just made a huge mistake. "... That's all," he said, mouth dry. "If all that's true, the Contract holds."

                "I-If th-that meets with y-your satisfaction, M-M-Master." Max gave an awkward bow, and waited nervously to hear the response. It would decide how this was all going to shake out.

A great hog, at least thrice Max's size -- not counting the layers of mud to cake on over years of wallowing. The creature was content to laze and sleep in his mire, and to gorge himself even fatter on the unmentionable substance in his trough. In terms of size and contents, said trough made the one Max had eaten out of earlier seem like a thimble of fine wine.

                                                        The little pig in his robe found it all repulsive, even as it seemed so alluring; he suddenly noticed how his mouth had watered at the "meal," and how his skin itched for a dip in that soothing mud. Who needed complexity? He could be happy and content with so little... And there was so little he'd have to change... He'd taken two steps before he caught himself with a gasp that was half-squeal. He turned his head sharply, hoping whatever awaited him to the right would at least balance it.
                                                        To the right, Max of the Boar. This, too, nearly overpowered: the stench of sweat and musk, but mingled with incense and perfume from other bodies. A boar in the shape of a grown man, brown bristles coating a well-muscled and exceptionally athletic build, with a large and wicked set of tusks. The boar-man dressed in loose robes of exquisite threading, and sat in a grand palace at the head of a golden table. The table offered a meal of tantalizing food, a rich meal shared with persons of power and prestige, though clearly less than the boar's. Beautiful women of every exotic description attended upon him, perhaps finding his company pay enough. The wealth, the respect, the affections -- the boar had earned it all through his power and skill alone.

                                                        Max drank in the heady smell of power. He found that he wanted it. How could he have ever thought of contenting himself with slop and a small sty? No, no, the boar? That was who he wanted to be. The little piglet could scarcely see a bristle of his weak, pathetic self in that future. Nothing to fear from anyone, nothing but the finest for him, and nothing but the desire to be or to be with him for all others. Max gave a grin, barely visible tusks growing more prominent. Bristles browned with every eager step he took. 'Yeah... Just like back at the pond. The boar was so powerful! And that was just a taste! It could be like that all the time. I could be that all the time! Nate was right, I could...!'

                                                        The Pig stopped suddenly, tusks and bristles receding in their growth, as the memory of hurting Nate broke the spell. And the fury and rage he felt in doing it. With a sniffle, the vision continued, with an acrid scent and something like iron.
                                                        One of the boar-king's attendants tried to feed him a grape, but was caught unawares when he turned his head suddenly. She cut herself on his tusks, and some of her blood spilled onto his robes. His eyes glinted in fury at her and he easily broke her unbloodied arm. This brutality earned gasps or screams from all assembled, including an outraged shout from a lordling across the table. After very little angry discourse, the boar-king cleared the table in a leap, swiping an ugly gash into the guest as he went, before flinging the young man roughly to the ground. Before the plates, platters, and chairs he had scattered settled down, the poor youth was very still. All others in attendance now began shouting protest all at once, drawing their weapons. The boar silenced them with a great bellow that shook the room. They were cowed, but no, not good enough. Particularly as one of his favored art pieces fell from where it rested and shattered. With another cry of fury at what they had made him do, the boar-king hefted up the great table itself and broke it in the course of breaking everyone in the room. Servants and soldiers had come at the commotion, gawked at the scene, and then attacked the Boar. Why wouldn't they? There was nothing of the lord of the palace in him.

                                                        ...

                                                        Hours later, the Boar stood on a hilltop, composing himself. He was covered in wounds that would have instantly felled lesser creatures, as the flaming and crumbling shambles of his estate continued their collapse in the distance behind him. The blood-soaked Boar gave it a contemptible snort. He walked off with the easy grace of one who has built and destroyed a life of splendor for himself many times, with no aim to change.

                                                        Max felt quite sick and shaken. Just a few more steps would have brought him through to the path of the Boar. Had his wits been more about him, perhaps he might have reasoned his way around it. Perhaps he might have cleverly thought up some manner of third option. All he knew was that a choice between the two destinies was no choice for him at all. Max gave Libra a nod, and walked past her, through into the path of the Pig.
