    The keeper's words about the contract only allowing the figurines to work on the customers who bought them rolled around in Jessica's mind. It seemed to suggest that the keeper had limitations, or at least rules he deigned to recognize. She wondered if that could be used somehow. Slowly, a plan stated to take shape in her mind. She had no idea if it would work, or make things worse. For her plan to work, she would need Max's help.

    Later, Jessica took Max aside under a towering tree. "Max, I might have a plan to get us out of this. But it might it not. In fact, it might make matters worse." Jessica went on to remind Max how the keeper had said that the figurines only would work on the first customers who bought them. She then explained that it occurred to her that they might sell each other their figurines and then resell them back to each other. Jessica said that she hoped that this would make them the third rather the first owners of their respective figurines. She concluded, "Then, if the keeper is telling the truth and my reasoning is sound, our figurines will no longer have any power over us. Of course, I could be totally wrong. Maybe being the third owner or customer does nothing to change that we were the first ones. Maybe I'm seeing hope where there is none. It might well be doing this will do nothing but make the keeper mad at us for trying to escape. What do you think, Max?" .

        Nate had been silent during their change of scenery. In fact, he had been a loner for days. Like all the zodiacs, he had freaked out when he woke up in the cage, and like almost all of them, he got to experience the Keeper's punishments for rebellion. He had tried to avoid speaking to the other Beasts. He didn't know why, but he felt uneasy conversing with the Dragon, the Snake or the Tiger. As for the Rooster or the Rabbit...well, they were too ridiculous to take them seriously. He liked talking with the Horse, it felt natural to him. A shame he disappeared.

        The last few days, he had watched the Oxen and the Pig. He was somewhat happy to have been born under the sign of the Ram, at least he didn't become fat with a curly tail and a pink skin. However, he felt that his animal side called out to them. Must be this domesticated animal kinship or something. It was easy to ignore this call when they were imprisoned, but now that they were on the ranch, well... Sheeps are social animals, aren't they?

        So when Jessica and Max went alone under a tree, he decided to follow them, and called them out. "Hey guys. What are you doing here alone? Hope you're not planning to escape. We all saw how well it went last time."

                After hearing Jessica's plan, Max raised a hand to his chin, thinking it over. He was grateful to discuss some way of getting out; he hadn't come up with any good ideas himself. Still, the trip to the Slaughterhouse and the other threats had him very nervous about trying anything. "That's... actually a really clever idea. I just don't know if--" His ears pricked up at the sound of cloven hooves heading toward them through the grass. He put on a smile as he turned to face the Sheep, catching Jessica's expression as he did so. The young man got the picture. Still, he wondered at any ability they had to keep a discussion truly to themselves: the "ranch" wasn't the biggest of places, and if any other of the bestial prisoners' ears were half as sharp as his own -- and he was sure they were -- then "private conversation" was sort of a theoretical concept.

                "We definitely don't want to go through... that again," Max said with a shudder. "Or worse." He followed Jessica's offered handshake and introduction with his own.

                "Sorry we're just meeting for the first time. I mean, if we're all in this together, we should at least get to know each other. Especially since it's been..." Max paused, remembering how that train of thought went nowhere, "... Well, however long it's been." He made himself smile despite the reminder, offering a shrug. "Not much else to do here but wander around, sleep, or chat, anyway." This was mostly true. The student had done quite a bit of all three, but had also tried to get a little privacy now and then to keep his skills sharp. His body was still very unfamiliar, but he'd made great strides in adapting his martial arts training to its needs. These efforts stemmed in part from not wanting those skills to atrophy and knowing he'd need every advantage he could get. Well, and a bit of vanity: Nate's assessment of "fat" might have been a bit much, but Max's porcine transformation had put some pounds on him. Pounds he hoped to shed.

                    He shook their hands and flashed a smile. "The name's Nate. And you're right, you don't want to go through that again. We can't escape." Memories of his own tortures flashed back in his mind, and he tried to do his best to look confident in front of the two. He remembered the Keeper tying him up with a prickled chain and leading him to some sort of stock-market, where he had waited in the mud and the filth with other animals for god knows how long. He remembered potential buyers examining him unceremoniously, looking at his teeth, his ears, and all that made him part goat. He remembered being bought and brought to a slaughterhouse, where he had being flayed alive, his skin destined to be leather, before he got chopped up and died... Only to wake up in the same pen with the same mud and the same animals, over and over.
                    Yeah. He didn't want to remember that, thank you very much, brain.

                    "Listen up, you two. When we were in our cages, we each had our problems, but the Keeper kept us separated. Now that we can properly interact with each other, I'm pretty sure he's gonna switch to group punishment, and none of us want to get hurt by your fault. You already did that to your cages, but they were people! So don't try anything weird, and be nice Beasts for the Keeper, alright? And after the Horse, I'm the next one to go out, so you'd better not screw me up."

                            The Sheep certainly wasn't very... well, sheepish. Max didn't know what had happened to Nate when the Keeper had taken him away, but made the safe assumption of "torment." He understood where Nate was coming from. He himself waffled between trying for a way out and simply trying to minimize his own suffering -- and those of others, as the horned man noted. Max wasn't so sold on their Keeper employing group punishment; he wasn't sure the horrible thing that had taken them understood people well enough for that. But given what had become of their cages, it might amount to the same thing. "Hmm... And even if he doesn't, if he thinks someone should have stopped an escape attempt, that someone's gonna be in just as much hot water..."

                            Still, Max did have an issue with Nate's reasoning -- aside from how much the idea of letting the Keeper win made his hackles rise. "Why do you think the Horse is doing better than any of us? I mean, yeah, maybe the day-to-day treatment is different, but the Keeper's been very consistent: he sees us as collectibles and doesn't care about us being happy." He took a breath to keep himself calm, but got swept away a bit in the rant. "That just doesn't mean we get to leave for it, it just means we're not right here. Having it be 'your' year could just mean you sit in a display case. Or maybe you spend more one-on-one time around the Keeper. What a treat. I'm sure none of us would stumble into any little rules to break."

                            Still, the rant brought Max to a helpful place. "Which, when you get down to it, is the whole problem: we don't know the rules. We picked our pieces for this game and didn't even know there was a game. And we still don't know most of the rules, but at least we know there are rules. We need to know if there's more to that contract. Or more to how this whole place works than just 'the whim of the Keeper.'"

                                        The creature who had been noticed by Jessica was not the Tiger she had assumed at first. It was, however, a fairly large cat, and as it began to make it's presence noticed, it was clear the creature was a hybrid animal like them. A Lion, to be precise, a fierce and regal looking cat man who displayed an heir of nobility. Judging from his appearance, he was stark naked, but everything below the waist was cleverly hidden behind the leaves of one of the fluorescent bushes he had been crouched under. Regardless, the muscle tone on his arms and torso was clear indication he was at least twice as strong as any of them if it came to a fight.


                                        "Ah, so he's moved onto the Chinese Zodiac?" the lion asked nobody in particular. As he leered at the trio of farm animals, he licked his lips as if savoring the moment, before regaining his composure. "I'm a member of the western Zodiac, I represent Leo." he introduced himself, "And I couldn't help but overhear you're trying to escape." he grinned, flashing those long, predatory teeth.
