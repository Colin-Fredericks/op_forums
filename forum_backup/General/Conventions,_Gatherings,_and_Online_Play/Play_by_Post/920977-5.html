            Background

            1. Character concept/archetype: Small bookstore owner with the perfect suburban life.

            Success is funny. It can build up in a family line, creating conditions that make success more and more likely. Building up a wealth that doesn’t provide excess so much as it allows freedom from financial woes. But that kind of comfort can inhibit success, too. When Marlin followed his brother and sister into college, he didn’t come out a doctor or a lawyer (like they did, respectively), he barely scraped through. Not because he got bad grades, or didn’t take enough classes, but because swapping majors 5 times in 3 years isn’t exactly an optimal path. Marlin was simply interested in everything. Eventually his counselor got him to stick to his half-finished lit degree, just so he would graduate with something.

            Not that a degree really mattered to Marlin. He knew that with careful management, the family trust would put him through life at an acceptable level of comfort. No, what he got out of college was Daisy. They married shortly after graduation, then he got a job as a paper pusher to put her through library school. She got a real job (at the Chicago Public) and he dropped his. He opened a small bookstore for something to do. They moved to the suburbs and had a son.

            2. Awakening:

            For the past couple years, Marlin gradually became interested in the occult. At first as a curiosity, then as an affectation. He kept occult books in a back room, locked it up, and told spooky stories to customers. His growing fascination with the culture surrounding magic only grew as he attracted more of that type to the store, though he certainly didn’t believe. He conceived of performing a ritual of sorts, inviting select customers, making an event of it. “It’s like performance art,” he said to himself. He didn’t tell Daisy; she wasn’t interested in or as comfortable with this stuff.

            Marlin prepared for a trial run. He didn’t want to disappoint anyone when performing the ritual for real. He got the silver candlesticks, the ritual robes, drew a pentagram on the floor of the store’s upstairs room. He read from the book, performed the ritual gestures, and enjoyed himself immensely. Until the demon appeared.

            The room filled with dark fog. Red eyes peered out from the shadows. Marlin dropped the book, staggered back.

            “You dare disturb me, mortal?” Its voice was hostile and corrosive. “You meddle with things you do not understand! I should destroy you for your offensive ignorance!”

            Marlin swallowed, reeling with fear and revelation. “I-I-“

            The demon sneered, and Marlin felt clawed hands grasp at his limbs.

            “I-If my ignorance is so offensive, then teach me! Help me to understand!”

            The demon roared with gales of laughter. “With pleasure!” The fog retreated into the summoning circle, and pulled Marlin with.

            In the Iron Gauntlet, Marlin was forced to confront his flaws. His complacency, his lack of direction, and despite all his knowledge, his lack of understanding. Despite that, when all was done, he mounted the stairs of the Watchtower and signed his name. When he awoke, the candles were out. And the book was gone.

            Since then, Marlin has been struggling to balance the family man with the warlock. He uses Mind magic to stay awake and sneak out at night, and has been leaving the shop closed more days. Despite his efforts, Daisy has noticed him growing distant. He’s terrified that one day he might decide that using magic on the people he’s closest to is acceptable.

            (Notes: The son’s name is Forest, and he’s 7. The store is called Marlin’s Books and the logo is of a swordfish. Marlin’s shadow name is Binder. It’s up to the ST as to whether he actually got his hands on a magic book that let him summon a Wraith or if it was just a figment of his Awakening!)

                                        Anyway here's my character's Acanthus Awakening, crunch to come soon(TM):

                                        For the past twenty one years, James' life had been mundane to say the least. Wake up, go to school, come home, sleep. Wake up, go to school, come home, sleep. The same song every single day, with only slight variations in the tune. Once he got a job he thought he would finally get out of this rut but life was not to be so. It then became wake up, go to work, come home, sleep. The lyrics changed but the song remained the same.

                                        But even rivers eventually are redirected by an impediment and thus change their millennia long course. So too was it for James and his lifelong rut on one fateful day when the boss brought in a new painting for his office. It was an ugly painting all things considered, and he couldn't have spent more than half a grand on it but nevertheless it was the talk of the office for a week. But one by one James' coworkers lost interest and went back to their daily grind. But James couldn't get the painting out of his head, not matter how best he tried. He lay awake at night thinking of its rough contours, its pale hue, and its damnable blue eyes.

                                        Just by its appearance had James' song been altered, now it was go to work, watch the painting, come home and repeat. Over the coming weeks he became gradually more haggard and only for a few minutes would he allow himself sleep. Even in his dreams had been infected by the painting, as he drifted through short slivers of sleep he was enveloped by the painting. Its silver-painted frame glistened in the void of his slumber and sung to him. It promised him all would be right, if only he would free it from his philistine boss.

                                        Eventually even his coworkers noticed his quickly slipping sanity, his tirelessness showing in every facet of his life, and began to drift away from him. But he didn't care, no soon he would have the painting. Yes he had come to that conclusion last night amid a short and restless slumber. Tonight, he would be the last to leave and when all others were gone he would free the painting. Time crawled and James could feel everyones eyes on him. They knew, they all knew! If only the clock were to go more quickly, then he could just free the painting and run away from this grey rut.

                                        Finally the hour had come and James was the last person in the office. He slowly moved towards his boss' office, the clicks of his shoes against the concrete continually echoed through the building filling him with heart-stopping fear. He would be found, of course he would, but it didn't matter now. All that mattered was the painting, he needed it, he must have it in his hands. And so he trudged forward, heedless of the sound of his feet slamming into the concrete floor.

                                        As he creeped into his boss' office, his heart was filled with joy. There it was, with the moonlight softly falling upon it and illuminating its pale blue eyes. His hands hovered over the painting for a moment, almost worshiping its grotesque form but a moment was all that remained between him and his prize. In an instant he finally held it and swiftly retreated to adorn his meager abode with his prize. That night was pure bliss and such a high unknown to even the most prolific of junkies.

                                        But like all highs, so too comes the following low. The next day his boss was furious, demanding however stole his painting return it immediately or they would be prosecuted to the full extent of the law. One by one his coworkers were interrogated, and with each name called, James' heart beat a furious drum solo. By some stroke of luck, the day ended before his name could be called. In a frantic flight, James returned to his home. He stared at the painting with hate in his eyes. It was all its fault his life was ruined, it had done this to him. James vision filled with red and when it cleared, he saw the painting in shambles. Its silver limbs splayed across his apartment while its soft hide was ripped into a billion pieces. Yet still its eyes gazed as him, imploring his mind to continue to look upon it in its malefic glory. James broke down and when he woke, the sun had rose and his cheeks stained by tears.

                                        As he strode to work, he felt as if he were a prisoner on Death Row coming to meet his chair. Today would be the end of him, he knew it. As he reached work, he tried to fall back into his rut but found he could not, his mind still filled with visions of the painting and its army of ugly blue eyes. Eventually his name was called and he felt his blood turn cold. Slowly he began the walk to his doom, his steps mirroring those he made on that fateful night.

                                        As he passed the boundary into his boss' office though, he found that was not where he was. Instead he found himself in a eerie world, silent and yet deafening in equal measure. Movement moved like quicksilver but also left afterimages of the movement, time struggling to keep up with sight. He found himself of a glass road fenced in by a hedge of thorn made of silver. Seeing a large figure in the distance, he knew his destination and began moving forward along the path. Gradually more colors joined the the silver fence, blue and white joined in equal measure with black began to paint the barrier and as he got closer to his destination he saw wood join the metallic hedge.

                                        At last he reached his destination, a towering figure whose wicked eyes had haunted him for far too long. He became enraged at its sight and, looking to strike it once again, found a glass dagger with which to stab at it. He carved into the tower his name, announcing he finally rejected the painting. It no longer held sway over him, nor would a rut ever again confine him to such a sorry fate. This he swore with his name, this and more. Upon leaving that strange place, he clothed himself in the name Zosimus, for he found its tune flowed more sweetly and true.

                                                    Background

                                                    Childhood & Adolescence - Oscar was born in Norway, Europe. His father was a marine biologist, his mother a lawyer. His upbringing was simple enough. His parents were engrossed in their careers, so he was often left to his own devices; as a child, this involved after-school activities, television shows, books and video games, the earlier years spent with a baby sitter; as an adolescent, it involved staunch rebellion, and the lack of attention had him run away from home at the age of 17.

                                                    By ran away, of course, we mean he ran to the nearest forest of considerable size and got lost.

                                                    He wandered between trees and up hills, and eventually found himself atop the regional mountain range. At first, he thought nothing of it. Woods and lakes joined with foliage, moss lingered where the shade lay. It was when the mist settled that he felt it – the jolt of fear. The wild had its aural environment, of course, but in the mist, it grew more prominent. He started seeing shadows skulking about, felt eyes on him where there were none. Yet the fear was not unwanted – it was an exhilaration. As the tension rose, he saw the greatest shadow yet before him, its eyes set on him like predator hunting prey. In spite of the thrill, Oscar ran away then – his metaphorical tail between his legs.

                                                    He returned home the next morning. His parents chalked his absence to “just teenage things.”

                                                    The event had Oscar filled with longing, an ache he couldn't quite put his finger on. It felt like a second world existed, somewhere just out of sight. His parents and peers dismissed his thoughts and ideas whenever he brought this up, and with that, he found only solace in solitude.


                                                    Awakening - His Mystery Play had begun in the forest, but it was slow and subtle. The Supernal had trickled into his life, little by little, strengthening his sense to the “second world”, but as the repeated dismissal of his experiences by those he deemed closest to him, the trickle slowed down to a halt. At last, he felt empty.
                                                    It was merely the interlude to the events of his 22st year.

                                                    By the time Oscar was 21, his daily life was not particularly productive. Sure, he stayed in shape with frequent trips up the mountains in hopes of recapturing that feeling; he practised a little bit of art and drawing to try to remember what he had seen and felt.. He held a couple of part-time jobs, though he half-arsed his way through those without exceptional results. His academic ambitions weren't anything to brag about either. He tried to maintain some spiritualistic aspect in his life – but the emptiness had crawled into the entirety of his being. Nothing he did mattered.

                                                    One day, though, he woke up, having had a dream that he couldn't truly explain – a dream so familiar it stirred a sense of wanderlust. During one of the rare moments his family was all gathered for breakfast, he mentioned he'd like to study abroad. Though surprised, both parents were in favour, offering to pay his tuition and rent – on the condition that he later produce results. They stated the experience would serve him well. He applied to a variety of schools all around the states, landing a college close to La Grange.

                                                    The dam that kept the Supernal truth at bay burst when he landed on US soil. What would've been a simple bus trip from the airport to his apartment in La Grange became a dangerous trek through the Primal Wild. It was an encore of the fear and longing that had overwhelmed him four years ago, only this time he did not freeze when the mist washed over him, nor did he flee when he came face to face with the large and advancing shadow. When they stood face to face, he saw the shadow for what it was: the great Lynx. Without thinking Oscar embraced it as an old friend that he had known for his entire life. He embraced the Wild. He became the Lynx. The Lynx became him. At last, he was Truth. At last, he truly felt                                                     alive.

                                                    Personality and Daily Life
                                                    Though Lynx often puts on the smile – and usually he means it – he is ruled by emotion and instinct. He can be calm and (rarely) prudent, but he prefers to let loose whenever possible. He'll exclaim excitement if the situation calls for it, he'll laugh without abandon, and he doesn't care about modesty when it comes to the human body. He doesn't hide himself, and he doesn't like lying either – whatever his mood, he is easy to read.

                                                    He isn't confrontational by nature, but he is quick to judge – he measures people by how they treat their tools, their pets, their equals and lessers, and the world around them. Regardless of how he judges others, his opinion is rarely set in stone. Dislike is met with avoidance – why deal with something if you don't like it?

                                                    Lynx struggles to express himself. To him, the truth's with his gut feeling – and he can rarely justify it further than that. Then again, he was never great at analysis.

                                                    Having a lawyer and marine biologist as parents has left Lynx with an understanding of multiple topics. Many of his hobbies – none of them truly enjoyed during his darker days – are emerging again. He reads – mostly fiction – and doodles; he'll wander around the suburbs and hike through whatever woods can be found. He can find enjoyment laying spread eagle on grass, staring at the sky. The local communities have him fascinated, and he'll mingle every now and again. He visits the zoo at least once a week. H usually studies with his study group, though sometimes he prefers to do it alone, usually at the library.

                                                    Other notes

                                                    His relationship to his parents is strained. They focused more on furthering their own careers, rather than family life. They weren't really there for him - sure, they had vacations and the occasional family outing, fancy dinner at restaurant, and he does love them, but there's a definite distance between them. In many ways, Oscar fears he was a mistake in their carefully planned life, and sometimes it seems like they would love nothing more than to tell him that. They do care, though, as they had no hesitation to let him study abroad while paying for his tuition and rent. He might have a sibling or two. Currently undecided.
