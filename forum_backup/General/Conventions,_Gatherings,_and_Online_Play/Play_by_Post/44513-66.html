                        Something else, hopefully also awesome!

                        It was a beautiful summer Saturnday morning. The sun had just begun to set the east ablaze, but Greheis lacked the usual pre-dawn flurry of activity. By Novaleski tradition, Saturnday was a day of rest and relaxation. Though many in the outlying homesteads still had their daily activities to attend to, in the city, businesses would be left shut, trading paused, and the temples closed. It was a time for the populace to engage in their hobbies. It was a time for family.

                        Which is why Ptreas Artemeon Derontikles, direct descendant of Ptreas, scion of wealth and privilege, was busy climbing over the walls of his family’s compound.

                        The walls were short and made of roughly hewn stone, making it easy for the scrawny fourteen-year-old to find handholds and pull himself over the short wall. The wall was little more than a stone fence, serving the sole purpose of delineating the property of the Ptreas household from the rest of the city and the Gens.

                        His escape was timed perfectly. The small handful of guards on duty had passed by in their rounds and wouldn’t be back in at least a minute. Plenty of time for him to get over the wall and hide in the recesses of the nearby meeting-hall.

                        She won’t get me this time, Dero thought as he cleared the edge of the wall and fell onto the streets below.

                        The sun sat at it’s zenith as Dero munched on his breakfast. He’d had a couple of close calls throughout the morning. Belisana and her dogs had almost caught him at the public baths, but luckily he’d not counted on somewhat prudish Novaleski social norms to keep her out of the men’s baths.

                        After ducking into the private staff area, applying liberal amounts of mildly caustic soap and changing into a spare suit of low-class clothing that he had dirtied up for such an occasion, the dogs had lost his scent. From there he’d made his way to his current perch on a massive warehouse on the waterfront, pilfering a bag of soft cheese and a roll of flatbread along the way.

                        There’s nothing right about stealing, Dero thought, Not ever. He’d left a half-dozen sesterces behind, slipped innocuously into the pockets of those he’d robbed. I’m not here to make a quick dinar.

                        Dero finished his meal and laid back on the sun-warmed brick roofing. As strange as it was, Dero kind of enjoyed these days. Sure, Belisana tried to chase him down, but over the course of the past couple of years, it had become something of a game. And while his older sister hated him getting away, she took her defeats with grace and poise.

                        Dero snapped his head to the side as something caught his eye. A glint of light, a flashing mote reflected from the hills just north of town.
                        The abandoned quarries? Dero thought. Those once-rich veins of stone were part of the reason that Greheis was located where it was. But they’d run dry years ago, and were now crawling with beastial earth elementals.

                        There it is again, Dero thought as the glinting returned, Something’s going on up there. And I’m going to find out what.

                        Getting out of the city was much more difficult that getting out of the compound. Belisana would be watching the gate, and there was no going over the walls. Unlike the compound walls, these were built by the finest engineers in the nation to fend off roving barbarian tribes and small armies. Dero could find no purchase there. So he had circumvented the issue by slipping into the river and swimming upstream.

                        My second bath today, Dero thought as he dredged himself from the muddy banks of the river, I’ll need at least one more before this is through.

                        It was less than a mile from the city wall to the quarries, but Dero crept there slowly, not wanting to catch the attention of the local wildlife, the local elementals, or whomever or whatever was up there. Staying close to cover as he did, it took him the better part of an hour to make it that far.

                        Reaching the edge of the quarries, Dero spotted movement. A large shape, blackish, was coming in his direction. A bear?, he thought, hiding in an instant. Two more shapes appeared, and as the drew closer, Dero saw they were men. Heavyset men, wearing dark shades of blue, green or grey. Each one was armed with a broad, short blade.

                        Guards, Dero thought, Something’s not right here. I’m going to have to sneak around.

                        Dero crept behind the trio slowly as they made their circuit East. They passed a few checkpoints, each one with a handful of similarly outfitted muscle-bound individuals scanning the landscape. Dero narrowly avoided detection several times, but his skill, paranoia and thick coating of mud served to keep him out of sight. Another patrol passed them making a clockwise circuit, and Dero knew he had his opportunity. Mere moments after the second patrol passed, he ducked past the perimeter and into the quarries.

                        Amateurs, Dero thought, they should be more careful.

                        Dero lay prone atop an artificial cliff, a brambly bush keeping the blazing sun off of his back. Despite his grandfather’s wishes, Dero, his cousins and his sisters had spent a great deal of time hiding and playing in these hills. This spot was one of his favorites; the path up looked too steep to climb easily, and the brambles looked deceptively thick. More to the point, it had a good view of the area, including an easy line-of-sight on the only path up.

                        And a good view of the proceedings taking place below him.

                        Two parties were gathered below him. The first was composed primarily of the dark-clothed meatheads he’d slipped by earlier. The second group looked to be made of Novaleski citizen-soldiers. The first group was handing off a number of large crates to the second, and several of the Novaleskis just cracked open one of them to examine the contents. A hooded and robed man reached into the crate and withdrew a short silver-grey blade.

                        Therian weapons!?, Dero thought. Iron-steel weapons were valuable in Novalesk, if only for the value of their metal. So why were they just being handed over, without payment? Had he missed an exchange of currency? Or were there other considerations?

                        The Black Cowl, Dero thought. It explained their peculiar dress, and their easy access to Therian weapons. And the Novaleskis were cultists themselves, survivors of the rebellion Dero’s Uncle Vadik put down a little less than a year ago. Uncle Vadik, who was knifed in a barroom brawl just six weeks ago...

                        Dero’s eyes flashed. I have to let the authorities know, he thought, The city’s in danger.

                        Dero began to climb down from his hiding-spot, but his mind was racing, considering all of the possibilities, all of the implications. Focused on the future, Dero misplaced his foot. He tried to catch himself too late, and lost his already tenuous grip. Dero tumbled down the cliff and passed into unconsciousness.

                        Dero awoke to a powerful, throbbing pain in his arms and side. He winced as he tried to roll over onto his left side, a hot stab of pain piercing him from hip to fingers. His vision, already hazy from the shock, swam.

                        Ribs, cracked. Left arm, broken. Shoulder dislocated, Dero thought, running through his list of injuries, This is serious-

                        A hand reached out and firmly pushed Dero to the ground. He saw a dark figure leaning above him, and he registered the sickly-sweet stench of blood and excrement.

                        Oh, damnation, Dero thought, They’re going to kill me.

                        Dero’s vision came into focus, and he got a good look at the familiar face of the robed figure leaning in above him.

                        OH DAMNATION, Dero thought, HE’S going kill me!

                        “You tumbled into something very dangerous, young man,” the figure said, “but luckily for you, we got what we came for.” Dero’s confusion must have shown in his eyes or expression, as the figure continued, “No, not the weapons. That was just a pleasant bonus. We now have definitive proof the head of House Aloia and her heir are both members of the Black Cowl.”

                        Dero winced, both in pain and in anger at his own stupidity, as he tried to sit up. House Aloia was the most powerful family in the northern Therian territories, the closest thing Gens Ptreas had to blood rivals. If that information became public in Theros, the House would tear itself apart. The resulting power vacuum would quickly settle, but during the chaos, Novalesk would gain a powerful advantage in the north...

                        The figure reached out again and pushed Dero back down, “You understand. Good. Now, one of my men will patch you up well enough to move. Then, you, your Grandfather and I will sit down for a nice long chat. Understood?”

                        Dero could only nod as Ptreas Artemeon Ilias, Scavenger Lord and Spymaster of Gens Ptreas, called over a Novaleski soldier to tend to his son’s wounds.
