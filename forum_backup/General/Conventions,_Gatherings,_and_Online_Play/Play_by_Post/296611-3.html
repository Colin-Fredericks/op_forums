    OK. It'll take hours (or the application of time-dilating effects) to read through both sets of files thoroughly, but here's what you manage to get from a relatively quick scan.

    Mr. Voyczyk is the owner and CEO of a good-sized communication conglomerate. The missing property in question is a basalt gargoyle statue which was taken from Mr. Voyczyk's penthouse apartment half a week previously. None of the security measures in the home (which include alarms on all of the doors and windows and pressure sensors in the floor of the gallery in which the statue was housed) were set off, but there are also no obvious signs that anyone circumvented them, either - the first sign that something was wrong was when the security guy in the building stopped by the bank of monitors and saw the protective case empty. Apparently the video recordings of the room fuzz out with static for about 30 seconds, and when they come back, the case was empty.

    Voyczyk's head of security found no fingerprints and no obvious DNA or fibers. The statue itself weighed several thousand pounds and was encased in a high-tech protective plexiglass case. You notice that there's not a great deal of information on the statue's provenance. It appears to have been from the late medieval period, but there's no documentation of precisely *how* he got it - the obvious assumption is that there was something extralegal involved in its acquisition.

    The redhead disappearances are described in a forum post that can kindly be described as "tinfoil hat chic." The author describes a number of boys just at driving age who disappeared anywhere from the day of their birthday to two months before they turned seventeen. All but one include full names - the second includes only a last name and a reference to a high school in the suburbs where the boy attended school before disappearing. There's enough here to start investigating, but there's not a whole lot to go on immediately.

    ((Give me a quick wits + Investigation each to put together any immediate conclusions about the cases, -2 for the disappearances 'cuz there's not much data there yet. Let me know if you want to use any powers or if there are any circumstances you think should offer a modifier.))

                If you are used to d20 and other heavily gamist and simulationist systems, it will seem like an amazing step forward (at least, when I find people who like it seems like they all have mostly experience with rules-systems that tend to have more minutiae like d20),
