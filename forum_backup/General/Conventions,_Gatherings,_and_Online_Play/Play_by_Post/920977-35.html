                                            I haven't thought about Legacies yet, in that I am still establishing Lynx's character. I'll get there in due time.

                                            Honestly, Legacies were something I never really got to explore or deal much with in 1E. I didn't really understand the purpose or benefit of them and didn't get the chance to learn, because I never had the chance to really play Mage 1E.
                                            Legacies in 1E had two major mechanical advantages. Firstly, their Attainments were often rolled with a dice pool much like a Rote's dice pool, i.e., Attribute + Skill + Arcana; it was also bound to the Fallen World, so Sleepers were not subject to Quiescence, nor would they erode the Attainment. This did mean that if you have a shapeshifting Attainment, you could become a bear in the middle of the streets, and people wouldn't go mad, wouldn't be subject to Breaking Points, and your spell wouldn't diminish - making it a Guardian's nightmare, obviously.

                                            Secondly, they had access to better Oblations than 2E's, in that they regained mana equal to successes, rather than being limited to dots in Ruling arcanum.

                                            As for purpose, it is the mage's Journey and Method used to find the ultimate Truth. The mage shapes her soul into the expression and embodiment of her magical style and beliefs, or integrates the tradition that most resonates with her, if taught an established Legacy. The soul becomes a lens through which the Supernal can be expressed in the Fallen World. In essence, the reason Legacies don't incite Quiescence is that the mage's Attainments are comparable to natural law: it's part of reality, whether or not you like it.

                                            Personally, I'm not a fan of 2E's legacies. They're cleaner, sure, but they also feel more tacked on than in 1E. They feel weaker. I also find converting them to 2E is difficult, personally. I enjoy the reduced Gnosis requirements, but that's about it.

                                            Or maybe I've been doing it all wrong, but y'know. That's my take on Legacies in 1E, as taught by my M:tAw mentor, Lankin, some odd 4 years ago.

                                                            You've got options.
                                                            • You can touch the target.
                                                            • You can "throw" the spell, which requires a reflexive Gnosis+(Athletics or Firearms)-Defense roll immediately after you finish casting the spell. "Aimed Spells" on p.115
