    Trish blushed a little, but tried to hide it. Oh, of course. Swedish. What was I thinking? And so polite. And something of a poet. He's the new band member. Flute, I think?

    She had been listening to a playlist of Utopia's latest music to familiarize herself with their work. The Swedish music scene was so fascinating right now. And it was a welcome escape from all the strange things that had happened to her the past few years. Motorcycle gangs, Yakuza, terrorists, secret societies, UFO truthers, and so on. She was looking forward to writing a story about something that seemed completely safe and not dangerous at all.

    Then she winced a little at the name mistake. It brought up painful memories of her girlhood. Being bullied and teased for her mixed parentage - a Japanese father and American mother. Gaijin. Takenashi. 'Tall tree'. Deru kugi wa utareru.

    "Ta Ke Ha Shi" she said, emphasizing each syllable distinctly. Am I being rude, correcting him? He is Swedish. He won't mind. "I think there is a Takenashi who writes manga. Takehashi means 'high bridge'. It is a very normal, common name in Japan."

    ...already tossed the spoon in the corner? Hmm, its an idiom. The best thing with Swedes is just to be as direct as they are.​

    "I don't think little Rahel here has given up on anything. She is quite insistent on getting to her parents at the Gare du Nord. She says they are going by train to Calais to get on a ship to America. I don't know why they aren't taking a plane. It is all a little strange. And she is so young to be traveling alone. Don't you think we should help?"

    Trish did not wait for Erik to respond, but began walking with long strides to the exit of the station with her luggage rolling behind her. "Come along, Schatzilein. Let's find mami and papa" she said in German to the little girl. The girl gave another little cough, and as she passed Erik, following after Trish, he noticed a slight smell of bitter almond.

    Outside the station, Erik saw Trish and Rahel waiting by the street. A car pulled up quickly. Jorunn was driving. And another person that he didn't recognize was in the front passenger seat. Jorunn rolled down his window and leaned over.

    "Get in idiot! You're being followed," she said in Norwegian.

    Trish turned to look at Erik with a questioning look.

    Chikusho! Here we go again...

                                Artuir stared at the water fountain in the garden out back. There was a statue of a nude woman with a pitcher in hand. From that pitcher flowed a water column. It appeared motionless and were it not for the ripples that echoed where it met the surface of the fountain Artuir might have thought it a glass sculpture. Artuir glanced around at the people preparing for the party. They seemed quite happy. Why shouldn't they be. They had so much. This was the biggest house he'd ever seen. There was so much food from all over the world. There was so much of everything. It was unsettling.

                                He felt that it was wrong for him to be here. Not that they would let him leave. No matter, after tonight, he was gone. He had a quest to attend to after all and he wouldn't be able to fulfill it hanging around here. He'd been watching everyone very closely and he was pretty sure that if he could slip away sometime during tonight's party. Artuir could be quite quiet when the situation called for it and as long as he made it to a decently large crowd of regular folks,sleepers he thought he heard them say, he would be safe. Artuir was pretty sure these people knew more about magic than he did, but they had serious hangups about doing it around people who didn't know about it. He'd figured out that much from his time here. That was his best shot.

                                Artuir glanced, to his left. There was a gardener who didn't seem to be gardening. He was just sitting there but Artuir couldn't shake the feeling that his privacy was being invaded. He began to wander away. Doesn't matter one way or another. I know what I have to do and I aim to do it. That's all there is to it.
