            Jensven wakes in the morning and promptly finds a vendor selling sausages. He purchases two, a spicy one for himself and a not so spicy one for Shadow.             Jensven will try to keep his bearings with where he is going by comparing landmark locations in relation to the Academy grounds. When he loses sight of the academy, he will pick a landmark that he knows he can see the academy from and use that as his point of reference. The Ascending Sun will repeat this process until he is far enough into the woods that he feels safe letting Shadow have full range. The point here is to allow the dog to run and get exercise himself. He trains with his new blade as well as he knows how. The feel of it in his hands is awkward and he intends to change that.

            And so, imaginary enemies appear before him and he strikes, parries, dodges, and defeats. This continues until he is winded and the dog is bored, the sunlight through the trees slanted at a wider angle to the ground than when they started here.

                                                    09-09-2014, 10:00 PM
                                                    Early, in the dark before the sun rises and only the barest wisp of grey paints the eastern horizon, Yi woke and made his way through the emptied streets to the academy of sorcery. He was alone, as he had been another night. The pale light cast its glow over the silent city as the moon had done in a cell of undressed stone. The tiny flashes were but dew now, not salt and silver and sulfur to hold a man-shaped demon fast. No crowd followed him, no peers shot him scornful glances, no students watched him with envy in their eyes, no young terrestrial prodigy's gaze briefly touched him. If there were eyes in this almost-night they were unfriendly, the fear and judgement behind them echoing around a single work; Anathema. He was not a sorcerer-engineer anymore, if he had ever deserved the title, nor was he a diplomat accustom to the halls of power, and if he was mistaken for a Lookshyan more frequently than ever before it was not to his benefit. His feet carried him on.


                                                    There was something oddly comforting about returning to an institution of learning. He had spent most of his life in one. Even if this lacked the prestige, it indeed it did, the academic life was... structured in a way he was familiar with as an old and comfortable chair. It hadn't always been so. Once he imagined his purpose in life was to rise through the ranks of the intelligentsia like a wargod's spear, shattering their assumptions and preconceptions like glass until all marveled as his genius. Sifu Zhu had been less than impressed. Explaining a single koan does not earn the master's suffix, and the grandson of Paisap had earned his a dozen times in twice a mortal's span. There were times he had imagined it was punishment He had been forced to translate and re-translate the Book of the Red Names by hand more than twenty times. Every time he finished he would present it to the Master for approval only be be berated for wasting good paper with his useless words. Once, for that was all it had taken, he complained about the Master's own error at wasting his time with such a long surpassed tome. When the Master had taken down a dusty old copy of the Book, from his own days as a novice, and let the young Yi read what was within the boy had nearly wept. In words neither poetry nor prose, neither opaque nor trivial, neither critical nor convinced Zhu had put in mere ink and parchment such wonders as the boy had never imagined. That was the day he had truly begun to learn.


                                                    The distance was not too great and the demon at the gate knew him. Perhaps some queer expression on his face convinced it not to question his errand, for it was silent. His bare feet, soles toughened by long travel, led him to the tower of sorcery. The grass was a softness he had learned to savor after the unpaved roads of the scavenger lands. His prison sandals had given out a month after his escape and he had neither the courage to steal any more or the time and materials with which to make his own. His food had run out not long before. As that day had grown hotter he had grown weaker until finally he collapsed before some temple or another. The irony was not lost on him, the vision on that day was far too fresh. So he had prayed. It was the first time he had really prayed, done more than offer ritual formality. He was breathless as he lay in the road, crying out to Sol for another chance and Sextes Jylis to strengthen him, and every god and goddess he could think of for just one more day. Nearly exhausted he spoke one final prayer, crying out to an unknown god. "Raise me up, and I will raise your temples in every city I see in all the days I have been granted." The priests of Asclepius, whose temple it was, emerged to investigate a commotion they heard they saw the sacred snake of their god defending his unconscious body from those who had meant to rob or kill him. Taking it as a sign they cared for him until he had the strength to leave. So far he had done so in all cities he passed save two. Asclepius would have his temples though, in every city in Novalesk, Ordon, Theros, and all the nations he would bind. He did not forget his promises.

                                                    As he reached the tower of sorcery he knelt in what he hoped was a pose of sufficient humility. He knew the stations as all in his position would and he had come to begin the training of a sorcerer. The learning would be easy, the journey to the war front should be sufficient for a journey. Yet what about fear? He had been afraid many times in his life, but nothing had shaken him like the day of the battle, the day he was Chosen. When the young terrestrial had fallen something in him had broken. Lookshy had never loved him, perhaps one or another of its people had though he had not noticed. Yet this one dragon blooded, of high birth and station who should have represented everything he hated about the city, made it bearable. For many agonizing moments he had been paralyzed with fear and indecision watching the dead throw back the honor guard. Then he felt the change. Orders be damned, the rest of the army be damned, he be damned; Karal Zhan would not die in something as meaningless as this skirmish. The glow of his anima may or may not have begun then, though who could say in the high noon, but the waking vision did. It was another place and time, and he was robed in splendor, yet in both he gave the same order. In both he took up a fallen blade. In both he heard the VOICE. It was like his father, who he did not remember, and like Sifu Zhu and Zhan and many more yet possessing a unity and identity greater than any he could compare it too. He had not been afraid then.

                                                    As his contemplation finished he was left with a strange thought. He saw as clearly as he had that day the crown of many kingdoms on his head as he stood before Zhan and the High Command and offered them an alliance of power and glory. Filled with ambition for power, want of strength, and not a little fear they would say, "Well done soldier. 'Come, and take your deserved place among us.' and Zhan would smile and finish, 'It is good to have you home again'." But then he stopped and looked, truly looked, around him; and he remembered. He remembered Origenes and Larissa and Eugene and the little circle of followers around him. They were not pieces on the gods board, or bribes to earn him back the favor of distant men. They were there own people with lives of their own and hopes and dreams just as worthy as his. He could not use them that way, not even if it meant gaining every thing he had thought he wanted. That was when the dawn came thundering and the ground shook and a loud crack ran from the step where his forehead had rested up to the very top of the tower, a sliver of light illuminating him through it. With sure steps he ascended the stops now, meeting those who had been within at the threshold.

                                                    "I am a sorcerer come to learn. Will any here teach me?"

Essence tokens from an earth-aspected demesne, earth-aspected hearthstone, token blessed by a powerful Earth-elemental, that sort of thing.

                                                            Jensven keeps his blade held down and to his side ready to come up at any moment to block an incoming strike. He moves slowly, neither circling his opponent or making an effort to approach. Finding the best spot to gain a defensive stance and then counter is foremost on his mind.

                                                            In the blink of an eye he has somehow assessed the entire clearing and taken note of the positions of every fallen branch, every exposed root, every shrub and weed. Anything that could hinder his movement is noted and potential avenues of assault, retreat, and general ebb and flow sorted and prioritized.

                                                            Despite the trust that he has in Shadow to move should the duel move in that direction, the Dawn also keeps a thought in the back of his mind concerning where the familiar is. Should the mysterious warrior move in that direction, multiple intercepting paths of movement are noted and prepared to be taken.

                                                            All that to say Guard. Guard Like a foo.
