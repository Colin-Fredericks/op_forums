                            Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                            Ok- let's think a bit.

                            the story told by the Freehold is the Fall of Iblis. As such, they see their Escape as being "Banished" from Jannah for not fulfilling their role in the story. The current Iblis and the Emir of the Freehold is the last changeling to be banished (that is, the last to return from the Hedge). It also means the current ruler is the least experienced one, and because of all the expats the Freehold must make sure no one shows up which breaks that Bulwark. The new Iblis must defy the older one and demand the throne for himself. The protection of the Freehold is always said to not be eternal, but only until "Judgement Day" would come. The definition for what a "Judgement Day" is could be either open ended or strictly detailed, with around 1000 different terms and conditions.

                            thoughts?
                            I really like this idea. What cool is that it also mirrors Dubai's history; the royal family was originally cousins of Abu Dhabi's royal family that broke away for form their own community. The act of banishment mirrors the story of Iblis' fall, but the return from the Empty Quarter to take the role of Emir mirrors the founding of Dubai itself. More thoughts coming, I think I've got a bead on the Bulwark.

                                Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                                Maybe it could be a "scapegoat" style Bulwark? That is, one of the Changelings in the Court must defy the current Monarch (Emir) and be banished away, which should put it in danger to the attacks of an Huntsmen. In that case, either she needs to survive in the role of Shaitan a certain amount of time or- if we want something a bit darker- get killed, and the Bulwark is broken if she survives and returns to Court. Considering the "either filthy rich or lowly poor" atmosphere, those who take the role of Iblis are usually from society's rock bottom.

                                Too dark, perhaps?
                                I'd almost tweek it like this: The Shaitan gets dumped in the Empty Quarter and has to make her/his way back and claim the Emir's role within an allotted time. The twist is that one must volunteer to be the Shaitan. The Bulwark fails if no one volunteers or if the Shaitan refuses to claim the Emir's role after defeating the Emir (whatever that looks like). And it is the Emir who gets the final say as to which volunteer becomes the next Shaitan.

                                Thoughts? I see lots of potential for politicing in this situation, and I think I have a mythology that makes the choice of this type of Bulwark make sense.
