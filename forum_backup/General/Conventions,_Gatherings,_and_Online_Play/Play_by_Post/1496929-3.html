                She thinks a moment, then bursts into activity, scrounging like a madwoman through books upon books upon books, til she at last holds one high, and shouts, “I’ve got it!”, before muttering to herself and tossing the tome away. After a few minutes of searching, she pulls out a very thin book. “Here we go,” she whispers.

                Bluefingers holds up the book. Written in the year 1910, it compiled various Native American myths. Bluefingers flips through the pages, then stops at a specific part. “‘Kin of Tar’? Hmmm. According to this account from the west coast, several tribes believes in water-demons that took wolf form. They worshipped an even greater demon, these hellwolves, and would rise up at night and whisper horrible truths into the ears of the sleeping.” She slams the book shut.

                “That’s it,” Bluefingers says apologetically. “We’re in Montana, boys, pretty far from the ocean. You should be lucky I could even dig up this much.

                “However...it’s said these seawolves kept great stone markers buried under the tide. Dunno if that helps.”
