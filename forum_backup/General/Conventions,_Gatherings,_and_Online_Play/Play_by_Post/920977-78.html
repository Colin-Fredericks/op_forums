            Background

            Gist of the background is the same, but honestly am not really happy about it in its entirety. Will rewrite a bit later.

            Childhood & Adolescence             - Oscar was born in Norway, Europe. His father was a marine biologist, his mother a lawyer. His upbringing was simple enough. His parents were engrossed in their careers, so he was often left to his own devices; as a child, this involved after-school activities, television shows, books and video games, the earlier years spent with a baby sitter; as an adolescent, it involved staunch rebellion, and the lack of attention had him run away from home at the age of 17.

            By ran away, of course, we mean he ran to the nearest forest of considerable size and got lost.

            He wandered between trees and up hills, and eventually found himself atop the regional mountain range. At first, he thought nothing of it. Woods and lakes joined with foliage, moss lingered where the shade lay. It was when the mist settled that he felt it – the jolt of fear. The wild had its aural environment, of course, but in the mist, it grew more prominent. He started seeing shadows skulking about, felt eyes on him where there were none. Yet the fear was not unwanted – it was an exhilaration. As the tension rose, he saw the greatest shadow yet before him, its eyes set on him like predator hunting prey. In spite of the thrill, Oscar ran away then – his metaphorical tail between his legs.

            He returned home the next morning. His parents chalked his absence to “just teenage things.”

            The event had Oscar filled with longing, an ache he couldn't quite put his finger on. It felt like a second world existed, somewhere just out of sight. His parents and peers dismissed his thoughts and ideas whenever he brought this up, and with that, he found only solace in solitude.


            Awakening - His Mystery Play had begun in the forest, but it was slow and subtle. The Supernal had trickled into his life, little by little, strengthening his sense to the “second world”, but as the repeated dismissal of his experiences by those he deemed closest to him, the trickle slowed down to a halt. At last, he felt empty.
            It was merely the interlude to the events of his 22st year.

            By the time Oscar was 21, his daily life was not particularly productive. Sure, he stayed in shape with frequent trips up the mountains in hopes of recapturing that feeling; he practised a little bit of art and drawing to try to remember what he had seen and felt.. He held a couple of part-time jobs, though he half-arsed his way through those without exceptional results. His academic ambitions weren't anything to brag about either. He tried to maintain some spiritualistic aspect in his life – but the emptiness had crawled into the entirety of his being. Nothing he did mattered.

            One day, though, he woke up, having had a dream that he couldn't truly explain – a dream so familiar it stirred a sense of wanderlust. During one of the rare moments his family was all gathered for breakfast, he mentioned he'd like to study abroad. Though surprised, both parents were in favour, offering to pay his tuition and rent – on the condition that he later produce results. They stated the experience would serve him well. He applied to a variety of schools all around the states, landing a college close to La Grange.

            The dam that kept the Supernal truth at bay burst when he landed on US soil. What would've been a simple bus trip from the airport to his apartment in La Grange became a dangerous trek through the Primal Wild. It was an encore of the fear and longing that had overwhelmed him four years ago, only this time he did not freeze when the mist washed over him, nor did he flee when he came face to face with the large and advancing shadow. When they stood face to face, he saw the shadow for what it was: the great Lynx. Without thinking Oscar embraced it as an old friend that he had known for his entire life. He embraced the Wild. He became the Lynx. The Lynx became him. At last, he was Truth. At last, he truly felt             alive.

            Personality and Daily Life
            Though Lynx often puts on the smile – and usually he means it – he is ruled by emotion and instinct. He can be calm and (rarely) prudent, but he prefers to let loose whenever possible. He'll exclaim excitement if the situation calls for it, he'll laugh without abandon, and he doesn't care about modesty when it comes to the human body. He doesn't hide himself, and he doesn't like lying either – whatever his mood, he is easy to read.

            He isn't confrontational by nature, but he is quick to judge – he measures people by how they treat their tools, their pets, their equals and lessers, and the world around them. Regardless of how he judges others, his opinion is rarely set in stone. Dislike is met with avoidance – why deal with something if you don't like it?

            Lynx struggles to express himself. To him, the truth's with his gut feeling – and he can rarely justify it further than that. Then again, he was never great at analysis.

            Having a lawyer and marine biologist as parents has left Lynx with an understanding of multiple topics. Many of his hobbies – none of them truly enjoyed during his darker days – are emerging again. He reads – mostly fiction – and doodles; he'll wander around the suburbs and hike through whatever woods can be found. He can find enjoyment laying spread eagle on grass, staring at the sky. The local communities have him fascinated, and he'll mingle every now and again. He visits the zoo at least once a week. He usually studies with his study group, though sometimes he prefers to do it alone, usually at the library.

            Other notes

            His relationship to his parents is strained. They focused more on furthering their own careers, rather than family life. They weren't really there for him - sure, they had vacations and the occasional family outing, fancy dinner at restaurant, and he does love them, but there's a definite distance between them. In many ways, Oscar fears he was a mistake in their carefully planned life, and sometimes it seems like they would love nothing more than to tell him that. They do care, though, as they had no hesitation to let him study abroad while paying for his tuition and rent. He might have a sibling or two. Currently undecided.

                        Background

                        Before Awakening, Michelle was a 'professional' thief, more or less. She didn't grow up poor or anything like that; it just came naturally to her. (Call her a kleptomaniac and you're asking for a grumpy spark from a nearby wall socket, but she'll grudgingly agree with you too.) Privilege and her parents' money kept her from catching worse than community service when she was caught while still a minor, but they never chose to confront whatever the actual problem was and just assumed their daughter would grow out of it, and at least she hadn't run off god-knows-where* like that Price girl she'd hung out with**. It's not like she was some hardened criminal, they figured, since one of the ways she kept getting caught was passing the goods on to someone she thought needed it more. (The other way was that she really couldn't lie with great skill.)

                        *Libby Price is one of the recent La Grange children to vanish without a trace, and one of those who (as of yet) haven’t come back.
                        **Dated, actually, not that she felt she could tell her parents.

                        Of course she didn't grow out of it, and when she went into Chicago for university she was soon getting into the same habits, and then quickly figured out which pawn shops nearby were truly in the business of fencing. Accordingly, she got the attention of the locals - both those of the criminal persuasion and the police (to the extent that didn’t overlap) - which is why she got kicked out of uni before ever deciding a major. Michelle couldn’t really just go home at that point, not to the disapproval at best that would await her. She managed to keep up enough ‘income’ to pay for a shitty hotel room with cash week-to-week, as long as she only ate what she could get away with shoplifting. Which… wasn’t really tenable. A pawn shop owner knew a guy who knew a guy and they were looking for some people. She was afraid it was going to be the Mafia or something, but she needed work. As it turned out, the Mafia would have been a lot less weird.
                        Mickey Jim’s* crew lived large, to an extent that positively mystified Michelle, especially with how few actual jobs they pulled. And she was never entirely sure if the worship toward the golden bull statue in the center of their ‘hideout’ was real or just debauchedly ironic. Or if that girl she spent a night with really had darkly prismatic wings, or if that was just the Ecstasy. And they only ever ran jobs right after whatever freaky weather Chicago sometimes had. It all seemed strangely normal and yet surreal at the time. She also didn’t get why most of the crew just seemed to be desperate hangers-on, passing money up from whatever small jobs they did and getting used for crappy, risky roles like lookouts, couriers, and such in exchange for basking in the revelry while she was brought into the core who seemed to actually do things.** And the jobs were… weird. Mickey Jim didn’t even seem to pick the locks he opened some of the time, and seemed to always know what the keycodes and combinations were.. Ivy could just slip her way through any security with perfect timing. Benny talked to security guards, mob enforcers and police who were getting ready to break knees to shoot like they were old friends. Mercy had these nurses… well they gave the impression of being nurses anyway, sort of… to patch people up while she just waited. And despite being ostensibly criminals, they carried themselves like they ruled the world.

                        *Michelle never figured out how he got that moniker, which is probably for the best.
                        **In retrospect, she figures her Awakening was already starting, and the Pylon was trying to guide it and groom her.
                        Maybe if she’d had more time to be drawn in, she would have ended up staying… but the Deathstorm happened. Mickey Jim and Benny disappeared, Mercy’s nurses went berserk, and Ivy apparently knew in advance to make herself scarce. Michelle felt something like a wall coming down or a rubber band snapping, and then the police were raiding the hideout. In a panic, she escaped through a door that she didn’t remember, into… nowhere.

                        Awakening - A maze of alleys, unlike any actual place in Chicago. Doors, all locked doors. A maze… a labyrinth. And all she really had with her were her lockpicks. But… doors. She could do doors. With lockpicks. But sometimes she’d pick a door, and just come out in a mirror of the alleys she was just in. Sometimes she’d end up… somewhere else. Bright and searing, like her mother’s disapproval. Or a swirling haze of heat like she’d felt when she first kissed Libby. Or the cold, clammy fear of knowing she’d been caught again.
                        Sometimes they opened somewhere she’d been in before. The museum where that statue was waiting. It shouldn’t have been there, but it shouldn’t have been where they took it, either. Michelle knew that now. The doorways curled back on themselves, halls twisting and churning to bring her back to that storage room where the brass thing had screamed as Mercy touched it. But that’s not what happened. Not what she remembered. Mercy hadn’t even been… she hadn’t… she had. The memories overlapped, what her mind insisted happened but she knew wasn’t true, and what her mind insisted was impossible but it was burned into the world, breaking through to her awareness like cracks forming in a pipe about to burst. More impossible memories, more wrongness, more sin against the world that could and should be but was held in barbed chains of iniquity…
                        She curled up against one of those treacherous doorways, crying and screaming. “I don’t want this! I’m not this! I’m not-”
                        And she fell back through the open door, into the wide open park, in the middle of the day. Lincoln Park? Or somewhere else? She wasn’t sure. There’d been some big charity thing for… kids with cancer or whatever. She’d palmed a roll of 20s into the collection box. It had felt… good. Oh. Right. This was the day it rained frogs. Lots of screaming children. She’d been told to catch as many frogs as she could. Which… made no sense at all. But it was what it was. All she’d gotten was a pile of squirming, broken bodies. No, this time one of the frogs was fat, far too fat, and the poor thing’s skin split to disgorge a pearl. It’s murky, grey, not like a pearl should be. The world is grey, foggy, twilight, but with more and more light growing blinding. All the park is gone now, except… is that a statue plinth? It’s calling to her. Something… She feels the urge to scratch her name into it, with her lockpicks. It’s a name that’s not her name, something cloudy, hard to see.
                        And then, she woke up, and there was a man standing over her. Immaculately dressed, especially in comparison to her grubby, torn hoodie. “A pleasure to meet you. My name is Valentine.”
                        It wasn’t hard to recruit her into the Guardians, particularly in the role of retrieving things from people that shouldn’t have them. Like the crew she’d been running with before. She could set all the wrongness… right.
