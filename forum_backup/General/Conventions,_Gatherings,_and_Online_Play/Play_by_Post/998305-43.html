                Erwin stuck around in town just long enough to deal with the police. The official story is that he was working on a metal-organic framework in the lab when it caught fire. It helped that Adonis' "friend" on the force was amenable to making sure the story took root. Then, after brief farewells to his unofficial Throng, Adonis vanished with his alchemist boyfriend into the night.

                They spent a few weeks on the road, driving across the Canadian prairies and holing up in dingy motel rooms that they never seemed to get charged for: a trick Adonis had learned in his long trek from Ontario to Winnipeg. They spent most of their time talking in hushed whispers, about the Principle, the Great Work, the Created, Pyros, and everything in between. Erwin had some familiarity with the principles of alchemy from his own explorations. But now Adonis was sharing everything he knew about true alchemy, the arcane science that could give life to lifeless tissue. He watched Erwin's eyes light up with wonder at the potential of Distillations, horror at the knowledge of Pandorans, and disgust as he realized the full extent of Amalgama's depravity.

                Erwin kept an ear out for news of Amalgama, and when they finished their investigation, Adonis and Erwin decided it was safe to return. Amalgama had suffered a serious set-back in Winnipeg, and from what Erwin could deduce from rumors, upper management had decided that the cost of rebuilding was too great, at least for the time being. Erwin and Adonis slipped back into the city. Adonis enjoyed the companionship while he could, but he knew that it could not last. He had to move on. He had explained the Roles to Erwin, and had felt nothing but regret as Erwin's eyes filled with heartbroken understanding. Two weeks after returning to Winnipeg, Erwin told Adonis that he understood why he had to move on, and encouraged him to. They parted ways, Erwin promising to help Adonis in any way he could.

                The Frozen Pilgrim had disappeared. When Adonis returned to Winnipeg, he heard rumors that she had fled north, towards Nunavut. He decided to follow her, damn the elements and Unborn, and try and find her. The news of her passing was everywhere- Created markings and scraps of paper left by passing pilgrims saying they had met a frozen Created in the area. Always two steps behind. Eventually, her tracks faded, and Adonis despaired in ever finding her, but then a traveling Extempore he met in Arviat told him of a frozen woman who had once been in Winnipeg, and had returned to that city a few weeks ago. Adonis turned back south, bound once more for the city of muddy waters.

                Adonis has still not found the Frozen Pilgrim, but other disquieting news has reached his ears: that there has been an upswing in Pandoran activity in the city since the raid on Amalgama: it seems that the laboratory reached deep into the earth, and misbegotten creatures had survived the fire, only to crawl out onto the surface in the days and weeks that followed.

                I want the Frozen Pilgrim to be relevant to Adonis' story for a bit longer, a figure he tries to find and make contact with for the time being. Assuming Companion is complete, he's probably going to try and complete the Leader role from Aurum now. So the tidbits he's learned are: Amalgama is licking their wounds and is leaving Winnipeg alone for the time being, the Frozen Pilgrim is back in town, and the city is now swarming with Pandorans more than ever.

                            Arcanist
                            Definitly still chasing the Aurora.
                            Mark 5 was initiated by Solomon. After some in-depth talking, he stormed off, and tried going around in his fulfilled Role as Soldier for a day or two, but soon realized that he constantly found himself thinking about Earth's insightful advice, Solomon's words, Joshua's claims of a 'mission', his own abhorrent actions and guilt, and impurity. Villains, bad people, humans, Created, and impurity. What was the connection? He realized he needed to know.
                            Finally, he returned to Solomon, confided his guilty Torment experience in him, and was taught the Cathar's Path.
                            What he learned was confusing, strange, at odds with everything he'd stood for in places, but also weirdly fitting in some other places.
                            And so, he became a low-rank mook, helping low-class criminals with their low-profile deeds. The money he earned was not important - the trust of his partners in crime was. Their willingness to share secrets about things they did and regretted was the highest currency. Learning how they thought, maybe even why they acted like this, was his goal.
                            Taking part in their crimes was the only way he could think of getting this trust, because he had to realize that, yes, his body was strong, and he was resilient and stubborn, but his mind was weak. It put a significant dent into his 'Hero' attitude.

                            As to why Solomon taught him the Path, he had no idea, because as far as he could tell, he was neither a promising disciple nor a successful Cathar.

                            So, in comparison to last time, this is a more "broken" Mark: less proud, less strong, less focused, a bit aim- and directionless, less 'useful'.

                            I would like it if Isla said "Thanks" for being rescued, which would resolve one of Mark 5's aspirations: Save someone's life and receive gratitude for it.
