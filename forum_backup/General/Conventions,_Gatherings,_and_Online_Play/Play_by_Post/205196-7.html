                                Max stood rooted to the spot at Nate's angry demand. He already wasn't feeling so great to see his friend hurting so much, nor some of his verbal lashing out. There were few things he wanted less than to see his reflection. Or the reflection he would see, at least; he didn't want to think of it as his. Seeing something like that... would make it harder to pretend, on some level, that it wasn't really happening. He choked a moment, closing his eyes. '... Just... Just go. Do it. You'll catch a look eventually, so just... Get it over with.' Another few moments passed before the Pig's hooves brought him to the water's edge, eyes still closed and leaking tears. '... Be a man.' Shaking, he forced them open. And saw the Pig's face.

                                Max did not think he looked good in pink. His cheeks were a tad plumper than he'd remembered, too. And the ears were as ridiculous as he'd feared; even Jessica's and Nate's weren't flopping over themselves! The snout was still worst. The enormous thing was sniffling and snuffling as he kept crying. It gave an excellent view of the slimy end flexing and contorting, exactly as he felt it doing, all the way at the end of his face. He wanted to throw up. After a sob, he did. He had enough presence to turn from the water and from Nate and Jessica, but there were a lot of part-digested mushrooms that came back up, off to the side on the grass. He breathed a moment. He found he actually felt better. Up until he found it didn't smell half-bad... He threw up the rest of the mushrooms. He turned, fallen on hand and knee, to pant into the water. Puking hadn't improved what he saw. Weakly, he punched the pond into ripples. "So..." he breathed, too quiet for human ears, but not for the other Zodiacs'. "This... is me, now..." It was real.

                                He stared for a bit. Then Max splashed water on his face, absently. He kept doing it. He felt cleaner just from that than he had in days (weeks? months?). It got some of the vile-but-not-vile-enough taste out of his mouth. He was hearing a lot of what the other two were saying, even if he wasn't processing it fully. He turned at his name, face dripping, and met Nate's gaze. It hurt to hear, but... Well, it wasn't a surprise. "I... I know," he croaked. "I... Th-Things were..." He shook his head. "I don't remember. N-Not all of it. I... I tried to kill you, too." He turned his head away. "Th-There was a reason, a-at first -- stupid, but a reason. Then there was red and..." He took a breath. "Nothing else mattered." He couldn't look his friend in the eye anymore -- for reasons only tangentially to do with their shape. "I don't blame you. I-I can't. You're still my friend, okay? I mean. At least, I'm still yours. Even if you don't..." Another sob. "God, why did I have to bring us here?"

                                Max listened to what Jessica had to say. Maybe she had a point. He wasn't sure. He didn't really care to be sure. His mind just wasn't in a state to process it. He did want one thing, though. With a grunt of a sigh, he brought a hand to his forehead. "Can we just... not talk. For five minutes. Or whatever."

                                                    Max nodded weakly to Nate, and accepted the hand up. He rose just a bit shorter and younger than he'd been before he started crying. Max went along with them back to the creek, listless, but quietly appreciative of the respect and compassion. He thought he could go for a drink by the time they reached the creek, but...                                                     Well, that was quick to change. Max lifted his head, nose snuffling. He was really starting to hate that sound. But it seemed a price worth paying this time: it gave an early warning. This was why he'd taken a step back and faced dead on when the lion emerged, as soundless as before. He cringed, heart racing. 'Did... Did he get even bigger from last time?' He wondered if he'd have taken off on all-fours into the underbrush if he'd been caught less aware or been less tired. And how good his odds were if he tried now, even if he was held by the Leo's remonstrations and offer. Still, he was very glad when Nate started mouthing off. Even with the chance it was an act, it took off a lot of the edge and eased his tensions. Never mind the strong points the Goat made.

                                                    Though Max was less sure the 'not some little defenseless' bit quite applied to him. Well, he could at least try to keep the lion from lunging at them. Even with Nate's good arguments against, he knew firsthand how easy it was for the animal to get carried away. He grinned. "You said you're here with an offer, anyway." The grin faded a smidge; his voice still sounded different. Well, the mushrooms might still be working their way through him. 'Just need some time.' He perked up again and went on. "One way or another, doesn't seem like there's reason to fight, here. Mind telling us a bit about this way out, you've got in mind?"

We could--" His eyes nearly darted in Nate's direction. No, the Goat deserved his pride. So did the Ox, even if she didn't seem to need it. "So, if I do, are there any ways you know to... y'know, change back?"
