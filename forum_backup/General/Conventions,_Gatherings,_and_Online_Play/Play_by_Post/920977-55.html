            I keep trying to work on a post but work has been crazy this week. Sorry. I have stuff in drafts, and might be able to post tonight, but could be some other time this weekend...we'll see. Sorry all!

            Also, FYI, I feel like the core book is unclear about which situations give Beats vs. Arcane Beats. It implies that turning a failure into a dramatic failure on a spellcasting roll is the only type of roll that gives an Arcane Beat, but I feel like anything supernatural or based on Gnosis, including Revelation/Scrutiny rolls, should give Arcane Beats and supernatural Conditions. I don't remember if that's what I've done in the past or not, but moving forward, I think it makes most sense for anything purely or primarily magical -- spellcasting, Mage Sight, etc. -- to generate Arcane Beats rather than Beats.

            If I'm wrong about this, let me know!

                                                        Thank you so much for the kind and positive feedback. My schedule and lifestyle lately simply don't allow for weekly (or probably even monthly) in-person sessions, and my wife doesn't enjoy or find any interest in these games, so I have turned to PBP games to scratch that itch without taking away from my time with her or other friends. I also have struggled as a Storyteller with in-person games before. I think my strengths are in exposition and creating an engaging universe, while my primary growth area right now is being able to generate exciting conflict and improvise on my feet; PBP games have been a good way for me to use my strengths while get opportunities to develop in the other areas I think I'm lacking.

                                                        Mainly, I have been trying to keep the game exciting and well-paced by just listening to what you all want to do, creating situations that require action, and making sure my posts give you something to think about or respond to. I'm glad that this one has been working well.

                                                        I think you will generally have no problem finding players -- I had more than twice as many people ask to play in this game than I could reasonably accommodate. But then there's the attrition rate, too, you never know how many will stick. I still think it's more fun to have access to games than not, but that's just me. If you're on the fence about it, I always recommend giving it a shot. You can always tell people up front that it might be a slower game, and if it doesn't work out, no harm.
