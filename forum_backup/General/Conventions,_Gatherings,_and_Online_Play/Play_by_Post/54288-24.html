    "HA! YOU thiiink that you are, the Hero?" The Fae shouts, his hair spiking out at exactly the same angle despite your shifting perspective.

    Suddenly, the room fades to dark, and all figures save the Circle and Larissa appear frozen in time. Any attempts to move are met with resistance, as if you are floating in a viscous fluid. A glowing screen appears before you, and piano music begins to play. Grainy sepia images begin to appear.

    A grotesque paper-cutout woman appears and begins stomping a tiny claymation village. [subtitle]Gaia: rawr.[/subtitle] The scene cuts to the Fae, who is wearing overalls and a ridiculous onion-shaped hat [subtitle]Shu-Yin:Oh noes! My village![/subtitle]. Cut back to paper-cutout Gaia, who wags a finger [subtitle]Gaia: To face me you must first defeat my Five Elemental Dragons, who are leeching off the Elemental Poles[/subtitle].

    The screen disappears, the scene returns to both normal lighting and speed, and the resistance to your motion disappears. Shu-Yin pumps a fist, "YOU seeeee? This is whyy II'M the, Main character?."

    Yi finds a large codex (that makes most college math textbooks seem like light reading) hurled at him. On the cover: Penultimate Phantasmagoria XIIII (C)

    "NOW, Stay ontooo, the script?.," Shu-Yin says.

        Directly after landing, Hyuga pushes back to enter the room and immediately grabs the codex off the ground.

        "Hold on a minute. We need to check through this..." Hyuga will read quickly and then say, "No, no, no... this is just not right. It doesn't highlight the hero and make them elegant. It seems just way too boring for a hero. You need to think of all the great stories like Bogard and the Seven Monstrous Giants or even The Adventures of the Spike-haired man and the inferno wastes. Those truly showed off the hero. True heroism is tragic and full of awesome battles where they take the lead."

        Hyuga will pause in thought for a second and then say, "Yes... I think I have it. Why don't you command all of your beasts not to attack the building, but us. You could command them from afar showing how elegant and charismatic you are. It would build up the hero's resolve and charismatic traits. Soon after, we kill a few of them and you charge in ready to save the day, showing your elegance and bravery. But we must not forget the tragedy and legacy that must be in the story. You can jump in front of one of the soldiers and take an attack for them. Your bravery would shine best and then you put up a short fight. In the end though, the hero must fall. But one of the soldiers has to escape to spread the story of our brave, noble, and wonderful hero. His legacy will live on forever and inspire so many countless others. That is how a true hero would lead his nation to the greatest heights and finish the great objective of the hero. Yes, this would be the most beautiful ending for such an amazing hero."

        Hyuga will close his eyes for a second seemingly in deep retrospect. "This is the best story, don't you agree everyone? Why don't we finish this beautiful story?"

                        (Tirien)

                        The feast is truly lavish, ripe with the bounty of the forests. II mean look at that, it's a whole roast moose!

                        The Skalds regale you with the tales of clan Donnkad and the Geherin in general. For the most part, they revolve around their ferocity in battle, besting bands of Fae, barbarians of the foothills, and the Ordon's phalanxes and "metal men". Two particular tales stand out; the Epic of High King Eckelbraun the Foolish, and the tale of the Silver-Horned Mother's birth (both are summarized below).

                        The Epic of High King Eckelbraun the Foolish
                        High King Eckelbraun the foolish was High King a long time ago, sometime before last Solday. He demanded that he be able to ride a horse in three dimensions, since it was the only creature noble enough for him to ride. To do this, they used a pulley system to get a horse into a tree. The horse then fell out, crushing King Eckelbraun to death. Everyone then realized it was a stupid plan, and agreed to never do it again.


                        The Silver Mother's Birth
                        The Moon was travelling around, doing moon things. Eating Fae, stomping demons, that sort of thing. Then the moon got a stomach ache 'cause she ate too many Fae, and puked. But instead of gastric juices with Fae bits in them, she puked out a silver-horned woman, fully clothed and on a silver-maned horse. The moon then went off to gloat to the sun about it (legend says the sun, not one to be outdone, then barfed up a gilded dragon). The woman decided to stay where she was born and became the goddess of the Geherin. Her twin brother (the Horse) went off to have awesome adventures


                        Yep. Ready to begin your travels to the court of the High King?
