    Pakari blinked and spoke his first impression: "You have six fingers on your right hand." Then was a brief pause while Pakari reorganized his thoughts and answered "I mean, eight. Eight in all."

    Things were beginning to become clearer - Endlessly Yearning Horizon was probably one of the strange folk from the Empty Sail or Honey Islands, which would explain both his odd appearance and behavior handily. How he came to be here, doing healing work for the Whispering Valley tribe, would probably make for a good story by itself, but right now that was not what Pakari wanted to know about. He wanted to know why he was in this cave, what happened to Hasya, and what was happening to Whitestone Island in that order.

    "How did I get here? I was over in the jungle by Windrest... Did the wind carry me all the way here?"

    It was a stupid question, and Pakari realized it shortly after the words left his mouth. He felt he knew a little about flying and falling, and was pretty confident that if he had really been thrown by the wind for dozens of miles, he should be dead. The alternative was that he had landed somewhere else and then been carried here while he was knocked out, but that just raised more questions...

                Wahine bristled visibly with a sudden, complex flurry of emotion. At first, utter revulsion that he could taste some part of her essence, then revulsion gave way to an anger that overpowered that quick jolt of fear that had spurred her to her feet. How dare he taste her much less talk about it aloud in such a flippant manner! The sudden change in his beautiful features, that shift to something softer with a hint of coyness, that soothing sweet voice melting into something gentler and thoughtful... it made her chest flutter until the content of his words struck her like a bolt of lightening. First, it was a shock to hear him speak so casually about something that had never been spoken aloud and had never been shared in great detail. Second, to hear that the monster from her past had a name and had left a taste on her soul... she felt as if she'd been thrown into an icy sea. Then a flash of anger at being called a pet and the insinuation that her escape was nothing more than the whim of a fairfolk who'd grown bored with her new toy.

                Though the fateful night itself was a blur from glamour and the passage of time, she remembered vividly the fevered nights where strange siren songs called to her in a voice she knew was once hers. Over time, those strange fits, fevers, and symptoms of invisible wounds faded. But half a lifetime forced to communicate through vivid expression and bold body language left unfiltered emotions boiling much closer to the surface than if she had layers of communication to obfuscate her deeper feelings. She wondered sometimes if that monster, Tarana he'd called her... maybe she had pulled some part of her closer to the surface when she'd pulled at her soul and ripped her voice from her lips.

                Again his honeyed words filled her head as he turned to speak to a man she couldn't recall ever seeing before. She shook the distraction from her mind, scowling at the fairfolk once more from her defensive stance.

                "Why are we here?" She barked silently, glaring daggers. "What do you want with us?" She pointed her staff at the fairfolk, emboldened by the iron pendent against her skin. It seemed he could understand her and from the stories she'd heard of the silent tribe, perhaps they too where skilled at reading the words on her lips.

                                    Wahine relaxed gradually as the others talked. Her posture no longer bristled visibly and her expressions became more controlled. She regarded the fair folk with mild caution, the way a creature might cautiously regard a predator that, for the moment at least, didn't seem particularly hungry or interested in giving chase. The fair folk did seem to have some sort of rapport with the tribesmen which eased her mind slightly. Truth be told, in the deepest darkest parts of her mind beneath the memory of mortal terror, she did find something magnetic about the fair folk and their strange magics. Glimpses of gossamer spun treasures beyond anything she might replicate within a lifetime of mortal toil was both a source of inspiration and deepest artistic frustration. She'd never actually been in the presence of a fair folk with her wits intact; never had the opportunity to really interact with one on if not exactly peaceful terms then at least neutral terms. Assuaged of her initial fear, she was actually kind of... fascinated, though she tried to keep it to herself as best as she could. There where more important matters she could focus on to keep from being drawn in by the creature's magnetic pull. She focused her attention on the tribesmen to distract her.

                                    'If a demon has been freed and the earthquake was an omen of things to come, then we should take whatever action we can.' Wahine signed quickly as she mouthed the words at a normal speaking speed. It seemed she was amongst company that could understand her far more clearly then the average person of the villages she frequented. How long had it been since she could 'speak' at a normal pace in a minimally restricted manner? No pantomiming, no need to constrain herself to simple sentences and key words spoken painfully slow. The thought of it excited her. She tried not to let her excitement show too much. '​You seem to know a lot about this demon creature and what it's capable of. I will follow your lead."

                                    She paused, thinking of the others. 'perhaps the Easterners have useful tools. A large ship probably has at least some useful goods for trading. Maybe those goods would be useful for our purposes'

                                    Secretly she also just wanted an excuse to board that beautiful vessel. Preferably with the captain's consent which reminded her... "I'd like to meet this captain if there's time. His consent may be useful. Hardened seamen are useful in a fight as well. I... understand sea tongue if that's helpful." There was no use in hiding it. Fighting a demon came far before being able to eavesdrop on the new arrivals to get the upper hand on a trade.

                                                        Pakari turned away from the warrior, rubbing his eyes and trying to think. He was, to be frank, completely at a loss about what to do next. This situation was seemingly completely out of his control, and Hasya wasn't around to give him instructions. The obvious next step was to go look for him, but the search was out of his hands.

                                                        He turned out the conversation between Horizon and the young girl - later, he would realize that they had been speaking the language of the gods (which Hasya had not yet taught him), and wonder how they both knew it. For now, he stewed in his own thoughts, trying and failing to come up with any plan that didn't involve waiting.
                                                        '
                                                        Eventually, he became aware that the other woman, the one with exaggerated body language, was standing in front of him and trying to 'speak.' Pakari blinked at her, confused. He did not understand hand signs, and it took a few seconds to realize she was mouthing words. "Uhh... Su... si... lo? Susilo the shipwright? Yes, I know him. I visit him, sometimes."In fact, Pakari used to earn extra bread for his aunt and himself by doing odd jobs for the old carpenter, and had learned a little about his craft.
