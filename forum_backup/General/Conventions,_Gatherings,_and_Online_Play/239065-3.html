    Wahine held her breath in the final heart pounding seconds of the decent, bracing for an underwater plunge of indeterminable force. Landing suddenly on the water was thrilling, and when they landed safely in one piece without so much as a chip on the lacquer or scuff on the wing, she let out a riotous laugh though not a sound escaped her beyond great inhales of breath. She quickly popped the buckles holding her against Haku and sought out her assorted small possessions before they floated away or sank. Her whistle managed to stay secure in her buttoned pocket during the landing. She put it back around her neck and blew bird calls from the shore. She didn't bother with pleasantries, occupying herself with calling out towards the ship on the horizon. Every few minutes, between securing the gliders and talking with the villagers, she'd go back to her bird calls, listening for replies from animals or other allies who knew her codes. Each whistle since they landed was the same. "Here I am. Where are you?" to anyone who knew her sounds.

    On the Deck of the Guild Ship Blue Lily, RY 767, 23rd of Ascending Fire, Half an Hour after Sunrise:

    "They're really quite good," the cook grumbled. "Especially with a good stuffing and cider..."

    The bird narrowed it's eyes a little. "Better men than you have tried..." he grumbled, his tone suddenly much more intelligible.

    He fluffed his feathers and shifted along his perch just above the crew's heads as the Captain made to shoo him along. "No please Captain? Fluffy has no manners." He reached over and grabbed one of the slip nots the crew had used to tie off bits of rigging, giving it a firm jerk. He fluttered over to a few other loose looking slip knots and gave them all his best yank, laughing at the sound of things coming undone on the ship. There was only so much damage one little bird could do in a few quick moments but it would stall them a bit. He took off, blowing a raspberry at the crew as he circled once more around the ship, pooping on the cook for good measure as he turned towards the shore.

    As he flew close to the shoreline he heard the familiar sound of Wahine's whistle. "I'm here. Where are you?" He repeated the whistle. "I'm hear, where are you?"

    Wahine repeated herself more frequently and he'd reply in turn, honing in on her location. Eventually as the cluster of village people became clear he circled around and landed on Wahine's outstretched hand, relenting to a brief cuddle. Wahine kissed her feathered friend on his scalloped, ashen head.

                    Pakari ran as fast as he could without tripping over anything. He wove through the trees, following water trails, streambeds, animal tracks, and other places where the footing was comparatively clear. Islanders didn't travel through this part of the island much, except to hunt or forage, which unfortunately meant that Pakari would have to bushwhack the whole way to and from the Tomb of the Stone Maw. Normally that was a non-issue, but just now time was of the essence, and Pakari cursed the lack of a proper trail at least once every thirty steps.

                    The forest felt deserted with no spirits around. The space felt desolate without them. Had Pakari been less focused on running, the eeriness of the depopulated forest would have been overwhelming.

                    Pakari had been able to see the spirits for most of his life. He dimly remembered trying to play with them when he could barely walk or talk, and being disappointed that he could not touch them. It took several years for him to realize that other people could not see what he saw, and that some thought him touched in the head or just stupid because he would talk to "imaginary friends" or spend long periods of time staring into "empty" spaces. For a long time, only Aroha understood his gift, and she only knew because she had the presence of mind to talk to the priests when Pakari refused to admit that the strange animals and faces he saw in the trees were not real.

                    By his early teens, he had gotten practiced at the art of pretending to be normal, which mostly meant not watching the spirits when there were other people around. Ignoring them was not always easy - spirits were sensitive to many things that people were not, and if something startled them (such as the earthquake today), Pakari was always the first to notice and react, which could be awkward when Pakari was among company. Still, despite the occasional hiccup, Pakari managed to do a reasonably good job of integrating into the Dreaming River community without getting a reputation for being insane or delusional. Still, he felt that the difference between his experience of the world and those of others put a gap between him and almost everyone else he knew.

                    Confessing to Hasya that he could see things no one else could had been one of the most cathartic experiences of his life, and the old priest had been surprisingly (from Pakari's point of view) understanding and supportive.

He was drawing interested looks from the people of Calm Sea Village who were gathered on the beach: first because the Kitemen are famous for their daring and bravery, and second because Cloudhopper Haku was graced by the gods and by his own discipline with a fantastic body and rippling muscles which were a simple joy to watch in motion.

                                        "Whoever they are," Haku declared as he finished packing away his glider, "we have about half an hour before reinforcements arrive. I'm Haku, First Kiteman for Chief Kalewa, and I signalled for assistance. The Chief will know about that ship in short order, and more Kitemen will show up soon. If they do seem hostile, we should try to delay any action until they arrive. I've seen a couple of ships like this before, usually way out at sea, but once or twice they've come to shore and tried to kidnap our people, gods only know why. We have some experience sinking these vessels from above with jugs of oil and flaming arrows. I recommend we send a boat out to meet them and do our diplomacy at sea: if the talks go bad, they'll be much harder to defeat if we allow them to make shore."


                                        Wahine grit her teeth at being addressed as 'dear lady’. She did have a name, if only people thought to ask. She found such empty politeness irritating, especially from someone she’d only just met. As someone who communicated largely through body language, her flash of annoyance was easy to see. She pushed her way through the thickening crowd of onlookers who seemed hopelessly in her way. If she had her staff she’d rap them in the ankles to force them out of her way.

                                        “Give way, give way!” her bird shouted as he fluttered to the ground and toddled through the cluster of feet around Foksi and Seskan. He began twittering away random snatches of conversation in a variety of languages and voices. Wahine thought she heard the occasional snatch of sea tongue but the rest was foreign to her.

                                        Her irritated expression softened when she approached Haku making short work of binding the glider into a portable bundle. She loosened the bundle enough to reach her hand inside and pluck out the staff which she had fastened to one of the braces. She smiled sweetly at Haku, placing a reassuring hand on his arm as she followed drifting gaze to the pieces of Seskan's damaged glider. She brushed a lock of hair from his face and looked him in the eyes.

                                        Don't worry, I can fix it. her eyes said. The moment of wordless intimacy lasted only a lingering second and the she quickly moved onto something else.

                                        Among her possessions she’d fished out of the water was her small little shoulder bag. It didn’t hold much and she hadn’t brought much with her, just the essentials; rudimentary pocket tools for an emergency, a pretty basic spyglass for sightseeing, and a small utility knife. The spyglass was small and basic but she reckoned she could make some field adjustments to improve the range a little. Unable to communicate clearly, she just didn’t bother with typical the social graces and was too focused on her spyglass to notice reactions as she combed through the crowd looking for materials she could use. It took a few minutes but quick as lightening she was able to make some field tweaks to the pretty basic device. She could make out the approaching landing party much clearer now. She handed the spyglass to Haku wordlessly and pointed towards the ship.
