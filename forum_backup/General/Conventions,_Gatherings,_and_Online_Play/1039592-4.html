    That's not quite correct. The Karpani were soothsayers and hedge mystics, the priests were the actual Magi. Karpani represented pre-Zartosht Persian spirituality, and in fact were denounced by him as charlatans.

    After Alexander's death Persia was ruled by Hellenic Seleukides for quite some time.

    Of some interest is the fact dogs and golden jackals (!) were considered holy animals. Other (in)famous feature was the practice of Xwedodah.

            In the game the Karpani went into hiding until things settled down. BTW Has Alexander died in the game yet? Because that would shape the Persian world, After his death they reclaimed the Iranian Valley.

            This is a real-world overview of the Persians. (Thought I would stop until I found out what you wanted so I wasn't researching the wrong thing)
            The Achaemenid Empire, also called the (First) Persian Empire, started in the Iranian Plateau and was founded by Cyrus the Great. The empire spread to be the largest of its time and at its height covered 2,123,562 sq mi and had a population of 17 – 35 million people. Zoroastrianism (Mazdayasna) was the religion of the time and the Karpani were the priests.

            The Zoroastrianism holy text is the Avesta and in these texts Zartosht (Zoroaster is the Greek pronunciation) proclaimed that there is only one true God and he is Ahura Mazda (Ahura The Lord Creator, and Mazda, Supremely Wise). There are Spirits called Spenta Mainyu (literally translates to Immortals).
            There were multiple sects of Zorastrianism, some of which were closer to dualism, holding that Ahura Mazda and Angra Mainyu were equal in power.

            Spenta Mainyu is actually one of the Amesha Spenta (lit. Immortal Bounteous) There were six and are the prime emanations (or whatever the appropriate term is) of Ahura Mazda.

                                    Although the old woman mystified Alketas ever since they left Greece, his first meaningful encounter with her was a week after his Awakening, when he was assigned guard duty to her tent. At first, he was outraged, even more so when she abused her standing as (apparent) nobility to make him run meaningless errands for more than a month. During that time, from bits of conversation and observing her surroundings he surmised that she, too, possesses the gift of magic. So he confronted her about it, and the argument carried long into the night, becoming a philosophical dispute. That was when she offered to make him her apprentice, and he accepted, hoping she will provide him resources and opportunity to flourish. Besides, at the time he was convinced that not only is this woman a master alchemist and willworker, she also appears to have an influence on the court and more than a few spies in her service. Not a pleasant perspective to have her as an enemy.

                                    He's met the others some time later (perhaps on some previous assignment?).The woman gave her name as Alkmene, although Alketas is certain that's not her real or even shadow name. She has a clear sense of superiority, but apparently is quite loyal to fellow mages in her own, cliquish way.
