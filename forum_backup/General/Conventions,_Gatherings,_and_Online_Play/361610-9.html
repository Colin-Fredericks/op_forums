            "Ironic lack of foresight for a Sidereal" was the term used on his last yearly review and the situation Flynn has found himself in was living up to that. Flynn could play all day, but it was hot and his mouth was getting dry. His headband was soaked in sweat even though he hadn't been outside for long.

            Flynn started to put his flute up to his lips when he noticed someone at the foot of the crate. The man reach up with a wine skin and said "You looked thirsty". Flynn took the skin and uncorked it. Wine had never smelt so wonderful. He took a couple of large mouthfuls and began to hand the skin back to the man.

            "Keep it," the man said. “There is plenty more where that came from.” He pointed at the wreckage of the tavern.

            "Thank you kindly, sir!" Flynn said back. Sailor’s Grotto was a good place and would be missed. Especially its cheap liquor prices. Taking a few more gulps of wine, he notice that the crowd was growing impatient with the delay in entertainment.

            Hmmm.... They wanted to dance? He set the skin down on the crate next to him and began the first notes of "A Walk upon a Daystar's Morning" Perhaps he could tired them out.

            Flynn knew Swimming Owl wasn't going to be happy with him about the wine, but really, the few sips he had were barely enough to get him tipsy. He wasn’t planning on drinking the whole skin. Anyways, she was busy with the other exalted and probably wouldn't notice him drinking.

                                    "The Guild is less an organization than an idea. Much like the language we speak it is a construct which exists nowhere in a pure form. Put another way, there is not one guild but many that have all agreed to use the same name to describe themselves. Each merchant prince is master of their own organization and it is the will of his or her peers rather than the mandates of Nexus that decide how business will be carried out.

                                    It could not be otherwise. A system of sorcerous communication sufficient to keep all major figures in touch would be ruinously expensive without a massive change in the availability of enlightenment, artifacts, or incentives for sorcerers. Even if it exists, there is no structure in place to enforce the will of the Guild in Nexus on the merchant princes. Ties besides force would be necessary and none of the leaders see the gain in giving up power and independence for the minimal benefits that greater unity would provide at this time.

                                    For the time being we shall have to hold to Neillzi's truism that 'all politics is local'. This applies even to the Realm and Thorns who cannot effectively project power all the way here."

                                                "A guild is like a tribe you chose to join, where everyone does the same thing and everyone is supposed to help each other. It doesn't always work out that way. The Guild," he emphasized the article, "is a guild where the thing they all do is make money and the help they give to each other is preventing anyone else from making money. They also fight against each other, when one believes he will make more money if he does so. They do not care about other people except to consider how they might make money from them."

                                                At the mention of cities Splendor looked around with a disappointed look in his eyes. "A city is not a large village, though that is where many of them come from. A city happens when a place becomes a person. Though it never speaks with words it has its moods and hopes and feelings. A city is the monument people build to their dreams and to their failure to achieve those dreams. A city is where all the secret desires people hoard in their hearts are spilled out into the world to be exposed to shame and honor. A city is ignorance that must be left to gain enlightenment and the destination where enlightenment is attained.

                                                A true city is all these things and none, for each is the model that all cities emulate or diverge from, and each informs all others. A city is great because other cities are squalid, a city is happy because others are miserable, a city is beautiful because others are ugly. You can enter the large collection of buildings and not see the city, yet it is there."
