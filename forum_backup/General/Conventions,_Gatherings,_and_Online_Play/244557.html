    [Recruitment] Rage Across the Delaware Valley- Werewolf: the Apocalypse

    08-30-2014, 01:59 AM
    It’s been quite a while since I’ve had the chance to run an Old World of Darkness game. Or are we supposed to call it the Classic World of Darkness now? I suppose that it doesn’t really matter; I never warmed to the nWoD games, so the original World of Darkness has always been the only World of Darkness for me.

    Rage Across the Delaware Valley, along with its cousin Philadelphia by Night, is a setting I first wrote when I was in college (back, of course, when the original World of Darkness was in print) and used for a few games on campus, and from which I’ve sampled bits and pieces along the way. I recently came across a few scattered remnants of my old notes, and was inspired to update the setting and try running a game again. So, through the collection of the few pieces of my notes that remain, along with painstaking recollections, I have endeavored to reconstruct, update and (I hope) improve the setting.

    My schedule these days, as my friends and family can attest, is highly erratic. A steady tabletop game, or even a steady Skype or chat game, would be virtually impossible for me to run. I simply could not commit to being regularly available at the same time every week, and I would not put a dedicated gaming group through the unpleasantness of never being certain whether I would be joining them from week to week. A PBEM or PbP game, on the other hand, is something that I can easily handle given my current workload. So, here I am and, being that I’m in more of a Werewolf than a Vampire mood at the moment, I bring you Rage Across the Delaware Valley.

    Below I will provide the basic information of the setting, information that would be available with minimal effort to any player character in the chronicle. It can be treated as IC information by all players, but should not be assumed to be 100% accurate! This represents information that is generally known or assumed by the Garou of the Delaware Valley, and some of it is flat out wrong. Before I get into setting information, however, let’s discuss some general information about the game.

    For starters, this is a game of Werewolf: the Apocalypse. There will be crossover with some of the other games of the oWoD, but this is W:tA. No players will be playing Vampire or Changeling or Mage characters. The cosmology will be W:tA based, with a few interesting tidbits cherry-picked from the other games. The Vampire NPCs in the setting may believe in silly things like Noddist Lore, but they’re dead wrong. If we were playing Philadelphia by Night, it might be a different story.

    That being said, I’m a big fan of Exalted, and I’ve always liked the idea of the Exalted setting as a precursor to the oWoD. So, Exalted fans might pick out the occasional easter egg referring to Exalted history, or bits and pieces of the deep cosmology of the game if players dig deep enough will have ties to Exalted, but it isn’t going to be a huge, essential part of the story, for the most part. It’s just there to amuse me and my fellow Exalted fans. And I promise, no actual Exalted NPCs will appear. ;-)

    Oh, and for the record, I’m not a big fan of Demon: the Fallen (nothing wrong with it, I just never got into it and I don’t have the books), so don’t expect anything from D:tF to appear in the game at all, not even background. It never happened. Every other oWoD game is fair game for me to use for background material at the very least, but no Demon.

    Now, I understand that some Hunter: the Reckoning supplements had some relatively detailed information about Philadelphia as a setting. I have nothing against H:tR, but I don’t own those particular supplements, and am not going to be using that information. So, if you have an idea of what Philadelphia and the Delaware Valley look like in the canon oWoD from H:tR (or other canon sources, for that matter), don’t expect Rage Across Delaware Valley to look like that.

    OK, now that that’s settled, let’s talk rules for a minute. We will primarily be using the W20 core book for our rules. Players without access to that material can get by with the revised core book- if any contradictions come up, we’ll resolve them and move on. That said, there are many gifts, rites, etc. from numerous revised sourcebooks that I am perfectly OK with, but if you intend to use such things, take a sec to run them by me first. Most times, I’ll just approve them or maybe tweak them a little to my satisfaction, but it’s still good to know. If it is from an earlier edition than revised… well, I’ll still say yes to anything reasonable, but more tweaking might be required.

    As far as in character timing goes, the first chapter of the game will begin on January 1, 2014, just after midnight, and will progress forward IC depending on how fast things move along. I’m a big fan of downtime blue-booking, so there will be jumps forward in between chapters.

    As far as the W:tA metaplot goes, I’ll be cherry-picking which aspects of the metaplot did and did not happen. My decisions about a few of the more significant events and individuals, from the W:tA perspective, are included in the sidebar below. If you have a question about a specific piece of metaplot and would like to know whether it did or did not happen, ask. However, if it is something that a new character in this game could not possibly know, I may not answer and simply let you discover the truth for yourself as the game goes on, if it becomes relevant (for example, there’s no way any starting character would know if the extermination of the Tremere Antitribu happened in this setting).

    Metaplot Sidebar

    Week of Nightmares- The Week of Nightmares did happen, in July of 1999. Even a decade and a half later, the exact details of what happened are not widely known, but it is still seen as a sign of the impending Apocalypse.

    Red Star- The Red Star, Anthelios, did appear to the Garou on July 15, 1999, during the Week of Nightmares, and still shines in the Umbral sky. Garou observers still claim that it is coming closer to Earth, but it seems to have slowed down steadily over the years. It is still not generally visible from the physical world, but individuals with special senses (including certain gifts) can see it. While average mortals cannot see it, it has been detected by certain astronomic devices. The fact that it has not yet been confirmed visibly continues to baffle scientists. The scientific name for the phenomenon is “2001KX76,” and it was first discovered by mortals in May of 2001. It gets occasional mention in scientific journals as more outlandish theories are put forward to account for its odd behavior, but its significance is not understood by humanity.

    Perfect Metis- The Perfect Metis has not been born. The prophecies that seem to refer to the Perfect Metis are extremely obscure in my setting, and very few Garou are aware of them. The prophecies themselves are ambiguous, seeming to state that the Perfect Metis may appear, and that if it does it will play a significant role in the Apocalypse for good or ill, but that such an appearance is not guaranteed, that the Apocalypse can happen without the Perfect Metis being born and that whether he is born or not depend on a number of other obscure conditions.

    Stargazers- The Stargazers have left the Garou Nation and returned to their ancestral homeland of Tibet, joining the Beast Courts of the Emerald Mother en masse. There are still a handful of individual Stargazers left in the west, but the overwhelming majority of them have gone to the Hengeyokai.

    Samuel Haight- Yeah, he existed. Yeah, he’s still dead. And because it amuses me, his soul is still an ashtray.

    Zhyzhak- She exists, and she’s just as scary now as she was in the original source material. Maybe even scarier. The prophecy about her crushing the “Last Gaian King” under her heel is slowly spreading throughout the Garou nation and becoming general knowledge. And speaking of him…

    Albrecht- Still alive, still the King of House Wyrmfoe, still has the Silver Crown, still insufferably self-righteous.


    Now that that’s settled, let’s talk characters. I am ideally looking for 5 PCs to form a pack, but I’m willing to start with 4 if I can’t find 5 players. 6 is the most I am prepared to deal with. 7 would be a little too much for me, though I MIGHT change my mind about that under the right circumstances. All players must play Garou.

    Let me say that again- all players must play Garou. I will not allow Fera player characters of any type, for any reason, nor will I allow kin player characters or crossover player characters from other oWoD games. I’m sorry if that disappoints anyone. There will be Fera NPCs in the game, but this is going to be a story of a Garou pack, not a mixed group of shifters. So, no matter how good your idea is for a Fera character, my answer is going to be “no.” So, please, don’t even ask. Not even the Nuwisha disguised as a Ragabash.

    All twelve extant tribes of Gaian Garou in the Western Concordat are playable. That’s the Black Furies, Bone Gnawers, Children of Gaia, Fianna, Get of Fenris, Glass Walkers, Red Talons, Shadow Lords, Silent Striders, Silver Fangs, Uktena and Wendigo. I will also allow no more than one player to play a Stargazer if (s)he has a good character background and story. No extinct tribes (Bunyip, Croatan or White Howlers), no Ronin, no Skin Dancers, no Siberakh and definitely no Black Spiral Dancers. And no crazy impossible combos, like Homid Red Talons, or non-Metis male Black Furies. Now, I would like to get a mix of tribes, so I don’t want more than two player characters from the same tribe, though I may make exceptions to this guideline on a case by case basis for very good stories.

    All three breeds are available, without restrictions (except as noted above, vis-à-vis Homid Red Talons and the like). Even if we end up with an entire pack of the same breed, so be it. I’ll not force players to create characters of different breeds to balance the pack. Players who want to play Metis, do not expect to ignore your character’s deformity, or the social stigma of being a Metis- these things must be role played. I generally prefer physical deformities to psychological ones, but I won’t insist on that. I am also very open to working with you to develop custom deformities, so feel free to ask.

    All five auspices are available. Ideally, I would like to have at least one character of each auspice, but I will not force players to adopt certain roles. As long as we are not completely overloaded with a single auspice, I’ll let the chips fall where they may. Just bear in mind that a wide range of skillsets would be useful. :-) Also, remember that the auspices aren’t straitjackets. There’s no reason that a character can’t be of one auspice while having some personality traits considered appropriate or stereotypical of a different auspice. A person is more complex than a set of lunar-derived personality traits, after all. Of course, if you insist on having astrological justification for your characters non-auspice personality quirks, feel free to delve into Garou astrology for additional depth in this area. A theurge with a surfeit of warrior-type traits may have been born under the sign of Nerigal or Rorg, while a philodox with a trickster nature may have undergone her first change while Mitanu was ascendant. I love Garou astrology. ;-)

    The game will begin with all of the characters going on their rite of passage together (yes, Werewolf fans, I know a multi-tribal Rite of Passage is slightly unusual- it will be explained in character). So, characters will be created using standard W20 rules as if they were Rank 1 Garou, even though they technically won’t be until after they complete their Rite of Passage in Chapter 1. This also means relatively young characters. For Homids, the First Change usually takes place between the ages of 10-16. Earlier is practically unheard of (and I wouldn’t allow a character that young anyway), but the change can be delayed for any number of reasons, both physical and spiritual. There is also a period of training and preparation after the First Change but before the Rite of Passage, which would naturally take longer for a younger Homid. So, to put it bluntly, I would like most of the characters to be in their mid to late teens (15-19). One or two slightly older characters would be acceptable, but anything over 21 or 22 would be pushing it. A character in their early teens might also be possible, but I would need to talk seriously with the player of such a prospective character to make sure that everything would work out OK. 13 is the absolute youngest age for a Homid player character that I am willing to accept. Metis and Lupus characters obviously mature at different rates, so we’ll work out the approximate physical age of the humanoid forms of such characters during character creation, though the above general guidelines will apply.

    I would love to hear character proposals and ideas. What I do not want is to be presented with a completed character sheet before we’ve even discussed the character. I am a very hands-on ST, especially when it comes to character creation, and I prefer to be involved in the process. So, tell me what kind of character you’re thinking about, we’ll work through the details together, and THEN you can write up your sheet. For ease of reference, we’ll all use the same format for our character sheets, which I will present in a later post, when we get to the point that we need them.

    The game will be based, as the name implies, in the Delaware Valley. The pack’s adventures will take them around the world and throughout the umbra, but their home sept(s) will be in the Delaware Valley. The Delaware Valley, for our purposes, comprises Philadelphia, South East Pennsylvania, South Jersey and northern Delaware. Characters do not necessarily need to be native to this area (though it would be nice if at least some of them were), but if they are from off, there needs to be a reason why they ended up here. That can be as simple as their tribe choosing to foster them with a sept in the Delaware Valley, but I’m willing to work with everyone to come up with plausible, interesting and workable reasons for your characters to be here.

    I like variety among the characters. I like healthy conflict within the pack. Irreconcilable differences are a whole other matter. However good a character concept is, if I am convinced it would inevitably lead to the self-destruction of the pack (or, worse, the rest of the pack ganging up on and expelling or killing the character in question), I will not allow it. So, please, don’t come with a character who has a pathological hatred of Silver Fangs and has an unshakeable desire to kill all Silver Fangs in their sleep if we already have two Silver Fang characters. Red Talons probably deserve special mention here. I like Red Talons. I hope someone decides to play a Red Talon. Red Talons can make fascinating characters and a great addition to many packs. The stereotypical Red Talon who wants to wipe out every human on the planet (such as, for example, a diehard member of the Lodge of the Predator Kings) is not that kind of Red Talon, though. So, please, if you want to play a Red Talon, don’t play into that stereotype.

    One more thing I should mention. The game, as I said, is starting with the characters’ Rite of Passage. If all goes well, we all enjoy ourselves and the game continues, it will proceed until the characters earn their way to Rank 5, at which point they will be major players in the Apocalypse itself. So, if all goes well, this game will continue until the world ends. ;-) It is, therefore, very important to make sure that the characters you create are characters that you are going to enjoy playing long term. I hope that’s something we can work together on.

    I have in mind a series of four major stories that will weave together into one chronicle- the first story will cover the character’s rite of passage and take them to about rank 2 or 3, with the PCs becoming respected among the local Garou (I hope). The second story will see them through to about rank 4, and will see them become major players in the Delaware Valley, with their fame starting to spread to other regions. The third story will bring them to rank 5 (or, possibly, rank 6 if the circumstances dictate, but I’m not planning on that happening) and will see the PCs ranking among the leaders of their tribes, recognized all over the world. This will set the characters up to take a leadership role for the fourth story, which will culminate the narrative of the chronicle with the Apocalypse.

    I think that covers the basics. I’ll post later with more specifics about the mechanics of character creation, including a few house rules, but this should be enough for now to get you guys thinking and proposing character concepts.

    My next post, as promised, will contain general information about the setting itself. As character creation goes on, each player will receive more specific information, mostly via private message, about the setting from the perspective of their particular tribe, but what follows will be information that can be considered generally known to all the cubs.

    I should probably apologize about the length of this first post. I suppose that it might be a little intimidating. :-) I can be verbose at times. I hope I didn’t scare anyone away. I know that there are a lot of great role players on this forum, and a lot of Old World of Darkness enthusiasts, too. I know that there are also people on this forum who have never played any oWoD games, and are here because they’re Exalted or Scion or Trinity or nWoD fans.

    I hope, with this game, to make something that is fun and accessible for all these types of players. So, if you’re a W:tA aficionado and are nostalgic for that old time game of savage horror, here you are. If you’ve never played Werewolf: the Apocalypse before, but are curious to try something new, here you are.

    I hope we can have some fun as we come together to find the answer to that timeless question:

    When will you rage?

      Basic Setting Information- Rage Across the Delaware Valley

      Here now is the basic information for the setting of Rage Across the Delaware Valley. As I noted before, this is information that should be considered generally available with minimal effort for any player character, and can be considered IC knowledge for each. In other words, this information is commonly known or assumed by the majority of the Garou of the Delaware Valley. Do not assume that this information is 100% accurate. I am telling you, flat out, OOC, that some of this information is right, some is wrong and most is incomplete.

      As we progress with character creation, I will communicate with each player some more specific knowledge that their characters have access to by virtue of their backgrounds, home septs and tribal affiliations. Some of it will contradict the general information contained here, as different tribes have different ideas about what is going on, and why. Your characters must decide for themselves what, if anything, they believe.

      Please note also that I am running a game, not writing a sourcebook. I am not making an attempt to create an exhaustive treatment of the supernatural history of Philadelphia and its surroundings. Nor am I writing a paper on the history of Philadelphia, Delaware and South Jersey. This is a brief overview only. There are a great many more details of the setting, some of which will become apparent as the game goes on, and some of which will remain mysterious.

      As with any RP setting, I am not trying to present a 100% factually accurate view of the region. A great many details will be different in the interest of creating what I hope is a more interesting game and setting. So, players who happen to be from the Delaware Valley (as I am), or who are familiar with the region, don’t be surprised to find details about the area are different in the game when compared to real life.

      A [Very] Brief Overview of Certain Garou Historical Highlights
      Prior to the arrival of the Europeans (or “Wyrmbringers,” if you prefer), what we call the Delaware River Valley was part of the heartland of the Leni Lenape nation. The Lenape were kinfolk of the Croatan tribe, and the caerns in the region were tended to and protected by the Croatan. When the Croatan sacrificed themselves to stop Eater of Souls, the Wendigo and Uktena tried to move into their former territory, to protect the Croatan lands and tend their caerns. The Delaware Valley was much closer to Wendigo lands than Uktena lands, so it was largely Wendigo who travelled to the area.

      The Wendigo, however, focused largely on the area north of the Delaware Valley, such as the lands of the Iroquois (who had also been Croatan kinfolk). As a result, the Delaware Valley had very few native Garou protectors when the European Garou began to arrive. A number of Croatan caerns were completely abandoned, and even the larger ones that the Wendigo had sent packs to were suffering from the lack of care. The European Garou used this situation to their advantage, pushing the Wendigo out of the territories and taking over the former Croatan caerns for themselves.

      Between the appropriated Croatan caerns and a few newly constructed ones, the Delaware Valley was able to support a larger than average Garou population (see the note below the caern listing for more on this phenomenon). Over time, the Children of Gaia and the Glass Walkers tended to be the most influential tribes of the region.

      In the late 1990s, something happened to the Shadow Lords of the Sept of the Thundering Pines. The details still aren’t completely clear, but somehow the garou of the sept (and the nearby Piney Sept of the Bone Gnawers) became tainted, falling to the service of the Wyrm. They kept this secret for several years, working clandestinely with the local Black Spiral Dancers, causing harm to the Gaian cause wherever they could. Over time, the other local garou, especially the Silver Fangs of the Sept of the New World’s Glory, began to suspect that something was wrong and started to investigate.

      Knowing that their secret would soon be out, the tainted Shadow Lords and their Black Spiral Dancer allies made plans for a devastating sneak attack, and waited for a sign. When a Red Star appeared in the sky, they knew their sign had come. They revealed their taint to the world by attacking the Fairmount Sept during a moot. Wiping out the entire Sept except for a single kin survivor, the act kicked off a war that lasted for twelve years. If a garou in the Delaware Valley mentions “the war,” this is the conflict to which she is referring.

      Every other Sept in the Delaware Valley became involved in the struggle. Many nomadic packs travelled to the region to lend aid, and a number of allied septs from outside the area sent packs to help as well. The tide truly turned, however, when several packs of powerful Shadow Lords travelled from their homeland in Eastern Europe to join the war on the side of the Gaian Garou and destroy their fallen tribemates. Eventually, the war was won. The Caern of the Thundering Pines was cleansed and reclaimed, the tainted Shadow Lords were destroyed to the last werewolf and the Black Spiral Dancers forced back into their holes.

      It has been three years since the fighting stopped, and the region’s Garou are still recovering. The Fairmount caern is still inactive, every attempt to reopen it having failed. Two caerns in Delaware that were lost remain lost, and many packs that go seeking them do not return. Most of the packs from afar that traveled to the region to aid in the fight have returned home, though some chose to remain and make their home here. The one good thing that came of the war was a renewed sense of cooperation among the Garou of the Delaware Valley, as the septs forced to work closely together for their very survival now work to maintain those close ties.

      Active Caerns

      Sept of the Thundering Pines
      Level: 4
      Type: Stamina
      Totem: Thunderbird
      Tribal Structure: Open, Shadow Lord led
      Location: Wharton State Forest, New Jersey Pine Barrens
      Notes: Once one of the most powerful Croatan caerns, the Sept of the Thundering Pines was taken by the Shadow Lords when they came to the New World. The Shadow Lord sept that had been controlling and “protecting” the caern somehow became corrupted by the Wyrm. Joining with the local Black Spiral Dancers, they attacked the other local septs of the Delaware Valley in a devastating surprise attack during a time known as the “Week of Nightmares” in 1999, destroying the Fairmount Sept and precipitating a war that lasted for over a decade. With the corrupt Shadow Lords finally defeated, the caern was reclaimed by other Shadow Lords from around the world who came to put down their tainted brethren and cleanse the caern. The past three years have been spent cleansing every last vestige of the taint, bringing the caern back to Gaia in full and helping the garou of the entire Delaware Valley to heal after the devastating conflict.

      Sept of the New World’s Glory
      Level: 4
      Type: Willpower
      Totem: Eagle
      Tribal Structure: Silver Fang controlled, open to others by invitation
      Location: French Creek State Park, Pennsylvania
      Notes: The Caern of the New World’s Glory claims the distinction of being the first caern that the Europeans actually built in the New World, instead of stealing from the native Garou. It is an important stronghold of the Silver Fangs, and is the ancestral home of an influential family of House Wyrmfoe, the Dawnstars, who have passed leadership of the caern through their lineage for centuries.

      Marshall Island Sept
      Level: 3
      Type: Humor
      Totem: Dana
      Tribal Structure: Open, primarily Fianna and Children of Gaia, traditionally led by Fianna
      Location: Treasure Island Scout Reservation, Point Pleasant, Pennsylvania
      Notes: Located on the unused portions of an island that is part of a Boy Scout Reservation in the Delaware River, the Marshall Island Sept has been multi-tribal since its inception. Traditionally led by the Fianna, but with a strong Children of Gaia presence and representation from numerous other tribes, the Marshall Island Sept was a beacon of cooperation in the Delaware Valley, especially after the fall of the Unity caern at Fairmount Park. It is said that the Fae have a stronghold of their own nearby, and that the Marshall Island Sept has friendly relations with these “changelings,” including oaths of mutual defense.

      Blessed Harvest Sept
      Level: 3
      Type: Plenty
      Totem: Corn Maiden
      Tribal Structure: Children of Gaia controlled, open to others by invitation
      Location: Salisbury Township, Lancaster County, PA
      Notes: Located in the farmlands of Lancaster County, the garou of the Blessed Harvest Sept have made pacts with the native spirits of agriculture, nurturing both Gaia and the local human population. It is said that some of the Children of Gaia of this sept are part of a local camp that advocates a lifestyle based on that of the Amish, rejecting advanced technology to be closer to Gaia, but if that is true they seldom leave their own territory.

      Sept of Dockyard Miracles
      Level: 2
      Type: Stealth
      Totem: Flea
      Tribal Structure: Bone Gnawers controlled, open to others by invitation
      Location: Waterfront, Camden, NJ
      Notes: Impossible to find without the help of its Bone Gnawer keepers, the stealthy caern claims the entire Camden Waterfront as its bawn. Originally a closed sept, the Bone Gnawers began allowing outsiders into their territory during the war with the corrupt Shadow Lords. A base of operations that the Wyrm could not locate was invaluable, and many packs owed their lives to the Sept’s timely efforts to rescue and hide them from pursuers. Besides earning the appreciation (if not always the respect) of the other septs in the Delaware Valley for their efforts, the Bone Gnawers of this sept are still known as important information brokers.

      Society Hill Sept
      Level: 2
      Type: Streetwise
      Totem: Clashing Boom Boom
      Tribal Structure: Open, Glass Walker controlled
      Location: Society Hill Hotel, Society Hill neighborhood, Center City, Philadelphia, PA
      Notes: After the fall of the Fairmount Sept and the disappearance of the New Castle Sept, the Society Hill Sept became the most important Glass Walker stronghold in the Delaware Valley. Built around an inn/bar in the wealthy and historic Society Hill neighborhood, the important structures of the caern are said to be underground. It is also said that the caern itself has become overcrowded, with too many Glass Walkers present for the caern to easily support. Despite its small size, it was said to be an important strategic location during the war with the corrupt Shadow Lords, housing a powerful transportation fetish of some kind that helped turn the tide. The leader of the sept is an ailing old Glass Walker Mafioso who is not expected to live much longer.

      Sept of Iron Visions
      Level: 1 (Formerly 3)
      Type: Gnosis
      Totem: Stourbridge Lion
      Tribal Structure: Glass Walkers, open to others by invitation
      Location: A now abandoned rail yard in South Philadelphia, PA
      Notes: A small sept built around the train yard where a powerful locomotive spirit appeared to the Glass Walkers almost 200 years ago, this caern has been steadily losing power for decades. A single Glass Walker pack work to stave off its destruction. The caern was briefly lost to the tainted Shadow Lords during the conflict with them, but was recovered and cleansed without any permanent damage being done.

      Sept of Friends
      Level: 1
      Type: Calm
      Totem: Unicorn
      Tribal Structure: Open, Children of Gaia controlled
      Location: Frankford Friends Meeting House, Frankford, Philadelphia, PA
      Notes: The Quakers (also known as the “Society of Friends”) of the Delaware Valley had long numbered Children of Gaia kinfolk among their number. The Frankford Friends Meeting House is the oldest surviving meeting house in Philadelphia. The Children of Gaia have seamlessly integrated the caern’s functions into those of the building, which is still used as for Quaker meetings and as a community center.

      Germantown Volunteers Sept
      Level: 1
      Type: Courage
      Totem: Boar
      Tribal Structure: Open, Get of Fenris dominated
      Location: Old Fire Station, Germantown, Philadelphia, PA
      Notes: Built around the first volunteer fire station in Germantown (which was not then a part of Philadelphia). The building, which takes most of the block, is still a functioning fire house (now absorbed into the Philadelphia Fire Department). All the fire fighters assigned to the station are kin, primarily of the Get of Fenris, but including a few Uktena, Glass Walkers, Fianna and Bone Gnawers as well. Even the occasional Garou joins for a time, though their Rage makes it difficult for them to be involved in such pursuits. The caern incorporates the entire block, which includes a bar right behind the station where the firefighters go to drink after their shifts.

      Cohanzick Sept
      Level: 1
      Type: Memory
      Totem: North Wind
      Tribal Structure: Closed, Wendigo Only
      Location: Cohanzick Tribal Grounds, Fairton, NJ
      Notes: Built very recently on land that was reclaimed by the Nanticoke Leni Lenape Tribal Nation as the Cohanzick Tribal Grounds. The Wendigo and their kin assisted in this reclamation, especially since the area apparently included a small, lost caern that once belonged to the Croatan. Once the land was back in tribal hands, the Wendigo reopened the caern and have protected it fiercely.

      Sept of the Mother’s Needles
      Level: Unknown
      Type: Unknown
      Totem: Unknown
      Tribal Structure: Closed, Black Furies Only
      Location: Brendan T. Byrn State Forest (Formerly Lebanon State Forest), New Jersey Pine Barrens
      Notes: The only remaining Black Fury stronghold of note in the Delaware Valley, very little is known about this caern. The Furies emphatically refuse to allow outsiders into their caern. Practically nothing is generally known about this caern beyond the mere fact of its existence, and the fact that it dates back to the time of the Croatan. The Black Fury refusal of outsiders has led to increased friction between them and the local Wendigo, who would like very much to visit the ancient Croatan site.

      Wait a minute, that’s a lot of caerns…

      Yes, it is, and it gets worse if you look down and see the list of defunct caerns. As has always been their custom, the writers of White Wolf were careful not to specify how many caerns a particular area should hold, or a definitive number for the garou population. The idea was that an area had as many caerns as the ST wanted it to have, according to the needs of the setting. However, by any standard, the Delaware Valley has a LOT of caerns.

      This is not an oversight, and this is not ST fiat. The Garou of the Delaware Valley are aware that there is something unusual about the area. There should not be this many caerns here. However, despite centuries of occupation, no modern Garou knows why there are so many caerns in the local area. Perhaps the Croatan knew, but if so, that secret died with them. There are plenty of theories, but with no definitive answer to the riddle forthcoming, the local Garou generally don’t think about it anymore, simply accepting it as a quirk of the local land and umbra that wellsprings of spiritual energy allowing for the creation of caerns are more common than usual here.

      I’m sure it’s nothing to worry about. ;-)

      Defunct Caerns

      Fairmount Sept
      Level: 0 (Formerly 4)
      Type: Unity
      Totem: Duck
      Tribal Structure: Was open, traditionally led by Children of Gaia and Glass Walkers
      Location: Fairmount Park, West Philadelphia, PA
      Notes: Built in massive Fairmount Park in West Philadelphia, the Fairmount Sept was once the center of Garou society in the Delaware Valley. A powerful caern of unity, open to all, it was easily the largest sept in the region. The sept boasted members from all twelve tribes of the Garou Nation (and Stargazers, who at that time had not yet left the west), was frequently visited by individuals and packs from all over the eastern United States and was a beacon of hope and cooperation.

      So, when the tainted Shadow Lords and their Black Spiral Dancer allies made their plans to launch a surprise attack on the Garou of the Delaware Valley, they knew what their first target would be. The appearance of a Red Star in the night sky was the sign they had been waiting for. The fact that the Fairmount Sept was coincidentally holding a moot that very same evening simply made the timing more providential, as if the Wyrm itself had blessed their efforts by making sure that every member of the Sept would be on hand… and distracted… for the slaughter.

      Three packs from the Sept of the Thundering Pines and one from the Piney Sept attended the moot, taking advantage of the extreme openness of the caern to infiltrate it. When the time was right, those four packs turned on the caern’s guardians, slaughtering them from within. Then the real attack came, with the rest of the tainted Shadow Lords and dozens of Black Spiral Dancers assaulting the sept simultaneously once the defenders were silenced. There were no garou survivors- only a single young girl, kin to the Children of Gaia, managed to escape, making her way to the Sept of Dockyard Miracles to tell the gruesome tale and expose the corruption of the tainted Shadow Lords, thus beginning the war.

      To make matters worse, the servants of the Wyrm enacted some foul rite, destroying the caern’s spiritual connections in a single night. Twice since then have the local garou attempted to reopen the caern. Both times, the ritemaster literally exploded, and all other participating garou were wracked with pain. Until the source of the problem can be found, the elders of the Delaware Valley have unanimously forbidden any more attempts to reopen the caern. Packs from the Sept of the New World’s Glory, the Society Hill Sept, the Sept of Friends and the Germantown Volunteers Sept take turns patrolling Fairmount Park.

      New Castle Sept
      Level: 0 (Formerly 3)
      Type: Plenty
      Totem: Cockroach
      Tribal Structure: Was open, Glass Walker controlled
      Location: Wilmington, DE
      Notes: The largest caern in Delaware and a stronghold of the Glass Walkers, the New Castle Sept was built on the top 5 floors of one of Wilmington’s major office buildings. The Glass Walkers of the sept embraced a corporate culture, dipping their fingers into dozens of industries throughout Delaware, and had frequent confrontations with the local vampires. Despite their local concerns, they still helped to fight threats to the entire region, and participated in the war against the corrupted Shadow Lords. Until they disappeared. One day, without warning, all contact was lost with the sept. Packs that were away from the caern at the time returned to investigate, and were not heard from again. When packs from the other septs of the region tried to make contact, they realized with shock that no one could remember exactly which office building the caern had occupied. Even systematic searches of the office buildings of Wilmington found no evidence that a caern had ever existed. The mystery plagues the Glass Walkers of the Delaware Valley to this day. What could make an entire caern disappear?

      Blue Hole Sept
      Level: 0 (Formerly 2)
      Type: Enigmas
      Totem: Bikanaki’hat the Water Keeper, the Great Toad (A water elemental and Lenape spirit)
      Tribal Structure: Was open, Population was split between Black Furies, Children of Gaia and Uktena
      Location: Winslow Fish and Wildlife Management Area, New Jersey Pine Barrens
      Notes: Centered around an unusually clear lake in the Pine Barrens (groundwater in the region is usually tea colored due to the high acidity of the soil), this former Croatan caern was protected by a powerful water elemental. The sept was besieged during the war with the corrupted Shadow Lords; during one of the battles, a pack of Black Spiral Dancers snuck into the caern’s heart and defeated Bikanaki’hat, capturing him in a fetish and carrying him away. The surviving garou of the sept were unable to maintain the caern, reluctantly abandoning the site and moving to other septs when the caern went dormant. It is said that the caern could be reopened if Bikanaki’hat could be rescued and freed from his fetish prison, but all attempts to find him have failed.

      Piney Sept
      Level: 0 (Formerly 1)
      Type: Primal-Urge
      Totem: Raccoon
      Tribal Structure: Bone Gnawers, was open to others by invitation
      Location: Southern Edge of the Wharton State Forest, New Jersey Pine Barrens
      Notes: A small sept in a community at the southern edge of the Pine Barrens, the Piney Sept was populated by Bone Gnawers and their kin. The humans of the area were (and are) derided by other residents of New Jersey as “Pineys,” considered inbred, backwards, dangerous hicks who flouted society and the law. The Bone Gnawers and kinfolk who lived among this population helped encourage these views, since it tended to keep the authorities from the area, which helped them protect the rare Bone Gnawer wolf kin that also secretly lived near the caern. The Bone Gnawers were closely allied with the Shadow Lords of the Sept of the Thundering Pines. Some say that the Piney Bone Gnawers willingly joined the tainted Shadow Lords in service to the Wyrm; others claim that the Shadow Lords tricked the Bone Gnawers, tainting them in some way by deception or force. Regardless of the truth, the Piney Bone Gnawers (and many of their kin) became tainted, and did fight alongside the Wyrm-corrupted Shadow Lords. Their caern was corrupted during the process, and the land was considered too tainted to reopen as a caern when the battles were finally complete. The community is now a small abandoned village, known to the locals as a ghost town.

      Canaresse Sept
      Level: 0 (Formerly 1)
      Type: Healing
      Totem: Bear
      Tribal Structure: Was open, primarily populated by Children of Gaia and Fianna
      Location: Bombay Hook National Wildlife Refuge, Kent County, Delaware
      Notes: Built at a wildlife refuge in the salt marshes, this small sept was devoted primarily to ecological concerns. They tended to concern themselves less with violent solutions and direct confrontation with the forces of corruption and more with healing the damage left by such confrontations and protecting the natural world, even while surrounded by the works of humanity. They did fight when necessary, but their focus on healing instead of combat, and their choice of Bear as their sept totem, did not endear them to many of the other local garou. Regardless, when the caern was attacked in force by Black Spiral Dancers during the war with the corrupted Shadow Lords, the other septs sent packs to their aid, but they were too late, and the caern had already fallen. Since the end of the war, three attempts have been made to reopen the caern. The first and second were met with massive attacks by the forces of the Wyrm, leading to the failure of the ritual. The participants of the third attempt simply disappeared without a trace.

      Delaware

      You may have noticed that there are no active caerns in Delaware- only two listed as defunct. One of the septs disappeared entirely. The other was destroyed in battle, but one of the groups sent to try to rebuild it disappeared without a trace.

      It is known that there is at least one major Black Spiral Dancer Hive in Delaware, and probably more than one. Packs of Black Spiral Dancers have been chased back to Delaware by garou from the rest of the Delaware Valley, as well as from neighboring Maryland. Once they are inside the state, however, they almost invariably lose their prey, sometimes under circumstances that defy belief. All attempts to locate these hives have failed. Packs and individuals sent into Delaware are frequently ambushed, and sometimes simply disappear. Spirits queried about the situation seem to have no answers. All attempts to investigate have failed- sometimes disastrously. Several septs in Maryland have outright forbidden their younger packs from travelling through Delaware, and some of the Elders in the Delaware Valley septs have spoken of doing the same.

      Something strange is happening in Delaware. Something very strange.

      Tribal Presence

      Black Furies: One of the smaller tribes, the Black Furies have never had a particularly strong presence in the Delaware Valley. Their only major stronghold is the mysterious Sept of the Mother’s Needles, which they keep isolated from all outsiders (this has caused some particular tensions between them and the local Wendigo). Besides this sept, they are present in small numbers at several of the other open septs of the Delaware Valley. They have small numbers of kin throughout the area, including a number located in Bryn Mawr, PA, a number of whom are part of the faculty and staff of Bryn Mawr College.

      Bone Gnawers: With the death of so many Glass Walkers during the war with the corrupt Shadow Lords, the Bone Gnawers are almost certainly the second largest tribe in the Delaware Valley (after the Children of Gaia). Their numbers have never made them particularly influential though, which is not unusual for them; they are, however, known to be the most well informed Garou of the region. Their only remaining stronghold in the region is the Sept of Dockyard Miracles in Camden, the safest caern in the Delaware Valley- it’s impossible to find unless you already know where it is. They used to have a second sept in the Pine Barrens, where they protected a significant number of their lupine kinfolk, but that sept (for whatever reason) joined with the tainted Shadow Lords and had to be put down. Their role in the war led to increased appreciation for the Bone Gnawers, if not necessarily increased respect. Regardless, the other tribes still sometimes come to them, metaphorical hats in hand, when they need information they can get no where else.

      Children of Gaia: The Children of Gaia are almost certainly the largest tribe in the Delaware Valley, and have historically been one of the most influential (alongside the Glass Walkers). Many of them first came to the region with their Quaker kin, and claim to have been in part responsible for the relatively peaceful contact between the European settlers and the Leni Lenape natives. The Wendigo scoff at these claims. In any case, the Children of Gaia control several of the local caerns and have a significant population in a number of caerns that they don’t control. The loss of the Fairmount caern has hurt them deeply, however, and they are at the forefront of attempts to reopen the caern and reestablish the sept. They also work diligently to maintain the close ties between the septs of the Delaware Valley that were established during the course of the war with the corrupt Shadow Lords.

      Fianna: There were a significant number of Fianna kin with each major migration of the Irish to Philadelphia and its surroundings. Most of their kin are in the city itself, and though the Fianna have no strongholds in the city, there are small numbers of them present in the local septs, and they had significant numbers at the now defunct Fairmount Sept. They are the traditional leaders of the Marshall Island Sept, where they are said to have powerful alliances with the local faeries.

      Get of Fenris: The kinfolk of the Get of Fenris, unsurprisingly, were originally settled in Germantown, though they have since spread throughout the region. They are closely allied to the Silver Fangs of the Sept of the New World’s Glory, and there are traditionally several Fenrir members of the sept. They founded the Germantown Volunteers Sept as their kin founded the first volunteer firefighting company of Germantown and have maintained the caern ever since.

      Glass Walkers: The Glass Walkers traditionally have been one of the most influential tribes in the Delaware Valley, but they suffered greatly during the war with the tainted Shadow Lords. Many of their tribemates, including most of the local elders, were slaughtered at the Fairmount Sept, and a major caern of theirs in Wilmington, Delaware mysteriously disappeared during the fighting. Another caern of theirs fell, but was reclaimed before permanent damage was done. They lost most of their leadership, and the current leader of their last major stronghold, the Society Hill Sept, is said to be old and failing. Fortunately, their kin populations survived the war largely unharmed, and there has been a slow influx of young Glass Walkers from outside the area, so they are starting to rebuild. Unfortunately, they do not have the space, and the Society Hill Sept is starting to get a little overcrowded. Put simply, the Glass Walkers of the Delaware Valley stand at a crossroads.

      Red Talons: There is only one Red Talon currently known to be living in the Delaware Valley. Sunset Howl is the Galliard of Rorg’s Eyes, an all Lupus pack that was nomadic until coming to the Delaware Valley during the war with the corrupt Shadow Lords. They joined the local Gaian Garou in their struggle and settled down with the new Sept of the Thundering Pines when the fighting stopped, where they have been ever since. There was one Red Talon, a young Ragabash, living in the Fairmount Sept, but she was killed when the sept was wiped out. The Red Talons are known to have a small sept in the Poconos, the Sept of the Hidden Peak. Rumor says they like to eat skiers.

      Shadow Lords: The Shadow Lords established a presence in the Delaware Valley relatively early. Some of the first members of the tribe who came to the New World settled in New Jersey, taking what is now called the Caern of the Thundering Pines as their protectorate. However they came to possess the caern (and accounts differ, especially if you ask the Wendigo), they protected it ably and well for centuries. Until they fell. To this day, nobody knows how or why they fell to the Wyrm; trying to guess the reason and method is a favorite campfire game of young Garou in the region. However it happened, they turned on us all, and needed to be wiped out. The war was long and bloody, and might still be going on if large numbers of Shadow Lords from all over hadn’t come to put down their tainted brethren. After the war, those newcomer Shadow Lords who chose to stay reclaimed the caern for themselves, which the devastated inhabitants of the other septs were happy to let them do, so long as they pledged to keep the structure of the caern open. The elders of the sept are now mostly Eastern European garou who work to restore the honor of their tribe in the Delaware Valley.

      Silent Striders: There is only one Silent Strider currently known to be living in the Delaware Valley. Chases-the-Horizon is the Ahroun and leader of Rorg’s Eyes, an all Lupus pack that was nomadic until coming to the Delaware Valley during the war with the corrupt Shadow Lords. They joined the local Gaian Garou in their struggle and settled down with the new Sept of the Thundering Pines when the fighting stopped, where they have been ever since. Occasionally Silent Striders from other regions pass through the area, delivering messages or portents. There were several other Silent Striders who were part of the Fairmount Sept, but they were killed when the Sept was destroyed. They are said to have had a small community of kin among the city’s middle-eastern population, but no one has kept track of these individuals, assuming they still live.

      Silver Fangs: The Silver Fangs have long been a major presence in the Delaware Valley. The Sept of the New World’s Glory claims to have been the first caern built by the Europeans in the New World, rather than appropriated from the Wendigo or Uktena or built on the ruins of an abandoned Croatan site. The validity of this claim is open to debate. In any case, the caern has been in the hands of a single Silver Fang family, the Dawnstars, since it was first founded. The leader of the caern styles himself a “High Lord,” and has always been a Dawnstar. The Dawnstars are one of the most prominent (and purely bred) families of House Wyrmfoe, and are staunch supporters of King Albrecht. The caern is currently led by High Lord William Dark-Eye Dawnstar, an old Elder Ahroun who was a renowned warrior in his day. All of his Garou children and several grandchildren have died, however, leaving a single young garou grandchild who has yet to complete their rite of passage to be the heir apparent. It is whispered that most of his descendants died fighting the same foe, a powerful Vampire who wounded William in battle decades ago, giving him the deed name “dark-eye,” when the Silver Fang tried to recover a powerful family heirloom of the Dawnstars (more on that below). There are several families of Silver Fang kin in the Delaware Valley in addition to the Dawnstars, all of whom are tightly monitored by their garou relatives.

      Stargazers: There are almost no Stargazers currently known to be living in the Delaware Valley, and no known community of Stargazer kin. The few that were here in years past left when their tribe relocated to Asia, or died when Fairmount Sept fell, with a single exception. The mysterious elder Galliard, Threefold Path, still resides in the area. He is highly reclusive, rarely appearing at moots or other gatherings and holding position with no sept. Much of his time is spent travelling up and down the eastern seaboard or through the depths of the Umbra, though he makes his home in the Delaware Valley. It is rumored that he will soon depart this world for good.

      Uktena: Dreamrunner, an elderly Theurge, is the Ritemaster of the Marshall Island Sept, and has lived in the Delaware Valley for over 50 years. He is the only Uktena in the region of Native American stock. However, the Uktena have bred with many oppressed and downtrodden peoples over the years, and have kin of many different ethnicities. There are a small number of African American Uktena spread throughout the Delaware Valley. It’s a little known fact, but some Uktena have also chosen to breed into Jewish populations around the world. There are not many of them, but most of them in the United States call New York City, New Jersey and Philadelphia home. There used to be more of both groups in the area, but most of them were based out of the Fairmount Sept and the Blue Hole Sept, both of which no longer exist. Their kin are still around, however, so their populations may rebound, given time.

      Wendigo: The Wendigo control a single caern in the Delaware Valley, the Cohanzick Sept at the recently reclaimed Cohanzick Tribal Grounds in South Jersey. There are a small number of packs based there, and a small community of their kin among the Nanticoke Leni Lenape Tribal Nation that reclaimed the land. Prior to its destruction, the Fairmount Sept included at least three Wendigo members, but all three were killed when the Sept was destroyed in 1999. Their kin, largely descended from Native Americans that moved to Philadelphia in the mid-20th century as construction workers working on the city’s skyscrapers (“Urban Indians”), still live in West Philly. At least one of their children has undergone First Change since the fall of Fairmount, and was accepted by the Wendigo of the Cohanzick Sept. There are also rumors that one of the new Canadian players on the Philadelphia Wings might be Wendigo kin; several of the Canadian players on the team are ethnically Iroquois, including the new owner.

      Some Noteworthy Caerns Outside the Immediate Area

      Sept of the Boardwalk- A Glass Walker dominated Level 3 caern of plenty at the Wyld West Casino in Atlantic City, NJ.
      Trexler Sept- A Glass Walker dominated Level 2 caern with some Bone Gnawer presence at Texler Memorial Park in Allentown, PA, named for Glass Walker ancestor Harry Clay Trexler.
      Sept of the Hidden Peak- A closed Red Talon controlled Level 1 Caern of Strength hidden in the Poconos, in the Appalachian Mountains of Pennsylvania.
      Trenton Green Sept- A mixed Bone Gnawer and Glass Walker Level 1 caern at Cadwalader Park in Trenton, NJ.
      Long Pier Sept- An open Get of Fenris controlled Level 1 caern on the waterfront of Cape May, NJ, near the Coast Guard Training Facility.

      Fera

      Bastet: The Croatan were not the only shape-shifters to call the Delaware Valley home. The were-cougars were also native to this area. Their feline kin have been extirpated from the region, but rumors persist that some of the solitary werecats remain. During the war with the tainted Shadow Lords, one pack claimed to have been saved when a powerful werecougar came from nowhere to join them in battle, only to disappear soon after. Several packs reported seeing a similar figure during the final battle to reclaim the Caern of the Thundering Pines, but if he was there, he was nowhere to be found after the fight was done.

      Corax: There are known to be a few Corax native to Philadelphia. One works as a private investigator, the other as a reporter. They occasionally provide information to the Garou, but do not tend to mingle in our society overmuch. Perhaps we intimidate them?

      Gurahl: Are there Gurahl in the Delaware Valley? Well, there’s a story going around about that. The Silver Fangs of the Sept of the New World’s Glory claim that they found out about a Gurahl in the area. Knowing what renowned healers the Gurahl were, the Silver Fangs put aside their prejudice and went to this Gurahl, asking him to heal their comrades wounded in fighting the Wyrm. Instead, the Gurahl attacked them. As to whether the Gurahl was wyrm tainted, or simply still angry about the abuses his kind had suffered, the rumors disagree. What they do not disagree about is the result- the Silver Fang pack killed the Gurahl in self-defense. Are there more in the area? Well, on the one hand, the Gurahl the Silver Fangs killed had to come from somewhere. On the other hand, there aren’t many Gurahl left in the world, and they’re supposed to be pretty solitary. Who can say?

      Mokole: You’re kidding, right? The whole “aligators in the sewer” thing is in New York, not here.

      Ratkin: Like any other urban area, Philadelphia, Camden and the other cities of the Delaware Valley are known to harbor Ratkin. Encounters with them are rare, however, and their numbers are presumably few. After all, they suffered like every other Fera from the War of Rage, and if their numbers had recovered sufficiently to be a threat, the Garou would undoubtedly know of it. They are largely beneath notice.

      The Wyrm

      Black Spiral Dancers: The fallen tribe has long had a significant presence in the Delaware Valley, much to the consternation of the Gaian garou. While their numbers were curtailed during the war, they were smart enough to effect a strategic withdrawal when it became obvious that their tainted Shadow Lord allies would fall. It is rumored that Delaware has become their stronghold, now that no Gaian caerns remain active there.

      Known Hives: Exact locations are difficult to pin down. The garou of Philadelphia have long believed that there is a hive buried somewhere under the city, but they have been unable to locate it despite decades of searching. There are believed to be at least two hives in Delaware and another in South Jersey, but their exact locations are harder to pin down. One small hive in the South Jersey city of Bridgeton was wiped out during the war, however.

      There are troublesome rumors and mutterings among the spirits about the Marcellus Shale in Pennsylvania. The environmental damage being caused by hydraulic fracking is already proving to be a boon for the creation of Banes. The possibility of the Black Spirals using the disruption to create a new, powerful Hive cannot be ignored, but so far no direct evidence of it has been found.

      Others

      Vampires: There are a lot of vampires in Philadelphia and Camden. A whole lot. They’re powerful, and they’re dangerous. To make matters worse, they seem to travel in packs. Don’t try to take them on alone, unless absolutely necessary. The local vampires seem to be organized along some twisted religion, with leaders calling themselves priests and bishops and such. The most powerful vampire in the area is, in fact, their Bishop and leader, a deformed monstrosity calling himself “The Warlord.” Whatever his real name may be, he lives up to his moniker. A twelve foot tall behemoth of twisted flesh, he moves faster than something his size should be able to, is as strong as any garou and is attended by a small army of deformed minions, disgusting mockeries of life twisted into war machines. He has four arms, razor sharp teeth and claws and eyes as black as the abyss. He wears no clothes, since his skin is like the toughest armor, and to his back is strapped his greatest prize- Dawn’s Pride, a Grand Klaive belonging to the Dawnstar family. The Warlord took it as a trophy when he slayed High Lord William Dawnstar’s father, Edward. William tried to get it back, and lost an eye for his trouble (that’s why they call him Dark Eye). All of William’s garou children, and some of his grandchildren, died trying to get it back.

      Changelings: The Fae, or “changelings” as they are sometimes called, have a strong presence in the Delaware Valley. They are believed to have a stronghold near the Marshall Island caern, and are said to have very good relations with the Garou of that sept, especially the Fianna (as might be expected). Beyond that, they mostly keep a low profile as far as the garou are concerned, but it is known that Independence Hall, where both the Declaration of Independence and the Constitution were written, is under their control. All cubs that spend any time in Philadelphia are warned of this, lest they inadvertently trespass where they shouldn’t.

      Mages: While there are undoubtedly human sorcerers in the Delaware Valley, they do not commonly have dealings with the Garou. Or, if they do, those dealings are not generally known. Some unexplained phenomena and happenings may be their doing, but the wise do not seek them out.

      Hunters: It has become obvious over the last 15 years that some new power has risen among the humans. Individuals with no obvious supernatural connection, who do not smell of Wyrm, Weaver or Wyld, have demonstrated impressive powers and a will to destroy anything non-human. Their powers are odd, but not overwhelming, their numbers are small, they do not seem to be organized and they tend to focus more on the vampires than they do on us. Most Garou try to avoid such creatures when they are encountered, as fighting them is rarely worth the trouble with so many other foes to face.


      And there we have it. That should be enough to get started brainstorming character concepts. I look forward to hearing from any interested players. :-)
