Now, I get where you're coming from, too, in regards to the East being largely about Jungles and all, and, my understanding of Aztec culture is that the two aren't entirely unmarriable.                 Thus, you character could be part of a noble house, dedicated to your clan and your way of life, deovted to the City Mother and her imposed culture and government, but, after your exaltation, could realize that, ultimately, the way things are is going to cause the City to spiral into self-destruction. However, this is the only life you know. Plus, now a buncha dick-foreigners are coming in and beginning to mosey in on your turf (I mean both NPC AND PC foreigners, mind). That opens up not only conflict in terms of inter-PC objectives, but also conflict in terms of what your PC knows and what he is comfortable with.

                        Generally speaking, the Scavenger Lands are culturally very different from the East, even if they are technically part of it. The Hundred Kingdoms are many, small, their power balance always shifting, and have something of a medieval Europe feel to them.

                        I don't know if you've read the King's Assassin series... It's probably not necessary... But an assassin loyal to one of these many kingdoms, who preserves it against more powerful opponents not by military strength, but by careful murder, would fit right in. Though it's just an idea. A diplomat-spy would work just as well - Elias is basically this (canon Haslanti Night Caste) and he's pretty cool.

                        On the other hand, because of the balkanized nature of the setting, it is very easy for a spy, thief or assassin for hire to wander the Kingdoms under a cover identity (say, minstrel, musician, merchant), getting hired from place to place without anyone ever really figuring out his true job. That assumes little loyalty to a single place, though.

                        Going from Greek inspirations... Orpheus as a secret spy as well as a perfect singer, or a wandering strongman like Hercules... None of which would work best as a Night Caste, but I don't remember much famous spies/assassins from Greek myth or history, sadly.

                        Sorry, I'm not having great ideas right now.

                                Hey everyone,

                                I have been extremely busy and have had so much chaos going on with the group I have been STing, work, and the testing, but I now have everything taken care of. Sorry I have not gotten back to the thread in a while. I am planning on playing a Twilight Crafter/Swordsman. His name is Hyuga.

                                He pretends to be gruff, but is secretly a kind person that really just wants to be a friend to everyone. He can't help but try to look cool and in control the whole time. He was born in paragon and sought to get out of the busy life of traders. He just wants to do his thing crafting and making friends. He has no real interest in making money, building alliances, or settling down.

                                His father had been a blacksmith and he sought to be like him since he was a wee lad. Sadly, his father died when he was eight years old. His mother raised him after his father's death until she finally died of overwork shortly after he turned 15. At this time he sought a job and was brought in by a friend of his dad, a master blacksmith in the city. Working for this gruff man, he picked up his habits quickly, but never forgot the loving care of his parents. He worked for the artisan and showed plenty of aptitude for the job. He even picked up Riverspeak from all of the travelling guildsmen and traders. After he turned 25, he quickly became a strong artisan himself. He eventually created many works of steel that impressed even his new guardian, Loheim. After Loheim had taught him all he knew, he sent Hyuga off to explore and get new ideas for crafting throughout creation. Hyuga became a nomad searching for inspiration for new works of art in the east. He traveled around until he was 32. He learned to craft even without the best of tools and started to appreciate the art of his objects even more.

                                Finally, he came across a town in distress. They were consistently pillaged and burned down by the surrounding beast-man tribes. After hearing about several people losing fathers, mothers, sisters, brothers, and more, he brought it upon himself to help these people with the only skill he knew: Blacksmithing. He forged many weapons for the villagers to defend themselves with. He became great friends with the villagers and stayed for many moons. Shortly after he headed out of the village, he heard the shouts and screams of friends and the other villagers. He knew the beast-men must have come back. He ran back into the village. He saw many of them falling in battle, unable to use his weapons properly and being outmatched by the beast-men. He swiftly grabbed a sword of the ground. It was then that he realized that he had never learned how to use these tools. He knew that he could never be a true artisan like this and could not help protect anyone as he was, but he was damn well going to try his best.

                                After fighting a few beast-men, he came to the middle of the city to see an anathema *a Solar* killing his closest friend, Chloe Mist, whilst laughing. Enraged he leaped at the anathema's back with his sword, but it was deftly broken and knocked away with complete ease. He rushed the Solar trying to knock him down and was swiftly knocked to the ground with a leaping kick. Stunned and very hurt, he watched through teary eyes as the Solar taunted him saying, "You have a problem puny man?" The Solar then grabbed his friend's mother by the neck and turned towards Hyuga. Once again enraged and full of adrenaline, he got up and ran at the Solar eyeing the orichalcum sword on his belt. He rushed in and managed to duck under the swift fist that Solar threw at him and dove at the sword. With all his might he tried to take and raise the sword, but it was impossible for him. The Solar then grabbed him by the neck and said, "Say goodbye to your friend," while making him face his friend's mother. With tears in his eyes he struggled and managed to kick the sword causing it to put a gash in the Solar's arm, that was carrying the woman; a complete miracle. He then felt he had solved some sort of riddle of artifact weaponry in his head. It was then that time seemed to stand still as the glory of the Unconquered Sun came upon him whilst hearing, "Use my power and knowledge well. Do not tarnish my gift. I expect many things of you." With his new caste mark and anima shining brightly, he escaped the confused and slightly blinded Solar's grip and dove for a sword and swung it deftly at his chest causing a great amount of blood to spurt upon the ground. The Solar in great amount of pain and fear, retreated quickly whilst dropping his sword. Hyuga was left there; an anathema for all the village to see. He had become something he had never believed he would... or could... he did not know what to do or how the town would react. He quickly picked up the orichalcum sword and darted from the town, never to return. He has not looked back at the town ever since....

                                This character is partially inspired by Cid from the Final Fantasy series. I will send in all his info soon. I can find a picture of him now.

                                Also, I do have the game I ST on Tuesdays, so my posting may slow down a bit then. I cannot wait to see everyone's finished characters.

                                Edit: I have uploaded a ​picture of how I imagine him as my avatar. Just imagine his skin being a bit darker. And a bit older than he looks.

                                        Looking at it there appears to be a communication error, which was entirely my fault. When I said vaguely Hellenic, I meant along the lines of:

                                        (1) The political climate is similar to Pre-Persian Wars Hellas. There are many poloi, which are culturally similar, but are constantly at low-level warfare with each other. Alliances are short-lived at best, and are full of treachery. Should one polis get significantly stronger than the others, nearby poloi will team up to put the smack down on them.
                                        (2) Each polis is comprised of a handful of Clans/Tribes/Gentes which collectively contain all Citizens. These familial groupings are the true political power in the polis. The actual government is more akin to a confederation which represents the collective Clans/Tribes/Gentes to the other poloi. Duty to your family is equivalent (and in many cases synonymous with) duty to your nation.

                                        That was really all I meant. Even here, I'm just saying Poloi because I don't want to have to keep typing out city-state all the time.
