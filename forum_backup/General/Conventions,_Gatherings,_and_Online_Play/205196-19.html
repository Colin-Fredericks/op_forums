        Max wasn't exactly eager to let go of human contact himself. Though the little guy did grunt a bit, and had to put in some effort to hold up both himself and Nate. Really, even more than the Goat's words, that sagging really made it clear there was a serious problem. "Nate..." the Pig said, note of sadness in his voice; such a good friend locked in the grip of despair... To put it mildly, this didn't do his own less-than-rosy outlook any favors. To put it more honestly, it broke his heart. With the added weight of guilt behind it, as well. After all, Max hadn't said much different in the courtroom, and it had clearly affected his friend. Except even then, the piglet had clung to one last feeble, desperate hope. Nate lacked even that much.

        Jessica's words encouraged him. Max was glad she still hadn't lost her resolve. Even so, a small bitter part of him found it a little naive. Like she was a dashing hero prepared to face down an impossible danger, like something straight out of a fairy tale. Which... didn't seem quite so stupid, after a moment's thought, given all the storybook overtones shot through everything here. In more than a few stories he'd read, it was exactly that kind of determination that led directly to victory. Max was actually a little envious, now; even thinking it, he couldn't bring himself to the same level of heroic resolve. It stung a little. He'd sometimes idly imagined he could manage it if he were suddenly pulled into a fantasy land like this. 'Probably shouldn't cling too hard to how you imagined things'd go. You didn't imagine you'd end up with a curly tail, either.'

        Still, perhaps Max was being too hard on himself. Whether or not it worked, he'd put the Keeper off-balance with his request. Even if it failed, and even though he'd whimpered the whole way through, there was some brazen trickery to it. And if it worked, it would get them all free. Big 'if,' but still. This little piggy was at least making a hell of a try at heroism. That chance of his weird angle actually working was probably what kept him going. Max had to at least see that through, for himself and everyone. 'Maybe... Maybe hearing that'll help snap him outta this.' He cleared his throat. "Nate... I-I know how y-you feel." He snuffled noisily, dabbing at his own tear-streaked face again. "There's n-not much hope or m-much of a chance. B-But I want you to know--"

        Max stopped very suddenly. If not for all the mud, the other three would have seen him go very pale at the thought he'd just had, and at what he'd just done. "The Keeper's watching," he murmured after a moment, meant solely for himself and too quiet for human ears to have picked up, but, well. None of the three nearby sets were human. He gulped, even more sure of it now that he'd said it. He rocked on his hooves a moment, dizzy. 'And you almost gave the plan away! You... You can't tell them...' The Pig breathed deep a bit, not getting anything like fresh air in that mucky hellhole. "I-I... W-Well..." He fumbled for something to say, and trembled; unable to lean on Nate, Max rested a free hand against a wall. Another breath. Two. A deep one. It bought him time to come up with something else to say, though, something else that might work.

        And he came up with another bit of trickery. Just as when he'd kept his plan secret from Nate and everyone in court, he felt filthier than if he'd been completely submerged in the mud flowing past his hooves. But Max forced himself to go for it anyway. It seemed the best chance they had. "N-Nate, J-Jessica. W-We can't stop." He gulped. "Y-You know how b-big he is on his handiwork. I-I bet you ran into s-some... t-trials tailored for you, j-just like me." Against his wishes, he turned his gaze toward the monstrous hog below. "L-Lovingly c-crafted," he added, with a sharp twinge. "I-If w-we don't keep going, our Kee-- Master w-won't be able t-to show them off. A-And he won't be happy about w-wasted effort. I-It won't go well. Y-You know it won't. W-We can't stop, e-even if we want to."

        Not that he'd said anything untrue, but he felt like a manipulative little sneak. Max looked directly into Nate's eyes, not having to fake the tears welling up again. He sniffed. "I-I'm sorry..." And looked for all the world like the child he was in shape. "B-But I-I know you." He said smiling a bit at the Goat. "Y-You're d-down now, b-but y-you're tougher than y-you think. You've... Y-You can keep going. Y-You can take it."

        As for what the goblin made of all this... Well, he gave no input, for the moment. Whatever his goal and intent, that was for Summernickel to know.
