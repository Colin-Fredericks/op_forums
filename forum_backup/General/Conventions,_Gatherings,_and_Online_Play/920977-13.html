A college graduate gets the opportunity they needed, a devious businessman gets the comeuppance they deserve, an innocent child is afflicted with tragedy that changes the course of their life forever, the unconnected situations of strangers weave into one another to bring them together.

If he cast an Unveiling spell on himself, his eyes would glow and cracks would form around them. If he cast a Fraying spell on someone, cracks would appear on the wound.

                                            Signature - The cracks shrink into words of High Speech. The High Speech describes the spell’s effect in a classic mythological sense. For the Unveiling spell, the words might read "And illusions were swept aside". For the Fraying spell, it might read "Tongues of flame had lashed against flesh".

                                            Background

                                            Sleepwalking - Timothy was born to an Awakened mother and a Sleepwalking father, who happened to be a former Thaumaturge, in Boston, Massachusetts. Due to this heritage, Timothy was born with a sensitivity for magic and knew of its existence ever since he could remember.

                                            His father resented magic. Years of practicing the art weighed heavily upon him. More often than not, magic was abused for selfish and depraved ends, causing terrible suffering. Even when using magic for nothing more than to other’s benefit, it still had a price. Save a life, and someone else would die. Prevent a disaster, and it would merely be delayed. Sooner or later, magic would cause pain and suffering. So he decided to abandon magic, believing it to be a force of evil.

                                            Before then, he married Timothy’s mother. Being Awakened, his mother couldn’t simply abandon magic like her husband had. Even if she could, she wouldn’t leave the world of magic for him. Inevitably, this created a rift that separated the two parents, eventually resulting in divorce when Timothy was seven years of age. They believed that he should have a chance at a normal life, which his Awakened mother just couldn’t provide, so his father took custody.

                                            Raised by his father alone, Timothy knew full well of his father’s resentment of magic and was taught to resent it himself, but he never really understood why. He was born with the ability to feel magic, so why did he have to reject it? Wasn’t it a part of him, and thus rejecting it meant rejecting himself? Not that it bothered him much. After his mother left, he didn’t feel much magic at all through the years. By the time he was at the tail end of high school, he suddenly felt the presence of magic. At first he didn’t even knew what he was feeling, but as he felt it more and more, the memories of his mother’s magic came back to him.

                                            The magic came from various people who looked to be members of a cult. Curiosity getting the better of him, he decided to investigate them, which lead him to a basement that set off his magical sense beyond anything he’d ever felt before. He broke in to find jars of organs, journals detailing human sacrifice to gain immortality, and a drawn circle that reeked of blood and gave off ominous magic. That’s when he saw a shadow appear behind him, along with that same magic, but far stronger.

                                            “Naughty boy, knowing too much,” the lich tsked, then they thrust a knife straight to his heart. They came from a distance long enough for him to respond, so he managed to grab the lich’s arm to keep the knife at bay. However, the lich’s momentum forced him to the ground to fight for his life. After a few minutes struggle, the lich finally gained the upperhand, the knife just above his heart and slowly lowering.

                                            Just before the knife began to pierce flesh, a gunshot shattered the struggle. The lich slowly looked down to find a hole in their chest, then more holes spawned with blood spatters as more bullets came. The lich fell on top of him, dead. He immediately pushed them off, to then find the barrel of a gun shoved between his eyes.

                                            “Who are you and what are you doing here?” the gunman barked. Timothy was far too terrified to even speak, he could only manage incoherent stammering. “Just another victim,” the gunman sighed.

                                            It turned out that the gunman was one of a small Hunter cell who had been chasing the lich all the way from La Grange, and Timothy had helped them find the lich. After he told them that he could sense magic, they saw him as an incredible asset to their cell. Besides, he now knew of the world behind the veil, he couldn’t go back to his former life. So, they offered him a place to stay there among their ranks. He accepted, believing it would be best to be missed than risk getting his friends and family involved.

                                            At least he understood why his father hated magic so much, it truly was evil. How else could a person commit such terrible acts for selfish gain?

                                            He was a Hunter for two years, taking on the name Garrick Royce as a code name. He utilized his magic sensitivity to take out those who practiced the forbidden arts. Most of them were gutter mages, and of the few that were Awakened, were usually troublesome rogue mages, Banishers, Scelesti, and the like. Oftentimes, he was used as an unwitting pawn by the Orders to take them out. However...

                                            Awakening - It all started with a dream. He saw a tall, thin, pitch-black shape jut from the distance. It birthed a light brighter than a thousand suns, frightening his eyes away to hide in his eyelids. He woke up to find the morning sun shining in his eyes. Later that day, he was in a populated cafe when he began to hear voices in his head. Worse, he could sense magic...coming from him. He was reading the thoughts of the ones around him. The message of his dream was clear: Magic had come for him.

                                            Every day since brought with it another incident. He read invisible words in between the inked ones in his book, he felt the ebb and flow of various energies against him as he walked down the street, his heart broke in the same manner as some stranger who had lost a loved one. Each night came with the same dream. The landscape always varied from windy plains, to thundering storms, to a calm sunset, but the event was always the same. The tower shone its light, blinding him. The only difference was that it got closer and closer every time.

                                            He refused the tower’s call. Pushing the daily events to the back of his mind, and shouting defiance at the tower even as it blinded him. He would not have magic claim him, he would fight it to his last breath. And he would fight alone, he couldn’t bring the others into this. He fought for as long as he could, but the magic pursued still.

                                            It all came to a head when the cell began hunting a sorcerer suspected of being a colleague of the lich killed two years prior. The dream collided with the waking world. The tower, now right in front of him, stood over reality along with a dragon who guarded the entrance. He cried rebellion against tower and dragon, to which they responded with burning him alive. Dead for a time in Fallen reality, his comrades mourned his loss, which was cut short by the ending of their own lives.

                                            He revived in the middle of the tower itself. His stalwart opposition to magic proved his resolve to the Watchtower, and that broke his heart. All this time he had cried defiance and rejection, resisting the temptation of power offered to him, not only was it for naught, but that it was the very key to his failure. The irony weighed down upon his knees as he beat against the wall, sinking into weeping against it. That’s when a long-forgotten part of his soul welled up from his dissolved anger and contumacy.

                                            It rose with the truth in hand: Magic is a pure force. It did not corrupt those who wielded it for evil ends, they corrupted themselves. Magic could just as easily inspire, enrich, and protect lives. Magic is nothing more than raw potential, how one used it was up to them and them alone.

                                            As the revelation dawned on him, the Watchtower burned his palms. Instinctively removing his hands from the wall, he saw the word “potential” written in High Speech on them, as well as on the wall itself when he looked up. Now, he understood. Now, he accepted.

                                            Now. he Awakened.

                                            Awakened - Upon returning to the Fallen World, he found all his comrades dead. With no other lead than the sorcerer, he doggedly chased them on the night of the Deathstorm. He couldn’t find them, however, and the trail went cold since then. The next day, the Consilium contacted him, telling him the basics of his new condition. They recommended him to come up with a Shadow Name for protection and a new identity for his new life.

                                            “Phoenix,” he answered before they could finish explaining, “I died in fire, yet was reborn as something different. Phoenix.”

                                            When asked if he had any questions for them, he asked if they knew about a mage that killed his comrades. They didn’t know, so Phoenix immediately left, he had nothing more to learn or say. Since then, he’s been drowning his grief in training, teaching, and alcohol. However, that will only get him so far...

                                            Personality
                                            Phoenix is very much a what-you-see-is-what-you-get type of person. He never wore a mask and probably will never fully understand why some people do that. He was also never one to take anyone's bullshit, no matter how much authority they had. If he feels like punching you in the face for making some snark, well, expect to have a fist in your face then. He's not stupid, though. Experience has taught him that punching authority figures results in lots of trouble. That doesn't mean he'll take things lying down, he'll just counter in a way that gives him less trouble.

                                            A lover of mythology, quite a bit of his free time is spent in the local library with his nose in a book. His Awakened perspective has not only enhanced his appreciation for the classics, but it's also given him some unique insight into the world. The tale is what's real, not the tired laws of science. He is the Hero of his own story, as is everyone else to theirs. While this perspective does give him a rare degree of compassion, seeing everyone as necessary to fill out their own roles in the world, it does tend to make his thinking rigid. He might make assumptions about how one's story is supposed to go, or certain possibilities about what could happen won't enter his mind.

                                            Daily Life
                                            Phoenix works as a martial arts teacher at the local dojo (many students who have watched a little too much anime have taken to calling him Royce-sensei). This was very practical as a former Hunter, as it helped pay the bills and he spent his down time training and keeping in shape, not to mention it was one of the things he was good at. The leader of his cell owned the house he lives in. With them gone, their next of kin decided to let him have the house, seeing as he was a good friend to the previous owner and had a job. Unfortunately, the bills are a bit too big for his income to handle all alone, so he’s looking for some roommates.

                                                Ok, so I think part of how rotes work isn't as clear compared to wording in 1E, so I wanted to clarify this.

                                                The skill that each Rote is encoded in may or may not be one of your Order's rote skills. I will allow you to pick a different skill than the ones listed as suggestions in the book, but it still needs to be at least loosely related to the symbolism of the spell. For Warding Gesture, for example, I don't think Expression makes sense. Most of the suggested skills have to do with hiding or protecting yourself. Speak with Beasts makes sense to me for Expression, so that one I'm ok with too. But Locate Object I'd also prefer something different.

                                                If the skill happens to line up with the Order rote skill, you get +1 to the roll. Your standard skill specialties do not apply to the roll, because those refer to mundane skill checks, not spell casting.

                                                Obviously, I want you to get significant benefit out of using a rote because the experience cost is meaningful. I'll be lenient with letting you pick skills that are a stretch if it lets you have a higher skill bonus. But remember that some other member of your Order is passing down a way of casting that another master created, probably decades or centuries ago, not just using skills that your character is good at or their own spell adding style. Does that make sense?
