            Saffron spreads his arms, letting flame-and-rose-colored silk glint in the faintly gold light of the treasure. His copper wrist bangles glint and chime, appealing to the beast's nature as a guardian of great wealth. His smile, previously wry and mischievous, broadens and seems to take on new qualities of magnanimity, sincerity, and helpfulness. To look at Saffron, one would think that butter wouldn't melt in his mouth. Though his anima remained subdued, he seemed radiant, a creature of splendor and glamour. The Night Caste extended a purely symbolic hand to the avian creature.

            "O great defender of these Wyld riches, we know that it is far beneath you to treat with us. But we also know that you are wise, a being who sees that the great narrative which controls us all will turn in your favor if you aid a heroic quest. You are nigh-forgotten, languishing beneath this earth to protect vast vaults from miscreants who rarely come. This one swears to you by the scepter which we seek that this one and this one's companions are not of such a foul nature. Milady swordswoman is correct in that we have no wish to kill such a noble protector as yourself. Were we the blackguards which you perhaps expect, we would not have any such reservations. The pact which this one proposes to you is one which would raise you to a place unworthy of this menial task. Any mere mortal may spread her name for a time, but we of longer nature know that true greatness comes when one's name and deeds are told of in the lays and legend of every land. This one is an entertainer, nay, a courtesan famed throughout the world and beyond the world. this one can grant you immortality in my ballads and dances. And no doubt we are to encounter artifacts which, though they will no doubt be of lesser quality than that which you guard, may prove adequate for your hoard. When the horde of enemies sure to assault our noble troupe has been beaten off, this one will bring back to you treasures in return for your generosity. All we ask in this moment is Aurem's scepter. Will you grant us this boon?"

                                Jensven starts his morning by breaking his fast sharing venison and oats with Shadow, "Eat up, Shadow. I don't know when we will be able to eat as good as this for a while. We are going someplace dangerous and I wouldn't trust the demons to have food ready for us." He then commandeers a kitchen and renders down venison, mutton, and various local plants in a large pot filled with water. The Lightbringer lets it boil down until there is nearly no liquid in it. After letting it cool just enough, he forms it into small dabs and cakes. He then bakes it on low heat until it is dry, compact, and not burnt. All through the process of preparing this special food for the dog, Shadow will poke his head into the kitchen, but stay well away from the heat. As payment for allowing him to use the kitchen, Jensven leaves the food that he didn't use in his cooking.

                                The Dawn will then go to a forge and purchase some of the best stoking fuel he can find. Cinder will need fuel and he was determined to get him something special to keep him going. Jensven also makes sure he picks up various grades of wood and coal, just to give the little ball of fire some variety.

                                His next stop is to make sure that he has plenty of water skins. It would not do to have anyone overheat when going through the large desert that Yi mentioned, especially Shadow. A sled or a cart would be impractical in the sand, so he arranged for Shadow to have saddlebags of the sort a small pony would wear. These he filled with a rough blanket and a couple water skins. Jensven himself had a pack and other types of straps and things that would allow him to carry the rest of what he, Shadow, and Cinder would need for the trip to the city of demons. Getting back would be the trick.

                                Lastly, the Sword of Heaven sits kneels down next to the canine and combs out his coat. He pulls many tufts of downy fur from under the coarse hair that protects Shadow from the elements. Humming while he takes care of his companion, the Ascending Sun soon finds that he has a pile of fur nearly the size of the dog when he is finished with the brushing.

                                Having finished the preparations needed for his companions, he checks the three swords he has with him and his chain shirt for any defects and corrects the minor dings, rust spots, and pits that he finds.

                                Only after going to all this trouble to make sure things are as ready as he would need them to be does he head off to the Artificery to find Cinder, a nice block of hardwood in hand.

                                I am going to be rolling to see if I can make the puppy chow just in case it is needed. This is a Craft (Water) roll I believe. So I have to use Perception (3) + Craft (Water) 0 for a total of three (3) dice and one success. I guess that means I just barely make the food edible. Good thing. I don't want to kill the dog.

                                                    Yi had returned to the temple of Originese after his conversation with Jensven, but not joined Larissa as he normally did. Instead he prostrated himself before the altar of Origenese, after the evening prayer was completed and said.

                                                    "Oh god, my grandfather, who has taken me into your city, your house, and your family: Let it not be said I came to you uncivilized, that I was ignorant of xenia and what is owed a stranger to his host. I swore an oath that I should pay back all you offered and so shall it be, and Heaven I call to witness. Yet now I must go forth from your city and return again after many days. That all men and gods may know my faithfulness now I shall go from this place and prepare for you the first payment of what is due you. As it was long ago, so shall it be again; the word of the Lawgiver shall be held most highly exalted."

                                                    So saying he turned and walked away, not to return till he had seen Malfeas' viridian sun and felt the hate of the Demon Emperor scouring decadence from his flesh. His steps took him through dusty streets. Once or twice a footpad or ruffian stepped from shadow to bar his way. The first threw herself to the ground chanting "Namo Zhinan". Some knelt awkwardly. Most found other streets to menace. Once he heard a scuffle a street over, and shouting of "the priest" but there was nothing but bloodstains when he reached it to investigate. In such fashion he reached the river.

                                                    Silently he walked down, the water rising up to his ankles, then his knees, then his waist. Slowly, deliberately, he began to sing on old song. He had first heard it long, long ago. When he had been a sun dark boy still lost in the halls of the academy. He had never seen the summoning, only heard it echoing through the ancient halls. It had seemed... forbidden then. Like a part of the world that was secret and not meant for him. Yet it had also excited him. The world was richer and more wondrous than he had ever imagined. All those feelings welled up again as he sun.

                                                    The sun descended in the sky. The mist rose over the river and the solar shivered beneath his sodden robes. When the golden disk was half obscured he opened his eyes and brought the jade staff down to strike the surface of the water. A single, perfect, ripple spread from the spot glowing as it grew. In the sudden darkness the golden mark on his brow hung in the air like a will o wisp. Yet there were other lights.

                                                    "Ainti Teph, it has been a long time. I don't suppose Lookshy's pact with your court still applies to me, or that the arrangement of being summoned to do as thou wilt will be acceptable."

                                                    He saw her shape, reclining on the water with more grace than many a courtesan on silken pillows. He knew she was beautiful, and that she enjoyed being thought as such.

                                                    "Now I, being poor, have nothing but a contest to propose. I saw once, when you and mistress Arada wove together, she at her loom and you where the many threaded river was woven into the sea. So I propose this. We will each weave a bolt, and you may decide which is the better. If you judge mine superior you will weave the blessing of cool water upon it as you did for General Karal before the Southern Campaign. If yours is superior I will serve you by the same submission as your kind often serves mine. I shall let Heaven judge your honesty, and the name the five gods who love me to witness. Are these terms acceptable, or shall our wills contest instead?"

                                                    Yi placed his left hand against the surface of the water, the tips of his fingers just barely touching the black surface. With careful motions, more gentle than a musician upon his strings, he began to upset the surface. Each little ripple, thin as thread, was caught by another finger until he had begun to draw them from the water like strands of gossamer. Lifting his hand up as though handing them to another they caught and began to slowly twist, spun by the wheel of the turning heavens.

                                                    Then he raised his right hand, weaving his fingers between the stars. Deep, deep, purple came away, the darkness between the stars on which the constellations were hung. This he fixed to the wheel of the turning earth, for why should the heavens think they and not the ever changing earth moves? Only the keenest eyes could see the fibers rising ever thinner into the sky.

                                                    He repeated the process and then repeated it again. When they had grown long, long enough to bind a strong man so that he strained but could not break them, he began to spin. It was not dance, not as the teachers of dance or the professionals who made their living on the stage would recognize it. He spun and spun and spun, and his robes whirled about him like a cloud. It was not excessively fast or frantic. As he spun he let go of his worries about what he had done and what he was soon to do. He stopped caring about his position or his image. He spun on, and thought of gratitude and excellence in remembrance of the sun.

                                                    When he at last came to a stop the cloth lay spread about him on the water and the scene he had woven into it as at last apparent. Water and mist, sky and shadow, it showed the shore and the city and the stars about the moon. On the shoulders of the Exalted it would show silver sands and black towers, bowed kings and kneeling lords, white cities and golden temples. Black as night and smoke and water beneath the sea, flashing with the infinite shades of darkness, glittering as though spun from ten thousand jewels. A black mirror in woven form, to show all and none of all that would ever surround it.

                                                    Yi did not speak, but turned to the elemental to see her response.

                                                            (Jensven)

                                                            Your cooking results in thick canvas bag filled with baked dog-food (though it is equally edible to humans I suppose). The chefs in the kitchen find your foodstuff to be a bit odd, but they file it away for future reference. Baked meat-and-vegetable balls? Could be good for the soldiers. Might want to bake them into loafs, though. Less work that making the individual balls, after all.

                                                            Procuring the fuel is slightly more difficult, but you obtain a fair amount of wood, charcoal, and even a bit of coal.

                                                            Heading into the Artificery, you are met with an unusual degree of activity. There are easily more people here at once than there were for the entirety of your previous visits. A half-dozen master smiths work at their individual forges, each with several apprentices and a journeyman or two, orbiting them, assisting where able.

                                                            And, at Cinder's forge, you see Maia working alone as she shapes a heavy polearm, made entirely iron, She seems to sculpt both metal and flame with her bare hands; aside from looking as if she just spent a long morning in the sun, she seems unaffected by the fires around her. Cinder's head(?) bobs around her, prattling on and on about a dozen topics, only one of which is Maia's current project.

                                                            Seeing Jensven, Cinder flits over like a wayward ember. "You're back! You know, you're the only one here I can talk to! Everyone else just ignores me, you know? When are we going on that trip? Is that for me?" Cinder asks with all of the energy of a sugar-crazed five-year-old.
