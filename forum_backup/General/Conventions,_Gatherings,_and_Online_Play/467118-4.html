            Joseph, for his part, felt slightly agitated at Aliza's shifting of his position. He had put himself there for a reason, but the following responses from his pack, and the resounding oath of the Behemoth's made it sink in for him just how wrong the footing they had placed themselves on was, and where to keep his focus. The question they had first been given burst afresh in his mind, and he could almost here his old mentor's “figured it out yet pup?” and could almost see that tilt of her head. There he had gone and rushed off again, rather than watched and waited. He could see it in the Behemoth's, their oath was as binding, more so, than chains, as it should have been. To assume Greg had been acting apart from them, to insult the pack by insinuating that they had allowed that, had been an insult. He knew they should have claimed totem and territory first, but even then he doubted this would have gone down too differently if they had.

            The young Ithaeur relaxed his posture and, suddenly in defiance to the mood of the entire room, began to casually walk up to the bar, signaling his pack to relax as he did so.

            “Which we certainly, are not able to do at present.” The words acted as a response to the chanted oath.

            As he approached there was a deep core of sadness over one particular stool and he wondered if it was the previous Alpha's, giving it a berth and stopping in front of his fellow Crescent Moon. His hands and eyes focused down for a moment as he pulled what looked like brown, twisted cigarette out of a pack in his pocket before putting it in his mouth. When crafted right the little bundles were wonderful talens, unfortunately he needed to wait on his things from the previous city before he would get them again. He had no light, and so the little stick simply sat there in his lips as he looked up at the woman.

            “What, exactly, do you drink?” He didn't wait for a response as he looked past her to the various liquors, beers, and wines, as if browsing. “I ask because my old mentor used to recite our tribal oath to me in all sorts of occasions. Except more often than not she left out spirit.” He took the twist out of his mouth like a toothpick and held it momentarily as he continued, eventually placing it back in. “For her it was something to be followed for more than just the Shadow, and as it stands now I, we, do owe you. Both a thank you and an apology.” This time his locked on hers, matching the serious tone in his voice. “You paid us a warning through your question when we came in. Whatever this is, its dangerous, killed your Alpha. Were we to get involved in any way, assuming it wasn't done, would likely only make us a much easier and softer target for it to turn to. To run with you as we are now would not only violate your oath but likely get us killed. So, you warned us, though it seems we're not the best of listeners. To top it off we implied your pack did not function as one, a foolish accusation on our part.” His eyes broke contact as he pulled out his wallet and began checking how much cash he had on him. The number of bills lower than he was comfortable with. The actions had to have seemed bizarre to his pack and even some of the Behemoth's, but the bartender he knew understood what he was doing. The spirits in this place made it clear, to buy a drink, at this time, was a sign of respect, one he was showing to the former patron of a now empty bar-stool.

            “Perhaps we will be worthy to stand with the Behemoth at some point, but for now, what would you say to us simply watching and learning, from time to time, if you would have us. After we claim our totem and territory of course.”

                                            That night.

                                            Aliza had promised to be in touch. She had their phone numbers and addresses. Fooling around a little on Google Maps earlier in the week had helped her put neighborhoods and locations and names and reputations together into something a little more coherent. All of them. Her maybe-pack. A band of nobodies that she, Queen Nobody, was softly ordering around, more like a schoolteacher than an alpha wolf. It was her way. She was not ready to inflict absolutes, orders, and violence on people who had not given her the necessary trust and respect first.

                                            Even so, they were all she thought about. Dinner at a local diner, all alone, her company had been a mental replay of the day's events. Afterwards, she'd taken a bus to the campus - her student ID still got her into their computer lab - and she'd been back on Maps, peering down at Victory City like a god or a history textbook.

                                            The elders had simply thrown a bunch of pups together. They were watching, judging, and perhaps more concerned with each other than the fresh blood. They had no guidance, they had no mentor, they had no help. This was a test. More than a test, this was them being told they just had to fend for themselves. No one was going to help them because they were all so tense around each other that no one could afford to - they had all agreed to abandon this fledgling pack because none of them wanted to risk the others getting an advantage.

                                            Maybe it had made sense to visit the weakest pack of all first, if things were this tense. They would have to avoid the others. No freebies. No hand-outs. No help.

                                            ...and no place to call their own. Opening Paint, of all things, a quick print-screen later had ugly blue and red slashes covering the map, noting territory that belonged to the People, Forsaken and Anshega, and again Aliza's eyes traced the borders and edges and pits and openings. They needed to find a place. She needed to find a place. Somewhere they could call home, somewhere they could use to make a name for themselves.

                                            She sent a couple of e-mails, a couple of texts. She let everyone know where to meet her tomorrow. It was a little unwieldy. That was the beauty of it - the reason it was still available. It was as deep into Victory City as you could go, with no way out. It was a place for people committed not to freedom and highways or the tenderest and most valuable parts of the beast, but for those who cared about the whole city, who were committed to it. Half of it was industrial - the rest had been unwanted territory five, ten years ago - but cities change. There was a seed there. Something that could be tended to - she'd read the newspapers, she'd lived here her whole life - she knew the neighborhood well enough. It was... it could be - theirs. If Luna willed it.

                                            They would find out tomorrow. Aliza knew the neighborhood well enough - but she didn't know the Hisil. So tomorrow, they were going to have to step across the gauntlet and see if it was a place worth calling their own.
