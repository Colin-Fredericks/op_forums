                            To the Strongest

                            I'm not going to be super worried about historical accuracy from you guys (or me), I just want to paint a basic picture of what's happening in the world and where the important places are.

                            The setting picks up in 323 BCE. You live in the Alexandrian Empire, a completely new construct made up of the many kingdoms his armies have conquered: Greece, Persia, Egypt, up to the edge of India. Over the last 15 years or so, Alexander has started from northern Greece and conquered almost the entire known world. Many, many people refer to him as a god. Along with war and destruction, this has been an opportunity for cultural exchange and sharing ideas, as various ancient rival cultures are now united under one ruler. Main idea: your world is currently experiencing massive, catastrophic change and upheaval, and no one really knows what the future holds. All bets are off.

                            Greece has traditionally been divided among rival city-states with a shared culture and language, but Alexander has united them into one part of the empire. Most of his soldiers are Greek, and Greeks are the ones doing the majority of the traveling to other cultures. Thermopylae, Socrates, Plato, all that famous stuff happened within the last century. Greece (or Hellas, as in "Alexander is spreading Hellenic culture") is now entering a new era where Athens and Sparta are just two more cities in a massive empire. The empire has historically been dominated by Greek culture, but it's absorbing and spreading other cultures as well.

                            Persia was an empire covering most of the modern Middle East, and the regional superpower, until Alexander's armies conquered it 8 years ago at the Battle of Gaugamela. These are the main guys Alexander had been fighting. The major religion is Zoroastrianism, which is all about sacred fire and written scriptures and angels/devils and dualistic Good vs Evil, but the Persian (Achaemenid) empire also contained the former Babylonia and Judah/Israel and lots of other cultures that are now in the broader mix. Alexander is super fascinated by Persian culture, he married a local woman, and his Greek generals and soldiers are very unhappy about it. In 323, Alexander is hanging out in the city of Babylon planning his next move.

                            Egypt (the locals call it Kemet) is very ancient and magical. Everyone knows they had a hugely powerful society for thousands of years. 99% of the population lives in cities and towns along the Nile, covered with ancient monuments and temples and tombs, and also busy modern trading ports. The Egyptians have been a subject people for a few centuries now, most recently to the Persians, but the culture and wisdom of their ancestors is highly respected. Alexander established the (culturally Greek) city of Alexandria here about 8 years ago, future site of the Library of Alexandria, after taking Egypt from Persia. There are also lots of famous and, again, very ancient cities like Memphis, Elephantine, Swenet, Bubastis, Abydos...(Cairo doesn't exist yet but Memphis is close.)

                            India is the edge of the known world. There is no single ruler of India (at this exact moment), but the various medium-sized kingdoms and smaller territories have many shared cultural and religious assumptions based on sacrifice, renunciation, and reincarnation. 3 years ago, Alexander won a costly victory against an Indian king (war elephants and all) at the Battle of the Hydaspes, and his homesick army subsequently "persuaded" him to turn back once he reached the base of the Himalayas. The army retreated to Babylon, but there's a vibrant Greek-Indian cultural exchange happening along the towns (Nicaea and Bucephala) and trade routes they established up to the monsoon-swollen Hydaspes river.

                            ---

                            Still with me? Alright. Let's talk about the Mage-specific stuff.

                            The whole mass-conquest thing is a rare opportunity for Awakened from very different cultures to meet each other and realize that they're using different words to describe the same things. Each of those 4 regions I described has its own overarching magical traditions called a Great Cult, but as mages travel the Empire, they start to form Darshanas, semi-formal societies and philosophical schools based on deeper ideas about the purpose of magic, rather than their cultural background. Instead of Orders, mages in this setting have a Great Cult and a Darshana that together provide the same benefits. Your Darshana is kinda primary, and your approach to that Darshana is informed by your Great Cult. Read the Dark Eras chapter, it has lots of helpful examples.

                            Darshanas

                            The Jnanashakti or Gnostikon are scholars and teachers who study the world in search of magic. Similar to the Mysterium.

                            The Mahanizrayani or Omphalos are priests and leaders who guide Sleepers, travel between the magical worlds, and keep those worlds and their creatures separate for the good of humanity. Similar to the Silver Ladder.

                            The Samashti or Phulakeion are mystics and deceivers who hunt dark magic and perform immoral acts for the greater good, damning themselves so the world can Ascend. Similar to the Guardians of the Veil.

                            The Vajrastra or Adamantine Arrow are warriors and soldiers who do Adamantine Arrow stuff. Existence is war, find purpose through combat, prove yourself worthy of the gift of magic by emulating heroes, etc. Very popular Darshana in a world convulsed by war and roaming armies.

                            Great Cults

                            The Arcadian Mysteries (or Pelasgians) are Greek, philosophers with big ideas about the Realm of Forms and reshaping the world like Alexander has. [cultural lens: Greek gods and titans.]

                            The Karpani (or Magi) are Persian, rural poets and singers who've had to step into a bigger role since the Zoroastrian high mage-priests were scattered. [Zoroastrian angels and demons.]

                            The Weret-Hekau (or Hemka) are Egyptian, heirs to a truly ancient tradition who manipulate the soul and try to protect their inherited glories from outsiders. [Egyptian gods and five-part soul.]

                            The Mantra Sadikhi (or Mantrikis) are Indian, wandering ascetics and nomads who try to see the world clearly and act without delusion. [Hindu and Buddhist gods and lore.]

                            ---

                            That's the big stuff. There are also some more fiddly terminology and setting changes that you can read the chapter for if you want, they aren't as relevant for character creation. Dark Eras page 79 has a helpful quick reference.

                                                Silver_Smoulder The fake-out about the Diamond Orders is one of the fun parts about this setting! As a big ol' history nerd, I really like it- it turns out, just like most people, mages in the 21st century believe a lot of stuff about the past that isn't quite accurate. I encourage you to check out the book, because the chapter deeply addresses all of those questions in a cool way, but I'll summarize it here.

                                                There were some setting changes from 1E to 2E, and in 2E the standard belief among mages in 2017 is that Atlantis is more of a mythical place than a literal city that was physically destroyed in the year 1537 BC or something. They know for sure that there was once an openly magical culture that fell and was removed from time and space, and there are Artifacts and ruins around the world that seemingly were left behind by that culture (or several cultures), but the specifics are harder to figure out. Atlantis is a common metaphor for understanding that lost culture, and actually the whole Atlantis and Diamond Orders philosophy is just now starting to develop during To The Strongest as Greek philosopher-mages encounter other magical traditions and share ideas. I'll quote the 2E core book on how the modern Orders see themselves:

                                                In the ideal Awakened society postulated by the Diamond’s
                                                Atlantean symbolism, the Adamantine Arrow embodies Ungula
                                                Draconis                                                , the Talon of the Dragon: righteous warriors who fight to
                                                keep the flame of human power alive in an age of darkness.
                                                [..]
                                                In Atlantean symbolism, the Guardians embody the all-seeing
                                                oculus, Visus Draconis. The Eye of the Dragon scrutinizes every
                                                detail of the Awakened City with an unflinching gaze.
                                                [...]
                                                In Diamond symbolism, the Mysterium are the Alae Draconis:
                                                the Wings of the Dragon, uplifting the Awakened City’s body
                                                politic with winds of insight.
                                                [...]
                                                The Ladder is responsible for the Diamond’s claim to wisdom
                                                from an ancient ur-culture, and it strives ever to maintain that
                                                magical symbolism in the form of the Diamond and Pentacle.
                                                They are Vox Draconis: Voice of the Dragon, priests of Atlantis,
                                                granted primacy not because of conferred authority or divine
                                                right but recognition of wise leadership.
                                                That Atlantean symbolism doesn't fully exist yet during To The Strongest. Mages from different cultures don't see themselves as all being one thing with a single shared heritage, they instead view their magic through Sleeper mythic ideas: the Great Cults. However, it's the Darshanas that will later develop into the Diamond Orders, shared philosophies of magic that are starting to transcend one specific culture and mythology. A Victorian Time mage who was spying on the past might hypothesize that the Darshanas are mages beginning to rediscover their shared past in Atlantis that was scattered by the Fall, but an Alexandrian mage doesn't see it that way in the present. However, most of the Great Cults do believe in Ages of Man, where humans in a previous era of heroes and gods and monsters were wiser, stronger, and worked magic openly. But over time, humanity degraded in subsequent ages, and in the present age magic is hidden and humans are in darkness.

                                                As for the Sleeping Curse: again, Dark Eras talks about this in depth, but I'll summarize. Rules-wise, everything is the same, Sleepers still unravel magic and cause Paradox. It's not about belief in magic, but failure to understand. If your character told a random Sleeper that they were a magician, they would probably be skeptical, but they might well believe you (and perhaps warn their friends to be careful around you, or inform the authorities.) That belief wouldn't stop them from unraveling your magic if you cast a spell in front of them. It's not about a modern idea of "different realities clashing", it's just that you're Wise and they aren't. You perceive the world more truly than they do, so of course they can't perceive your pure magic. It's all the same single world, there aren't multiple dimensions or anything, but you see it more clearly than most people. You're probably not going to see a vampire ruling openly as an undead king or something, but rural people especially might warn you to avoid the river crossing because a monster lives there. The supernatural is hidden, but many people know/believe that it's out there somewhere, and some claim (truly or falsely, there are still plenty of charlatans) to have contact with it.

                                                Mages in the Alexandrian era don't believe in Paradox (same mechanics though) or a single Abyss that's eating reality with every spell. Instead, poorly-controlled spells cause Nemesis, which is caused by lack of virtue and self-control, as well as demons from the primordial Chaos that every Cult sees differently. If you're a Greek/Hellenic mage, you identify that chaos with Erebus, the formless primordial darkness where the Titans are imprisoned. This is easily proven, because a mage can summon those lesser Titans (that modern mages call Gulmoth) from the darkness. It's an extremely dangerous practice that only the foolish or extremely brave would attempt (much like conquering the world, as Alexander has), but it's not viewed as an abhorrent "crime against reality" or anything. As a matter of fact, the shattered Babylonian Great Cult called the Baalim were known and feared for their mastery of Chaos and summoning devils from it. Modern mages would call them Scelesti, but that term doesn't exist yet. No Latin!

                                                And just a few words about early-world and stories about items of power! One-eyed or otherwise wounded craftsman is a solid concept and you should run with it, but just to be clear, people in the Alexandrian era don't see themselves as being in the "early world." Egyptian culture has existed for thousands of years, Mesopotamian empires have come and gone, and it's widely believed by educated people that the age of heroes and myths has passed. Just as an example, most Greeks see Egypt as a place of faded ancient wisdom and magic, and the Weret-Hekau specialize in long-lost items of power (if they can just find the damn things.) Sleepers know the stories about the Golden Fleece and the helmet of Hades and stuff, and they wouldn't expect someone to make something like that in their lifetime. But those are the kinds of legends and figures that the Great Cults draw on for their view of magic. You could absolutely play a Greek crafter who explicitly says "I create magical items worthy of the gods, in the same way that Hephaestus and Arachne did." And you're going to have a market for those things, especially because a living god is currently in the process of conquering the world, and lots of people want to stop or support him. If you told someone in 2017 that you could make an invisibility cloak, they'd look at you funny and laugh about Harry Potter. If you told someone in 323 BCE, they'd say "oh, word? I'm listening. Prove it."
