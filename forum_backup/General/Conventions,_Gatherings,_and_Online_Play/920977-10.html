                Background

                BEFORE
                Vivian, or "Vo", had been happy before. She was a bright child growing up, she didn't get picked on too much for being a nerd, and her parents even handled it fairly well when she came out in high school. She hit college a yar or two early and just didn't stop, devouring courses with ravenous abandon, tearing her way into theoretical physics. Besides truly finding her passion, she also found herself a pretty girlfriend named Sandra, a cute art major with sparkling eyes and a tongue piercing. Life was pretty good.

                Then the car accident happened. Nerve damage, they said---oh, she was still fully functional, more or less. She could walk and talk and she could think just fine. But there was always a twitch, or a tremor, or something somewhere. Always the sta-st-stammer, the, uh, hesitations. But that was okay, because she had her studies, she had her girlfriend. Before long, she had her Master's degree, then her wife, then her doctorate. She smoothed over her fears with Sandra's litany of assurances that everything was fine.

                AWAKENING
                It's not a story you haven't heard---coming home early from work with flowers for the missus, discovering her in another's arms. Sandra was wrapped up in another girl on the couch as Viv's slightly jerky footsteps brought her in to see them. She paused, the silence stretching endlessly until she went into the kitchen, filled a vase with water, put the flowers in. The fornicators watched as she twitched and juddered out again, mumbling about some work she really needed to get back to doing.

                Her coworkers asked what had her back so early, but she said nothing. She locked herself in her office, fired up the computer, started on the whiteboard. Deeper and deeper she threw herself into the comforting equations, wondering what she was going to do with herself. She'd seen glimpses of it before, but one look into Sandra's eyes as she lay half-frozen with apprehension in the other girl's arms had confirmed it---if there had been love before, it hadn't survived the accident; the less-and-less-guilty revulsion from Viv's herky-jerky movements, the less-and-less-patient exasperation with her stammer, the less-and-less enthusiastic reciprocation of awkwardly warm affection. She'd seen it, and not realized it---the slow decay of her wife's love and fidelity. Should she kill herself now? But she still had things to live for. But would it be worth living for them, the rest of her life colored by being the poor, naive nerd who couldn't hold on to a spouse?

                But as she worked, she started to fall deeper into the physics, the monstrous moonshine and no-ghost theorems. She started to see more than there was. This was her awakening, and with it she paid Charon. He took his boat across the Styx, burdened not with her soul but with her old life.

                She staggered back home and found the lovers still there. Calmly, quietly, she booted them out of her house. Her house. Divorce followed soon after, whirling a wind of scandal through La Grange.

                Awakened - [insert history since Awakening]

                Personality
                Vo is, at her core, someone whose Awakening didn't sweep away the agony of betrayal---oh, it freed her to stand up against it, but she's still deeply, deeply hurt inside. She's sure that in some way, Sandra did it not just because she couldn't hang on through her wife's problems, but because Sandra thought she could get away with it---that she thought Vo to be ultimately powerless. And while she dreams of throwing off that shroud, she paradoxically plays into it. She plays up the image of harmless, tremorous eccentricity, even walking with a cane to put on a good show. Beneath, though, she quietly sizzles.

                In general, she's not a bad person to be friends with---if she can trust you, of course. Trust is hard for the victim of betrayal, but once you have it, she's loyal as anything. Though she tries to hide it, attractive women make her nervous even as she finds them entrancing.

                Daily Life
                Vo mostly works as a researcher at U of C's physics department, but she's been roped into teaching a First Year Seminar as well as a Physics 101 class. She's not a bad prof to have once you get used to her stammer, and her students are sometimes favored with her dry wit. When not working, she doesn't go out very much unless it's for something she needs.
