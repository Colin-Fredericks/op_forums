    Iremite religion and the faith of Hinduism
    To be blunt, benevolent gods are not part of the pantheon of Irem. No, to the Nameless Empire, the gods are divine monsters too powerful to interact with directly without going mad. The wine of Shezmu is distilled human suffering.

    Hinduism, while it has gods as cruel as any Judge of Duat, has a great number of very benign divinities. Vishnu has repeatedly set himself to Earth in avatar form to help save the world, the most famous of which is Krishna. Gods, Hindus believe, have and indeed a great many possess similar temperaments.

    Reincarnation, however, is a thorny subject for the Arisen. They have something like reincarnation with their Descents, but close examinations causes the similarities to disintegrate. Shades in Duat either ascend to A’aru or are crushed by Shezmu’s stomping feet, or worse, Fed to Ammut. This is an irrevocable journey.

    As a result, syncretizint “the true faith” (as the Arisen see it) with Hinduism takes some doing. Certain Hindu deities, however, are very Familiar to mummies. Who is Kali? Who is Shiva? Ammut. Whether this is true or not is a matter of some significant debate.

        Sekhem sorcery in Kamakhya
        In many religions, the division between praying and casting a spell is irrelevant, and Kamakhya is no different. Due its long dominance by the Arisen, the temple complex has a sacred tradition of Sekhem sorcery, in addition to the fell magic titled Blood bathing.

        Several Open Rites are practiced in Kamakhya, but it’s Closed Rites are particularly interesting, as they affect daityas, Amkhata and fiends, most through exorcisms.

        Ghosts in Kamakhya
        At least one Avernian Gate is in Kamakhya, protected by a geist called the Headless Guru, and several other places exist, Resonant with death but not quite portals to other realms.

        By meditating at these places long enough and with the assistance of three Sekhem casters casting the same Closed Rite, a mummy can enter...another Neter-Khertet. It is no desert, has no storms, is under no Law of Suffering....just lost and lonely and dim. Mummies can never stay in these strange “other-Twilights” for more than a few minutes, and the Judges flay the Deathless’ Sekhem in retribution. In truly awfully cases, Anpu himself will arrive and unceremoniously deposit the Arisen back in the mortal plane.

        Vessels in Kamakhya
        Several Vessels’ve been gathered over the centuries and not offered up to the Judges for whatever reason.

                            Amkhata
                            Several Amkhata are in Kamakhya, imprisoned in Twilight inside statues, prayer wells and ceremonial bells. They cannot escape of their own volition, but the Ted-Aabhi’s primary cult has a different Closed Rite to release each Amkhata.

                            peret
                            Not all physically embodied undead are mummies and vampires. The Peret, for instance, is something that never lived, yet retains a strong presence in India. A tiny undead worm, a Peret burrows into the living. The host breaks outin sores, lesions, boils and rashes, making them easy to mistake for leper’s.

                            The host is driven, slowly but surely, to commit heinous acts of ever-greater depravity to feed the chaotic whims of the peret inside them.

                            From whence did the peret come? Is it of Duat, some sort of permanently embodied fiend? Is something the wolfmen refers to as a doo’gah’theem (whatever that is)? Or has it been hereall along?

                            No one knows.
