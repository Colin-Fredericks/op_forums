                        Flynn finished the song and put his flute down. Picking up the wine skin, he uncorked it and took a few gulps. He was just getting warmed up. With the wine, he could play all night. The crowd was at his command. It had been months since he played for such a responsive gathering.

                        The crowd seemed to be tired from all the dancing and was using the break in music to salvage some more beer and wine from the destroyed tavern. He saw Swimming Owl and the others talking in a group. The blue haired man from the Sailor’s Grotto was among them. He pointed with his spear towards the woods. With the group ignoring Flynn for the moment, he drank some more wine and considered his next action.

                        Information is what they needed now. And playing for this crowd wasn’t going to give him that. It would be better to go out on the town and talk to people. Flynn has a basic understanding of the political situation between the Guild and the Thousand Rivers Court, but they needed more. Names, Alliances, Dirty Secrets. There were enough bars and taverns in Puyo to find such things out.

                        I need to know if you got my back. Do you have my back? Swimming Owl’s words came back to haunt him. Going out on his own without his full mission briefing was stupid. He could be wasting his time if he went off on his own.

                        Lifting up the wine skin, Flynn realized that it was already empty. How had he drank a whole wine skin so fast? He shook it mournfully and threw it aside. The crowd was scattering and heading into the various drinking establishments on the street. Every bone in Flynn’s body told him to follow them. He even had a good reason. It didn't matter how drunk he was, if he came back with information.

                        The group of exalts turned towards the forested road and began walking. “Shit, where the fuck are the going?” If he didn’t follow them now, he would lose them. He ignored the siren call of the bar and followed the group.

                                                            It was a small thing, a very small thing, but helping someone, anyone, right now felt very appealing.

                                                            With a craftsmen's eye he looked at the wild plants growing by the side of the road, noticing the brown stalks where the tiny seeds could be found. One of those he plucked, rubbing his fingers over it and looking at the tiny grains that settled in his palm.

                                                            "If you don't mind grass, I will see what I can do."

                                                            Without another word he kept walking, though now he ran he hand along the tops of the grasses and singing softly to himself.

                                                            A light breeze picked up and bent the foliage, shaking a few seeds loose and flinging tiny drops of nectar as they sprang back up. Yet they continued to sway, far more strongly than the wind should have caused them to, until pale, rippling waves seemed to flow down the stalks. Tiny flashes of sunlight on dew began to accompany the rushing grain, bursting each seed with a fleeting beam of focused light.

                                                            A few minutes of this passed, and Splendor finished his song. Where he finished, by the side of the road on a cushion of clover, was a goodly sized loaf of bread; just big enough to share.

                                                            "I hope it turned out all right, I have never done that before."
