        Max nodded to Nate's admission that things were and might not be completely rosy. He smiled back sadly as they shook on it. That the Goat would try did encourage him, believe it or not. Perhaps the Pig was too trusting -- as the zodiac sign is said to be -- or perhaps there was just some relief at having cast the die, whatever way it turned up. But Nate's trying counted for no small amount to Max.

        Speaking of dies he'd recently cast, as Max listened to the interesting insights Summernickel provided, he found his mind turning to the gambit he'd laid out in court. 'So that's how it is... That means if my plan works, then... Then it's even better than I thought! But... If it doesn't work out... Oh...' He had admittedly been surprised the Keeper seemed accommodating of the request he'd made. With this new information, though, the Pig had a new grasp of how highly the Collector valued what he'd put on offer.

        Max wasn't alone in rolling these revelations around in his head. Of course, Nate and Jessica touched on a somewhat... darker possibility. One which the Ox quickly jumped toward, in a horrifyingly literal fashion. Before the Pig even let out a squeal of a gasp when he realized what was happening, the Goat was already trying to keep her from that mistake. Without a word, Max was right behind him, even before Nate had finished asking for help. "Jessica, don't!" He grabbed her other arm and tried to pull. For all the good it did. Max was plumper than he'd been, but his body weight was nearer to a piglet's than a full-grown hog's, and nowhere near that of an ox. Likewise, even beyond lacking Nate's greater strength, Max had no nearby wall to grab: the other arm was all him. Between learning just how terribly suited porcine hooves were for the task and how forcefully oxen could go where they wished, he found himself getting dragged ever so slowly along. There was only so much difference he could actually make.

        At least, physically. It struck Max that he and Nate were trying to hold her back on a slick muddy surface. If not for the Wilds' blessing on the Pig and Goat, all three beasts might have probably already gone careening down into the gargantuan jaws below. And there was more than one way the Wilds could help. With no thought for whether it would even work, Max focused on his Contract and called out, "Wilds! D-Do something! H-Help hold her back!"

                                06-13-2015, 02:04 PM
                                Max fell back next to Nate after pulling Jessica up, panting away. His body felt like it was on fire. But at least the flames were receding and Jessica was safe. Much as 'safe' was a relative term in this place, Summernickel reminded him. With a grunt, the Pig forced himself to sit upright; even with his mud-drenched robe, lying on his back in the new mud sliding down was far too comfortable. Never mind the ever present danger of more things hurtling down from above.

                                Max gave a sympathetic look at Summernickel's sad little smile and then heard him out. The plan made good sense. And now that he knew how much the Wilds could do down here... "I... I can try." He pulled himself to his feet. Turning to the nearest wall, his hooves made audible little clicks against the dried earth as he went. He wondered when he'd get used to that. Regardless, he set his hand against the muddy surface. He paused.

                                A few things ran through his mind. The piglet had his plan waiting for him if he made it to the end. It might become voided if he didn't reach that end as agreed. And with what the goblin told him, he didn't think he'd get another chance like it. Of course, the same could be said for leaving now. It was like he'd cast the dice when he had nothing left to lose and, before being told the result, was informed that the stakes were much higher than he'd thought -- and was offered a chance to take a lesser, safer gamble. But it wasn't just Max's life he'd be chancing: that "lesser" gamble might mean a better chance of freedom for Nate and Jessica and someone who had come to help. Did he have the right to risk all that?

                                'Well... Maybe I can hedge a little.' He took a deep breath. "W-Wilds. I-If you would..." Max closed his eyes and pictured his desire, a burrow like Summernickel described, opening as a side-path for them to use or not, not simply a new chasm down which to slide. "M-Make a new tunnel, safe and b-big enough for all of us." -- 'Or just most of us.' -- "T-Tear a path out t-to the Hedge."

                                Just as the little pig made his request, he went rigid. Summernickel's plan hinged on the Wilds cooperating. But Max had gotten the sense that while they would obey him, these were still the Keeper's wilds: requests too far out of the Collector's wishes might be denied. Or worse. And even if it could work, he was calling on it so soon after it had saved Jessica. The Wilds struck him as reasonable, and he had done his best to make it well-disposed to him. But Max dreaded that he might offend it by simply asking too much, too soon. "P-P-Pleeeeease! Please!" His voice was scarcely more than an animal's squeal, plainly begging for mercy. They would find out soon enough if his fears were off-base or terrifyingly well-founded.

                                            As the seconds ticked by with no apparent response from the Wilds, Max settled into hyperventilating. And without a paper bag in sight. Fortunately, Nate's reassurance and support did the trick, coming as a huge relief. "R-Right." He took a deep breath, and smiled back at the goat. "Thanks." It settled things for him; if his friend had enough trust in him to take care of something like that, he couldn't justify letting his plan go to waste.

                                            At that moment, the chamber wall began to shake. Max jerked away from it with a short squeal, and watched as the filthy wall began to reshape itself. Like a curtain of filth rising before a christening ceremony, it revealed a tunneling path, winding off, down, and away. The floor was mostly dry, though muddy fluid pooled in places and drabs of mud dripped down often. Bits of vine, and luminescent little crystals and mushrooms poked out from the curvature of the ceiling and walls, just enough to see by for those with beastly or goblin eyes. The pig heard his stomach rumble and tried to ignore how tasty the little fungi smelled. He certainly couldn't live with himself if he let something like that change his mind.

                                            Max sighed in relief. "Thank you, Wilds. A-And this'll lead to the Hedge?" He felt no stirring from the primordial force. A gulp. "A-A-At least as close as you c-could?" he stammered, anxieties rising. A pleasant breeze of confirmation blew down the tunnel. "O-Of course. Thank you again." He gave an awkward bow toward the tunnel and started breathing again, not realizing he'd stopped.

                                            After a few deep gulps, he went for the next step, eyes turning to the dreaming ox. "N-Nate, w-would you take Jessica? It's, uh... I'm not strong enough to help carry her, y'know?" Their respective physical showings while she'd tried to kill herself had made that plain. Max looked away after a few moments and whispered, "Wilds. P-Please show me the way to... t-to the end of the K-Keeper's path." He spotted the green easy against the brown and gray, as a vine sprouted from the mud and extended up the way Nate had come. He frowned at it, almost hoping he'd have an excuse to take the easy way out. 'Well... Guess I'd better break the news...'

                                            But he'd miscalculated, and wouldn't be breaking it like he wanted. Max's words had been too soft for human ears. The little piglet had forgotten there weren't any human ears among the assembled.

Jessica...!" he said to himself, pointing and reciting the sense the Wilds had given of each new path as it opened. 'Wait, their paths, too? Nooo...' Max frowned.                                                         The little pig got lost in his thoughts, and turned with a surprised grunt as the Goat asked about his plan. "... P-P-Plan? Whaaaat?" Max put on an innocent smile. "W-Why would you...?" It was as believable as a kid who's been caught with his hand in the cookie jar and crumbs on his face. His childish physique made the likeness even stronger. And he knew it. The smile slowly shrank, giving way to a pained expression. His face then cycled through a range of contemplative, unhappy looks, his eyes darting from Nate, off and away, to Summernickel, or to some empty spot in the chamber as he wrestled with himself. To tell his friend the truth, or keep it from prying ears? Finally, eyes and face scrunched up, the piglet seemed decided and eased a bit, sighing.

                                                        Max looked at Nate. And began to act. Poorly. "I-I... I d-don't know why you'd think I had a p-plan. Heh... Y-You heard me in c-court: I-I'm just a st-stupid... pig." The last word remained hard to get out. He trembled a moment, about the only genuine and believable part of this 'performance.' The grin he faked was about as convincing. "Ha ha, m-me with a plan. Ha." He shook his head a moment, and then inclined it toward his path. "N-No wonder I'm g-going t-to..." The swine swallowed dry. "T-to t-tie m-myself even c-closer t-to the C-Collector." he trembled as the full danger and risk of his plan crossed his mind; he'd tried so hard not to think about it.

                                                        Changing tracks, and taking a few breaths to calm himself, the little pig raised a hand to his chin.* "O-Of course...
