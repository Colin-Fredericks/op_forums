            The Dawn that shared his power from long ago has gifted him with no incites for how he would improve his endurance beyond the economy of movement that Jensven has already employed. Perhaps it was time to dig down and bring forth the things he had learned in his life to the point of his golden awakening. Hours spent at the forge, or tramping through woods, hunting, cutting lumber, and the little training he did when it was thought he could have been one of the reavers. These are the memories he draws on as he presses to give Solon a good match at the very least. He knows his fatigue is something he needs to work on. So, like he has many times since his life changed, he just focuses on the rhythm of the dance of blades and tries not to think of how tired he was getting. His arms were not lead. His feet are light when he needs to shift them or lift them, steel when he needs to press the advantage.

            Block. Step back. Shift. Thrust. Advance. Block. Push back. Advance.

            So it goes for what seems to be an eternity. Time slows to a crawl as his senses are heightened and his blood rushes through his body. The Dawn was in the battle now. He didn't use his golden power, but he was a better warrior just as a side effect of having been chosen of the sun. His arm had more strength and his blade was more skillful. It was almost not fair to Solon, but it was as fair as he could make it. Besides, a good mortal warrior could best him if he held back the sun within.

            Jensven only has two (2) Stamina and one (1) Resistance. This means he only has three dice to roll for this one without any stunts. So Stamina (2) + Resistance (1) = Three (3) dice = two successes. Not bad at all, if you consider the odds were that there would be only one success there.

                Minutes drag on. The Warriors are too well matched.

                It reaches a critical point. Solon attempts a downward slash to overpower Jensven's defense before exhaustion takes him. His attack is just a little too clumsy, though, and Jensven bats it aside. The weight of Solon's heavier weapon works against him as Jensven steps inside his guard and swiftly cuts upwards, taping his blade on Solon's armor just below his right armpit; had this been a real fight Jensven could have easily removed the Althea's arm.

                Solon lets go of his blade and stumbles back a few steps before falling to the ground. Alopekis rushes over to his people, licking the reclining warrior's face.

                "I yield," Solon croaks, laughter in his voice. The stunned crowd beings applauding Jensven's success. Shadow rushes over to Jensven, his bushy tail wagging so hard that it is difficult for him to walk straight.

                What do?

                    [Yi]

                    The wyrm dances, its shell casting off bronze and topaz and limestone, powered by the prayer of Solar and worker. The cast-off shell forms into the massive temple-hospice, sanctified by the Light of the Zenith's soul.

                    One thing is conspicuously absent; an altar. There remains a place for it, but none was formed. Was that Yi's intent, or perhaps an oversight? Mayhaps he was thinking ahead, if only subconsciously, to the task ahead....

                    (Meanwhile, elsewhere)

                    Yu Shan was clouded in misty darkness. The sky above glistened with stars, and the emerald star of secrets gleamed Ascendant. An auspicious sign, given the nature of Origenes' meeting today.

                    His sorceries had quickened the journey to the Celestial city. Even with the wealth of ambrosia his sizable cult afforded him, it was insufficient to grease the wheels of the celestial bureaucracy to mobilize a divine force in Novalesk's defense.

                    As expected. Now, to turn to his other task.

                    It took some bribery, calling in some favors, and even promising a few more, including one to a Terrestrial in service to the Bureau of Nature (and someone else as well, if the silver whirls on the exalt's arm were any indication), but he'd found out what he could about the ex-Lookshan solar who had offered him service. Even better, he'd found another spirit, a minor deity of healing in the Bureau of Humanity, who'd had dealings with the Zenith before.

                    The meeting was in the slums of the Eastern Districts, but the healer-god's reputation was immaculate. Origenes sat across a marble table from the bearded deity in a snake-skin robe.

                    "It seems the solar built you a Temple in my city," Origenes said without preamble.

                    Asclepius' eyebrows rose, "That changes the nature of this meeting."

                    Origenes created a steeple with his fingers, "Indeed. I'm sure we can come to an equitable arrangement."

                                    [Wyld]

                                    Amelia, Tirien and Dero climb down the fountain, Teleri levitating down beside you. They travel deeper and deeper into the bowels of the earth. Some ways down, the silvery walls break into gilt and you all lose your grip, falling down into a large cavern, the illuminated by both Amelia's Solar Saber and the firelight of a monstrous forge of gold and copper. Therein is a veritable horde of identical figures. They are short, squat humanoids nearly as wide as they are tall. They are seemingly formed of some type of yellow-white lustrous stone. Though the shape of their bodies and features are similar to those of humanity, something about their proportions gives them an insectoid cast.

                                    These figures are working their forge, drawing forth tools and ornaments of all shapes and sizes from its blazing depths. Works of bronze, tin, silver, and gold abound, and many a worker stands over a gilt anvil rhythmically hammering ruby, sapphire and diamond into red-hot metal. The two rhythms of the hammering join with three strains of horn-like (singing? grunting?) originating from the throats of the workers to produce a hard, bouncing song that sets the pace of the labors.

                                    Finished works are placed upon massive carts, which are then pushed deeper into the bowels of the earth. Despite seeing dozens of carts shipped down in the moments since your arrival, you have seen nothing come up, save that of a man draped in gaudy silks and copper bangles.

                                    High above the workers, in front of a massive door of gold, sits a great ruby throne, on which an equally impressively sized gold figure sits.

                                    (Saffron)

                                    Your quest into the Wyld, has hitherto been unsuccessful. In fact, you're fairly certain that you're lost. You'd heard tales of a nameless dread in the Far East, a black beast whose songs strike terror in the hearts of all. But that damnedable Guildsman left you to rot in the Wyld after taking your money. Luckily, a small lizard-porcupine whose quills were are swords helpfully pointed you in the right direction. Either it was mistaken or lying, because this golden forge-cavern isn't a city of copper.

                                    Damn it, you'll never make it to Lost Tarhoun at this rate.

                                    Taking stock of your situation, you notice a quartet of figures, one in gilt armor, one in silver robes, a third in a ragged and tattered brown cloak, and the last a flamboyantly dressed troubadour atop a backwards goat. They look almost as out of place here as you do.

                                        The crowd is awed; they clearly did not expect this. A pregnant silence falls over the salle; perhaps they expect you to speak out in favor of the war effort?

                                                The Dawn looks around the salle at the warriors gathered around. They were expecting him to say something. He had always believed that it is best to tell the truth, especially when people were relying on you to give them guidance of some sort. He sees no reason to not follow that ideal now.

                                                "Yi is an ally of mine. He fought with me and two others to defend a 'foundry' I believe it is called," Jensven starts his explanation simply. There is no need to embellish the tale. It has enough impact on its own. "I claimed a kill of one of the Fae creatures. Yi claimed one of their nobles. This strike was the one that caused the Fae to flee the foundry. It also served to defend the life of one of Origenes' daughters and my Shadow here." He gestures to the dog as this point is mentioned.

                                                "At dawn, I go with Yi to the Hell Realm. We will return with an item of value to Origenes. I will not ask any of you to go with me." The Northerner looks over those gathered. "I wish to tell you how good Yi is. The man I believe he is."

                                                "I have hammered out shells for large suits of armor that the artificers will animate for the battle ahead. I did this because I have heard of a large Fae force headed this way. There are Novalesk warriors that are fighting these Fae now. I built the armor in order to help them." The Ascendent Sun pauses for effect. "I intend to go myself and help them with my blade, Cinder." He touches the blade in question as he speaks it's name. "This is iron. Fae fear it. I know. I had to defend my home from ones and twos of Fae with our clan's reavers. I know what they can do to people. I have seen it. I won't let it happen here." Another pause for effect. "I will die first." His gaze falls to the floor and he is quiet for a long moment.

                                                "I believe Yi would die with me." A quick glance at Shadow, and Jensven's head snaps up, "I won't ask any of you to go with me there either." The Bronze Tiger's gaze slowly scans the faces looking at him. "I would be glad of any that would come. I know you know what can happen if the Novalesk Warriors lose. Stay, go to fight, leave here for a safer place. The choice is yours. I will not fault any who won't go to fight. What a test for your strength, to fight the Fae." A glint of excitement is seen in his eyes and a lupine grin spreads across his face.

                                                "What do you say, warriors? Will you fight, will you stay here, or will you leave for other lands? The choice is yours. You have my respect any way you choose."

                                                I will activate Presence Essence Overwhelming using two (2) personal and two (2) peripheral motes for a total of four (4) motes spent on the activation. This leaves me with ten (10) motes in my personal pool and twenty five (25) motes in my peripheral pool. This allows me to add four (4) dice to my Charisma (3) + Presence (1) roll to attempt to convince these warriors that I am a decent fellow and my judgement of Yi is sound. I will assume a one die stunt and use the reward of two (2) motes to bring my personal pool back up to twelve (12) motes.

                                                This means my total roll is Charisma (3) + Presence (1) + Excellency (4) + Stunt (1) = Nine (9) dice for six successes.
