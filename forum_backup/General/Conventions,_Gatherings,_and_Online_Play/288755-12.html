                                                Originally posted by DeathmatchFM View Post
                                                Just a reminder guys, but Jim said not to post too much of our character's thoughts.
                                                Yeah. Thank you for bringing this up, James. I know this may seem a little odd to you guys, but posts that include lengthy portions of the characters' thoughts are very jarring. Think of it like this- if we were all together, sitting around a table and playing this game, would everyone be able to see into your characters' thoughts? Nope. They'd hear what your characters say, and they would have the actions that you describe, but they would not know what the character was REALLY thinking, under most circumstances. When we're sitting around a table, we have to infer what everyone else's character is thinking and feeling based on the described actions.

                                                If, on the other hand, we can simply read every PC's innermost thoughts and feelings like a first person narrative... it takes away a lot of the uncertainty of personal interaction.

                                                There are exceptions to this rule. Obviously Mindspeak and similar abilities, or if you're narrating an internal dialogue, such as the character having a mental conversation with a past life or some such (though, naturally, half that conversation would be me). The other big exception will be blue booking during interludes- stylistically, you guys will be free to write those in any style you'd like.

                                                But, during play, it's not a good idea to include lengthy portions that detail the character's inner thoughts, especially verbatim internal monologues. A little bit is OK once in a while, and if we're in a situation where a character gets separated from the rest of the party and has no one to have meaningful interaction with for a period of time I will relax this considerably, but when everyone's together, try to confine what you write to what the other characters can experience with their own senses (which, remember, usually includes smell since we're talking about werewolves here, which does give some additional latitude).

                                                I understand the desire to express as much of the characters and their motivations as possible. But remember, while we are telling a story, we are not writing a novel. This is a cooperative storytelling experience, and part of that cooperative experience includes misunderstandings and misinterpretations which are muted or stifled when everyone knows OOC what every other character is thinking.

                                                Tone. Inflection. Gestures. Body Language. Scent. Facial Expression. Tail Position. Gaze. Breathing. All of these (and many more) are tools you can use to express what is going on inside your characters' heads without spelling it out (and that's not even counting good old fashioned speech).

                                                I think the Robot Devil put it best, if you'll indulge me by watching this clip. :-)

                                                -Jim

                                                    Actually, for an example of what I'm talking about, let me take a selection from one of Bashir's posts (hope you don't mind me putting you on the spot, Bashir).

                                                    Originally posted by Rall Bashir View Post
                                                    Jon could hear it all in his head now. All the complaining no matter what road they take. It's too cold out here. It's too wet and icy in the canoe. Why are there so many empty packets of strawberry cheesecake trail mix and cans of Dr. Peppers back here? And he must not forget about being trapped in a car with a wolf that smells like... that.

                                                    Jon didn't like where any of this was going. He glanced briefly over to the elders. He was about to ask for advice, but he then stopped. No. No running to the "adults" for help. After choking back his words, he turned to Val and responded, "Well, we can take my car. I mean, I could fit all six of us in there. Bit cramped. Also, the heater only has two settings: off, and subtle. But if we're going to be inconspicuous, do you think the Diplomat is the way to go? No, I'm honestly asking your opinion." Though maybe he wasn't, as Jon cocked his head to the side, a pained look on his face as he tried to indicate Echoes. On one hand, he genuinely wanted to respect her. On the other, would being trapped in a closed car be good on the olfactory senses?
                                                    This is what I want to avoid. We can all see exactly what Jon was thinking just by reading. Think about other ways that these things could have been expressed. Bashir did a good job of giving descriptions of what Jon was doing, his expressions and gestures, but then goes on to explain exactly what each of those gestures meant, leaving us no room for ambiguity.

                                                    Let's take the last three sentences especially. Bashir details exactly what Jon's feelings are about Echoes and why. Wouldn't it be more interesting if we had to figure it out for ourselves from Jon's actions? For example... "Jon cocked his head to the side, a pained look on his face as he tried to indicate Echoes. He reached out one hand a little, gesturing for her to include herself in the conversation, but with the other hand he rubbed his nose and sniffed slightly without seeming to realize what he was doing."

                                                    To be clear, I'm not trying to re-write anyone's posts, I'm just trying to give an example of how these inner thoughts and feelings could potentially be expressed without verbatim dictation of those thoughts.

                                                    Originally posted by Rall Bashir View Post
                                                    Jon kept his back to the Barbie doll while giving his opinion to the others, "I think we should walk it, as she suggests. Though I for one will give the 'as wolves' thing a pass. I've only tried it once, and I'm still finding my legs. Or all four. I think we should do what we feel we do best. If we aren't in our best form, we may end up fucking everything up..." He stretched his words to emphasize them, "...on our first miiiiission. I've got some old blankets in the trunk. They need to be washed, though. That could keep you warm, I think."

                                                    A hand thrusts into his jacket pocket and pulled out a packet of strawberry cheesecake trail mix. Jon opened it and crunched down on a couple of the oat cluster pieces. Breakfast of champions.
                                                    This part, on the other hand, was perfect. Jon turns his back on Hannah, but we're left to interpret for ourselves what he means by that gesture. Bashir provides emphasis for Jon's words by explaining Jon's tone and inflection, drawing out the words, rather than simply saying what he was emphasizing and why.

                                                    Do you guys get what I mean now?
