    MAGE THE AWAKENING
    A    NTHOLOGY OF POWER
    Game Intro

    Synopsis
    Anthology of Power is a play by post game for Mage: The Awakening. It is set in Boston and the surrounding area of Massachusetts, featuring the Consilium and its respective Cabals described in Boston Unveiled. I have written a story consisting of nine chapters, and I'll continue to write further stories which will be played upon completion. The Anthology kicks off with:


    'THE BLACK PYRAMID'
    • Chapter One: What Words We Haven't Read
    • Chapter Two: The Door to Somewhere Not
    • Chapter Three: Sanctum of the Sleeping
    • Chapter Four: To Thine Own Self
    • Chapter Five: Eater of Eternity
    • Chapter Six: The Stones Before Nothing
    • Chapter Seven: It Beckons to Thee
    • Chapter Eight: Like Shards of Glass
    • Chapter Nine: A Third Temple Midnight

    Player Recruitment
    I'm recruiting three players. This is not a "first come first serve" scenario. I want to insure player compatibility. I'll choose the players and characters I feel are best for the game. I'll require anyone interested to fill out the Character Template given below, complete with a Prelude written by the player. This is a writing sample that shows me your style. Effectively, think of it as an “Audition Post”. About 3-5 solid paragraphs will do, but if you've got more in you, then go for it. Feel free to message me with any questions you might have. I plan on being very patient about this, waiting until I have enough submissions to choose from. So take your time. Once I've chosen the Players for my game, I'll update this section to indicate as such. Until then consider it open.

    *Update*
    This game has been filled. However, if this game is your cup of tea, let me know. I may run future games that I'll need players for.

    Character Motivations
    As the title implies, power is the driving motivation behind these stories. This could be power as a means to a noble end, as a goal unto itself, or even to serve completely immoral reasons. However, your character must always put the Cabal first. There may be some conflicts that crop up between Characters at times, but a Cabal that has no ties of loyalty or friendship can ultimately be destructive to the game.

    Honor System Mechanics
    I don't know about you, but I like the feel of real dice in my hands. For this reason, I won't be using any digital dice rollers for this game. Everyone may roll their own dice, and trust in one another's desire for a game in which your characters' failures are just as interesting and exciting as their successes. If not more so.

    PROLOGUE: THE TRIUMVIRATE


    April 13th, 1984

    I'd swear the rain was the size of golf balls. It splattered the windshield of my old Plymouth Colt as I followed the winding driveway through the hills. When I got to the gate I swore, grumbling loudly to myself as I grabbed my umbrella and waded out into the storm to unlock it. Even with the umbrella, I was soaked through by the time I got back into the gray car. "I'm too sodding old for this." Needless to say, I didn't get back out to close it. The Yanks would give me a lecture about that, but right now I couldn't bring myself to care. I was running late, which was simultaneously ironic and not the least bit unusual. It was all part of an Acanthus' charm, at least that's how I always explained the perpetual lateness. Maybe I even convinced myself sometimes.

    The old Gothic mansion came into view, and every time I saw it I couldn't believe that I was a partial owner. The courtyard was huge, with a big fountain at the center, and a series of archways along the path to the two double doors of the mansion. I parked the Colt next to the fountain. If the sky hadn't been falling I would have taken it to the parking area with the others' cars, but I didn't want to spend any more time in the storm than necessary. Even so, I was twice as drenched before I made it inside. The Yanks were waiting for me.

    "You're late John." He said this to me more out of habit than as an admonition. They were used to this by now. We were both in our fifties, but his shoulder length hair was still dark brown, where mine had already turned mostly gray.

    "Well in case you haven't noticed Miles, it is a bit soggy out. Now where's the bloody coffee, I'm freezing." He nodded towards the kitchen around the corner, and throwing my sopping coat onto a chair I poured myself a hot black mug of lifegiving caffeine. I joined my old friend at the small coffee table at the entrance hall, taking a seat across from him. "Where's Amelia?"

    "Here" said a tall redheaded woman in her mid forties, but who looked more like she was in her late thirties. "I saw you drive up from the window. Tell me you didn't just buy that car?"

    "My dear, just because one has money, doesn't mean one wastes it. Also as I do understand it, the purpose is to transport an individual from one location to another. In that regard, it would seem to live up to the advertising. If you want to flaunt your wealth, get a bloody remote for the bloody front gate."

    Miles sat his newspaper down and looked at his watch. "It's nearly time. We should prepare." The Moros was a tall, fierce looking man in far better shape than I. Everything about him screamed an air of authority.

    "You're kidding" said Amelia. "You can't possibly expect us to perform the rituals in this? Besides, the ritual space will have been completely washed out."

    "You know what day it is. If we don't do it now we'd have to wait three months."

    "It's Friday the thirteenth. So what? There's nothing Supernal about that!"

    "It has symbolic signifigance. That matters for us, and you know it."

    Amelia sighed, but didn't argue the point any further. Silently we each retreated to our rooms to dawn our ritual attire. Mine were a set of green robes, made from thick wool, lined with blue trim. Written upon the trim was script in Old Welsh, alongside a series of Atlantean Glyphs. I gathered a small cloth sack containing powdered incense, and my silver wand.

    We exited out the back door, Miles in a black Russian kaftan with a long staff of bones scortched black, and Amelia draped in red Buddhist robes with a string of wooden pentacles flowing from her neck. It was a long walk in such dreadful conditions, and we arrived at the ritual site I had spent exactly one month preparing only to find Amelia's earlier point validated. It was a mess. The wicks of all thirty candles were dripping wet, and the runes, carefully drawn in chalk, had been largely washed out. For a moment I was beginning to think that perhaps Amelia had been right. Clearly this was no condition to in which to conduct such an important ritual.

    Then I noticed the cup.

    Like my wand, it was made of silver. But most importantly, it had been filled to the brim with rainwater, and I began to smile. What perfect symbolism, after all! Knowledge raining down onto the Fallen World from Supernal Arcadia above. That, coupled with the timing made things perfect, despite the setbacks. I nodded to my fellows, and they took up positions within the stone circle we had erected behind the mansion. We formed a triangle, with Miles at the point, and I began to chant. I cleared my mind of the downpour, and the resulting cold in my bones, reminding myself that Amelia would be able to heal us of any adverse effects. It took a little over three hours, but I felt the Supernal reach down, calling on the sympathy with my Watchtower. As I neared the end, I spoke out in English for the benefit of us all. "We, of the Triumvirate part the clouds of time, and bear witness to the sowing of our seeds. We gaze upon what fruits they bear, for good or for ill. Let the Black Pyramid casts its shadow across history."

    Images began to form in the water of the silver cup. Three people, staring back at me. Our own future, as I'd intended. Wait...no. Something was wrong. Very wrong. The image was erratic, and the waters within the cup began to froth. There was a great sound welling up with that water, like moving one's finger along the rim of a glass, but growing painfully loud. And then, the glass shatters into a million tiny pieces. The sound was filling my soul, and the shattering was terribly painful. My eyes had involuntarily closed, but I forced myself to stare back into the cup. I saw that it was not us in the waters...but three others. Mages I had never seen before. That harmonic note had reached a fever pitch, like being in the eye of a Tornado. I had the sudden sensation of being blasted along, like falling into rapids. This river of sound carried me along against my will, and reality tore away around me. The rain stopped and began again, the grass around me died and was reborn. Snow fell across the land, then gave way to blooming flowers. All of this and more happened in the blink of an eye. And still I was swept away, carried beyond.

    I was lost to the rapids. I couldn't breath.

                      Prelude
                      Three times Sam Carter dreamed of the unshakeable tower, and three times was he snatched away while still he paused on the high cliff above it. All iron and glory, it stood against the rending wind as if determined not to play by the world's rules. Mystery hung about it as clouds about a fabulous unvisited mountain; and as Sam stood breathless and expectant on that cliff, he felt his own weakness sharply. He was a tumbleweed in that wind, not the tower. He had neither goal nor determination. And as always, Sam stepped on the ladder leading to the tower, and was torn by the wind.

                      The clock rang at 7, as it did every day, and Sam                       grudgingly got out of bed, as he did every day. The morning routine flowed slowly- shaving, coffee, breakfast. It was when Sam went outside to pick up the mail that things got weird. Or rather, when Sam tried to go outside. The front door of his appartment (which doubled as his office) was impossible to open. There was no handle, the locks lost their buttons, and thick metal bars hugged the door tightly. Sam ran to the windows, but they were boarded shut, with no visible way to open them. Sam dashed to the phone, but every number he tried no longer existed. He banged on the door until his hands began to bleed, but there was no answer. Panicking, Sam sat down tried to calm himself. Clearly he was still sleeping, this couldn't possibly be true! You can't invade someone's room and change all locks, weld the door and bar the windows completely silently! This is impossible! But, no matter how hard Sam tried to wake up, nothing changed.

                      Time seemed to drag on on, and nothing changed. Sam got hungry and ate his lunch. He tried to scream for help, or kick the door, but no one answered. Sam ate and slept without dreems. It seemed like time has stopped. No one was comming to help him. Sam was all alone in his room, with nothing to do but think. As days went by, Sam wasn't even sure if there's any point in levaing his room at all. He didn't do much with his life anyway, right? The job was pointless, the education was pointless. Why do anything? Why go anywhere? Life has no meaning, so he might as well sit in the room, right?

                      Days went by, and as Sam pondered his life (what else was there to do?) he saw that he was wrong. Private Investigation didn't pay well, but it was fun. Sam may have been a fuck up in the university, but he made friends and learned things. If he never left the room, then it's like he doesn't exist, right? Sam couldn't allow that. He is here! He exists! He needs to leave the room and be part of the world! He needs to dream and see the Tower! As Sam focused on himself, on his identity and dreams, he felt iron on his hand. He knew what he has to do. The door shattered as the iron gauntlet struck it. For the first time in ages Sam went outside, filled with purpose. He stood upon the wide granite cliff, and the Iron Tower stood before him. Sam descended the seventy steps to it, and though the deadly wind of Pandemonium shred his flesh and broke his bones, it couldn't stop him. Sam walked into the Tower and signed his name in blood, claiming his right over reality and self.

                      The clock rang at 7, as it did every day...

                          Okay Mahasamatman, this is mostly solid, but with three problems:

                          1. You'll need to trade either Fate or Prime for Space. In the Mage Quick Reference on Page. 69 of Mage: The Awakening, it explains that two of your starting Arcana must be your Path's Ruling Arcana. If you choose to replace Prime, it also means you'll have to change your Prime Rote to a Space Rote.

                          2. Your Gnosis should be 1 since you haven't spent any of your Merit Dots to increase it. This also means that you can't have a Legacy, since you must be Gnosis 3.

                          3. On Page 39 of Banishers, it explains that the Merits listed in this supplement are impossible to learn for the other Mages. So you'll need to replace Extemporaneous Affinity.

                                                  Also, regarding the Merits, you have more than your 10xp would allow. If you put your remaining starting Merit Dot in, say, Hallow, then Status 1, Status 1, Mentor 1, and Extemporaneous Affinity 1 would collectively cost 8xp. Extemporaneous Affinity 3 would require an additional 10xp (4 for 2 Dots, 6 for 3 Dots), since Merits have to be bought successively.

                                                  Regarding legacies, as far as I can see from the Core, you only gain the effective Ruling Arcanum benefit from the Legacy's primary Arcanum. So if you already have that Arcanum as part of your Ruling Arcana, it has no additional effect. That being said Legacies are one of the few aspects of Mage that I haven't explored much beyond the Core, so if you find something in a supplement that contradicts this, let me know and give me the Page number.

                                                          Nimbus
                                                          Fer's eyes take iron glint, burning with unbendable will. The world around seems like a perfect mechanism for a moment, every thing in its place and every man doing exactly what he needs to do. The air takes on a metalic taste, and hard choices seem easier to follow through.

                                                          Character Biography
                                                          Sam Carter was born and raised in Boston. His father, Igor, is a fairly successful layer. His mother, Elizabeth, is an accountant. Sam was raised in good conditions, and given good education. However, he's about as sharp as a hammer, so Sam never managed to get good grades and become a real lawyer himself. With whatever law knowledge he got from the university, Sam went to the proffession playing to his strengths - perceptiveness and charm. Sam became a detective (disappointing his parents), and has been working for Boston PD for almost 5 years now. The job doesn't give him much wealth, and Sam can barely support the lifestyle he's used to. Four years ago Sam Awakened. His life, previously a meaningless drift from day to day, now had focus. He has seen the Tower of Stone, and saw the world as it truly is. Sam plunged head first into research and training. He secluded himself from the world for months, searching the limits of his abilities, honing the newfound power over mind and probability. Two years ago he was introduced into the mage society, by his future mentor, and a member of Bene Ashmedai, Patrick Stein (aka Foxhound). Since then Sam has completed his formal education in the Silver Ladder taking on the shadow name "Fer", and is ready to face the world once again, now with determination guiding him to power.

                                                          Personality and appearance: Sam is somewhat selfish, but loyal. He prefers to solve problems in a peaceful way, through diplomacy and trickery, if possible. Sam places his covenant over his legacy, and his legacy over the order. Sam usually wears red and black and has a liking for designer trench coats. He almost always wears his mirrorshades, as they are his magical tool - a modern substitute for a mirror.

                                                          Goals: Currently Sam is working on climbing the career ladder in the police department, subtly influencing it to improve the efficiency. He rouses spirits in equipment, calms down overly agitated people with Mind magic, and stimulates minds. Sam's magical research is mostly introspective, and devoted to Goetia. Sam seeks mastery of his own flaws, and looks for grimoires and artefacts left by others who walked this path before.

                                                          Morning routine: Each morning Sam carefuly grooms himself, performing his oblation. Before going to work Sam visits the Hallow to cast Glorious Shield (day) and Mental Shield (day). Because of this, Sam usually has to get up quite early

                                                          Prelude
                                                          Three times Sam Carter dreamed of the unshakeable tower, and three times was he snatched away while still he paused on the high cliff above it. All copper and glory, it stood against the rending wind as if determined not to play by the world's rules. Mystery hung about it as clouds about a fabulous unvisited mountain; and as Sam stood breathless and expectant on that cliff, he felt his own weakness sharply. He was a tumbleweed in that wind, not the tower. He had neither goal nor determination. And as always, Sam stepped on the ladder leading to the tower, and was torn by the wind.

                                                          The clock rang at 7, as it did every day, and Sam                                                           grudgingly got out of bed, as he did every day. The morning routine flowed slowly- shaving, coffee, breakfast. It was when Sam went outside to pick up the mail that things got weird. Or rather, when Sam tried to go outside. The front door of his appartment was impossible to open. There was no handle, the locks lost their buttons, and thick metal bars hugged the door tightly. Sam ran to the windows, but they were boarded shut, with no visible way to open them. Sam dashed to the phone, but every number he tried no longer existed. He banged on the door until his hands began to bleed, but there was no answer. Panicking, Sam sat down tried to calm himself. Clearly he was still sleeping, this couldn't possibly be true! You can't invade someone's room and change all locks, weld the door and bar the windows completely silently! This is impossible! But, no matter how hard Sam tried to wake up, nothing changed.

                                                          Time seemed to drag on on, and nothing changed. Sam got hungry and ate his lunch. He tried to scream for help, or kick the door, but no one answered. Sam ate and slept without dreems. It seemed like time has stopped. No one was comming to help him. Sam was all alone in his room, with nothing to do but think. As days went by, Sam wasn't even sure if there's any point in levaing his room at all. He didn't do much with his life anyway, right? The job was pointless, the education was pointless. Why do anything? Why go anywhere? Life has no meaning, so he might as well sit in the room, right?

                                                          Days went by, and as Sam pondered his life (what else was there to do?) he saw that he was wrong. Police work didn't pay very well, but it was fun. Sam may have been a fuck up in the university, but he made friends and learned things. If he never left the room, then it's like he doesn't exist, right? Sam couldn't allow that. He is here! He exists! He needs to leave the room and be part of the world! He needs to dream and see the Tower! As Sam focused on himself, on his identity and dreams, he felt things change. He knew what he has to do. The door shattered as Sam's hand struck it. For the first time in ages Sam went outside, filled with purpose. He stood upon the wide granite cliff, and the Stone Watchtower stood before him. Sam descended the seventy steps to it, and though the deadly wind shred his flesh and broke his bones, it couldn't stop him. Sam walked into the Tower and signed his name in blood, claiming his right over reality and self. He felt the world around him change. Where once there was a barren waste now were lush gardens. Where deadly wind once tore everything now ran wild beasts and spirits. The world had purpose.

                                                          The clock rang at 7, as it did every day...
