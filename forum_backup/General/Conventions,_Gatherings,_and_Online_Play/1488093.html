    [Recruiting]Mummy: The Curse 2nd Edition

    07-26-2022, 05:48 PM
    I need three players, with myself as GM. You’d be members of a meret, of the five Guilds out of the corebook. We’d play around with asynchronous time, weaving together a tale of a self-fulfilling prophecy. The PCs must thwart the prophecy, only to find out it was THEY would set the whole thing in motion to deal with an even bigger threat. Big cosmic mysteries and body horror would be the theme of the campaign, a sort of gnostic quest filled with extremely the horrors of Duat liberally peppered throughout.

    The game would stay as Mummy-focused as possible. The other splats certainly exist as written, but I’m wanting to focus on the Mummy side of things.

          MtC in India would be very interesting to me. If we want to explore things of body horror, I'd strongly recommend the temple of Kamakhya, in Assam (North of India). The area is strongly associated with strange traditions differing strongly from the rest of India - for example, the temple is not far from Mayong, sometimes dubbed the "capital of black magic" in India. The temple itself is the stage for annual rituals where performers, the Deodhani, cover their skin in the red cinnabar of the Brahmaputra river that flows nearby (this dye is seen as the blood of either Kamakhya, the Goddess worshipped at the temple, or Sati) and start dancing until they get possessed by one of the deities worshipped in the temple - like Manasa, the local snake-goddess and mistress of venoms, or Smaashanakali, Kali of the Funerary Ashes, or Chhinamasta, who holds forth her own decapitated head to drink the blood from her own severed neck... so yeah, pretty Mummy-fitting all in all.

          Of course, we can pick anything else, but since I have to study this specific area for my uni, contributing to a setting doc would be a great way to combine fun and work for me lol

                                  Ok, some preliminary setting information:

                                  Mummies have been in India a very long time, and the entire country seems awash in relics, Vestiges and Sekhem. Kamakhya is ground zero for several feuding Arisen cults, who use the overwhelming presence of belief in magic and the supernatural to openly perform Utterances.

                                  Unfortunately...hungry ravenous ghosts of things that never lived, the div, have plagued the temple complex since time immemorial. The div were never human, but exactly what they are is unclear. The div have a hunger for Sekhem, though, and viciously feud with local Arisen over vessels of all sorts. The div are statted up as Rank 2 and Rank 3 ghosts, but rumors of particularly large and bloated ‘elders’ have persisted down the millennia.

                                  Because cults feud so viciously over Kamakhya and it’s pilgrims and priests, the Guilds in the region are deeply divided. It’s not to the point of open sectarian conflict, but it could, in theory, get there. The Tef-Aabhi currently hold the upper hand, as their cults designed many of the country’s grandest temples.

                                  EDIT: All the splats are obviously in India, but the few times they cross paths with the Arisen it is usually blood and Utterance vs fang and Gift, as the wolf children of Wepwawet hold secret rites in Kamakhya that mummies are never allowed to attend. The Arisen, not wanting conflict, simply do as the wolfmen ask.

                                  The Ganges River is a natural channel for Sekhem, an odd spiritual purity for such a mundanely polluted river. Arisen find that they can briefly lengthen their Descents by bathing in the Ganges, though not by more than a few days. Furthermore, there are rumors—-never substantiated!—-that Su-Menent have figured out a way to store Pillars in the river, as opposed to a cultist or a lesser immortal.

                                  EDIT2: Considering the blood motif Kamakhya’s celebrations revere, among the lesser Immortals, in this area blood bathers would be most common. Most are grim-faced bearded Brahmin, practicing Rites so old even they can’t ascertain a beginning. They honor obscure, strange gods and are deliberately severe in personality. They have a taciturn relationship with the Arisen, a grudging respect that nevertheless has a profound echo of envy.

                                  EDIT3: The Red Priest, a Deceived, is the bogeyman of Kamakhya. Whether he even exists in unclear. But the div call him their brother, and practice strange rituals in his name. No one has seen the Red Priest, and evidence for his very existence is, at best, circumstantial.

                                  EDIT4: Known only to the Arisen, there’re cave entrances protected by powerful Utterances that lead to deep within the earth beneath Kamakhya. At the very bottom is a cave barely large enough for a Size 5 creature to fit into...and a crack in the wall, leading...somewhere. Kepher detects something of great value in Sekhem on the other side, but none can get to it, quite yet.

                                      The Deceived
                                      The Deceived do not exist. They died during the Rite of Return. Any stories to the contrary are heresy.

                                      ...so, of course a Deceived dwells here. The Red Priest, a horrifying aberration of a bloated, vastly obese man, sits crippled on his throne, gestating Amkhata in a womb he made inside himself with knives and clamps and industrial-scale paperclips. Flies gather to him, and beetles root through his viscera. He is a mad monster, and his cultists are quite zealous in preserving his secrets, no matter the cost. He rants and he raves, but often sits drooling for weeks on end.

                                      The Red Priest has an alliance with the daitya, though no one can be sure of that. Circumstantial evidence certainly points to it.

                                      The Shuankhsen
                                      Though she is worshipped under the name Kali, Shuankhsen recognize their hateful mother anywhere they go, no matter the name mortals apply to her. The Thuggee were a series of Shuankhsen cults.

                                      The Kher-Minu
                                      A single Kher-Minu, an unknown male, guards Kamakhya as a whole. He says nothing, just obeying mutely any commands a Deathless gives him. It has been three centuries since his last activation.

                                      Maar-Kherit
                                      Kamakhya’s Su-Menent have a secret. Hidden away, somewhere in the temple complex, is a library containing knowledge of Maar-Kherit activities in India. The Priests guard this lore zealously.

                                      Thankfully, no Maar-Kherit are in or near Kamakhya.

                                      The Daitya of Kamakhya
                                      Though the Deathless have many theories, none can satisfactorily explain the daitya. Ghosts from some prehuman civilizations, these Pygmy-sized insectile spectres hunger for Sekhem as badly as do the Deceived and the Shuankhsen. Resembling one meter tall, rain thin humanoids of an indeterminate insect species, the daitya speak in buzzing voices and rant of a coming time when humanity is as dust on the wind, and of consciousnesses echoed down millions of years to “prepare the way”.

                                      They cannot use Manifestations during the day, but are otherwise unaffected by sunlight. Certain Hindu prayers drive them away, but also, certain antediluvian Rites can call them forth. They nest in great spirit-burrows in Neter-Khertet, fighting a never-ending war with Duat’s fiends.

                                      Most daitya are statted up as Rank 2 or Rank 3 ghosts, with Anchors of unknown origin. (At least, they’ve yet to be found.) Rumors speak of much larger daitya at Rank 4 or even Rank 5, though no Memories record such an encounter.

                                      The daitya are found only in Kamakhya, nowhere else. Their burrows do not match any locality in the physical world, but are clearly nonetheless in Nether-Khertet. They call the Red Priest a brother in their practices.

                                      The language they speak among themselves in unintelligible, but many know various Indian languages. They claim to have been once much different than they are now, and bitterly anticipate the coming of a far-distant enemy.

                                      Other supernatural entities of Kamakhya
                                      Beasts
                                      The Begotten sometimes pay the temple complex a visit. It always heralds, as the Chinese say, “interesting times”.

                                      Changelings
                                      Hmmm. Pondering ideas, now.

                                      Demons
                                      Kamakhya is old Infrastructure and, though the Arisen know it not, the temple complex is protected by a powerful angel.

                                      Deviants
                                      “The what, now?”

                                      Leviathans
                                      The Wicked Tribe covets Kamakhya. The Arisen refuse to relinquish it. Trouble is eventually guaranteed.

                                      Mages
                                      In the 1890s, British Mages feuded with the Arisen for control over Kamakhya. They lost; the Arisen won. The Awakened are much more circumspect in their dealings with the Deathless, now.

                                      They are curious of the daitya but refuse to say why.

                                      Prometheans
                                      Strange things happen, sometimes, such as the False Azar. He claimed he was of Azar’s blood, though Kamakhya’s mummies sensed the wrongness in him. They drove him out with fire and Utterances, and he’s never been seen since.

                                      Sin-Eaters
                                      Some mortals come close to the Arisen in necromantic might, though the Deathless are unclear as to the specifics. They are cordial to visiting Sin-Eaters, and promptly usher them on their after a few pleasantries. They keep coming back, however, and’re mightily interested in the daitya.

                                      Vampires
                                      They come here to feed during festivals, and are vaguely aware of the Deathless. They call themselves “the Crone’s circle” and practice magic as unnatural as any Utterance. They respect the “enlightened vampires” (ie, the Arisen), and don’t cause much trouble.

                                      Werewolves
                                      India’s werewolves claim an ancient right to the territory of Kamakhya, but cannot dislodged the Arisen and have since ceased trying. (Some sort of wolfman-only sectarian conflict has demanded their attention for centuries now. They refuse to elaborate.)

                                      On certain days of the years, the werewolves politely ask for sole possession of the temple-complex for the next twenty four hours. The Deathless oblige, though beforehand they set wards and Sadikhs to keep watch tombs and relics, and afterwards they always paranoiacly check for lingering magics. Eventually, this compromise will fall through, but it’s workable for now.

                                                  The Maa-Kep
                                                  Not sure, yet, still pondering.

                                                  The Mesen-Nebu
                                                  Money flows into holy places like a flowing river, constant and steady, and the Mesen-Nebu are there to reap it all. Civil servants and outside contractors are hired on a weekly basis. In this way, the Born of Gold have their fingers in the Kamakhya pie.

                                                  The Sesha-Hebsu
                                                  Many Brahmins serve in Sesha-Hebsu cults, finding that Guild’s obsession with the Scroll of Ages to be fascinating.

                                                  More than that, the Sesha-Hebsu are in Kamakhya looking into the idea of the Mahashruti, or “authorless books”, which they believe is simply the Hindu name for the Scroll of Ages, or else a mortal reflection of it. Studying and comparing these two concepts of knowledge take up a lot of Sesha-Hebsu cultists’ time.

                                                  The Su-Menent
                                                  Surprisingly, the Su-Menent are rather weakly represented in Kamakhya. There are only two of them. Their cults, however, are huge, highly literate and keep several repositories of occult knowledges.

                                                  The Tef-Aabhi
                                                  The Tef-Aabhi reign supreme in Kamakhya, which is, interestingly enough, mostly Neithian architecture. Dedwen flows pure and hale here, and using Kepher is much easier.

                                                      Tef-Aabhi reigning on Kamakhya makes sense, the sculptures there are superb, and only the Architects have the influence over populations needed to keep a temple relevant from the 7th century up to this day. Interestingly enough, the most important and sacred item in the temple is not a human representation: it's a pierced stone kept inside the most sacred and remote room of the temple, representing the yoni (womb) of the Goddess in her form of Sati, fallen there after she jumped in a pyre...

                                                      Also, common misconception, but...

                                                      Originally posted by Demigod Beast View Post
                                                      More than that, the Sesha-Hebsu are in Kamakhya looking into the idea of the Akashic Record, which they believe is simply the Hindu name for the Scroll of Ages. That mere mortals claim to’ve accessed it both offends and intrigues them, in equal measure.
                                                      ... the Akashic Records are not really a hindu thing: they're a latter concept of Theosophy, an occult Western current that had a great influence in the end of the 19th century. Akasha is one of the elements and associated with sound, but no hindi sources I know describes it as memory storage. In one of the great religious currents, shaivism, trikālajñatvam, the knowledge of past and future, is supposed to be one of the siddhis you gain through meditation and spiritual practices - but it's seen as a power of the devotee freeing himself from the illusory world, not tapping a preexisting archive.

                                                      There's a set of notions in Indian philosophy we can levy though. Shruti is the corpus of indian sacred texts that are apaurusheya, authorless (as opposed to smrti, traceable tradition where you can point the author). The very process of commiting them to memory and being able to restitute them at the drop of a hat, svādhyāya ("the Lesson of the Self", or "Lesson of the Soul"), is deemed a devotional act.

                                                      In my opinion, Sesha-Hebsu in India would probably qualify the Scroll of Ages as Shruti (or maybe with an intensified prefix > Mahashruti, "the Great Tradition"), or Svādhyāya.
