    (Jensven)

    "Put your hand up here," Cinder sighs, "I need to put a palm in my face."

    Even if Jensven were inclined to let Cinder face-Palm himself by proxy, he does not get the opportunity. The fire-spirit shoots into the air and begins his enthusiastic bobbing and flitting that is his analog to talking with his hands.

    "Okay. To start, like, ninety-nine percent of those stories you hear about baleful gods and demons and ghosts controlling the bodies of unwitting people are complete fabrications. Or maybe three quarters," Cinder amends, "Half. Half of them are fabrications. That's as low as I'm going. Half.

    "Anyways, the point is that you people are way too focused on that meaty bag you live in for a few decades. You know it uses a derivation of burning to get the Fire essence required to stay in motion? And a really inefficient one at that. Why can't you just burn your food? You'll get more out of it, and can eat a much wider variety of foodstuffs. Where was I? Oh. Yeah. Meat-bags.

    "Okay. For the sake of this conversation, let's imagine that the bag of muddy water you're irrationally attached to is a boat. No, a cart. Carts are better. So, your body is a cart, right? Now, attach a dog to pull it. I mean a cow. What's the difference between a dog and a cow anyway? The guy who taught me this said that cows are four-legged hairy animals. Dogs are four-legged hairy animals too. Why do you even need more than one word for a four-legged hairy animals? I'm getting away from the point. Your muddy-water cart has a cow. That's your animal soul. 'Cause it's represented by an animal in the metaphor. Simile. Analogy? Whatever. The animal soul is what does all the work of pulling your body around and making it do things and stuff.

    "Now, give your mud-cart and non-descript hairy animal soul a driver. That's the higher soul, because it sits up higher on the cart than the hairy animal soul. That's the part of you that does all of your thinking and talking and whatever, and becomes a ghost or goes to your next body whenever the mud-cart you're in now breaks down. It holds the reigns to your animal soul and tells it what to do, where to go and stuff. With me so far?"

    Cinder halts his tirade for a moment, but launches back into it before Jensven has a chance to answer. "So, spiritual riding is basically like me hoping into the back of your cart. I could try to wrestle the reigns of your cow soul from you, but lets face it, you're way stronger than me and your driver soul would tie me up and throw me out of the cart. Metaphorically. I'd have to be like as much stronger than you than you are over normal people to turn you into my puppet. As powerful and stuff as I am, I can't even do that to a regular person, let alone you.

    "The guy who told me this then went on about how in some rare cases the riding spirit and higher soul share the reigns, but that requires a deep spiritual bond and abiding trust and stuff. Then he went on about some stupid things about me and a primeval devourer blah blah blah legacy of Zem-nu blah blah blah something not seen since a anti-divulge-ian age... What's an anti-divulge-ian age? An age that people don't tell each other enough stuff? Because that pretty much describes now. "

    Cinder pauses again, collecting himself after his latest tangent, "the long and short of it is that I won't be able to control you, other than maybe shouting in your ears about whether to turn left or not. I'm going to do that anyway, but if we do this then I get around easier and you get to use some of my most miraculous divine powers. Win-win, right?"

        Jensven chuckles at appropriate, and sometimes inappropriate times. However, he listens intently to the spritely little fire and does not interrupt. He only responds after the last question is presented to him.

        "In case you haven't noticed, I already have miraculous divine powers," He starts. "Also, I think you mean that you want to put your face in a palm, not the other way around. I believe things go in palms instead of in faces. Things in faces are generally not good." A short pause and then he gets more serious and introspective. "I am so attached to this bag of muddy water because it is all I have. It is my home even more than any building could be. The thought of having that taken away from me is understandably very uncomfortable. I don't blame anyone else for that sentiment."

        "You are correct that I seem to have more mental and emotional strength than the average person. The Sun has given me gifts that I still do not understand the strength of. That said I will still have to think over what you have said. I won't be willing to let you ride me yet. I don't think I will ever let you ride Shadow. I value him the way he is. I don't want anything to change him other than what would naturally make him better in the same way he is a good companion now."

        The Dawn stops and gestures to the dog for his next point, "Let's talk about dogs and cows. Shadow is a dog. He is an animal that is built to be a predator so he can eat. He would eat a cow if he could get away with it. I doubt he could kill a cow alone, but he might be able to do that. A few dogs like him and you definitely have a dead cow and well fed dogs." Jensven points to the dog's eyes and his ears, "You see how he has eyes and ears that point ahead of him? This is so he can focus on what he wants to kill and eat. Thankfully, he doesn't have to worry about that. I get him all the food he can want. This way he can serve as my companion and sometimes pull heavy things through snow. His breeding has made him well suited to pulling sleds through snow."

        The Lawgiver starts to walk in the direction he believes Yi to be in as he moves on to the traits of bovine animals, "A cow is much larger than Shadow. They also have shorter, less dense hair. The primary purpose of a cow is to serve as a means to get foodstuffs for humans. Whether they are milked, used for a plow, or slaughtered for meat. They have eyes and ears that are more to the sides of their heads so they can see and hear in a larger area than a predator. They also live in herds, large families if you want to think of it that way. This is so they have the safety of numbers for when wolves or other predators show up. Wolves are very much like Shadow, only more wild and less willing to obey commands. When predators do show up the cow doesn't necessarily need to be able to fight off or even outrun them. It just needs to be faster than at least one other cow. If the predators bring down the slower cow, this one lives another day."

        "Do you see the reasons we need different names for different four legged, furry things? They serve different purposes or do different things."

                                In the silent hour before the dawn Yi shivered, letting the world’s breath flowing down the river refresh his spirit. When he walked, still aflame with radiance, he called out to those he knew waited in the shadows he cast. “Come forth and do not hide yourselves like those ashamed. Fetch me flame and ready yourselves, this day you shall win glory worthy of song in after days.”

                                They went. Some with drawn knives, others readied improvised clubs, some skulking still between shadows; but they went. Such was the power of his voice and bearing that none questioned and none disobeyed. They returned, and his worm followed after for the breath of expectation rose from them. Then they set all together to work.

                                Seven tripods he wrought of amber, bronze, and crystal flame and each set alight drank the flame they sheltered and gave it back in ripples of gold and white; ten whirled amphora of the river opal, drawn from the common shells scattered on the strand, and rosy quartz and he filled them with the golden wine pressed by the dawn into fragrant dew and ten bowls of water for every bowl of it would not have made it thin; twenty gleaming seas fashioned like lilies out of silver and glass and their span was not less than ten cubits; twelve full sets of gear for racing horses and they were set with sparks of iron upon stone as lesser men might deck their stuff with jewels and no horse that wore them would slack its pace for want of the thunder of hoofs behind it. Last, and with greatest care, he wove seven of the marriage veils which in local speech were called kredemnon. Seven secrets and seven spells he whispered in each as he made them, binding each into the threads as he did. To those who knew these were of greatest price, a great blessing to the city and a terrible and fateful burden was each to bear.
