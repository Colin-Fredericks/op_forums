    [Realms of Pugmire] Extreme Environments

    11-22-2021, 08:50 AM
    Scorching deserts. Endless blizzards. Hurricanes and sandstorms. There are places in Realms of Pugmire that any Uplifted Race have problem to live in, but daring cats and brave dogs are coming there, nonetheless. Here are rules for those extreme areas in RoP mechanics. Hopes you like them.

    Basic idea

    Basis of those rules is that character lose 1 Stamina Point per Time Interval exposure on the environments. Sometimes the short or long rest will solve the exposure problems to character – like warming by the fire when freezing - other times only instantunos cooling or warming the body will save character. In some cases, characters with Know Nature Skill or Natural Explorer Knack related to environment can solve those problems for themselves or even their group.

    Extreme Level – SP lose Intervals

    More extreme conditions, more often Stamina Points reduction come in there. On the lists are also exemplar extreme situations those loses can come. Characters having resistance to the element generating extreme environment do not lose Stamina Points – characters resistant to fire are resistant to the scorching heat of the desert, for instance. Those are only examples, Guide can decide their own intervals.

    Extreme 1 – 1 SP lose per 4 hours – Example: Very hot summer day
    Extreme 2 – 1 SP lose per 1 hour – Example: Traveling through blizzard or desert
    Extreme 3 – 1 SP lose per 1 turn – Example: Fire on characters body

    In some extreme examples, short or even long rest cannot restore lost Stamina Points that way.

    Example Environments

    DESERT

    When the temperature is at or above 38 degrees Celsius / 100 degrees Fahrenheit, a creature exposed to the heat and without access to drinkable water lose 1 Stamina Point per hour. Creatures wearing medium or heavy armor, or who are clad in heavy clothing, lose 2 Stamina Points per hour. Creatures with resistance or immunity to fire damage automatically do not lose those points, as do creatures naturally adapted to hot climates.

    Typical ration for desert travel assumes water for time equal of Long Rest, so 8-10 hours. If creature lose water source, after 1 hour it starts to lose Stamina Points. Draft animals ( like camels on desert ) let to get rations for on length of 160 kilometers / 100 miles of travel per day. Typical transport camel can get water rations for 1 week of travel on itself for one character and transport up to 200 kilograms of cargo with water.

    Camel speed:
    Average - 16 kilometers / 10 miles per hour
    Maximum - 32 kilometers / per hour

    These animal can travel 75 to 120 miles ( 120 – 200 km ) a day at a steady trot of around 10 miles ( 16 kilometers ) per hour.


    Camels info - https://www.sciencetimes.com/article...hout-water.htm
    https://factsanddetails.com/world/ca.../item1182.html

    EXTREME COLD

    Whenever the temperature is at or below -15 degrees Celsius / 0 degrees Fahrenheit, a creature exposed to the cold lost 1 Stamina Point. Creatures with resistance or immunity to cold damage automatically do not lose those points, as do creatures wearing cold weather gear (thick coats, gloves, and the like) and creatures naturally adapted to cold climates.

    STRONG WIND

    A strong wind imposes disadvantage on ranged weapon attack rolls and Wisdom (Notice) checks that rely on hearing. A strong wind also extinguishes open flames, disperses fog, and makes flying by nonmagical means nearly impossible. A flying creature in a strong wind
    must land at the end of its turn or fall. A strong wind in a desert can create a sandstorm that imposes disadvantage on Wisdom (Notice) checks that rely on sight.

      I’ve been trying to design an environmental condition as well… I’m still not entirely satisfied, but here’s where I’m at:

      Extreme Environment: Fel Lands
      There are some lands that carry an ancient and powerful sickness, capable of killing uplifted within weeks - even if they flee. Fortunately, many such regions are marked by bizarre and sickly plants and animals along their borderlands, and many still bear ancient sign posts declaring them Cursed.

      Severity 1:
      The fel borderlands retain the curse, but are not yet deadly. Creatures can dwell within for decades before succumbing to the sickness, though will live Cursed.
      Characters who spend 4 hours within a borderland roll a Constitution Save (difficulty 15, with a cumulative +1 per 4-hour interval); on a failure, they suffer the Sickly condition, and Disadvantage on Con saves versus poison and disease (this does not include saves against the fel curse). They feel nauseated and fatigued, with a piercing headache.

      Severity 2:
      The truly cursed lands are places of wild magic. The plants and animals here have been transformed into demonic fel monsters, with unpredictable abilities, toxic blood, and madness.
      Characters who enter must make a Constitution Save (difficulty 20) every hour; on a failure, they suffer a level of Exhaustion. In addition to nausea and fatigue, they experience brain fog, will start to lose hair, and gain patchy discoloration on their skin. The character permanently suffers disadvantage on Con Saves against poison and disease (not including the fel curse) for the rest of their life - no magic can remove this.

      Severity 3:
      The wasteland at the center of fel lands is so intensely scoured that not even fel demons may dwell here long. Only a handful of monsters that are completely immune to the corruption can be found within - some powerful undead, abominations, and unique constructs. Even magic begins to fail here: divination spells (such as Sending and Clairvoyance) automatically fail, and Masterworks with complex parts stop working (though may be restored later with a Know Arcana check and exotic parts). Most fel wastelands have a terrifying ruin sprawling across them; but many have nothing more than an enormous crater, and shadows cast by the Unseen.
      Characters who enter must make a Con Save (difficulty 25) every 10 minutes; those who fail suffer a level of exhaustion, and permanently lose one Stamina Die (and that number of Stamina Points). In addition, all characters permanently suffer Disadvantage to Con saves against Poison and Disease (not including the fel curse), regardless if they pass or fail these saves. Characters begin to vomit blood, lose all of their fur, suffer blisters and boils, and are extraordinarily dizzy.


      Fel Sickness is an enduring and powerful curse. It’s affects may only be lessened with the Greater Restoration spell (each use reduces Exhaustion by 1 level, or removes Sickly); although the Old Ones designed masterwork potions specifically designed to remove the curse. It may yet be possible to find these potions in ancient ruins near (or within) the fel borderlands. However, the effects described as permanent cannot be removed, even with Man’s greatest magics. Legends do tell of ancient armor (or possibly vestments) that make the wearer immune to the Fel Curse, and daring mancers claim that resistances to Necrotic damage may bolster one’s resilience against the curse.
