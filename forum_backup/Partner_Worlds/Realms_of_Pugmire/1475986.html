    [RoP Fan] Playable Badgers and other Species

    03-07-2022, 11:30 AM
    With Pirates of Pugmire and coming of Squeaks in the Deep we finally have mechanics for making playable Species, other than Cats and Dogs. As I’m just after cool convention presentation of Badgers, I would like to give a shot on making fan rules for them. I also personally love Hamsters and got idea on their rules. So here are fan takes on new Species. 😎 Of course, you are more than welcome to add your ideas. 😉 I heard someone wanted to make Bats, few months back…

    Badgers

    Those are mixed Tribes of otters, polecats, weasels, ferrets, wolverines and ratels, mostly living in harsh climates and wilderness. As those Species intermix so much and create combined tribes, biological members of one group are raised in culture of other. ( Similar to mouse and rats Upbringings. ) Each Tribe is more archetype for new Badger family member than literal factions of eras after Man – they create structures of mixed tribes members.

    Wolverines

    Ability bonus: +2 Str
    Species knack: ???

    Otters

    Ability bonus: +2 Dex
    Species knack: Swimming?

    Ratels

    Ability bonus: +2 Con
    Species knack: Hard Body – Resistance to direct damage and Advantage on resists poisons?

    Polecats

    Ability bonus: +2 Wis
    Species knack: ???

    Weasels

    Ability bonus: +2 Int
    Species knack: ???

    Ferrets

    Ability bonus: +2 Cha
    Species knack: ???

    Hamsters

    Rodents cousins to Mice and Rats, they are much more resilient, more rounded and like to collect things. They often become merchants or long travelers, wandering between other Species settlements. Hamsters frequently play role of liaison between Underneath settlements of their Rodent family and roads of other Uplifted.

    Ability bonus: +1 Con, +1 Cha
    Species knack: Big Cheeks – Hamsters can stuff their mouth with small items – or blow out them, to seem bigger. In the second usage, they get Advantage on Intimidate and Persuade rolls for one scene.

    Still needs Species Knacks ideas…

      I don’t personally have a good sense about what makes a good starting Tribal Knack, versus one they should use a level up on. But I feel like a lot of the CC ones are pretty situational and primarily non-combat abilities. So these suggestions may or may not work…


      Wolverines have wide paws, adapted to move full speed in snow; that might make a good Knack for them. Maybe something like…
      “Ignore difficult terrain from snow and ice; natural immunity to the Extreme Cold environmental condition; advantage on physical skill checks (traverse, balance, notice) in snow”.
      … For something that has a narrow application, but sizable bonuses. A more general Knack could just be “Natural Explorer” - especially the Unearthed Arcana version.

      Weasels are especially adept at squirming into burrows and other tight places to attack vulnerable prey. That might get turned into a knack along the lines of…
      “Infiltrate - Weasels double their proficiency bonus on actions to find and exploit an entrance into a cavern or structure - usually finding a tight crawl space, thin wall they can burrow through, or a normally inaccessible gap. They can squeeze through any space roughly as large as their skull (equivalent to a Tiny creature; a domestic feline, or a toaster). In addition, Weasels have Advantage on initiative while in someone else’s burrow / lair.”
      The most distinctive thing about Polecats to me is their “prancing” hunting style, where they confuse their prey with quick jumping movements, then latch on with a bite. Between cutting off escape routes and Never Letting Go, a pair working together can take down a rabbit, like, 5 times their size.
      As a Knack, that might look like…
      “Whenever a polecat makes an Attack of Opportunity against a target leaving their touch range, they may also move up to their full movement speed to keep pace with the target - even if they have already moved this turn.”
      A swimming Knack for Otters might be…
      “Swim speed of 30’; double the time before needing to breathe; and do not take a penalty to actions from being underwater.”
      Otters are also really good at using teamwork to drive off bigger animals. When I used them as antagonists, I gave them Pack Tactics - with higher CR ones getting the hobgoblin one … formation fighting or whatever - bonus damage if next to an ally.

          I would also strongly favor giving playable badgers nature magic as their spell-casting option, in particular a form of “low-magic” more closely tied to herbalism and scavenging, rather than very obvious magic that some Druid spells get into. I used this a little when I expanded on Badgers as antagonists, but I’d happily develop it more for playable uses.

          As a starting point…

          Nature’s Bounty: Badger Magic
          Badgers revere the natural world, rather than study the arcane principles left behind by Man. Their Shamans find mystical boons (and deadly weapons) in the unique qualities of plants, beasts, and monsters, in a kind of scavenging and alchemy - one they do not even consider to be magic.

          Elixirs
          Each time you take this refinement, learn 4 herbalism recipes, as specific spell effects. The character gains a number of elixir uses each level equal to 2+ their Intelligence modifier. Each day, a shaman scavenges herbal components from the wilderness, as part of their Sleep, to refresh their uses of Elixirs - they are assumed to spend about an hour of gathering before or after sleeping for the day. When they Rest, shamans may spend their Stamina Dice to regain a number of uses equal to the number rolled plus their Int bonus, by preparing, mixing, and brewing raw components into usable forms.
          If the shaman is not in the Wilderness when they Sleep, they may not regain uses of Elixirs that day - although they may choose one specific location to cultivate an herb garden, which fulfills this requirement. Shamans may also attempt to purchase useful herbs at a market, as a downtime action, requiring at least Some Coins (in a proper city) or Many Goods (in a tribal village).

          Sample Elixirs:
          • Nettle oil: add +1d8 poison damage to any singular weapon or ammo pack. One use lasts the whole scene.
          • Numbing bandages: grant 1d12+Int temporary HP, per use, to any willing creature
          • Curative potion (varies): each use cures 1 specific status condition; Conditions must be specified each time this elixir is learned.
          • Spore Cloud: 20’ radius, thrown; creatures make a Con Save against your Spell Save DC, or fall asleep
          • Choking Pollen: 10’ radius, thrown; creatures make a Con Save each round against your Spell Save DC, or are incapacitated for that turn. This breaks Concentration. Lasts 3 turns.
          • War Paint: grant advantage to skill checks and saves with 1 specific Ability; 3 targets may benefit for 1 hour per use (same Ability for all targets).
          • Purify Food and Drink: 1 meal for up to 12 people per use, or 1 day of travel rations for up to 5.
          • Spoil: curse the food of a target to cause Sickness when eaten; touch range; Wis Save vs Spell DC to notice; affects all the supplies of an entire group.
          • Chewing Oil: 1 target, touch range, Dex Save; spray the target with a weak acid - not enough to deal damage or break equipment, but causing them to suffer the Cursed condition until they take a full round to remove their armor and/or wash off the burning ichor.
          • Musk Ward: mark an area of up to a 30’ radius; for 24 hours, any living creature must make a Cha save against your spell DC to enter the area. Creatures who succeed may become intent on defeating a rival, becoming hostile even if they ordinarily wouldn’t.
          • Soothing Spray: a living creature must make a Wis save or become Charmed by you for the scene; this also removes any emotional condition such as Scared or Rage. This can affect an entire group, but all targets must be the same species.
          • Adrenaline Chew: a willing creature chews or ingests a wad of stimulating plants, gaining 1 additional Action each turn, for up to 1 minute. The target must maintain concentration, or the effect ends. When the effect ends, they become incapacitated for 1 round.



          Gifts of Conquest
          When the shaman comes across a freshly killed monster (one that hasn’t been scavenged or started decomposing; including one they have just defeated), they may make a Know Nature (Difficulty equal to 15+CR) check to scavenge unique parts, recreating one of the creature’s Knacks which reflect natural abilities. Scavenging may only be attempted once on any single monster: failing the roll ruins any useful parts; succeeding recreates only 1 ability as a Fix. At the Guide’s discretion, the Fix may have multiple uses, up to the number remaining at the end of the encounter; likewise, some fully expended abilities may be unavailable. The Guide may also allow Huge or Gargantuan creatures to be scavenged more than once.
          It requires about 10 minutes to scavenge a single monster, but this may vary by size and CR. It requires one hour to transform the scavenged parts into a usable Fix; only 1 Fix may be crafted at a time (multiple uses do not require multiple Fixes), but the character may still benefit from a Rest.
          Innate or Permanent Knacks (such as Natural Armor or Weapons) may not be scavenged with this Knack, and instead must be gained as equipment or Masterworks. This Knack may justify gaining Masterworks derived from Monster traits (taking at least 1 Sleep to craft), but are only distributed at the Guide's discretion.


          Sample Conquest Fixes:
          • Cobra Venom: As a Bonus Action attach the venom sack of a monster to a piercing weapon; on the next successful attack, it deals an additional 3d8 poison damage and the target must succeed a Con Save or become paralyzed for 1 round.
            • Cockatrice Venom: Con Save vs Petrify
          • Webbing: as per the Web spell
          • Dragon Breath: 60’ line OR 15’ cone, Dex save 4d10 (or 1/2) of one damage type
          • Boiling Blood: when the character is targeted by an attack, use your Reaction to turn into the blow to burst hidden pouches of acid. All enemies within touch range must make a Dex save, or suffer 2d4 acid damage.


          Sample Conquest Masterworks:
          • Scurrying Bone Armor: once per Rest, after being disarmed or suffering damage from an attack, use your Reaction to animate your armor - use 1 a stamina dice to recover stamina, or cause your equipment to crawl back to you.
          • Dragon Hide: gain Resistance to damage types appropriate to the dragon; OR gain advantage on Spell Saves
          • Cloak of Displacement: distorts the senses of foes, making them unable to precisely see where you are, imposing Disadvantage on attack
          • Quill Sling: Thrown; Loading. A glove weapon with monstrous spines growing from it, equivalent to a Masterwork Dagger (+3 attack and damage). By squeezing the muscles beneath, the spines may be released and flung, as a thrown attack. This weapon grants 2 attacks, which may occur on the same turn against the same target, or on different turns, before it must be reloaded (which requires recovering the spines first). The character may load this weapon with ordinary quills (equivalent to a common dagger) as well.

                  Personally, I relate herbalism and elixirs much more closely to alchemy and the processes of discovering properties and how best to unlock them, so I prefer to have it based off of Intelligence (Knowledge Nature). Actually finding the plants is Survival, sure, but that has barely anything to do with the researching, distilling, mixing, and infusing to get a useful effect out of them.
                  Plus I find the idea of a badger ‘scientist’ pretty compelling as a compare and contrast against the Mancers and Artisans: marauders have their intellectuals too, but it looks very different.

                  But, wisdom is more in line with the RAW and the rest of the genre assumptions, such as they are; so as you like it.

                      I had some thoughts to Archetypes as well; wrote these all as Calling equivalents (I like the sound of "Kin and Claim" for their splats), but I could see an argument for Burrow-Guard and Echo-Keeper to be a background equivalent instead.


                      Marauder
                      Badgers who war and raid for the good of the tribe, specialized into battling the Uplifted, to break threats and claim supplies.
                      Stamina Die: d12
                      Primary Abilities: Strength and Constitution
                      Skills: choose 2 of... heal, intimidate, know arcana, know culture, notice, search, steal, survive, traverse
                      Rucksack: a Spear, a melee weapon, choice of another melee weapon or a shield, a set of armor, rations, rope and grappling hook, demolition tools, and a unique war paint
                      Aptitudes: simple weapons, martial weapons, light armor, medium armor, heavy armor, shields

                      Hunter
                      Those who focus on roaming the wilds for food and other resources. As experts at tracking, stealth, and ambush, they are also terrifying assassins.
                      Stamina Die: d10
                      Primary Abilities: Dexterity and Wisdom
                      Skills: choose 2 of... balance, handle animal, know nature, notice, sneak, survive, traverse
                      Rucksack: a short or long bow, choice of a melee weapon, a set of armor, rations, rope, flint and tinder, hunting tools, and a luck charm
                      Aptitudes: simple weapons, martial weapons, light armor, medium armor

                      Shaman
                      Those wise in the gifts of nature, able to identify the special talents of Plants, Beasts, and even fellow Badgers, preserving and capturing those gifts for the strength of the tribe.
                      Stamina Die: d8
                      Primary Abilities: Intelligence and Wisdom
                      Skills: choose 2 of... handle animal, heal, know arcana, know nature, know religion, perform, search, survive, handle animal
                      Rucksack: choice of a weapon, choice of another weapon or a shield, a set of light armor, rations, preservation tools, sacred items and herbs, and a token symbolizing their guiding monster or force
                      Aptitudes: simple weapons, light armor, shield

                      Burrow-Guard
                      Those who raise the young and tend the village. Most of their effort goes into crafting tools and shelter, but they also the primary defenders when the tribe is threatened, and those responsible for finding and making a new burrow when disaster comes.
                      Stamina Die: d12
                      Primary Abilities: Charisma and Constitution
                      Skills: choose 2 of... bluff, heal, intimidate, know culture, notice, persuade, sense motive
                      Rucksack: a weapon, choice of another weapon or a shield, a set of armor, rations, torches, cooking tools, crafting tools, and a handmade token from a loved one
                      Aptitudes: simple weapons, light armor, medium armor, heavy armor, shield

                      Echo-Keeper
                      Those who keep the Tribe's Stories, whether in song, paint, or statue. They are responsible for remembering and retelling the great deeds of the Tribe, and as such, teach the most valuable lessons and lend their wisdom to the most serious matters.
                      Stamina Die: d8
                      Primary Abilities: Charisma and Wisdom
                      Skills: choose 2 of... bluff, know culture, know history, know religion, perform, persuade, sense motive
                      Rucksack: choice of a weapon, a set of armor, rations, choice of an instrument or an artist's toolkit, charcoal and bark strips, funerary tools, and a cultural artifact passed down from their mentor
                      Aptitudes: simple weapons, light armor


                      I see being hardy as a Species trait, so used larger Stamina Dice than these classes might otherwise have. Seemed appropriate.
                      In my personal interpretation of Badgers, the magic and works of the Old Ones are taboo, so Know Arcana is essentially a forbidden skill. Shamans have it because their knowledge of the special powers of monsters and plants sometimes includes magic (even if they don't call it that); Marauders have it because they specialize into recognizing the cursed powers of other Uplifted - and destroying them. But that's just how I like to play it.
                      Also, I imagine that most Badgers multi-class as well. There would probably be strong overlaps between Marauder and Hunter, Marauder and Burrow-Guard, & Shaman and Echo-Keeper - perhaps enough of an overlap that they aren't distinct roles in the characters' / tribe's point of view, but are useful out-of character distinctions for specialization.

                          And here's some proposals for new Backgrounds that fit better in a tribal society:


                          Scavenger
                          Hunters and gatherers for the tribe, who spend most of their time in the wilderness, following marauders, and braving remote places, to get resources for the tribe.
                          • Skills: Survival, Traverse
                          • Rucksack: wilderness garb, many trade goods, camping kit
                          • Knack: Almost everyone seeks you out for favors, sharing rumors of the clan’s needs, dangers in the wild, and interesting salvage. If you can’t find something you’re looking for, you know who to ask for more information.
                          • Spend Fortune ... to declare that you’ve previously traded with an NPC to get them unusual items, and are on friendly terms: perhaps shamans, provisioners, craftspeople, and outlanders (even of other Tribes or Species)


                          Border Guard
                          Scouts, lookouts, and infiltrators. They spend most of their time on the edges of the Tribe's territory, watching for threats, as well as spying on other Uplifted, finding valuables to target, learning defenses, and strategizing.
                          • Skills: Notice, Sneak
                          • Rucksack: warrior’s or wilderness garb, a few trade goods, and scribes tools (bark carving or hide painting)
                          • Knack: The clan hangs on their every word, eager for rumors of danger and opportunity. You can usually secure the hospitality of outlanders and border guards, even of other Tribes and Species, and may exchange rumors and assistance, if mutually beneficial to your clans.
                          • Spend Fortune ... to declare that you know the glorious deeds of a rival or enemy, able to recount their skills or failings, or possibly earning their friendship.


                          Mentor
                          The heart of the Burrow, responsible for training the youth, tending to the ill and injured, and often keeping good relationships between Kin.
                          • Skills: Heal, Persuade
                          • Rucksack: comfortable garb, a few trade goods, and cookware.
                          • Knack: Intimate relations with everyone in the clan, treated with deference and gratitude. Rival clans treat you as an avatar of your tribe - the reason for its success and reputation. Able to secure rest and hospitality at nearly any Burrow (even rivals).
                          • Spend Fortune ... to declare events in a character’s childhood that would embarrass or inspire them into listening to you.


                          Mystic
                          The clan’s wise, master of ceremony and knowledge, keeping traditions, stories, and good advice.
                          • Skills: Know History, Know Religion
                          • Rucksack: mystic garb, some trade goods, and unique ceremonial tool
                          • Knack: Venerated status among the clan; treated with deference and superstition, able even to counsel the chiefs. Rival clans will likewise show fearful respect. When a mystic speaks, everyone takes heed - even if they don't end up following the advice.
                          • Spend Fortune ... to declare a relevant Story in clan lore that emphasizes your point, convincing others to listen.


                          Veteran Raider
                          War is at the core of Badger lifestyle - fighting to take whatever they need from anyone of another Clan. While most Badgers engage in raiding at some point in their lives, characters with this background have survived several successful battles, and have reached a skill where they are trusted to train warriors, lead troops, and even advise the war chief.
                          • Skills: Intimidate, Persuade
                          • Rucksack: warriors garb, a few trade goods, and unique war paint recounting your most glorious deed
                          • Knack: Respected status among warriors, able to recruit minor allies and ask rumors - but not if it risks the clan. Even rivals and enemies respect your prowess in battle, and may be willing to speak on friendly terms.
                          • Spend Fortune ... to declare an NPC has been your ally in a past battle - even if they are currently a rival or enemy - and is willing to show you trust.


                          Provisioner
                          Assistants to the chiefs, whose business it is to manage the Tribe's supplies. Provisioners are responsible for knowing everyone's needs and skills (most especially crafting), protecting and distributing shared resources, and even negotiating with outsiders.
                          • Skills: Persuade, Sense Motive
                          • Rucksack: comfortable garb, some trade goods, and a token of status from the chief.
                          • Knack: Respected but tense relations with everyone in the clan. Working relations with rival provisioners and outlanders. You always know a nearby place to trade for equipment.
                          • Spend Fortune ... to declare that an NPC is your debt from a previous trade.


                          Carver
                          A dedicated craftsperson. While nearly all Badgers know the basic skills to craft and repair all of their daily items, Carvers hold special knowledge and skill, and spend most of their time crafting for their family’s or the clan’s communal use. Carvers may be the most skilled in general handicrafts, might build structures, or may have a near-mystic specialty - such as pottery, tattooing, smelting, or repurposing Masterworks.
                          • Skills: Know Arcana, Survival
                          • Rucksack: comfortable or mystic garb, some trade goods, and a tool kit
                          • Knack: Carvers gain advantage when assessing or appraising tools and structures created by the Uplifted. They can usually figure out how something was made, it’s relative value or quality, and at least get a hint as to how it’s used or secret quality to search for.
                          • Spend Fortune ... to declare that an NPC (even an outsider) recognizes & is impressed with your craft, and is friendly toward you out of respect.

                              (Removed because research)

                              Ok, looked into what I thought I knew, and discovered some new things. Standing by ferrets, weasels and stoats being the same creature, because they pretty much are all part of the same family. Think dog breeds, no real difference apart from looks.

                              Martens are related to wolverines and badgers more than ferrets. Minks, too.

                              Polecats are their own family. Nothing exists in that branch other than polecats. Basically ferrets who think they're skunks.

                              Otters are their own thing, too. And they prefer to play rather than fight. Hard to get angry when you have a nice river/lake/sea to swim in. Oddness: the smaller the body of water they're native to, the bigger they get. River otters? HUGE. Sea otters? Petit. Freaking huge lakes? Asian Small Claw otters are very tiny.

                              So basically there are four major branches of the mustelid family.

                              Also, Minks are a lot bigger than I thought they were.
                              Ferrets, Minks and Martens are major rodent killers. They're more likely to kill rodents than a cat.
                              And weasels particularly love to eat bunny brains. Not so much the rest of the rabbit, specifically.
                              Only Wolverines (not badgers) will fight a bear over a five day old carcass. And probably win.
