                    Well, one of the reasons I mention places like that is that, while steampunk tends to focus on Britain or the Wild West, I think that there's a lot more going on during the century that can be played with. Brazil, for example, depending on what part of the century you set it in, is either the Empire of Brazil or the First Republic. With the former, you have a fairly stable and economically strong constitutional monarchy, viewed as a very clear regional power and a possible growing international one, that works really well with some of the not so dark "steam fantasy" elements of something like Castle Faulkenstein, IMO. If the latter, you have a nominally democratic oligarchy with a growing urban population and industrialization that plays in to a lot of the same classic darker steampunk tropes as Britain or parts of the United States.
                    Also, if I was trying to do a steampunk setting, one of the things I'd be interested in doing was mimicking what Aeon did, where you have these regions like South America, Africa and Australia taking a much larger and different role than they usually do in science fiction settings.

                                                What I understand as “-Punk” is what the Punk movement fight against, that is basically a system where people have less value than what they produce in general. So Steam-Punk is about people having less value than technology, but with steam tech involved, diesel-punk is about people having less value than technology, but with diesel tech, cyberpunk same thing, but with cybernetics. Punk Gothic (the original VtM setting) was about people having no value while the city is big and oppressive due to the religious architecture (some buildings treat business as religion for that end) and presence of monsters.

                                                Castle Falkentein is definitely Steam Fantasy, and have little to no punk aspect on it, as it’s about important people doing important stuff to the population in general. Shadow run is Fantasy-Cyber-Punk as people are marginalized by the corporations and technology and magic.

                                                But I agree that the idea of -punk is usually used to make it sounds rebellious, while it is usually not at all.

                                                    Originally posted by Mateus Luz View Post
                                                    Punk Gothic (the original VtM setting) was about people having no value while the city is big and oppressive due to the religious architecture (some buildings treat business as religion for that end) and presence of monsters.
                                                    Sort of. There was an article in White Wolf Magazine around 25 years ago that actually went to the trouble of explaining what "Gothic Punk" was supposed to mean, and I rather wish something along those lines had been included in the revised edition core books (or even the 20th anniversary ones).
                                                    The Gothic part refers to the Gothic literary genre of the 18th and 19th centuries, as well as its various decedents and spin offs (Southern Gothic, German "Shudder Novels", Pulp Horror, Hammer horror films, etc.), as well as to the late medieval architecture, especially symbols of authority such as castles and cathedrals, which played a big part in it. This combination of age, size, and power over people gives off a mood of oppression and of the protagonist's inherent weakness in the greater scheme of the world. Likewise, the literature has a recurring theme of the protagonist being at a severe disadvantage against various oppressive forces from both the past and the present.
                                                    The Punk part has less to do with the broad (and rather disorganized and widely varied) music scene of the 1970s (or with its stylistic look) and more with a lot of the attitudes that drove it. Namely, Be yourself and fuck anyone who has a problem with it. It's about being a person and an individual rather than just a number or a cog. You - yes, you - are important and your voice is worth being heard, provided you are willing to speak up and accept the consequences that come with not going along with the crowd. It's not a particularly new phenomenon, existing in some way, shape, or form for much of human history. "Punk" is just a convenient label to put on it.
                                                    So when you combine the two, you have a setting where the primary theme is of the weight of the past and the vast impersonal powers of the present are constantly bearing down oppressively on the world and those in it, contrasted with the primary mood of the protagonists rebelling against the status quo of banality, surrender, and ignorance. The dignity and dreams of the individual must constantly strive against the uncaring and corrupt forces that want to crush such things.
                                                    (I actually hold up things like the Harry Potter novels, the Luke Cage series from Netflix, and the movie Frozen as decent examples of Gothic Punk, in which the main characters are all haunted by past events and constrained by present circumstances, but continue to strive against those things in the hope of something better. They just tend to have better weather and lighting than the World of Darkness.)
