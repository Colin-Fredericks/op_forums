        Nothing really comes to mind. Although originally there were mechanical horses, and those aren't really around anymore.

        Originally posted by Scutarii View Post
        Is there a 'magic' expy or is the strangeness just the wildlife of Mars?
        Certain abilities, such as the psychic powers of the adept career, astrology, and the aforementioned fixing are classed by the book as "uncanny." Some things happen that don't have easy explanations, and Martians also have a wide variety of superstitions that we don't say are right or wrong. In general, we as writers treat the beliefs of our Martian characters with respect, even when there's no mechanic that clearly proves or disproves that belief.

        But as far as actual schools of sorcery in the vein of some other fantasy settings, no, there isn't an equivalent.

                    Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                    Given the source material - any plan to address "Earthmen" in the setting?
                    Very unlikely. To Martians, Earth is the blue star of ill-omen.

                    That's not to say there's never been travel between other worlds. Deep in the Labyrinth of Night dwells the witch-queen of the Shadowed Citadel, wanderer or exile from the amber star Venus. And if you spend the time to understand an oleph's sibilant almost-language, it will tell you that it comes from someplace Other, and that its time as a beast of burden on Mars is merely an illusion from which it will eventually return.

                    Originally posted by Rhebeqah View Post
                    Are there the equivalent of Martian gypsies roaming the canals, rumoured to be the heirs to an ancient legacy and with mysterious powers?
                    The canals are one of Mars' great mysteries, a legacy of the vanished First Martians. Their general routes are known, and the flow of water is reliable (if often insufficient) in most places. But no trip up or down the canals is quite the same as any other. The paths seem to shift, sometimes being longer or shorter than before, and sometimes taking you through dark tunnels in which time is... unreliable. Canal sailors and pirates have their own languages and lore, and if you listen to one long enough, you might hear speculation that a great machine toils in the heart of the world, causing the routes to shift.
