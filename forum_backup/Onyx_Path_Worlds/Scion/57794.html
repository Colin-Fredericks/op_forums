    Epic Dexterity

    01-25-2014, 08:58 PM
    So I'm looking at running a Hero-tier Scion game, and I'm trying to learn the ruleset and suchforth, since none of us have played it before. And while I like the idea of Epic Attributes, Epic Dexterity does seem to be the be-all and end-all with regard to physical combat. It affects offensive capability through both being more likely to hit and increasing likely thresholds, thus increasing damage (albeit not as much as Epic Strength would in the same context), and makes you harder to hit, and less likely to be hit hard through decreasing the likely thresholds of incoming attacks. This seems to the point that not putting points into ED seems like a very bad move in terms of character creation, which of course is kind of silly in terms of character concepts and suchforth.

    Is the issue in actual play as bad as it seems it might be? And if so, has anyone come up with a workable houserule solution?

      No, that's quite true. Your observations are accurate. This is a flaw with Dexterity itself, that is only exacerbated by Epic Dexterity.

      Some people have said that they do not have additional attack roll successes add to damage, but that still leaves Dexterity as improving both your offense (hitting at all) and your defense (dodge/parry/block). It's an issue because Strength is Damage, Stamina is Soak but Dexterity is both Accuracy and Defense.

                      Something I've done with the cWOD, which suffers a similar though less pronounced issue with Dexterity in combat, is to allow you to make hand-to-hand attacks with Strength or Dexterity. Strength based attacks don't add extra successes to damage but still benefit from increased damage via high Strength. Dexterity based attacks add extra successes to damage, but without high Strength are less reliable for doing high damage/overcoming tanks.

                      If you're keeping to a Hero level game, I can see this working fairly well with Scion (I'm not sure it really works if you keep to the escalating numbers of higher level Scion). Dex is still superior if you're just going to invest in one physical Attribute for combat, but if you're going to be sinking points into multiple combat Attributes, it makes Strength/Stamina builds more appealing because you can tank and still reliably score hits on higher DV targets.

                                                  Basically, the extra successes impact on damage is generally not enough to deal with the increased soak.
