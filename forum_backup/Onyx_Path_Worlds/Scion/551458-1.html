        There's good reason everyone's moved away from changing it on the fly (it alters dice probabilities too much to adjudicate wisely, messes with success counting, and requires a decent eyeball) but this isn't on the fly - they're static depending on Tier, which remains (relatively) constant in play. It worked well in Exalted and Mummy, and it works fine here.
        It was one of the things that players always moaned about at my table whenever we played Exalted, so we stayed away from Sidereals for exactly those reasons. But as long as the target numbers are static throughout an entire tier it should be stable enough that players will get used to it. It will require adjusting dice scripts every tier (for those that prefer to use phone Apps, or other dice rolling aids) but that is infrequent enough that it just might work. I'm definitely willing to give it a try!

                                                So Teaser #3 is out.

                                                The first thing I get out of initiative is that you can't be good at anything, unless you have a large dice pool in everything. That's kind of pushing things toward 'everyone is mediocre' level, especially with comments (from RPG.net readers) about different combat actions requiring random dice pools.

                                                I especially hate that when you have focus (ie. you are taking an action) there is no 'just fail', you will botch. That's making it a damn system of failure, not success. Like back when 1s used to subtract from any successes and you didn't succeed unless you rolled more successes than 1s.

                                                Looking back on Tiers, I think the thing I hate about how it is currently set up is that it tends to give a median maximum that is higher (by the difficulty target being easier) but that if you have a bout of bad luck, a lower tier can actually do just as good. The best either of them can do is all 10s, but that's only a difference in the maximum success of maybe 2 successes.

                                                It's only a tendency to do better, but you can't actually have a truly 'greater' success.
