    Originally posted by Corax View Post
    You seem to have given the Anatolian gods a lot of thought, do you have any plans on doing a write up on them? How would you link them (if at all) to the Illiad's Troy and the Theoi?
    The Thousand Gods of Hatti are my current pet project, twenty five pages deep at the moment. I have a whole section set aside to talk about Troy even! However, I actually haven't written that section yet, so I can only talk about my brief knowledge on Troy. It was on the very edge of the Empire, and I don't think it was kept for a very long time. The Thousand Gods of Hatti are still probably mildly offended that the Theoi used it as a playground for a decade though.

    With the Theoi themselves, both Pantheons are quite interesting. Both are Imperial Pantheons, focused on expansionism. The Thousand Gods of Hatti have subsumed numerous Deities of conquered people into themselves, with mass State headed synchronization projects, similarly to what happens with the Gauls and Etruscans, but on a far larger scale. However, the Hittite Empire ends in the same period as the Greek Dark Ages kicking off, so there is not a lot of well detailed cultural contact with the Greeks as much as we get with the Egyptians and Mesopotamians.

    However, there are some features that the Pantheons will find similar. Zeus battling Typhon and Tarhun (I am just using the Hittite name rather than listing all of them, there's several) fighting Illuyanka is a big similar feature the Pantheons might find a connection through. However, Typhon and Illuyanka are very different, one a giant amalgem of animals in a divine form, and the other a... pretty much reasonable person (for Hittite Divinities) who happens to be a giant snake.

    The biggest point of issue would, as almost always, be the Greeks being ethnocentrist dicks about it. They would throw around the term barbarian a few times, possibly boast about sacking Troy even though half of them were fighting to stop that, and they were on orders to quit it. The Thousand Gods of Hatti are really quick to piss off, so things might get out of hand. However, the Thousand Gods of Hatti are pretty accepting, and welcoming to foreign ideas, so if another Pantheon isn't openly hostile to them, there would probably be a good relationship.

    Oh, and the Greeks would really not like the Hittite Titan situation. Hittite Myth is essentially just two divine dynasties arguing over who should get to be King of Heaven with everyone else dragged along for the ride. The losers just live in residence with the Underworld Goddess, not a Tartarus situation, just hanging out. Both sides have Gods, Titans, and what would count as Primordials in the Greek mindset on them. It's a bit of a mess, and while just as nicely divided as the Greeks, people are not sticking in their 'God' / 'Titan' camps. Both sides are made of a collection of both.

    The Triumuri are most certainly Gods though, far too closely bound. Exceptionally powerful though, even in a Pantheon of power houses.

            Originally posted by Havoc View Post
            By the way, seizing the opportunity, how exactly do the Titans work? I see they are conceptually different than the ones from the books, but are they mechanically different too? Thanks in advance!
            Watcher went into great detail about the Titans, and we run them pretty similar to each other. The important thing is that we do not see the Titans as "Evil". Instead, we use the term Titan to refer to entities that are so distant or aloof about humanity that they do not consider our feelings. Usually this has nothing to do with malice towards us, but because we are beneath their notice. Sometimes these beings might actually take notice, but their idea of helping would be actively harmful to us.

            Consider Oceanus, the primordial embodiment of the ocean itself. We know that he invites Hera over for some small family gatherings now and then, so he seems sociable on some level. But on a day to day basis he probably does not even think about all the humans who drown at sea or wreck their ships. Even reaching his home (where he is both the ocean itself and someone who can apparently be visited) might be exceptionally lethal simply because of what he is.

            Now, some Titans have personal preferences and goals that are downright murderous to humanity. The infamous Apep would love nothing more than to devour the sun, which is certain to be a no good, lousy, pretty bad thing for humanity. If someone ever put Kagutsuchi back together, he would immediately start turning the rest of the world into cinders, or burn his way into Takamagahara to confront his father.

            But being a Titan doesn't make you Evil. In fact, in a game about so many different cultural beliefs from across the world, what counts as Evil changes from one pantheon to the next.

                        Anyway, thank you beforehand, happy holidays!
                        Ah right, the Scent Knacks. They are left over from RAW and Gotham by Night. In our settings, you tend to not need divine senses to notice someone's a Scion. The burning spear, owl head, armor made of obsidian animal hides, those tend to give it away. Theoretically they could be used to help pierce disguises, but then there's the scent disguising knacks, and it becomes a manufactured arms race pretty much.

                        Anyways, right. I have axed Greater Titans for the most part. And Titanrealms as well. I found Greater Titans unnecessary, and were altering source mythology without adding any fun to the game, so I cut them. The Greater Titan system also tended to work best with the Titanic Templates, and the general 'union' of a specific Purview's Titans against a specific Pantheon. Since I cut both of these since they were manufactured relations that, once again, did not add fun, and deviated, removing Greater Titans worked.

                        Titanrealms were neat, but personally I found they... just did not fit in how I was running things. Firstly, I am a crazy person who is unnecessarily accurate, and most of the Titans are imprisoned in very specific locations that are not Titanrealms. So, for example, Atlas is not imprisoned in a Titanrealm. Izanami is imprisoned in an Underworld. Lir is just crying on a rock somewhere (or in the Land-Under-Wave, which would be a Terra Incognita). Furthermore, their function as locations for God level adventures just did not click with me. Overworlds already worked for that, and when Gods or Titans throw down outside of an Overworld, Titanrealms gave a collateral damage free zone to do so in. Typhon and Zeus could fight without leveling a fourth of Greece. It just didn't work for the way I run things.

                        So, yes. Scent the Titanic doesn't actually work like that in my games. I actually have never had someone take it, you tend to know the giant man with one eye, one hand, and one foot is a Fomori, not needing a Knack to let you know they're connected to Balor. If someone wanted to use it, I would suggest having Scent the Titanic give hints as to what Titan a Titanspawn is connected with / related to. So, Cerberus would smell like Typhon and Echidna, both of whom would have unique scents and sounds. (Probably the smell of animals, the mewling of baby creatures, and the distant roars of every animal in creation as a mixture of both)

                                No Masquerade, none at all. Normally our games start with an 'unintentional Masquerade' in effect just to transition through the return of the Gods though. One of the ways I have explained it is the Gods never really intended to withdraw, but by imprisoning, physically or otherwise, a bunch of Titans, Legend was slightly detached from the world. Over time, this resulted in, to use the Greek term, an Age of Iron. To represent this, we do something like, "The Highest Legend accessible while on Earth is 2" so all Gods are forced to assume an Avatar with a Legend rating that low when on Earth. When the seals holding the imprisoned Titans start to break (sometimes caused by a God who wants to be able to flex their might on earth, or Scions messing up, or just time passing) this Legend cap bumps up as Myth starts returning to the world.

                                The Gods have stayed away from the world, resulting in this 'unintentional masquerade' (which is not enforced by anyone. The world just forgot Myth and Legend with the sealing of the Titans) for various reasons. The Tuatha are mostly all dead, and more interested in their Otherworlds. The Anunna stopped paying attention to the mud-flesh slave species they created for only a few millennium.
