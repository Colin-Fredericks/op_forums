    Opinions on a ST

    12-07-2015, 04:35 PM
    So rp group gets together every two weeks to play Scion with a friend of mine being the story teller. According to him, he's run Scion campaigns before with much success, however I'm having my doubts. I feel like things are being forced on us a bit much, and we're also being, for lack of a better term, bullshitted on the regular. Examples below:

    - During a scene in a bar we are confronted by Sisyphus who has taken up a position as bartender. Our scion of Hades has orders to take him back to Tartarus as well as two others. The storyteller keeps his identity a surprise even from the scion of Hades. That aside, he then proceeds to just auto-use knacks at us instead of rolling for them and giving us the opportunity to use willpower points in order to convince us all as a group to drink wine made from the "Grapes of Wrath" to push the story along quicker.

    - Next scene, we're fighting an "Amazon scion of Aeries" who is demigod level (we are all just starting as heroes) and proceeds to not only use a guard, attack, and then a knack to "Hurl to Horizon" my character over a warehouse building (My character is a scion of Sun Wukong, I also for some house rule reason have Ru Yi Jin Gu Bang at hero level and legend 3) Taking three actions in one tick. Previously when I tried to attack her, her shield canceled out my auto-successes from epic strength. The storyteller also, right in front of me, flipped a dice roll for his amazon from a 1 to a 7 when moving it "close for inspection".

    To me, this feels like I have a storyteller who is a load of rubbish (putting it nicely). I've talked to him twice about the rules regarding some of our experiences, to which he always seems to have some answer that is later proven wrong. As for the willpower point problem, when I brought that up and explained how it works and that it would be nice to be able to play the characters more in npc interactions; the response was "Eh, no matter, I'll just force it next time".

    If anyone has suggestion on what I could do short of just discontinuing to show up, I am very open to suggestions. Thank you!

      You got quite a problem at hands then. as it seems talking to him does not help there. although did only YOU talk? or did also the others talk with the gm? (else it could be that he thinks only one has a problem).

      I've seen similar problems time and again raise up. Gms suddenly ruling that some powers don't work or work differently than they should because they are not flexible enough to properly let their setting respond to what the players are doing (thus they can't adapt). In other cases its more a "my char" problem as the gm for some reason or another feels that he likes a specific npc so much that he bends rules for it to get the spotlight (seen it most often though in another system........where the gm used a group of npcs that had a few hundred more xp than the players and showed up to "rescue their behinds".......without even need for that though).

      My personal experience from that his.......make sure that he gets it that its not only your problem but the groups as a whole, and if that doesn't work leave it be......and tell him that you can't play with that.......my personal experience there is......in 80% of the cases they won'T change a thing even if they say they will.

              Well not being able to recognize the bartender can be explainable. I mean would you be able to recognize a Greek man from thousands of years ago if you've never met them? But the auto knack thing is not right. Hell the book actually encourages ST's to give the edge to the players, not the other way around. As for canceling out auto successes. I don't know how to respond to that. How do you cancel something that automatically succeeds?


              Open conflict with a Demigod, however, is a major red flag. Heroes are powerful, sure, but they're still mostly vulnerable to mortal threats. Demigods could swat mortals like flies, barring the risk of a Fatebind. In other words, the ST put you in a fight you had no hope of winning because the plot says so!

                  Sometimes your hero has to lose in order to advance the story. I do that, too and I've only heard minor complaints about it, usually from players who never played before. They usually redact their opinion later on after then succeeding, feeling that it adds much to the game.

                  That being said, however, your SL doesn't sound like someone whose philosophy I could get behind. If you want to cheat for your characters, Scion in its "pure" form makes this so easy already that he shouldn't need to blatantly cheat. I'll have to take up the cudgels for him insofar, however, as that Sisyphus is a Demigod and will stomp Hero-Level Scions in Manipulation without any effort whatsoever, eliminating the need to actually roll.

                  If you find that your SL is, for lack of a better word, bullshitting you, theres always confronting him. Talk to the other players about this and have sort of an intervention with hin, explaining why you don't think this kind of storytelling is to your liking. A Storyteller isn't meant to be, in fact, your enemy. He is meant to be someone who plays WITH you, but is responsible for a lot more things and characters. This can mean, of course, that he has to be harsh towards you, but at the end of the day, you play for fun. Which brings me to what I'd do if I was in your shoes and talking to my SL didn't bear the fruits I hoped for: Then its time to quit. There is no point in forcing yourself to play a game you don't enjoy.

STs do fudge rolls to make the story work, yes, but you don't blatantly change dice in front of players so that your NPC can win a fight that was stacked in their favour to begin with; that's BS. Honestly, if the ST is of the "Me vs. Them" school of GM'ing, and you've tried talking without success, there isn't much you can do beyond either a] accepting that this is the only game in town, or b] quitting. Sorry!
