            95) The Gods have always known that somebody freed the Titans from their prison, but were never able to figure out who. Now they know the answer to that great mystery. Fate itself kicked in the door and broke the Titan's shackles. Fate never intended for the Gods to rule eternally from their thrones in the heavens and now that they are considered obsolete in the modern world, it is time for a new generation to take over. Just as the Titans rebelled against their creators and the Gods against the Titans, so too are the Scions destined to rebel against the Gods. A new age is dawning and the Gods are now faced with an assault on two fronts!

            Story: By Fate's decree, the Scions are to replace the Gods as the overseers of a new age of human civilization. While the Gods are preoccupied diverting forces to the war effort, the Scions launch a coup against their pantheon, attempting to seize the Overworld thrones for themselves. The story is probably best played with a Band who are all from the same pantheon to make things easier.

                96) When humans die and are given the proper funerary rites their souls are carried to the appropriate afterlife to be judged, sorted, and ultimately kept in trust. A displaced soul results when the proper rites (regardless of culture) are not performed and it will either linger forever as a sad ghost, or become corrupted by titanic influence and rage becoming a specter. Since the old gods fell out of favor and were replaced by modular humanistic religions, no valid examples of such rites have been performed, so where are all the dead going? The world is not awash in ghosts and specters, but the underworlds of various pantheons haven’t seen much in the way of new arrivals. You’ve been tasked to track down where the flood of modern souls is going, and report back to your pantheon’s resident psychopomp.

                            98) The old Gods abandoned the World because they wanted to escape the possibility of mortals transforming them beyond recognition. Yet their plan may have just backfired tremendously. Without the presence of the Gods to set the story straight, their legends began to get twisted with the retelling through the ages. The modern age presents the worst of the lot, depicting Hades to be the Greek devil, Zeus is some wise and dignified ruler, while Hera is his evil queen. Loki is mistaken to be Thor's brother and the child of a Frost Giant, while Thor is depicted as being some blond haired, blue eyed heart throb. These are just some of the more popular examples!

                            The Gods stopped spreading their Legend, and so the mortals continued to do it for them, putting their own spin on things. If the Gods don't figure out a way to resolve this issue, they may end up falling victim to the very threat they sought to avoid. But how do they fix this mess without revealing themselves to the entire world and setting off a wide scale pandemonium?
