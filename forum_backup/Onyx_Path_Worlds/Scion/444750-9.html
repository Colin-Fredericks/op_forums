                            Well the relation between the Aos Si and the Tuatha De is pretty explicitly one of being the same people. After the Second Battle of Mag Tuired, the Tuatha agreed to leave Ireland to the Milesians. Those of the Tuatha that stayed agreed to move underground. They became the Daoine Sidh or Aos Si.

                            It's not like there aren't a ton of complications, like why is Finnvara the King of the Aos Si when it sure as hell looks like Aengus Og was still hanging around at Bru na Boinne and not having fun back in Tir na Nog? It's hard to see how the son of the Dadga (once king, royal enough that marrying his daughter was enough to make a Fomorian king of the Tuatha De, etc.), who was something complicated with Midhir, one of the kings of the Four Quarters, etc would cede pride of place to someone else (especially since we are talking about Aengus Og here.... subtlety and humility are definitely not his style).

                            I think trying to assemble a mythology of Britain/Prydain/Lloegr/Albion is going to be a tough one. Especially if you want it to be separate from Welsh mythology. Welsh myths include lots of things that happened as far north as Scotland, as far East as London). Cornwall and Brittany as well. When Romanized Britain fell, the Britons that could, fell back to Wales (which may or may no have ever been Romanized... there are forts, but Claudius and Vespasian didn't think of it as being a part of the Empire), so a lot of what would have been the "myths" of further East would have been integrated.

                            There are bits of Celtic mythology scattered here and there, but a lot of them have some analogue in Welsh legends and folk tales. It would be like, if when Scotland was overrun by the Irish, the Picts had fled to the Orkneys and established themselves there. Then later on, we come along and try to separate out "Orkney-ish mythology" from "Pictish mythology". They may not be a different enough of a thing for that to work (especially in the context of a game that has lumped together Mycenaean, Classical Greek and Roman mythology... mantles aside).

                                    Maman Brigitte is from the Ghede Nanchon, so her relation to the Yoruba pantheon is pretty attenuated anyway. Vide the Kongo Nanchon. Where do Papa Ghede, Mamman Brigitte, the Baron(s), Nibo, Masaka, Oussou, Babaco, Doubye, Ti Malis, Lino, etc, come from? It's a REALLY good question)
                                    We have to remember than Afro-American religons are syncretic and adopted or assimilated figures from different cultures, African, European, Amerindian, and perhaps even Indian in the cases of some Afro-Caribbean religions.                                         .

                                        Most, if not all, polytheistic religions are syncretic, which is problematic for a game that is trying to hit the "target" of a core mythology to represent in game terms that are accessible to someone who picks up the books because the idea of playing a mythologically-based game where you GET TO PLAY THE GODS (eventually). All the comparative mythology, comparative modern religion studies, are informative and interesting, but you finally have to pin down a version of each deity that you are going to detail out, whether you are the game-designer or running a game for you and your friends. It's really really hard, if not impossible to get an accurate read on who "Hecate" would be when she's at home (and your mom) if you try to go with every different myth about her, all the evidence of her cult that we have records of, supplemented by what archaeologists have ground-truthed. That's effectively dozens of different people, many of whom are mutually exclusive.

                                        As much as one can look at the Loa through the lens of the Orisha or the Orisha through the lens of the Loa, the fact is that they AREN'T the same thing. Parts of the Orisha line up with parts of the Loa (and with those two, it's very nice that the Loa are sub-divided into the Nanchons, which pretty much map out that relationship). If you try to shoehorn the non-Orisha Nanchons into the Orisha, you've made a hard row to how for yourself. Maman Brigitte and pretty much ALL of the Ghede Nanchon fit into that category. There are a few distaff Orisha who are a little Ghede-like, but even they don't match up very well. Maybe as parents? Maybe the Ghede are Scions of the few appropriate Orisha that are associated pretty much exclusively with the New World? I could see some very grim (from "laugh in the face of death" to downright monstrous) figures arising from the awful conditions associated with the development of voudoun and other Caribbean religions.

                                        I think the whole idea of "mantles" is trying to deal with aspects of this situation. Whether or not they will work remains to be seen. Particularly in the context of moving the game away from a more exclusively "gods" part of mythology to a more "Urban Fantasy" centered around mythology.
