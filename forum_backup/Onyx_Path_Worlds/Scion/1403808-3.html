        So, prejudice is a big theme in Werewolf:the Apocalypse. While the Garou are technically, technically counting under the label of a prejudiced/oppressed minority, throughout their history they have largely been the oppressors. The Garou hate "the other", they despise it, and their reasons as to why change from person to person. Without that hatred of everything that isn't them, there wouldn't be the Impergium, the War of Rage, the extermination of the Bunyip, or even the entire existence of the Red Talons tribe. There are of course some exceptions, but they're not the rule. The Garou, as a whole and in individual tribes, are hated in turn by a ton of people, and for good reason. This hatred of each other in the metaplot is why the Garou are on the brink of total failure, because they couldn't get their heads out of their asses fast enough to prevent the world's degradation.

        Sorry for the rambling, I just emptied my mind on the subject. As to your earlier point, while removing all other references is possible, I'm worried that it wouldn't be recognizable as Werewolf. Or at least, Werewolf: the Apocalypse. I'm fine with changing the First Nation tribes Totems, or even switching which tribe is dead or not, but removing all connections would mean:
        • Pegasus is literally just a flying horse instead of the Pegasus. Now, granted, the basic story structure that White Wolf made of the myth, a wild mythical creature tamed by a hero aided by a foreign power, who after helping the hero gets castrated, and then gets his revenge by tossing the hero into the ocean, is still serviceable, but it does lose just a touch of flavor. Also, why would the Black Furies be in Greece if they weren't going to have interactions with the Greek Gods? Do they live somewhere else?
        • The Fianna are very, very Irish, and removing them from Ireland would take away the entire basis for their culture, even if it is based on a stereotype.
        • The Silent Striders would not exist in their current form at all. If they can't be expulsed from Egypt because that would involve another pantheon, then how did they become wanderers?
        • Stargazers are no longer Buddhist, even if it was a strange variant of it.
        There's obviously a lot of minor details that I skipped over, like Ratatoskr no longer being friendly with the Get, the Bunyip with the mythology of the Australian Aborigines, etc. Point is, by removing all connections, you take away a large portion of each tribe's character. And with enough of that character gone, we no longer have Werewolf: the Apocalypse, we have a generic "spirit" pantheon that just so happens to have werewolves in it. The objective is to convert WtA, not to create an entire pantheon wholesale like it's DnD.

            Not to be blunt but maybe if these contradictions are so prominent, conversion just isn't going to work?

            Just saying that Scion has basically been built on the assumption that while the pantheons aren't perfect, we're at least not going to be told that they were all monsters in ways that aren't already in their texts.

            This is kind of a problem if we try to cross it with a cosmology like Werewolf the Apocalypse that quite frankly never really focused on mythological accuracy but insisted on making connections to myths anyway. You can make your own decisions on whether or not that's a problem for Werewolf as a setting, but it definitely becomes a problem when a new fictional cosmology starts making judgments on real world mythologies (many of which are still practiced to this day)

            And yeah, quite frankly, Scion doesn't really need Werewolf the Apocalypse. Werewolves already exist in Scion, in the forms of all the mythologies that already featured them. If you want to play a Werewolf in Scion then play one of them. If you want Werewolf the Apocalypse's totems, then play Werewolf.

                    I mean yeah, ultimately you run whatever game you want, and make up whatever new stuff you want as well.

                    I think the issue is more that Scion isn't just a game about fictional entities, its a game that involves real cultural practices. You can do whatever you want with your own games, but saying stuff that's actively wrong about someone else's culture and expecting no push-back or criticism because it's just a game is another matter.

                    A lot of it depends on context. Like saying Athena's a tool of the Patriarchy's one thing or that Fenris is unfairly imprisoned by the Aesir is one thing. People from the time and Historians might disagree with you, but the Theoi and Aesir aren't widely followed in the modern day aside from fairly modern pagan revivals. But going around claiming the Yazata or the Deva (both of whom have active faiths around them that have experiences with persecution and colonization) are violent fanatics starts to get iffy. This is even more extreme when you get to actively appropriating figures from First Nations myths like Wendigo and Uktena.

                    Like by all means make new stuff, it's messing around with old stuff where things become a problem.
