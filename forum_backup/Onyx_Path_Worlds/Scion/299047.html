    Alternate Roman PSP: Jurisprudentia

    11-01-2014, 01:22 PM
    Would anyone be interested in helping with an alternative Roman PSP? The overarching concept is civil law, contracts and politics; Roman religion is often thought of by scholars as distinctively legalistic and the Romans themselves were famous for their law code and (particularly during the Republic) as being litigious and respectful of the rule of law.

    So far I have a names and a general idea of what each Boon could be capable, just not mechanics:

    * Patria Potestas - Under Roman law, children were under the authority of their father until formally emancipated. If adult children were not emancipated, this power of life & death could extend to the children's children. This Boon would somehow show the strength of that bond in a useful way. One idea is that, at the cost of some extra legend, the Scion is able to reduce the probability of Fatebinding for a using a Legendary Deed, since the Scion's act becomes part of the legend of the whole family rather than his or her individual Legend.
    As a side effect of this Boon, any children the Scion has at Hero or Demigod level are treated as direct children of the Scion's divine parent, meaning that as long as a Visitation occurs every other generation a family can retain their divine heritage through any number of generations - explaining why Julius Caesar and Augustus were able to become gods despite the distance of their inheritance from Venus.

    ** Do ut Des - This is both a religious and legal term, so it's perfect for this PSP. Basically, it means that you give something in exchange for something else, whether your agreement is with another mortal or a god. My idea would be that the Scion invests a number of points of willpower in an agreement (based on the difficulty of the obligation - the chart with "Demand a Labor" can serve as a model). If both sides hold up their end of the bargain, the Scion gains those points back with interest and perhaps the other party gains a similar benefit. If either side breaks the deal, both suffer a cost, and the guilty party hopes the other doesn't have Vengeance.
    Penalizing both for a transgression is a way to ensure that this doesn't eliminate the Justice Boon that enforces contracts: instead uses of this Boon will be looking for someone with Justice to provide extra insurance against default.

    *** Sacer - This is a term which refers to an object becoming the property of a god; at this point the Scion can render his own property sacred, although it isn't clear what effect this might have mechanically.
    Since everything a non-emancipated Scion owns is actually the property of their divine parent, objects would actually be sacred to the Scion's father or mother until the Scion reaches God level.

    **** Devotio - This was a ritual in which Romans would put themselves into such danger they were virtually certain to be killed (usually running into the ranks of an opposing army), dedicating the lives of both themselves and their enemies to the gods.
    In game terms, it might reduce the DV of both the Scion and her or his enemies by the same cripplingly large number, or it might impose another equally life-threatening penalty on both. Romans who survived would offer expiatory sacrifices in exchange for their lives - Roman Scions might have to do the same before the penalty vanishes.
    This Boon is least linked to the concept of law, but it does represent another agreement with the gods ("take my enemies and you can have me").

    ***** Sacrosantus - This is quality possessed by the ancient Tribunes, that anyone who attacked them became sacer and forfeited their lives to a god.
    As a Demigod, the Scion has graduated to be a sort of magistrate of the cosmic city, and those who attack her or him likewise suffer some sort of terrible penalty. Although Legendary creatures are able to resist and eventually recover, mere mortals generally don't.

    ***** * Evocatio - Another legal/religious term, this could either be the process of calling witnesses to a trial, or the ritual to invite foreign gods to abandon their native cities to become protectors of Rome.
    As a Boon, it would allow Scions to call up the native Legendary beings in a particular area, and at least give them a chance to state their case (and use their Social knacks) before being attacked.

    ***** ** Quis Nomine - Romans would make sure the gods heard them by listing off all sorts of names used by the god to which they were praying, sometimes finishing with something along the lines of 'or whatever name you wish to be called'.
    This boon would elaborate on that idea by granting the ability to alternate between a number of different names based on the Scion's legend score. Mystery and some other abilities which specify a specific target would simply not work if the user was targeting a name not currently in use.

    ***** *** Genius loci - This boon would allow the Scion establish ownership of an area becoming the tutelary deity of a region. I'm not sure what this would entail.

    ***** **** Interpretatio Romana - This ability would allow the loan of one of the Scion's names to someone else, allowing that other individual to temporarily be treated as a Roman god. The drawback would be that both the Scion and the name's user would be effected by any Fatebonds incurred while the loan is active. Eventually, this could lead to two gods having so many associations in common as to be indistinguishable

    ***** ***** Pax deorum/Ira Deorum - This would allow the Scion to declare either war or peace between the mortal and divine worlds, causing positive or negative effects over an empire wide area.
    The Ira Deorum option would cause strange prodigies out of Livy and property damage, both appropriate to the APPs the Scion is specialized in so that mortals with the Occult skill can figure out which god has been angered.
    Not sure whether it should be possible to target rival pantheons/Titanrealms.

    Not the daimon, just a namesake.
