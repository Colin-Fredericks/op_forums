                                                            The game isn't all about creativity. There really isn't a game out there about that. I did play one once where the players essentially created a universe from the ground up as the gods. That was about as purely creative as I've seen it ever get. Even then it was constrained with concepts like "gods", "creation" & "universe".

                                                            This game is about a specific setting with a specific type of character. The setting is a world where myths, legends and/or folklore are factually true. It's the core conceit from which all others follow. The following conceit is that the player characters are connected (almost always genetically) with the figures of said myths, legends or folklore. Coming up with stories about the gods (or whatever Paul Bunyan is) is all well and good. Adding to or embroidering the story. But coming up with everything from the ground up and you start loosing the point of this game vis-a-vis others.

                                                            Your slacker-jack son of Paul Bunyan with his faux lumberjack chique, his man bun and his blue bunny is a perfect example of a Scion Tall Tales character. He riffs off the lore of his father. You've set up room for conflict and for resolution. Everything makes sense and is immediately reconizable.

                                                            If I was to make up a Scion Tall Tales character who was a sous chef at a high end bistro, who is the son of Sloppy Jane the Best-est School Cafeteria Lunch Lady.... well, it's kinda funny, but even if I add in that he has a small carton of milk that never empties and has prophetic pictures of the next victim the band will save, or a super special hair net that he absolutely HATES, but, when worn makes him so intimidating that a diner will be intimidating anything he serves them, this dude isn't really on the level of Paul Bunyan's son and never will be. In fact, he is closer to the "joke Scions" from the April Fool's Day Scion supplement.

                                                            That character is a little extreme, but it's a sort of reverse "proof of context". A character that's generated out a set of legends that are created out of whole cloth is going to lack resonance with the game being played. Even if a player is willing to go balls to the wall and write up a myth cycle for their fictive legendary parent, it's still going to feel pretty hollow compared to the slackerjack or the weatherman son of Pecos Bill, the civil rights lawyer daughter of Brer Rabbit, Mike Fink's Bayou-based Army Corps of Engineer son, or even the fashionista daughter of Betsy Ross (and the last one is pretty weak as it is, considering Betsy's legend and the history involved...)
