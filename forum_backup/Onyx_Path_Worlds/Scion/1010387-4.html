    EPISODE 11: Ninja High School (Continued)

    Aftermath: Jaeda’s Choice

    United Martial Arts burns to the ground. No bodies are found, but more than a dozen students and other people around Avalon and Etna mysteriously disappear. Burt Broder and his two cronies disappear as well. At a guess, Dark Master Chiba found them and made them shinobi, too, but the three bullies went off on their own to revenge themselves on Landon. Jack likely killed two of them, and the third likely died in the conflagration at United Martial Arts.

    The Scions come out of the battle with three Trophy shuriken, from the two shinobi that Jack killed and from Dark Master Chiba (they couldn’t collect any others during the fight because they did not actually kill anyone else at the dojo: All the other shinobi either ran away or were merely knocked out and died in the fire.) These shuriken deal double base damage against titanspawn. The Scions also got Chiba’s mask, which is a Relic for channeling the War Purview. Unfortunately, Gary lost the Black Bodhisattva’s mask during the fight (he was more interested in keeping hold of his axe).

    Earlier, Gus had promised to help Jaeda find another classic muscle car. Jaeda’s first thought was another Roadrunner, but she decides that she’d learned all she could from restoring one already; she should move on to some other car. Whatever she gets, she plans to hang the shuriken from Dark Master Chiba from the rear view mirror as a personal trophy.

    Gus also promised Jaeda a night of incredible, superhuman sex as consolation for losing her car. Here, however, Jaeda faces another choice. Gary joins the Scions in their post-battle wind-down party. (Are they going to say no, when he fought so bravely and effectively? The Scions are all pretty sure that without him, the shinobi, Chiba and Black Bodhisattva would have killed at least one of them.) When Gary notices Gus paying that sort of attention to Jaeda, he comes on to her too and promises her the best night of her life.

    Way back in the first episode, Gary had expressed his opposition to giants dating Scions, or at least to his sister dating Scions, but:

    * Gary’s drunk (well, as drunk as someone with two dots of Epic Stamina can be);

    * Jaeda’s not only incredibly hot, she appreciates fine automotive engineering;

    * Gary’s incredibly competitive, especially with Gus. (An actual Fatebinding of rivalry between them.) And…

    * Holly detected, with Unlidded Eye still running, that Gary came out of the battle with his Legend subtly altered. It’s more like his sister Reese’s now. Make of that what you will.

    Jaeda finds it difficult to choose between these two gorgeous young Nordic supermen. She fears the Scions are dispersing into factions, and sees sex with Gus as a way to keep a group together (or at least keep Gus with her). OTOH, Gary is also strong, brave, fought on her behalf and shares her automotive interests. Quite apart from physical attraction, she’d like to get to know him better. Anyone who loves classic muscle cars can’t be all bad, right?

    Gus, however, spots Jaeda’s inner conflict. While Gary visits the bathroom to recycle some of the beer, Gus assures Jaeda that he won’t think less of her if she boinks Gary this night, instead of him, and backs off.

    So, Jaeda spends the next several hours with Gary, first in The Car and then in a motel room. And the giant didn’t lie. (In fact, he channeled his Rapacity Dark Virtue and spent a Legend for a Legendary Deed; I presume Jaeda contributed through Perfect Partner.) Morning finds them snuggling in bed (under a sheet that miraculously survived to preserve Scion High’s suitable-for-network-TV status), still awake but extraordinarily relaxed and Talking Car. Jaeda’s next car will have to be a fixer-upper, but Gary has promised his help in repairing it.

    Addendum: Jack’s New Sword

    Using the shinobi’s sword agains them worked so well that Jack decides he wants a sword of his own. (Strangely, he doesn’t want to use the perfectly serviceable relic Sword of Xibalba that would doom the World if it fell into enemy hands.) Real, battlefield-quality swords are not that easy to obtain, though. Jack turns to Jaeda. He knows Holly could obtain the sword for him, but she would very pointedly ask nothing in return, and Jack’s pride won’t stand for that.

    Jaeda phones some of her old gang contacts in Los Angeles. She finds someone who can supply a katana. (Some of those gang-bangers like that sort of thing.) They’ll bring it in two days. It will cost $1000, and that’s a discount for Jaeda’s sake.

    When he hears the cost, Jack starts dithering and wondering if they’d accept payment by installments. Jaeda knows that they don’t. She firmly says, “It’s a deal” and leaves the problem to Jack. Who despite his vast intelligence cannot think of a more-or-less legal way to make $1000 in less than two days. Gritting his teeth, he turns to Holly. Who gives him the money without hesitation and, as Jack feared, asks nothing in return.

                                    And now a bit of background I wrote up for the players after the Scions' meeting with the Bindahnas. Most of this is authentic jinn lore.

                                    Regarding Jinn:

                                    Having met the Bindahnas, the Scions might want to learn more ablut jinn. Here’s what they can find out through basic research, Know-It-All and their Guides.

                                    These lesser immortals have no connections to any specific pantheon. They instead form a supernatural nation of their own with commoners, an aristocracy and several rival kings. Many jinn are evil and follow Iblis, an avatar of the sky-titan Ehekatoyaatl. Other jinn converted to Judaism, Christianity or Islam. Very few of them worship the Gods of Legend, despite frequent contact with these beings — or rather, because of that contact. As with mortals, most jinn believe there must be something greater than themselves. The jinn decided long ago that the all-too-human Gods were not that “something greater.”

                                    Middle Eastern mythology says that whereas God shaped humanity from clay, he made the jinn from a scorching wind or from black, smokeless fire. Jinn who follow Abrahamic faiths accept this myth of their origin. On the other hand, the followers of Iblis claim the jinn are the whirlwind-demons that the Babylonian Epic of Creation lists among the chaos-Titan Tiamat’s army of monsters. Whichever account is true — if either — the jinn are moral free agents, responsible for their actions in ways that titanspawn usually are not. Jewish, Christian and Muslim jinn follow divine Virtues. Iblisite jinni follow Dark Virtues.

                                    As with Gods, a jinni has a body of ichor but can dematerialize completely. In this immaterial state, jinn may dwell in the atmospheric heights, the Earth’s surface, underwater or underground, at their whim. The surface-dwellers favor abandoned buildings, wells, ruins, tombs, deserts and desolate places for their homes, though some jinn prefer river banks, gardens or the foot of great trees. A few Legend-touched mortals — unusually perceptive children, the holy, the mad and the dying — can see immaterial jinn. Middle Eastern tradition gives other names for jinn — jann, ifrits and marids — but an ifrit is merely a powerful jinni, and a marid (meaning “contumacious” in Arabic) is merely a hostile jinni. They are all one race.

                                    Many jinn choose to live in the World in a territory stretching from Morocco to Central Asia. These jinn also hold a few Terrae Incognita. Most notably, Iblis built his capital on Mount Qaf, an otherworldly peak or mountain range in the Middle East. Otherworldly palaces also lurk at the bottom of desert pits full of fluid, quicksand-like dust. On the other hand, Istanbul folk say the king of the sea-jinn has a palace in the Bosphorus near Leander’s Tower.

                                    Jinn can manifest in various ways. They can gather whirlwinds about their immaterial forms. They also materialize as animals, humans or huge, ogre-like creatures. Jinn can be male or female: jinni or jinniyah, ifrit or ifritah. The ogre-form is hideous, with tusks and horns, but human forms are often notably attractive. Jinn have children, grow old and die, though they live for thousands of years.

                                    These lesser immortals can have many other powers too, from inflicting disease to possessing poets, but are notable for vast strength, rapid movement around the World, and creating and transforming matter. Strictly speaking, even the mightiest jinn cannot grant a mortal’s every wish the way folktales portray them — but noble jinn may wield so many powers that they can perform almost any task a mortal can imagine.

                                    Only the mightiest magic can compel a jinni, but that spell was cast long ago. According to legend, King Solomon bound the entire race of jinn in the name of God. Henceforth, jinn must fulfill any pledges that they make in the name of Suleyman bin Da’ud. The oath to Solomon binds even the Iblisite jinn.

                                    In addition to the Iblisites, the jinn have several other nations with kings of their own. The great Masitash, Caliph of the Muslim jinn, has 300,000 vassals, each of them a lord of other jinn. Muhammed himself converted Masitash and the kings Abdur-Rahman and Abdul-Kadir (and the Koran specifically says that God sent Mohammed as his messenger to both humans an jinn). Malik Afshan rules the Muslim jinn of Persia. Masidus rules Christian jinn (Orthodox: They don’t hold with Roman Catholicism or newfangled Protestant doctrines); Tututash rules Jewish jinn. The jinn monarchs have their differences, but the Abrahamic kings all oppose Iblis.

                                        Regarding Landon

                                        Holly’s mysterious Guide “made inquiries” about Landon Smith’s becoming a shinobi. Here’s what he reports:

                                        Landon is a shinobi, now and forever. He chose this, and such choices are irrevocable — even if they were made based on false information.

                                        Nevertheless, Landon is not necessarily bound to the Titan of Darkness that empowered him. Long ago, the Japanese Pantheon (called the Amatsukami) worked a signing statement into the pact that turns a mortal into a shinobi. Most shinobi are bound to the Darkness Titan and serve horrors such as the Black Bodhisattva of the Endless Night Clan. Some shinobi, however, are bound to serve the Japanese Pantheon as high-end Followers. All shinobi have a degree of free will for the affairs of their everyday lives, but when their masters command, they must obey.

                                        Landon is currently in an undefined state because the leaders of the Endless Night Clan have never commanded him. If a senior shinobi such as Dark Master Chiba should get to him and give him an order, he would have great difficulty resisting. If he kills at their command, he belongs to the Titans and becomes evil.

                                        There are only two ways to prevent this happening:

                                        * Make sure the Endless Night Clan (or other agents of the Darkness Titan) never command him. This means abandoning his life and family. It still might not work if the Titan’s agents truly want to find him.

                                        * Bind him to some other power. The Amatsukami know how to bind shinobi to themselves, but you’d need someone from the Amatsukami to do this. Other pantheons might have their own methods. The closest the Kaleva have is a spell — which only Gods can cast — for transferring patronage of a latent Scion from one God to another. The Anuna might have better and easier methods, since binding covenants with Gods are one of their big schticks. You’d have to ask a knowledgeable Anuna. (If you check, the lion-demon bound to Leo doesn’t know.)

                                        As a new shinobi, Landon has Legend 1. He has no powers in his human form. When he turns into a shinobi, he has increased combat prowess, minimally superhuman agility, makes no sound at all, and can teleport between shadows. As Dark Master Chiba showed, shinobi can gain greater power through practice and experience. Dark Master Chiba had Scion-comparable combat prowess, and his Relic mask suggests he could channel War Boons. If Landon can avoid enslavement to the Endless Night Clan, it might be possible for him to become more powerful as well.

                                                    Now a long adventure. Conveniently for this posting schedule, it's the Scion High Christmas episode! It also brings in several new characters and conflicts that become important in later episodes.

                                                    And there's Aten. (Ducks as people throw things.) You have been warned. ADDENDUM: Also some speculations about the metaphysics of the World which I am quite sure have no support in the game as written.

                                                    EPISODE 12: SOLD MY SOUL FOR CHRISTMAS

                                                    Act One: It’s Raining Shoes

                                                    In the final week before Christmas, the Scions all find their lives getting more complicated.

                                                    GUS is somewhat surprised, though not worried, when a police lieutenant accosts him after school and asks — in a friendly way — if Gus could come down to the station and help the police reconstruct the last few days of a missing student. Gus agrees.

                                                    Down at the station, nice old Lieutenant Picinich wants to know about Gus’ relationship with the missing Acacia. Gus says it was limited to a fairly incredible one-night stand. (Plenty of people saw Acacia proposition him, so there’d be no point in lying.) Picinich presses for more information, though, on how well Gus knew Acacia before that night. Then he asks where Gus was on the night after Acacia was last seen (the night when Gus saw her fatally give birth to the Tomb-Spawn, in Episode 10) and where he was on the night Acacia was stabbed (trying to open the Gate to Xibalba, back in Episode Six). Gus lies that he doesn’t remember. He uses God’s Honest to make Picinich believe him when he lies, or at least must leave out important aspects of the truth. Picinich is a hard case, though. He also asks Gus about his relationship with Sheena Delisle. Gus can guess that the lieutenant has suspicions about the rich young combat-trained athlete who has had two girlfriends disappear without a trace, but Gus feels confident that he can keep the law off his back.

                                                    His confidence is unexpectedly vindicated when the precinct captain walks into Picinich’s office with news that they’ve found a witness for where Gus was the night Acacia disappeared: Another girl with whom Gus had another one-night stand. Which is news to Gus. Picinich lets Gus go, but no longer pretends to be friendly. He tells Gus he thinks the Sobecks are trouble, and money won’t protect them from everything.

                                                    Outside the station, a black stretch limo with tinted windows waits. A window rolls down. From inside, a very old man calls to Gus. The old man says he arranged for Gus’ alibi this time, but he wants the Scions to leave town. Their force of Legend is calling disaster onto them and everyone around them. The old man rather pointedly refuses to give his name, saying he’s sure Gus can find him if he wants, but why make it too easy? After a few more vague-yet-unhelpful ominous warnings, the old man signals his unseen driver and off they go. Gus memorizes the license plates.

                                                    Back home, Gus mentions his brush with the police to his father. Mr. Sobeck is properly concerned, but he says he’s not surprised at Picinich’s suspicion and ire. Gus’ father and his older brother Gav had run-ins with Picinich when they were young.

                                                    Gus urges his father to say more: His father hardly ever talks about Gav, the uncle who joined the Marines and died in the Beirut barracks bombing. The elder Sobeck merely notes that Gav had some pretty wild adventures, got into trouble because of them, and sometimes got his younger brother involved. Gav joined the Marines because it was that or jail. Gus’ father asks him to please be careful so he doesn’t end up like Gav. Gus would like to learn more about his mysterious uncle, but Gus’ father must go supervise the decorators who’ve come to install the Sobecks’ usual spectacular and soulless light display. This year’s will be even flashier than usual to impress the gorgeous twentysomething redhead who will soon become Gus’ latest stepmother.

                                                    Gus asks Holly to ask her hacker minion to track down the old man’s license plate. To no one’s surprise, there is no such license.

                                                    HOLLY has family issues as well. Her parents phone to say that instead of having Holly fly home for Christmas, they will fly out to visit her. That way, they can meet her new friends. She should throw her friends a Christmas party!

                                                    This gets Holly pondering how to hide her resident Moomins from her parents. (The Moomins, meanwhile, say they’d like to look for a place of their own or some honest way to earn money for plane fare back to Finland.)

                                                    Of greater concern to Holly is her campaign against Vanessa, the Fashionista who tried to humiliate her. Most of the time, Holly tries to be a very nice person… because she doesn’t like the person she becomes when she is truly angry. Not that that stops her from channeling her mother Louhi, and the Kaleva’s Virtue of Vengeance…

                                                    Between episodes, Holly already acted to ruin Vanessa’s friendships and place in school. She also sets out to ruin Vanessa’s family. To this end, Holly sneaks into the gated community of Rowanfield, where Vanessa lives, and curses Vanessa’s father with the Evil Eye. At the moment Holly casts the spell, though, she feels a stabbing migraine. She also realizes her curse was not as effective as it should be. Many of the trees around her — rowan trees — suddenly stand out to her sight with painful, hallucinatory intensity and she remembers that rowan is a traditional protection against witchcraft. Holly decides that she should make a strategic retreat. On the way out, she sees an unusual number of Rowanfield’s security guards tooling around in their little golf carts, and in the back of the cart they carry… flamethrowers? Eep. Clearly, there is something very strange about Rowanfield!

                                                    But such inquiries must wait until after Holly’s date with Cole. They go dancing at a Seattle nightclub that features techno music and flashing lights. The three skinwalkers from Episode 10 interrupt them and demand that Holly come with them. Holly doesn’t like this idea. Neither does Cole, who pulls out his Cobra and tells the skinwalkers to get lost if they don’t want to get whacked in the head again. (Yes, he recognizes them from their first encounter.) The skinwalkers sneer, start shifting into coyote-man form, and attack.

                                                    Holly curses one skinwalker. Cole has trouble striking his skinwalker foe — they are too nimble. A skinwalker claws Cole across the chest; he manages to strike the were-coyote once but does no damage. Holly manages to judo-flip one skinwalker into a bank of hot lights, and that skinwalker runs away yipping with his fur on fire.

                                                    At that point, though, Holly decides that discretion is the better part of valor, and running away is the better part of discretion. She grabs Cole and they skedaddle, Holly dragging Cole along while she uses Lightning Sprinter. She drives him to an emergency room for treatment.

                                                    Afterward, Holly admits she doesn’t know why the skinwalkers want her so badly. She also finds that Cole believed his eyes when he saw the skinwalkers change (but he already realized there’s weird stuff going on in Avalon and Etna). The evening ends with Holly rewarding Cole’s courage with smoochies (though nothing beyond that). All in all, Cole thinks the evening is a win.

                                                    As she thinks back on the fight, though, Holly remembers one odd detail she missed at the time: One person on the dance floor did notice the fight, seemed mildly surprised when the skinwalkers turned into coyote-men, but did not freak out. He merely moved further away from them. Holly resolves she must make further inquiries, later.

                                                    Holly also covertly casts The Unlidded Eye to make sure the skinwalkers did not infect Cole with lycanthropy (coyotanthropy?) when they slashed him. They did not — but she sees that Cole is not only a heroic mortal, he is also now strongly Fatebound to her — and not just because he was the subject of her spell. Hmm.

                                                    JACK has another wants-to-slit-his-wrists day working at Stick It. This being the week before Christmas, Stick It is of course selling Christmas On A Stick (don’t ask).

                                                    Getting to work, Jack has to push past a crowd of people gathered by Reverend Stringer, the preacher at the Glory of God Ministries, the church Leo’s family attends; Leo is there. Stringer has a Nativity scene and a big cross with a golden sunburst in the center. He makes a speech about bringing Christmas to the mall, the schools and City Hall, challenging the Godless Liberal Secular Atheist War on Christmas, forcing everyone to choose between Jesus and Satan, yadda yadda.

                                                    At Stick It, Jack again does not try to get better acquainted with MariLee, the Orange Julius girl. She smiles when she looks at him, but every time he thinks about asking her for a date, Holly appears in his mind’s eye, he thinks about what she could do to him (and MariLee), and he decides to stay faithful to his “girlfriend.”

                                                    Jack’s Follower Moose stops by to complain about Christmas shopping. His four younger siblings all want either Wii or Cuddle Me Eggbert (the season’s “hot” toy), and he doesn’t have that kind of money. Heck, his parents don’t have that kind of money! He grouses that he wishes his family was Jewish, like Jack’s, so they wouldn’t need to deal with this crap.

                                                    It’s worse that two teens are smooching in one of the booths nearby, reminding Jack of what he isn’t getting. The two kids are interrupted when a very pretty, well-dressed girl stalks up to them and exclaims, “Jason? You’re cheating on me? With her?” Jason replies that he isn’t cheating, he’s breaking up with her because she’s vain, spoiled, demanding, etc. and he can’t imagine what he ever saw in her. He leaves with his new girlfriend, and the rejected girl crumples on the floor, sobbing.

                                                    Jack recognizes the girl: Vanessa, a Fashionista. He leaves his post to try comforting her (at least enough that she stops crying and attracting Mall Security — that’s all Vanessa would need, to get thrown out of the mall for making a scene). While doing this, Moose stops by again, jubilant that a store at the mall had a Cuddle Me Eggbert, and for a lot less than he thought! Moose barely seems to notice Vanessa.

                                                    JAEDA, on the other hand, feels that life is going pretty well. Yes, she lost her car and she expects another pathetic, impoverished, barely-making-the-effort Christmas from her father, but she’s having fabulous sex with Gary as his secret girlfriend. Though she doesn’t like the “secret” part.

                                                    Gary explains, however, that his father hates, HATES Gods and their Scions because he was the only survivor when the Gods exterminated his family. Suttung Vetterson hasn’t moved against the Scions only because he hasn’t thought of a way to murder them and get away with it. If he found that Gary was dating a Scion, he’d probably go nuts and try to kill them both. So, Gary can’t hang out with Jaeda where anyone from Etna might see, lest word get back to his father. Still, he’s also helping her look for another classic muscle car to restore.

                                                    (Gary also tells Jaeda about how frost giants celebrate the Winter Solstice. It’s their biggest holiday. They extinguish a bonfire, pray that the Sun similarly dies, and feast because if their prayers are answered it’s their last meal, so they should make the most of it.

                                                    (The Vettersons also exchange presents. Gary caused that innovation. When he was eight, he learned about human kids getting presents at Christmas, and he threw a tantrum when his father refused to adopt the custom. So, Gary burned the house down. He insists it was an accident: He brought a fir tree into the house, tried to set lit candles in it just like the Olde Tyme Christmas scenes on cards, and… he was eight. Fortunately, his father took it for revenge. Beat Gary within an inch of his life, but proudly. Since then, though, they’ve had Solstice presents.)

                                                    One night, though, Jaeda wakes up to crackling sounds and yellow light coming in through the trailer’s windows. Jaeda immediately realizes this is an attack. The whole exterior of the trailer is on fire. She grabs the First Flower and her father, dives out the nearest window and runs — right before a car smashes down on the burning trailer. If she’d been a few seconds slower, they’d both be caught in the flaming wreckage. Jaeda sees a female giant silhouetted against the fire, poking at the wreckage. She suspects it must be Cailleach Sarsen, the mother of the late Don and Dave: She knows that Mama Sarsen is out for revenge, and she doesn’t think Gary’s mother would use fire as a weapon (frost giants and fire don’t get along).

                                                    Jaeda’s father wakes up enough to see that A) their home is destroyed, and B) Jaeda is stark naked. He quickly gives her his shirt. Jaeda runs back to check on the trailer’s propane tanks, but the impact of the car broke the lines from them and the tanks are venting harmlessly into the flames. The giantess is gone, though not before pushing the car (a neighbor’s old Ford Taurus) out of the flames so it looks like it crashed into the trailer, not that it dropped on top of it.

                                                    The police soon arrive. The cop in charge is, of course, Lieutenant Picinich. He notices that Jaeda is carrying a rather unusual obsidian-bladed knife. The cops also remove her Tepetlacalli from the remains of the trailer.

                                                    Later, Picinich asks Jaeda some probing questions. The burning of the trailer was pretty clearly arson, and a background check turns up Jaeda’s gang past. He wants to know more about Jaeda’s gang connections — such as possible enemies — and what she’s doing with Mesoamerican antiquities. Jaeda says (truthfully) that the knife and box are presents from her mother. Her answers seem to satisfy Picinich for now, but she can tell the shrewd cop has mentally filed her as a “person of interest.” For the moment, he keeps the Tepetlacalli as “evidence,” and Jaeda does not protest. When she gets a chance later, she retrieves her sunglasses and the shuriken of Dark Master Chiba’s soul from the ashes of the trailer.

                                                    Of course, all this means that the Magdalena family has lost everything. They get new clothes and Red Cross vouchers from the cops so they can live a while at a motel, but they had no bank account and no insurance. Such matters, however, must wait. Jaeda calls the other Scions and lets them know about this deadly escalation by Cailleach Sarsen.

                                                        EPISODE 12: SOLD MY SOUL FOR CHRISTMAS (Continued)

                                                        Interlude: The Christmas Party

                                                        The Scions get to meet Holly’s parents. As requested, Holly throws a pre-Christmas party. She first introduces her parents to Gus, letting them see the dangerous, sexy, well-fed beast she hangs out with. Then she introduces Jack as her boyfriend, making the big goofy lug look much better, or at least much safer, by comparison. Moominmamma stitches together an outfit for Jaeda that looks suitably presentable.

                                                        The Scions’ mortal parents come too, though it’s a challenge making Jaeda’s father Mateo not look like a bum. He tries flirting with Jack’s mom and is not entirely rebuffed. (Jack frowns.) Gus’ father brings his fiancée. If Mr. and Mrs. Frost have qualms about their daughter’s friends, or their parents, they are too polite to say anything.

                                                        Holly, Gus and Jaeda also give gifts to the other Scions. Holly’s and Gus’ are tasteful and/or useful, and not so expensive as to be embarrassing. Jaeda must settle for writing out coupons for services, such as detailing Holly’s car. A good time is had by all, and Holly’s parents do not see anything that would result in awkward questions.

                                                        Act Two: A Very Old Man With Enormous Secrets

                                                        The Scions gather and compare notes. Once again, obviously, Something Is Up. They guess it probably has a connection to the approaching Winter Solstice (a mythically potent date). After a bit of discussion, Holly pulls out her magic mirror and uses Mystery to find out what’s going on. Fate gives her seven questions:

                                                        * What’s the real name of the old man who accosted Gus? Aristotle Faustus Dee. Really.

                                                        * What disaster does Dee fear, that he thinks the Scions’ presence makes worse or more likely? Eruption of the Titans into the mortal World.

                                                        * Did Cailleach Sarsen attack Jaeda’s trailer? Yes.

                                                        * Who, if anyone, goaded or manipulated her into doing this? Guibourg. (Jack and Holly recognize the name of the hideous defrocked priest-turned-Satanist who conducted Black Masses for the notorious Madame de Montespan, back in pre-Revolutionary France. But it might just be a guy using the same name.)

                                                        * What is Guibourg’s ultimate goal? Eruption of the Titans into the mortal World.

                                                        * Where is Guibourg’s primary base of operations on Earth? Etna High Principal’s office.

                                                        * What is Guibourg’s plan for the Winter Solstice? Summon a Hand of Aten.

                                                        Well. Again, the two occult-literate Scions can fill in the rest. Aten is the Titan of Light, once worshipped by the heretic Pharaoh Akhenaten, whom some call the inventor of monotheism. Aten’s servants, the Hands of Aten, are beings of light who may have inspired legends of angels, or (equally plausible) were shaped to exploit such legends. Hands of Aten range from minor cherubs to archangels who can battle Gods. All Hands of Aten are fanatically — even suicidally — loyal to the Titan and inspire similar fanaticism in other beings.

                                                        Such a summoning would need a Relic as a focus. Something like, maybe, a big cross with a sunburst in the center? With a crowd of people consumed with self-righteous anger and zealotry as a power source? Suddenly, Reverend Stringer’s little crusade looks a lot more sinister.

                                                        But that is not the only game afoot. What of the so-mysterious Mr. Dee? Holly uses Ariadne’s Thread to find him. To no one’s surprise, the trail leads to Rowanfield. Secluded from the surrounding McMansions by a screen of oak and rowan trees, there is a real, no kidding mansion, clearly much older than the rest of the gated community. Holly sees that it’s warded up the wazoo.

                                                        A male nurse answers the door. When the Scions say who they are and they want to talk to Mr. Dee, the nurse says (with an embarrassed shrug), “I won’t invite you, but I won’t stop you. Enter if you can.” The Scions can. After a brief wait in the opulent-but-creepy vestibule (Is that a stuffed griffon? Why yes it is. Is it watching us? Could be) the nurse guides them to Mr. Dee.

                                                        Dee is not surprised to see them. The Scions’ first question is why he thinks they are bringing disaster onto Avalon. Dee explains that Legend calls more to itself. Because the Scions are gathered in Avalon, other mythic entities are drawn to the region as well. Eventually, Avalon could become so mythic that it drops out of the Mortal World and becomes a legend of its own.

                                                        Another danger, though, is that the Gods are not the greatest force protecting the mortal World: More than any God, the Titans are barred from the mortal World by humanity’s collective belief that such things cannot exist. People say they believe in Gods and devils, but they don’t really think the sky might rip apart, the seas give up their dead, that armies of flaming giants could burn the World to ash, or other sorts of apocalyptic cataclysms. Not here. Not now. Such stories are… just stories.

                                                        The Titans and their servants, however, want people to believe in Gods and monsters. The more people believe in small intrusions of the mythic or horrific, the easier it becomes for greater entities to invade the World and further erode people’s sense of stable reality. If people come to believe that anything can happen, why, it shall be so — and the Titans will be loose in the World, as they were in the ancient Mythic Age.

                                                        Most Gods would rather this not happen. They like the World as it is. Some Gods disagree. As the Scions think back on their adventures, they suspect that Coyote might have given the mythic cascade described by Dee a jump-start by his transformation of the Stone People into puppies — and that might have been his goal. After all, Coyote’s irresponsible enough to do something like that just for shits and grins.

                                                        Dee thinks the Scions are right that Guibourg is using Reverend Stringer as his pawn. An “angel” appearing before hundreds of people, filmed and broadcast to the world on the news, would serve the Titans’ goals very well. Dee has heard of Guibourg: a powerful warlock, a mortal servant of the Titans given magical powers as Acacia was. Dee still wants the Scions to scatter as the best hope to quell the building vortex of Legend, but he agrees that for the moment, they are the best hope to stop this particular scheme.

                                                        When the Scions ask Dee for his own story, he merely says that he is a mortal who once worked for various Gods. Dee learned more about them than is usual for their servants — enough to recognize that their interests were not always the same as humanity’s, and so he stopped working for them. Since then, he has used what little power he has acquired to protect the World from supernatural intrusions by both Gods and Titans. Dee is aware of the irony that sometimes he uses (and abuses) people as ruthlessly as he says the Gods and Titans do. The difference, he says, is that he does so because of weakness, rather than strength.

                                                        Dee also semi-confirms that Rowanfield is more than just a personal fortress. “Don’t go digging in Rowanfield,” he says. “It would be bad.”

                                                        The Scions leave Dee’s mansion fairly convinced that he is not their friend. In fact, Gus now wonders if it wasn’t an agent of Dee’s who shot Gary. However, they think he sees them as more useful than a liability, at least for the moment. They also think he probably knows a great deal about past Scion activity in Avalon… such as, maybe, previous Scions in Gus’ family. For now, though, they need to focus on preventing a Christmas miracle.

                                                            EPISODE 12: SOLD MY SOUL FOR CHRISTMAS (Continued)

                                                            Act Three: Angels We Haven’t Heard on High

                                                            Back at Dee’s, the Scions decided they needed to reduce the number of people at Stringer’s Solstice zeal-fest. They continue discussing ways to achieve this. The Scions know that secular society activists will show up for a counter-demonstration, which will only make Stringer’s Evangelicals feel that much more persecuted and zealous (and the zeal of the counter-protesters might feed into the summoning as well).

                                                            After a lot of brainstorming, the Scions simplify their plan down to visiting the leaders of such counter-protest groups, making them believe they need to protest something more important somewhere else, and having Marshall Byrd shoot out their tires once they arrive at the supposed White Power Against Gays Anti-Feminist rally, so they can’t get back to Stringer’s shindig. Then, Holly, Barbara and Delia do whatever they can to distract the Evangelicals and break the mood. While everyone’s distracted, Gus runs in (wearing a ski mask), carries off the Relic cross, and chucks it in the back of a pickup Jaeda’s hotwired. She drives away as fast as she can. Then they hope the cross must be present at the resulting explosion of holy outrage when people realize what happened. Jack, meanwhile, will stand ready to protect the girls from Leo in case the pious Scion of Nergal flips out and tries to kill them.

                                                            Alone among the Scions, Jaeda thinks Leo should be told what’s going on. When she tries telling him that his church is being used in a Titan plot, though, he won’t listen, cuts her off and angrily stalks away. But he doesn’t know the Scions’ plan.

                                                            As another ploy to reduce the worst-case damage, the Scions decide to make the Solstice a skip day since that’s the day Stringer chose to protest at Avalon High. Fewer students mean fewer possible casualties. (Or hostages.) Everyone needs to mobilize their cliques: Holly for the brains, Gus and Jack for the jocks, Delia for the Fashionistas, Barbara for the student government types, Marshall for the military brats and Jaeda for boys who have a pulse.

                                                            Holly puts the plan to Barbara and Delia. Barbara’s okay with it, but Delia says no. Not unless Holly calls off her vendetta against Vanessa. Delia understands why, and it was the dumbest thing Vanessa’s ever done, but… Vanessa is a Fashionista and hence more or less Delia’s friend. Holly can’t carry this any further without Delia taking umbrage on Vanessa’s behalf. Holly says she will cease her vengeance when Vanessa gives a sincere apology. Delia says it shall be done.

                                                            And it is. The next day, Vanessa tells Holly she is very sorry for trying to embarrass her. Holly can tell that Vanessa speaks with a sincere repentance born of stark terror. Holly smiles a Disney Princess smile, hugs Vanessa and says all is forgiven. And that is that.

                                                            The plan goes off without a hitch. Between a third and half the students skip class (even a bunch of the brains and grinds who normally don’t even hear about such plans, let alone follow them). Principal Flecker bites his lip; Vice-Principal Stone growls that he’ll expel whoever’s behind it all; most of the teachers don’t give a damn.

                                                            As noon approaches (the time the Scions think the summoning is intended to take place), only half the expected counter-demonstrators have shown up at Avalon High. Holly uses Unlidded Eye to confirm that the cross is indeed a powerful Relic of the Titans. Stringer begins his rant about bringing God back to the schools… and Holly and Delia start singing while hitting the crowd with Center of Attention. (It was never said what they sang, and it doesn’t really matter, but I suggest “Jesus Loves Me.” The Evangelicals can hardly object to it, making it hard for them to resist; and it’s one of the most pacifying hymns ever written.) Leo is there among the line of burly young parishioners Stringer has recruited as guards for himself and his cross, but even Leo is caught up by the girls’ blast of Epic Socials. Gus, fortified by Full Of Yourself, recovers his presence of mind first, dashes through, and carries the cross away. It’s in the pickup and heading away from the rally before anyone in the crowd knows what’s happening.

                                                            As Jaeda and Gus make their escape, the other Scions attempt further damage control. Leo is white with fury at what he can see only as a deliberate insult to his faith. The Scions try to talk him down, but he thinks their story sounds preposterous. At least he isn’t pulling out his mace and laying into them.

                                                            Jack turns to Reverend Stringer, who sees the theft of the cross as proof! PROOF!!! of his paranoia. Jack’s deft questioning, however, strips away the layers of false memory implanted in Stringer (or confabulated by him to explain inconsistencies in his own thoughts and actions) until he blurts out that he received the cross from Mr. Burgen, the principal of Etna High, along with the idea for his confrontational crusade.

                                                            At this point, even Leo has to admit that something is hinky. He doesn’t know that Burgen is (allegedly) the warlock Guibourg, but he saw how someone must have tampered with Stringer’s memory.

                                                            On the other hand, Leo isn’t entirely convinced by the Scions’ story either. Jack puts it to Leo that the proof lies in whether the cross turns out to be a Relic. Leo believes that Christ and the Gods have nothing in common; therefore, if the cross is a Relic of the Gods or Titans, it cannot be a holy vehicle for the power of the true God. Leo agrees that is reasonable, and demands the Scions take him to the cross.

                                                            The Scions rendezvous at the abandoned barn where Jaeda partied back in Episode 10. They now face the problem of what to do with the Relic cross, which like all Relics is nigh-indestructible. Gus figures that now the Scions have saved the day, it’s time for their divine sponsors to pull their weight. His smartphone has an app for hieroglyphics; he leaves a message on his own Facebook page in hieroglyphics, asking for the Pesedjet to deal with the cross. He figures that Thoth or someone is keeping an eye on him, or at least on his online presence. Sure enough, he soon gets a message back from the artisan-God Ptah, saying he’ll be right over.

                                                            On seeing the cross, Leo prays that God will forgive him for striking His symbol. Then he pounds it with his mace as hard as he can. He doesn’t even scratch the gold. Leo turns away, even more miserably shut in on himself than usual, and the Scions leave him be.

                                                            Ptah arrives in a flying saucer. He examines the cross with some sort of mixed supernatural/high tech scanner goggles, and confirms all the Scions’ guesses about how it works. It’s still armed, but Ptah can deal with that. He is, after, all, the builder of, you know, THE UNIVERSE. (Leo stiffens when Ptah says this, but says nothing.) A simple Relic is nothing for him. Ptah calls a squad of little robots out of his saucer, which quickly assemble into a magical buzz-saw whose blade is edged with little diamond ankhs. (“Ptah always has the best toys,” Gus mutters.) Ptah saws the cross in half and announces it is now powerless. His work done, Ptah reloads his gear into his saucer and zooms away.
