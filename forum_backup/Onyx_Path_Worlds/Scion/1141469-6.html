        So... were Dyaus and Prithvi Primordials who no one has heard from in millennia?
        Dyaus and Prithvi, along with the third member of the Vedic Triad Aditi, are most definitely Primordials... or at least they were in the Vedas.

        Unlike the more complicated 14 + 2 Plane cosmology of the Puranic era, the Vedas saw all of reality divided into a much simpler 3 Tier system : Heaven (Svar), Earth (Bhu) and everything In-Between (Bhuvar). These three Goddesses (Yes, three godesses, I'm getting to that) are the Primordial personifications of these three Planes. Dyaus (Shining One) is the Embodiment of Heaven, whose Principal deity is the Sun and who is the abode of the Gods; Aditi (Unbound One), is the Embodiment of In-Between, whose Principal Deity is Indra/Vayu and who is the abode of the blessed ancestors; Prithvi (Vast One) is the Embodiment of Earth, whose Principal deity is Agni and who is the abode of humans.

        While Dyaus probably originally was a Sky Father in the far away mists of time, their gender appears significantly ambiguous in the Veda, which do call Heaven and Earth the parents of all things, but also refer to the sisterhood of Heaven and Earth. Texts that provide commentary and additions to the Vedas often consider Dyaus a female divinity. The Taittritya Brahmana, which has an entire section dedicated to worshiping goddesses, mentions Dyaus as the very first Goddess on that list. So, for symmetry's sake, I always prefer to take Dyaus as a Goddess, Mother Heaven. Sadly, Dyaus is pretty much non-existent in modern day Hindu worship... already in the Veda Indra was taking over much of her role, and there's basically no Dyaus worship today.

        Prithvi is an interesting case... her importance in the Veda seems greater than Dyaus' by a fair margin (Dyaus has no independant hymns, she is worsipped only as a pair with Pritvi, whearas Prithvi has a few hymns where she is worshipped alone). The Puranas introduced BhuDevi (literally, Earth Goddess) who is the principal earth deity, but there's a lot of back and forth on whether BhuDevi is the God form of the Primordial Prithvi, or a Mantle of Lakshmi, who is also hymned as BhuDevi... the latter tradition seems to have come out both as a way to maintain Trimurti and Tridevi ascendance over the old Vedic Gods, by literally making one of them merely an aspect of Lakshmi, and a way to get around the awkwardness of the myths where Vishnu's Varaha Incarnation has sex with BhuDevi (if she's an Incarnation of Vishnu's wife, then that doesn't count as cheating, which is a big deal in Hinduism, where faithfulness to your partner is a very big deal in most traditions). This does of course get kinda weird in the Ramayana where Queen Sita, another Incarnation of Lakshmi, is born as a daughter of Bhudevi/Prithvi, but I guess even the Gods can't keep all their Incarnations straight sometimes. Whichever version you decide to go with though, Prithvi does get a modicum of worship and respect and mentions in myth so she's arguably doing the best of the three.

        Aditi has possibly had the greatest fall of the three... at least Dyaus got away with just becoming obscure. Aditi in the Veda is the Goddess of Unbound Infinity, whose Infinite Might keeps the Worlds in Order... she is perhaps the earliest conception of what would later become Shakti. Aditi is Indra's mother in the Veda, as well as Varuna's and sometimes even Vishnu's and Mitra's and a host of other deities who are collectively identified as the Adityas, the Sons of Aditi... all, especially Indra, are implied to gain their phenomenal cosmic powers by virtue of Aditi... Indra is said to have become mighty as an infant by drinking her milk. One hymn in the Veda even declares:

        "Aditi is Heaven, Aditi is In-Between, Aditi is the Mother, the Father, and the Child... Aditi is verily All the Universal Gods, Aditi is the Five Classes of Men... All who have been born are Aditi, All who will be born are nought but Aditi".
        That declaration has similarities to Durga's pronouncement in the Devi Mahatmyam, where She states "I alone move throughout the Universe... Who Else is there but Me?" and indeed Durga is called Aditi and Devamata (Mother of the Gods) due to her Unbound Power and Motherly Functions. Unfortunately, the actual Aditi got downgraded severely into one of the wives of the Sage Rishi Kashyapa, and while she's still mother of Indra and the Vedic Devas, basically ends up portrayed as a meek housewife figure, where the Primordial Aditi had no male counterpart, and indeed had no need of such.

        So Yeah, the Shining One, the Unbound One and the Vast One definitely were Primordials at some point... what they are now depends on which version you want to go with (Personally I prefer to leave all three of them as Primordials, and treat Bhudevi as a shared Mantle between Prithvi and Lakshmi and basically assume the Primordial Aditi either became Durga at some point or became the Primordial Shakti of whom Durga (and all Goddesses) are a part).

        (That was probably way more info than you were looking for, but I'll take any chance I can get to talk about Aditi who is one of my favourite 'Why does no one talk about her!' figures ever )

                            Originally posted by Nicolas Milioni View Post
                            the preview states Vishnu is the leader of Devas,surpassing Indra,although the Indra doesn't technically agrees.
                            Are there any tales where Indra fights with Vishnu over something?
                            So, I'll get your question about opposition out of the way first... In general, Indra does not oppose Vishnu in myth. In the Vedas, Vishnu is a minor Sun god possibly representing the Sun's power as keeper of Time and in some versions of the Vritra myth he helps Indra to kill Vritra (in some others Sarasvati does, so both a future Trimurti and a future Tridevi member have a story of helping Indra in his great battle against Vritra in the Veda). In latter day Puranic era myth, we see Indra actually becoming Vishnu's supplicant... Indra comes to Vishnu after the demon of the week has taken over Svarga and asks him for help and Vishnu usually does, often by taking on an Avatara.

                            However, while Indra doesn't get into fights with Vishnu, he does get into not one but two scrapes with Krishna, Vihsnu's most popular Avatara. In one story, during the rainy season, the inhabitants of Krishna's village get together to worship Indra... Krishna, then just a child, argues that they should worship the nearby mountain Govardhan instead as it is the mountain that stops the clouds, causing the rain. The villagers agree... Indra is furious at this and attacks the village, causing torrential rainstorms. To protect the people, Krishna picks up the mountain with his little finger, keeping it aloft with that one single finger of one hand, while the entire village takes shelter underneath the mountain. He also plays the flute one-handed while he's doing this cuz Krishna just likes to show-off sometimes Indra admits defeat and apologizes to Krishna.

                            In another story many years later, Agni approaches a now adult Krishna and his friend Arjuna, Scion of Indra himself, and says that he wishes to eat the Khandava forest (i.e. burn it down) but he can't as Indra himself protects the place. In return for Arjuna's protection while he eats, Agni gives him the Gandiva bow, forged by Brahma himself and a match for Vishnu's Sharanga and Shiva's Pinaka bows, along with two quivers of infinite arrows. Agni starts to burn the forest down, and Indra turns up with many other Deva to battle him, but is fought to a standstill by his son and Krihsna until a Divine Voice tells him to just give up already, which he does.

                            That question being answered, I want to tackle another question... that of Deva leadership. It's a funny thing that while Indra is the top God of the Vedas, I hesitate to call him the King of the Vedic era Gods, not because he isn't the most powerful, but because calling him king implies that he rules them, and no one really rules the Vedic Gods... in the Vedas the Gods are mostly independent and rarely if ever interfere with each other... they roam throughout the World, attending sacrifices, abiding in the Fire and Wind, in Sun and Soma, in Thunder and Sea. Indra is the most honoured and mightiest among them, but he can't be bothered sitting on a throne all day making policy or passing Judgement... in the Vedic era Virtue set of Honor and Prowess that I mentioned earlier, Indra is the epitome of Prowess... Varuna is the Epitome of Honor, and in his time as ruler he probably did hold court, but that is so long ago that we have next to records of what Hinduism was even like back then.

                            The funny thing is, it's the Puranic era that actually makes Indra a proper 'King' with a court at Amaravati in Svarga and the like... but it was also the Puranic era that decided that 'King' is not top job in the Hindu Pantheon. The Trimurti and Tridevi all outrank Indra, even though he is King. It's just that, the two triads rarely if ever interfere in the day to day workings of Heaven or Earth. The Trimurti and Tridevi work on a cosmic scale, keeping the universe going through it's eternal cycle... for everyday things like the rainy season hitting on time, or sunup or sundown not being interrupted, or the everyday death of living things happens, the Vedic era Gods still answer to Indra. Sure, the Trimurti and Tridevi could interfere and micromanage the day to day running of the universe, but why would they? The Vedic era Gods have been the caretakers of Nature for eons at this point... they know how to do their jobs, and do it well. The two Triads would have been stupid to try and insert themselves into that system and mess it up, so they just placed themselves above it

                            So, tl;dr Indra is still officially king of the Gods, but the Trimurti and Tridevi outrank him, he's still in charge of the Nature Gods of the Veda and keeps the everyday natural world running on schedule, while the two Triads usually work on a more cosmic scale and interfere only as and when necessary.
