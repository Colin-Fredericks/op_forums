                Originally posted by Samudra View Post
                By the way, a question for those in this forum better versed in the subject than I am: I know that Mithra was widely worshipped amongst Roman legions, but was he ever worshipped as part of the Roman Pantheon? As far as I know, he was always worshipped as an independent deity.
                The thing to keep in mind were the Romans were the God-Borg. They just kind of assimilated religious beliefs and cults, a lot like Christianity does today. Not being "in the pantheon" itself is something of a misnomer, since nearly every time you read about a Roman pantheon it's limited strictly to romanized classical Greek deities, rather than even hellenized classical Roman deities (so, the classification itself is rather arbitrary).

                This is mostly if I remember correctly -- it's been forever since I've studied my Roman history, and may be mistaken in places. The best place to start with Mithraism in the Roman Empire is sun worship. Sun worship predated Mithraism for a few centuries -- during the Republic period there was worship of Sol indiges which is believed to be a mishmash of various sun cults (and the Romans' own Phoebus) and Apollo during hellenization, and by the beginning of the Imperial period sun cults were completely dominated by romanized Apollo worship. During the Imperial period, there was a rebirth in sun worship (Sol invictus) through which Mithraism worked its way into Roman religion (interpreting Mithra as a solar deity with a bunch of other Roman virtues tacked on).

                If you really wanted to put it in Scion terms and play it straight, with that in mind Roman Mithraism would actually be a titan cult (Akhenaten). Would certainly make sense, considering Akhenaten's other pet projects.

                ​And, to that end, should a God be considered part of a Pantheon just because he was worshipped by the same people? I can see Sarasvati as a Goddess legitimately belonging to two different Pantheons, because she is actually worshipped as both the Deva Sarasvati and the Kami Benzaitan. But Mithra was always a Persian God who just happened to be worshipped in Rome. With great devotion and fanfare, undoubtedly, but not as a member of the same group of Gods as Jupiter or Mars. Again, my knowledge on this is lacking (I'm far better versed in mythology than I am in religion), so any corrections are welcome.
                That's why I believe there should be rules for gods having different aspects, and being worshiped and producing scions of different aspects. Perhaps something like once a god hits Legend 12, their current Fatebonds, abilities, and purviews become "locked in" (they can continue improving associations up to their respective Ultimates, but no new stuff) -- and if they become further Fatebound, they develop multiple avatars to reflect the new Fatebonds rather than get new goodies to their original avatar.

                That way you get an alternate route for character progression at the god level while maintaining a semblance of game balance, and a venue for ancient gods having associations with multiple pantheons while also allowing classical deities taking on more modernized roles, but not threatening scions with obsolescence. Not to mention, it would provide an actual explanation as to why characters that have been around for dozens of millennia have such limited character sheets -- they had other avatars and associated Legends to build -- and provide for rebirth and regrowth of forgotten or "dead" gods.
