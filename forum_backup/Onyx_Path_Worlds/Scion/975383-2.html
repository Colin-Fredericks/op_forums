                • Aphrodite: I like this for the most part.

Not sure why Ares would hate Drones in particular if he was fine with all other technology improvements. So perhaps a sentence on why he doesn't like drones in Scion?

                  • Women Soldiers: As the god of war, there wasn't a comment on women's soldiers in modern militaries.
                • Artemis: I like the write up here. the Modern day has plenty of story seeds which is good.
                • Athena: I like her write up for the most part. Particular the story seed of corruption in democracy annoying her.
                • Demeter: Generally I like this.
                  • Modern hunger: In the comment about how people are still hungry despite her gifts. Perhaps a line about how she tries to help and motivate people to alleviate it in whatever ways she can?
                • Dionysus: Again mostly liked though one thing stood out.
                  • Theater: Dionysus is the God of Theater among the Theoi. One of the bigger festivals for Dionysus was the Greater Dionsyia, which could be viewed as religious event + theater festival. There should be Some about that and how he views modern Theatre and movies.
                • Hades: Generally good.
                  • Digging for gold: It seems like the sentence about digging into the earth for gold was cut short? In any case it isn't that clear on what he thinks of those digging into the earth for gems and minerals.
                  • Modern world: While I get the impression of a tired Hades in the Modern world, beyond that there isn't much story seeds here that pop. I would like to see more of his view on the modern world.
                • Hephaestus: Another one that I quite like.
                • Hera: This is probably the only writeup that I actually didn't like.
                  • Hera and Zeus: I know the general view is that Hera does not really liking Zeus, but from all the different myths I don't really get that impression. She doesn't like his cheating and has a famous fury, yet, despite this she does love him. While there are a number of stories of them fighting, it ends with them making up.
                  • Hera and Marriage: As someone who does worship the Theoi. I generally see Hera as supportive of Marriages made for love. After all a Marriage that is made out of love is a marriage that isn't likely to break. That is important to her. More over I see her quite in favor of Gay Marriage. That is a fight for marriage, and yet this write up comes off as she isn't carrying about one of the bigger successes in one of her central purview.
                  • Women: She is Queen of the Gods, as such one of her principal things she looks over are women. She is far more then just the goddess of Marriage, though that is her major role. I would assume that there would be a few sentences in the modern day on the changing role of women. If anything she would be supportive IMO of women taking charge in the mortal world as well as helping to care for women's issues.
                  • Her Agency: Overall this section reads for me as if Hera has little agency of her own. That isn't true in the slightest. Most of her furies are her own, more over she also aides heroes in their own quests. Most famously she helped Jason and the Arognauts in their quest for the Golden Fleece. This ties into the first comment about her relationship to Zeus. She sticks with Zeus because SHE wants to. The Modern day section also didn't feel like there were any story seeds here.
                • Hermes: I quite like this one. I particularly like the relationship implied between Hermes and Thoth.
                • Hestia: A decent one though a few things.
                  • Home Goddess: She is the Goddess of Hearth and Home, not sacrifice as sacrifices to the theoi is the central act of worship. Would like to see a bit more in the Modern day section on the modern home for Hestia.
                • Persephone: Another decent one.
                • Poseidon: I like this one. It should be noted droughts are one of the things he is known to afflict on those that wrong him. Perhaps mention some modern droughts like the California drought as perhaps his ire?
                • Zeus: this one was meh to me. Now I didn't come away disliking it but hey this is for improvements.
                  • Zeus and Hera: I mentioned a good bit in the Hera Section. So not to repeat myself. I regard Zeus as loving Hera the most of all the women. He may sleep with other women and have affairs but he reserves his heart for her despite all their fights and make ups and fights.
                  • the King of the Gods: Zeus is the King of the Gods, as part of that he is the god of Law and Order. Hence why oaths were made in his name. I would like to see more in this write up as his role as the King of the Gods, particularly in the Modern world.
                  • Modern world: Aside from the comment on numerous Scions there is not much to go on how he regards the modern world. Particularly America, which can be seen as the spiritual successor to Rome.
                • Religion: Now I am not sure on the setting atm, but judging from this I am going to assume that either 1.) the religion surived underground somehow in the age of monotheism. Or 2.) the polythesitic religions of Hellenismos and Religio Romana expericed a huge revival. I really want to know more on how it survived despite the Roman empire stamping it out.
                  • Olympic Games: Nothing mentioned on the Olympic Games which were festivals and games in the ancient world dedicated to Zeus Olympus, and there were other games for other gods such as the Haraea Games for Hera. However being as how big the Olympic games are in the modern world, perhaps a sentence or two about them?
                  • Hellenismos: It mentions two of the major mystery cults as 'religions' without actually mentioning the faith that worships them in the modern world?
                  • Sacrifice: No mention in the Religion section about basically the central act of worship to the theoi(and most polytheistic faiths tbh) of Sacrifice, which includes votive and libations, and perhaps some challenges/non-challanges that faces in this setting.
                • Relationships:
                  • The two relationships: it was a little unclear at first that the two main relationships were the Annua and the Netjer. So perhaps the first sentence about it could read something like: “The Theoi have historical ties to two major pantheons – the Netjer in Egypt and the Annua in the middle east.” ?
                • Motif: A neat little paragraph, though Perhaps this should be at the front of the chapter?

                All in all though, I did like the write up for the Theoi. I would like to see some sample characters here but I assume that comes later?

                        Weighing in late, because of stupid time zone differences and a stupid need to not get fired from my job for following this with the rapt interest that I'm currently following it with.

                        We kind of all have a horse in this race; We're all fans of legend and mythology. That's why we play, and it's really nice for the narrative and the people in it to be as factual as possible. It's cool to interact with a "big guy who can cast lightning". It's really, really cool to be interacting with Thor in all his red-bearded, ham fisted glory. It's more immersive, and you're not thrown out of the moment with jarring inconsistencies.

                        On the other hand Scion, as an RPG, is fan fiction. No pantheon I know of (and I'm happy to be corrected) has stories of progenitor creation-deities waking up/escaping into the modern day with the intention to destroy it all or remake it all in their own image. The moment that's the premise of the game then things are already different to how they factually are. Not to mention doing what any other fan fiction does, and inserting your own character. It's already 'not the same'. It's even less so with your character in it. And some of the modern gods have changed their patterns, behaviours, and in some cases "their thing". Twitch is right; hard core scholars are likely to look funny at you for mixing up Loki and Loge. But Glamourweaver's also got a good point. Loki, on deciding to interact with the world in a more meaningful way, stealing someone else's mantle is in character. So is the thematic fallout at the end of it, where he comes to his usual sticky end for over-reaching.

                        There's some give and take needed. Or some houserules for a reasonably simple fix.

                                                        Regarding the Loki-as-fire-God debate: I distinctly remember reading that the sound of crackling fire had been described as "Loki beating his children" in later folklore.
                                                        Also, check this out:
                                                        "The expressions: "Lokke (Lokki) sår havre i dag" (Lokke (Lokki) sows oats today), or: "Lokke driver i dag med sine geder" (Lokke herds his goats today), are used in several regions of Jutland, for example in Medelsom shire, the diocese of Viborg                                                         etc. ... and stand for the sight in the springtime, when the sunshine generates vapour from the ground, which can be seen as fluttering or shimmering air in the horizon of the flat landscape, similar to the hot steam over a kettle or a burning fire"
                                                        Also, how about Lokabrenna (Loki's bonfire/burning) as an old name for Sirius?
                                                        So, one could say His connection to fire is not entirely baseless.
                                                        After all, in the world of legend binary thinking just doesn't work. Loki travels through air, He changes shape (and sex), He creates chaos that helps support a world order He is not entirely a part of, He is there at humanity's creation and one of the central figures of Ragnarok - why assume Lodur can't be a fire God just because at a certain point He enters a contest with another being representing fire? Whether it was there originally, or developed over time, the association is very much valid.
                                                        Not to mention the fact that His personality and ambivalent role can so easily be symbolized through the beauty and danger of flame,that the metaphor is just irresistible.
                                                        Legend and poetry have much in common, so whatever feels true might very well be true in both.

                                                            As a pagan follower of the Norse gods, I feel like I need to chime in on the Loki fire discussion as well.

                                                            Historical accuracy is nice and everything, but the problem is that it ignores the fact that Norse paganism is still practiced today, and seeing as how we largely had to rebuild our religion from the ground up, it was inevitable that things would be lost in translation and get changed.

                                                            Who cares that the ancient Norse didn't actually associate Loki with fire? Modern Norse pagans do. Times have changed, and if you ask modern pagans, the gods have changed too.

                                                            And what better accomplishment for the God of Lies than to gain a new purview based on a lie. If that's not one of Loki's greatest tricks, then I don't know what else to tell you.
