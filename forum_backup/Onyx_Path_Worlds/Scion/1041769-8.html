    Originally posted by Purple Snit View Post
    Then again, unless you intend to run a game where women, minorities, and LGBTQ characters (and players) are discriminated against, it's probably best to go with the idea that the various deities are open to gay rights, women's equality, and the rights of people from other cultures. We can argue all day about what we believe the Gods and Goddesses would think, but in the end, a) humans have no idea what a deity would really think about these things anyway, because the information we have is all filtered through the originating cultures and then through Christianity/Islam/whichever faith, all of which have their own inherent biases, and b) it's a game, not a religious reference, so do what works for you and your players.
    Do we really want Scion 2nd to state that the Teotl will hate/disown/kill LGBTQ scions? Or that the Aesir only respect "masculine"men? Or that the Chinese and Japanese pantheons hate each other over long-standing cultural problems? For the sake of playability and fun, maybe "accuracy" (whatever that means in this context) needs to be binned in favour of fun and playability.
    ​I suppose it would be cool if Scion 2E at least draws some attention to how the Pantheons might view these issues. But they could do that in a way that looks more like presenting storytelling options, rather than setting down hard-and-fast setting rules.

    ​Instead of saying "The Teotl WILL kill any Scion who isn't 100% hetero on sight!" The books could say "Historically, the Teotl, and their people, had a very dim view of homosexuality, and that could be an element of your story, should you wish it to be."

    ​Different gaming groups may want to do different things, so it's a good idea to present as many options as possible. If you WANT to deal with the social issues of being an LGBTQ Scion in a setting in which several Pantheons may have a problem with that? Then sure, go with the traditional depictions. If you don't want to do such things, just ignore that and make up your own rules. Both play styles are equally valid.

    ​So yeah, drawing the reader's attention to the fact that these issues existed, and may still exist, is a good way to let them know what kind of world they could build for his or her purposes, but it should be made clear that more modern viewpoints can be used as well. It's really all about planning your story and deciding which Scion themes you wish to emphasize within it.

                    Originally posted by Manbat View Post

                    That is a way to go in a game, but I think it might be trick. I mean, if you decide to say that some elements of the legends are not true because they are incomplete or incorrect, this can potentially change all mythology, everywhere. Is interesting, because it bring up some mystery to the players of what is the truth on the myths they already know, but also bring the potential that the legends aren't true, what I feel like invalidating the concept of the game that "the myths are true". I wouldnt go on this route, at least not in something as canon as Hera hatred for Zeus bastards (I think it is said on the myths that she hates him, or at least made very clear), maybe to something smaller like how the Gods made giant animals (not by a wave of hand, but with some special dust or something) but is a way to go, and I can imagine it working very well. Plus, in the game you need to consider things like the Fate, that would try to make the Gods as they were in the legends.
                    Part of the problem with "all myths are true" is the fact that in many cases there are so many different versions of the same myth in the same culture. Most of them started as oral traditions, and as anyone who's played telephone can attest, things get muddied the longer they are passed around. In the myth of Arakhne, everyone agrees that there was a contest that resulted in Arakhne being transformed into a spider, but different versions can't even agree on who won the contest. A person who wished to depict Athena in their games as compassionate might take the tactic of going with the version where Arakhne was devastated by her loss and hung herself, and Athena took pity upon her and made her a spider so that she could weave forever. One who wished to depict Athena as more of a bitch could go with the version where Arakhne wins and Athena throws a tantrum. Arakhne's punishment could be for winning the contest, but it could just as easily be a punishment for hubris and lack of respect for the Gods. It is the same with Herakles. The myths agree that Hera caused a whole lot of trouble for him, but the reason for why the trouble was caused could still be up in the air. Hell, even if she actually did hate Herakles, the reason why she did so could vary. Even if she doesn't hate Herakles and had good intentions, nothing guarantees that Herakles doesn't hold a grudge about it. For a game like Scion, these distinctions are important, but should be decided on a game-by-game basis depending on the needs of the story. If you want the Gods to be respectable, then when a Scion says, "You guys aren't so great. I know your stories, and you did this, this, and this, so spare my your bullshit about needing to act in a virtuous manner," the Gods should be able to respond with, "No, you don't know the full story. Here's what really happened back then..." If you want a game where the Gods are unworthy and the Scions are the only redeeming thing to ever come out of a pantheon, then they instead respond with "So what? You're my kid and you'll do as I say, not as I do." I've had one game where Hera changed sides in the war because she was sick of Zeus cheating on her. Most games, it's more that Zeus starts hitting on the character, character smirks, and Zeus responds with, "She's right behind me, isn't she?" Hera herself in those games tends to be quite benevolent and often proves to be a valuable ally to the player character, despite the fact that the characters are seldom if ever married in a traditional monogamous union (the characters usually being either "not the marrying sort" or the sort that says "If I'm going to get married, I'm going to marry all the men and women that I love. Wanna join?").

                    TLDR: The myths are true, but the version and context of said myths must be decided based upon what's good for each individual game.
