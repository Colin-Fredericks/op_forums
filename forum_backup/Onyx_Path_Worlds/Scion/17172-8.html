                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                    Why can't the RAW 1e model have this Boon? It didn't really do permanents, but 1 Legend to have those effects for a scene is not too resource intensive to maintain. And I've agreed that I think this kind of Boon would be a good thing for the game.
                    Because it currently does not, and I have no interest in making boons for the RAW 1e model. You are free to do so on your own if you wish!
                    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                    What you're not answering is why the Linear Purview is important to this. How does Linear Purviews as you've designed them make this viable? As far as I can tell, it is viable because you've added in an allowance for permanent effect Boons, not because Linear Purviews did anything. The Linear Purview is simply acting as a metric for how powerful it is, which could be accomplished in other methods.
                    I did answer this question already, but I will try to elaborate on my previous answer.

                    A large number of boons are already mechanically attached to epic attribute scaling by virtue of being attached to epic attributes. I took efforts to divorce purviews from epic attributes themselves because it makes no sense to me for (example) every fire god to be charismatic in order to be good with fire. I wanted to enable a wide range of character options which included such things as (example) a strong, witty fire god. Or a nimble, manipulative health god. Or any kind of spellcaster archetype at all where their epic attributes are lower than their purviews, such as the signature character Bridgette from the Scion: God book.

                    I certainly could have gone with a system where Boons were completely self contained and had their own scaling, but that encountered the issue of comparing against static physical defenses, the issue of comparing against static mental defenses (which did not exist but I had every intention of adding into the game), and the issue of not using a mechanic everyone was already familiar with. For me, the path of least resistance for the greatest reward was to use the already existing framework which referenced epic success scaling, and to give purviews their own identical scaling.

                    Coincidentally, there was a group of people running a website called John's Scion Resources who were busy rewriting all their boons to have better epic scaling already, so it was very easy for me to use my new 'Epic Purview' system and just combine them with JSR's boons. So that is what I did for almost two years.

                    Along the way there were a bunch of other core mechanic overhauls. It was not until about eight months(?) ago that I decided to go back and rewrite all the boons in the game and give them more equalized dice bonuses and passive traits. That was actually triggered by Telgar. When he shared a gaming table we exchanged many ideas, agreed on some things, and disagreed on others. One thing we did agree on is that John's Scion Resource heavily favored over-inflated dice pools and unnecessary mechanics that result in too much bookkeeping. My goal for adding those passive traits is to create an even larger number of mechanically viable character concepts.

                    Ultimately, I decided to stick with the 46 success epic scaling metric from the very beginning for a few simple reasons:

                    It was easy. It was effective. It was elegant. It was referencing a mechanic that everyone was already using. It was only a small change for existing players (who are sometimes resistant to change). It was a similar mechanic to attributes for new players (which helps them get into the game faster).
