                One more voice to the thread. Having read it thoroughly, i see where all going in roughly the same direction. I’m going to write my opinión and my main points about the Netjer, but having read all this i’ll write them down more as a way of letting the developers know what i find weird about them than anything else.

                First things first:

                Atamajakki’s point of view fits mine like a glove. Just as she is and says, i’m just an amateur regarding Mythology, but a passionate one when it comes to the Egyptian God and Goddesses.

                At the same time, i’m very aware of the truth spoken by Glamourweaver: this was a religion alive and evolving for more than four thousand years. In that regard, almost everything shifted and changed, and not homogenously. Frequently, the power and position of a God changed with each Nome.

                But, and here i agree with 3Comrades completely, these specific parts chosen to portrait the gods of Egypt feel… wrong in many ways. At least, to me. Is something akin to describing Ares as being an abused child and a shame to the Greeks Gods because Zeus mistreated him as the abusive father he was, or just as Afrodite’s lover. These stances are true, but they are not considered the main description of Ares, God of War.

                And lastly. I’ve loved everything i’ve read about Scion, so far with the exception of this particular Pantheon. For instance, the Tuatha were simply exquisite. (That tiny little bit about pronunciation was a fine delicious detail in particular) I think we have a great game developing in our hands. Let’s give Neall and his team some faith.

                So, my two cents:

                • About the feeling of the Pantheon: yes, they are gods of life and it was considered that Egypt –Kemet- was the Blessed Land, something akin to Paradise on Earth, but they themselves should be, as Atamajkki says, relatively grim and stoic. They all follow Ma’at dictates, and there are very few rules more strict than Ma’at’s.
                • About Ma’at, it always seemed to me that she was more a “it”, and definitely atemporal and unconcerned about humans. In a way, it is something akin to the Rule that keeps the universe going. That’s why the gods seem so focused in making the humans respect it. I’m a little disconcerted by the way is treated here as a goddess. I support wholeheartedly the notion of Ma’at as a Titan, or even a more distant entity. Atamajakki’s explanation fits perfectly with what i know about the subject.
                • And about the gods and goddesses: i too woud have liked too some emphasis in their appearance: the symbolism after the animals whose head they carry is essential for these gods.
                • Seth: Seth was the God pof Potency. He is feared, and while in some periods of Egyptian history it was regarded as more destructive and demonic than just any other God in the pantheon, it was never forgotten. Why? Because his tasks were absolutely vital: first he is the God of the Red Sand, and therefore the master of the desert that surrounds Egypt –he is the violent protector of travelers-; second he is the master of storms (something that in all the history of humanity was a really big point by itself)- the vessel and executor of violence-. And last but no least, as Atmajakki says aswell, he is the most commonly-depicted guardian of the solar barge and his eternity-long fight with Apep. I blinked hard when i read about him being “jovial but with a mean streak”. Jovial? That defintely sounded to me the same as calling Ares jovial. And by the way, why does he have a “friendly competition” with Loki? Seth never was a trickster (in fact, Horus was much more of a trickster than he was –the thingy about lettuce and sperm and all that?-). I don’t understand that at all. [And while the pantheon had historical correlation with others (i could see them having solid ties withthe yazata, and the roman pantheon, for instance) with the Aesir specifically… i don’t think so. I get perfectly that this is a roleplaying game, not a history class, but well, is just that. It feels weird to me.] Giving him Beasts is something i don’t mind, but maybe change them or add “Red Hyppopothamus” to it.
                • Hathor: I can't think of any reason for Hathor to have not made the list, either. I agree wholeheartedly with all the points Atamajakki raises, most specifically with “I'd sooner see her cut entirely than merged with Isis, a combination I'm not familiar with having ever happened historically”. Special emphasis deserves that “if any merger were to happen it would be with Sekhmet, her fellow solar deity and shared holder of the Eye of Ra title.”
                • I cannot agree with Anubis being passionate, at least in the way it is written here.
                • I would not have a duality between Bast and Sekhmet. As is been said, if Sekhmet merges with any other goddess at all, it should be with Hathor. I won’t repeat or elaborate more on these trio, as what has been written sums perfectly how i feel about it.
                • Isis, to me, was always the loving wife. The wife with esoteric knowledge and master of true names, the wife manipulative enough to cheat when Horus and Seth clashed, fighting for the throne, yes, but loving nonetheless. I don’t have any problema with the notion that Isis would not stand something being or going the way she wanted to, but implying that now despises Osiris is beyond me.
                • Speaking about Osiris… everything is been said already.
                • I’d want to emphasize this fragment too: “Duat is absolutely full of demons and other perilous threats, to the point where most of all recorded Egyptian religion recorded is advice on how to safely cross through it and protect yourself from its threats. Duat is a dangerous land of magic and monsters with flaming seas and constant challenges for the dead. The text should reflect any of this.” The Book of Going Into the Light –wrongly but usually called The Book of the Dead- is almost purely that: esoteric formulae and words of power the deceased had to know in order to cross the Duat and appease the guardians of the otherwordly lands.

                And by the way:

                Originally posted by Neall
                I'll give a longer response, but let me clarify one thing - we didn't merge them because of wordcount. I'm sorry I gave that impression. We merged them due to similarity and aspect or because of historical synchretization or trying to look at the gods as singular figures over a period of thousands of years, not because of wordcount. Wordcount is why we chose to put in Ptah instead of Nephthys, for example. Again, sorry about the miscommunication.

                Let me be clear too - I see there's a lot of concern about the Netjer and I share a lot of those concerns. I expect they'll see lots of conversations and redlines coming in the next few weeks and days. I'll probably open develop their second draft for folks so you can see a bit of the process.
                This? This is the best answer we could have. I’ve said it already, but once again thank you for the attention and the will to work so openly and thoroughly, Neall.

                Userhat Aegpts Vlrs. Coren "Ojos de Fuego". Mithrael. Menehet.
