    What should each Purview do in 2e?

    02-18-2014, 12:01 AM
    So, I've been turning this topic in my head for a while. While I'm not sure what's currently being discussed among the Onyx Path people and how Purviews are gonna change, I wanted to start a discussion about the various Purviews as we know them and what sorts of Boons those Purviews should offer, based on what gods with those powers can do in mythology...

    So, I'm gonna start with Fertility, because it's a common associated Purview in divine parents (every pantheon has at least one), it's a cool, mythically thematic concept, and also one that many Scion players dislike because few powers in it are really effective or consistently useful. Also, I personally think that if any Purview needs a rename, this is it, just to clarify that this purview has everything to do with plants and the like, and nothing to do with childbirth, which is over in Health. Perhaps Flora?

    So, the first thing that Fertility gods are in charge of is Agriculture. This is the big'un. Everyone loves you when you're the one providing them with their next meal (or else is VERY eager to appease you if you've the power to take it away). This is the most important role for Fertility gods while simultaneously being the least impressive one to game. Deities like Demeter embodying the harvest, or Quetzalcoatl teaching humans to cultivate plants are highly necessary to ensuring survival of the civilization, and the first three 1e Hero Boons were all about that. And they were also very, very circumstantial and not really worth the XP. However, there's room for growth, thanks to folks like Idunn, the Norse Goddess who tends the golden apples of immortality. Making a huge geographic region flourish with crops or suffer endless blight is one thing, by the time you reach God, you should have the ability to provide divine food, creating crops that change those who eat it.

    From Agriculture, we have those gods who represent Specific Plants. These are those who represent an important plant, usually harvested as a crop, the way that Animal focuses on a specific totem beast. Aztec, Maya, and other Mesoamerican pantheons have several dozen deities who are all about corn and corn alone. The Incas have a number of lesser deities who focus on a specific crop, such as Axomama, Zaramama, Quinoamama, and Cocamama, goddesses of potatoes, maize corn, quinoa, and coca leaves respectively. I think there should be a boon that's essentially a reverse of Protean Understanding for this, wherein your Fertility god chooses a plant of some sort, and gets a sweet bonus when using Fertility Boons on that plant (with a subsequent penalty for all others).

    And speaking of Cocamama, she joins other plant specific deities like Xochipilli with his psychedelic mushrooms and especially Dionysus with his grapes in being gods of Inebriation. Dionysus may be the most popular in this area, but there are many gods of the plants people ingest to alter their states of consciousness. And, from communion wine to peyote ceremonies, drugs have been a key part of religious ceremonies across the globe. There needs to be a way for a Scion to harness that power. Toxic Thorn from Companion is a good start to this (I tried to check, not many poison-based fertility gods, outside of a few myths in Africa and the Amazon. Most poisonous divinities tend to be animalistic, like Serket's scorpions or the Midgard Serpent)

    From here, we're getting to a totally different direction, going from Agriculture to Wilderness. Rather than the gods of human-based crops, these are the gods of Fertility unfettered. This is where we get gods like Pan, Cernunnos, or the orisha Oshossi. These are gods who embody the wild part of nature, the imposing trees, the creeping vines, the thorns, the rashes, the general unpleasantness. This is where Boons like Natural Camouflage belong. Gods who take Fertility travel through the thickets and brambles without harm, evade detection among the greenery, and get people lost in an impenetrable sea of trees.

    And, last, we come to a huge deal with Fertility, one that has no Boons associated with it in RAW. I refer of course, to the Seasonal Cycle. Fertility gods embody the changes in the seasons, lesser deities sometimes having sway only during their assigned time of year. And, of course, the most extreme case of this are the deities that die in the winter and return to life in the spring. Most of the cases where gods are killed and are restored tend to be gods of Fertility. Osiris, Xipe Totec, Tammuz, to a certain extent Persephone, all embody that concept of the god that dies with the crops and is reborn with them. I feel a Boon like GBN's Circle of Life fits that. I also think that there should be ways to reflect seasonality in Boons and also to further embody that tenacity represented by plant life.

    Now, some might think that steps on the toes of Stamina, particularly Ultimate Stamina, but I honestly don't think so. For example, Osiris is a god of Fertility who is restored from death, but does not return to the realm of the living, so he's not rocking Ultimate Stamina. Likewise, Tammuz isn't particularly hardy in Mesopotamian myth, yet he spends half the year in the underworld to represent winter. And on the other hand, Odin and Shango both have famous myths where they kill themselves and return to life, but they're not Fertility figures in the slightest. However, certain figures clearly do have both, like Xipe Totec stoically flaying himself along with the rebirth cycle, or Dionysus being able to hold amazing amounts of liquor. Deities like that should be able to rely on both Stamina and Fertility to do their job.

    So, that's one Purview, I'd post more, but long post is long. Please, discuss, add more ideas as to what you think Purviews in 2e should accomplish, or criticize mine. When I next get the opportunity, I'll discuss Health

                                                          That's because of a fundamental difference between Scion and "Fantasy" RPGs. In most Fantasy games, "Magic" is a thing that only SOME characters do. In Scion, ALL characters have magic, do magic and are magic. You want to throw fireballs? Learn Fire. Raise Zombies? Death. Summon beasts? Animal. Waterbend like Katara? Water. Smite people with lightning? Sky.

                                                          You can do everything a typical fantasy wizard can do, but it's not in one "Wizard" set of powers. Everyone in Scion is a wizard. You can't specialize in magic, except by specializing in Purviews. Admittedly, the system does make it hard to bypass Epic Attributes because they are hands-down BETTER than Purviews and are, in fact, REQUIRED to use Purviews on-level. So, there are some flaws (big ones, tons of them), but not being able to play a magician isn't one.
