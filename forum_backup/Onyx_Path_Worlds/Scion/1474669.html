    Pantheons & Ethnicities

    02-14-2022, 09:26 PM
    Should I assume that any character from original ethnicity of Panteon is under this Pantheon godly 'jurisdiction'?

    For example, Netjar of Egypt are connected to Middle Eastern people - even if majority of people there are monotheists following Islam. So do all Islam worshiping people come under Netjar preview? Do Anubis have claim on them after death? What about people worshiping some Netjar - like maybe Horus - but not acknowleging Anubis as their Death God, only Osiris. Would they come under jurisdiction of Anubis, even contrary to their belief?

              I wouldn't handle it using ethnicity or geography; I'd handle it using worship. Think of it as a very mild form of Fatebinding: if you become actively involved in the Netjer religion, then your fate gets bound to that Pantheon and when you die, your soul goes to its Underworld. If your fate gets bound up with multiple Pantheons, well, that's one reason why the Pantheons have Gods of the Dead: if the Theoi and Netjer both have claims on your soul, then Hades and Anubis get to negotiate over which one gets it.

              My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                              My head-Canon is that an Abrahamic “Pantheon” does exist, even if not detailed in any official material (though I have that in quotes because with the advent of Dragon and Masks of the Mythos, I'm debating whether Abrahamic concepts should be forced into the same mold as the Pantheons or if Legend should be replaced by a more suitable power stat; but that's something to be debated elsewhere), and that the Abrahamic gatekeeper(s?) for the souls of the dead would be involved in deciding the fate of a person's soul. With that in mind, my answer to “where does the soul of the Egyptian Muslim go?” is that the respective Abrahamic and Netjer psychopomps responsible for dealing with the souls of the dead would decide that among themselves on a case by case basis. There is no automated resolution to this; it's something that the respective “Gods of the Underworld” need to work out among themselves: who has the stronger claim on this soul?

                              That is, after all, a basic truth of the Scion setting: the Gods run reality; and how reality works is through the will of the Gods.

                                          The impression I get is that it'd be possible for our Egyptian Muslim to end up in Duat (or for a Greek Christian to end up in Elysium, or w/e), but that the default assumption of the story is that if this were to happen, it would indicate that something had gone terribly wrong, and is not the proper course of events. Ending up in an afterlife outside of your faith would imply some sort of meddling from supernatural sources, and while you might get something like, "Look, Bob, I know you were expecting the Pearly Gates but your family's been in my good graces for a thousand years and you did burn an offering for me that one time when you were eight; I can send you back to The World if you can do a solid for me. No other strings attached, and you can see St. Peter next time you step into traffic," you might also have rulers of the dead drawing in the dead of a rival pantheon--or deflecting their own away--for some reason.

                                          Other folks have pointed out the very real and real-world dangers of lashing ethnic identity too tightly to a religion. There's stories to be told, but they need to be handled with the right spirit.
