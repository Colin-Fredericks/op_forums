    An alternate Olympus/Dodekatheon/Theoi

    04-22-2017, 12:40 PM
    So, in Greek Mythology, Gaia, a little annoyed at her son Kronos for castrating his dad Ouranus, told him that one of his kids would supplant him the same way. So he ate them all, until Zeus got swapped out for a rock and grew up to fulfill the prophecy. Flash forward and, again annoyed for reasons, Gaia tell Zeus that the kid he's about to have with Metis will supplant him. So he decides to outdo his own dad and just eat Metis before she even gives birth. And a bit later, a fully formed and grown up Athena pops out of Zeus's skull.

    My thought is that, sometime in, say the middle ages, Athena finally fulfills her destiny and takes over the Greek pantheon. Zeus, Hera, Hades, and maybe Demeter and Poseidon are imprisoned in Tartarus. Ares, a loyalist and never liked by Athena, is banished from Olympus and forced to walk the earth. Aphrodite, also having a mutual hate-on with the new queen, also leaves.

    So, the current pantheon (a Dekatheon, if you will) looks like:
    • Athena, Tyrant of Olympus, Goddess of Wisdom, Craft, and War, Ruler of the Heavens and wielder of both the divine thunderbolt and the aegis. She considers the realms of Science and Technology to fall under her purview. A rather cold-blooded and super-logical dictator who is always right and willing to commit numerous brutalities in the name of the "greater good" of order and progress. The sort of leader who'd fit in perfectly well in 1984, Brave New World, or some similar dystopia.
    • Apollo, the Sun King, God of Truth, Prophecy, Music, Art, Medicine and Light, the platonic consort of Athena. Like Athena, he's also a patron of modern science. Very much the ideal of ordered creation in rejection of unrestrained chaos (drawing heavily on the theories of Camilla Pagilia and Fredrick Nietzsche). Attended by the Nine Muses.
    • Persephone, Goddess of the Earth, producer and destroyer of all living things, keeper of the Underworld and jailer of Tartarus, having originally transcended the role of daughter by becoming the willing wife of Hades, she has now transcended that role by becoming a dark Queen in her own right.
    • Hermes, God of Transitions and Boundaries, Messenger of Olympus, patron and overseer of business (including organized crime, modern professional sports, politics and mass media) travel and the Internet. A rogue and one of the few willing to speak truth to the power of Athena.
    • Artemis, Goddess of the Wild, the Hunt, Childbirth and of Youth, she now also serves as the Assassin of Olympus, charged with hunting and slaying enemies of Athena. Her loyalty to the regime is primarily a personal one to her brother.
    • Hephaestus, God of the Forge, Metal, Fire and Invention, the chief engineer and armorer of the gods, also holding modern technology as part of her purview. As always, he remains hopelessly in love with Athena, who is unwilling (if not unable) to reciprocate.
    • Hestia, Goddess of Hearth and Home, and of the State. The only remaining member of the six original gods, both because she is loyal to Olympus itself and not individual rulers, and because as the oldest child of Kronos and Rhea and essentially the goddess of the foundation of human civilization, it is feared that her personal power may be too great to challenge.
    • Dionysus, God of Ecstasy, Fertility, Tragedy and Comedy, the Devil's Advocate of an Olympus devoted to order. Almost the antithesis of Apollo, it's almost shocking how well the two seem to get along with each other. This, combined with his personal power and continued popularity in the mortal world, are why Athena tolerates him.
    • Amphitrite, Goddess of the Seas, who helped overthrow and imprison her husband Poseidon in order to claim her long denied birthright as the daughter of Oceanus.
    • Hecate, Goddess of Crossroads and Magic, Protection and Prosperity, who serves Olympus for reasons known only to herself, but also out of friendship with Persephone, Artemis, Hermes and Hestia.
    I'm sort of torn on what to do with Herakles and Hebe, as they could work as part of the above group, but I also sort of see them as being loyalists to Zeus.

    Anyway, I just thought it was an interesting idea. Perhaps as part of a setting in which the Aesir have already been through Ragnarok, with Baldr as their leader, and Modi, Magni, Thrud, Vitar, and Hodr as the main gods. And maybe a version of the Aztecs fused with Mexican-Spanish Catholicism.

                                  Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                                  Or is it? As far as I know it's exact wording wasn't part of the story. Could be the "son" element was a sexist assumption.
                                  There are a few different prophecies regarding The Son of Zeus, but the main focus is that it is a son specifically. Since No One Of Consequence's neat little write up here seems to be going with the Metis side of things, then the exact lines are,

                                  Originally posted by Hesiod: The Theogony
                                  For from Metis it was destined that clever children should be born: first a pale-eyed daughter Tritogeneia [Athena], with courage and sound counsel equal to her father's, and then a son she was to bear, king of gods and men, one proud of heart. [Emphasis mine]
                                  So, the prophecy regarding the Son of Zeus does specifically mention it being a son, and also actually mentions that it is not Athena. But, there are totally ways around this that we have discussed on various threads here a whole ton.

                                  Just on some more discussion of the setting though, I can see Poseidon and Hades both getting roped into supporting Zeus during the war due to the theoretical equality of authority they have, though Zeus tends to forget this and place himself as the king of all. I would say that, according to the pattern of these stories, Athena probably had support from the Titans and broke them free of the prison.

                                  When Zeus rebelled, he broke the Hundred Handed Ones, and the Primordial Cyclopses, and the Giganties from the pit to support him against Cronus, as Cronus had imprisoned them just as Ouranos had done in the past. Following this system, Athena probably jailbroke the Titans, sealed away the original turncoat Titans, and imprisoned the Hundred Handed Ones, the Cyclopses, and the elder Theoi. In doing this, Gaia will speak a prophecy against her that her son shall overthrow her, and be a greater King than she. Gaia in general doesn't want any of her kiddos imprisoned, and that thus far has been the bane of the Greek Deities, the inability to forgive each other, mostly because if they didn't imprison one and other, they would constantly be trying to counter-revolt.

                                  So, Cronus is probably hanging around as an advisor to Athena, same with the other big Titans. Prometheus too most likely. I can see Hecate having turned coat again, since her primary interest seems to be getting on the winning side.

                                          I'll note that a large amount of inspiration for this comes from reading George O'Connor's incredibly amazing series of graphic novels, The Olympians.
