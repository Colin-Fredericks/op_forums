                        I'm not trying to derail this thread, so I'll keep my response as brief as possible.                                     I hope they have sat down and brainstormed on exactly that. Isn't it going to require a whole lot of re-thinking to include all the powerful Pantheons that the game will center around? How different is a Middle East without Islam as almost the only surviving dominant faith? Do the Netjer fill that void? The Anunnaki? The Alihah? Or Western Europe or North America, where the Norse, Bogovi, Tuatha, Theoi, Finnish, Algonquian, Inue, Anasazi, Coast Salish, Tarascan, Olmec, Maya, Aztec, etc. Pantheons are still vibrant and alive, in place of the near-exclusivity of Christianity and Judaism? China and Japan? Aboriginal Australia? The Incans, Moche, Guarani, and their kin? Makemake on Easter Island? It requires a huge rethinking anyway, which is part of the fun - the whole "what if" world-building of it. And including the Abrahamic faiths, just as a way of minimizing having to make change in the world history, seems like a lazy way out that I don't think Neall and the others would take. The point is to write your own myths, in the spirit of the myths of our own ancient world, using the Pantheons of Old as your source, not to have "the modern world as it is with a few odd cults of pagans worshiping nearly-dead myths."
