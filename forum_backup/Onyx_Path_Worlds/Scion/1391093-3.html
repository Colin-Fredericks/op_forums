        Originally posted by Watcher View Post

        Ragnarok is an interesting book? Like, I highly suggest people read it to get an understanding of how the Developers of 1e imagined full Legend 1 - 12 Campaigns to go. What I find so interesting about it is that the... the image the Devs had of how a game of Scion would 'work' was totally different than every campaign and game of 1e I ever ran or played in, and I played once or twice a week for six years.

        It has some... fictional elements, but thankfully nothing as flat out offensive as seen in other books. IIRC they actually fuck up the order of events of Ragnarok which helps throw off your players, but also... I mean that's not how Wyrd works? The order of events of Ragnarok are being enforced with the same force of the actual conclusions. There is also a really interesting focus the writers had on trying to make the events of Ragnarok semi-scientific which is interesting, again, from a like... designer perspective of how people would play their game.

        Oh! There's this weird thing where Hel becomes beautiful with Baldur showing up which is like... that's got some implications. And the writers really cast Nana in a super negative light.

        If you aren't interested in the history and developmental growth of Scion, I would say it is a sort of 'meh' read? But, like, if you are interested in the weird and finicky and development and growth of Scion, totally check it out. Just, bring a critical eye to it.
        My friends and I got Ragnarok and I decided to run the campaign setting almost beat for beat. I mean, I tweaked the story here and there. I added some new stuff along the way. But the campaign was pretty much the spine of the entire game.

        It's not a coincidence that my friends still say that that was our best campaign ever.

            Originally posted by Zealot_Vedas View Post
            I'm synthesizing stuff from both editions together, personally. I, much like you, like a full-scale divine conflict to provide a backdrop, but I much prefer the depictions of both the titans and the gods in 2e.

            My work around is that the conflict isn't ALL titans vs. ALL gods, but there is a large scale war erupting, with a single Big Bad having rallied a bunch of the more destructive/sinister titans, monsters, and renegade gods from multiple mythologies under his banner, to try and impose a new cosmic hierarchy on the World. This attempt at totalitarian cosmic conquest is what has brought the pantheons together, including any titanic allies they maintain good relations with. I haven't figured out who I want as that Big Bad though.
            This. This is what I would be going for with my concept of a Titanomachy, and what I suspect the writers have in mind with the Titanomachy book. Such a scenario would provide boundless opportunities to have figures from multiple pantheons interact, which is a major draw of Scion for me.
