    Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
    Ancient peoples waged war on each other all the time while believing in gods including those who may favor their enemies. Why would that be different in the modern world?

    Why are you assuming gods would take notice of such wars beyond expecting heroism on the part of their Scions?

    I'm assuming based on individual cases in accordance with how mythology viewed the behavior of such figures. Real world mythology likes to paint the Theoi as overreacting to every slight ever made against them, and they were extremely sore losers. While there have been cases in which the pantheon is depicted as losing a competition that they are supposed to be the pinnacle of excellence at, these stories always resulted in the God throwing a tantrum and the one responsible for beating them suffers a fate worse than death!


    And as for the Aztecs, if the "All Myths Are True" aspect is what we're going with, then they're telling the truth in that they NEED human sacrifices in order to stop the world from ending. How are they going to do that if the Europeans show up on the Aztec's doorstep and start conducting mass genocide on their entire culture, while forcing the survivors to convert to Christianity? If nobody's performing sacrifices anymore, then that means the Aztec apocalypse is upon us!



    And then we have the Loa, who's mythology, as I understand it, blatantly claims that Gods are constantly interacting with mortals on an almost daily basis. Heck, they even possess people's bodies and take them out on joy rides!


    History in the real world turned out the way that it did because we know better now. There were no gods to answer our prayers and aid us when the enemy was kicking down the gates. But in the world of Scion, the Gods are very much real, they are very much paying attention, and they will very much take exception to seeing the cultural systems they spent thousands of years influencing being wiped out by interference from foreign powers.

                    I look at it through a distinction between History and Mythology.

                    Gods care about Purviews, Virtues and Fatebinding. At what point is direct intervention for the sake of Purviews or Virtues worth the risk of Fatebinding?

                    The answer is never. Myths already exist to tell mortals how they should act and why, and the supernatural has enough presence in The World to remind mortals that they should respect the Gods even if they don't affect their daily lives. Slavery still happens, wars still happen, epidemics still happen, technology still happens, because that is the course of History, which is the domain of mortals, not Gods, except its trappings are superimposed with a layer of Mythology because of the Gods' influence.

                    Like, Ireland still fought during World War I, but instead of looking like this, the recruitment posters looked like this, because when the Morrigan wants dead bodies, she's getting dead bodies one way or the other.

                            Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post

                            History in the real world turned out the way that it did because we know better now. There were no gods to answer our prayers and aid us when the enemy was kicking down the gates. But in the world of Scion, the Gods are very much real, they are very much paying attention, and they will very much take exception to seeing the cultural systems they spent thousands of years influencing being wiped out by interference from foreign powers.
                            First, I find the way you phrase that to be a bit dismissive (if not insulting) towards people who have believed in God(s), both past and present. Saying “we know better now” implies that we have systematically disproven any form of the Divine, and there are a lot of religious people in the world today that would be offended at that suggestion (at least in relation to their own religion).
                            Back to the topic at hand: Consider Sarpedon in the Iliad. He was a son of Zeus fated to die in battle. Zeus considered stepping in and saving his life anyway. He is convinced not to on the grounds that the other Gods might do the same for their favored children. That was during a “family squabble”. How much more care would be needed when there is potentially apocalyptic warfare at risk? If your kid and the neighbor kid are having a fist fight in the street, how much good is it going to do either of them (or the neighborhood) if you and the other kid’s parent come out and whip out machine guns, or flamethrowers, or rocket launchers?
                            So, how did the Gods influence history then?
                            Loki: “Hey guys, there’s a bunch of Christians coming, and when they say convert or die, they’re serious. I don’t like them. No, no, put your swords down. I have a better idea. Pretend to convert. Then you can keep doing things properly when they’re not looking, and laugh at them behind their backs.”
                            When the Library of Alexandria burned, Scions and trusted worshipers were already transporting the most important and sacred texts to a secret and secure location.
                            That’s the sort of thing that differed. When a bunch of people started screaming for the slaughter of those who wouldn’t convert, the Gods didn’t gear up and head out to crush the foolish mortals and silently hope that their enemies didn’t also have divine protection. No, they helped their most critical people escape, along with sacred objects or teachings. Rather than starting massive god wars that would destroy much of humanity and leave the World a wasteland, they let the mortals fight their own battles with Scions as their protectors. They didn’t worry about whether their people were dominant, only that they would survive to birth the next generation and pass down their important teachings. The fact that the World has noticeable populations of people that passed down the old religions in an unbroken line isn’t a tiny change. It’s fucking huge!
                            So why do we still have large populations of monotheistic religions? Because people are people. It only takes the right argument to convince someone. “If your Gods are so mighty, why haven’t they stopped me from doing this to you? Clearly it’s because they fear the might of my God, which is far greater than they could ever hope to match!” “Sure, your ‘god’ is real, but they’re not really a God. Mine is, and if you don’t convert, well, I’m sure you’ll have plenty of company in the eternal fiery tortures that await you in Hell.” Monotheism also has the benefit of exclusivity. The followers of Dionysus don’t care if you go to Christmas mass with your family. What would the church group think of your plan to attend a ritual celebration for Dionysus, though? When you only honor one God, it cuts off potential worship from others.
                            Wow, I’m really rambling on at this point. I’m going to stop myself here for now.

                                        You never stop to think about those footnotes in the news. "A local girl was attacked by a demon wolf. Authorities have assured that a child of Thor is seeking it out. The girl is recovering in a hospital." This is something that sort of gets thrown in at the end of the broadcast and you pay enough attention to realize it was in a different neighborhood far away from your part of the millions of people in this city. Then you forget about it and go on with your daily life.

                                        If it's something bigger, like an active monster rampage or zombie uprising? That'll get more details and calls for caution in the affected areas. You'll cover your mouth and gasp or cross your arms and frown. You'll talk about "those poor people" or "who let this happen?" or something else. If it is something pleasant or good, like a kitsune marriage or a blessing from a djinn, you might feel a little jealous. But then the event ends and in a week or two it becomes a faded memory for you. Like the tornado that didn't hit your town or the war in foreign lands that you're not fighting. You just tend not to think about it.

                                        At least until you're part of the footnote. I mean, if you're that college student that took a wrong turn into a satyr kegger, or that actress that got cursed when her agent toyed with Egyptian sorcery, or that girl who survived an attack by a demon wolf. If you're that person. Then everything's changed.

                                        We always forget that it's not a footnote to that person until we are that person.

                                        -------------------------------
                                        Yeah, I like the fringes thing. I'd venture to say that the supernatural population of the world is probably in the six digits which a lot less than 1%. Dispersion would keep them outliers rather than full on minorities. There would be Terra Incognita where people have day-to-day lives with satyr post-men and angelic police officers (for a mixed pantheon incognita) and a few overworld shards, and these might very well have large populations of their own, but they're not going to be interacting with Earth usually.

                                        On Earth, one college campus somewhere might have a collection of satyr descended denizens that goes off and parties in a tiny terra incognita somewhere, but I'd be surprised if there were more than three such things so a mortal stumbling on to it would have to be pretty dang unlucky (or lucky depending).

                                        Very different from Bright.

                                        In The World, you can talk about your great-great-grandmother, the dryad, and most people would be like "oh, interesting trivia about you." and then maybe forget it. It's like me saying "I descend from one of the guys who signed the Declaration and Constitution." There are millions of people who can say the same thing and it's not like they personally had any part of that event so it doesn't really say anything about them. (Though, admittedly, a dryad might still be around to talk to).

                                        Or for a better example.

                                        I can say "I live in Japan" where there are currently millions of other people living, including quite a few foreigners like myself. I exist. People who live in Japan exist. But I imagine the majority of Americans or Englishmen or South Africans or so don't think about that most days. Most of the time Japan, or a lot of other foreign countries and even distant cities in your own country, enjoy at best a sort of "I know exists" sort of place in a person's thoughts. And "people who live in Japan" is population size that is higher than The World's population of supernaturals by several orders of magnitude.

                                                Taken from a design perspective.

                                                It makes the most sense to assume divine cancels out divine resulting in basically the same history but with certain legends actually being real supernatural events. Like say the Beast of Gevauden really being a spawn of Fenris or something similar.

                                                Because this creates the easiest platform for people to mod off of.

                                                If you take the default setting too far into Alternate History territory then you make it a bit less accessible.

                                                Most urban fantasies follow the same conceit that, despite the presence of real supernatural things, that history mostly went the way the real world history went.

                                                Dresden Files, Supernatural, Harry Potter, Young Wizards, Buffy/Angel, etc...they mostly don't toy with history and just focus on the supernatural elements.

                                                If you want to toy with alternate histories, go for it. But it's not the ideal for the default setting.
