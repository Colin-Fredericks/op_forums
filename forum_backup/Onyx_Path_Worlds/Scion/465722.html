    New Storyteller Here!

    04-02-2015, 02:37 AM
    Hey guys, so I've recently become very interested in Scion and I've read almost all of Hero, the Companion, and most of Demigod and Ragnarok. Tonight I finally proposed playing the game to my friends and they want to give it a try. I'm excited at the prospect, however I've never actually DM'd or acted as a Storyteller before so I don't know what to expect. Are there any pointers that you guys might have for me in this endeavor based on your own experiences? Should I stick to the Adventures written up in the Core book or try to make my own? Is a single-pantheon game easier or better than a mixed-pantheon game? Any additional information you'd like to add? Thanks!

          My best advice would be to be prepared for how out-of-control it can get. Even beginning Hero characters can be wildly different, and it only gets moreso as the game progresses. This can lead to trouble challenging the group, when you have a group of people invested heavily in different attributes. A challenge that can harm your group tank could possibly annihilate the rest of the party, and a scenario where your Epic Social character has to shine and convince a business magnate to help them out when another rival social character is trying to do the opposite leaves the group's physical types twiddling their thumbs and with no chance to help. All things being well your group will be happy to step back and let the expert in the current situation step up and manage things but it can get tricky in designing conflicts.

          And my favourite piece of advice, which translated over from advice I recall from a ST for Exalted, is that when your players tell you they want to do something outlandish or unlikely the appropriate answer is always "Yes. Now how do you plan to do it?" and let them work toward those way out goals, no matter how unbalancing they may seem (and man does the game get unbalanced and even more out of control at demigod!).

          As regards multi-pantheon or not, your mileage may vary. I've had great experiences with a single-pantheon game (we ran through Ragnarok) and great experiences with a multi-pantheon group where their differences made it harder for the Titans to single out a 'weakness'. That's up to you

          In playing, if you wanted to run the core book adventures you might find that the Long Road to Heaven adventures are pretty broken in places and need some fixing, and that some of the signature characters in the Hero book are pretty awfully thought out and put together (Eric Donner, for example, has trouble hitting any damn thing with his handgun) but it's a nice framework and structure to try a new game out on. Some of the officially published SAS adventures are good, and better written if you wanted to take a look at them. Otherwise, if you have a great idea, go to it. If you're not sure if the scale of the threats are right maybe playtesting them in a mock-battle might help you check out how the group will fare.

          Overall though, as long as you and your group are having fun it's all good

              Originally posted by thedonnie View Post
              The most important lesson that you want to learn about being an ST is this; Listen to the players. If the players like action? Then do not make a mystery based chronicle. If they want to be multiple pantheon, then you will be running a multi-pantheon game. Once you talk to them and get a general idea of what they want, then you can plan out the chronicle and go from there.
              Caveat: Don't run a game YOU don't want to just because the players want it. It's a team effort and you're on the team too. In fact, you're like a player/manager in baseball, you don't just manage it, you are IN it. Moreover, you are the one who will be doing the MOST work on the game as well, so it has be something you want to pour your soul into. So, like Augustus, you are prime inter pares, first among equals. Be flexible, but don't be jello-flexible.

              Another caveat: This also refers to players. It is a team. If you have people who want to play REALLY incompatible characters, or someone wants to play a character that would be the kind of person everyone else in the group would hate, revile and oppose in every way possible, there is going to have to be some negotiation.

              Caveat 3: And this REALLY applies in Scion if you are going to be running the Virtues as written. An Aztec Scion with Conviction 5 can be a very scarey being indeed. (Again, how many hearts do I need to cut out to make sure the sun comes up tomorrow?) Having people in your band with Courage 5, you need to expect them to get into a lot of fights and even be seeking out fights when it is absolutely unwise for them to do so. A sniper character with Valor is going to have some problems from the get-go. You can handwave or partially handwave the Virtues to minimize this, get the players on board with creative work-arounds (like the sniper who fires a warning shot) AND just realizing that the two hot-heads who love their high Courage are going to be doing that stuff and having tons of fun with it.

                                          The adventures in the corebook trilogy are not great. I say this having written one of them. :-(

                                          And Telgar's right: The system starts showing strain even at Legend 4. (It did with my campaign.)

                                          Other advice: Prep characters and critters in advance. Just writing up NPCs and creatures is good practice for working with the system, and when the players take the adventure into unexpected territory (it *will* happen -- frequently), you'll have something ready to pull out and use. When you create adventures, don't get too attached to the thought of them reaching particular conclusions. Supply a goal to give the players a direction for their characters, but don't insist on it: If the PCs want to do something else, roll with it. Nobody likes being railroaded. Some of my best adventures came just from setting up a situation and letting the PCs react to it.

                                          Dean Shomshak
