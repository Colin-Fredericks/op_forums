            I knowing this, as conqueror have won my husband for mine own.

            2 I am the banner and the head, a mighty arbitress am I:
            I am victorious, and my Lord shall be submissive to my will.

            3 My Sons are slayers of the foe, my Daughter is a ruling Queen:
            I am victorious: o’er my Lord my song of triumph is supreme.

            4 Oblation, that which Indra gave and thus grew glorious and most high,—
            This have I offered, O ye Gods, and rid me of each rival wife.

            5 Destroyer of the rival wife, Sole Spouse, victorious, conqueror,
            The others' glory have I seized as ’twere the wealth of weaker Dames.

                                                        A New Nat:

                                                        Badr al-Adin Auliya
                                                        Aliases: Buddaw Auliah Nat, Madda, Pir Badr, Shaha Badar Alam

                                                        Across the ports of the Arakan State are the shrines of Badr al-Adin Auliya, all constructed from bare rock. The Bengalis say that he came to Chittagong, a city infested with evil spirits to bring the good word of the Most Merciful. Through his teachings, he warded off the demons from the city and freed it from their torment. For his miracles, his name reached not only to the Muslim devotees in Bengal but to the Hindu and Buddhist devotees as well. Such power brought his influence within the court of the Burmese Nat, something he has never considered.

                                                        It was said that in Akyab (now known as Sittwe), a merchant named Manick dreamt that Pir Badr demanded him to build a maqam for him. To comply with Badr's demand, the saint offered Manick to transform his boats of turmeric into gold. Seeing the next morning that all the turmeric had been transformed into gold, the merchant and his brother sought a cave and dig a well as a station for the saint. From then on, Badr’s miracles protect his worshippers from storms and floods. They pray to him for protection through their journey.

                                                        Pir Badr enjoys the company of other multi-loyal gods, Quetzalcoatl, Mithras, Guanyin and a number of Orishas. Perhaps his closest is the fellow sea goddess, Manimekhala. Like her, he has garnered attention from Southeast Asia, on a scale never imagined. Truly, he serves no one, not the Nat or the Deva but Allah, the Most High. He appears in the world on rare occasions, driving out unruly deities and spirits from mortal presence. When it came to animosity between regions that worshiped him, Pir Badr once lent his hand to placate them. Now, the stations of Buddaw Auliah are silent. He chooses no priests or brings no dreams. The Saint has forsaken all of them, they say.

                                                        Scions of Badr are the only source of communication that worshipers have with their Saint. They are committed to their causes, ones that their patron has ordained. The Arakanese employ their skills in resisting black magic and curses from enemies. They are excellent in manipulating the arcane as expected of his Scions. His Chosen are one of the best manipulators of sorcery among the Nat. They work their skills into miracles, warding off evil by invoking the power of the Immutable One.

                                                        Callings: Guardian, Healer, Sage
                                                        Purviews: Beasts (Fish), Health, Journeys, Moon, Sky, Water

                                                        Minor Nat

                                                        Taungdaw Thakinma
                                                        Aliases: Me Oo

                                                        Miss Me Oo was the first Incarnation of Taungdaw Thakinma, the guardian goddess of Tha-Kin-Ma mountain. Pursued by the Shwe Hpying Siblings, Me Oo rejected their advances for she had already been in love with Ko Yin Maung, a handsome woodsman in her village. Drawing the ire of two rejected gods, they set a tiger to devour her. They dragged her to the Tha-Kin-Ma mountain, her blood transforming into the first tharaphi flowers. The mauled maiden became Taungdaw Thakinma. After hearing news from the village that Me Oo had been punished, Ko Yin Maung searched for her in the forests, eventually dying of a broken heart. She transformed him into a Nat, the protector of woodsmen and foresters. Their village commemorated their tragic fates, building a Nat-house for them.

                                                        Taungdaw Thakinma remains celebrated on her mountain, her worship localized to the area. She still maintains an ire for the two gods who killed her. Shwe Hpying Siblings and their Scions have a hard time entering her worldly domain. The only day she makes a truce with them is on Taungbyon Festival, the largest Nat festival in all of Myanmar. As a patron goddess of women and their careers, she fights actively for their well-being in the modern world. Her Incarnations have an inner drive for equality and social justice. Most of them come from rural backgrounds. They are activists, protestors, and freedom fighters. She is also a patron goddess of weaving, trading her skills with other goddesses, particularly Dewi Sri and Lakshmi.

                                                        Callings: Creator, Guardian, Lover
                                                        Purviews: Forge, Earth
