    Originally posted by Ajax View Post
    I believe, if I am reading you correct, we are in complete agreement about cross-mythos interactions. There aren't any myths of multi-pantheon divine summits, but they almost certainly took place in the world of Scion. Unless the pantheon is really solipsistic, they are going to know that those guys over there worship somebody different and have different overworlds, underworlds etc.

    You CAN'T give a detailed description. Myths and religions and all are confusing things that create moving targets that are hard to pin down. Even when you have a LOT to work with, like, say the Greeks or the Hindu, you still have to settle on things and exclude others. It's an editorial choice or its unworkable chaos.

    You can say that pantheons will not be covered for practical reasons. Like "They won't all fit into the books" or "We don't have money to pay people" or "Sure, fans can do it, but it won't be "official" because we're not having an open license for this game for various money related reasons". To say you CAN'T create pantheons for groups that have robust mythologies that just aren't well known is intellectual laziness... Why not? Because we're not going to plumb those myths for 12 gods, some monsters, whatever is replacing PSPs, and some magic items and a signature character or four? It's possible to put together a working pantheon for a game night in a few hours if it's going to see limited use. It's not too hard for a full on gaming company to do it up much better with the resources of several writers, a line developer, etc. It's just whether or not it's worth it. Nothing should be tossed for the sake of convenience. If it can't be done NOW, it shouldn't just be consigned to never-never land. "Not only are we not detailing the pantheon of the Asmat/Chumash/Lenape/Saami/Waka/Athapaskan/Basque, WE NEVER WILL. We're just ignoring them and consigning them to Oblivion?" Why?

    As for, "We're not covering the Olmecs and Toltecs and Moche" Of course they're not. At best, they can make them up, pretty much out of whole cloth. There's no there there. No mythology. It's archaeology. It's art. It's like the so-called "Nemetondevos". You've got a handful of inscriptions, some art and a little bit of mention that's pretty much loaded with syncretism to the point of uselessness. And, for those tribes, you really have to go with just the art and archaeology. Sure, you can create it all and dress it up to make it look cool and ancient, but it's pretty much almost as fictional as the Atlantean pantheon, the Cthulhu pantheon or the Narnia pantheon.
    We are in agreement about the cross-pantheon stuff, absolutely. As for the rest, sure it's possible to come up with 12 gods and some other stuff for a given group - my point is, it isn't necessarily a requirement to make the game work. As long as "build your own" guidelines are in there, then the company doesn't need to make them all, and it isn't likely they would all get used anyway. Not because they don't matter, but because it's hair-splitting in most cases.

                                                    Honestly, I've always said that the Abrahamic pantheons don't belong in Scion. First, because they are part of the real-world faiths that tend to get really hot under the collar when you try to personify them, and second, because it's a game of the ancient religions and myths, not modern ones. They aren't needed in order to make it a dynamic, immersive, and enjoyable fictional world, and trying to work out who to include, how to deal with "Thou shalt have no other Gods", and how to try to shoehorn real-world faith alongside ancient faith [really, would Islam have allowed the Alihah to continue? Or Christianity to tolerate "pagan" faiths? They didn't in the real world], just make a whole lot of extra headaches for everyone, If you want to house rule Abrahamic faiths, go for it. But my vote is, keep them out of the official releases.
