    Attempting to 'fix' firearms by changing combat

    01-25-2015, 01:10 AM
    I think we can all agree that firearms are extremely underpowered in Scion as we move on to Demigod level Stamina. Whether that is a bad thing or not is entirely up to each group for itself, but there have been some cool ideas for fixes, mostly adding either Perception or Dexterity to the damage roll. To me, neither method works really well because on one hand it would make Dexterity a much more valued Attribute in combat and on the other Perception just feels wrong (can't really explain why, but seeing your target better for extra damage is just somehow off).

    In any case, I wanted to throw in my own idea and hopefully get some feedback. Some of you will hate it, some of you might like it, but I want to know what you think and if you spot some possible pitfalls that I have missed. Two brains think better than one. As for balance... well, Scion is not very particularly balanced, now is it?

    My idea is to ditch the "Dexterity for attacking" mechanic and replace it with a new value, which I am currently calling "Attack". Attack is the average value of Strength and Dexterity ((STR+DEX)/2) and is only used in place of Dexterity in making attacks. Attack does not have its own Epic Attack, but rather uses the average bonus successes of the two Attributes mentioned (not the average Epic Attribute rating). In all other respect, Attack works as if it were an Attribute. An attack is made using Attack+Ability+Accuracy. Grappling no longer allows you to either use DEX or STR to make the attack roll.

    Damage is made using the appropriate Attribute: Strength for Brawl, Melee and Thrown attacks, and Dexterity for Marksmanship attacks. Appropriate Epic Attribute adds bonuses as normal. This allows Dexterity to be used for damage rolls with firearms without making it super valuable.

    Creating a new derived value is possible because it is already done with Defense Values. I've thought about adding Legend into the calculation, but since it is Attribute+Attribute and not Attribute+Ability (like the DVs), I decided against it. It also allows strong characters to be great warriors without the specific need to increase DEX (although increasing both STR and DEX can make you a much greater warrior). A character who focuses on STR could be as good at fighting as one who focuses on DEX (although the latter would still hold superiority when it comes to avoiding an attack). Finally, as far as I can remember, there is no Knack that affects attacks directly by influencing either STR or DEX, so there is little or no need to alter them to incorporate this mechanic.

    So what do you think? I'm thinking about using this the next time I run Scion, but I wanted a second brain to pick at it in case there is a flaw that needs serious fixin' first.

      Seems very complicated, especially when you add in effects that modify either Strength, Dexterity or Accuracy.

      What I suggest doing, not just for this but for all WW games, is taking a page from D&D or many other systems: one stat per function. Dexterity is now your Accuracy Attribute. Strength is now your Damage Attribute and Stamina is your Defense Attribute. No attack will ever be made using Strength. Not even Clinch.

      Remove entirely the concept of DV. The only way to reduce an attack's effectiveness is Soak. The whole idea of a two-layered defense only even kinda worked back in Old World of Darkness, when defending required an action, and action economy was hard to manage. Then, it meant that if you dodged or parried, you were going fully defensive to survive a big attack, and it had a significant cost. Moving to DV just adds gives a second passive layer of defense at no cost, which is entirely unnecessary in a cinematic combat system. It also artificially inflates the (already high) value of Dexterity. So, drop DV. One line of defense. This negates a third the power of Dexterity. It now does only two things, instead of three.

      Remove the concept of "spill-over" damage. Extra accuracy has no benefit. If you hit, you hit. You can't hit extra well and get bonus damage. You just hit. That's it. This negates the second third of Dexterity's power. It now does one thing, just like Strength and Stamina. The three Physical Attributes are now even.

      At this point, Firearms can be fixed just by saying that they add Strength to their damage, like all attacks do. Why? Magic. You're a magical demigod. If it makes more semantic sense, you can rename Strength to "Power" or "Ferocity" and say you're shooting them REALLY hard. You know, like Rambo does.

                                                  I've been following this and I think good points are being made, and I don't know if I have any kind of integral wisdom to bring insofar as how to fix what we all know is a broken system as regards firearms.                                                   Do firearms need to be fixed?

                                                  Firearms are propelled by a chemical reaction and so on and so forth but the simple matter is that that reaction can produce only as much as it can produce. No matter how good a shot you are, no matter how accurate you are or how much you aim beforehand, your 9mm pistol will not shoot through a bank vault door. It can't. It doesn't have the power. And there is no real reason anyone could expect a guy with a 9mm pistol to be able to drop Ares. Even if you gave the handgun to Hermes, it's just not going to be effective. It can only do what it can do. Enchanting it may give it a few extra dice here and there, given special ammo or just a Fate-twisting sense of accuracy and placement, but it's still just a piece of lead coming out at a given velocity to a given range.

                                                  Altogether not a particularly valuable weapon to give your kids. Or, rather, not as a long term weapon. At Hero level (where I imagine most scions get killed) something like a Mossberg would be quite useful in handling the riff raff but the forward thinking parent would probably realise that it won't hold up against the hardier stuff and plan accordingly.

                                                  Think of any military in any monster or mecha anime/manga. They pour on their technological hardware but the bad thing just walks though it unaffected. Only the hero and his/her <insert weapon/power/mecha> can rise up and defeat the bad thing.

                                                  Bows, slings and other bodily propelled weapons would, as usual, default to Strength for damage. Which sucks for the would-be Dex-bunny who would prefer to spend all their points on the likes of Dex and Wits but there are ways to mitigate the need to pile on Strength. Fight With Your Head for one. Mortal Stroke if/when you can get your hands on it. Tactically lowering the bad guy's DV to give you as many bonus left-over dice for damage.

                                                  I'm not saying that the system is perfect, because it isn't. But I wonder if re-writing the whole thing to find a way to add a damage stat to something that really shouldn't have one is all that necessary.

Etc, etc. There's a bit of the same thing with Wits/Intelligence, too. They're fuzzy areas because non-combat rolls tend to rely heavily on narrative, player creativity and player justifications. They're roleplaying problems. Combat, however, is not a roleplaying problem. It's quite literally a roll-playing problem. It has fixed, specific rules. Narrative descriptions matter, in the form of stunts, but mechanics are mechanics. That's why most games have chapters and books dedicated to combat, but virtually nothing on social interaction. You deal with that through, well, social interaction. Or powers.

                                                      So, yeah, being a combat character (that is, a mechanically based character) costs more XP than being a social character. That's a flaw in the system. It's actually hard to see any benefit to your character from having high scores in 2-3 Social or Mental stats. The difference between Charisma 4 and Charisma 5 is hard to define. Conversely, it's super-easy to define, statistically and anecdotally, the difference between Strength 5 and Strength 4. People notice that difference, right away. They roll those dice more often.

                                                      Does that suck? Yeah. But I don't see it getting better any time soon, and I certainly don't see adding Wits to combat as being a significant step towards worsening that issue. Yes, it adds a stat that combat monsters "have" to invest in. Or they can just be alright with only mastering some of the combat system instead of all of it. I'm ok with that. I think it should be hideously expensive to be amazing at every single aspect of combat.

                                                      Unless you either systemized non-combat encounters (which people tend to dislike because it takes the roleplay out of roleplaying, and limits their freedom of action), or move to a far more narrative combat system, I don't see that particular issue getting solved, or even getting much help. No one wants to be told that someone just rolled enough successes to beat their Mental Dodge and now they think their best friend is a traitor.

                                                      Originally posted by Squee
                                                      Do firearms need to be fixed?
                                                      Yes, I think so. It's true, there's no logical way for a 9mm bullet to go through a bank vault, or slay a 400 foot tall giant. However, there's no way for those things to be done with a dagger or a slingshot. A dagger would break, or a slingshot bullet would ricochet off, or disintegrate. The wound trauma would be too small to seriously injure a huge being. Still, the fact that Scions are demigods and using Epic Strength makes it happen. Magically. It should be equally viable to use guns. It's an aesthetic choice. The option of being a more modern style of demigod should exist. You should be able to use guns, chainsaws, laser beams, whatever. That's part of your narrative. The system needs to support those options.

                                                      What I'm far more often concerned with is making sure that the magical heroes stay the focus of the game. If you try to bring too much physics into things, you end up with the solution always being to call in the cops, the SWAT team, the army, the air force, etc. Explosives are always better than swords. Sniper rifles are always better than arrows. Missiles are always better than knives. Bombs are the solution to your Leviathan problems. Drop an A-Bomb on Nidhoggr, problem solved. Titan War solved. And that's boring. So physics and reality need to take a back seat to telling a modern epic with gun-slinging amazons fighting right along side the hacker with a katana.
