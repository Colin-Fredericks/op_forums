                    I can see the arguments for replacing the Loa with the Orisha, made both here and by John & Anne, but it seems like there is a real opportunity being missed here. (Not mind you, that it is one that I think can, or even should, really be capitalized on when you are overhauling things from the ground up). Namely the formation of new(er) pantheons.

                    Loa are explicitly divided into nanchons. It seems like an easy fix to USE that and say the Petro Loa are the Orishas in their new world masks. But then there are the Rada. Explicitly younger, often angrier and different (mostly) from the Petro. How about having the Rada be an example of a new pantheon that formed in the wake of a significant event? The "duplicate" deities, aren't. They are examples of children of the older Orisha/Petro. Which can seem a little weird in some cases - Erzulie Danto (Rada) seems like she should be older than Erzulie Freda/Oshun (Petro), but considering their different foci, it makes perfect sense that they would do so.

                    Then we have a group of Legend 12 Gods that were actually Scions that made it up through the ranks as it were. As many players (and to a degree, the developers) envision their own Scions doing. In fact, that might explain the Loa that are not Orisha derived. (Like Damballah). They are "joiners" from other pantheons (or gods with an inter-pantheon presence, which is another topic entirely). That would even go to explaining the other nanchons. They are obviously derived from the mythologies of other cultures than the Yoruba. Might they be like the Petro? Explicitly those Gods with New World faces? Or like the Rada? Newly ascended Scions that are the children of non-Orisha African Gods associated with enslaved peoples from non-Yoruba peoples?

                    The big outlier is, of course, the Ghede meaning the good Barons, Mamman Brigitte. I so don't buy this "Celtic immigrant theory". It's super bad from a comparative myth standpoint and wouldn't even past a basic sniff test AT ALL if it weren't for the "Where the Hell do they come from?" problem with the Ghede. Irish immigrants sharing the very few and fragmentary stories about at least one pretty vague hero myth? It seems MUCH more likely that, if the Irish immigrants (which weren't really in the right time and place to even have had this effect at all on voodoun as it was forming) would have reinforced the Catholic Saint attributions. (Brigit and Brigitte being the same? Isn't it more likely at one remove that Brigit is the same as St. Brigid who is identified with someone else who is Mamman Brigitte? Both Gods being deliberately confused for the same non-God as a mask for very separate mythic identities and roles with a few similarities in common?). So where do the Ghede come from, Scion-wise? Well there are some Orisha gods like the Ghede that get left out in the wash.... Sopona/Shapona/Omulu/Babalu-Aye, the god of smallpox. He is definitely a dark guy and not really "lesser deity" kind of stuff. Or, maybe the Ghede are some new gods born from the Titan/Primal force of Death? Things were definitely bad enough for the slaves that Death titanspawn would have been... interested. Interested enough to get their destinies entangled with mortals and become less Aspects and more Fatebound Gods? Interested enough to spawn a few children with some mortals who ascended alongside their Rada contemporaries/allies? A combination of the two (Ex. the Baron and Mamman Brigitte from category two and Papa Ghede from category one)?

                    An aside: IMO, saying that Lugh is Llew is Lug is NOT the same as saying Ares is Mars. Lugh and Llew don't have even vaguely similar roles, stories or functions. We know really close to next to nothing about Lug. Are they related in a cultural sense? Sure, just like Zeus, Indra, Thor and Tarhunt are - though the split is newer and the cultures are chronologically and culturally closer together. We shouldn't confuse linguistic similarity and misty past connections for identifying them as the same person. Being the child of Lugh would be a connection with very very different stories and attributions (and related game mechanics) than children of Llew. It does a disservice on the order of replacing the Orisha with the Loa to equate them all. Or worse.

                    Now Mannanan and Manawydan being the same guy.... that I will listen to and can make a case for... )
