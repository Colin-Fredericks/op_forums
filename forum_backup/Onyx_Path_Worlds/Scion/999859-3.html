Regarding the "policing", I also have to make amends: As others have pointed out already, I should have suggested another thread instead of a PM; I don't particularly like posting on forums, and am still not quite used to the etiquette and mechanics of doing so, so please forgive me on that. It was not about silencing your concerns, because addressing mythological accuracy is very serious to our project, but about keeping this thread on-topic.

    That's why I won't reply to your post point by point now, although you are, as I've tried to stress, more than welcome to continue the arguement somewhere else. I tried to write that reply quickly yesterday evening, trying to get it out before the discussion could move on somewhere else (an empty concern as I realise now), I'm sure we can solve a lot of our misunderstandings by getting more nitty-gritty with our source materials than I managed to do yesterday. Maybe I'll convince you after all. Maybe you'll convince me. But for now, our write-up will stay as it is.

    I'd still, in the end, like to make a note on research and sources, because that's obviously important to the approach of Scion 2.0 as a whole. You said I used the word "speculation" too often, and maybe I did. It comes from working in academia - you can't say half a thing before they rip you for speculating there. That doesn't mean well-founded speculation is a bad thing, and yes, while we think our reading is closer to the original meaning of the primary sources, that is still interpretation, and maybe even speculation. I never meant to say that your interpretation or speculation is invalid, but what I did mean to say was that neither interpretation nor speculation can really be argued. We're not saying what you got from reading the sources is wrong, but we kinda got the feeling, especially from the passionate way you addressed it, that you were saying that ours was, and that you wanted it changed. It's probably just another misunderstanding, and I hope this solves it. And while you are absolutely right that the recorders of Norse myth were falsifying it (although it could be argued that the Poetic Edda is far more authentic), it's not like the fact that Scion 2.0 is sticking to primary sources means we just take them at face-value, and act as if we hadn't noticed the obvious Christian bias. In interpreting a historical source, we of course try to understand and examine the authors intentions and agenda. That doesn't mean that someone like Snorri is equal to a 19th-century scholar creating an unhelpful category like "shamanism". Snorri was from the culture he's writing about, and while he's obviously attempting to euhemerise and Christianise his past, he also obviously deeply adores it, or otherwise he wouldn't have tried to "save the face" of those gods and heroes by making them mortal kings instead of demons. He also quotes liberally from authentic, pagan sources that are lost to us, so you really can't call drawing on him (or others like him in the context of other pantheons) equivalent to speculation, or to drawing on the theories and hypotheses of scholars and enthusiasts.

    A last word maybe on what I do know about the Norse, because you seemed to call that into question entirely at some points (again, maybe I just misunderstood you). Now, as I said, the Norse are not my main area of expertise, and Scion 2.0 hasn't done a full rewrite of them yet, so opinions might change as we go deeper and learn more. But that doesn't mean we're just making uneducated gut-decisions either. I've worked through both Eddas a time or two, I've read Beowulf and some Sagas, and maybe about twenty titles of academic writing on Norse mythology. I'm assuming about the same for the members of Scion 2.0 itself. That's not a lot, and, as I pointed out, it doesn't compare to what I actually consider a topic I've researched in depth (namely Japan, on which my personal bibliography counts about 160 titles and 2 uni courses), but it's not like we've had a cursory look at Snorri and ran away with it either. So when I say "Freyr seems a more likely candidate for original Scandinavian high god to me", I mean "I've read some scholarly work on it, and its arguments have mostly convinced me". When I say the opposite of Tyr, I mean "there seems evidence both archaeological and linguistic that suggest such a standing amongst the ancient continental Germanic peoples, but I've yet to see comparable scientific proof for Scandinavia". As I said, I think we've both made a number of false assumptions about each other. Please understand that nothing we have written here is drawn from thin air, every word and idea has been researched and argued over - and on many points we still don't agree, but we've made our compromises to present things the most reasonable and accurate way we could. Because this is (unlike how we treat primary sources, one might say) not a definite canon - but it is the product of our research and of our application of scientific method and academic research to the treatment of mythology.

    You're referring to Mimir? 'cause - yeah, that's not at all why he did that, actually. He wanted to ask Mimir questions, the price of this was a game of wits, and if Odin lost he'd lose his own head. See how that's meant to be poetic, instead of just an event for the sake of having an event, and how it ties into Odin's 'change destiny even if it kills me' attitude?
    I was referring to Vafthrudnir, and from the way you tell the story, I guess you mean the same poem. And that contests pretty certainly wasn't about Odin learning something, because he states in the first stanza already that he wants to "seek[s] to match" Vafthrudnir's knowledge. Oh, and because I just noticed it now, you keep speeling it "Amateratsu", but it actually is "Amaterasu". Not a good point to address that, I guess, but better than the others? Anyway.

His translation then goes on to form the backbone of most Western work and understanding, so this one little error gets repeated over, and over, and over, so much so that it is compounded into the base cultural consciousness.                                 Originally posted by Corax View Post
                                Since you have Buddhist deities like Kannon too, I'm curious do you guys use Siddhartha Gautama around the table, if so how?
                                Oh man, yeah, so. This is complicated. Siddharta Gautama has never shown up at our table, but when writing the Kami and looking at the Shen, we spent a lot of time talking about the Buddhists. We think we may one day like to write a PsP, possibly with some alternate powers, for the Buddhist deities like Kannon, and Jizo. This PsP would be shared by all other Buddhist deities across various Pantheons, with possibly some unique powers here and there for different Pantheons.

                                The Buddhists occupy a fascinating but utterly tricky spot in Scion. We have not spent a huge amount of time talking about it, but my general idea is they are a Pantheon-Less-Pantheon. I think I will represent Guan Yin, Kannon, Jizo, and their like as Gods and Goddesses who are 'part' of a local Pantheon (such as the Kami or Shen), but don't share that Pantheon's PsP or Virtues. It's... very strange, I realize, but I think it would be a good way to represent the way in which Buddhism interacted, and was incorporated (in Japan, with great resistance by some groups. Amaterasu still bans even mentioning anything that even relates to Buddhism in her grand shrine) into local traditions.

                                But, for the Buddhas themselves. We have not spent a large amount of time on this, but we are thinking Titans. They are just so monumentally powerful in-universe that they don't work as Legend 12 Deities (such as when the Buddha intercedes when SunWukong has single handedly fought the Shen), so, Titanhood seems to be a good spot for them. We are not totally sold on it though, it's a bit of a toss up. We want to respect their overwhelming power and understanding, which the best way to do is make them Titans. They have never shown up at our table though.

                                                Yeah, Buddhism is the big complicated issue for Scion east of the Bosphorus. I think the way we're debating it is the... not the best, but one of the least problematic options out there. Making a unified Buddhist Pantheon doesn't really work since there's a huge amount of difference place to place, but making them actual 'members' of local Pantheons takes away their own nature, and causes issues. Big one with the Kami is that Amaterasu doesn't like a system that messes with Imperial Authority, and Buddhism does just that.                                                 Just making them part of the Deva also creates a... lot of issues, lots of internal religious strife there between Buddhism and Hinduism. So, I'd personally be strongly against just making the Buddhists a sub-sect of the Deva for many, many reasons. I understand why people may do it, it is the simplest answer, and if that works for your games, go for it. I'm just a stupid academic who worries about the legitimacy of me trying to address cultures I have no connection to (which is all of them, the past is a different country, cultural relativism, all that) so I try to go to the farthest yard just to combat issues I wrestle with.

                                                That's actually one of the reasons the Kami is fifty pages, mostly single spaced, and font size ten. It is possibly the longest thing I have ever written, and it's that long since I desperately tried to understand the context so I could try to make as few mistakes as I possibly could.

                                                        Originally posted by Crying View Post
                                                        So, on a scale of 1 to 10, how bad of an idea would trying to seperate the Shen by dynasty be?

                                                        Because I know that they're kind of just a bunch of different mythologies that all got thrown together, so seperating them wouldn't exactly be accurate, but I also know that it would take so much effort that it's ridiculous.
                                                        How good of an idea would it be? I can see why someone may want to try it, but it would become an absolute mess. The Shen are a large collection of different mythological traditions that are all combined together, just like you said. But, under the influence of the State, though just which State it technically is may change, these traditions are... I would not quite say harmonised, but unified to one degree or another. I think the Shen are best represented as the unified conglomerate, or Divine 'State', it really encapsulates a lot of the feel, and historical themes from what I have read of them thus far.

                                                        The biggest difficulty in trying to divide the Shen by chronology would be a massive number of cross-over Deities. Especially by the later, it would just be compounding upon itself, with more and more. I think it would be an undertaking in the realm of the possible, but, not in any sensible time frame. And then you would have the mess of each Pantheon probably having a wildly different PsP in some cases, it would take years. Or, well, it would take us years.

                                                        I find the Shen exceptionally interesting due to their shifting traditions. In the Shen, we see a huge slosh of ideas, Gods, Goddesses, mortals, politics, all influencing each other in a frankly big mess. But, I do love messes like that. I look forward to the day myself and Sac sit down together and resume our work on the Shen, I think they will be very interesting in the end.

                                                        Honestly, biggest thing with the Shen that will prove tricky is the habit of mortals to become Deities. With the Kami, it can be explained as humanity being the descendants of various Kami. (We actually have a little idea for how Kami Scions come about based around that, it will likely be in a side bar when we put the information up on our website.) But the Shen just have mortals become Deities, and that is a lot of fun to play with in multi-Pantheon games.

                                                        The Theoi, Anunna, and Bogovi have their little dirt-dumb-barely-even-people mortals, while the Shen have mortals who occasionally become Major Deities. And then the Irish have mortals who not only waged war against the Gods, but won, and killed most of them. Mortals are great fun in cross-Pantheon games when you have such different variations in just what it means to be a mortal. It has never come up in my games yet, but one day I might have the differences between mortals come up. Or I might possibly bind it to geography as I have done other things. So, either a descendent from Mil is potentially dangerous to beings with Legend, or just when you are in Ireland, the line between Mortal and Divine is a bit blurry.

                                                        Bit rambling there, sorry about that. It's late over here.

                                                        In short. It is possible to divide up the Shen by dynasty, but in the same way that climbing Everest is possible, and I think it would lose a lot of the uniqueness of the Pantheon in doing so. Further, you would have compounding issues in later dynasties, and in earlier ones, they exist in a Mythic-History where we don't have the best grasp of what was going on Pantheon-wise.

                                                        Edit: So that number out of ten. I'd give it a 9/10. Not necessarily the worst idea ever of all time, but in doing so, the Pantheons would be a mess, and something of their primary nature would be lost.
