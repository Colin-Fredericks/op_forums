    Non-combat scions in combat?

    02-26-2014, 10:02 PM
    So, I'm just starting my first Scion game, my first character is a Scion of Loki, and he's set up entirely for social interaction. My character is decked out well enough that I can lie, cheat, swindle, sneak or steal my way past most situations...but I'm not so naive as to think I can BS my way past every situation, and even if I did that would be a very boring game for the rest of the group, half of which are playing combat characters.

    Plus, there's the non-zero possibility I accidentally succeed a Courage check; luckily my Courage isn't nearly high enough that I'll ever go berserk, but that doesn't preclude having to fight anyways due to Metaphysically-enforced Aesir Idiot Ball. And the fact I'll probably be starting as many if not more fights due to aforementioned lying, cheating, swindling, sneaking, and stealing than I'll be preventing -- I just hope that when that happens, the PC's with the monstrous epic strength and stamina is between me and whatever I righteously pissed off.

    Now, before anyone starts, I'm not griefing the party or trying to screw any PC's over. I'm not playing "stereotypical game-derailing munchkin evil rogue type" -- my character is decisively good, and I will be applying my character's skills for the greater good of the group and to achieve mutual goals.

    Now...that said, I've heard horror stories about Scion balance and combat. When my character finally gets what's coming to him, I'd like to avoid being a Loki Scion-sized smear on the carpet in one hit. Are there any knacks or boons at which I should look to avoid this? Right now, I have Solipsistic Well-Being, Rabbit Reflexes, and Not the Face; at what else should I look to get by, especially when it comes to the "not getting attacked in the first place" realm?

      Game Face, Serpent's Gaze, Skin-Shedding, Divine Damage Conversion, Untouchable Opponent...

      Basically because of how Scion scales, unless your GM is incredibly skilled and careful with combat, non-focused characters will die horribly if they take even a glancing blow from a combat-focused NPC or antagonist. This gets truer the higher Legend things go. By mid-level Demigod Epics are scaling up so fast that anyone with even one Epic higher than you is gunna murder you to death pretty fast. If you've still got Hero-level Epic Physicals or, holy crap!, NO Epic Physicals? You're pretty doomed as soon as a combat-focused character even blinks in your direction.

      Basically, in order to survive, all characters need some levels of Epic Dexterity and Epic Stamina, preferably only 1-2 dots behind the current Legend curve.

      I'm a professor! Why is no one listening to me?!

                  Originally posted by Telgar View Post

This gets truer the higher Legend things go. By mid-level Demigod Epics are scaling up so fast that anyone with even one Epic higher than you is gunna murder you to death pretty fast. If you've still got Hero-level Epic Physicals or, holy crap!, NO Epic Physicals? You're pretty doomed as soon as a combat-focused character even blinks in your direction.
                  Telgar has the right of it. Single focus characters never fare well in Scion, so you are going to want to have at least one physical (if you want to survive) and at least one social (if you want anyone to care about what you are saying). Epic Stamina is the most popular option in my experience, but even if you focus on Epic Dexterity you are still going to want a little Epic Stamina to allow you to heal faster and get some extra health levels.

                  You absolutely want to grab effects that will mitigate sudden bursts of damage, such as Solipsistic Well-Being and Skin Shedding.

                  On the flip side, being the social character also means you are one of the most powerful people in the entire game. As long as you play your cards right and avoid becoming a splatter on the pavement you can manipulate and convince combat characters to do whatever you want. Including kill each other, or sitting down and shutting up while you work your magic. Physical focused characters do not get any kind of decent AOE without going into Purviews, but you can rock Hard Sell, Mass Hypnosis, Crowd Control and other similar knacks.

                  If you play with the RAW willpower resistance rules, entire social combos revolve around Borrowed Credibility / Kill the Messenger and reducing an opponents Willpower from 10 to 0 in a single round.

                                      RAW explicitly states Raise Your Glass only requires the Scion using the knack to drink a single serving of alcohol: mug of beer or mead, glass of wine, shot of alcohol. Moreover it only states "...at least a bit of flair" which means the player does not have to turn the act of drinking the alcohol into an elaborate, by-the-rules, stunt -- they just have to drink in a cool and/or memorable way (hell, the book suggests merely shotgunning a beer and slamming, but not smashing, the mug as sufficient). And, alcohol as a poison is very, very lenient: even without Epic Fortitude, it takes a buttload of alcohol to get drunk, let alone take significant penalties (my scion with his newbie 3 stamina, 3 fortitude has nothing to worry about until his eighth shot).

                                      It's a once-per-scene knack anyhow, so PC's with the knack can't go on infinite-legend-harvesting pub crawls.

                                          I've had some experience playing non combat characters in Scion. A couple suggestions:

                                          Oh Shit buttons are good. Skin Shedding I recommend to any character. Soliphistic Well Being is useful too depending on perception. If you are a wits guy, invest in talent mirror. You want 1 Epic Stamina minimum anyway, just because you don't want to be the one member of the party who needs to sleep every day when everyone else can go for weeks without, because then you are holding things up.

                                          One important thing in combat is not to make yourself a target. Some enemies will attack the weak looking ones, especially in an ambush, but more often it makes less sense to go after the weaselly guy not doing much then he huge beserker who is killing two enemies a round. This is obviously something to be sure you are on the same page with your GM about.

                                          Serpant's Gaze and even moreso Compelling Presence are useful, but tend to draw a lot of hate on you. If you paralyze half the enemy forces with Epic Appearance, expect a lot of attacks coming at you. It is hard to justify enemies not trying to crush someone who frees up half their forces if he goes down.

                                          If you know magic, one way to keep active and keep your head low is a one-two punch of Trading Fates and Evil Eye. Buff a stronger ally, curse an enemy with your tiny-ass die pool (the max penalty is Presence, so die pool does not matter for that) then your ally should have used their dice and you can buff anouther. Your friends will like the guy who gives someone ~8-10 dice every 10 ticks. In Bohemian Front, Saltmarsh Andy's dice have several times made the difference between the heavy hitters being able to hit and them haivng to use precious legend channels. If you are a Wits/Man guy, as tricksters often are, pick up Paralyzing Confusion. Don't use it unless an enemy comes after you. Stop them, keep buffing and cursing. Illwind Curse is also low level and works well on enemies who use social abilities, but tends to draw more hate then Evil Eye. Andy will also be using Body and Soul and Bards Tongue to buff in time, but that is a Tuatha thing.
