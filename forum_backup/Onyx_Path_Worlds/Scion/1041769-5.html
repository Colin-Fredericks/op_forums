                    It kind of gets dicey, it's really interesting! I believe it is because there are several Prophecies running parallel to each other at different times IIRC. Gaia prophesied that Zeus, too, would be overthrown just as his father, and grandfather before him were by his own son. This was done in the wake of the Gigantomachy due to Zeus imprisoning the Giants alongside the Titans.

                    Now, what is interesting about this is it allows us to construct a chronology. During the Gigantomachy, Heracles is an important character and Athena is around, therefore we are after Metis being consumed by Zeus. (Metis is his first wife, so it has to be before Heracles is born, since Zeus has to be married to Hera by the time Heracles is born) So, Gaia's Prophecy takes place after Metis' own 'Prophecy' that her son would be mightier than Zeus himself. So, that one is still possibly up and running, Gaia's one that is.

                    While this could just be nonsense conjecture, we have another myth (or well play, but it's a mythic play) called Prometheus Bound which has the Theoi going to Prometheus one after another (so this is before Heracles freed him, but after he tricked Zeus) to him on Zeus' behalf trying to get Prometheus to spill the beans, because Prometheus, as the personified force of Foresight knows who the Son of Zeus will be. Prometheus laughs and foils the Theoi through the entire play, and he never reveals the identity of The Son of Zeus even through his daily torture, and then escapes.

                    So what we know is there is at least one Prophecy on the topic, possibly two, and they can both be true. Metis, as primordial Wisdom, says that her son will be mightier than Zeus. This is a true statment. Zeus consumes her, and from his head is born Athena. Then when the Giants fight the Theoi, and Zeus tosses them into prison beneath the earth (just like Cronus did with the Hundred Handed Ones, and the Primordial Cyclopses, and as Ouranos did with all of his children) Gaia makes a prophecy that he too will be overthrown by a son of his. So, this continues to be true even after Zeus ate Metis. And adding to this, Prometheus knows who The Son of Zeus is/will be.

                    The tricky thing about this is Metis. Since all Fates are true and will come true no matter what for the Greeks, her foresight must be correct. Therefore, she will have a son who is mightier than Zeus. She might not have yet. Just because she was eaten does not mean she is dead, the Theoi don't really die (Pan is a weird exception, Asclepius dies pre-Apotheosis) and they are proven capable of surviving in someone's stomach (Hades and the gang in Cronus). So, she could escape, have a kid with Zeus somehow, and if it's a son, the Prophecy works.

                    However, there's another way it can be played. If Metis is inside Zeus, and Zeus has subsumed her entity (which he may have as Zeus becomes an All Knowing figure which he totally was not at the start) then any of Zeus' kids may also be Metis' kids. And since Metis is pure primordial Wisdom, she can probably figure out a way to do this super easily.

                                    I think the issue at hand is a larger one than that of "canon," which is already fraught when we're trying to subjectively interpret characters and beings whose primary sources are both contradictory and hundreds of thousands of years old; it's bad enough when we're trying to figure out if Het-Heru turns into Sekhmet or if they're different deities that share the same title (and if Sekhmet then becomes Bast or if Bast is her own deity), but when we're trying to establish their opinions on a topic that likely existed at the time but had minimal writing on it. With that in mind, the question is less "how would these gods respond to such topics?" and more "how do you want these topics to factory into your narratives?" and for me the answer is simple: I deal with enough misogyny, transphobia, and everything else in my real world, so I'm in no hurry to wrestle with that in a game of fantasy, especially one that overlaps with my own faith. Anyone trying to say that forcing modern acceptance and morality on the old gods is foolish is doing the same thing by trying to force current conservative (and often rooted in the Abrahamic faiths) ways of thinking onto beings from another age, and is often doing so in a way that deliberately rejects precedent from the cultures and faiths Scion draws from.

                                    Simply put, I'd rather put my effort into figuring out why the gods would be supportive than why they would not.

                                    The Theoi's signature Purview is that of changing shape, and Greek myth is full of characters changing the sexes of their bodies, to say nothing of the intersex Hermaphroditus. I would ask anyone arguing that Greek homosexuality was explicitly the sole domain of asserting masculine dominance over socially weak submissive tell that to the face of Alexander the Great, who dearly loved Hephaestion as equals, or even offer their prayers up to Roman emperor Hadrian's deified male lover Antinous. Both the Greeks and the Romans not only tolerated but accepted and helped to spread the cult of Cybele/Magna Mater, whose faithful were often self-castrating men who went on to live as women; to suggest that they were transgender in a modern light is imposing modern terminology and thinking on them, but at the same time it's damn hard to argue against something similar happening there. Odin is mocked for her practice of women's magic, but at the end of the day he is the Allfather; if the head of your Pantheon is skirting around traditional gender roles, I see no reason not for that to spread on down to your mortal followers (to nothing of how fluid Loki is).

                                    I see no reason to suggest that any goddess of womanhood would be some sort of TERF or biological essentialist; if anything, I would suggest the divine would be more capable of seeing inherent womanhood, not less. They are there as patrons and guardians of all women, and I don't feel like any argument to the contrary has solid ground to stand on other than some cruel need to see transphobia reflected in the media they consume.

                                    EDIT: (In the name of disclosure and to justify why this thread has made me so heated, I'm a transgender woman who leans pretty heavily on Egyptian paganism to keep me going. This is less an academic debate to me and more people trying to argue to me just why transphobia has a place in a game I love and among gods I worship, and I'm not really ever going to stand for that).
