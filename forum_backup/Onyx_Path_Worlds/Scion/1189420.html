      Grand Theft Solar, or Scions in Spaaace!

      I GM’d a lengthy scenario involving a group of demigods reassembling a seal that had been used to mystically close off the Basque underworld when that pantheon died. After the underworld had been locked off, the seal was broken into five pieces, which were cast to the four winds and the final piece was thrown into the heavens. Basically, an excuse to have them travel the world and interact with various cultures and pantheons while they retrieved the pieces. I’ll skip over the earthbound pieces for now, as the coolest part was the skybound fragment.

      Through various divinations and such, they discover that fragment is in space, on an 18-year, slow ellipse around the earth. Currently, it’s on an outbound leg, and won’t be back within the orbit of the moon for another 7 years. So, they review their options regarding space transport. Their mission is actually something that they’ve been explicitly told not to do by their pantheons, but they’re doing it in secret anyway, because they believe they’re doing the right thing. So, no help from the sun or star gods of their pantheons, and the one moon god that they’re on decent terms from another pantheon can’t get to the fragment because it’s way beyond the moon’s orbit. So, they decide to steal some transport.

      They’ve had some encounters with avatars of the Devas before, and know that Surya, God of the Sun, visits the World often, and is crazy about sport, particularly football (soccer), which he loves to referee. He maintains a mortal identity as the referee, Shaji Kurien. They also know there is an important football game between Germany and India coming up next week at the Jawaharlal Nehru stadium in New Dehli. So, using some Health and Epic Manipulation, they nobble one of the existing referees and arrange for Shaji Kurien to fill in. Surya is thrilled, and since it’s a night game and will be played under lights, he goes there directly from work, which is the plan. He arrives with his chariot (which presents as an ostentatious gold Mercedes) and his two sons, the Ashvins, Nasatya (dawn) and Dasra (twilight).

      The PCs know that the chariot can be stolen (Indra did it once), but they have to get past the Ashvins. If they can be overpowered and the chariot used and returned before the match ends, then it is unlikely that the Ashvins will admit their humiliation to their father Surya. The Ashvins stay in the underground parking lot with the chariot, playing chaturanga, and drinking cocum squash. The PCs drug their drinks with poison they got from defeating the Amphisbaena (while retrieving an earlier fragment) and the two are soon out cold. Then they have to deal with the huge seven-headed horse that draws the chariot, which looks like it could capably curb-stomp a main battle tank. They calm it by reciting the Gayatri Mantra, and climb aboard the chariot. Even then they have to use all the collective Epic Strength to keep the chariot under control, but they finally get it into the sky and space.

      They plot an intercept course to the fragment, all the time aware that they’d better get back before the football match ends, or there’ll be hell to pay. So, there’s not much time. En route, they are mistaken for Surya by a group of Kalakeyas, who are figure they’ll attack (since it’s technically night, so Surya must be weak, right?). The group manages to fight them off and retrieve the seal fragment, but lose precious time in the process, and have to race back to earth, splitting the chariot horse into its rainbow-hued seven horse incarnation in the process (and destroying a number of inconveniently-located satellites). They get back just in time (India won the game, which they also fixed, to ensure maximum excitement). By the time Surya gets back to his vehicle, the Ashvins are just waking up, and PCs are long gone.

          Not my character, but I was the GM.

          So, I had a tough day of classes. I decided to do something silly for the session.

          My Players' God-tier characters wanted to take a break from God-ing. So they manifested down as weak Avatars and went to go watch a baseball game. And I decided to have a bit of fun. I had it set up so that some Giants decided to spike some of the food with a poison that only reacts to Ichor, so to most of the audience, the hot dogs just tasted fine. For the people who have Divine Heritage, however, it was a powerful hallucinogen.

          So to my players, they started to see something that emphatically was NOT a baseball game. They saw a three-faced Asura smacked a comet thrown by a hydra with a massive tetsubo, it was knocked out of the sky by a roc, and was caught by a giant purple two-headed dragon turtle doing a handstand.

          One of the players was freaking out at this BAD TRIP, and pulled out a spare Styngian Bird feather and threw it at the Dragon Turtle. The magic drugs wore off in time for the players to see the consequences of this.

          And that's how my players killed Sammy Sosa.

                      The aforementioned band of miscreants who were searching for the seal fragments in space, actually had a pretty fun you-meet-in-a-tavern kickoff to the campaign, back when they were just ‘lil heroes. This is a bit long, sorry.

                      So, via various twists of Fate, the PCs – who don’t yet know each other – wind up in Monument Valley, AZ/UT. They stop a nationalist militia from doing something nefarious involving explosives, and thwart (or so they think) a dark ritual of some kind centered around an elaborate sandpainting next to Rain God Mesa, performed by a ravenmocker and – what will become – their evil rival scion band. The evil band had actually manipulated the situation to bring one specific PC – a son of Odin – to the scene (Fate just figured to throw a spanner in the works, and so supplied the other PCs). Anyway, at one point, the Odinson is manipulated to shout “The son of Odin calls you out” (the player walked right into it; it was bloody perfect). Whereupon Rain God Mesa explodes, as a gigantic thunderbird bursts forth from beneath it.

                      The ravenmocker and the evil band get away in the confusion, and the PCs are left to deal with something way beyond their weight class. After getting beaten, battered and fried a bit, they start paying attention to what the rolling thunder is saying (one of the PCs can understand all languages), and realize that it is calling for Thor to come out and do battle. They manage to convince the T-bird that Thor is probably in Asgard if he’s anywhere, but what do they know, and maybe the bird should go look for him in Scandinavia. The bird leaves in a fury, and the PCs wonder what the hell just happened and count themselves lucky to have survived. Then they hear a slow clap and see on old Navajo man. He tell them to come with him, and they since they figure he’s Manitou, they do.

                      They end up – still in the desert - at Valley of the Gods, UT, where the old man, who by now they’d realized is Raven, breaks out some beer and tells them the following story around the campfire.

                      “You know, not 300 miles from here, as the crow flies, a woman (Myrtle Botts) saw a Viking longship in the desert. Out in California; just sticking out of the side of an arroyo. This was back in… oh, 1933. Course, no-one believed her. She wrote down directions on where to find it, and you can even see them at the Pioneer Museum in Julian, CA. Course, it wasn’t there when other folks went to look, but that’s the desert for you.”

                      “Not that it’s impossible, mind you. The Colorado River used to be navigable all the way from the Sea of Cortez up to Yuma, AZ. Course, nowadays that river doesn’t exist, at least in any meaningful way. But before agriculture, it was a pretty damn big, so big that it sometimes overflowed its banks and flooded the desert. In 1905 the river burst its banks in Mexico, and flowed north in two channels for more than two years. The entire Colorado River poured into the Salton Sink in California, filling it to become what's now the Salton Sea. These two rivers still run today, draining the town of Mexicali into the Salton Sea.”

                      “This has happened many times. Lake Cahuilla (as it was called then) was about 30 times the size of the Salton Sea today. At its highest, the Lake covered what's now Indio, California to Yuma. During these times, you could sail from the Gulf of California as far inland as the Carrizo Badlands.”

                      “Now, as your buddy there (nods to Colum) can tell you, there’s two things the Aesir like almost as much as kicking ass, and that’s sailing, and getting pissed. Which is not to say these are mutually exclusive pursuits. Take your man Thor, for instance; for a sky God he sure likes his boats. And fishing. Loves his fishing.”

                      “A long time ago, back even before Loki tricked the dwarves into making Mjollnir and the Aesir’s other toys, Thor went fishing and nearly caught the Midgard Serpent. I say nearly caught, cause he hooked it and maybe he would even have landed it if the giant Hymir, whose boat he was using, hadn’t panicked and cut the line. Still, if you’re gonna brag about the one that got away, they don’t come much bigger than that.”

                      “Now, Thor, he’s nothing if not stubborn and he wasn’t going to let matters stand at that, ‘specially with others snickering and saying, “Midgard Serpent, right. You probably just got drunk and hooked the bottom of the boat again.” So, Thor got this bee in his bonnet and determined to bring back the serpent’s head and mount it on his longhouse wall.”

                      “Now, fishing is thirsty work, so Thor made sure to bring along plenty of mead to quench his thirst while waiting over his fishing pole, and it wasn’t long before he was blind drunk and passed out in the gunwales. The thunder of his snoring shook the sky and oceans so much that the creatures of the sea went to Njord to complain. “For god’s sake, drown the bastard and let us have some peace, “they pleaded. But Njord had taken an oath when the Vanir made treaty with the Aesir and, much as he would have liked to, dared not sink Thor’s boat. Instead, he commanded the wind and tides to carry the boat as far away as possible. This they did, and when Thor awoke, he found himself in strange waters, far from the snowy lands that he knew. This land was green and lush and its people prosperous and friendly. But Thor was in no mood to be a gracious guest. Having failed at the fishing and succeeded all too well at the drinking, he fell back on the third Aesir pastime. He stomped through the land, shouting for those who ruled this strange place to come out and face him. The spirits fled from him, afraid of this violent outlander. Even today, the rain sprits remember that time and shun this area in fear of him.”

                      “At length, worried that Thor would lay waste to the land and drive all the animals away, the spirits went to Too-Yah-La-Kekt, greatest of the thunderbirds, and begged him to drive the intruder off. Too-Yah-La-Kekt, angry that another should challenge his rule of the heavens, agreed. The sky turned black with his rage as he flew in search of the outlander. He found Thor in the valley of the rocks, and the shadow of him was like a cloud upon the land as the heavens thundered with his challenge: “I am Too-Yah-La-Kekt, the Thunder Arriving on the Plain,” he shouted, “Who are you that presumes to command the storm?”

                      “Thor, who had been growing bored with the lack of sport, replied, “I am Thor, son of Odin One-Eye. Glad or war, I am the death of giants. I wield the lightning and my voice speaks in the thunder. I will meet you, braggart and we shall see who is master of the sky.”

                      “And so saying, the lept into air, striding from cloud to cloud, swinging his fists in an awful uproar of lightning and storm. Too-Yah-La-Kekt beat his mighty wings and the wind howled across the land as the pair moved on to battle. For days they fought, laying waste to the land. In their wake, the seas boiled, the air burned, and the very mountains shook on their foundations as the earth was beaten with storms.”

                      “But, mighty as the thunderbird was, Thor was the stronger, and at last Too-Yah-La-Kekt fell to earth, defeated. Thor stood over his vanquished foe and proclaimed, “You have dared to challenge Thor and fallen, as all must, and for such presumption you shall forever more be denied the sky.” And so saying, he picked up a mountain and slammed it down on Too-Yah-La-Kekt, saying, “This shall be your place, to dwell in darkness until the son of Odin calls you forth or Ragnarök claims us all.” And so the first among the thunderbirds fell in battle and passed out of legend.”

                      “After that, Thor wandered for a time, but after finding no-one willing to spar with him, he turned southward and came to the Gulf of Mexico. After trying to remember where he had last seen his boat, he resigned himself to swimming home. Not renowned for his sense of direction, he was soon lost, and trudged ashore near Veracruz. There he tried once again to find a worthy opponent. Only this time Quetzalcoatl answered his call, kicked his ass and threw him across the Atlantic; where he became the Orisha’s problem. But that’s another story.”

                      “In time, this legend inspired other Northmen to cross the ocean in search of the vast land to the west, and many of them made it; if you look, you can find markers and runestones dotted here and there across the land. And this place of Thor’s victory over Too-Yah-La-Kekt stills calls to them. Did you know that Denmark supplied more immigrants to Utah in the nineteenth century than any other country except Britain?”

                      Then we go into introductions and pledges, and a pretty kickass band of scions was formed that day. Someday I’ll tell you about that time they stopped the Book of Revelation script from playing out – at the request of an alliance between Jesus and Lucifer – and kicked the crap out of the Four Horsemen.
