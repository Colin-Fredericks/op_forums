                                            I don't really care for Evangelion but I enjoy the result of this kind of thing. I think one can be a bit overly reliant on textual analysis with regards to intentions for a lot of things and easily ignore how important the reception of a thing is.

                                            If the Christian symbolism of Eva is not of deep textual interest to Hideaki Ano, that's interesting. What about when it is significant and relevant to Christians watching it? Their takeaway is certainly different than Anno's, and I'd argue it's more interesting, because it's fascinating to consider all the subtle and indirect ways these things can be meaningful to us.

                                            That works in two ways with this video. I can't speak to that choir's experience of Evangelion, but even if it's as simple and straightforward as "we really enjoyed the opening song" I think that's interesting and meaningful, too.

                                            It also can be significant in how we receive it, and what it means to us. I think Lula provided a great example in his personal reception of this, as the question of "why is this meaningful to you?" Is actually a very complex question with a lot of interesting answers, including personal experience with gospel, Evangelion itself, religious affinity and the divide between secular personal ideology and the reception of gospel in that secular perspective, race, cross-cultural experience and other things. There's a lot of interesting stuff to unpack and explore in the reception of the thing, even if you think the thing itself is fairly simple.

                                            And I think this taps into a really interesting space that Scion exists in too. These gods and mythologies, almost all of them do not have a "canon" of some kind. They have tendencies, and our notions of canon are largely limitations of the texts we derive them from.

                                            Like, think about how Scion is a syncretic work itself and how syncretism as a practice is not an austere, refined objective and distant process, but a messy, intimate and personal process that has a lot to do with how we receive and value things.

                                            So for me, as a Harlem kid who grew up with lots of kids that watched anime and constructed facets of their identity from it, Evangelion and this video have a personal value and express a complex relationship between people and stories that speaks to Scion in my eye.

                                            The gods are themselves composed of stories, because those are how we derive identity and value from them, and on a personal level, know them through these stories.

                                            The personal conflicts characters will have with the gods IMO mirrors the complex relationship between people, faith and stories and that seems like one of the things that will make the game really awesome.

                                            so I like it for that reason. I like the actual singing and the idea of it, and what it can represent to people. To me, that goes beyond a simplistic idea of multiculturalism that is just A + B and speaks more to how we can have complex relationships with things culturally and personally, regardless of what the author may have wanted or failed to think about.
