    The wyrm
    Of the triat (what the totem pantheon call there titans) there is one that all there official followers can agree must be fought. The wyrm is what Apophis is to the Netjer, or what the various star demons are to the teotl. But it was not always this way.
    Once the wyrm, and the trait at large, were peaceful. Once they existed in harmony, and the changing breeds (the garou and fera) had no problems with titanspawn or even titanic hostility. But then the weaver had an existential crisis and went mad. She tricked the wyrm and imprisoned him in the pattern web, the torture of which caused the horrible Corrupter Wyrm to arise. A tulpa of titanic scale, in both power and size, whenever the totem pantheon and its followers refer to the wyrm they fight they are referring to the corrupter wyrm.
    Where the balance wyrm was a massive serpent, the corrupter wyrm is a hydra. Indeed, it is split into many heads, each of which is a rival with the others. The heads are divided into three types, the first being the triatic wyrms. These heads are physical (or at least as physical as spirits can be) and have minds, each is a corrupted representation of the triat. The second category is the urge wyrms, which lack physicality but spread dark emotions. The urge wyrms are insidious and lurk in every heart that feels there emotion, pushing healthy negative feelings to the point of mania. The final type of head are the elemental wyrms, which are purely physical and lack minds. They are four in total, each embodying a wyrm element.

        With that said, here are 3 of the in-depth descriptions I've written so far. Let me know what you think.

        Pegasus, Totem of the Black Furies
        Hear the tale of Pegasus! After the death of the last Gorgon, Luna, in the guise of Artemis, created a protector spirit to guard the Black Furies and to temper their rage before it could spill out onto the rest of the world. An equine creature modeled after Poseidon’s invention but also granted a set of wind-catching wings, his power left many envious, including Athena herself. With the assistance of the hero Bellerophon, they captured Pegasus and made him a mere mount, a tool for Bellerophon’s achievements. It was only after being gelded by his would-be rider that Pegasus had enough, and tossed the man off of his back to die from the fall.
        Ever since this incident, Pegasus has served as an advocate for women, as well as those rare few men who became sterile via injury, and counts them among his Chosen if he judges them worthy. He is also known to Create some Scions out of horses that have been abused and mistreated by their owners. Due to his injury, he is unable to have direct progeny, though he does not waste his time dwelling on such a subject. In those times he takes on a human Incarnation, his selves are either disfigured men, transgender individuals or, rarely, women. All are involved in some form of agriculture or military career, and are noteworthy for their disdain for cruel “traditional” methods of animal care.
        His tribe are the Black Furies, a group of women, both Garou and not, who band together to combat the predations of Man. While they are often stereotyped by outsiders as raging misandrists, their main focus is on the protection of women, rather than the harming of men. Under Pegasus’ direction, the tribe accepts all women who seek shelter there, and kicks out all men once they are of age, however there are some men raised in the tribe, such as the Metis, who were born sterile, and are thus allowed to remain. While sterile men from outside of the tribe are technically allowed in as well, they must prove to be above any sort of hate for womankind if they hope to retain their place. Should any who dedicate themselves to him be cruel to women for no good reason, Pegasus casts them out into the dirt for their hypocrisy.
        • Callings: Guardian, Judge, Therianthrope
        • Purviews (4): Beasts (Horses), Journeys, Metamorphosis, Sky
        Rat, Totem of the Bone Gnawers
        There is no tale to tell for the Totem of the Bone Gnawers, the God of Rodents and Disease. No spirit, save Chimera, is as amorphous and changing as Rat is. Regarded by some as a male God of War and Plagues, and others as a caring Mother Goddess, there are many facets to Rat’s character, so much so that it’s difficult to keep track of all of them. This is not helped by a largely obscure backstory with conflicting details, where there are any. Because of this, the tale of Rat cannot accurately be told. But, perhaps… that’s just how Rat wants it.
        Out of all the totems, Rat prefers Born Scions the most, though the “how” in accomplishing this is not always moral, or even legal. Though never a rapist in his male assortment of forms, as a woman Rat has been known to extort high-profile targets with the fact of her pregnancy, while in other Incarnations she raises loving families with poor endearing men. Because of the risk in guessing wrong with which Incarnation embodies which aspect, many foreign Gods have decided to avoid “sticking their dick in crazy” as a safety precaution. Hardly anyone can blame them when taking into account a mainstay of Rat’s incarnations besides children: Diseases. Whether this be an office worker with a perpetual cough, or a bug chaser looking to knock an illness off the bucket list, Rat is disgusting. Filthy. Just as Rat wishes to be.
        Nearly all of Rat’s Scions, save for the Incarnations, receive their “Visitation” in the form of a sickness known as the Birthing Plague. Rather than being selective in which of his children have their divine heritage awakened, she instead puts them through a trial by fire, with those who die of the Plague obviously not being worthy enough, and those who survive receive newfound strength as is their right. Even among the other Totem spirits this practice is quite contentious, to say nothing of how other Pantheons view it. Many of her children understandably hate his guts, even as they enjoy their Gifts. Beyond this, her children tend to be so varied in terms of appearance and personality as to not even be identifiable, except for a high resistance to illness in adulthood.
        Rat’s tribe, the Bone Gnawers, don’t tend to focus on Rat’s disease aspect as his critics do. Instead, they focus on her cunning, battle prowess and just overall survivability. Treated as garbage by many, they embrace this image in order to recruit new members. Falcon may be exceptionally picky about who joins his tribe, but just about anyone fits right in with the Rat pack. Just don’t mention being a Scion of Owl, those two have some bad blood going back millennia.
        • Callings: Lover, Trickster, Therianthrope
        • Purviews (5): Beasts (Rat, Insects), Death, Deception, Epic Stamina, War
        Fenris, Totem of the Get
        Hear the tale of Great Fenris! He who was one of the first, and the greatest, of the Garou, who battled that bastard Odin and made him flee in fear! While every other Garou founder has disappeared into the eroding river of history, he alone stands firm and strong. Though greatest among his kindred, he refused to take a wife who could not prove herself to be his equal in strength and character. This left him to wallow in loneliness for countless years until a chance encounter in battle would provide a glimpse to his future spouse, Sigun. Though they fell for each other quickly enough, Sigun was already promised to another, and so Fenris challenged her prospective husband, a Jotun, to single combat for her hand. Was it any surprise that he won? Long after the two had wedded and had children, the Gallows-God, Odin, was tricked into razing Heimhalla itself and killing Sigun for fear of Fenris’ power, to which the Great Wolf responded by pursuing Odin’s demise for the rest of time.
        Even today, the Aesir and their spawn fear the might of Fenris. They portray him as Titanspawn, and seek to ruin his reputation with other pantheons by promoting this image. Not like any besides the idiots among the Yazata and the Tuatha actually believe them, of course, they’re smarter than that. Great Fenris does not care for such false claims, not when action speaks far more than words. When he acts, he does so at his full strength, with no quarter given to his opponents. In the World, he takes the form of soldiers, police, athletes, or any other kind of “strong” archetype or career. He Choses individuals which demonstrate an inner strength, not necessarily an outer one, that can’t be questioned. Those that falter, he turns away from, injures or even kills. While he is a Wolf, he does not uplift these as Created Scions as often as you might expect, though it’s not unheard of for him to do so. He also does not have direct children, refusing to have any child with a woman who is not as strong and willful as Sigun was, quite the challenge for the average woman. Uniquely among the Totems, he produces the greatest amount of Incarnation Scions, due to his origin as one of the first Werewolves.
        He presides over the Get, a tribe of strength devotees who occupy Scandinavia and venerate the Great Wolf. While not as muscle-brained as some make them out to be, they have had a history of misogyny which has only been purged recently, and some say not fully. Beyond this, they are exceptionally judgemental of others in regards to their strength, and are even more so towards themselves. Before any individual can be considered a tribe member, even the kids of one, they must pass various trials to demonstrate both inner and outer strength. In the case that they are accepted, the tests become subdued, more to temper the member’s spirit rather than find his flaws. Climbing up the ranks requires further tests to ensure that whatever chips in the armor there were are fixed or at least being worked upon. No matter one’s position, however, all heed the Great Wolf’s decree: If any refuse a worthy fight, they can, and must, leave the tribe immediately.
        • Callings: Judge, Warrior, Therianthrope
        • Purviews (5): Beasts (Wolves), Epic Stamina, Epic Strength, War, Wyrd

                I would say the Túatha Dé would likely be very quick to believe that any and every wolf is evil and awful. Medieval Ireland (the culture that produced the Túatha Dé) had thoughts about wolves. Mostly that they were forces of degenerate disorder that were solitary creatures that killed to feed perpetual hungers. The term Cú (Hound, Wolf) is used as an element of a large number of warrior names (Conall Cernach, Conchobar, Connla, Cú Chulainn) which is representative of their role as dangerous people who as often lash out at society and betray it as often as support it. The term Cú Glas, 'Grey Hound' or 'Wolf' is a legal term applied to outsiders who have low status, or one applied to people who commit the two most heinous crimes under their legal system, fingal, 'kinslaying,' and incest.

                However, the Túatha Dé are actually very good at... lets say 'breaking' wolves. Ireland under good kingship renders wolves as docile creatures.

                Don't know how much that would influence what you're working on here, but if you want to find ways to fit this into the broader setting of Scion, things like this may be helpful. The Fomori might be a challenge though as the Fomori from Werewolf and from actual Irish sagas are like... the only common feature is the name. You might want to make it just being two different groups that have the same name rather than connecting them.

                (Also as a note about the Yazata, they are beings of ultimate truth. If this guy isn't a Titan they would never in a million years be fooled into a lie to believe he was. The Yazata are righteous, not stupid or cruel.)
