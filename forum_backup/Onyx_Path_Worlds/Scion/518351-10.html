        Originally posted by Úlfhéðnar View Post
        I think we might be disagreeing on exactly what the legend difference means in terms of raw power. I totally agree that Odin's personality and suite of influence lends itself to playing the political game and functioning as a Germanic King, even If Thor might kick his ass in a white room brawl (Odin is more about leading troops than being personally a badass warrior) or that Freyr might out talk him and have more straight respect and admiration, Odin has the ability to beat them in different ways and the will to do so. Odin is basically the God of the Xanatos Gambit.

        My argument is that this is more a matter of characterization and attribute choices than it is a true measure of raw power. Thor, Freyr and Odin are each the most prominent and powerful examples of the things they each specialize in (Roughly Physical, Social and Mental). That's why all three should be legend 12, they are comparable to each other even if in the situation of "Who gets to lead the pantheon" Odin ends up winning. There is much more of a power differential between say, Freyr and Freya (Freyr is canonically more powerful even though they are basically divine twins and share almost entirely the same suite of powers and influence) or Thor and Tyr (Thor just wins). Putting the Triumvirate at legend 12 establishes them as roughly of equal levels of power and the others as being roughly less powerful overall. (Not to mention opening some very interesting possible stories and internal tension).

        Putting Thor and Freyr at Legend 11 puts them both at roughly the same power level as say Tyr or Heimdall, when both are quite superior and more comparable to Odin in what they actually do do.
        Disagreeing is fine if we are wrastling our way through a thought problem. (Since it's all academic anyways. I doubt it means anything to game of Scion as is or will be).

        I don't disagree with your reasoning on power levels, I think. I guess I think there should be an additional "bump" or "perk" from being the acknowledged leader of a pantheon that other gods of similar powers don't have. Essentially a "card" that they can play that doesn't necessarily trump everyone else, but it a meaningful and powerful expression of their current acknowldeged rulership. So, Thor and Freyr might share the same raw level of power as Odin, but, ya know, Odin is still the King and that does MEAN something when comparing him to Freyr and Thor. He isn't king of the gods at their sufferance and they do (mostly) respect his position and defer to him, except when they don't and then Odin has to figure some way to play that card or use his powers to reinforce his position.

        Usually it would be something one god has. (Most pantheons have some designated singular leader or leadership position.... the Celts definitely have one that transitions between different divinities) Sometimes it would be shared with another god, typically a divine marriage. And sometimes perhaps in a very tight group of deities who effectively share leadership. Then they would exercise the "rulership" collectively and probably individually when dealing with any other god who is not part of their clique.

        Meaning Odin, Thor, and Freyr may be the "Big Three" of the Aesir(Vanir), but Odin is still KING. Whereas Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva are the "Big Three" of the Devas, but they are all equally powerful and share the "idea" of being the ultimate divine power together as the Trimurti. Brahma doesn't rule over Vishnu and Shiva. He does different things than they do and each gives way to the others as is appropriate. And they are definitely in charge of the Devas now, even though Indra was once the King in a more typical rulership role.

                                Originally posted by Nervaqus987 View Post

                                1) You mentioned that there's four different kinds of Scion and that Aphrodite had Scions of all varieties. Would you mind telling what those are?
                                I'll tell you three, and the fourth you can guess if you read every supplement in 1e. Ask me at Gen Con and I'll confirm it.

                                1) Be a Scion of god and mortal born, like 100 percent of the people on this planet (though I'm not sure in vitro would work, and I don't think it would, so knock a few decimal points off, I guess). Also works for adopted Scions, but you always retain some part of your parentage, even if Auntie Athena raised you and you really take after her.
                                2) Be a Scion created by a god from inanimate materials, brought to life by miraculous means. Enkidu is my go-to example. It takes a decent bit of work on the part of the god, even if you're Ninhursag (a top-tier creation deity), and it takes some association with mortality (thanks, Shamhat), but you're essentially custom-created by the god.
                                3) Be transformed in infancy by miraculous means. You must be the descendent of mortal nobility, or possibly have some sort of divine lineage far enough in your line to be suitably transformed. Triptolemus is my go-to example; Adonis would also be suitable, though it raises some questions from his inclusion in the Phoenician pantheon*. But I'm not about to limit character concepts, because clearly the vehicle towards Scionhood is there, so you can take his Greek origin as an acknowledgement of "yeah, that's a way".

                                2) How possible is it to create something new? Like, there's a lot of myths about Gods creating new kinds of animals or things like that. For example, Poseidon creating horses or whatever. How doable is that and if so, what general ballpark would you have to be in to pull it off(Hero, Demigod, God)?
                                Demigod to God. Ixion created the centaurs, both horse and biker variants. He still does, the jerk.

                                Creating new subrealms and sub-pantheons is something you can do as a god. New Overworlds, pantheons, creation myths and altering the structure of the World is an appropriate end of a major, years-long epic campaign.

                                3) Revisiting an old question from a couple months ago, is War still your favorite Purview or has it been ousted.
                                Actually, right now it's Fire, because we've been using it as a go-to example of how gods with a similar Purview have radically different expressions; the three characters we've been bandying about are Brigid, Loki and Agni. The first only has it because of syncretized Catholicism, and the metaphorical association with ritual purification and healing. Loki's expression is because his mother is lightning and his father is pine needles, but also because he (maybe) beat up a god with a similar name who was the literal expression of fire, stole that god's mantle, and threatened to burn the whole fucking place down**. Agni's expression of the Purview encompasses fires in virtually all forms metaphorical and literal, including inspiration and also blowing shit up.

                                *Maybe Adonis was originally Greek and joined the Phoenician pantheon in the Scion setting. I haven't really decided yet either way.
                                **We also discussed expression through dogs as a joke, but talking Marvel raises a lot of hackles around these parts.

                                                    Just some questions:

                                                    Was Loki being a mare used against him in the flytting? He definitely had no issue using every event in Norse mythology against them. Flytting are two way streets, insult contests. (To which the Aesir were pretty poor losers since they were supposed to offer Loki a drink for beating them).

                                                    Was screwing animals cited as a specific offense in legal codes? Germanic and Norse people were ridiculously litigious. If they had a problem with it, they would have a law against it, a prescribed punishment and a fee you could pay to get out of it.

                                                    I don't know the answers

                                                    The Norse weren't as self-effacing and face based as your are portraying them. Being a canny individualist who could take advantage of a situation was very well-thought of. And it wasn't to the just make one's family more prestigious. Self-aggrandizement and individual initiative was prized. Coming from a bilateral non-lineage kinship based on difficult acquisition of resources (common to Inuit and modern Western Culture), it is common to see cultures with strong lineage and clan collectivization mechanisms as being face based, but, they . ertainly don't have to be. People may be accountable to family, but family is not their measure. Lots of sagas about setting out and doing better on your own for yourself. And you get stories about that. Which means it's considered laudable. Also, even having bragging and flytting means you aren't looking at a culture where honor is specifically tied to a lineage based self-identity.

                                                    And, no, it's still not Cthulhu-esque. It's obviously not unthinkable if people think badly of it. It's ENTIRELY thinkable. We can think about cannibalism and consider it awful Same with slavery and incest. People who do it are bad people. If you think people who do it are bad people, you've thought about it. None of that is Cthulhu-esque. It's not like the Norse would see said act and their minds would not be able to process it. It wouldn't be like suddenly there was a 5th lateral direction or an explosion of a new color that no one had ever seen before or both man and horse turned inside out, conjoin and then sprouted pustule/eyes and tentacles or simply that seeing such a thing would reveal to them that the world was so much less than they thought it was and they were less than nothing with consciousness just a delusion of dancing motes in the eyes of, not the unknown, but the unknowable. They would see it and have a very specific set of thoughts, like, say, "Better kill that guy for doing something that is just wrong."
