                        Originally posted by Telgar View Post
                        I'm not sure that makes a lot of sense. If you have the Arabian Pantheon, why are they wiped out by the rising Abrahamic Pantheon (which they coexisted with for thousands of years)? The Greek, Norse, Irish, Slavic and Gaulish Gods weren't wiped out by the Christanization of their people.

                        And I'm still having trouble seeing Gabriel coming down and banging a mortal woman to create a Scion. Sure, the entire Pantheon could do nothing but adopt, but then they're adopting the literal spawn of demons (non-Abrahamic gods) to be their own warriors... and plus you have that issue with Apotheosis where mortals are becoming Archangels? Or whatever else you use to stand-in for the "Gods" of a one-God religion.
                        A few points:

                        First, I’m not necessarily positing a Pantheon, either in the singular or as a “pantheon” in the same sense that Egyptians, Greeks, etc. have pantheons; the jury’s still out on that.

                        Second: according to the “Gaulish Pantheon” Scion supplement published in France, the Gaulish Pantheon was all but destroyed (by the Greek Pantheon’s Roman Empire rather than by the Christianization of its people; but the precedent is there). I’m personally of the opinion that Pantheons of religions for which real-world research material is sparse should be treated as either devastated or missing in the context of Scion. As well, the posited devastation of the Arabian Pantheon isn’t the result of the mortal population being converted to Islam; it’s the result of the Islamic “Overworld faction” going to war with the Arabian Pantheon directly.

                        Third: if we posit that the Abrahamic “Pantheon”(s) is/are composed of angels who rival the power of the gods, then the scions of said Pantheon(s) would be nephilim. Obviously, it’s not a practice that the Pantheon condones under normal circumstances; but with the siege of the Titans, they may be willing to do things for the sake of survival that they wouldn’t otherwise even consider.

                        Fourth: this is another place where the notion of Champions (mortals who can achieve Hero status without actually being descended from any known gods) could prove useful, especially if the PSP includes some means of identifying and empowering potential Champions. And if we go with the Catholic notion of Saints as the end result of what happens when and if an Abrahamic Champion achieves Legend 9 (assuming they can even go beyond Legend 4), you have the potential for a “Pantheon” composed of a mixture of angels and saints.

                        Again, I don’t have a fully fleshed out and rigorously playtested concept here; just some rough ideas of how it might be made to work.
