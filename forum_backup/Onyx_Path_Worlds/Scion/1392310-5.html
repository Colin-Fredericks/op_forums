                The thing is, to me the problem I have with Scion is not the rules per se, but how it is written. Look at other RPGs that are even more flexible than Scion: Cortex Prime (or the older and awesome, Firefly, Smallville, Levrage, Marvel Heroic), The Spire, The Heart, Blades in the Dark, City of Mist, Dresden Files Accelerated, Ironsworn, Mutant City Blues, and others. They are all RPGs more narrative driven instead of mechanics driven, some have more detailed mechanics and systems, others have so few, but everyone of them are clearly written. The rule is X or Y, and is intended to do A or B, if the Game Moderator and the Table wants to change, now they know how to game behaves.

                Scion has the problem of a more narrative driven game baked inside a more mechanical driven game. Scion is like trying to make Fate or Powered by the Apocalypse inside the Chronicles of Darkness system. It can work, but either you make everything very clear or it will not achieve anything you intended.

                And yet, in the campaign that I'm the Storyguide here in Brazil, we are now after 14 sessions of a Origin campaign, transitioning the characters for Hero level, and the book still doesn't have all the errata in it (the digital version). We still have many doubts about what the system is really trying to say, still in the Hero book there are parts that contradicts each other, like the Knack section about the knacks everyone starts. Still there are references to parts in the book that either doesn't exist, or are not clearly defined, still there are tags that does the same thing other tags do (like Bashing and Stun).

                The other RPGs I mentioned are much more cheaper and present all the system much more clearly. If I want to play Scion as a Hero, I need to buy 2 books that still deliver a system where it can be difficulty to really understand what the devs intended. It is a critique about what the people on Scion have been doing. It is not to thrown people under the bus, but they need to take a closer look to these things and learn from their mistakes. Scion 2e is still a game I won't recommend to people if I must choose between recommending Scion or City of Mist, for example.

                When I Moderate games I try to always be the fairest possible, and as with the rules as written today, sometimes it is really hard, specially when dealing with Birthrights.
