            Personally I would just avoid having a "this is how the World is" section at all, and make the Myth Level dials the default, so everyone can build the setting to their personal liking.

            I kind of like the semi-covert Masquerade from 1e, but it would need to be done better. The 2e setting where stuff like "Oh, everyone knows Astarte is real, look, here's her instagram account" doesn't sit right with me at all. It's kind of a Superheroes without costumes setting as it is, which is cool but not what I'm after. I know there's probably a commentary to be made somewhere about big name superhero teams being modern Pantheons, which reminds me I need to read Grant Morrison's book Supergods, but nevertheless.

            I would have the gods be absent because of a cold war between pantheons, where they haven't been meddling in the World in obvious ways to avoid setting off their rivals. And I'd have the Titanomachy explicitly take advantage of the fact that the gods were watching each other, not their "defeated" enemies.

            I personally would also just have Dragons be Gods or Titans if they're powerful enough, and regular monsters if they aren't. I feel like the setting has tripped over itself to try and make the "all myths are true and accurate" thing happen, while then also making Dragons a Secret Cohesive Third Faction in a way that isn't really accurate to anything.
            Don't get me wrong, it's NEAT and I'm not dragging the people who wrote or enjoy it, it's just not what I would have done.

                Mythological heroes act more like a band of roving murder hobos, than they do super heroes.

                Cu Chulainn, for example, slaughtered his way through an entire castle, when he found out the king had no intention of allowing him to marry his daughter.

                Hercules was cursed with psychotic rage, that lead to him being responsible for the death of both his family, as well as his best friend.

                Odin and Thor were taken prisoner by the dwarves when they accidentally killed their prince who was taking the form of an otter at the time. Rather than agreeing to pay the ransom in an honest attempt to make amends, Loki decided to fill the prince's pelt with cursed gold that causes the dwarves to begin murdering each other in a struggle to claim it for themselves.


                Originally posted by Zealot_Vedas View Post
                I would have the gods be absent because of a cold war between pantheons, where they haven't been meddling in the World in obvious ways to avoid setting off their rivals. And I'd have the Titanomachy explicitly take advantage of the fact that the gods were watching each other, not their "defeated" enemies.
                It's not a conflict between the gods we have to worry about. The gods are defined by their connection to humanity, and giving them a greater sense of agency.

                The problem with a cold war, is that the Titans are very much different in that regard. They are defined by a complete detachment from humanity, and are dominated by the urge to fulfill their purviews.

                Titans and their respective Scions lack the very capacity to wage a cold war. It's like starting an open flame in the middle of the forest, and then expecting the fire will honor a civil agreement not to burn the place down.

                Cold Wars might be possible, if the setting takes place in a world where the Titans are still imprisoned, but trying to break free. If they're already free and roaming the land, however, there's no way in hell that anybody is keeping a lid on that.



                Even if we ignore their real life personalities and just assume the various pantheons would be cool with letting each other do their own thing... the mortals who worship them are a completely different story. History is replete with accounts of us trying to convert each other, either peacefully or at the point of a sword (or some other equivalent). As well as there being cases where rival cults try to prove which god is superior by calling out to them to perform miracles.

                So regardless of how much they might want to avoid drawing each other's ire, the Gods are going to be pulled into conflict with each other one way or another.

                        Have you made one to sell to a general audience and then had to deal with the reactions to it? Because there's a significant different calculus for profession products than fan ones.

                        And no offense, but as a practicing Jew? I've never seen an attempt to do an "Abrahamic" "pantheon" that doesn't come off as wildly misinformed fan-wank. Mostly because you need to draw 99% of your inspiration from apocrypha over scripture (or decide that Ethiopian Orthodox is the correct version of scripture). "Biblically accurate angels" just look like humans. The Internet meme entirely comes from post-Exilic stories that never made it into the vast majority of canons. Biblical Nephilim? Again, accurate to what? Two (maybe three) sentences that tell you nothing (again, unless you dip into the Book of the Watchers)?

                        If you want to actually stay true to Scion's whole premise... you should be making a Western Semitic Pantheon that combines the Ugaritic, Assyrian, Canaanite, and pre-Excilic Israelite practices we have tons of actual evidence to reconstruct. The Divine Council is actually a structure of pantheon that isn't as prominent in Scion because the members of it are all patron deities of individual people-groups with lots of overlap in portfolios instead of each filling a cosmological niche and the differences stem from who they were worshiped by and what that land was like. You can leave it up to individual groups if you want to keep the Pantheon members that were diminished in reality through wars and conquests as still being around and active, or if only one of them managed to survive all of that with serious power and has claimed the leadership of the Pantheon for themself.

                                    Other issues you encounter with including Abrahamic Pantheons; 1) You immediately start the whole "Isn't capital 'G' the ultimate deity? It isn't worth the hassle, frankly, and I can see why they avoid it.
