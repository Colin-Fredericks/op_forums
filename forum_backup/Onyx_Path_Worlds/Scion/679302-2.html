    And the Aeneas thing trumps pretty much everything else in the "Rome" thing. Though the "Aeneas" thing was mostly Caesar pushing his agenda and getting some serious help on the literary front.... The Remus/Romulus & Mars story is much much older and intrinsic. But that only solidifies the Mars/Venus role in the cabal. Plus, Mars would probably want her along, and she would probably do it in a locked negative homeostatic feedback loop with her husband Vulcan/Hepheastus on the other side of the divide. (There might be some issues between Athena and Hephaestus after that whole rape thing too...)

    Roman sea power was of central importance and Neptune wasn't a non-entity by any means. Roman sea power was key to the strength and integrity (even the existence) of the Republic and then the Empire. Some of the greatest battles of Rome were naval battles (like 11 during the Punic Wars and then the Battle of Actium, which created the Empire, etc.). One of the biggest claims to fame of Pompey was defeating pirates that were plaguing Roman trade. An integral part of the Pax Romana was keeping the Mediterranean open for said trade. Neptune's temple may not have been on the forum, but it was on the Campus Martius, So, not inconsequential at all. And he had a much more significant cultic presence in the logical locations of the actual ports that served Rome (Cosa, Ostia). Mussolini may not have built up his navy, but Neptune would probably have a serious interest in having a new Empire controlling the sea. Much more than, say, Njord might.

    Minerva was nigh as important as Juno in Roman worship, but you put her on the other side, in fact LEADING the other side, so, while I like Juno where you put her, it's not like it's a lock. You've split the Capitoline Trio into it's constituent thirds. So Jupiter is off puttering around not taking sides, even though he's as, if not more important, to Rome than his wife. Juno was the guardian of Rome as polity sure, but she seems to have been relatively cool with whichever way the government swung, though the implications seem that she was more important to the Republic (vis-a-vis the Senate) than the Empire (vis-a-vis the Emperor). (Though, that is actually dipping out of mythology, Scion's source, into religion, which is, in Sun Tzu's parlance, "dangerous ground" when used as background for game-related decisions.)

    I'm not sure I see the role of Dionysius in regards the mural (other than it's actual existence as a piece of art, but, even then, there are other deities who fit the bill as muses for such things). In any case, if that's enough to put him on the side of the Allies, why isn't Zeus? I mean, the whole eagle carrying thunderbolts as the national seal and all. That's a much bigger overall link than the mural. In fact, it's even in the mural. Plus, apparently his own son raising Hades out in the West. It just seems like Dionysious/Bacchus would be the proverbial fiddler while Rome was burning, either intrinsically or burning something else.
