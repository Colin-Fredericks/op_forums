    It's pretty silly to argue that your position is more "realistic" when it concerns the opinions of gods. Ultimately, gods are fictional, and what opinions they would or would not have is a matter of internal consistency, not realism. You can make the gods believe whatever you want them to believe, as long as you can present it in an internally consistent way. And personally, I would rather spend my energy coming up with internally consistent explanations for why the gods might accept LGBTQIA+ people than explanations why they would reject us. We get enough people bending over backwards to justify rejecting us in real life, we don't need more of it in our leisure activities.

    Side note: not to bring cross-fandom drama into this discussion, but this is also my stance on the "biggotry should be more rampant in the World of Darkness because it's supposed to be Darker Than Real Life" argument.

            I figured Charlaquin would come around here eventually. Good to see you.

            Anyways, I suppose this is all a consequence of a much larger discussion as to what Scion is supposed to be about. It is it a game about modern people encountering ancient, all-powerful and perhaps very, very different ideological beings which than breeds an interesting and perhaps disturbing culture clash? Or it an adventure series where valiant heroes fight monsters while Zeus/Shango/Odin/Amaterasu acts as the protagonists cankerous but benevolent mentor figure?

            I'm not seeing that this game can't be American Gods and Percy Jackson at the same time, or that has to be either Grimdark or Noblebright, but I do think that there needs to be some kind of general understanding about where Scion as a game stands when it comes to the Sliding Scale of Idealism vs Cynical. Is it a game where Zeus regularly commits rape by deception, or is he just a lovable lech that can't keep it in his pants? Is it a game where the Primordial vs God War is a mostly unseen conflict that barely affects mortals, or it an apocalyptic nightmare that often gets entire nations wiped from the map? This kinda of relates back to the LBGT acceptance debate in that in a darker game setting those "realistic" issues are probably going to something that I imagine will take up a lot of word space in the Core Book, instead of being perhaps to quickly dismissed as a non-issue in a lighter, fun adventure setting.

            Of course, the Sliding Scale of Idealism vs Cynical could be completely irrelevant if it turns out that Scion is agnostic in what kind of tone it is assumed to have.

            So, yeah, before I can even get a understanding on how the gods would react to modern isms, phobias, and other forms of bigotry I think it would help if we got a confirmation about whether Scion is taking the Aeon route and going with a decidedly optimistic tone. If Scion does goes optimistic it will be interesting to see how Neall makes events and deities like Ragnorak and Kali less grimdark.

And people opinions on different Gods might bring different ideas of how they work, specially on Gods I'm not familiar with.

It is all about of suspension of disbelief, how much you can accept of "unrealistic" things before saying is too much. Scion is a very unrealistic Game, 1ed even had a small text box about "physics of cool" or something like that.
