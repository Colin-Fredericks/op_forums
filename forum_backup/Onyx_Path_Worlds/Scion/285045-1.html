        Originally posted by BlueBiscuit View Post

        I'm still thinking on what to do when it comes to the actual plot of my game. I kinda want to have the first story be the actual first meeting and adventure as a group for the PCs, unlike handwaving it away like we always do.
        I'm a bit stumped at the moment however. I'm looking back to my old mythology books to try and get inspiration, but I haven't really found anything that I like as the focus for the story. The example story in the book isn't really to my liking, even though I still like Vegas as the setting for it.

        Does anyone have any thoughts on the PC's? Suggestions or ideas from your own games would be incredibly appreciated.
        You can try to go the wrote where there is some chain of previous relation and interaction. It can work. It usually doesn't provide the impetus for a group to STAY together which is a very important and neglected part of building a group. Unless they are siblings, lovers, childhood friends, brothers/sisters-in-arms, etc. you are going to have to fall back on something that might be as arbitrary as handwaving them all into group. Arbitrary in the sense of - FATE forces you to stay together, one/some/all of the divine parents DEMAND the group to stay a group, etc. Which can really effectively beat down on some characters who have concepts that would lead them away from the group. You can offset this by getting the players on board with making their decisions bend towards solidarity to the band when faced with a choice. But overall, 'we all know each other' or 'i know him and he knows her and that guy is his SO and her brother' might work, but it might not.

        Particularly since you have really solid character concepts in place already, you are going to need them to retool their histories or go another way. Wouldn't the cat burglar want to burgle? What if the OCD assassin doesn't want that b/c he feels compelled to find his next target? And the bouncer might want to stay near the bar, figuring that a job which provides cash for food is worth keeping.... etc. etc. There definitely needs to be something to glue them all together. Or plan in lots of downtime for them to pursue their own objectives.

        Hmmm..... ways that I have started off a group with the hope they stay together:

        1st game: a 'strange attractor' - something happens that all the characters have some independent reason to get involved in - rumors of zombies lead to the forensic scientist, the tabloid reporter and biker who seems to have lost a buddy. Trick was to keep them acting together until the group got settled as a functioning unit by having the initial plot be big enough to keep the arc going whenever they needed a push. Others started regarding them as a group before they thought of themselves that way and began to act ON them as a team... That promoted some cohesion.

        2nd game: (Probably the best overall game) they started off in a hospital-prison hooked up to IVs with some clear shining fluid in it and amnesia that went back to before their Visitations. (They did stumble across their Relics and know that they were 'important' somehow... I was not THAT cruel). So it started with a Great Escape feel, then a Wilderness Trek, then trying to recover their memories and figure out what had been done to them, all while dodging the people who captured them then hunting them down....

        3rd game: The Venn diagrams of what their parents wanted and what their enemies wanted overlapped (thanks Fate!) then all ended up in a highway reststop when an unseasonal blizzard hit The rest stop restaraunt workers were possessed by wendigo and had adulterated the hamburgers with human flesh... all the mortals started becoming cannibal monsters.... the master of the wendigo got away, but left a clue as to where they were headed next (which also explained the blizzard).... They also found out that there was some kind of restriction or geas on them all that kept them confined to the American West. Lots of plot points were all about the PCs as a group.

        4th game: easy.... setting was Iraq during the war. They were all military personnel and associated contractors that got put together into a specialized unit. The military characters were invested in being in the military, so they were following their orders. The contractors stayed b/c the group was doing the stuff they were interested in.

        5th game (current): the band members were independently approached by a corporation and offered very lucrative positions in a division within the company. Sorta Faustian. They were definitely enticed with things their characters wanted. They are the least nice Scions we've ever had and they were okay with the situation. Though, now, at demigod, they are FINALLY starting to question what is going on....

        Hope that helps.

                    .
                    It was a combat heavy, gruesome beginning, but it didn't stay that way. Though, something you WILL find out.... Scion is designed to be pretty combat heavy. Everything else is pretty vague and you WILL have to do a lot of handwaving to make social characters not useless or over-useful. And a LOT MORE handwaving to making mental characters useful at all. And even the combat stuff falls down around demigod. Also, no decent monsters for them to fight. They don't seem to be made up to face the sort of characters the game generates. Be warned.

                    If your group is good with a really vague reason to stay together, those might work. That means that they will largely be chucking all those great backstories as, whatever that was all about, they aren't going to be doing that anymore. Which means the PLAYERS need to know that their backstory and current situation was all flavor text. Otherwise, with a group of diehard roleplayers who invest as much in character creation as your characters do, they are going to be weighing "this nagging feeling I should stay with this guy" against 'staying with these people means giving up everything important to me" or "it's more important to me to stay with these guys then try to get my life back" (with the memory loss thing). The memory loss worked okay for us b/c one character was a 14 yr old outcast type (spooky swap girl), another was effectively Paris Hilton (missing time? like when I got drunk last week?), another was an NFL player who had lost his hearing and with it his job (Thor's thunder can be LOUD when you are right in the middle of it). The other guy was a low level exec who hated his job and had no problem never going back....

                            I'll follow Ajax's example and list off my starts.

                            1) The characters were sent to a "Super Secret" Company that was designed to "keep the peace" between all the pantheons ... and give budding Scions something to do so they didn't cause havoc. This was my first ever Scion game run so it was very "go here for random hookup" but it worked.

                            2) They were all recruited as slaves aboard a ship only to realize they were essentially undead and needed to return to the living.

                            3) Three of the group knew eachother and were recruited by the fourth's parent to get them out of a city. Naturally, this is when Apep devours the sun.

                            4) My current game: The Scions were accused of a crime and Fateful Aura placed them all on the same prison transport bus which is then attacked by government black cars.

                            One BIG thing to remember is Fateful Aura. It is your best best best friend as an ST and once you embrace it's potential for sheer happenstance and probability wackiness, the happier you'll be.
