    ​Using the Tuatha de Danann as the as Alfar of Norse Legend

    04-17-2014, 01:10 PM
    I'm a busy person without the time to put extensive research into various legends, most of my knowledge on the Tuatha and the Aesir comes from Scion books.

    Anyway, I'm running the Ragnarok line of adventures for my group so we limit everything to fitting with Norse Legends. One of my players wants to have some association with The Morrigan, thus far we've kind of glossed over it as some odd aspect of Odin. The other issue is limiting myself to Norse characters present in Scion sources very much limits what I can throw around at my players, most of whom are actually fairly knowledgeable of Norse Legends.

    Anyway I had a sudden epiphany as to what may serve to take care of my problems. What if I made the Tuatha into Alfar that didn't join up with Aesir. Justification of this is basically, most references to the Tuatha outside of Scion that I know of, all of which are some form of pop culture, seem to make the Tuatha out to be faeries or elves, and I seem to recall some reference to the Alfar that didn't join the Aesir leaving the world and secluding themselves in Alfarhiem. If we take the world to be the World as known to the Norse peoples, and the Tuatha are themselves immigrants to Ireland it doesn't become to much of a stretch of the imagination that the Tuatha could be the Alfar.

    So, here I am, asking those that know much more than myself, how viable is this idea or is there far to much conflict of information to make it work?

    Unrelated, but I seem to recall something saying the Vanir are the Alfar that joined with the Aesir? I can't find where it is said, but I recall reading it.

      Sup, blue-sun-guy. Mind if come along all smooth like and try to blow your Irish conundrums with my oh so massive mythological think pan. I'll take your silence as a yes, blue-sun-guy.

      One of the problems your getting to get from turning the Tuatha into fairies, let allow the Alfar in particular, is that the Tuatha connections to the fair folk is a relativity recent add on to their mythology. In fact, the Sidhe (basically the Irish word for Fairies) aren't really mentioned at all in their older myths. No fairy queens, no changelings, no brownies, no goblins and definitely no androgynous looking, pointy eared elves with bows and arrows. So if your trying to re-imagine them as Tolkienesque elves or even Shakespearean fairies, your not going to get much help from the actual mythological to back that up.

      To be honest, character-wise the Tuatha are incredibly similar to the Aesir in regard to their love of war, alcohol, poetry and their hatred of tricksters and fated doomed, which is why I suggest you just have the Tuatha as distant cousins to the Aesir or alternatively as Vanir that never assimilated into the Aesir proper like Freya and Freyr did.

      That being said, if your really bent on having them as Alfar there is a much later legend in their mythology that states that after having their asses kicked by the Milesians (Later immigrants to Ireland from Spain) most of the Tuatha decided to retreat into Tir Na Nog (The Land of Youth and the Tuatha's Overworld.) Those who decided to stay eventually turned into the Sidhe, who as already mentioned were basically fairies.

              Originally posted by Hark View Post
              The question really being, do Norse Legends tell enough about the Alfar to say that the Tuatha really don't fit the role?
              The Alfar aren't Gods, they aren't on a parity-level with the Aesir or the Vanir. They're Legend 2-3, maybe Legend 5-6 for some of them. They're distinctly not a foreign culture of Gods that the Aesir are putting a label on, they're a race of supernatural creatures that exist firmly inside Norse cosmology. There are Aesir and Vanir (especially Vanir) with very strong connections to the Alfar. Freyr and Idun especially.

              So taking the Tuatha, an entirely different Pantheon of gods with their own stories and identities and trying to fit them into a role as magical creatures from another cosmology is doing a pretty big disservice to both sides. If you're limiting yourself to Norse cosmology only, then your player that wants to chill with Morrigan is already breaking your rules. Morrigan isn't a Norse figure. The nearest anything comes to her is maybe one of the three Norns, since the Morrigan is sometimes a triple goddess figure and the Norns serve the three-fates role for the Norse.


              It doesn't take long to read the Eddas (a few hours) or the Sagas. There aren't exactly tons of primary sources for the Norse myths. Really, man, it just depends on if you actually care if you're being true to mythology or not. If you don't care at all, sure, say there's an Alfar named Lugh and one named Nuada and all that stuff. It'll certainly confuse and surprise your players.

                              I did something very similar in my first Scion game. The Tuatha may not have been the Alfar or the Vanir but they were closely enough related that Ratatosk saw the hosting of the Tuatha "from afar" when he was high in the bows of Yggdrasil and some the Vanir that live with the Aesir were getting a little squirrely about having to check in "back home" in Vanaheim.

                              In that case, I was having the Vanir be one pantheon of a overarching collective of Celtic pantheons that included the Tuatha De and the Dau Twyleth ("Two Families", my take on the Welsh pantheon). The Tuatha were going to war and they were calling in all the old markers with their distant kin.

                              As far as it being mythological goes.... well, the Germanic peoples who gave rise to the Norse and the Germans moved into territory previously dominated by Celtic people (hard not to since Europe was Celtic from Iberia to Anatolia). So, Celtic people = Celtic gods which I was fine with interpreting as being Vanir, Germanic people = Germanic gods which the game has defaulted to Aesir (quite correctly). There is definitely something to be said for tying the Vanir to the pantheon of some pre-Germanic people in the area, so why not the Celts? The two are temporally adjacent in their dominance of the region.

                              As far as Sidhe/Alfar/Tuatha/Vanir goes, I think it's a tohmaytoh/tomahtoh thing. The Sidhe are pretty much explicitly the Tuatha after the Third Battle of Mag Tuireadh. Angus Og is explicitly identified as King of the both. Midhir goes from being the King of that quarter to the King under the particular mound associated with that quarter. Etc. Etc. It's hard to tell whether the Vanir and Alfar were different people. Harder than whether the Svartalfar and the Dvergar are. Freyr may be King of the Alfar because he is, or should be, King of the Vanir. When he gets exiled in the big hostage trade, maybe he lost the title of King of the Vanir to Hoenir and was allowed to keep the title of King of the Alfar....

                              So, I think, if it fits your story, go for it. It's all a card toss in a hat anyways. It worked for me because one character was a Scion of the Morrigan and another was a Scion of Odin with Ratatosk as his Guide. So I decided to connect a few dots to make the story more interesting and didn't feel like I was sacrificing my "mythological integrity" in doing so.

                                      There really is not a lot of mythological precedent for it. In fact, there is more mythological precedent for many of the Tuatha and the Aesir to be the same gods. They have so many things in common that they are more than likely diaspora branches of some earlier proto-pantheon.

                                      I would recommend against trying to categorize the Tuatha as Alfar. It is probably better to simply cameo the Morrigan as an ominous and dangerous figure from another pantheon. Otherwise go with the suggestion Aynie made: make the Morrigan be a particularly commanding Valkyrie that spreads blood lust upon the battlefield, and Chooses the Slain.

                                          Under that logic, the pan-Celtic, Germano/Norse, Slavic, Baltic, Hittite, Greek, Latin, Persian, Tocharian, the Vedic Indian gods are all the same pantheon. Just run the names back to proto-Indo-European and ignore all the family relationships, since they re-sort all over the place.

                                          Since mythology is an artifact of culture, culture changes and a lot of it can't be recovered, do what makes your game work for you and your players. You can be a lumper (as per the above) and glom pantheons together or a splitter who separates things out as much as you can (no Dodekatheon for me! Roman gods, Etruscan gods, Greek gods - all different pantheons).

                                          There's no reason from a comparative mythology standpoint not to relate the Vanir to Celts. There is some good historical linguistic evidence to indicate that is a definitely possibility. It could go the other way and they may be more related to the Sami (or, to be precise, the people whose gods the Vanir might represent may be the same people who are also the ancestors of the Sami). Or not.

                                          Do it your way, Hark. Have some fun with it. You want to include the Morrigan in an Aesir-o-centric game, the Alfar are probably your best shot. It's pretty definite there is some relationship in cultural ideas behind the Alfar and the Sidhe, even though the first are one of the "peoples" of the 9 worlds created when things were getting separated out and the second are the "guys who got left behind" when the world shifted from the divine to the mortal. It's even a good bet that the Vanir may have a very similar relationship to the Alfar as the Tuatha De have to the Sidhe (sort of the elite to the "commoners").
