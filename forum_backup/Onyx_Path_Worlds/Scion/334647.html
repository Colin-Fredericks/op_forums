    I am trying to figure out by-the-book Hardness.

    Since Soak subtracts from levels of damage (usually), and it is difficult to gain Hardness in excess of your Soak, I do not understand the purpose of this mechanic.

    Is there something I am missing?

      Hardness, in Scion 1e RAW, is pretty weak. It's something that's a holdover from Exalted 2e that didn't really need to get ported over.

      However, remember that Hardness kicks in compared to the raw damage dice pool, not the rolled damage. It basically protects you from lucky hits.

                                  1) Hardness isn't effected by piercing (much more relevant for Squee's point on mortals and soak).
                                  2) Hardness compares to the raw dice pool value, where Soak compares to successes. This technically means that Hardness sets in before either 10s counting as double or bonus successes to damage apply in the order of operations.

                                  Neither of these guarantee Hardness will matter (and with how Scion is written it pretty much guarantees it doesn't), but it means that Hardness has a valid space in 1e's rules. It was just never used.

                                          I'm not sure why you're arguing on these points Telgar. I'm not championing Hardness RAW as being implemented well. You asked what Hardness can do that Soak can't. I answered. In my answer I flat out said that the way Scion is RAW, Hardness is useless except maybe in a few very edge cases (which is basically Aegis in application). All of my posts to this thread have not been pro-Hardness as written.

                                          And Hardness' ability to stop Epic Strength, while impressive, is fundamentally nullified by Epic Dexterity which can easily generate enough extra raw damage dice to overcome Hardness and let all that Epic Strength come blasting through.

                                          And since we're feeling a bit pendantic about this, it isn't an editing error. It's a design error. There's no mistake in how Hardness is written. There's a giant gaping hole in how the system design uses it (and the very questionable choice of even bothering to include it in the design if you're almost never going to use it in a meaningful way); though it is possible that it was originally supposed to be fairly useless/inaccessible at Hero and was supposed to matter more in the later books, but they never got around to it.
