        Originally posted by Telgar View Post
        Basically, the issue is that Scion says "All Myths Are True" and there's a conflict there. Monotheism specifcally says that All Myths Are NOT True. Only their myths are true. So there's a conflict between Abrahamic God and the polytheistic Gods like Odin, Thor, Ares, Shango. If Abrahamic God is right, then he's Legend Infinity. He's all powerful and all knowing. The other Gods are basically just pretenders, and so are the Titans.

        So there's a few soltuions, all of which compromise either a basic assumption of monotheism, or a basic assumption of the game.

        1) God is really God. He is the ultimate power of the setting, all other Gods are wrong, liars, stupid or pretenders. This renders the entire setting moot, since God could solve everything with a blink of His eye. No conflict, no drama. Scion assumes active Gods, and if God is active and He is All Powerful, He ends the Titan War instantly. Also Titans don't exist, or are really demons, because God is the only God.

        2) God is NOT really God. He's just an idiot and he's really on the same level as all the other deities. That causes issues because then God is by himself in his "Pantheon" of one. If you want to have other figures in there at Legend 12, suitable for standard Divine Parents, then you have to assume that figures like Jesus, Mary, Lucifer, Gabriel, etc, are equal to God and that's not following the mythology. Cept maybe Jesus, and that depends on if you believe Jesus is God or if he's the son of God.

        Either one has some pretty big issues. If you want really in-depth discussion, because this comes up a lot, here are some links to the foremost Scion Blog (one of like, two) and some very cogent thoughts on monotheism in Scion.
        .

                                                        Originally posted by The Revenge of TV Head View Post

                                                        But as you once viciously snarled at me in the old forums, there are people who really do believe in a number of deities featured within Scion, and those belief systems are not always uniform either. So why should those gods be given any more of a pass?
                                                        As a practicing Asatru who is in a long-term relationship with an Evangelical Christian, I may be uniquely qualified to explain.

                                                        Because the nature and identity of the Norse Gods is covered in two texts with a bare handful of apocryphal works intruding upon what might be taken as, our admittedly fallible, canon there's no sense that the nature and identity of these deities needs protecting from the apocrypha and misconception. It really doesn't matter that most of my friends identify Thor with the Marvel character. Nor does it impact my life on any particular level that most people don't realize the days of the week are named after Norse deities. Frankly, when you tell people you believe in Valhalla, glorious death in battle, and Ragnarok, most people admit that it seems like a pretty cool idea of an afterlife. My significant other just nodded and said "That's not really different from Revelations," which is where the problem begins for Christians.

                                                        Revelations is an amusing Cliff's notes take on Ragnarok to most Asatru. These "appropriations" littered the apocryphal texts of Christianity in its infancy. Until they began to be included in Bibles compiled by Emperors and Kings several hundred years later (around the time Islam was codified, incidentally). This creates a problem for most Evangelical (e.g. Bible) Christians, who have to reconcile their faith with the original stories sacred to other faiths. This is made even more morally hazardous because of the belief is that the Bible is divinely inspired (or written by God). If the material in it is plagiarized that bodes ill for the infallibility of the god of Abraham.

                                                        Which is the point people have been trying to make. As Monotheistic faiths, Abrahamic practice requires that there be no other gods, no idols worshiped, and no more prophets. That's Judaism, Christianity, and Islam. Collectively, that's about one-third of the world's population you're going to offend by including an Abrahamic pantheon in a game of urban fantasy, because you cannot reach an end result that is going to satisfy all three faith groups, much less the hundreds of sects within those major faith groups.

                                                        You simply can't squeeze an Abrahamic pantheon in without making it virtually unrecognizable. I'd humbly suggest you consider, instead, a hard look at a Sumerian pantheon, as that's the religion Judaism was derived from.
