    [2E] Damage Conversion Knack Clarification

    10-12-2019, 09:26 AM
    So the Heroic Healer Knack of Damage Conversion reads as follows:

    Damage Conversion: When you administer medical attention to a patient or yourself as an action, convert any Injuries one lower: Maimed becomes Injured, Injured becomes Bruised, and Bruised injuries heal completely.

    Is the purpose here that all Damage is downgraded vs the normal action of First Aid, which only downgrades a single Damage level and only if there is a free Damage slot below? That would make sense as a Heroic Knack, but the wording is not very clear.

    If so, is there a roll or does it activate automatically if I spend my action to provide medical attention via the First Aid action? If it requires a roll do I need one success for all, or does the number of successes determine the upper level of the damage type I can convert? (Ala the First Aid Difficulty table)

    Lastly, is the difference between Damage Conversion and Combat Medic that you can use Combat Medic during combat, but Damage Conversion can only be triggered outside of combat by using the First Aid action? Also Combat Medic can heal armor, while Damage Conversion can not?

    Thanks to anyone's help on this.

      It does indeed seem to be plural with the Knack. So all Injuries can be downgraded by a step (Allowing for the lower ones to be cleared before the heavier ones get downgraded). If you think that this is kinda OP, then you can probably say that a single character can only benefit from Damage Conversion once per session. Also keep in mind that Aggravated Damage, even if downgraded a step, will still require magic to fully heal. But yes, Damage Conversion seems to be, as it happens, a strictly better version of First Aid. Which makes sense for a Healer Scion.

      Most Knacks just work. Like, if a Knack doesn't say "Make a Knack Skill Roll" or "Spend Momentum to-" then it just works unless it's resisted, and in this case Damage Conversion just seems to go off.

      And given that Damage Conversion doesn't explicitly say that it can heal Hard Armor, while Combat Medic says it does, I wouldn't let Damage Conversion fix armor. I would also say that, for the sake of balance, I wouldn't allow Damage Conversion to work in combat just to give Combat Medic a bit more of an edge.

          Thanks for the feedback Kyman201! I think your thoughts align with my initial assessment.

          I would say that Damage Conversion is too OP if it doesn't have a limiter. Because at that point, nothing stops you from using the Knack multiple times in a session and just clearing through all damage. I think something of that power level would be Immortal and not Heroic, though there isn't really an analog if you look through the Immortal Healer Knacks. So limiting it to once a session per player might might sense.

          So completely agree that there is missing "Make a Knack Skill Roll" etc for this one, but it also doesn't say "without needing to roll" (such as with Combat Medic). How it is written seems more in line with something like "The Bare Minimum", which augments/supports an action, rather than being an action. To perhaps clean it up, it needs to be better linked to an existing action to ensure clarity.

          Those being said, I usually air on the side of more balanced. I would love to know your thoughts on the following: (this could include too powerful, or not powerful enough)

          Damage Conversion: When you administer medical attention to a patient or yourself via the First Aid action, with at least one enhancement, convert all Injuries one level lower in this order: Bruised injuries heal completely, Injured becomes Bruised, and Maimed becomes Injured. Taken Out is not affected by the use of this Knack, and all Conditions downgraded in this way do not count against the limits for First Aid. The use of Damage Conversion can only be performed once per session per target.

          I believe this fits the power level and scope of a Heroic level Knack, without being too powerful. It also is linked to, and requires, a First Aid roll. This means that it will be difficult in unfavorable conditions (which might drive up the difficulty) and allow for synergy between other Knacks such as "The Bare Minimum" or "Surgeon with the Hands of God".

                                  After sleeping on it, I thought it would be good to think through it with a few scenarios with Stats (best I can do, as I'm not a Stats dude). I will use FAL (First Aid Limited) and FANL (First Aid Not Limited) to indicate if the healing from Damage Conversion will go against the limits of First Aid or not. FANL will assume First Aid done after with a pool of 8 dice (no additional enhancements), and the target has two (2) Bruised Health boxes.

                                  8 Dice Statistics:
                                  Reducing Bruised to None: 76% chance
                                  Reducing Lethal to Bruised: 50% chance
                                  Reducing Maimed to Lethal: 12% chance
                                  Reducing Taken out to Maimed: (variable based on conditions, and assuming no Maimed) 50% (3 conditions), 76% (2 conditions), 94% (1 condition)

                                  Bruised Only (FAL): No injury
                                  Bruised Only (FANL): No injury

                                  Lethal and Bruised (FAL): One Bruised
                                  Lethal and Bruised (FANL): One Bruised (24% of the time), No injury (76% of the time)

                                  Maimed, Lethal and Bruised (FAL): One Lethal and one Bruised
                                  Maimed, Lethal and Bruised (FANL): One Lethal and one Bruised (12% of the time of the time), One Lethal only (38% of the time), Two Bruised Only (12% of the time), No injury (38% of the time)

                                  Taken Out, Maimed, Lethal and Bruised (FAL): Taken Out, One Lethal and one Bruised (24% of the time), Maimed, One Lethal and one Bruised (76% of the time)
                                  Taken Out, Maimed, Lethal and Bruised (FANL): This one is too complex for me to figure out, so I'll keep to some descriptions:
                                  1.) Maimed, One Lethal and one Bruised (Slightly likely)
                                  2.) Taken Out, One Lethal and one Bruised (Not very likely)
                                  3.) Maimed and One Lethal (Decently likely)
                                  4.) Taken Out and One Lethal (Slightly likely)
                                  5.) Maimed and Two Bruised (Slightly likely)
                                  6.) Taken Out and Two Bruised (Not very likely)
                                  7.) Maimed Only (Decently likely)
                                  8.) Taken Out only (Slightly likely)

                                  So with the assumption of a REALLY good pool (4 Medicine and 4 Intelligence), neutral conditions... you still have a number of instances where people end up still injured. So without using additional Knacks/Boons/etc those injuries are stuck there without normal healing. (days/weeks). If someone gets Taken Out, it's basically a guarantee that they will have lingering injuries unless you get creative with magic, since you have to use First Aid on the Taken Out (and thus not be able to First Aid it again). Factor in environmental difficulties, and it might be tough to even get any healing off.

                                  I have a character with a focus on Healer. I have the pool of 8, Combat Medic Knack, and the Fertility Purview Innate power. If I take on Damage Conversion, if I blow through everything I have after a major combat (so that would be two Knacks, a Purview Innate that's once a day, and investment in to stats/skills) I might be able to get everyone out of there with minor injuries. Those still will take quite a number of in game days to heal completely. Combat is deadly, and minor injuries don't just drop like other systems (or heal after a nights rest). If a very invested Healer can't help keep a party going in a significant manner, then I think it devalues the use of Healing for a story.
