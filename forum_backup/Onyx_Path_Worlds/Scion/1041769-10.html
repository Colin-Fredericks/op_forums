                                                    Gods change with the times. Ancient Greek culture doesn't exist anymore, and it changed considerably over the course of antiquity. Pederasty arose among the aristocracy at a particular point for particular reasons related to power dynamics and prescriptive gender roles, and in time it disappeared as a practice. There's nothing essentially Greek about it. Moreover, there's no reason to believe that the cultures of the ancient Greeks and their gods were identical in all respects to begin with, as evidenced by the frequency with which mortals in ancient Greek literature express mystification at the ways of the gods and how their sense of right and wrong do not always seem to line up with those of mortals. In addition, Zeus is depicted as a dynamic figure in the mythic literature, to the extent that the character of his reign shifted over time.

                                                    Therefore, there's no reason to imagine that Zeus would hold a static opinion about something that was dictated by the cultural values of the mortals who worshiped him millennia ago. That's on top of the fact that ancient Greek gods were never much for micromanaging the cultural practices of their worshipers in the manner of Yahweh. How and whom people married was never regarded as much of a religious issue, much less one the gods expressed strong opinions on. If Zeus's behavior in the myths is any indication, he always regarded mortal institutions like marriage as beneath his notice to begin with.

                                                    In other words, I think there are a lot of Christianizing assumptions built into the original question. The same goes for the rest.
