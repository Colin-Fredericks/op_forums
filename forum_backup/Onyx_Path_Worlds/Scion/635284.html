    [Fan-Made Pantheon] The Great Old Ones (Cthulhu Mythos)

    08-21-2015, 09:11 PM
    Well, it's finally over. I hoped to post this on Lovecraft's birthday but of course I had to catch a fever.

    Hello everybody. Six years or more ago, under the name of Atomic Hermit, I began writing a pantheon based on the creation of H.P. Lovecraft. With the help of some other forumites, I kept writing for a couple of months but then I began the university and I could not find enough time to complete my work. I left it in a pen drive with the intention to finish it but never got around doing that. I recently found that pen drive and the intention resurfaced but it was the message of another member of the forum who wondered what happened to my work that really persuaded me getting back to it.

    Now that I'm almost done with my studies and blessed with some free time, I took a brief pause, spent a couple of weeks writing and now I've finished. For all those who waited this for so long, I apologize. Sorry, life's gets in the way and the last few years were quite turbolent for me. I sincerely hope that what I've done will satisfy you. For all the others, I hope you'll enjoy what I wrote as well.

    I could say more, but most of it is covered in the Introduction Chapter. Please take some moments to read it.

    There's a chapter at the end called Storytelling. It's empty for a reason: while I have a few things in mind that could probably deserve being pointed out when using the Great Old Ones in Scion and roleplaying a Scion of Cthulhu, I prefer to see what points arise from the discussion so I can write the chapter in a coherent way. If no discussion happens, well, I'll write what I planned to do in the first place.




    Any criticism of comments you want to make is really appreciated.

                      I'll add something to the long-dead thread, mostly because I love taking a piss at old HP's...less modern attitudes.

                      Sedibans (Guide ***):

                      Quote: "You. You are of the cousin race, but also of the star race. Why have you come? Speak quickly, and perhaps we will treat you as cousin and not star."

                      Evolution is a messy thing. Contrary to the popular image of it as outlined in The Evolution of Man, it is not a straight line of forms leading to an increasingly intelligent and humanoid being. In fact, it isn't even just survival of the fittest; even before a being has become so genetically distant from its original species that it no longer is capable of breeding with it, there are numerous sub-species that are still more than genetically similar enough to breed with each other, and indeed, said genetic exchange actually is part of what drives the new subspecies into a distinct species of its own, with its own ecological niche. Humanity is no different; while homo sapiens is pretty much the end-all be-all of the non-divine hominids these days, once there was an entire smorgasbord of different kinds of humans with their own unique quirks and appearances. While the pessimistic would say we killed them all for looking strange, in truth the real answer is proof that cynics aren't nearly as all-knowing as they fear; the many subspecies got along so well that their tribal groupings merged, and as children sprung from that union, it just turned out that the appearance and quirks of the sapiens subspecies is dominant (the vast majority of people with European ancestry have at least one Neanderthal in their family history, for example). That same interbreeding and hybridization wasn't something new to hairless apes; back when humans were just bipedal, unusually intelligent monkeys known collectively as australopiths. the subspecies bred easily and freely, powering their eventual loss of hair and ever greater intellect. In fact, most anthropologists believe that, before they went extinct, early hominids had children with the remaining australopiths, ensuring their predecessors would live on through their descendants.

                      These anthropologists are mostly right about that, except for two things: One, "early" can be dropped, and two, because it should really be in the present tense.

                      Sedibans are the sole remaining species of australopith alive, a genetic quirk meaning the homo sapiens in their ancestry is actually recessive. Despite being far more histrute and somewhat more hunched than modern humans, they are actually just as intelligent, and can speak other languages perfectly fine, albeit with something of a lisp due to the shape of their teeth. Sedibans resemble man-sized, white- or grey-furred bonobos that prefer to walk upright, with a rather human face-mostly because, genetically and mentally speaking, they are human. Sedibans are close enough to homo sapiens to produce fertile offspring with the help of some of their sorcery, at which point the hybrids are quite capable of having children with either race until the genetic drift fully reintegrates them into either subspecies. They actually evolved full sapience and souls first due to their less-than-effective natural camouflage forcing them to evolve the capacity to outwit predators. In another world, they might have been the dominant species, but a quirk of Fate caused them to attract the destructive attentions of the obscure Outer God Daoloth to their first real civilization-while he is not nearly as malevolent as much of his kind, Daoloth destroyed most of the Sedibans and sent their entire culture back to subsistence hunter-gatherers, and by the time they recovered in their Congolese Terra Incognita, hairless hominids had gotten too far of a head start and simply taken up too much room and resources for the Sedibans to rebuild. They made their peace with it-these days, while the subspecies is not precisely thriving, they are no longer on the verge of extinction, and are working up the bravery to reveal their existence to their far more numerous cousins after being rather soured on the notion due to chain of events when one of their princesses ran off with a merchant that ended with her mother's corpse being burned by racist Victorians unable to accept that a nobleman could sully the line with someone of such foreign heritage (okay, technically it was due to the fact that, when it comes down to it, Sedibans still look like giant monkeys, but burning such an earth-shattering find as the queen's corpse in the heyday of discovery? That kind of reaction was motivated by rich white idiots unable to accept the fact that humans are of African origins-actually why the discovery of australopiths upset many of those same rich white idiots).

                      Sedibans are not, generally speaking, creatures of Legend, but their lingering memories of Daoloth and the relics of his passing have given them a certain empathy and tolerance for the hidden world; they are very inured to the more insanity-provoking truths and abilities of the Great Old Ones, and a Sediban with the inclination can easily become a witch, or more accurately a "shadowed shaman". Like the shamans of tribal homo sapiens, a Sediban shaman walks a lonely and feared, but usually valued path; their name comes from the fact that they walk in the shadows of their still-standing city for lore of the Great Old Ones they can use to protect their people, while constantly also being shadowed by the temptation to use it for selfish and destructive ends. More importantly, shamans know the secret spells and arts by which Sedibans can disguise themselves as their cousins and walk the world outside their hidden homelands (which isn't nearly as much a culture shock as it may seem-Sedibans regularly import modern technology to help them survive the remaining monsters in the Terra Incognita and stories of what to expect out there with it), and to help them produce children with the sizable minority of homo sapiens tribesmen that dwell with them (although an inexperienced or incompetent shaman often ends up infusing Daoloth's power into the parents-to-be and creating deformed and/or mentally disturbed offspring, as one House of Jermyn discovered). And of course, many Sediban-Homo Sapiens hybrids lack the genes that produce their distinctive fur pelts, allowing them to pass as rather long-limbed but otherwise completely normal members of the cousin race.

                      While Sedibans and Sediban hybrids aren't pacifists, they generally are concerned with their own problems, and thus not open to becoming Followers to some nosy Scion. However, they are very open to sharing what lore they can find in their ruined city and their own broad knowledge of the Great Old One as Guides in return for a regular tithe of food and tools to help their tribes. While they're open to anyone, Scions of the Great Old Ones are far more likely to know of the reclusive people, though, and thus it's most likely to be one of them who has a Sediban Guide. Most of these are going to be shamans who have gone a little too far into the shadows and secretly worship Daoloth and all of his siblings, but a Scion who shows a respect for humanity might gain a more normal (and more trustworthy) Sediban as a Guide. Said Guide is also likely to be a moral voice, constantly reminding the Scion that he's or she's only half-god, and it's Earth he or she lives on; if the Great Old Ones destroy it, will said Scion be able to live with themselves, knowing all the purely human people they know are gone?

                      (if you want, I can make the last Jermyn as an example of a Sediban hybrid).

                                  Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                  Actually, now that I think about it, this doesn't sound like it would be all that good as a pantheon.                                   Well, you're not wrong. The way I see it (and the way I wrote it) is that the Great Old Ones are forced into conflict with the Titanspawn not because they want to defend mankind or the world, but because they're defending themselves. Losing the human race and other Pantheons would mean giving a foothold to the Titan forces from which they would then inevitably proceed to defeat the Outer Gods as well.

                                  Once free from the Titanspawn menace, the Great Old Ones would probably try to assert their dominance with no regards to humanity. Which brings us to one of the things that I think would be most interesting in a game about the Scion of the Great Old Ones: making them care. Being a Scion of the Outer Gods brings you not only in front of the question whether you should keep traces of your human nature within yourself as you ascend to eldritch godhood, but also that, if you are still attached to mankind, how you manage to conciliate this fact with your divine relatives. Even for Scion standards, the Outer Gods are unequivocally unsuitable at being good parents, especially from a human standpoint. And yet, being one of their Scions puts you in the best possible position to make them start to care about mankind, in a way or the other. On paper, this should be impossible, and my fellow Lovecraft's purists tremble at the mere thought, but if there's a game where you can at least try to accomplish such unconceivable feats, that's Scion.
