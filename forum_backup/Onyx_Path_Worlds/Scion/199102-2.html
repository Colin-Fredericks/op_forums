    Originally posted by James McMurray View Post

    I can agree with that if it's changed to "the system" instead of "the game." As an RPG the game is more than just the system of rules and how it works best is completely dependent on the group playing it.Even the combat system is quickly going to fall apart if the group using it isn't ready for it. For instance, if you have a GM who thinks he can use nothing but stock printed monsters and a group of players (or even just one) who is happy to stretch the system as wide as it can go.
    Sure. I agree on the basis of your differentiation of those terms For the purposes of all of us, as a community, we don't share in any one gaming group's game. What we have in common is the system. I've run 5-6 Scion games which have gone very well to somewhat poorly. The somewhat poorly almost always had to do with a character concept that looks good and even seems workable, then falls flat, leaving a player at the table getting more and more frustrated.

    Ex. We were playing a cycle based in Iraq during the war. The majority of the characters were militadeadlry personnel. One character wanted to be a combat officer. So he built his character as a physically proficient fighter then as a commander with the troops. All the investment in his platoon sucked. They were a drag on the group. I even started throwing platoons of mooks at them, but it just didn't work.

    Ex. 2, Same game. One character was a military intelligence, sneaky and social, another was a sniper, sneaky and deadly. No matter how things got set up, the sniper was always more effective. It wasn't a role-playing issue. Both were great role-players, it was just mechanically better supported to be the sniper instead of the spy.

    Ex.3 In another game, a character played an "expediter", she was the nascent goddess of logistics, mental and social all over, no real combat except to avoid and keep her head down. Great idea, but the mechanics made her very vague as to what she could do, aside from my fiat. The other social character was also the healer/buffer/morale booster (that was the one area the logisitician kept out of), so she was doing fine. But the best and most fun characters in the game? The Hollywood martial arts movie star (physical with some social), the sniper (same as above, since the Iraq game folded we slightly re-tooled him and he ran with it) and the heroic firefighter (brick style physical). Nothing to do with the players, again. I have been playing with most of them for over a decade (the logistics character for two decades). It was just that the rules REALLY supported their characters. It even allowed them to differentiate easily.

Let me 100% point out to you that, as a Storyteller, you having to go the extra mile for a player to make the rules work AT ALL for them puts them at a disadvantage in the game. Like trying to play a mad inventor in D&D (before that became a thing.)

                                        Let me emphasize this AGAIN. THIS IS 100% ABOUT THE RULES AS WRITTEN. You are describing, as are most others, again and again how to work AROUND the rules. Not through them. This is about the lack of support. I'm happy that you can ST your way around the lacks. Me too. YOU SHOULD NOT HAVE TO.

                                        I know that a lot of us find ourselves re-writing rules, borrowing house rules from each other, etc. I know that I have a House Rules file folder FULL of documents . It's getting to the same size as a my re-write up 2nd Ed AD&D back in the mid 90's. I know that I'm in games where we spend tons of time going over how to deal with the lacunae in the system. Guess what? We can handwave all of that away and just game, but the lack of rules STILL comes up. When the rules don't work, you may not even realize that there is GOING to be a problem until it happens. The Iraq game suffered from a problem player. It had multiple characters whose players had managed to find significant gaps in the rules right under their character concepts... like sinkholes.                                         What I am and have been saying 100% explicitly is that the Rules, that we all SHARE, the base code. Sure I can take ANY crappy system and run it and blow past the crappy rules and everyone can have fun. But, when the rules just don't support a character type at ALL, character types they have built into the system and then didn't support with anything remotely resembling decent workable rules, characters who are true to the underlying theme of the game, then there is a failure in the system. And with any failure in the system, you have put a burden on the ST and the player to work past it that doesn't exist for other players and the ST. So, you have a new player unsure of their RPing ability (which was excellent) who felt very overshadowed by a character with whom they should have BARELY overlapped. But could I say, "I know you can both sneak around really well and that he can KILL with deft skill, but here is what you can do that he cannot <insert descriptions of rules for effective measurable social actions>" NOPE Not as rules. I could say, "I know you both can sneak around really well and he can KILL with deft skill, but when you want to do something just tell me and I'll have you roll some dice then pull the result out of my ass." To a degree, ST have to do the second all the time, but usually not in the fundamentals.

                                        As for the "Filter of Awesome" idea... When, say, a character who is attacking someone with a sword or their fists has a lot of dice and rolls well, do you actually compute damage, soak, mark off health levels, or just say, "Wow! That was a lot of successes to hit. He's dead." ? If not, is it fair to ask those characters to play by rules different than the other characters in the game?

                                        And vice verse, Do you kill PCs outright when the bad guy rolls a ton of dice, had big dice pools and gets a lot of successes? Or tell the PCs, "The bad guy has mad socials and rolled really really well, so just do whatever he tells you."?

                                        To me it looks like your "Filter of Awesome" is you making up for crap rules. To me, it also looks like I would NEVER play a fighter character in your games, because, you, as a ST, have privileged kick-ass roleplaying to the point that social and mental characters will take over so long as the player and you both agree that what their social/mental character does is Awesome, lost of dice are rolled and lots of successes come up. The fighter-type has to follow RAW. The others follow your whim.

                                        We all need the rules as a baseline from which to work. We don't have rules for one area that handle it as well as another. That's either a damn shame.

                                        WHICH brings me back to the OP's question again. My answer (still provisional, but better more definite) is "YES". Scion is about monster-killing. That's what the design works best for.
