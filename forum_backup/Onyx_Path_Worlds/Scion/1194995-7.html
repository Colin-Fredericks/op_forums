        I'm back from a back-to-back reading of Origin and Hero, and my dominant feeling is spurned lover's disappointment and frustration: I kept getting way excited about all those excellent character-building ideas about Pantheons, Mantles, different brands of Scions and legendary creatures, Callings, Paths, Purviews, marvels, and incredibly frustrated about how the system goes out of its way to impose strict power-level and niche-preserving limits that make the game all but unplayable for my (high-powered and broad-ranging) playstyle tastes. The utter inability to buy more than an handful Boons in the very best of cases (and hence build up decent use of all but the narrowest set of Purviews, given the extreme scarcity of Legend juice to use Marvel rules) no matter the character-creation tweaks and even an exceedingly long time playing the same character (one might play a scion the time it would take to build a mage or beast to Archmage or Incarnate levels, and fail to make a noticeable bump in their abilities ), and the strict dependency on cumbersome physical tools and props to have an array of supernatural powers exceeding a single field, no matter contrary character concepts. Moreover, almost all the combat-worthy and really impressive supernatural power applications are gated behind expenditure of a resource that is very scarce and quite difficult to refuel (you can only perform sacrifice w/o making it a session-defining event once in a great while, and there obvious limits to how many Fatebindings you may accumulate w/o making it ridiculous and unmanageable), much worse than it would typically take a CotD supernatural to refill their power pool stat.

        I came to this game expecting I would be able to play a divinely empowered if still somewhat inexperienced high-tier comic-book or anime superhero, say Avatar Korra at the beginning of her series, then I realized I would not even be able to play the equivalent of an experienced bender, wizard, or mid-tier superhero, and the game in practice is going to keep the play nailed to the level of street-level action-adventure with a few supernatural tricks once in a great while or low-level Vancian magic-user, and no change being possible beyond the release of a book that for what I know has not yet reached first draft status. In the meanwhile, you can play a Daredevil, Punisher, or James Bond clone that can perhaps throw a lightning bolt or shapechange once every two or three game sessions or so. Not really my notion of playing the second coming of Hercules or Sun Wukong, even at the beginning of their heroic journeys. If one looks to the situation hard enough, it seems one would do much a better job of using rules for say CotD Mage or Beast, or superhumans in Mutants and Masterminds, reskinned with Scion flavor and setting, than using this "look, don't touch" system to play these supposedly awesome divine champions a properly developed supernatural can run circles around and wipe floors with.

        At the very, very least, I'd deem the game only playable with an extensive set of hacks, such as a) making the players able to buy up more Boons (and Knacks) with experience in each of their Purviews and Callings/Supenatural Paths, say up to 1-2 Boons per Purview and 2-3 per Calling, even if they only can activate a limited amount per scene b) providing a less cumbersome way for scions to refuel Legend, such as performance of dramatically-appropriate heroic deeds, or alternatively switch all Boons that aren't more powerful than fireball-throwing or changing shape from spending Legend to imbuing it c) explictly allowing use of 'nominal' Relics that cannot be effectively lost or stolen to channel Purviews for those characters whose concepts are not supposed to be reliant on physical props and tools d) make all starting characters begin at Legend 4.

        Or alternatively, I suppose I'd just drop Origin and Hero into a distant corner of my HD and library, and pretend hard the game doesn't exist until Demigod and God get written and released, in the hopeful expectation the system limits enforced by the game get less hardcore hostile to my playstyle tastes. And hopefully by Demigod or God at last I get to play my Avatar Korra clone with extensive mastery and easy use of say a half-dozen Purviews (or generalist jack of all trades expertise in a dozen ones) and zero dependency on tools and props without having to rewrite half the system. But at this point I'm much more dubious than when I pledged to the KS. Again, I feel weird that the setting and the flavor portion of the game provide all these incredible ideas that I can't really use in play except once in a blue moon, and then I realize I can make a much more properly impressive version of these supposedly awesome divine heroes with reskinned CotD supernatural or superhero RPG rules.

            What a long winded way of saying that not every game meets everyone's tastes, and that this one doesn't meet yours. Origin and Hero were never intended to be "high-powered and broad-ranging", nor should they be. This is the beginning of the journey, not the middle, or the end. Exalted (Solars) TC: Aberrant (When it comes out), or perhaps even Scion: Demigod (Once again, when it comes out) are more likely to be to your tastes, but please don't act like Origin and Hero don't set the stage for your basic starting level Heroic Myths, because they do, and they do it well. Jason, Paris, Orion, Cassandra, Theseus, Cadmus, Achilles, Odysseus, Orpheus, Hector, Ajax, Perseus, even Heracles once you get a few more points of legend under your belt, are all possible with Scion: Hero, and that's just a fraction of the heroes, and only the Theoi.

            Points of Legend aren't often spent except for Big Effects, and Miracles, and getting Legend back isn't that hard since gaining, or compelling, a fate binding are things that refresh it, and you can make a minor sacrifice to get a point of Legend back once per session with simply a neat description of what you did. Most boons simply require you to imbue it into the effect, and you can retrieve it when ever you wish by simply deactivating what ever it was that you were doing.

            Boons are also only needed when you want to be able to always* do something, and not have it be an every once in a while kind of thing for your character.

            *For varying degrees of always, some boons still require legend to be spent.

            You seem to want to play in a super heroes game, which is fine, but Scion is not a super hero game. Legendary Items, Followers, Guides, Creatures, and their relationship with you character speak to the Mythic origins of the Scions, and having a lightning bolt that allows you to control the weather and fly is perfectly in line with the type of game that Scion is going for.

            I'm sorry that this game isn't for you, but no game is for everyone.

                                                            I have some of the same issues, and said months ago that I was worried about too much emphasis being on mortals and low-level stuff in a game that is supposed to be about playing much, much larger-than-life characters.
