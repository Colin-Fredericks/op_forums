    EPISODE TWO: AN EYE FOR BEAUTY (Continued)

    Act Three: You Always Hurt The One You Love
    Holly quickly organizes a sympathy visit to Laine and Derek after school. The first thing they find is that something ripped out Derek’s eyes. Laine merely suffered an emotional breakdown. Both teens say they don’t remember what happened. Holly can tell that in Derek’s case, this is traumatic amnesia. Laine, however, simply doesn’t want to talk. After a conference featuring wild speculation about what happened, the Scions first assure Laine that they will believe whatever she says, then use Overt Order to start her talking.

    Laine says she was smooching with Derek, hoping that that night he’d go all the way, when suddenly Derek screamed in terror. Two snakes then reached past Laine’s head and bit out Derek’s eyes. Laine screamed and reeled back. She saw a horrible monster in the room, humanoid but with scaly skin and snakes for hair, before she fainted.

    This narrows the field of speculation somewhat but opens other possibilities. For their next step, the gang decides to break into Laine’s house. Gene opens the locked door with disconcerting ease. He also notices that he is not the only person to pick the lock recently. Jaeda and Gus keep watch outside while the others look for clues. All they find is that the living room where Derek and Laine snogged has a large wall mirror.

    (Outside, meanwhile, Gus mentions his “performance issues” to Jaeda. She recognizes the effects of a Health Purview curse to which she, fortunately, knows the counter-curse. She performs it and, um, helps assure Gus that his problems are over.)

    Back at the hospital, Jack uses Know Tapu and confirms what the Scions already suspected: Laine is a supernatural entity. Questions about possible weirdness in her family lead nowhere, so Holly pulls out the Mirror of True Seeing and invokes the power of Mystery. Her questions to the Infinite reveal that Laine has been transformed into titanspawn; specifically, a lesser version of one of the legendary Gorgons. She doesn’t know this has happened, though — or that she took Derek’s eyes. The reason for her sudden transformation into a monster is “time.” The Scions are still left with unanswered questions, though (in part because some questions were unanswerable as phrased).

    Holly sets out to learn more about Gorgons. She goes to the school librarian, Mrs. Weihs, and asks to see “the secret library.” Mrs. Weihs raises her eyebrow and says there isn’t one, but Holly can browse the clipping files in the back room for information you won’t find on the internet.

    The back room holds the magazine collection and banker’s boxes stuffed with manila envelopes full of photocopies and clippings on diverse subjects, as a research resource. Much of the envelopes are about diseases, foreign countries, and the other sorts of things that students write reports about, but Mrs. Weihs has some odd stuff too. In an envelope labeled “Mythology/Archeology,” Holly finds a copy of an article from the Journal of the Warburg and Courtald Institutes (1923) with a picture of a Gorgon feeding a woman blood from its arm, Dracula-fashion.

    Holly finally breaks down and asks her Guide for details. The mysterious voice in the Mirror of True Seeing explains that as the Greek myths describe, a curse from the Gods turned the three Gorgon sisters into monsters (titanspawn, in fact) and Perseus slew one of them. After that, the two remaining Gorgons kept a low profile. They remained enemies of the Gods, though, and learned to transform mortals into lesser versions of themselves using their blood. The mortal becomes incredibly beautiful for one month before turning into a hideous monster. The only way the new “medusa” can regain her beauty is to eat the eyes of an attractive mortal. That only works for a month or two, though, before the medusa must eat another set of eyes. The eyes of a Scion grant a longer period of beauty. From then on, however, the medusa can revert to her monster form at will.

    The only cure is speculative: If the transformation to monster is not complete, a second dose of gorgon blood (either from the true gorgons or the heart’s blood of another medusa of higher Legend) might return the victim to humanity. Laine has eaten a mortal’s eyes, but she didn’t kill Derek and she acted in ignorance of her condition. Therefore, it is possible that she has not completed her transformation into a monster and can still be saved. No one has ever tested this cure, though. The only cases where medusas tried to keep from eye-eating ended within a year as the medusa either killed herself or broke down, ate someone’s eyes, and became irredeemably evil.

    The gang asks Laine some more questions and connects the dots. A month ago, Laine had plastic surgery as her Sweet Sixteen birthday present — just a nose job and a little liposuction to pull out remnant baby fat. She thought she just got a lot more out of it than she expected. Her surgeon? Doctor Madelyn Sosa, of Vancouver, B.C. Everyone groans at the transparent pseudonym.

    Act Four: Radical Therapies
    The Scions decide to try saving Laine by forcing Dr. Sosa either to give up her supply of gorgon blood or by killing her and feeding her heart’s blood to Laine. The latter course means that Laine has to come along. While they plan the expedition, Holly and Gene break the news to Laine that she’s a monster and the one responsible for maiming Derek. Laine finds this believable only because of Gene’s Knack for making people believe any true statement he makes. Laine thinks that drinking the heart’s blood of a medusa sounds utterly disgusting, but she really, really doesn’t want to be an eye-eating monster.

    Since Marshall proved he could be useful in the boulder demolition, the gang brings him into the plan. Marshall loves the idea of an expedition to kill a monster, since he missed out on the lindwurm. There’s some concern about bringing his M-16 across the border, but Gene assures the others that this truly is not a problem.

    The Scions come up with some flim-flam about an educational field trip to the preparations for the Winter Olympics, then set out in Holly’s and Jaeda’s cars. Holly passes the border, no problem. Jaeda’s beat-up car (and Jaeda herself) look suspicious to a border guard, though, and the drugs-and-explosives dog barks “J’Accuse!” at the glove compartment. (More likely for past drugs than for explosives, to be sure.) The border guard opens the trunk and sees Marshall’s small arsenal. Gene immediately spins a line of bullshit about Marshall being a biathlon competitor, and you wouldn’t believe how cheap the AOC is being with the less famous sports, making people drive themselves to the games, now the ice skaters and ski jumpers get flown in first class and they can carry drugs by the pound and nobody says boo… The guard is too dazed to realize that you don’t use machine guns for biathlon, and he waves them through.

    In Vancouver, the gang easily finds Dr. Sosa’s house and breaks in from the rear, making sure that nobody sees them or their vehicles. It’s a nice house — and creepy as hell, because even though Dr. Sosa has supposedly lived here for years, there is no imprint of her personality. The interior is as impersonally pretty as a picture in Architectural Digest. The Scions hide and wait for Dr. Sosa to come home. Jack, however, decides to pose as a burglar for Dr. Sosa to discover: The gang is pretty sure she’s a murderous medusa, but Jack wants to test her by her reaction to him (it’d be bad to make a mistake and kill some innocent plastic surgeon).

    Doctor Sosa, a very attractive middle-aged woman, finally comes home. She has a bag of groceries in her hand. Jack, pillowcase of silverware in hand, tells her not to freak out, he doesn’t want trouble. Sosa screams and drops her bag of groceries. As the Scions wonder if they’ve made a mistake, she transforms into a scaled, clawed, snake-haired, hideous medusa and darts at Jack! Sosa hits him with the full force of her monstrosity, and Jack is paralyzed with terror. Before the others can act, she has grabbed Jack and holds her razor-sharp claws over his eyes. She snarls, “I can smell you, Scions. Come out and surrender, or I take his eyes!”

    Fortunately, the medusa doesn’t know where everybody is. What’s more, Gus can throw off the paralyzing terror through sheer force of egotism, Marshall is in another room using a mirror to aim his M-16 at her, Gene averts his eyes, and Jaeda is hiding behind the couch and behind Dr. Sosa’s back. They manage to distract Dr. Sosa enough that she no longer has Jack’s eyes covered. Gene lassoes Jack and pulls him to safety, while Gus grabs Dr. Sosa. This is possible only because Holly curses Sosa with the Evil Eye (“You should not cross a witch of the frozen wastes of the North!”) This reduces Sosa’s otherwise insanely high Dodge DV. (Sosa has three dots of Epic Dexterity. Uh-oh, that means she has higher Legend too…)

    The gang pounds on Dr. Sosa a bit, to very little effect (she’s very tough) but nevertheless enough to make her ask for parlay. (Even more than Gus threatening to rip her limb from limb, she seems to be unnerved when Marshall emerges to stick the muzzle of his rifle in her mouth and ask if she can bounce bullets from the inside, full-auto.) The Scions demand her supply of gorgon blood. She temporizes, saying it’s back at her lab. Gene has a solution to this problem: He summons his great big raven Creature and gives it the instructions on how to retrieve the cursed blood.

    As the Scions debate options, they realize they can’t let Dr. Sosa go. She is clearly an experienced medusa. She has killed many times before. She will undoubtedly kill again — they have no legal way to stop her. Sosa tries using Overt Order to make Gus let her go, but she has no escape from the group of Scions around her. She also tries resuming human form in hopes the Scions can’t bring themselves to kill her. They can. Marshall makes good on his threat to shoot Sosa in the head, Gus starts ripping her apart, and Jaeda reaches into the wound in Sosa’s abdomen and cuts out her heart. They call Laine into the room and manage to squirt the blood from the still-beating heart down her throat, without Laine fainting or throwing up. Within minutes, Laine fades from movie starlet-gorgeous to merely a rather pretty girl.

    (“I’ll always be beautiful to Derek,” she rather unnervingly says. “Oh. Did I say that out loud?” Well, being a titanspawn — even if not completely, and only for a short time — is bound to have some psychological effects.)

    That leaves a medusa corpse in a room with blood everywhere and bullets in the walls. Gus calls his crocodile Creature and feeds it the body, and the Scions clean up whatever other evidence they can think of. Doctor Sosa’s house is still clearly the site of a messy homicide; they can only hope the Canadian cops can’t trace it back to them. The Scions slip out the way they came, get in their cars and head back to Avalon.

    Aftermath
    The adventure leaves Holly deeply bummed. They saved Laine from being a monster, but only by committing a brutal, cold-blooded murder. Gus tries consoling her with the thought that they are only at the beginning of their path to godhood. They’ve found themselves in a dark new world with nasty creatures forcing nasty choices on them. When Holly comes into her power, though, “the frozen wastes of the North will melt at your command, and you can make a better world.” Seems there’s more to Gus than his tough-guy persona lets on.

    The Scions also acquired a few ampoules of genuine gorgon blood. It remains to be seen what they do with it, if anything. But, they also have unanswered questions — such as, how many other people did Dr. Sosa turn into medusas? They also still don’t know who killed the girl at the cursed boulder, or why. They can only hope that inscrutable Fate will bring them answers in time.

                    EPISODE THREE: HOMECOMING OF THE GODS

                    Act One: Old Business, New Problems

                    \The Scions continue to settle into Avalon High and deal with the aftermath of the killing of Dr. Sosa.

                    GENE is with the school’s drama club. They debate what play to produce for Fall Quarter. This is the traditional season for “arty” plays, since new members are at their least skilled and nobody comes to see the plays anyway. (Only the Spring Quarter musical gets significant audiences.) One student, Josh, suggests this kinda weird old play he found at the bottom of a trunk of costumes. Delia Bright suddenly marches in, grabs the slim, leather-bound playbook from his hand, swats Josh on the head with it and says, “No!” Then, she marches out as abruptly as she came. Gene follows. He protests at Delia burning the play on the sidewalk, despite her insistence that she just saved Avalon High from a world of trouble. The playbook’s covers survive the fire, and Gene — true son of Coyote that he is — decides that he will find another copy of The King in Yellow.

                    JACK is pulled from his Introduction to Ceramics class (better known to students as “Easy A”) by a school clerk who apologizes for the error in not putting him in the class Jack really wanted. Especially when his father wrote that nice letter asking for the class as well.

                    The clerk leads Jack down a few halls and into another classroom, saying, “Here he is, Doctor. Once again, we’re sorry for the inconvenience.”

                    “And so should you be,” says the teacher. “Sit down…” (he consults a note) “Jack. Your last math teacher tells me you have a gift, if only you would apply yourself instead of… coasting. Well, in my class there shall be no… coasting. You will apply yourself. Or you will fail. Ignominiously.” Welcome to AP Math with the formidable (and formidably eccentric) Dr. Demerit, who does not seem impressed with Jack’s “Yes sir, you’re absolutely right sir, I’m looking forward to it sir” ass-kissing routine.

                    JAEDA overhears Bob telling a group of boys that Jaeda lured him to the party, came on to him and offered to trade sex for doing her homework — the complete opposite of what really happened. She calls him on it. Bob mutters that that’s his story and he’s sticking to it. (Likely to keep Alice from dumping him.) And anyway, Jaeda comes on to everyone male… Another boy tells Bob that trading schoolwork for sex was over-charging anyway, and Jaeda would probably settle for less. He pulls out a $20 and suggests that he and Jaeda do it after school. Whatever her self-esteem issues, Jaeda finds this sufficiently offensive that she punches the boy in the nose.

                    Vice-Principal Stone moves in before matters can escalate further, and sends Jaeda to the Principal’s office. Principal Flecker accepts Jaeda’s version of events, but feebly protests that fighting in school is bad. Then he sends her to the school counselor. Lotus Ho (she’s Chinese) tells Jaeda that while adolescence is a time to try out new identities and work out who you are, kids need to be careful not to make bad choices they can’t take back. She then asks Jaeda to think hard about who and what she wants to be when she grows up. Of course, Jaeda can’t say “Goddess,” so she says that she hadn’t given it much thought, and didn’t think she had many options beyond prostitution or porn. Ms. Ho firmly tells Jaeda that she has plenty of other choices if she’s willing to work for them, and notes that Jaeda seems to do well in Mr. Oldfield’s auto shop class. Ms. Ho also brushes aside Jaeda’s doubts about the feasibility of girl mechanics, pointing to Barack Obama and the Chinese-American former governor of Washington/current Commerce Secretary Gary Locke as proof that old barriers and social stereotypes don’t matter so much anymore.

                    The speech doesn’t entirely convince Jaeda. After school, though, she makes sure to intercept Bob in the school parking lot and give him a bloody nose too… and whammy him with Saltpeter in the Coffee. For a few days, Bob won’t be doing it with Alice or anyone else.

                    GUS feels pretty good about life and himself. How could he feel any other way, after enjoying brain-melting sex with the gorgeous Sheena Delisle? As they relax under a tastefully-draped sheet, Sheena says she’s entirely satisfied as well. She also thinks that becoming Homecoming King and Queen will be just the start to an epic year, and they’re gonna rule Avalon High. Sheena reports one worry, though: Her father wants to meet Gus. Not that he’s an ogre or anything, it’s just that she’s happy with a relationship based entirely on sex. She fears to complicate it with anything else, and parents paying attention counts as complication. What if they have to hang out, do things together, develop common interests, etc.? Gus isn’t worried, though. They clean up, get dressed and depart, Gus to football practice and Sheena to aerobics and other activities that Gus never sees.

                    HOLLY finds a new acolyte. She sees a boy alone in the chemistry lab, mixing up chemicals. She finds that this is Maynard Salkind, junior pyrotechnician, making his own fireworks. (The chemistry lab has some chemicals he can’t obtain on his own, for coloring the fireworks.) He’s thinking of setting some off as an unsanctioned part of Homecoming celebrations. Holly thinks this is a fine idea.

                    Holly has also decided to make Derek and Laine the Homecoming King and Queen. She lobbies relentlessly throughout the student body, presenting the two as the candidates for everyone who isn’t in a “cool” clique. Later, she joins Laine in visiting Derek in the hospital. They wait their turn while another student, Alex, tells Derek about the latest swim team activities and his own weekend visit to Ocean Shores to swim in the Pacific. Then Alex realizes that Derek likely will not be on the swim team anymore and, awkwardly, makes things worse by saying how much everyone misses Derek and hopes he can join them on a team outing to Ocean Shores sometime soon, even if he can’t… um. Once Alex leaves, Laine and Holly make their visit. Derek says the doctors say he can become an outpatient by the weekend. Holly suggests that he and Laine attend the Homecoming Dance that Friday. Derek’s not enthusiastic.

                    After the visit, Laine remembers that she asked her parents why they chose Dr. Sosa as her plastic surgeon. Turns out, her mother heard Sheena Delisle’s mother say that Sheena had gone to Dr. Sosa for a few tweaks, as part of Sheena’s ambition to become a supermodel. Mrs. Farber took that as a recommendation, and Dr. Sosa’s prices turned out to be lower than the Farbers expected.

                    So, Sheena Delisle was one of Dr. Sosa’s patients.

                    And over the summer, she went from “beautiful enough to appear in local TV commercials” to “beautiful enough to turn heads in Hollywood.”

                    Oh, crap.

                        EPISODE THREE: HOMECOMING OF THE GODS (Continued)

                        Interlude: I’m Sure He’ll Thank Us, Really…

                        Gus told Jack and Gene about Sheena’s trepidation about Gus meeting her father. When Holly passes on that Sheena was Dr. Sosa’s client, the gang decides they need to learn more about Sheena’s family. Did they know what Dr. Sosa was? Is Sheena trying to protect Gus?

                        The two decide to handle the meeting for Gus. Jack easily collects a stray hair of Gus’ for Gene to use as a focus for Stolen Face — and off they go to see George Delisle at his job (VP for Marketing at a large shipping/import company in Seattle). They easily talk their way in, since Mr. Delisle wants to meet his daughter’s new boyfriend.

                        They make a good impression. Mr. Delisle already knows about Gus as a local football star. They easily steer the conversation toward Sheena and her visit to Dr. Sosa. Mr. Delisle explains that Sheena wants a modeling career like her mother once had, but going beyond small-time local gigs. Dr. Sosa did a few tweaks to bring out her full beauty. It cost a bit, but nothing’s too good for his princess. Sheena already appeared in a few local commercials, and Mr. Delisle thinks she could hit the big time.

                        Mr. Delisle also suggests that “Gus” might like to appear in a few local commercials too, or try for a summer internship. “Gus” says he’s interested, and Mr. Delisle offers to send him brochures. Gene agrees and gives Gus’ home address.

                        Jack and Gene are also somewhat unnerved, because Mr. Delisle is played by Ray Wise, with slick, diabolical cheer on full display. Delisle’s explanation of his job in Marketing — “I make people want things” — comes out sounding just a bit sinister. The two Scionsleave Mr. Delisle’s office filled with wild surmise.

                        Back at Avalon, Gene and Jack tell Gus about the favor they’ve done him. Gus doesn’t see it that way. Whatever their motives, they stole his identity, and that is about the rudest challenge you can make to a Pesedjet. Jack gets off with a frown and a warning never to be party to such a thing again. To Gene, Gus snarls, “As for you… Run!” and hits him with Game Face. Gene runs. As Gus tells Jack, this is him being forgiving and merciful. If Gene weren’t more or less a friend, Gus would have fed him to his crocodile.

                        Gus is also not exactly thrilled to find Holly campaigning against him and Sheena for Homecoming King and Queen — and with Gene’s help. Holly soon lets Gus know, however, that he has much bigger problems than a trifling bit of high school celebrity.

                        Act Two: Love Kills

                        That still leaves the issue of whether Sheena is a medusa, and what connection (if any) she has to the murder at the Evil Boulder. Holly decides to make use of the smart kids she’s cultivated as Followers. One of them, Bobby Kilmer (no connection to Bob the Lying Horndog) is the star of the school’s computer club and robotics club. He says, sure, he can get Dr. Sosa’s medical records along with the rest of the file from the Vancouver, BC police investigation. He just hacks into the FBI computer system and uses it as a false front for his request to the Vancouver PD. Sure enough, next day Bobby hands Holly a file of the investigation. Sheena was Dr. Sosa’s patient exactly one month before Crystal, the girl from Etna High, was reported missing. Meanwhile, Holly talks to other Fashionistas and extracts accounts of Sheena’s activities over the summer. The day before Crystal disappeared, Sheena phoned various friends and abruptly broke off planned events. No one saw her for two days.

                        From there, the Scions easily reconstruct a possible sequence of events: Sheena gets her “operation” and achieves her current level of hotness. A month later, she turns into a monster. Somehow, she knew what to do to restore her beauty. She ambushed Crystal (likely a victim of opportunity), paralyzed her with fear, conked her on the head to knock her out, and dragged her to a secluded spot — the patch of brush around the Evil Boulder. There, she ate Crystal’s eyes, killed her and buried the body. The killing activated the Evil Boulder to generate lindwurms, which skeletonized the corpse.

                        But they aren’t sure. Maybe Sheena’s parents are monsters, or cultists, or something, and she’s a victim of a more-than-usually-dysfunctional family. Maybe Mr. or the former Mrs. Delisle did the murder, or told Sheena to do it at the Evil Boulder.

                        As the Scions ponder ways to fill in the blanks, and what to do about Sheena, Holly decides she should warn the other Scions. Marshall says he’s in for any more medusa-killing. Barbara thanks Holly for the heads-up and promises to help with any cover-up. Holly doesn’t tell Leo, who continues to avoid other Scions. Delia is outraged by the suggestion that a fellow Fashionista could be a murderous titanspawn. When Holly points out that medusas prefer the eyes of beautiful Scions, Delia icily says that she and Sheena are Fashionistas, and they stick together against the plain and the poor. No matter what. She further accuses Holly of trying to pull a jealous, evil trick — nothing less than trying to get Sheena killed to thin the competition for Homecoming Queen. Disgusted, Holly decides that Delia is beyond help and beneath contempt.

                        Shortly thereafter, Holly gets a phone call from Laine. Laine says she just received a call from Sheena, saying, “I know what you are and what you did to Derek. If you want to keep the police from knowing you maimed Derek, and thinking you killed the girl found in the woods, meet me at Holly’s house at thus-and-such a time.” And Laine found Crystal’s ID card in her bedroom.

                        At first the Scions think that Delia must have told Sheena, and this is Sheena’s response. The timing seems awfully tight, though. It’s more likely that this is Sheena’s own plan, that just happens to come at the same time as the Scions’ activities. But, it answers some questions and gives the Scions a chance to confront Sheena in circumstances of their choosing.

                        Gus decides that if his girlfriend is to disappear, he needs an unbreakable alibi, and the only way to do this is for him to genuinely not be there at the confrontation. The Scions decide that he will protect Laine. They will drive around Avalon, be seen by people who know them both and by surveillance cameras, and (as the crowning touch) get a traffic ticket.

                        Gene, on the other hand, will use Stolen Face to pose as Laine. Holly makes herself visible in an upstairs window at her rented house. Jaeda, Jack and Marshall take up hiding places near her, to ambush Sheena just as they did Dr. Sosa. Everyone carries hand mirrors.

                        As “Laine” approaches Holly’s house, Sheena slips out from some bushes and points a gun at her. She tells “Laine” to go into Holly’s house. Holly answers “Laine’s” hail by calling her to come upstairs. “Laine” and Sheena do so.

                        Sheena says she knows there are Scions at Avalon High, and it wasn’t hard to guess that Holly is one of them. Her eyes will keep Sheena beautiful for most of the school year. Laine will take the blame for it all, once she’s dead. From hiding, Jack confirms (using Sense Tapu) that Sheena is in some way supernatural; the rest see it themselves as Sheena assumes monstrous medusa form. Battle is joined!

                        Jack starts by throwing a kitchen knife to knock the gun from Sheena’s hand. Sheena attacks Holly with her venomous medusa claws, but Holly dodges. Marshall shoots; Jaeda stabs. Their attacks are hindered by looking at Sheena only through mirrors. Sheena shows that she doesn’t know the Scions undid the curse on Laine when she screams for Laine to help her, they can work together as fellow titanspawn! Gene does not disabuse her, which is probably why Sheena didn’t run when she realized she was overmatched. Once Holly whammies Sheena with the Evil Eye, the Scions’ victory becomes a foregone conclusion. Sheena can’t touch them, but even without looking at her directly the Scions can still attack her. Holly coordinates the others so they can all attack Sheena at once: Jack throwing from the other room, Marshall shooting, Gene (still looking like Laine!) hooking his lasso to the ceiling fan to whip around and kick Sheena, and Jaeda doing the Limbo to avoid an attack from Sheena while stabbing up into her chest. Sheena dies in that second, with a bullet in her brain and her heart in Jaeda’s hand. (Erm. Jaeda’s getting really good at that… Must be an Atzlánti thing.)

                        After that, it’s all over but cleaning up the mess. Bloody carpets are collected and burned. Marshall picks up his shell casings. They find that Sheen carried Crystal’s wallet, watch and jewelry, evidently ready to plant on Laine. Later, Gus’ crocodile disposes of the body. As far as the world shall know, Sheena Delisle simply disappears.

                            EPISODE THREE: HOMECOMING OF THE GODS (Continued)

                            Act Three: Dansus Interruptus

                            After the trauma of killing two medusas, the Scions are all ready for some fun. They have high hopes for the Homecoming Dance taking place that Friday.

                            Several girls strongly hint to Jack that they’d like him to invite them to the dance. Some angle for Gus, too. They don’t seem to care what happened to Sheena, only that Gus seems to be available again. A few dozen boys ask Jaeda, but she decides this time, she’ll give Marshall a chance: He’s shown he’s not a complete asshole.

                            Holly tries to draw out Leo by asking him to the dance. She finds him in the gym after school, practicing with his mace. Leo’s a lot more buff than Holly expected, and handles the mace well. He also seems to be instructed/hectored by his lion-demon tattoo, and not to be too happy with it. Leo turns Holly down. It’s all lust and sin, and he’s having none of it. Bobby Kilmer and Maynard Salkind ask Holly to the dance, but by then she can honestly say that some other boy (of higher status, and so better worth cultivating) asked her already.

                            Gene already promised to be Reese Vetterson’s date for the Etna High dance. He shows up with tuxedo, corsage, the whole hokey nine yards. Reese looks very nice, too. Once inside, of course, they run into Gary and the Sarsen twins, none of whom are pleased to see Reese on the arm of a Scion. (Yeah, Gary figured it out. Gene was with other Scions, and no mere mortal could have put on the oratorical display he did.) Reese, on the other hand, is none too pleased to have her big brother telling her who she can or cannot date.

                            Gene decides the best way to avoid having three giants pound him to hamburger is to attract sufficient attention that the giants balk at making a scene. Anything worth doing is worth overdoing, so Gene asks Reese if she’d like to be Homecoming Queen. The vote is about to take place: He and Reese jump up on stage and Gene presents them as last-minute candidates. All very irregular of course, but… Scion of Coyote. Not big on picky little rules. Like, Gene not being a student at Etna High. Nevertheless, he and Reese both turn on Center of Attention, and Gene hits the Etna High student body with the full force of his oratory (thank you, four dots in Expression!) In a few minutes, he has the crowd roaring its acclaim for the two as this year’s Homecoming King and Queen.

                            Gene also knows that minds will clear before long and people will realize that no one has actually seen him at Etna High. Gary and the Sarsens also still look mad enough to spit fire. After a few dances, therefore, he suggests to Reese that they depart to somewhere less fraught. Like, maybe, Avalon High. Maybe they can be Homecoming King and Queen at both schools! Reese thinks this sounds fun. Neither of them has a car, but look — there’s a whole parking lot full of them! Even the Scion of Coyote balks at stealing Gary’s beloved convertible (some things are truly unforgivable). Instead, they steal the Sarsen twins’ pickup.

                            They arrive too late for the election. Despite Holly’s politicking for Laine and Derek, Delia and Warren win handily as the Homecoming royalty. Holly gets some satisfaction when Maynard’s smuggled, thoroughly unsanctioned fireworks upstage the Scion of Apollo. Gene and Reese arrive to find the dance in full swing. Vice-Principal Stone stalks around as arch-chaperone, breaking up lascivious couples who try to go beyond what can be shown on network TV.

                            Reese and Gene manage a few more dances before Gary and the Sarsens stalk into the Avalon High gym. Jaeda immediately tries intercepting Gary in hopes of preventing a fight (and seeing more of The Car, barely visible through the open gym door…) Gus is ready to throw down and blow off some of his frustration at the week’s events. The other Scions tense, expecting trouble.

                            And then the lights go out.

                            The large double-doors out of the gym crash down, squashing a student underneath.

                            Dark, ominous music blasts out to replace the now-silenced DJ. (The main title music to Omen III: The Final Conflict, if you’re interested.)

                            And a huge, dark shape, four-rooted and furry, with eyes burning eerily in the faint light from outside the door, stalks in.

                            Hell of an entrance. If a touch… theatrical.

                            “SCIONS!” the creature growls. “Learn the name of Bolvarg, heir of Fenris, for I am your doom! Not tonight do I seek your deaths, but other lives I hold in thrall. I know where you live, and whom you hold dear. If you would ransom them, give to me the growler’s treasure, liquid purse-fire dropped from heaven! Come to me at midnight tomorrow, in the great hall of many merchants. In the steel steeds’ pasture, give me what I seek. If you do not — THERE WILL BE BLOOD!” And the creature backs out the gymnasium doors. The music ends abruptly with a click. Those near the fallen doors see a wolf the size of a Clydesdale pick up a CD player in its mouth and lope off… No, they don’t. That would be insane. Later, the mortals say there was a man making incoherent threats (something about science?), accompanied by a big dog.

                            First things first: The Scions manage to get enough people’s heads together enough to call an ambulance for the student crushed under the doors. The Sarsens are still willing to fight, but Gary is sufficiently distracted by getting Reese home (this time, she does not protest) that he’s willing to let pulping Gene slide for now. The dance breaks up. Delia is furious that her big night was spoiled.

                            Some of the Scions are ready to chase Bolvarg right away (well, Marshall wants to get his guns first). Others hold off, thinking they should first figure out what the heck is going on. The resident Know-It-Alls (i.e., Holly and Jack) put their heads together to try deciphering Bolvarg’s melodramatically cryptic speech.

                            First, they recognize that Bolvarg used kennings: multi-layered metaphors common to Norse sagas. “The growler” is the bear. “Purse-fire” is gold. Liquid gold is… um… honey? And honey could be a treasure for a bear. “Honey dropped from heaven” actually rings a bell with Holly: In the Kalevala, Lemminkainen’s mother raises him from the dead by anointing him with honey obtained from God in Heaven. So, a big talking wolf threatens to kill the Scions’ loved ones if they don’t give it honey obtained from the Gods. Which they don’t have.

                            Holly decides she’d better consult her Guide. The mysterious figure who speaks in her mirror says that Bolvarg is, as he said, a fenrir — the descendants of the Fenris Wolf from Norse mythology. Bolvarg has a record. Though he’s known as a drama queen, he is also quite dangerous. Lately, he ravaged through the supernatural creatures of Finland, seeking something. Now, they know what: It’s been rumored for a long time that the divine honey used to resurrect Lemminkainen wasn’t used up, and some was left on Earth. The divine honey is also known as Amrit (the divine elixir of Hindu mythology) or Ambrosia (Greek). It is very potent. It must not fall into titanspawn hands, er, paws. Clearly, Bolvarg thinks someone is bringing it to the Scions — Holly, most likely.

                            The Scions head home to sleep and plan their next move. Holly arrives home to find three small, humanoid creatures emerge from hiding under her porch. Two of them are about three feet high (and one carries a large handbag); the third is just two feet high. They are rotund, have large, round snouts, round eyes and short tails like those of a cow. “Thank the Gods we found you,” the largest of them says. “You were the only person we could think of, and reach. Please — we need your help!” Once inside, the creatures introduce themselves as Moomins. From her handbag, the other large Moomin pulls one of those bear-shaped plastic bottles of honey.

                            “The honey from heaven,” she says. “Daughter of Louhi — we give it into your care.”

                            TO BE CONTINUED.

                                                    EPISODE FOUR: I’LL HUFF AND I’LL PUFF…

                                                    Act One: Now What?

                                                    This episode begins immediately after the end of “Homecoming of the Gods.” To recap: The homecoming dance breaks up in confusion after the fenrir Bolvarg’s melodramatic entrance. EMTs come for the student that Bolvarg crushed when he broke down the gym door. Delia is furious and mortified at how events have ruined her big night. Holly is first to leave, intending to research more about the divine honey that seems to be the fenrir’s goal. She finds a small family of moomins — Finnish house-trolls — waiting on (or rather, under) her doorstep. They give her the very item that Bolvarg seeks.

                                                    As Episode Four opens, Holly thanks the moomins for bringing her the divine honey while wondering what she’s supposed to do with it… and with a family of moomins that look at her expectantly with trusting eyes. She soon finds the moomins have no place to go: Bolvarg destroyed their home back in Finland, they’ve been on the run ever since, and they don’t know anyone in America. Sighing, she invites them to stay for a while until they can make other arrangements and gives them a spare bedroom.

                                                    Back at Avalon High, Marshall is excited to see there are giants in the area. Jaeda is casual about it: She and the others knew about the Sarsens since the football game against the Etna Titans. (Marshall missed it. He was out looking for lindwurms.) The Vettersons, she says, are giants, too. Marshall wants to get his guns and go kill giants, but Jaeda points out that Bolvarg presents the more immediate problem. (Plus, the Vettersons and Sarsens don’t seem that bad, as titanspawn go.) Gus, Jack and the other Scions agree. They also decide the problem can wait until morning. Chasing after a giant wolf at night, when only a few of them can see in the dark, strikes everyone as suicidally stupid. Instead, they agree to meet at Holly’s for a council of war.

                                                    Everyone comes to Holly’s house bright and early. Even Leo answers the call, a bit to the surprise of the other Scions. The kids find a hearty breakfast waiting for them, courtesy of Moominmamma. (Holly gives her cook the day off.) Holly shows the rest the divine honey and fills them in about what she learned from her Guide.

                                                    More than a thousand years ago, the mother of the demigod Lemminkainen used divine honey to resurrect her murdered son. Some of the honey was left over. It’s kicked around the Finnish mythic world ever since. No one ever used its miraculous powers. Either they felt they didn’t have the right to use something that belonged to the Gods, or they always thought something more important might come up later. After all, the divine honey can raise the dead and work many other miracles besides. The divine honey is also the Ambrosia of Greek mythology, the Amrit of Hindu myth, the Food of Life in Babylonian myth, and so on. It’s rare and precious even by the standards of Gods. Just eating it will give anyone less potent than a God a significant, if temporary, power boost.

                                                    The Scions then digress a bit, wondering why Bolvarg wants the honey, and what they should do with it. They suspect Bolvarg wants the power boost to obtain some other goal, but they decide that ultimately, Bolvarg’s goals don’t matter. After all, they absolutely will not give in to the fenrir’s threats. If necessary, one of them eats the honey and uses the power boost to beat on Bolvarg! Most of them go along with the suggestion that if they can defeat the fenrir without using the honey, they should try using it to do some good instead of holding onto it for a “just in case” that might never come. Most of the Scions think that restoring Derek Huckovich’s eyes would be a good use for the honey.

                                                    Delia and Barbara say they have minimal combat abilities, so they wouldn’t be much use tracking and fighting Bolvarg. (Barbara admits that her father Shango gave her a pretty spiffy relic axe, but she knows zippo about fighting.) They will instead handle the inevitable cover-up, making sure mortals stay ignorant of things they are better off not knowing. Marshall, on the other hand, is stoked to go wolf-hunting. Leo agrees to come along, pushed by the lion-demon his father Nergal foisted on him. Leo’s good with animals, sees in the dark, and has discovered supernatural prowess at tracking. Plus, he has a relic mace. The gang figures they have a pretty good chance of getting the drop on Bolvarg and fighting him on their terms.

                                                        EPISODE FOUR: I’LL HUFF AND I’LL PUFF… (Continued)

                                                        Act Two: Wolf Hunt, Snipe Hunt

                                                        Before the PCs set out (plus Marshall and Leo), they acquire some extra weaponry. Jack spent much of the previous night in the school’s shop, grinding lengths of rebar into javelins. They might not be very good javelins (Jack doesn’t have Craft: Metalwork, after all) but he figures that any lengths of pointy metal are better than nothing. The Scions also buy some cans of habanero pepper spray-gel: They figure it should work even better against Bolvarg’s sensitive nose than against humans. As a final preparation, Jaeda calls her father and tells him to get the hell out of town for the day, as far away as possible and staying among crowds. She’s the only Scion whose mortal parent knows about Gods, so she can warn him about supernatural threats in ways the others can’t. (Though Barbara says she thinks she can get her folks out of town for the day, too.)

                                                        Bolvarg’s trail from the school gym leads into the woods east of Avalon. Bolvarg tried to hide his trail by splashing through a stream, making a long jump across a ravine, etc., but with both Gus and Leo using Predatory Focus, the troll-wolf has no chance of losing them.

                                                        Still, the Scions stay on guard. As a result, when Bolvarg’s trail leads through a narrow, muddy, weedy ravine whose sides are thick with brush and briars, more than one Scion thinks, “Huh. Good place for a trap.” And indeed, both Gus and Leo spot the fine tripwire running through the weeds. Simultaneously, they grab Marshall’s arms and hold him back. A closer inspection leads them from the wire to the hand grenades hidden among the bushes.

                                                        Ulp. Bolvarg may be clever monster, but there’s no way he set a trap like that. The Scions realize they are up against more than one foe.

                                                        From a distance they hope is safe, Jack throws a rock to trigger the trap. (The Scions don’t want to leave something so lethal, when mortal kids or hikers could run into it.) Then they return to following Bolvarg’s trail… but more slowly, taking greater care.

                                                        About a mile further, the trail leads to a pass between two hills or ridges. There’s a gap about 50 yards wide between the hills, with steep slopes covered in brambles, weeds and small trees and shrubs. An old railroad bridge crosses the gap from hill to hill. (Actually, it looks like someone cut out a section of a long, winding hill decades ago, maybe for a road or railway line that was abandoned or never built, and then bridged the cut for a different rail line.) Dirt and rocks cover the floor of the gap — fresh dirt from the large cave dug into one hillside. Bolvarg’s tracks, and those of many people, are clearly visible in the dirt.

                                                        Out from the cave come six of the Scions’ fellow students from Avalon High. Jaeda recognizes Bob, her horndog semi-nemesis. Other Scions recognize students Adam, Clay, Davis, Elsie and Flicka. They’re some of the larger and healthier boys and girls at Avalon High but nobody special. Though, they all look somewhat larger than before, and a whole lot stronger. And meaner, though that might be because they all carry machetes. The Scions realize that Bolvarg must have turned their classmates into thralls! (Or he has a giant accomplice who did this: At the moment, they aren’t sure whether fenrir can create thralls.)

                                                        The six thralls announce that their master Bolvarg told them to kill anyone who came looking for him. Bob further taunts Jaeda, suggesting that maybe she should reconsider his proposition, now that he’s so strong. He’s such a buff stud he can do better than Alice, and if Jaeda’s real nice to him, maybe he’ll give her a second chance… Jaeda feels even more disgusted than the other Scions.

                                                        There’s not much point in trying to negotiate with thralls, so battle is joined. The Scions hold back somewhat: Sharp-eyed characters spot the camera phone wedged in a bush, watching the fight. Jack leads off by knocking down the camera with a thrown rock.

                                                        Jack then charges another convenient cobble-sized rock with Warrior’s Mana and chucks it at one of the thralls. For his stunt, he tries throwing it so it bounces off one kid’s head and smacks another, for Rockem Sockem Pinball Thralls. It works, injuring two of the thralls (though not much). Holly decides her best use in the fight is to activate Lightning Sprinter to zip forward and retrieve the Legend-charged rock so Jack can use it again.

                                                        The other Scions close for a thrall brawl. Leo smiles as the thralls charge him with their machetes, and says, “Go on, hurt me. It’s my practice for Hell.”

                                                        The thralls are strong, but not very skilled. They have very little chance to hit anyone. The thralls try to surround a Scion so one of them can attack from behind, but the Scions separate them and defeat them too quickly. Gus’ attempt to slam two thralls’ heads together doesn’t work very well, but it hardly matters. Jaeda starts by activating Lightning Sprinter, racing forward and kicking Horndog Bob in his previously broken nose. She follows this by kicking Bob in the nuts, which takes the fight out of Bob, and Bob out of the fight. Jaeda moves on to Davis. She lifts her shirt to flash the thrall. His Rapacity kicks in (on top of adolescent male hormones): As Davis grabs for her in the best anime-boy fashion, Jaeda puts him down with a single kick to the groin (very good damage roll; Davis might be singing soprano for life). Marshall has the presence of mind to use his rifle as a club, instead of shooting kids. For the first time, the other Scions see Leo in combat as he swings his mace at an already-wounded thrall and nearly pulps the unfortunate kid (Clay, IIRC). The other thralls are defeated with similar speed, and most of them are pushed into lethal damage.

                                                        And inside the cave? The Scions find it empty, except for another fine tripwire at a bend in the short tunnel. Jack triggers it with another thrown rock. Instead of an explosion, a sign pops out of the dirt, saying: BOOM. And below that, Scared Yet? One or two Scions aver that yes, they are — not from the physical threats they’ve faced so far, but from the way Bolvarg is toying with them.

                                                        More importantly, though, the student thrall that Leo struck is dying. The others with any medical skill (mostly Jaeda) help him as best he can in trying to stabilize the crushed kid. Leo expends Legend and channels his Piety for a legendary deed while praying for the student not to die on account of Bolvarg’s evil and Leo’s own sin of wrath, and just barely saves the kid’s life.

                                                        The Scions rouse one of the unconscious thralls to interrogate (Elsie, one of the less wounded). Gene pretends that this was all a test from Bolvarg, and they failed. Elsie blubbers with shame, but the Scions manage to extract the information that Bolvarg grabbed them with the help of two human accomplices, and fed them his blood to make them strong and tough. Bolvarg is wonderful, she’d do anything for Bolvarg and now she’s disappointed him, boo hoo…

                                                        Gus looks for Bolvarg’s trail departing the den. He doesn’t find it: The fenrir apparently has some supernatural means of making himself impossible to track. Gus does find some men’s tracks, though, leaving the site as well as arriving.

                                                        So, Bolvarg led them to the den as another trap. But why? The fenrir had to know that a group of six student thralls would be no match for a comparable number of Scions. Thinking further, they suspect Bolvarg is dicking with them on more than one level. First, he warned them that other people would pay a price for the Scions’ defiance. Second, he maneuvered them into inflicting grievous harm on a group of kids (even using bashing damage, the wound tracks circled ‘round on most of the kids), nearly killing some of them, and how do they explain this? And… while they hunted Bolvarg, the fenrir has been free to do Gods know what.

                                                        Leo is adamant that Clay and the other thralls absolutely must get to a hospital. The Scions call Delia and Barbara, who arrange for a medevac chopper to collect the wounded thralls. Leo stays at the den to protect the kids and ride with them back to the hospital. He is out of the hunt for Bolvarg, saying he’s done enough damage already. He gives the other Scions his relic mace, though, in case they need it (and so the cops can’t match it to wounds).

                                                        The Scions followed the tracks of Bolvarg’s henchmen to an old logging road, where they end at tire tracks: The two men and the fenrir got into a truck that drove away. The logging road leads to a rural road, which leads in time to a highway. Predatory Focus won’t trace a truck through that much traffic.

                                                        Along the way, the Scions call their parents and check online news feeds. Most of their mortal parents are okay — but Jack and Gene can’t raise their mothers. Instead, Gene gets a cop, who tells him his mother was kidnapped shortly before. She’s moderately well known, so people noticed when armed men bashed down the door of the Blackforest home and dragged her out. Jack’s mother, they soon find, was similarly kidnapped. The wolf den wasn’t just Bolvarg dicking with the Scions: It was a distraction.

                                                        [NOTE: In retrospect… Why didn’t the cops try calling Gene as soon as they learned about the abduction? I apologize for the hole in the plot, and the following necessary retcon: The Scions were in fact outside regular cell coverage. The camera phone in the bush, however, was a satellite phone. I would also accept that Holly has fully tricked-out satphone as well. Thus, the PCs could call out to Barbara, but the cops couldn’t reach Gene or Jack until the Band got closer to town.]

                                                            EPISODE FOUR: I’LL HUFF AND I’LL PUFF… (Continued)

                                                            Act Three: Yo Momma!

                                                            Holly decides she’s fed up with tracking Bolvarg on foot. It’s time to bring out divine magic and, by the way, modern transportation. Jaeda easily hitches a ride with a middle-aged man in an SUV, who’s a bit disappointed to find that the hot babe he picked up has a bunch of teenage friends who pile in after her. But, he’s bedazzled enough by Epic Socials to drive the Scions wherever they want to go.

                                                            Holly pulls out her Mirror of True Seeing and invokes Ariadne’s Thread. For the next half-hour, she guides the driver in following Bolvarg’s trail. When the trail splits, though, the Scions figure that Bolvarg is just tooling around, killing time and confusing his trail. (Bolvarg went down the same highway in opposite directions, creating an intersection in his trail.)

                                                            The Scions check the phone they took from the den and find that, as they expected, the fenrir left a number. Maybe Bolvarg’s henchmen keep a party line speed-dial; maybe the troll-wolf meant them to call. Either way, they phone Bolvarg.

                                                            The fenrir answers the phone. He confirms that he’s holding two of the Scions’ mortal mothers for ransom: Their mothers aren’t harmed yet… much… but if Bolvarg does not receive the honey and make a call to another of his thralls, the two women will die. The fenrir admits he took a chance. After all, maybe the Scions don’t care about mortal kin since they learned of their divine parentage. The Scions sneer at Bolvarg for setting up a gotcha-last plan in case of his death, as it suggests a lack of confidence in his prowess. The fenrir responds with insults of his own, and the Scions cut the call.

                                                            The Scions agree that tracking Bolvarg is pointless, and indeed unnecessary: His gotcha-last plan shows that he doesn’t have Jack and Gene’s mothers with him. Holly sighs, spends the Legend and invokes Ariadne’s Thread again, to lead them (and their increasingly alarmed driver) to Ms. Blackforest. The mystic trail leads promptly to one of the trailer parks in Hooverdale. In fact, it’s not far from where Jaeda lives. The Scions dismiss the relieved driver, and follow the trail to a specific house-trailer. Gene calls his raven, which spies through the windowsHoove. Sure enough, one room of the trailer holds the two women, unconscious, and a hard-bitten Nordic man in military gear. The thrall has a shotgun at the ready, a sword and various small arms.

                                                            The Scions guess that the thrall might have orders to shoot the two women at the first sign of an attack. They must plan their attack so he doesn’t have a chance. They also notice a lot of witnesses/injurable bystanders, such as children playing nearby. Jack fiddles with his smartphone a bit and finds an ice cream truck melody, hoping it will lure away at least some of the kids when the time comes. Mostly, though, the Scions decide to rely on swift and blinding violence.

                                                            Gene goes and hotwires a truck while the other Scions move into position. At the signal, Gus charges through the wall of the trailer (he rips the aluminum wall like it was paper), grabs the two women and dives through the opposite wall, to deposit them in the truck. As Nils the thrall turns to face Gus, Jack and Marshall charge in through the hole Gus made and engage the thrall. Jaeda soon follows, and Gus returns as quickly as he can.

                                                            The Scions then notice the bottles of gasoline scattered about the room, some of them knocked over by Gus’ passage, at least one poised over a little Sterno lamp. In seconds, the trailer is ablaze. In between shooting at the thrall, Marshall says, “Isn’t there a Klingon proverb about fools fighting in a burning house?” — but he won’t be the first out the door. (Gus and Jack file the datum that Marshall might be a Trekkie for future blackmail or embarrassment.) Jaeda resolves the issue by flipping in, clinching the thrall’s head with her legs, and flipping him out of the trailer. The thrall puts up a good fight (he’s tough, skilled and has a bulletproof vest) but has no real chance: The Scions defeat him in seconds. In the process, Jaeda kicks Nils in the nuts. The male Scions uneasily see a pattern developing. Nils defeated and unconscious, the Scions pile him and themselves into the truck and make their getaway.

                                                            Once again, the Scions call Bolvarg. They let him know his blackmail plan failed. What’s more, they’re through reacting to his cowardly ploys. They want to get this over with. They challenge the fenrir to meet them in battle in one hour and take the honey from heaven — because if he doesn’t, one hour and five minutes from now, one of them will scarf it down. After a moment’s thought, they decide the school athletics field makes a nice, private yet open venue for a battle, and they tell Bolvarg to be there or lose the divine honey forever. CLICK.
