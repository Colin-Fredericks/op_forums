        Originally posted by Sacerdos View Post
        Once again I'd reply that there is a great difference between creating a malignant or inaccurate depiction of Greek, Norse, or Christian figures in a society predominantly shaped by those cultures, and doing the same to the representatives of minority cultures.
        This is an issue close to me as I was suspended from school for a week because my Vampire books were decreed to be anti-Christian. My school declared they had ownership of the idea of God and the right to determine how he was presented.

        Essentially, the idea is that there is a ONE TRUE DEPICTION of cosmological figures and myth and that religious figures are beyond reproach. It denies them use in satirical or countermanding narratives that challenge the fundamental assumptions about mythologies. It's the attitude that the Church of Satan should not exist because they are atheists misusing the mythology of Judaeo-Christianity or disrespecting the faith of people like Jesus.

        It also implies ownership of shared cultural heritage that absolutely everyone should own. Far Right believers in Odin and Thor should have no more ownership of them than Marvel comics or someone on the street because they are part of the shared cultural heritage. I'm a believer in Christianity myself but everyone should have the right to satirize the Church, Angels, God, and more if they believe or don't. Dogma, Steve Jackson's In Nomine, DC's Lucifer, and more benefiting society as they challenge assumptions about my faith.

        Also, I think it runs into the fact that shared cultural mythology can and should be something we, the audience, know is used in a medium that we know is fantasy. Wonder Woman's Greek Gods are not the Greek Gods of any actual believers and no one should tell Percy Jackson's writer that he can't use them in a story. Same for Michael Carpenter having supernatural Christian powers in the Dresden Files. Artistic license should be allowed in fiction because it is not affecting the whole.

        Take note that I say that while saying the PURE ONES and the Native American tribal depiction in Werewolf is bullshit because it's badly written dumbassery that is presented as fact rather than satire.

        On the inverse, anti-White and anti-Christian prejudice is very real in e.g. Japan or India. I'd be just as outraged about a piece of media from there beating down on the minorities in their own societies.
        I should mention that my defense applies to NEON GENESIS EVANGELION, BAYONETTA, and SHIN MEGAMI TENSEI.

        In Bayonetta, you kill angels. You eventually kill God.

        Not my bag but that's the story and it has no effect on my religion.

        I think that there's a pretty large state between, "using the mythology of another society as part of your fictional cosmology" and "actively misrepresents real-life history in order to make said cosmology." Which is to say the treatment of Native Americans in Werewolf is terrible because its bad history but it's not the same as saying that no one can use anyone else's mythology as researched.

        Victor Lavalle is a black man and he wrote THE BALLAD OF BLACK TOM which is an inversion of the Cthulhu Mythos. H.P. Lovecraft would not have approved. And you know what? Fuck HP Lovecraft. Victor could also write a bunch of books about Odin and Thor getting their ass kicked by his black protagonist and good for him. It has artistic merit as social satire despite the idea he's not "of" Nordic culture.

        Which I suppose all means:

        "I think the problems with Werewolf aren't presenting that the myths of Native Americans differently, it's presenting them as if they were fact in RL."

        And you are right, punching up is very different from punching down.

        Sorry.

        Complex issue.
