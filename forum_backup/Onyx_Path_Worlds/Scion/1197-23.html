                                            We obviously have a different view of how to play this game. That's fine. The "Hero" in the name doesn't necessarily mean "good guy" to me. It means what it meant to the Greeks who coined it.

                                            I don't want to reject out of hand pantheons that have beliefs radically different to those held up as "good" in our current society. I will take the baby-sacrificing Canaanite gods on their terms, the Mesopotamian gods who perpetrate genocide for the pettiest of reasons on theirs, the homicidally jealous and petty Greek gods on theirs. If I'm talking Abrahamic, I'll even take the Old Testament guy on his terms, which are not very nice ones.

                                            At the same time, I don't buy into the Titan War very much at all. I have run games where the Aztec gods were the vanguard of the bad guys and there were no Titans at all. I've run them where it was exactly "Wake up kid! I'm your deadbeat dad! I know your life was miserable, but it's going to get worse. You're going to war, son!" I've split the difference and made their Titans into gods and chopped up the different ways they use that word for so many different things (like Avatar) to create different echelons of being/creation/etc.

                                            For what it's worth, the only time I've used the Abrahamic stuff explicitly, it was with Yaweh as a the primary Avatar of the Greater Titan of Truth. Not subjective truth, but Absolute Truth, which is a very scary thing. His minions started out as the bad guys, but as things progressed they met other minions of other Greater Titans and found out that what they thought was simple, wasn't. Some of the gods were bad guys. Some of the Titan (Avatars) were good guys. It was a complicated, messed up world.

                                                    For what it's worth - not saying this is the "best" way, or saying it should be in the core book, but here's how I plan to handle it if I can ever get a Scion game running:

                                                    Aten really is the Judeo-Christian God. No coy flirting with the idea. In the book, Aten was really, really blatantly meant to be the Judeo-Christian God, so just come right out and embrace it.

                                                    The thing is, you don't really need to tweak the idea very much to actually resolve a lot of the problems discussed in this thread! Aten actually canonically *is* an over-god more powerful than all the other gods. Aten actually *is* canonically the first being in existence in the game universe. Aten arguably *did* make Creation, with all other Titans (and then gods) following after his Light at the dawn of time.

                                                    Aten is also an antagonist to the player characters. But... well, wouldn't the Judeo-Christian God be expected to be an antagonist to a bunch of pagan deities? Just because he's an antagonist doesn't mean he's evil. Not all Titans are evil, so you could play it however you want. My personal preference would be to play Aten as a full, to the hilt, fire and brimstone Old Testament God, but that's just my game.

                                                    I think the only thing I'd be likely to change is that in my campaign, Aten was never cast down in the Titanomachy.

                                                    Although if you want to throw in a bit of Milton, I rather like the idea that some of his Titanspawn actually *did* break free once upon a time, same as with the other Titans and Pantheons. In this case, though, it was Aten who won and cast that Pantheon down into the underworld. Now we know them as Lucifer and the fallen angels, and when the other Titans smashed their way out of the underworld, guess who else got loose?

                                                            And now we're back on the subject of Good Titans, which a good amount of people would believe considering the current religious landscape of Christianity, Judaism, and Islam. Hell, after reading the Titan Avatars in the God book of 1e, the only avatar that, seemed to me, filled the role of "white-hat" the best was Prometheous, though his plans seem to be half-assed at times. Aten, in 1e, was a total dick who believed completely in his monotheistic dogma. To make him a "Good Titan" would mean that he'll have to get a total psychological redo on his personality. The reason why I had my two cents on this thread was that I was concerned that the Scions weren't harassed (to the point of bodily harm) by the "God hates fags" minority of Christians and the "Preach with the sword" mentality that some Muslims have, just like they do in our real world. To me, Scion is a what if scenario where the gods of old still existed. Not having that very visual and vocal part of our world makes me break the immersiveness that I would expect in a Scion game.

                                                            Throughout this thead, two solutions came out that I do approve:

                                                            1. A Canaanite pantheon that includes Yahweh. Adding the henotheistic side of ancient religions will give more diversity, plus it would explain the omnipresence of our current religions and the opinions on that.

                                                            2. Good Titans. This was hinted before as a possible character creation point with the incluison of Chronos and Nyx as divine parents. I think this is because we now realize that the Titans were really the pantheon that precluded the Olympians and that the Theol pantheon just is a result of a couple of timely coups (Chronos pulling a coup on Uranus, then Zeus pulling a coup on Chronos, which then, would allegedly result of Athena pulling a coup on Zeus (if the prophecy was correct), and so on. Because of the Angels minions, and with a little tweaking, Aten could be a Good Titan which orchestrated the Abrahamic Three.

                                                            Hopefully when the game finally ships, then we can see where Christianity, Judaism, and Islam fall in the Scion universe.
