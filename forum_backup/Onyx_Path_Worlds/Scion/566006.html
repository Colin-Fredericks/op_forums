    Hoping for some changes to Aten in 2e

    06-23-2015, 11:42 PM
    I hate to make one of my first posts here a ranty one, but I'm really really hoping that Aten and his servants get a bit of a rewrite in Scion 2e (when the time comes, of course; that won't be till Scion: God).
    Basing the Titan of Light on the original Egyptian monotheism, and then having him appropriate/possibly invent the later Abrahamic ones, was a cool idea. Creating that secret society of Catholics dedicated to Fatebinding Gods into Saints (or something like that), was also a cool idea. But then you actually travel to the Greater Titan Akhetaten, and oh wait! Every single one of all of its minions is a suicide bomber angel who can be bypassed either by shouting dogmatic slogans at it or debating it into submission on pointless theological points. Was the writer just working out some internal anger about Jews/Christians/Muslims? Because everything else about the presentation of the Titans in that book is awesome and makes me want to start a campaign right at God-level, and this one part is just so strangely bad. It's like depicting the MtAs Celestial Chorus as composed entirely of fundamentalist Christians. Who are also suicide bombers. And lobotomised.
    [/rant]
    Anyway this is also my first post. Hello everybody! I got into the WoDs, both C and N, via an older cousin who had a lot of the older game books. Then I sort of forgot about White Wolf games for a while, and then I just recently came back to discover that all their stuff is being handled by this great big wonderful transparent thing called Onyx Path and I want to play in all of the universes at once.

If Tlazolteotl is running around cleaning the sewers, then the entity behind the greatest organized monotheistic religions of the setting can be as crazy as necessary. Bring on the slogans. Bring on the cheese. Fundamentalists in the real world might be srz business whatever religion they belong to, but in Scion? You get to laugh and enjoy the madness.

                                          But why choose between scary and funny when you can have both? Tragedy and comedy are not mutually exclusive. Laughter does not diminish the seriousness of a dangerous or dark situation. It does not devalue serious matters, it is an integral part of the world, and sometimes it happens at inappropriate times.
                                          Think about it. The Trojan War started because three goddesses wanted to be acknowledged as the prettiest, and Zeus couldn`t be bothered, and it sort of went downhill from there. Is it funny as hell? You bet it is. Does it diminish all the tragedy and bloodshed? No. Same goes for the famous episode when Ares and Aphrodite overreact to minor wounds they recieved from a mortal. That`s ridiculous. But neither Olympian becomes any less divine because if it. Even one of the greatest tragedies of all time - "The Bacchae" - will make the reader snicker more than once.

                                          Regarding the scenarios you mention, Horace actually has a terrible crisis on his hands. Brainwashing minions might not be a sophisticated or dignified strategy, but it certainly is more efficient than it seems. Especially if it can happen to pretty much anyone. The ridiculousness of an enemy does not make said enemy harmless. It just makes all the havoc they can wreak more surreal.

                                          Actually, surreal is a good term for Aten. Sanity just doesn`s work with him. So yes, this Titan can probably never be "played straight" (unless you go for "obfuscating stupidity"), mainly because it`s one of the most twisted beings in the setting. And it`s brand of madness is contagious - so be happy we can`t take him seriously. Because as long as he makes you laugh, as long as you see how little sense everything about him makes - you are free from him.
