            Originally posted by Shadowflame View Post
            Something about that word "God" that should say something.
            It should! But they're two games separated by a common vocabulary:

            A God in Scion is a mythic figure writ large, lasting through the centuries, possessed of ineffable power and wisdom. Their children are champions blessed upon the world, the making of legends which shall never be forgot.

            A God in Exalted is a spiritual figure who may be in charge of the Heavens, this here rice field, or that slightly bent nail over there in the wall. Their children are a bit...*more* than normal, but not by that much. Assuming a shared area of expertise, the titular Exalted will generally rapidly outgrow and dominate: They were made to wrestle with things larger than mere gods.

                                    Edit: I cut up my original post because honestly, i don't care that much to defend that little table i made up or whatever. Distracts too much of the main topic, might add unnecessary fuel to a fire better quenched and the OP manifested itself to further explain what it has in mind.

                                    So, on with the boat and here goes nothing:

                                    On adding Sorcery and Martial Arts to Scion? Well, i don't really know how that would work out. What makes those things special in Exalted is their nature as "uneven but common ground" between the many varied types of supernatural entities tied to Creation a function of their role "stuff to let characters get powers outside the themes of their type".

                                    Sorcery with its "catch as catch can" attitude to spells is closer to most purviews in arrangement than Scion's Magic from what i remember, the idea of effects more powerful than usual for one's Legend level that require "loading time" and the potential option of an "auto-undo" in the form of countermagic certainly has merit. Summoning & banishment are cool but could probably be done just as well or better through Purviews.

                                    Meanwhile on Scion "type themes" is far less of a deal - if it can even be said to be one - scions and lesser immortals/titanspawn are more embryonic gods/greater titanspawn than actual different entities. The greatest "distinction of nature" in the setting would be titanic x divine divide that defines the main conflict of Scion - and what actually separates them in those is how much Fate influenced them or not in developing a certain "more human" set of virtues/culture/values and a (pantheon) specific Purview that resonates with that set of virtues. Commonalities prevail and theme more of a matter of what an individual being favors, it's the opposite of the situation that makes Sorcery and Martial Arts a sort of sub-system in Exalted.

                                    That said, they do possess a "fire stolen from the gods" appeal, terrain with very little coverage in Scion, that is a niche Sorcery & esoteric martial arts might fit in. However, the fact mortals don't have or use Legend naturally, energy costs in Exalted in relation to energy costs in Scion among other things would serious tweaking unavoidable. Also, there are a few things already in that niche a ST could more easily work from, like mortals as low-level guides, the rules for heroic mortal companions or the artifacts of the Order of the Divine Glory (both in Scion Companion) are a few i can remember.

                                    Though on a little aside, Thaumaturgy with its basis on resources to sacrifice, be they expensive materials, blood, jade or magic energies harvested from relics or places of mystic significance offers just as many possibities as Sorcery with none of the hassle to adapt, as you can basically use it as is on Scion games and little to no work to port over. Might cook up some extra rituals with a virtue requirement for color, or some rule to lower ritual costs when focusing an appropriate virtue in the roll or "sacrificing" a virtue-ressonant action/stunt for legend, but not much beyond that.

                                    Another thing that crossed my mind is - Magic, from what i can remember of the books, IS Scion's equivalent of fate-weaving, so sidereal astrology and charms might be a much better comparison/foil than Sorcery, that is about shaping and conjuring things, beings and forces up from the fabric of reality. But either way, reworking Fate means reworking Sorcery as a whole, because they're inextricably connected afaik.

                                    Well, longish post, but those are my thoughts on the objectives you brought up, blindST - my initial impression is that overall there's not much call for either Sorcery or esoteric MA in Scion (specially the latter, as the setting lacks the strong wuxia influence/imagery that defines it) and while there is at least one good niche in "fire stolen from the gods" theme, resonant but little explored in the game, there are also quicker and cleaner alternatives to mine in Scion (guides & aforementioned stuff in the Companion) and Exalted (Thaumaturgy) both for that and if you do, they can rock mightly. Hope these ingredients help with cooking some this game you are brewing.

                                    Would really like to hear of what you have to say on this, maybe give it a little try myself.
