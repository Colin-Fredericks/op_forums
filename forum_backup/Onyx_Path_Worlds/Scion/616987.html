          Mangle's pretty much got it nailed.

          Knowledge of mythology is helpful, if you want games that really capture the feeling of the ancient myths. On the other hand, you can just as easily base the gods off pop-cultural versions of the gods if you wish. Hercules the Legendary Journeys? Sure. Why not? I'm not terribly familiar with American Gods, but as I do believe that's another perfectly fine example of what you can do with Scion.

          If you want to be as mythologically accurate as possible, there are books and web sites all over the place that can fill you in. But again, it's not absolutely essential. Take the gods in the books and do what you like with them.

          Demigod and God are absolutely necessary if you intend to have your players advance to the higher levels of power. It should be mentioned, however, that Scion's system really starts to fall apart at the higher levels. I have yet to meet a Scion group that doesn't have to do a LOT of houseruling to make the game work. There is a Second Edition in the works right now, so you might want to keep your eyes open for that when it's ready.

          What is eye-catching about the game to players? Power. LOTS of power. Scion is one of the most high-powered games in the RPG world. Also? If players like high-octane action in a setting where the old world of myth and the modern world collide, then Scion is definitely for them.

          For ST's? It can be a challenge dealing with players and NPC's that are as powerful as Scions can become. Plus, there's the appeal of telling modern myths and playing with the epic action and romance of the mythological world.

              Hero, Demi God and God are all part of a continuing story arc that follows your character's path towards divinity. So while you don't actually need Demi God and God to enjoy the game, you'll be stuck with low level characters if you run a strictly Hero game. There is also a 4th book called Ragnarok that revolves around the end of the world.

              Even if you don't know much about mythology, the books should have at least some introduction to the pantheons. In fact, one of the books includes the Atlantean pantheon who I believe are completely made up, rather than being from a real world religion.


              The game has a pretty large collection of pantheons to choose from. You may not have heard of all of them, but you should at least be familiar with one.


              Greek Pantheon

              Egyptian Pantheon

              Celtic Pantheon

              Japanese Pantheon

              Chinese Pantheon

              Norse Pantheon

              Yankee (American) Pantheon

              Allied (British / France / Russian) Pantheon.

              Aztec Pantheon

              Voodoo Pantheon

              Hindu Pantheon

              Persian Pantheon



              The Yankee Pantheon includes American folk tale heroes. Like Paul Bunyan, the guy who dug the Grand Canyon. Yeah, you can be his kid! Or better yet, you can be the child of Uncle Sam himself!



              And you don't have to base your game around real world religions either, the game has details on how to create your own player made pantheon as well. Not familiar with any of the official religions? Make up your own!



              What's eye catching to me about the game is that it is essentially White Wolf's version of a super hero game. Your characters will become massively powerful in this game, to the point where they can obliterate anything the World of Darkness can throw at them. I even read one example of a character who can lift a skyscraper over his head. Your characters are the children of gods, and the game involves their journey to become gods, while at the same time battling the Titans who threaten to destroy the world.
