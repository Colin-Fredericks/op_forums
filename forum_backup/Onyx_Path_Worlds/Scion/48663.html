    The Grand Kami Rewrite: Preliminaries and Input

    01-13-2014, 10:33 PM
    Alright, so we all know the Scion corebook is a bit of a horrendous mess, especially in regards to Associated Powers for gods. (I mean, have you looked at the Teotl there?) And while I saw a lot of work done for people's personal favorites (the Greeks, the Norse, etc.) I saw a dearth in Kami discussion or rewrites or anything really. So, I took it upon myself to completely rewrite them, from the ground up, Associated Powers, Pantheon Specific Purview, Opposing Titan, the whole nine yards. And now I'm about a third through this project, and I guess it's time to put what I'm thinking out there for feedback and new ideas.
    I'm trying to make this as mythologically accurate as possible, so if anyone has any good sources they're just dying to share, please let me know! The majority of my research has come from Davis' Myths and Legends of Japan, and Ashkenazi's Handbook of Japanese Mythology, Along with the Kojiki and Nihongi as well, though the translations online are more than a bit dated.

    At the moment, I've got my God Roster done, and when I go through them I'll explain my changes from the Core Books. Besides that, I have two very vauge ideas of what their opposing Titan will be, Death and Storms, which we'll talk about also later, and the basic concept for a new PSP, because while Tsukumo-Gami is very Japanese, it is neither remotely equal in power to the other PSPs, nor is it the best fit for the Kami as a whole.

    Also, I gave them new virtues, which are at the moment: Duty, Harmony, Intellect, Valor.

    God Powers: The Core Book Gods
    Amaterasu: Epic Appearance, Epic Charisma, Epic Strength, Sun
    Amaterasu's rewrite was pretty easy. Epic Appearance and Sun are givens due to the myth when she hides in the cave, her presence as both the Sun and someone of great beauty is indisputable. I kept her with Epic Strength for the time she performed the war dance, and each strike put her into the ground up to her knees, which is something no other god does. Giving her Charisma is the main thing I'm iffy about, but her intimidation factor due to the war dance, along with her status as pantheon head made it a reasonable choice.

    Susano-o: Epic Wits, Chaos, Death, Sky
    Susanoo, on the other hand, is an odd duck. Essentially the god of everything the Kami do not like, he is nonetheless a important member of their pantheon and while he is often the antagonist in myths, his tale of the slaying of Orochi firmly places him in the roster as a God. Now, there's a lot different here. First of all, he doesn't have Psychopomp, because what? He's not a ferrymen of the dead, or a god of liminal places (his son, on the other hand...). He is more of a Death god. After he whines and complains about his mother, Izanami, being dead, Izanagi gets fed up with his shenanigans and forces him to give up the sea-plain, and banishes him to Yomi, land of the dead. I took this as a sign that he does not have Water, since otherwise he'd be more associated with it. Later, when his son Okuninushi arrives at his home, which is in Yomi, he has centipedes in his hair which are a sure sign in Japanese symbology of corruption and death. His assocation with the "Weapons of Life" that are later stolen from him further places him as a figure of Death and the dead. Sky is a given for the god of Storms, and Chaos was a toss up. But as a god concerned with screwing things up and making life less rigid, like when he chucks the horse through Amaterasu's wall, I figured Chaos wasn't a bad idea. Wits is iffy, but I gave it to him for his quick thinking to drug Orochi into being killable, though something else might fit better, but I haven't found it.

    Tsuki-Yomi: Moon?
    Seriously, what else can you give this guy in good faith? His one myth I can find (the slaying of Uke Mochi) doesn't lend itself to any particular purview or ability, and so I can't give him anything else without just making things up, which is counter to my intentions.

    Raiden: N/A
    Raiden is not a god. While powerful, he's a pretty antagonistic figure, especially for the Kami who don't like storms or anything else uncontrollable. He also eats children's stomachs, which tends to push him to Titan.

    Izanagi: N/A
    I'm pushing Izanagi to Titan, because of his distance from mortal affairs, his creation of the world (which puts may figures into the Titans, regardless of antagonism), and the fact that his wife is also probably a Titan.

    Izanami: N/A or Epic Appearance, Death, Earth, Health
    Izanami sits in a weird position for Scion. She pretty much hates everything because her husband defied her and left her in Yomi, which she now rules. This would at a glance make her prime Major Titan material, but she also fulfills a vital role in their cosmology as the ruler of the their Underworld. I personally lean more towards her being a Titan, with Yomi being the greater Titanrealm of Decay or Death, but I'm including her as a god in case others want the Kami to have a legitimate underworld, which is understandable. I debated giving her Stamina, but being burned alive and never coming back to life might not be the best case. Death and Epic Appearance are obvious powers, being unfathomably horrifying and the Death Goddess. Health is for her sheer amount of creation of deities, and Earth is due to the fact that she literally gave birth to islands. Though, I'm also iffy on Earth.

    Hachiman: Epic Intelligence, Fertility, War
    Hachiman is a really important god, but his stories are muddied by the fact that he may be a conglomeration of several, previous figures. War is the obvious purview for him as the War god and patron of Samurai. Epic Intelligence was also something I though was a no-brainier in his role as he is supposedly a giver of counsel and Oracle, though there's no record I can find of him giving prophecies. Fertility was a bit of a last-ditch effort. He is worshiped as a god of agriculture, but I couldn't find any myths where he specifically grew plants or taught people agriculture, but his sheer shrine worship (second only to Inari, who we'll get to later) convinced me to give him it. I debated Guardian and Stamina, but couldn't justify them.

    Ryujin: Epic Appearance, Epic Strength, Sky, Water
    Ryujin is the guy to go to when you want a Water Kami. He lives under the water, controls the tides, and is a giant dragon. Japanese dragons are huge shapeshifters, so I gave him Appearance to represent that. He gets Sky for the same reason Tlaloc does; he controls the weather. He is worshiped in festivals in both of his roles as God of the Sea and as a God of Rain, which only further cements the two purviews. I gave him Strength because he beats the proto-jellyfish that he reduces its bones to jelly and none of its descendants have bones afterwards, which is pretty hardcore Strength.

    Marishiten: N/A
    Marishiten is originally a Chinese goddess called Marici, and she's better known there than in Japan, so when I eventually (emphasis on "eventually") get around to re-writing the Shen, she'll be there.

    The New Arrivals
    Ame-no-Uzume: Epic Appearance, Epic Charisma, Artistry*
    Now, I know Artistry isn't a vanilla purview, so ignore it if you're not using it, but I know many games use an Industry, or Forge, or Artistry or Creation purview of some sort and as the creator of an entire art form, I figured if anyone had it, Uzume would. Now, Appearance is easy, as she's described as the "Heavenly Alarming Female" and uses her beauty to win over Sarutahiko in a manner very similar to Hathor with Ra over in Egypt. Charisma is a toss up, because as "The Heavenly Persuader", she clearly has either Charisma or Manipulation. I gave her Charisma for her antics and stripteases bringing all the gods to uproar when they tried to convince Amaterasu to leave the cave. I'm debating Sun, but I'm not sure if she has The Glory.

    Inari: Epic Appearance, Animal (Fox), Artistry*, Fertility,
    Why Inari wasn't listed as a core god (or even a god at all) in the books is baffling, but we're rectifying that issue now. As the god with the honor of having the most shrines in Japan, Inari is seriously important. He literally does almost everything, which puts me at a loss of what to give him. As a shapeshifter who often appears in many forms, male and female, Appearance was a given. As the god of foxes and occasionally a fox themselves, I gave Inari Animal (Fox). Inari's basic role is as the god of Rice and Rice Fields, which means they definitely need Fertility. Artistry is iffy, but as a god of blacksmiths and noted weapon maker, I figure it wasn't too much of a stretch. Though there are a multitude of other powers would could give them, ranging from Charisma to Health to Magic, but none of them fit their myths the ways these do, in my opinion.

    Okuninushi: Epic Manipulation, Health, Magic, Psychopomp
    The son of Susanoo and The Lord of The Land, Okuninushi is also a big deal. As the healer of the Hare of Inaba and the god of Medicine, Health seemed to be a natural fit. He is often described as the God of Magic and a Wizard, but I'm unsure if that gives him Magic or not, since the Kami don't particularly have a concept of Fate the Western gods do. Now, I mentioned earlier that Susanoo and Okuninushi are in a myth together, which is why I gave him Manipulation. Since he's trying to win the hand of his half sister, Suseri (which Susanoo does not want), Okuninushi is given the task of cleaning out Susanoo's hair. But instead of lice, he has venomous centipedes crawling on his scalp. Suseri comes up with the plan to have Okuninushi chew nuts and red clay and spit them out to give the illusion that he was chewing the Centipedes and risking his life for his future father in law. (If I was stating Suseri, I'd give her Wits for this) Okuninushi has to pull this off, along with trying to steal all of Susanoo's Life Weapons and tie him to the rafters in his house, which I considered sneaky and manipulative enough to give him Epic Manipulation as Associated. He escaped Yomi back to the land of the living in an obvious parallel to Izanagi and Izanami, which was the foundation for giving him Psychopomp. Further in adding to him as a god between life and death, he is murdered by his brothers on many occasions, but is brought back to life each time, which again makes him between both states. Finally, when he is forced to give up the Mortal World as his domain, he gains the world of spirits and the unseen as his domain, which again, cements the liminal powers that Psychopomp deals with.

    Gods I debated putting on this list, but couldn't find enough evidence for them being Legend 12: Tsuki-Yomi, Sengen/Konohanasakuya-hime, Sarutahiko, Tenjin.

    Titan Ideas
    The first is the most obvious, with it being Yomi, Titan of Death/Corruption. Being associated with negative Health boons, Death and potentially Earth, it would be headed by Izanami and other figures of death and decay. It may or may not be the same place as Tuonela, the Finnish land of the Dead. I'm not incredibly versed in Finnish myth, but I do recall on the writeup someone did that Death was also their opposing force, which would make for some interesting stories and cosmological questions.

    The other is Storms, which would be probably headed by Raijin and Fujin, being storm demons who like destruction and such. However, Storms is also a really great antagonist for the Polynesian Atua, which could pose some problems. This is the one I'd run if you want to play Izanami as a playable Goddess, because otherwise you run into some baffling questions like "Why does she rule our Titanrealm?" and the like.

    New PSP: Harae
    Now, this only the most nebulous stage right now, which is brainstorming. Harae are the purification rituals and the like that are one of the bigger parts of the religion. However, mechanically, I'm not sure what they'd do. Give you a better resistance to Disease, Negative Health Boons, and Death Boons? I'm debating it being like the Yazata purview of Asha where it simply doesn't work id you've been recently corrupted (Death boons, Blood, that sort of thing) and haven't properly purified yourself. Now, this might put a bit of strain on characters who want to pursue Death, but that shouldn't be too much of an issue, especially since Death as a negative force is so ingrained in their cosmology as a whole to begin with. Sure, Susanoo has Death, but does anybody like him or want him around? Not really.
    Now, if anybody wants to brainstorm ideas, feel free to, I need all the help I can get with this.

          I'm currently working on an RPG which delves into a lot of Shinto, so let me toss in my two cents here.

          O-Inari
          Yes, a thousand times yes. This kami has more shrines than any other god in Japan. We're talking 30 thousand+ officially sanctioned shrines, and over one million unofficial shrines, including personal shrines, house shrines, and roadside shrines.

          Amaterasu Omikami, because yes. Her husband / brother? No, in fact, he would probably not be in the core book. Why? Because he is considered almost a non-person in the myths after his screw up with Amaterasu Omikami.

          Izanagi no Mikoto and Izanami no Mikoto. No, they shouldn't be in the core rulebook. Why? Because once Amaterasu Omikami took over Heaven, they fell into obscurity. They could be around in theory, but unlikely. Part of this is due to the Japanese Underworld - if you go there, you are considered unclean, and the only way you come back is as a yurei. Not really a thing you want to introduce to your PCs. In fact, the entire point of Shinto's rituals of the dead is to prevent you from going to the underworld. Instead, you become promoted to godhood. Every single person who's enshrined is a god. Not even a 'little god' - if you have any warrior's blood in you, and someone ticks you off, you can create storms, earthquakes, and tsunamis to show just how pissed you are. So, yeah - these two shouldn't be on the list.

          Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto. Yes, she should be on the list. She's the very first shrine maiden. She is the goddess who brought Amaterasu Omikami back out into the world. She is also the one the celestial kami sent to the leader of the Kunitsukami - Sarutahiko Okami - as a wife. Sarutahiko Okami is the only terrestrial god to have 'Okami' in his title, and only four celestial gods have that title in their name. In fact, Sarutahiko Okami and Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto are the only gods to have an actual shrine in North America (just north of Seattle). (This shrine also doubles as a Shrine to O-Inari, as both Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto oand Sarutahiko Okami are both "aspects" of O-Inari).

          The Seven Fortunes of Luck. These are also Hindu and Chinese gods, but they should be available in Japan as well, because they are a big part of Japanese culture (and... two of them are also aspects of O-Inari... go figure).

          Susano-O. Yes, he's a thorn in Amaterasu's side, but he is also a big damn hero, and would very much play a part in immortal affairs in Japan. He redeemed himself in his sister's eyes, which is also a good thing.

          Really, that's about it.
          Amaterasu Omikami, Susano-O at the top of the chain. The Empress and her errant brother.
          Ame-no-Uzume-no-Mikoto, the shrine maiden, and her husband, King of the Terrestrial Gods, Sarutahiko Okami
          O-Inari, the god/goddess who is damn near everywhere, appearing as a dozen different gods (and has been associated with Izanagi and Izanamo no Mikoto, as well as Amaterasu's sister, and her grandson).
          The Seven Fortunes of Luck - which between them cover the gamut of abilities.

          That's 12 - enough for a pantheon, I think.

                                                  I don't tend to go back on my word.

                                                  The New PSP: Harae
                                                  The Japanese Concept of Harae is rooted in the idea that there are corrupting forces in the universe, and to come into contact with them is to become spiritually unclean. If a Scion comes into contact with Disease, Death, or Blood, she cannot use her Harae boons for a number of days equal to her amount of Harae Boons or until she ritually purifies herself, whichever comes first. In order to count as Defiled, she must either have used a Negative Health or Death boon with a roll, or failed her resist roll on corrupting influences.

                                                  Purifying: The purifying rituals of the Kami have trickled down to mortals, and for an enterprising Scion who either is interested in Death or finds themselves afflicted with Kegare more often than they'd like can go to a Shinto Shrine and request to be purified. Also, being above mortals in a fundamental way by being a divine, an intelligent Scion could theoretically figure out a way to purify themselves with an Intelligence + Occult roll surpassing 5 Successes. However, since such rituals require stern concentration and a bit of time, they're not possible during combat.

                                                  1. Kessai
                                                  Cost: None
                                                  Roll: None
                                                  Taking her first step onto the path of spiritual purity, the Scion now has a supernatural avoidance to the effects of those trying to defile her. She gains a number of dice equal to her number of Harae boons on any roll to resist corruption.

                                                  2. Temizuya
                                                  Cost: 1+ Legend, 1 Willpower
                                                  Roll: None
                                                  Having grown in power, the Scion with Temizuya can purify the hands of her compatriots, granting them a number of dice equal to her number of Harae boons to resist Corrupting influences. After spending the Legend and Willpower to activate the boon, the Scion must pay 1 Legend for each additional person she wants to cleanse.

                                                  3. Shubatsu
                                                  Cost: 3 Legend + 1 Willpower
                                                  Roll: Stamina + Integrity
                                                  Riding on the cusp of Demigodhood, the Scion with Shubatsu can now ward a room with salt in order to purify it. Anyone (including the Scion herself) who tries to use corrupting powers while within the room suffers a penalty of -Harae dice to their activation rolls. The ward stays up for a number of days equal to the Scion’s successes, after which she must perform the ritual again if she wishes to continue enjoying its effects.

                                                  4. Misogi
                                                  Roll: Harae+Legend
                                                  Cost: 5 Legend + 1 Willpower
                                                  Now more divine than mortal, the Scion with Misogi can perform a ritual purification for herself under a source of natural falling water. This boon is unaffected by the Scion’s state of Defilement, and is the only one to do so. If the Scion is defiled, it will purify her once the ritual is complete. This Boon can only be used once per story, any more uses is at the Storyteller’s discretion. After the ritual is complete, the now purified Scion gains a pool of Successes equal to her activation Roll that can be spent in any manner she pleases. However, if she becomes defiled once again, that pool of bonuses vanishes, regardless of how many are left.

                                                  5. Kannushi
                                                  Roll: None
                                                  Cost: None
                                                  The Scion is now a priest of the Kami in their own right, such is their mastery of Harae. From now on, the Scion can choose to substitute their default resist roll with their mental resist roll (Default is Will+Integrity+Legend, but can be Int/Wits/App) to resist forms of corruption. Also, the Scion may choose to replace Fortitude with Integrity on any activation or resist roll, if she sees fit.

                                                  6. Nagoshi
                                                  Roll: None
                                                  Cost: None
                                                  The strength of the Scion’s Shubatsu wards increase, now able to completely wall the Undead unless they overcome her activation roll with their own Stamina + Integrity roll.

                                                  7. Oharae
                                                  Roll: Charisma + Presence
                                                  Cost: Varies (See below)
                                                  As the Scion prepares for their ascension, their mastery of Harae grows ever stronger. By performing the proper rituals in the presence of the people they want to purify, the Scion can purify a number of people equal to her Legend. If she can overcome the boon user’s initial activation roll with her own Charisma + Presence roll, she can pay the cost of the boon to remove from her charge. Boons affected by this boon include: Curse of Frailty, Intoxicate, Control Aging, Lingering Malice, and Poltergeist Beacon. The Scion can purify a number of people this way equal to her Legend. Those who are cleansed of their afflictions this way are also considered spiritually clean, if they happen to have Harae themselves.

                                                  8. Shinzen
                                                  Roll: None
                                                  Cost: None
                                                  As the Scion divorces himself from the mortal and becomes truly divine, he mastery of the rituals of the gods increases. From now on, his dice for Kessai become successes, but only for himself, (Temizuya is unaffected). Also, his sheer divinity increases the powers of all of his purification rituals. He may now add his number of Harae boons to any roll as dice to activate Harae boons.

                                                  9. Mitama
                                                  Roll: None
                                                  Cost: None or 10 Legend
                                                  The Scion is now an established god at this point, and her very soul has changed to reflect this. They now possess a mitama, a honorable spirit, which harnesses their purity to smite the corrupted. Any undead or otherwise Defiled creature who attempts to strike her automatically takes a penalty in dice equal to the God’s level of Harae. If she wishes to pay the legend cost, she may make that penalty become Successes for the duration of one attack. Regardless of whether or not the attack connects, the assailant has been weakened by the Scion’s sheer purity, causing the next attack to hit them to deal Aggravated damage.

                                                  10. Kagura
                                                  Roll: None
                                                  Cost: 30 Legend
                                                  Having mastered the entire Harae purview, the Scion is rewarded with the ability to perform the divine dance, the Kagura. After spending the legend, the Scion becomes a paragon of purity, becoming immune to all negative Health boons bar The Scourge for the rest of the scene. All positive Health boons used on or by the Scion gain a bonus equal to the God’s Harae Boons in Successes as long as the user is around the Kami using Kagura.
