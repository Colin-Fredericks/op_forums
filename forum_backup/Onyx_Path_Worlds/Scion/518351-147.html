                                                Originally posted by Nicolas Milioni View Post
                                                they are born with the magic or do they somehow steal it from the gods and scions?
                                                Well, saying they are "classic antagonists" is a bit much already - in general, witches are beyond good and evil; they are destructive, but assumed to be just, and are often revered, e.g. in the Gelede mask festival. The great, powerful witches the gods can get in trouble with (the Iyami, "Our Mothers", often wrongly and offensively called Aje) are like a weird mixture between harpies and wise elders - they only punish the guilty, they even debate punishment in council before exacting it, but when they do, it is chaotic, destructive and extreme. As I said, there are myths about them confronting the gods, but those are by far not common enough to have the Iyami called "classic antagonists", and usually, when gods and witches fight, the witches are in the right - the Orisha are anything but infallible. Obatala is attacked by witches for stealing water from their well, and Orunmila is cursed by them for threatening them with a sword while they were his pupils in divination.

                                                Goddesses like Onile/Nana Buruku, Oshun, and Oya can even be said to be members (or even leaders!) of the Iyami themselves. That's because Eye, the "bird magic" that gives the witches power, is inherent to every woman. Not every woman develops and uses that power, not every woman has it in her to become a true witch, but every woman could be a witch. And thus there are of course also lesser, more human witches than the grand "Mothers" or "Elders of the Night", who are fully capable of acting injustly, even evilly. But these are not fit opponents for the gods, they trade in small curses and poisons, enacting revenges and jealousies, and can be punished for it by human courts (although calling upon the judicial might of Orisha Oko).

                                                So, to answer your question, the power of Yoruba witches is not stolen, but to say they're born with it is not quite enough either. Every woman has "bird power", but only old age and proper cult practice will turn her into a proper witch. Then, however, her power is great: She can turn into a bird, can cause wounds without touching the victim, or make the words of even everyday smalltalk painful reality. The line between the oldest of human witches and the immortal, seemingly divine witches of myth and cult is smudged, and supposed to be so - all witches are like one, one community, one unity, all servants of the Earth. Some Gelede maskerades will, however, depict them with a leader, the "Great Mother", a goddess-like hag, hunchbacked, bald, and bearded. Even this one, though, is revered and worshipped as much as she is feared.

                                                So yeah, cool in-game antagonists if your characters commit any crimes, sins or offenses against the Iyami, but they aren't really big-A-Antagonists like Titans who constantly and dangerously oppose the gods. The Orisha really lack in those - they have some elder figures, like Yemoja and Onile/Nana Buruku, but those are not consistently depicted as really being a former generation, and they really pale in comparison with Olodumare; they meanwhile also lack truly antagonistic figures, because even obvious assholes like Olokun and Shopona/Babalu Aiye have mixed morals and a lively cult base, not to mention that they lack the overwhelming power associated with Titans. Long story short, it's a quite accurate descision to depict the Orisha without Titans, and witches like the Iyami can't really fill that gap. Their biggest story catalyst, I'd say, is intra-pantheon strife - never forget those are the gods of many sepearate kingdoms compressed by some shared culture and the pressures of colonialism and slavery.
