                                                I never liked the modern national pantheons as written. My idea instead was to just have a Demigod-level coalition of Scions from various Pantheons representing American folks heroes (playing up the melting pot), with Athena and her allies backing Western Democracy since the Classical resurgence of the Enlightenment as the little project she's been tinkering with since Athens didn't work out. She is called Columbia, Britannia, and Marianne in her modern guises.

                                                I my take on WW 2, Olympus split in half like they did over Troy. Allied with Athena (inspired by "the Apotheosis of Washington") are Hermes (commerce), Hephaestus (industry), Poseidon (sea power), Demeter (agriculture), and Dionysus (counter-culture).

                                                The "we want a new Roman Empire faction" are Venus, Mars, Juno, Vesta, Apollo, and Diana. In addition to Quirinus of course though he doesn't get a vote on Olympus. Janus being Janus played at supporting the "Roman" faction but was really backing the anti-Mussolini Italian resistance. Two-faced bastard

                                                Zeus and Hades remain neutral as they always do in mortal wars.

                                                But as for the American Folkheroes, Pecos Bill is a Tornado riding Scion of Zeus, Harriet Tubman drew (through Christianity) on a lineage going back to Eshu-Elegba opening ways, Rosie the Riveter is a daughter of Brighid, etc etc.

                                                        This was originally posted by Telgar on the old forums.

                                                        Betsy Ross – Immortal Woman (Guide 2)
                                                        Remembered now for sewing the first American Flag in 1776, Betsy Ross was otherwise an unassuming woman. Now historians doubt that she even performed the single deed that garnered her fame. True or not, Miss Betsy Ross gained the attention of one of the few Scions walking the World in the 18th century. She was a hard worker and she survived a life full of many trials. Something about her spurred the Scion to grant her a gift. Ross is blind, old and fragile, but she is most certainly alive. She has almost three hundred years of experience and knowledge to bestow on young Scions, though her viewpoints are a little old fashioned.

                                                        Br’er Rabbit – Trickster Avatar
                                                        No one is sure exactly who Br’er Rabbit is, because there are many Br’er Rabbits. The name and form of the old Southern trickster are worn by many different Gods dropping by the Deep South for a visit. Sometimes he is a regular hare that acts in extraordinary ways, other times he is an anthropomorphic rabbit who walks about upright, wearing trousers and a collared shirt. He’s even appeared as an old country man with buck teeth, a harelip and too-big ears. Some suspects for Br’er Rabbit include Coyote, Anansi and even Loki. Most commonly he is assumed to be either a West African or a Native American deity assuming a more modern guise. Sometimes Br’er Rabbit helps Scions, but he’s just as likely to be stirring up the trouble that has drawn Scions to the area in the first place. You just never know.

                                                        Columbia – Minor Goddess (Guide 4)
                                                        There are conflicting tales of Columbia’s origin and she isn’t clearing them up. Some say she is a daughter of Athena, others say she is an Avatar or aspect of the older Goddess, still others suggest that Columbia is a Titanspawn, possibly a creature of Logos, somehow related to Themis. Whatever her true origin, Columbia presents herself as the Goddess of the United States. She has little respect from more established figures and, in truth, has little power of her own. She has no Scions and, so far as anyone can tell, never has had any. She does take an interest in any Scion who meddles too closely in the affairs of the United States and sometimes appears to guide them onto the ‘correct’ course for her favored nation. Since the 20’s, Columbia has more and more often appeared as Lady Liberty, often carrying a torch and wearing robes and a crown resembling those on a certain famous copper statue.

                                                        John Henry – Ghost, Former Scion of Heracles (Guide 2)
                                                        Remembered in story and song, John Henry would have been a great Scion if he hadn’t worked himself to death in the earliest stages of his Legend. He is now a ghost and since the breaking of Tartarus, has returned to the World and finds much to his distaste. Too much automation, too much laziness and reliance on machines and mass production instead of hard work and quality. He still has knowledge of strange and Legendary World that Scions inhabit to offer, as well as his own experience.

                                                        Johnny Appleseed – Adopted Legendary Persona (Guide 2)
                                                        The real Johnny Appleseed, John Chapman, was an extraordinary mortal man who lived a life that earned him some measure of Legendary status, but in 1845 he died, as mortals do. Chapman had so impressed some of the lesser Immortals and nature spirits of North America that they continue his Legend. Johnny Appleseed appears here and there, a form worn by a variety of nature spirits native to the United States, especially in Pennsylvania, Ohio, Indiana and Illinois. He tends forests, protects animals and encourages preservation. Scions who are attempting to protect nature and its creatures in the areas he frequents can sometimes find Johnny Appleseed and ask for his help. He may even take an extended interest in preservation-inclined Scions in his ranges.

                                                        Paul Bunyan – Earth Giant (Guide 3)
                                                        Now known as the archetypical lumberjack, Bunyan’s history goes back much, much further. It goes all the way back to Vinland, where Paul Bunyan was simply a Giant who pursued a Norse Scion across the ocean. The Scion slipped through his grasp, but their fight forced the young Hero to abandon his favorite mount: a blue ox. The ox was taken by Bunyan as a trophy of his victory and became his constant companion. Over time, he acclimated to his home in the New World and grew to care greatly for the earth and forests of Canada and the northern United States.

                                                        Pecos Bill – Demigod (Guide 2-3)
                                                        A brash Scion from the American West, Pecos Bill’s deeds were enough to earn him the status of Demigod, but never to propel him towards Apotheosis. As the wild west was tamed, Bill slowly withdrew from the World more and more, spending time in several of the Terra Incognitae scattered through the West. Pecos Bill claims to not know who his father was and there are various suspicions cast on figures such as Coyote, Thor, Zeus, Apollo and even Vayu. He still appears sometimes, often drawn out of his adventures in other Worlds by news of Titanspawn causing trouble in his old stomping grounds. He sometimes acts as a Guide for young Heroes, but he always wanders and his attention can be fleeting.

                                                        Rosie the Riveter – Avatar of Brigit
                                                        Following along with the Irish immigrants to the United States, the Goddess of Flame and Forge came to America and took on a new persona during the Second World War. She became an icon of female power and industrial might. She worked tirelessly to help uplift and empower female workers and inspire bravery and courage in all the descendants of her people, both at home and on the battlefield.

                                                        Uncle Sam – Avatar of Zeus
                                                        The personification of the US government sometimes comes to life and walks the World, encouraging and directing the United States towards greatness. He has another name, one far more famous: Zeus. The King of the Greek Gods was all too pleased with the attention lavished upon the culture he oversaw by the fledgeling nation across the oceans. Sometimes he takes on this identity and connects with the New World in a way slightly more effective than togas and laurels.

                                                        Britannia – Minor Goddess (Guide 4)
                                                        Once a Scion of Minerva, Britannia was the Goddess of a faraway land that her Empire had set its eyes upon. She embraced the society that she patronized and as Roman influence in England waned, Britannia grew closer to her worshippers and farther away from her divine relatives. She has forged close ties with the native Gods of the British Isles, though she is always a foreigner among them. If young Scions wish her guidance and aid, they must only make clear that they have the best interests of England at heart.

                                                        John Bull – Hero (Guide 2)
                                                        The only known Scion of Britannia, John Bull represents not England as a whole, but the middle class, common sense, salt of the earth people. He has walked among them for decades, locked forever in the form of a portly man just past the prime of life but with many years ahead. He reminds the people of tradition and stability during times of tumult and serves as a beacon of national identity that people can relate to, rather than sharing the remoteness that his mother and other, similar goddesses have about them.

                                                        Robin Hood – Demigod (Guide 3)
                                                        The Scion most recently known as Robin Hood has worn many faces through history. He has been the Green Knight of Arthurian tales and Herne the Hunter who roamed the forests of England. Some claim that he has only played these parts occasionally and that others contributed to the legends. Cernunnos has been suspected of lending to the tale of Herne, but Robin Hood and the Horned God have always been on good terms. It may be that Cernunnos is Robin’s father, but neither is inclined to discuss the matter.

                                                        d’Artagnan – Legendary Ghost (Guide 2)
                                                        Though he may not know it any longer, d'Artagnan is not the character of fiction created by Alexandre Dumas. He began life as a mortal who became captain of the Musketeers under Louis XIV. His deeds inspired Dumas’ stories and the confused spirit has been captured by the webs of Legend woven around him. He has gone insane under the pressure of Fatebonds, not having the innate resistance to their shaping that true Legendary beings do. He is no longer certain if he was ever alive, and truly believes himself to be the character of Dumas’ stories. While insane, he can offer guidance in certain matters to Scions willing and able to deal with his dementia.

                                                        Madame Guillotine – Avatar of Andarta
                                                        The bloodthirsty Gaulish Goddess of War returned to France during the Revolution and enjoyed the offerings of blood and severed heads. She walked among the French under the guise of Madame Guillotine. Occasionally Andarta still takes on the form of La Terreur, but only rarely.

                                                        Marianne – Demigoddess (Guide 3)
                                                        One of the last Scions of Teutates, Marianne rose from a long-lived Hero to a powerful Demigoddess during the turmoil of the French Revolution. She is one of the few connections between the modern nation of France and its ancient Gaulish heritage and that connection is thin indeed. Marianne fully embraces the modern world and her Gaulish family questions her loyalties. Undaunted, the brave Demigoddess seeks to always support and protect her people and her country.

                                                        Baba Yaga – Lesser Immortal (Guide 5)
                                                        The Little Grandmother of Slavic Folklore is not a Goddess, though she is more powerful than some. She lives in the Dark Forest, in a hut with no door and giant chicken legs that allow it to walk around. Gifts of blue roses can appease her, as can proper manners and heroic deeds. She exacts a great price for her aid, but she is said to know almost everything and she is one of the most powerful sorceresses in Europe.

                                                        The Citizen – Legendary Ghost (Guide 2)
                                                        Once, the Citizen had a name but he no longer does. He died during the Russian Revolution but his death was of no consequence to his mission. He still defends Russia from her enemies. Disembodied and deceased, the ghost’s pure strength of will has allowed him to possess the bodies of mortal Russian soldiers. He has engaged in every conflict Russia has been involved in since his death, fighting bravely and without fear of death. After all, he is already dead and it is the duty of a soldier to lay down his life for his country.

                                                        Rodina Mat – Scion of Mokosh (Guide 3)
                                                        Daughter of the ancient Slavic Earth Goddess, Rodina Mat (called Mother Russia in English) has abandoned her mortal name and assumed the role of patron for all the Russian people. She encourages fertility of the land, health of the people and distributes fortune and wealth where appropriate. Like other nationalistic figures, she will offer her aid to those who act in the best interests of the nation she favors. Unlike Columbia or Britannia, Rodina Mat is too young to have become a Goddess; she is still a Demigoddess, though one of age and power.

                                                        Caligula – Titan-Sworn Scion
                                                        A tyrant during his rule, Caligula was sadistic and capricious. He outraged his father, Jupiter and was cut off from contact with his divine family. Spitefully, the disgraced Scion turned to the Titans and pledged himself to their service. His assassination was faked to remove him from the notice of the Roman Gods. In the pits of Tartarus, he gained dark power and now serves the Titans’ purposes by taking on the role of a Guide to young Greek and Roman Scions, leading them slowly down the path of corruption he himself followed centuries ago.

                                                        Quirinus – Minor God (Guide 5)
                                                        Originally a Scion of Mars under the name Remus, the founder of Rome reached Godhood after establishing the foundations of an Empire that would shape the history of the World. He became a figure of civil order and martial prowess. He still lives on Olympus with the other Roman Gods, occasionally mentoring Scions and watching over the descendants of the people he lead to greatness.
