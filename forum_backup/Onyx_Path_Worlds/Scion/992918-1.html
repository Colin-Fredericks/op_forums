                I agree. I don't know why I did this. I never wrote anything before, it's super short, but I guess I just wanted the ball rolling.



                Umber tapped the table, her nails made a steady staccato in the otherwise empty house. Impatience wasn't her trouble so much as lack of sleep. Between the divorce, homeschooling, and work, Umber never achieved more than a few hours of rest. Movement, even the tiny clicks against the plastic table, kept her from drifing off.

                Nothing in her life was negotiable. Bills needed payment, and her children, Yousef especially, needed a good education. Others didn't understand her son was special. Not "special" the word in quotes like it was some joke everyone needed to make. Or even special in the sense everyone believed to be a mother's blind devotion. They were wrong. Umber's daughters were special too, a sly intelligence glinted behind her eldest's eyes, and the youngest possessed more spirit and hardiness than any child she ever knew, but her son was different. Much to Umber's chagrin, Yousef was Special.

                She wasn't able to accept it at first. Loving Jake and marrying Jake was never a choice, neither was divorcing Jake, but that wasn't the issue. She hoped to use their marriage as an escape from the Gods. Their omnipresent force on her life shaken loose so she and her children could live in peace.

                Three years ago a woman Umber had never met, but whose visage she remembered from years of instruction, came into her home. A Queen she always heard about but never met, walked across her tiled floor like it was a palace. Even Umber, always rebellious of the God's proper order, gave her deference even as she wept.

                "I knew it would be you." The goddess had said, smiling, wiping away Umber's tears as if Hathor truly was her mother, a part of her life all these years. "I knew you would raise him best."

                A short embrace, and then the pronouncement defining Umber's life for the rest of her days. "You are The Pharaoh's mother. You are his teacher, his guide, and his regent. Until he is old enough to stand on his own you are The Queen. Not a mother of mortals but Divinity. I trust you with my children. Teach them Well."

                Umber grunted, pinching the bridge of her nose, brought back to the present. This wasn't time for reminiscing, she rolled up the scroll she was studying and paused only a second to call out to the children upstairs playing their handheld games. "Kids! Bedtime! You need plenty of sleep tonight. Tomorrow, we're going on a field trip."

                        3Comrades
                        Okay, so we're getting somewhere.

                        See, I like that you're taking a mother as a main character here, someone for whom being a mother is important, but you're doing kind of a similar thing that Nicolas did, which is that even Hathor is erasing her identity in favor of her son.

                        There's a lot of interesting pathos around being the mother of the next Pharoah, absolutely, but this should be about her first, as a person and an entity, and her children second.
                        The desire of a mother to put her children first should struggle against that, not be erased by it.

                        Take a mother figure from a story that I wrote some time ago, Naomi Quinn, in a setting very like Scion. Naomi started off her journey attached to another Scion, more of a supporting member, but she was far more rational and self-possessed than they were. She took care of the team, despite not knowing that she was more than mortal, and was always the one thinking of supplies and other details that would keep them alive in difficult times. After the big adventure, rather than sink into irrelevance, she uses her influence to form an organization among mortals to help them, and becomes a (socially and politically) important figure. She finds a man and has children off him, never subordinating herself.

                        Despite the fact that she loves each of her children to distraction, she's ultimately a career woman first and struggles to maintain a work/life balance that's acceptable to herself and to her kids, several of whom feel that she never has time for them, especially because she had too many children too young.

                        When her Visitation finally, finally comes, it's not just to realize that she's a divine mother, but that it enhances what she's always done - mothering the world. Her divine powers give her the time to attend to each of her children individually and her company and all of the people that she mentors, advancing the causes she finds important to her.

                        Her being a divine mother enhances who she is, it doesn't subsume her.
