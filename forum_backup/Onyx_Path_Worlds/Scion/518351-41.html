    Originally posted by ArthurHansen View Post
    Well, as noted they totally gutted the Epic System to put in 'tier changes' that are supposed to put differences between a normal mortal and gods, etc. Weirdly, if you just left Epics but only let them go up to 5, you get an almost identical spread (0-12 vs. 0-11 autos). Oh, and the Tiers only apply "sometimes". So you can have a skilled mortal beat a god at their best skill. And the tiers apply (sometimes) to all attributes, even something you aren't necessarily known for but have decent stats.
    It's pushing more 'general hypercompetence' rather than the extremes of the old Epics. Are the specifics of the old Epic system important to you or is it more the ability to make a statement about your character through their hypercompetence in a chosen area? As in, would tweaking Epics be the only acceptable solution to you or would a new system that leads to similar results in a more elegant way be fine?

    Worth noting that skilled mortals do occasionally defeat gods/powerful creatures/etc. often in games of wits where a mortal comes up with a cunning solution

    Then they decided that Appearance is no longer an actual attribute, pushing New WoD's trinity type of balanced attributes (that you attack and defend with) for 'social combat'. While socials needed something of a fix, I'm not for totally re-doing attributes as the actual attribute of appearance was one of the most defining traits for a couple of my player's characters.
    I dislike Appearance as a stat and prefer the nWoD attribute spread as much more generally useful, so many times I've seen Appearance be relegated to near irrelevance. There are often (in fact almost always) other ways of representing the mechanical effects of how someone looks in game.

    Then in this latest explanation of how Relics work, Donar's gun 'calls storms', but that is all it can do. If he wanted a gentle rain or to shape clouds, he can't. Compared to the different types of actions you could take with the Sky purview previously from his relic, that's very much a limiting change. (Purview 'powers' could be totally unrelated from your Relics, but that isn't what I got from that discussion.)
    Are you concerned about Donar specifically? The way I understood it is that your character has a theme and the kinds of powers they get from a purview will fit their theme. So a 'Weather' purview combined with your PCs overall theme of violence and aggression means you'd not be using the gentle rains and breeze powers from 'Weather'.

    However. because your PCs decide on their themes if the players want to be able to do everything from summoning mighty storms and riding tsunami to sending a single drop of rain to make a statue appear to be crying with their 'Weather' purview then they can by deciding that their character's overall theme includes that.

    I.e.: the use of a purview is limited by your PCs themes. You choose your PCs themes and can make them as broad or specific as you like. Therefore your use of a purview is limited by your PCs deciding what their limits are.

    I could be wrong though.

                                                    Last thought before I go to work: Scale is taken directly from “White Wolf Comics presents the Cabal” in Mage Chronicler's Guide, where everything is handled either through Impact (which grants from one to sixteen successes) or through SFX (which multiplies successes by a factor ranging from two to a hundred or more). That's something I'm hoping will change during development and playtesting: I'm hoping that the Impact will scale more or less linearly (a big problem with Scion 1e was that eventually a difference of a single dot of Epic Attribute was essentially an “I win” button), and that instead of multiplying successes, SFX will be built into what a success does for you: someone with low-Scale Strength should spend a success to pick up a barrel, while someone with moderate-Scale Strength should be spending a success to pick up a pickup, and someone with top-Scale Strength should be spending a success to lift a building or larger object. That is, the higher a trait's Scale, the more each Success means.
