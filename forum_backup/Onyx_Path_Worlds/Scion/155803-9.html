                    Hey, what's the latest on Heku, folks? I'm really interested in seeing what you all come up with, especially for a Boon such as JSR's 5-dot Spread Ren.

                    We haven't done much with the Egyptian PsP yet, we are still working on Me, and the as of yet unnamed Russian PsP, but we just quickly talked about a few different possibilities for the Egyptian Pantheon Specific Purview.

                    The three main ideas that Iry came up with off the top of his head, being the one of us which knows the most about the Egyptians, is using the Passage of Rulership, the many-parted soul, or possibly some of their ideas of reincarnation. Now, those are very much brief ideas, and we may not use a single one of them when we do reach the Egyptians to rewrite them, but those are likely the main possibilities.

                    Currently, our ideas for Me are rather standard for the concepts of Me, though our focus is likely going to be more on the Me being a lot like a "Divine Permission Slip" or authority from the Great Gods of the Anunna to use some special powers. We have been sidetracked by this into possibly redesigning Virtues, and using this to work Order into Me. But, we may not do so.

                    The unnamed Russian PsP is likely going to be based around their worship. Some parts of the idea of taking themes from other cultures, but more of focusing on the innate parts of Bogovi cults inside the cannon of their mythology. Such as having your own priest, and having the much desired horse. Most of this PsP will also technically be violations of the Bogovi's Divine Laws, but a large theme in the Bogovi's myths is the presence of these rules, and the Bogovi constantly doing their best to get around them.

                    We have also been working with Griffinguy to revise the PsP of the Aztecs to attempt to give it more of a unique feel, and also avoid being entirely un-Aztec (cutting people's body parts off, and Familial Sacrifice both apparently are totally incorrect). We hope to actually avoid the Scion directly gaining power from making sacrifices until very late Demigod or after their Apotheosis, since all of the power needs to go to the Gods for their eternal battle. The powers will likely benefit the Scion in other ways, such as regenerating Virtue Channels, and other such things.

                    Thunder (renamed Storms) and Sky are on the backburner slightly, as we are focusing on the PsPs for the moment. But, currently, our main obstacle is trying to find a way to make creating rain as important in-game as it is for the ancient cultures that would pray for rain and the such. There need to be powers to bring rain, and they need to be quite important, but how to make them mechanically attractive for players is being difficult.

                    If anyone has any suggestions, we would, as always love to hear them. Be they about possible Egyptian PsPs, ideas for Me, how the Bogovi could function in violation to their own laws, and so on and so forth.

                                The metrical Dinnseanchas volume that you have contains essentially all of the Dinnseanchas written in Old/Middle Gaelic that is still extant. They would have been standard knowledge for bards, part of their job being able to entertain/educate the nobility by answering questions relating to how towns, fields, rivers, e.t.c. got their name. There is also an element of snobbery in this as the Bards themselves spoke Classical Gaelic*, but would recite the Dinnseanchas material in the Middle Gaelic it was written in, with the result that others often didn't understand the Bards.

                                Outside of this, there is far more Dinnseanchas in modern Irish (the collections I possess come to about ~1,000 A4 pages in 12pt print), gathered from people from all over the country from around 1750 to the modern day. I have checked through these to see what image of the Tuatha Dé Danann they give. It is essentially the same as the saga literature that you have read. Ogma appears several times as the explanation of how a stone arrived in a certain area (he threw it) and is a general strong man in local folktales. The other gods do not feature much in modern Dinnseanchas, so my conclusion is that the materials you have at hand are not only an accurate representation of the gods, but essentially the only representation of the gods. That is:

                                (a) The metrical Dinnseanchas
                                (b) The book of Invasions
                                (c) The Táin
                                (d) Minor texts that you can double check here to see if you've read them: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythological_Cycle (some of these texts repeat material from (a), (b), (c) or are just minor stories in which the gods make guest appearances)

                                Other Dinnseanchas barely mentions them and they don't appear in folklore. Growing up for example, I would have only heard the Daghda mentioned in the common phrase "an daghda!" meaning "indeed, really!" or when I was told the Táin.

                                *Also known as Early Modern Gaelic, the difference between it and Modern Irish is something like that between Modern English and Shakespeare, where as Middle and Old Gaelic are like Chaucer and Beowulf respectively.
