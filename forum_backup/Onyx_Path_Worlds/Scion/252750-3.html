                                            From my perspective, what RichT is asking is not for people to be unable to express their ideas and disagreement with others ideas, but that if you choose to do so, do so by providing constructive criticism rather than straight on aggression.

                                            Taking the fictional quote "Zeus is the son of Thor and Hera" as an example, I could break this down into 4 general methods of reply that each produce a vastly different feel.

                                            1) Positive Specific: "Although I know what you suggest is not how the actual mythology is written, having Zeus as their son opens up a lot of interesting options especially if Loki is now his step-brother."

                                            2) Positive General: "Your idea seems pretty cool."

                                            3) Negative Specific: "I find that your proposed option of having Zeus as the son of Thor and Hera is a bit strange. Part of the whole charm of Zeus is having him be the father of the Olympian gods rather than mixed in with the rest."

                                            4) Negative General: "Your idea sucks"

                                            If we look at these different examples, the first 3 can contribute to a fully valid discussion even if the negative specific doesn't come across as rainbows and sunshine. The difference between Negative Specific and Negative General, is the Negative Specific is challenging the idea and following it up with a logical argument while the Negative General statement is a direct attack on the poster rather than the idea.

                                            In summary, what RichT is asking is to play nice and challenge ideas as they are posted in a constructive manner rather than attack the poster itself. With Scion especially, arguments can be extremely heated and once a thread has devolved into direct attacks against a person, there is no longer any meaningful discussion to be had.
