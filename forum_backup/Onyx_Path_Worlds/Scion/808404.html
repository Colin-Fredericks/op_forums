    that time someone said or did something racist or offensive in your Scion game

    01-13-2016, 11:00 PM
    One cool thing about Scion is its wide-ranging representation of world religion and culture. One problem with Scion is that its wide-ranging representation of world religion and culture sometimes sets players up to say or do racially or religiously insensitive things. Has there ever been a moment in your Scion game where you or another player said or did such a thing? How did it come about, and what happened?

    As we go forward with Second Edition, we'd like to give players tools to prevent those mistakes from happening, or—more importantly—to respond kindly, thoughtfully, and productively when they crop up. To be clear, if you think racism and religious intolerance in your group or in Scion in general aren't a problem, that's great! But this thread is for talking about the exceptions.

    Mendez

                                  Not off topic, that's a great question. It's definitely part of that necessary social contract, but not everyone—new players and groups especially—knows about social contracts inherent in gaming. I was thinking about some text pointing out common problems that come up when you're role-playing in this kind of cultural territory and examples of constructive ways to respond when you or someone else makes a mistake, the kind of discussion we're having here. We learn lots of social skills in the metagame space; sportsmanship is a really good example. When I learned to wrestle as a kid, treating my opponent fairly and grace in victory and defeat weren't inherent to the mechanics of wrestling (although traditions such as the handshake do point towards sportsmanship), but our coaches taught us verbally and through demonstration how to operate in the social space surrounding the game. Text isn't as good as a living, seasoned coach, but it's way better than nothing, and can introduce topics which an inexperienced player might think to ask a friend about or research on their own, sometimes even presenting them in stronger language than we might feel comfortable using when we speak to friends or acquaintances.

                                  When I or someone I'm talking with says something racist in real life, I have some social mechanics that I rely on to respond. "Not cool man" is concise and effective when it works, but it's sometimes not enough if, for example, I say something racist in good faith but don't realize how or why the thing I said is hurtful. Jay Smooth's video about how to tell someone they sound racist was really helpful to me here. More RPG-specific tools are John Stavropoulos's X Card, or the Cut and Brake keywords used in LARP.

                                  We can't eradicate racism or other injurious behavior at the table, but we can help by suggesting productive ways to talk about the subject.
