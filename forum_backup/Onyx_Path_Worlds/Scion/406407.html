                  I wrote up the jinn as an NPC race (and used some characters in my Scion High campaign), but they didn't have the far-reaching wish-granting power of the Infernal Exalted Charm tree. Instead, I wrote up a special Purview of Transformation; mostly just transforming/creating inanimate matter, but able to transform/create living creatures at the higher-rated Boons). As a jinn's rating increased in the Purview, they could transform larger masses, with a wider range of options.

                  A lot depends on how far-reaching you want the wish-granting power to be. I thought that making and transforming matter could do for most of the classic jinn stories. (Even Aladdin can't just wish to be a prince. He can, however, wish for the jinni to grant him all the trappings of a prince -- up to and including his own palace.) Some stories go further, especially when the point of the story is to show how the jinni screws over the protagonist by following the word of the wish while creatively interpreting the intent.

                  I can post my version of the jinn, if you think it would be useful.

                          First off, I misremembered: I intended to write a Transformation Purview, but never got around to it. (Sorry, I wrote this years ago.) The jinn only have ad-hoc transformation powers. The write-ups might be a useful first step in writing such a Purview, though.

                          In case it isn't clear, the numbers in angle brackets are header codes.

                          This writeup contains a few of my own ideas about Scion cosmology that are not official, and probably never will be. So, caveat lector.

                          But enough hedging; on with the jinn!

                          <2>JINN (Part One)

                          These lesser immortals have no connections to any specific pantheon. They form a supernatural nation of their own — in fact, several nations with commoners, an aristocracy and rival kings. Many jinn are evil and follow Iblis, an avatar of the sky-titan Ehekatoyaatl. Other jinn converted to Judaism, Christianity or Islam. Very few of them worship the Gods of Legend, despite frequent contact with these beings; or rather, because of that contact. As with mortals, most jinn believe there must be something greater than themselves. Long ago, the jinn decided the all-too-human Gods were not that “something greater.”

                          Middle Eastern mythology says that whereas God shaped humans from clay, he made the jinn from a searing wind or from black, smokeless fire. Jinn who follow Abrahamic faiths accept this myth of their origin. On the other hand, the followers of Iblis claim the jinn are the whirlwind-demons that the Babylonian Epic of Creation lists among the chaos-Titan Tiamat’s army of monsters. Whichever account is true (if either), the jinn are moral free agents, responsible for their actions in ways that titanspawn often are not. Jewish, Christian and Muslim jinn follow divine Virtues. Iblisite jinni follow Dark Virtues.

                          As with Gods, a jinni has a body of ichor but can dematerialize completely. In this immaterial state, jinn may dwell in the atmospheric heights, the Earth’s surface, underwater or underground, at their whim. The surface-dwellers favor abandoned buildings, wells, ruins, tombs, deserts and desolate places for their homes. Some jinn prefer river banks, gardens or the foot of great trees. A few Legend-touched mortals — unusually perceptive children, the holy, the mad and the dying — can see immaterial jinn. Middle Eastern tradition gives other names for jinn, such as jann, ifrits and marids, but an ifrit is merely a powerful jinni, and a marid (meaning “contumacious” in Arabic) is merely a hostile jinni. They are all one race.

                          Many jinn choose to live in the World, in a territory stretching from Morocco to Central Asia. This race also holds a few Terrae Incognita. Most notably, Iblis built his capital on Mount Qaf, an otherworldly peak or mountain range in the Middle East. Otherworldly palaces also lurk at the bottom of desert pits full of fluid, quicksand-like dust. On the other hand, Istanbul folk say the king of the sea-jinn has a palace in the Bosphorus near Leander’s Tower.

                          Jinn can manifest in various ways. They can gather whirlwinds about their immaterial forms. They also materialize as animals, humans or huge ogre-like creatures. Jinn can be male or female: jinni or jinniyah, ifrit or ifritah. The ogre-form is hideous, with tusks and horns, but human forms are often notably attractive. Jinn have children, grow old and die, though they live for thousands of years.

                          These lesser immortals can have many other powers too, from inflicting disease to possessing poets, but are notable for vast strength, rapid movement around the World, and creating and transforming matter. Strictly speaking, even the mightiest jinn cannot grant a mortal’s every wish the way some stories portray them; but noble jinn may wield so many powers that they can perform almost any task a mortal can imagine.

                          Only the mightiest magic can compel a jinni, but that spell was cast long ago. According to legend, King Solomon bound the entire race of jinn in the name of God. Henceforth, jinn must fulfill any pledges that they make in the name of Suleyman bin Da’ud. The oath to Solomon binds even the Iblisite jinn.

                          In addition to the Iblisites, the jinn have several other nations with kings of their own. The great Masitash, Caliph of the Muslim jinn, has 300,000 vassals, each of them a lord of other jinn. Muhammed himself converted Masitash and the kings Abdur-Rahman and Abdul-Kadir. Malik Afshan rules the Peris, the righteous jinn of Persia. Masidus rules Christian jinn (Orthodox: They don’t hold with Roman Catholicism or newfangled Protestant doctrines); Tututash rules Jewish jinn. The jinn monarchs have their differences, but the Abrahamic kings all oppose Iblis.

                          [BEGIN SIDEBAR]
                          <3>Jinn and Religion

                          The Bible says that about 4,000 years ago, a Mesopotamian man named Abram made a covenant with a God. In token of this, Abram added an ‘h’ in his name. From this covenant grew a religious tradition that half the World now follows.

                          However, the God of Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Baha’i, Mormonism et al., no longer bears much resemblance to the God of Abraham. Yahweh was quite a human God: He appreciated sacrifices, spoke to Moses face-to-face, walked in gardens in the cool of the evening and was prone to fits of temper and jealousy. He may have claimed to be omnipotent, omniscient and omnipresent — and the only true God — but the early stories don’t bear that out.

                          The world of Scion includes a number of plausible claimants to Abraham’s God. The tales in the Pentateuch accord well with the myths of the Mesopotamian God Enlil. The Titan Aten explicitly claims to be the omnipotent One True God: He assumes the classic form of an old man with a long beard, shapes his servants as angels, and brainwashes captives to believe his claim. Aten is a big fat liar. The Jewish, Christian and Muslim jinn do not worship either Enlil or Aten.

                          Rather, the jinn believe that Fate is the manifest power of a true Supreme Being. Fate transcends time and space; it binds mortals, Gods and Titans; it often seems to show a will of its own, but works in mysterious ways. Jinn theologians concede that stories about God might have begun with Enlil, Aten and other entities… but these entities acted as unwitting proxies for the Almighty, compelled by power they could not comprehend.

                          The jinn cannot prove this claim. On the other hand, no one can prove they are wrong. Many Gods find the argument disturbing, and don’t want to think about it.
                          [END SIDEBAR]

                          <2>Minor Jinn (Guide •••)

                          The least among the jinn are children of their kind, or are beggars and slaves whom other jinn treat with scorn. They are not especially powerful combatants, but wield diverse powers; minor jinn can become useful allies for Scions.

                          Attributes: Strength 3, Dexterity 2, Stamina 4; Charisma 3, Manipulation 2, Appearance 2; Perception 2, Intelligence 2, Wits 2
                          Virtues: Endurance 2, Expression 1, Piety 3, Vengeance 3 or Ambition 1, Malice 3, Rapacity 1, Zealotry 3
                          Abilities: Animal Ken 2, Art (Poetry) 1, Athletics 3, Awareness 1, Brawl 3, Craft (all) 1, Investigation 1, Larceny 2, Melee 3, Occult 2, Politics 1, Presence 3, Survival 2

                          Supernatural Powers:
                          Epic Attributes: Epic Appearance 1 (Serpent’s Gaze), Epic Stamina 1 (Inner Furnace), Epic Strength 1 (Holy Rampage). Every has one dot of Epic Appearance — positive in human form, negative in ogre form — with a “reversible” Knack such as Serpent’s Gaze or Lasting Impression. A jinni also has Epic Stamina equal to half his Legend, rounded up, with at least one Knack per dot. A jinni has one dot of Epic Strength in human form, but (Legend – 1) dots in ogre form, with Knacks.
                          Immaterial Spirit: No physical force can affect an immaterial jinn. While immaterial, a jinni can move through solid matter at its normal Move or Dash speed. Going all-out, minor jinn fly at up to 50 miles per hour. To those who can see the immaterial, a minor jinni looks like a burly human with skin colored dark red, blue, green or black, possibly pied or spotted with white or other colors. They have short tusks and clawlike nails, cloven hooves for feet, triangular eyes and long, unkempt hair. Horns are optional. Some jinn have batlike or birdlike wings, but all jinn can fly equally well. An immaterial minor jinni can be any size from one inch to 10 feet tall. Immaterial jinn change size as a reflexive action, and this does not affect their traits or powers in any way. On the other hand, immaterial jinn cannot affect the material world except as specified below, and they cannot assume other forms.
                          Intrinsic Boons: In every form, a jinni gains the benefits of Night Eyes (Darkness •), Safely Interred (Earth •), Fire Immunity (Fire •) and Water Breathing (Water •), with no need for relics or Legend expenditure.
                          Materialize: A minor jinni can materialize in the form of animals ranging in size from a house cat to a donkey. Each jinni knows a number of animal forms equal to his Legend. In such forms, a jinn has the normal Attributes of the animal (see Scion: Hero for examples). Favored animal forms include black or white cats, spotted dogs, snakes, donkeys, and carrion birds such as vultures. A materialized jinni can talk in whatever form he assumes. A minor jinni’s repertoire of forms might include a human form, but this is rare and usually results from an unusual connection to a mortal, such as a jinni born at the same time and place as a mortal, and so mystically becoming that mortal’s twin sibling. Materializing or changing material form is a miscellaneous action that costs one point of Legend. Dematerializing does not cost Legend.
                          Transformation: A materialized minor jinni can transform any inanimate matter into almost any other inanimate matter. The jinni’s player rolls (Intelligence + Craft + Legend). Allocate the successes to determine the mass that the jinni affects, and the substance and complexity of the final product, as per the chart in the sidebar. The Legend cost equals the number of successes spent on the target’s mass. Minor jinn cannot manipulate more than 100 pounds of matter at a time. Jinn can create any object or substance that is generally available for sale: Thus, a jinni could create a rifle or a heap of diamonds, but not a nuclear bomb or a Moon rock.
                          Whirlwind: An immaterial minor jinni can evoke a whirlwind up to 2 yards wide and 5 yards high. The whirlwind is harmless, but in suitable surroundings it can kick up enough dust and debris to inflict a –2 die penalty on all sight Perception rolls for characters caught within it, or who try to see through it. Any ranged attack through the whirlwind also suffers a –2 die penalty, even if reduced visibility or air currents shouldn’t be an issue: The immaterial jinn can still bend a missile’s course. It’s just part of the magic of being a jinni. A jinni with a whirlwind can travel at its normal Move or Dash speed, but no greater velocity. Evoking such a minor whirlwind is a miscellaneous action that does not cost Legend. If the jinni wants, it can spend 4 Legend to evoke a mightier whirlwind that exerts an Epic Strength of 1, as per the Tornado Tamer Boon (Sky 7; see Scion: Demigod, pp. 87-88). Jinn, however, do not need a dice roll to control their whirlwinds.
                          Join Battle: 3
                          Attacks:
                          Clinch: Accuracy 5, Damage 4B, Parry DV —, Speed 6, Piercing
                          Unarmed, Heavy: Accuracy 4, Damage 7B, Parry DV 2, Speed 5
                          Unarmed, Light: Accuracy 6, Damage 4B, Parry DV 3, Speed 4
                          Bite: Accuracy 5, Damage 4L, Parry DV —, Speed 5
                          Scimitar: Accuracy 5, Damage 8L, Parry DV 4, Speed 5
                          Soak: 1L/3L/5B
                          Health Levels: -0x3/-2/-2/-4/Incap
                          Dodge DV: 4 Willpower: 6
                          Legend: 2 Legend Points: 4

                          Other Notes: To this basic lesser jinn, add 15 (or more) bonus points’ worth of other traits. All traits have the same bonus point cost as for Scions (see Scion: Hero, p. 101). Building from scratch, a minor jinn character has (6/4/3) Attributes, 5 points of Virtues and 25 points of Abilities. Treat Animal Ken, Art, Athletics, Craft, Occult and Presence as Favored Abilities. Epic Strength and Epic Stamina are associated powers, but minor jinn cannot learn Boons beyond those intrinsic to their being.

                          BEGIN SIDEBAR]
                          <3>Combat Transformation

                          Jinn can use transformation as an attack, such as transforming an enemy’s gun into gumballs or his clothes into acid or a rigid casing. Storytellers can assign each jinni a limited number of transformation tricks, of a power comparable to the highest-rated Boons the spirit’s Legend would permit. Even minor jinn, however, can make an opponent’s weapon disappear. (Relics, however, are immune to transformation.)

                          Transformation is a ranged attack with a range increment of (Legend x 10) yards. The attack roll is (Perception + Marksmanship); clever attacks can gain stunt bonus dice. Characters defend with their Dodge DV. Base damage is (Legend x 2) dice of Lethal damage. Extra successes may add to damage for some attacks, but this is not universal and definitely does not apply to basic transformation.
                          {END SIDEBAR]

                          Dean Shomshak

                              <2>JINN (Part Two)

                              <3>Standard Jinn (Guide ••••)

                              The average jinni or jinniyah is a potent supernatural creature with a wide range of powers. They make powerful allies, or dangerous foes. Jinn are often easier to outwit than to fight. In combat, a standard jinni typically materializes a weapon, either normal-sized (for human form) or giant-sized (for its fully expanded ogre form).

                              Attributes: Strength 4, Dexterity 3, Stamina 4; Charisma 4, Manipulation 3, Appearance 2; Perception 3, Intelligence 2, Wits 2
                              Virtues: Endurance 2, Expression 2, Piety 3, Vengeance 3 or Ambition 2, Malice 3, Rapacity 3, Zealotry 2
                              Abilities: Academics 2, Animal Ken 3, Art (Poetry) 2, Athletics 3, Awareness 3, Brawl 3, Craft (all) 2, Investigation 2, Larceny 3, Melee 3, Occult 3, Politics 2, Presence 3, Survival 2

                              Supernatural Powers:
                              Boons: Standard jinn can learn Boons from the Artifice, Chaos, Earth, Fertility, Fire, Health, Illusion, Psychopomp or Sky Purviews; each jinni can pick one Purview as associated. As with Demigods, standard jinn must own relics to channel the Boons of their associated Purview, but do not need to carry them — though the jinni’s effective Legend is reduced by 1 if he doesn’t carry his relic, which may limit the Boons he can wield. Jinn must carry relics to channel Boons from other Purviews. Few jinn know more than (Legend x 2) dots worth of Boons.
                              Epic Attributes: Epic Appearance 1 (Serpent’s Gaze), Epic Stamina 2 (Inner Furnace, Self-Healing), Epic Strength 3 (Holy Rampage, Knockback Attack, Uplifting Might).
                              Immaterial Spirit: As with minor jinn, but the standard jinn can range in size from an inch tall to (Legend x 10) feet tall. While immaterial, standard jinn can fly at 500 miles per hour.
                              Materialize: A standard jinni can assume forms that range in size from a mouse to a cow. In addition, a standard jinni can assume a single human form, outwardly indistinguishable from a mortal; jinn can emulate all the ethnic groups in the arid belt from Morocco to Central Asia. Finally, a standard jinni can take his ogre form, ranging in size from 10 feet tall to (Legend x 10) feet tall. In this form, the jinni can fly at 50 miles per hour.
                              Otherworld Sanctum: A jinni can create his own little Terra Incognita located wherever he chooses to dwell, immaterial and undetectable by mortal means, but that mortals may enter by accident or by the jinni’s will. Scions can locate a jinni’s sanctum using Terra Incognita (Psychopomp •••••) or spells such as Ariadne’s Thread or The Unlidded Eye. Unbarred Entry (Psychopomp •••) suffices to pass the sanctum’s immaterial entrance. A jinni’s sanctum typically extends (Legend x 100) yards square, or an equivalent area.
                              Transformation: A materialized jinni can transform any matter into any other matter. In addition to the capabilities of minor jinn, a standard jinni can create living creatures. These jinn can also transform living creatures, but can transform sapient creatures only if the target commands that it be done. A standard jinni can also reverse any transformation inflicted upon a sapient creature: In this case, it is enough for the person to be willing (for cases when a person cannot speak a command, such as a person turned into a dog or a statue). Standard jinn can affect up to 10,000 pounds at once.
                              Whirlwind: A standard jinni’s whirlwind can extend up to 4 yards wide and 10 yards high. The whirlwind can exert Epic Strength with an upper limit of half the jinn’s Legend (Epic Strength 2 for this standard jinn), costing 4 Legend points per dot of Epic Strength.

                              Join Battle: 5
                              Attacks:
                              Clinch: Accuracy 6, Damage 5B, Parry DV —, Speed 6, Piercing
                              Unarmed, Heavy: Accuracy 5, Damage 8B, Parry DV 2, Speed 5
                              Unarmed, Light: Accuracy 7, Damage 5B, Parry DV 4, Speed 4
                              Bite: Accuracy 6, Damage 5L, Parry DV —, Speed 5
                              Scimitar: Accuracy 6, Damage 9L, Parry DV 4, Speed 5
                              Giant Scimitar: Accuracy 8, Damage 13L, Parry DV 4, Speed 5
                              Soak: 2A/4L/6B
                              Health Levels: -0x5/-4/Incap
                              Dodge DV: 5 Willpower: 6
                              Legend: 4 Legend Points: 16

                              Other Notes: To this standard jinn, add 15 (or more) bonus points’ worth of other traits.

                              <3>Noble Jinn

                              Jinn aristocrats carry titles such as Shaykh, Bey, Dey, Amir or Malik, with no particular connection between title and actual rank. These lesser immortals wield tremendous personal power, and may rule a cohort of standard and minor jinn. Each noble jinni or jinniyah surely owns a splendid palace hidden immaterially within a ruined tomb, at the bottom of a well or high in the clouds. Diverse and formidable powers make a noble jinni the equal of any demigod. Jinn monarchs may have Legends as high as 8: They are significant and sovereign players in the supernatural World.

                              Attributes: Strength 6, Dexterity 3, Stamina 6; Charisma 5, Manipulation 3, Appearance 3; Perception 3, Intelligence 3, Wits 2
                              Virtues: Endurance 4, Expression 3, Piety 2, Vengeance 3 or Ambition 4, Malice 3, Rapacity 2, Zealotry 3
                              Abilities: Academics 3, Animal Ken 3, Art (Poetry) 3, Athletics 3, Awareness 3, Brawl 4, Command 2, Craft (all) 3, Investigation 3, Larceny 3, Melee 4, Occult 4, Politics 4, Presence 5, Survival 3

                              Supernatural Powers:
                              Boons: A noble jinn typically knows (Legend x 3) dots’ worth of Boons, from the same Purviews as listed for standard jinn. As with Gods, noble jinn must own relics to channel Boons, but do not need to carry any of them, if they are willing to accept a reduction in their effective Legend.
                              Epic Attributes: Epic Appearance 1 (Serpent’s Gaze), Epic Stamina 3 (Holy Fortitude, Inner Furnace, Self-Healing), Epic Strength 5 (Holy Rampage, Knockback Attack, Uplifting Might, personal choice of two others)
                              Immaterial Spirit: As with standard jinn, ranging in size from an inch tall to (Legend x 10) feet tall. While immaterial, noble jinn can fly at 5,000 miles per hour.
                              Materialize: A noble jinn can select its animal forms from any natural animals between a flea and an elephant in size, plus a single human form and ogre form. In ogre form, the jinni can fly at 500 miles per hour.
                              Otherworld Sanctum: As for a standard jinni. Standard jinn can affix their sanctums to that of a noble jinni to build otherworldly palace-compounds, networks of treasure-filled caves, and similar wonders.
                              Transformation: A materialized noble jinni can transform any matter into any other matter. In addition to the capabilities of minor and standard jinn, a noble jinni can create intelligent creatures (though not more intelligent than the jinni himself, and not possessing any Abilities the jinni lacked). Noble jinn can affect up to 1 million pounds at once — enough to create a small skyscraper or hydroelectic dam, given time. But what the jinni can create, he can also destroy.
                              Whirlwind: A noble jinni’s whirlwind can extend up to 8 yards wide and 20 yards high, and is equal in strength to a tornado created by the Tornado Tamer Boon (see Scion: Demigod, p. 87).

                              Join Battle:
                              Attacks:
                              Clinch: Accuracy 7, Damage 7B, Parry DV —, Speed 6, Piercing
                              Unarmed, Heavy: Accuracy 6, Damage 10B, Parry DV 3, Speed 5
                              Unarmed, Light: Accuracy 8, Damage 7B, Parry DV 4, Speed 4
                              Bite: Accuracy 7, Damage 7L, Parry DV —, Speed 5
                              Scimitar: Accuracy 7, Damage 11L, Parry DV 5, Speed 5
                              Giant Scimitar: Accuracy 9, Damage 15L, Parry DV 5, Speed X
                              Soak: 3A/6L/10B
                              Health Levels: -0x6/Incap
                              Dodge DV: 6 Willpower: 7
                              Legend: 6 Legend Points: 36

                              Other Notes: To this noble jinn, add 15 (or more) bonus points’worth of traits.

                              [BEGIN SIDEBAR]
                              <3>Jinn Transformations
                              Successes Quality
                              1+ points Basic mass (1: 10 pounds; 2: 100 pounds; 3: 1,000 pounds; etc.)
                              +2 points Create object from thin air
                              +1 point Create edible substance
                              +2 points Create precious substances (gold, diamonds, money, etc.)
                              +1 point Create a moderately complex object (a gun, an article of clothing)
                              +2 points Create a complex object (a working radio, an oil painting)
                              +3 points Create a fiendishly complex object (a computer)
                              +3 points Create life
                              +5 points Create sapient life
                              Note: In cases when a jinni changes the size of a target, it doesn’t matter if the target becomes bigger or smaller. Allocate successes based on the larger of the target’s two sizes.
                              [END SIDEBAR]

                              <3>Miscellaneous Powers

                              Legends ascribe many other powers to the jinn, which don’t fit exactly among the standard Knacks or Boons. Here are some examples. Any standard or noble jinni might possess a few of them.

                              Cleave the Earth: Subterranean jinn don’t need to open a chasm in the ground in order to reach the surface; but they may do so anyway just to make a dramatic entrance. This is comparable to a four-dot Earth Boon. For 3 Legend, the jinn opens a crack in any stone or stone-like substance, up to (Legend) yards long, (Legend/3) yards wide and (Legend x 2) yards deep. The jinn may do so while materializing, so as to rise out of the crack it creates.

                              Forced Slumber: Some jinn can force a person to remain asleep despite any attempts to wake the victim. While this resembles Cradlesong (Health ••••), it only affects people who are already asleep and there is no mundane way to awaken the sleeper. On the other hand, the jinni can release the sleeper to awaken at any time. The jinni must materialize to use this power, and the Forced Slumber ends if the jinni leaves the sleeper’s presence. Each target of this power costs the jinni a Legend point.

                              Inflict Disease: As Heal/Infect (Health •••), but using a dice pool of (Stamina + Occult). The jinni can so afflict mortals without materializing, but must become tangible to affect Scions or other creatures of Legend. Costs one Legend.

                              Inspire Poets: An immaterial jinni can confer moments of genius on a mortal poet. The poet must be willing, and Middle Eastern tradition tells of various ways by which a poet can seek a jinni partner and muse. The jinni spends a Legend point, and the poet adds the jinni’s Art (Poetry) and Presence ratings to her own for the rest of the scene — augmenting both the poet’s compositional talents and her performance of what was once an entirely verbal art.

                              Paralyze Limb: Some jinn inflict paralysis or twisted limbs upon mortals who offend them. This power costs 3 Legend, with a roll of (Stamina + [Medicine or Occult]), at a difficulty of the target’s Legend. Success means the crippling of one of the victim’s limbs. A crippled leg inflicts a –3 dice penalty on footwork-based Athletics checks, and reduces movement rates by half. A crippled arm cannot be used in combat, makes two-handed tasks impossible, and imposes a –1 die penalty on most vigorous activity (including combat) from inability to use the crippled arm to balance the body’s motions. Such crippling effects are permanent on mortals (barring supernatural intervention), but Scions heal the paralysis as if it were a single level of aggravated damage. Oddly, jinn with this power cannot completely paralyze a victim: At most, they can inflict hemiplagia (paralyzing the right or left half of the victim’s body). Any jinn with Paralyze Limb can also cure the paralysis inflicted by any other jinn. All jinn must materialize to use this power.

                              Possession: An immaterial jinni can occupy a living person’s body and use it as his own. The attempt costs the jinni 3 Legend and 1 Willpower, while the jinni’s player must win a contested roll of (Charisma + Command) against the defender’s (Willpower + Integrity + Legend): Since Epic Charisma and Command are not among the traits favored by most jinn, Possession is a fairly uncommon power. A jinni who manages to possess a mortal can do whatever he wants with the victim’s body, using all the mortal’s Attributes and Abilities. A jinni who possesses a Scion can employ all the character’s Epic Attributes, Knacks and Boons as well. However, a possessing jinni loses the use of his own supernatural powers until he releases his victim. The jinni can stay in his victim’s body at a cost of 1 Legend per day, plus another contested roll; if the jinni places his host in mortal peril, the jinni must spend another Legend point, and the players of the jinni and the host engage in another contested roll. If the jinni’s player ever fails the contest roll, the jinni is expelled from the victim’s body. At such times of crisis, both sides may spend Willpower for a bonus success or to channel a Virtue.

                              Summon Other Jinn: Standard jinn can call minor jinn who are their personal slaves, and the minor jinn must obey and appear at once before their master. Noble jinn can summon standard or minor jinn who are under their authority. Nobles can also call other nobles of lower Legend, but fellow nobles are under no obligation to respond. Minor jinn cost 1 Legend point each to summon; standard jinn cost 2 Legend each; and nobles cost 3 Legend to call, even if the other noble refuses to respond.

                              Dean Shomshak
