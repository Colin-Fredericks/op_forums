        Surprisingly, Scion nationalists overthrowing nations to bring back the "old ways" isn't the massive, constant problem you would expect for a setting about super powered embodiments of ancient cultures running around. This is partially due to the syncretic, ever changing and omnipresent nature of the gods. Most pantheons change and adapt with their people, so regressive and traditionalist ideas about nation-states are about the same as our world. A threat, but not an all encompassing one, Not to mention history is better taught and remembered because of the existence of immortal beings who actually remember that the good old days weren't always so good.

        However many mortals and their governments are very aware of the possibility of being overthrown by the Scions of a "dead pantheon." A daughter of Al'Uzza not happy with Saudi Arabia's treatment of women could overthrow things overnight, An insane Incarnation of Taranis might declare war against the children of Divus Julius and Italy by proxy. Even an active Pantheon like the Kami might have a rouge son of Amaterasu who becomes a wanna be Yukio Mishima and try to bring back the Imperial Cult in Japan. It's unlikely and hasn't happened in the modern era but it's a fear mortals can't help but have.

                            The International Games (formerly Olympics) are a nightmare. Only 100% human mortals are allowed to compete, and no one is allowed to directly attack contestants. These rules are followed (mostly... recently... we think), but Gods tend to see the rest (DO NOT INTERFERE being at the top of the list) as guidelines when it pertains to their favorites. Several Scions observe from the sidelines, with the thought that one of them will report any blatant interference, but it still happens. To minimize temptation, there are very few multi-day events, and everything is a little shorter in the World. Still, underdogs win with surprising frequency, simply because no one is working against them. Every few decades, a pantheon (esp. the Theoi) petitions to use their name for the games. Fairly frequently, it goes through. Of course, when the 'home name advantage' inevitably backfires and leads to embarrassing losses, the sponsors don't argue against IG for the next several rounds.

                            Tired of all this nonsense, a group of Scions is working on a special God Channel with a significant delay, but the logistics and sales pitch are proving about as difficult as expected.

                                                            Just numbering these because, you know, they're mine and I have more than one of them. XD

                                                            1) the Modern Age has seen amazing breakthroughs not just in the sciences, though fields like Quantum Physics, but also in Pan-Theology, where noted scholars have been putting together more and more advanced understandings of how the mythological underpinnings of reality come together and create The World. That said, while there have been great strides forward in this field, these have been hampered by the fact that so many of it's foremost minds end up a little... unhinged... by their attempts to peer into the abyss between different mythologies. It would appear that humanity just isn't supposed to know how the mythological underpinnings of reality really function.

                                                            2) Local myths and legends abound in every part of modern society, and even though the average modern person hasn't had any direct experience with such things most people trade around stories about numerous topics. A small handful of Denizens that live 'nearby', how a small branch of their family migrated to some Terra Incognita a few generations back, or just an oft-repeated tale about some "seven degrees of Mythology" apocryphal tale. The most common of these, buried in the family trees of a thousand different families, is some vague association with one deity or even multiple different gods that lurks almost forgotten in their ancestry.

                                                            3) It's rare to see the mass media dealing with the doings of Heroes and Titanspawn, excepting only the largest and most blatantly obvious acts. Even then, mostly these issues get regional attention at best, showing up on local news outlets as small mentions or the occasional fully-developed story. It's in these gaps that godbloggers tend to find their niche, covering these issues in more detail at some cost of accuracy, but every so often a local story will get picked up for wider distribution-- becoming a national or even worldwide thing. When this happens, most Scions 'in the know' become profoundly glad that it's not a more common occurrence, as this is pretty much always a sign that things are about to get much worse.
