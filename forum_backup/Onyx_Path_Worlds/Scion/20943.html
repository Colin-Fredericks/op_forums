    Scions and Titan Spawn - What's the Difference?

    11-30-2013, 10:45 PM
    So for someone who hasn't got any of the books, but is interested in the game mechanics, is it possible to have a Scion who's parent was a titan?


    I ask, because I was thinking of making a Cthulhu Scion, but according to the website I looked up, The Great Old Ones are classified as Titans (yet calls them gods, oddly enough).


    I was thinking it might be possible if a Titan Spawn / Scion decided to switch sides and work for the other team if they might be considered one or the other. I've already read a couple notes around here saying how some gods choose to 'adopt' scions, rather than actually sire them, and wondered if that could apply to children of the opposing factions.

      Well, the children of titans are Titanspawn and the children of deities are Scions. It is possible for the Titanspawn to switch sides and work with the gods. I have an NPC who is like that. He is a rakshasa who was adopted by Vishnu. The same can be said of the reverse with a Greater Titan taking in a Scion since all it takes is a deity to renounce his ties to the Scion.

      The only real debate is if your gaming group allows Cthulhu as a Titan or as a deity. If he/she/it is a deity, then you can have a Scion. If they are Titans, then you would be playing a Titanspawn that could be adopted by a god to help in the war.

              There are no three stages of advancement for Titanspawn. Not unless you want them to be for your creation. Titanspawn just show up with their power. They start (and end) with the power to turn people to stone or to burst into flame or to cause a brain aneurysm by smacking their chewing gum. They do not usually gain power.

              Again, this is the general principle but you can change it as suits your whim as an ST or else by working with your ST to come up with something homebrewed.

                  A Titan can be the patron of an acknowledged Scion. That's usually when they go evil. A titan spawn adopted by a god can work but often has complications. It was also posited in Scion: God that Titan avatars can produce dark scions. They don't go into too much detail though. The big issue would be the virtues however. Either they would have titan virtues or they would have the virtues of the pantheon that the titan avatar is most closely associated with. It also depends on how closely they are related to mortals and what the titan wants them for. The Shinobi for instance serve the Titan of Darkness but have Amatsu-kami virtues.

                      The sidebars you are looking at in God on pg 206 and 209 do indeed mention titanspawn scions or Dark scions. It presents them as an optional ploy by the titans to create servitors that are as powerful and mobile as those of the gods. The only details you get are that they; A) don't get birthrights (instead using titanspawn trophies as relics, with nothing about guides or followers mentioned), B) get slapped with some unusual twist (which I interpreted as physical mutation or quirk) whenever they learn boons, and C) can upgrade their legend whenever they want by replacing a previous virtue with a dark virtue. It can also be extrapolated that they D) have no PSP.

                      I actually played one in a home game, the character ended up working for his titan, but since that titan was one of the handful of indifferent/neutral ones he was allowed to cooperate with godly scions against that titan's rivals/enemies/annoyances.

                      Another detail they never get into is what a scion of an allied titan would be like. Examples of allied titans: Helios, Thanatos, Hypnos (who apparently crashes in Hades's underworld), Rhea (Zeus's mom), Chronos, Oceanus (whose daughter married Poseidon). These are titans who never got slapped into tarterus because they aren't titan-avatars, but they ARE titans.

                                          Originally posted by Man in Black View Post
                                          Where is this? I'm interested in reading more of this.

                                          The only Greater Titans that weren't imprisoned were Logos, Greater Titan of Order (who sided with the Gods on the Titan War), Hundun, Greater Titan of Chaos (who doesn't have Avatars to imprison) and Vritra, Greater Titan of Drought (who was deeply wounded and regenerated deep on the Earth, don't ask, I also don't quite understand it).

                                          So note that all Gods are the decedents of the Greater Titans or from their Avatars, so there is no problem with the daughter of Oceanus marrying Poseidon. I also was under the impression that Helios was killed and absorbed by Aten, but I might be mistaken.
                                          Logos, Hundun, and Vritra aren't the sole exceptions, they're examples. It is dismissively written in God that one or two overtitans were not imprisoned, but there clearly must be a lot more than that. It's not explicitly detailed anywhere, I just saw this as a glaring inconsistency left over from their description of titans and overtitans, as well as leaving out more obvious players of actual myth. I mean look at these holes;

                                          -Apollo doesn't drive the sun chariot full-time, Helios does, and Helios is a titan. They make vague mention that Aten might have diablerized (for lack of a better word) Hyperion, but Helios has been working for Zeus ever since the 1st titanomachy driving the sun chariot. He even had a scion after he started work called Phaeton in actual myth.
                                          -Oceanus is the original greek titan of the endless ocean, and the husband of Tethys, why would he NOT be in the drowned road, and where else would he go if he's not listed as an avatar? Nobody ever killed him in the actual myths, and they certainly never mention such happening in God, so he just retired or something and went away...but to WHERE?
                                          -Artemis is associated with the moon yes, but Selene IS the moon, and she isn't even mentioned even though she too is a benign titan (of Hyperion in fact) that lots of people actually know of. She had some affair with a human guy called Endymion whom she sent to sleep forever so he'd never get old and she could always have him.
                                          -I mentioned Rhea, who would logicaly appear in the titanrealm of Terra, as she too was a earth/fertility being, but she's nowhere to be found. And she sheltered Zeus from his father Kronos, I can't see her being thrown into tarterus either, when she was instrumental to the rise of the dodecatheon as we know it. The greeks may act like dicks but (like the Lanisters) they always pay their debts.
                                          -Zurvan's sole avatar is Ahriman (who actually has more in common with Darkness and Chaos than Time), but Chronos has ALWAYS been the controller of time. The universe begins in greek myth with Chronos and Ananke, but he is never mentioned EVER. If Ahriman is the sole avatar of Zurvan, when did he dispose of Chronos or where did Chronos go?
                                          -They punked Erebus when they slapped Mikaboshi (actually a star-god) into the realm of darkness, but he and Nyx were major powers on the level of Chronos, Ananke, Gaia, and Oranus eons before Kronos and that generation of titans were ever born. His children by Nyx are all the death, sleep, and light titans believe it or not, NONE of which are mentioned anywhere else in the material unless you go delving into the french supplements we didn't get. Then you hear mention of Morpheus (a god) child of Hypnos (a titan) when you read about the Key of Morpheus.

                                          What I deduced from all this that A) you can be a titan and NOT an avatar, and still not be a god; or B) more than just Logos sided with the gods, Hypnos (sleep), and Thanatos (death), must have done so as well; or C) an allied titan-avatar can go into some kind of refugee status and leave the titanrealm, escaping its fate but not specifically becoming a god in the process.
