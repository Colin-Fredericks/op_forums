            Originally posted by Jetstream View Post

            Am I the only one who has no idea what we're talking about here? Did Rowling put some kind of Native stuff in HP that I missed entirely?

            She's been doing "Magical History of North America" reveals on Pottermore, in lead up to the new movie out later this year (set in a strikingly white version of 1920s New York... you know... during the Harlem Renaissance). Some of the stuff is kind of oblivious about the US*, but the first entry, the one on Native Wizardry was basically a "What Not to Do" on the subject and set off an online storm via the voiced complaints of Native peoples.

            She just posted the history of the founding of Ilvermorney (the US counterpart to Hogwarts), which is a good read (it was founded by a runaway Irish Heir of Slytherin, who married a Muggle back in the 18th Century), along with a new test on the site to determine your Ilvermorney House (named for indigenous magical creatures: Thunderbird, Horned Serpent, Wampus, or Pukwudgie).


            *It's weird that Salem is as big a deal as it is defining American Wizarding World's extreme culture of secrecy compared to Britain, when Britain had far more witch hunts. Also, I'm not sure if the policies of complete separation between the Magical Community and the Muggle World are meant to be a stand-in for racial segregation (i don't think so, I think it's supposed to fill the role of prohibition in the movie's 1920s setting), but if it's not, she needs to realize how loaded the word "segregation" is in a US context. If it IS meant to be a racial politics allegory, it's a really bad one, because her narrative presented both sides as baring guilt.
