                                I did run my last Scion game with an Epic Purview system. Until player arguments due to the linear-is-right mentality came up, I also allowed skipping in Boons. But, since there's only so many existing Boons and I didn't feel like writing another 50+ Boons, testing them and ensuring they're balanced, I had to make changes to ensure my players felt things were being fair. I compromised to a system that I wasn't happy with. It sucked, but there you go. Sometimes you have to make shortcuts because doing things the best way just isn't practical. Now, since Scion is being totally revamped, the paid writers totally can come up with enough Boons to make a cloud system functional.

                                The whole Epics-in-Purviews is actually part of a much larger discussion about how Epic Attributes work. They're really two distinct systems: Knacks and Epics. I'd actually love to see Knacks moved over to Abilities and divorced totally from Epic Attributes. That gives Scions three kinds of powers: Knacks, Epics and Boons. They can choose to be magical (Boons), superhumanly powerful (Epics) or superhumanly talented (Knacks). In this way, it kinda mimics the Trinity system of Psions, Aberrants and Talents. You can choose which aspect of your character you want to focus on, or dabble in all three! It's utterly flexible and removes a lot of the issues the original system had.

                                If all Epic Attributes do is add dice, that's all they do. Nothing else needs to worry about adding dice to anything. Good-bye Animal Aspect and similar messy things. No double-stacking from your power-bloated dice pools and your auto-successes. They're simple, straight forward and still devastatingly powerful.

                                Then we have Knacks, which can be distributed among the Abilities and provide supernatural manifestations of trained skills. They don't have to add dice, they let you do more stuff. That's cool all by itself. It also means that Knacks aren't free with Epics, which they never should have been. In the base system, Epic Attributes gave you a double-whammy of power, access to a huge pool of additional low-cost powers, all with only a super basic pre-requisite system. Heroes can invest wisely and end up throwing around 5th-tier Knacks very, very quickly and very, very cheaply. If Knacks are instead Ability-based, we can add Ability requirements to the Knacks and not only give Abilities a function (they're rendered mostly useless by exponentially-expanding auto-successes) but also tone down the immense power of Epic Attributes.

                                That just leaves the interaction between Boons and Epics, where you pretty much are forced to buy certain Epics in order to be competitive at the Boons you want to use. That's stupid. It's enough that you have to invest in the Abilities and Attributes, much less a whole set of supernatural powers just to use your first set of supernatural powers. The Attribute+Ability roll for powers comes from systems where there is only one form of magic. Aberrant is the single exception to this, and Mega-Attributes were not nearly so powerful as Epic Attributes are. Aberrant was also, frankly, much better designed than Scion is. The way Quantum Powers worked meshed much better with Mega-Attributes so that you didn't have to have Mega-Attributes to throw around awesome Quantum Powers. In fact, trying to invest in both was basically futile. You could either be amazing at Quantum, or amazing at MegaAtts. If you split your attention, you were "meh" at both things. Scion doesn't have that. In Scion, you have to do both... or focus entirely on Epics. You can't focus entirely on Boons and be competitive. It just doesn't work.

                                So what is the choice? Well, you can tone down Epics about back to the level of Mega-Attributes and reduce the success-inflation of Scion overall. Then if Epics aren't so overwhelming, it becomes feasible to beat them out through Boon-power by itself, backed by strong mundane traits. You could tie Boon rolls to Legend and Virtues instead of Epics. Or, as Iry suggests, you can have "Purivews" as Epic Magic. I think this is silly, because it gets back to that whole idea that Purviews are basically Disciplines or Modes: linear progressions of powers instead of categories of individual, fully independent Boons. We need to get people saying "I just bought Rainbow Bridge!" instead of "I just bought Psychopomp 7!"

                                My solution to keeping Boons independent from Epics is just to scale down Epics and scale up Boons so they're roughly even in power. If you want to combine both of them, you're gunna be splitting your XP awfully thin. It's also important that the different spheres of influence be clearly defined. Epics add successes. Knacks and Boons add new things you can do. No number of successes, ever, is going to allow teleportation or fireballs. No number of fireballs is going to let you succeed at an Athletics check. By removing Knacks from Epics, you stuff Epics back into their "box" of being dice-play. If you want "Cool Powers", you need to invest in the other two categories. You can't get it all in one place, as you currently can.

                                I'm a professor! Why is no one listening to me?!
