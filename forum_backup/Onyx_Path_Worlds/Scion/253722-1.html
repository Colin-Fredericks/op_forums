    Originally posted by That Other Guy View Post

    See, for me I don't like the idea of gods always holding the 'classic' era of their views - some would, of course, but that's more a reflection of their personality than their 'nature' - particularly since it makes no sense in relation to gods who cultures and customs still very much exist, such as Orisha/Loa or various Asian deities. How can the gods not react and adapt to the culture they supposedly fully encompass, especially when they are supposed to walk amongst mortals, like, all the time?
    We're completely opposite then.

    I run Scion (and have a group of players who play Scion) to interact with the Gods of the Mythologies we adore as they were(are). Hera is always going to start out with a raging murder-hate for any bastard scions of her wayward husband. The Norse as a whole will probably be way too concerned about Global Warming. The Egyptians are in a very cold civil war, and the Morrigu is quite frankly insane and powerful.

    Think about another thing for the Gods: Time and the passage of it. Think about to summer afternoons when you were a kid and how they almost never ended. They dragged on in glorious adventure and fun. When you were in 1st grade, that summer was 1/6th of your life. Once that kid hits college, that same length of time is now roughly 1/18th to 1/20th of their lifetime. It goes by quicker.

    Now think of a God living for 2000+ years, shoot, the Sumerians go even further to around 6000+ years. A summer is 1/60000 of their time. It's a blink. 60 years is just a blink a fraction of a second longer. They're not going to give any remote inkling of a care to human events or the way the world turns because as soon as they notice it (if they ever do) it's going to go away in the blink right after that first one.

    It's only once the PCs are within the game and interacting with the Gods on a regular basis that personalities shift and evolve. Human values and human morals aren't strong enough to shape the belief of immortal, near-all-powerful entities who were never really human in the first place; but the dramatic effect of a Scion can and will shape the world around them as they're shaped by it.

    Being a lady myself who is routinely driven to fits of rage over women's history, I can assure you that misogyny doesn't last forever in games i tend to run or play in, but it does not go away over night, or sometimes, ever go away entirely. PCs have to fight to change the divine societies they're a part of, and perhaps even bring them into the modern setting and continue to push them beyond that. It's something that has become a huge underlying theme in any of my games, be it social issues or just teaching Zeus that constantly cheating on Hera is a LITTLE uncool.

        I run my own games under the assumption that the myths as existed were more diverse than the ones that patriarchal authority actively preserved. Women's oral traditions and how they differ from much of cultural "canon" is an active area of anthropological study - though obvious much of the actual content is forever lost to us.

        When in doubt, lean toward diversity in the Pantheon. Zeus has a definite history of rape, and Hera a vengeful victim-blamer - but I wouldn't have Athena curse anyone for being raped (the gorgons are just straight up Titan-spawn, via the monster Ceto as the Greeks described; that whole Roman story of the cursed priestess of Minerva story was made up whole-cloth by Ovid - at most Athena cursed a beautiful monster to reveal her true face); and Artemis is down right vindictive in the defense of women from men.
