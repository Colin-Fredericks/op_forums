    Attempting to figure out the distribution of religion in the World

    11-14-2022, 04:44 AM
    Origin (p.28-29) implies (if I understand it correctly) that religion in the World differs from that in reality in that attempts to spread monotheism are replaced by much more halfhearted and unsuccessful attempts to spread henotheism. This makes sense considering the premise (the gods aren't going to want their worship to be wiped out, after all), but the books mostly don't give much detail as to the specifics of what this has entailed, despite the fact that it seems likely to have significant influence on the game. My guess is that the writers wanted to leave it to storyguides to figure out, which makes sense, but I thought I'd start a thread trying to figure it out.

    My thoughts:
    - South, East and Southeast Asia, the Pacific, and Sub-Saharan Africa are (with some exceptions) relatively simple, since attempts to spread monotheistic religions there were recent enough that to the degree they've succeeded it's easy to see how things would have gone had they failed - most Indonesian Muslims are probably Hindu, the replacement of traditional religions with Christianity has presumably been less successful, and the CCP is probably more tolerant of religion since I can't imagine they'd miss what a bad idea antagonizing every god with a significant following in China was, but overall I imagine things are fairly recognizable. The section on the Deva in Hero mentions Pakistan, so it was clearly still founded in the World. I'm not clear on how to square this with Indian "Muslims" being almost all crypto-Hindus, which I would otherwise have assumed.

    - Religion in pre-Muslim Central Asia was (according to my extremely cursory research) a mixture of Tengrism and Zoroastrianism, with some Buddhism spreading in from the east. The other religions I'm aware of that are most likely to have gained significant presence in the area in the intervening time are Mat Syra Zemlya, Arabian polytheism and the worship of the Annuna, via the various empires that have conquered parts of the region. I do not know enough about Central Asia to have a good idea of the religious situation there beyond that.

    - A major question with the Middle East and Northern Africa is the extent to which Arabisation replaced local religions with Arabian polytheism. The most straightforwards approach here is to model the situation of Kemeticism and Marzeh on Coptic and Maronite Christianity, with Arabian polytheism in the place of Islam, but there's a case to be made that since the state religion in the World's version of the caliphates was still Islam rather than Arabian polytheism they would not have spread Arabian polytheism as effectively as their real world counterparts did Islam. The fact that Netjer, Ilhim and Annuna would have had stronger fatebindings in the region and would most likely have been more invested in preserving their faiths than the Arabian gods were in spreading theirs is another reason to expect the spread might have been less extreme, although it's hard to say by how much. It's also likely that the division between Arabian polytheists and the indigenous religions are much less clearcut than those between Islam and Christianity, as pre-Abrahamic Middle-Eastern religions were, to my understanding, much less concerned with preserving such boundaries.
    However, this is a place where the rules have something explicit to say, and it doesn't match with any of this. Hero (p.94) describes the Netjer as having only hundreds of thousands of remaining worshipers, but Wikipedia estimates there as being millions of Copts. It's not clear to me where this disparity is supposed to have come from. Did most Egyptians convert to Hellenismos in the post-Constantinian Roman empire? Were people less attached to Kemeticism than to Coptic Christianity? I can't really see anything that seems like a convincing explanation for why this would be.

    - I would imagine Helenismos still dominates Southern Europe - the only reason I can see that it wouldn't is if the Gothic invaders were much more successful at spreading Laukr in the World than they were at spreading Arianism in reality despite still being trying to spread Arianism rather than Laukr; Helenismos isn't as evangelical as Catholicism, so it's possible that the invading elites would have kept their Laukr religion and it would have spread from them to the masses. I don't see any case for the Ottoman empire spreading Tengrism more effectively than Islam.

    - Central Europe, Northern Europe and England are probably still predominantly Laukr, but I'm less confident there. Would Helenismos have spread the way Christianity did in reality? Would Helenismos have come to Germany via Charlemagne, or to England via William? Would the Italian-dominated medieval Vatican have zealously suppressed Laukr while turning a blind eye to Helenismos? Would the Renaissance idolization of ancient Greece and Rome have extended to adopting their religion? My guess is that while these would all have contributed to a significant presence of Helenismos in the region, the majority of the population still follow Laukr, but I'm not confident in this.

    - Iberia is its own kettle of fish, and given its influence on South America a fairly significant one. Religion in pre-Christian Iberia (again, according to my extremely cursory research) was a mix of Marzeh, Helenismos, Celtic religion and unique native beliefs, and later invasions would have brought Laukr, Arabian polytheism and the traditional Berber religion into the mix as well. We know from the rules that the Celtic religion was eventually wiped out, but beyond this, much as in the case of Central Asia, I would need a considerably better knowledge of the region's history than I in fact have to guess how the religious situation shakes out.

    - In Australia and North America, the population is predominantly descended from English colonists, who would presumably have brought their religions with them. I would expect this to lead to Laukr to be the most widespread religion, and to dominate things on a national level. However, the fatebinding of indigenous gods to the land, and the greater openness of Laukr to syncretism compared to Christianity, would likely mean a greater uptake of indigenous religion among the colonists that actually occurred. That attempts by the British colonists to wipe out native religions would have gone less successfully is a direct implication of the "attempts to spread monotheism in the World have been much less successful" premise. Indigenous religions thus likely have a significantly greater presence in the World's version of these regions than they do in reality.

    - Latin America is similar in having a large population of European colonists, but differs both in that they make up a smaller portion of the total population and in that the colonists were from Iberia, and thus likely more religiously diverse. In addition to these and the native population, there is a large Black population descended from West African slaves, whose religions would also have been brought with them.

    Thoughts?

      This is one of the biggest hand-waves in the whole concept - "All Myths Are True", but everything is essentially the same as it is in the "real world". This is probably largly due to an effort on the designers' part not to reduce, trivialize, or offend existing religions, and partly because everyone has their own idea of how things would have worked, and so having an official world wasn't worth the effort.
      Oddly, what almost works better, is 1st edition's premise that the old Gods have been away, and are just coming back again. Then history can be as it is until now, and you can change it as you want going forward, when the pantheons return.
      But yes, it requires work on the GMs part, because players will ask all the questions.

              One thing that is clear is that the Abrahamic religions have been active in the setting and the Abrahamic myths are as true as any other. The chapter in God on antagonists (just released to backers today) explicitly states the "God of the Israelites" killed Leviathan. So, the non-Abrahamic religions might not have been marginalized as much as they have in the real world, but there's no reason the Abrahamic religions shouldn't have become influential.

              My own head-canon the matter:

              The God of Abraham straddles the line between a God and Primordial and has many Mantles, showing different faces to the faithful of different traditions. He does not intervene directly in the world, but acts through Messengers (Angels), Saints, and Prophets. He has no Scions, at least not any time recently. An idea I picked up from someone else is that sites holy to the God of Abraham--churches, mosques, synagogues and the like--negate the magic of all other Pantheons, explaining the distinctive power and appeal of his religions in the World without needing him to have Scions. I think the official position of Abrahamic religious leaders could be that the Gods are actually fallen angels, ones who did not side with Lucifer, but tried to remain neutral; they were cast out of Heaven, but only as far as Earth, not hell. (This was a real world explanation for the Fey.)

              In the real world, the word "pagan" comes from the Latin term for someone who dwelt in rural areas, because the old religions clung on longer there. We can kick that up a notch and say that they lingered and, in some cases, even thrived there throughout the Middle Ages, but the aristocracy and intellectuals were, for the most part, Christian or Muslim. Perhaps some places remained devoted to the old Gods--I could see Nubia remaining devoted to the Netjer, even after Egypt became mostly Muslim.

              Then, in the Renaissance, as intellectuals became interested in ancient philosophy and Hermeticism, there was a revival of interest among them in the old Gods as well, particularly the Theoi and Netjer. In the real world, this interest in ancient philosophy and Hermeticism happened, along with an accompanying intellectual interest in ancient Greco-Roman mythology and religion. During the 1800s--the era of romanticism, nationalism and Victorian occultism in the real world--there was a dramatic wave of revival of devotion to the old Gods, especially the Pantheons romantics and nationalists saw as fitting with the newly developing national identities. Occultists would have developed a strong interest in pantheons like the Netjer and Aesir whose PSPs involve magic. In the 1960s and 70s, which in our world saw the rise of neo-paganism, there would have been another wave of conversions to the old religions in the World.

              Many people in Christian and Muslim regions would probably honor the old gods alongside the God of Abraham, despite the official disapproval of Abrahamic religions leaders. After all, in the real world, lots of Christians and Muslims believe and practice all sorts of non-orthodox stuff like astrology or reincarnation.

              I think the World of Scion developing in parallel to our world is somewhat plausible, at least enough for game purposes--you just need to tweak some real world trends to make it work. The outcome looks both different and familiar, like what the game is going for.

                      My own take is that this sort of thing depends on how you adjust the Evidence knob. Iron looks like the real world, with the gods in hidden nooks and crannies. Gold is essentially modern fantasy with the Pantheons replacing a lot of modern cultural cues.

                      I frequently think of the World as having been at Bronze level for at least the last third of the 20th century, with the current wave of Scions (including the PCs) and their adventures since the start of the 21st century having raised the World's Evidence level to Silver (what is supposed to be Scion's default setting). This includes an explosion of new worshipers of the various Pantheons and gods springing up all over the world. These cults need not be bound by history, race, or other factors. Just as an example, use the band Wu Tang Clan as the inspiration/basis for a Shen Cult in inner city America, mixing hip hop culture with ancestor worship, Neo-Confuscionism, and martial arts. Similarly, you might find sizable Cults devoted to the Mesopotamian gods in New York and London, the Theoi in Australia or Egypt, or the Loa on the Internet.

                          I politely side step this by having the major Abrahamic religions still major cultural forces in their various areas, and the setting starting off as Iron, more or less. I had an initiating event trigger the return of Myth to The World at the edges (this was during my players Origins arc) with the ball starting to roll faster through following events and the players becoming more aware that something has been out there for a while. They just never noticed.

                          While the upper crust of societies have been as cynical as ours is (excepting quiet cults working the gods various wills), those in the lower ends of various societies have been more aware, and have been working various Gods or Titans wills for a long time. And the triggering even has increased their power.

                          I have rolled this into a major Cult threat referred to as the Harbingers of Ragnarök, An over cult that draws from all of the Aesir Titans formed by a cadre of betrayed and disgruntled ex-military officers that too a look around and said, in essence, "Corruption is everywhere. It ALL has to go. Burn it down." Most people would think that that means Coup to put themselves in power (like the players). Until they managed to capture a few of them. And were told to their faces exactly what they mean. and I had the Harbingers level Portland, WA to get the point across.

                          As the players discover that the Harbingers of Ragnarok (HoR) have tentacles world wide, they have some some globe hopping but have run into some of the other pantheons in the rough areas they have historically have been (with some exceptions) and have worked to warn the locals if they are not aware of them. But they have found out the HoR use lots of locally oppressed populations as catspaws. As they have travelled and seen things and various mythical creatures, the setting has moved slowly towards Bronze.

                          The players have scored a major victory recently by blowing up a central co-ordination base that the HoR have used, and also have run off with a fair amount of intel. Which included maps to various Axis Mundis and Overwold places the HoR are aware of and use as shipping paths to move around in.

                          It's been a way I use to introduce the players to the setting, and slowly scale things up as they advance. What was once secret...not as much anymore.

                          As to the various religions wandering around in the US, I tend to play it fairly low key, but lean on The Republic variation of the Theoi Pantheon, but their are the Roman (imperium) versions and the original that have quietly seeped it over the centuries. The U.S. Military definitely has more Roman aspects towards it, such as Triumph awards to victorious generals, The Agoge (Spartan military schools) are traditional military schools that turn out hardcore soldiers (they view West Point graduates with more than a bit of contempt).

                          In short, I work small things in at the edges, enough to show major and minor differences, but enough to keep things relatively recognizable.

                                                          We also must remember that the syncretism of the belief in supernatural beings in the Abrahamic religions has been around since the time of the ancient Israelite religion.
