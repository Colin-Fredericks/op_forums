    Originally posted by Yig1015w View Post

    You say that like my view is wrong. (Not sure if thats the intent) Clearly I'm in the minority here or have a dated way of thinking so if that's the case is there anyone here willing to give me examples as to why it works better and how I should argue that same case to players who are unfamilier with it? I'm fine with being told im wrong, provided the other person can tell me why. I'm also the type of person to want to learn/adapt if I got a bad perspective.
    At least for me, a game where the Game Master/Storyteller/Storyguide/Keeper of Arcane Secrets/whatever is soundly in charge of the narrative with the player characters are pretty much exclusively a reactionary role is boring to me. Many games in recent years (especially among the indie scene) have been working to break down the wall between GM and players in terms of wider control over the narrative (beyond just deciding what your own character can do), since tabletop roleplaying is already a collaborative exercise.

    Having the dice fail isn't interactive in the least. Having the dice fail you and getting to decide if you want to succeed at a cost anyway, have something else happen, or let things go catastrophically wrong is by contrast much more engaging and fun, and lets players have more of a hand in the story. "Why would I ever want to botch?" is very much an old-school "I want to win the game" mentality, whereas taking the botch in the name of drama (and Momentum to spend on a later, more critical roll) is better for the story.

    Sorry to come off a little harsh. I'm used to lots and lots of hardcore grognards losing their minds whenever any sort of story-focused indie game comes up and it makes me defensive.

    Remi. she/her. game designer.
