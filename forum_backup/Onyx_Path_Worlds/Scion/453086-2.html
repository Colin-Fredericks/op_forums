                    Absolutely.

                    The later list of "good guys" may have had "the courage of their convictions" but they had neither Courage nor Conviction the Scion Virtues as described in RAW. The first three have something more like Scion Conviction. Look at the description - "take any measures necessary to further her cause or take action to further her beliefs", you get dice adds "to commit heinous acts in support of their belief", you have to fail a Virtue roll or spend a Willpower "refuse to perform an action that will benefit their cause (even if it results in the suffering of others)". Note "any measure necessary", "heinous acts", "result in suffering".

                    Like almost all the other Virtues, it may share a name with something identified as "virtuous", but they are NOT the same thing. What is normally thought of as the virtuous behavior associated with that word is usually a part of the behaviors that characterize the Scion Virtue, that it encourages or that it discourages, but there are always, other, darker parts of them.

                    Conviction, as a case in point, definitely dances with the dark side quite openly. It's there on the face of it. (Not like Courage, which sounds good on paper, but really encompasses not only bravery, but bravado, foolhardiness, being a braggart, having a very thin skin where certain insults are concerned.)

                    So, if you want Conviction, Courage, etc. to mean what they normally mean when used, you need to, once again, change the rules. You need to edit those lists and change some of the fundamental assumptions of the game, which starts from the fact that the orginal meaning of the word "hero" in Greek doesn't have very much to do with being a "good guy" at all. Heroes can be right bastards, they just have to be greater than human. And thereby take out some gods or pantheons as playable options, because much of mythology is NOT about the good guys beating the bad guys.

                                            That is emphatically another example of the same bias, based on a false equivalency. Cultures are not humans who age through various stages of moral development (in fact, much of what humans consider to be moral or ethical at any point in their lives is completely culture bound. We definitely do things in modern American culture that are absolutely abhorent to other current cultures, even those we would consider to be "on the same wavelength" and would be equally heretical/disgusting/naive/laughable to previous cultures. Modern America is not the perfected end result of some moral evolution of humanity. First, that's not how evolution works at all. It has nothing to do with it. Nothing is more evolved than anything else. All things are equally evolved as they are all the end result of the same process applied across the board. Some traits, some cultures, some ideas might be more adaptive in the current situation, but, when the situation changes, the selective pressures change and what was adaptive yesterday might get you dead today. In fact, as far as most professional academics today are concerned, there are piles and piles and piles of evidence that indicate that modern Western culture is, in fact, malapative in the extreme on several fronts. We aren't just "not better" than previous cultures, we may be worse off. Actually we ARE worse off in terms of things like free-time to resource acquisition and many other fronts. (For an example, think about dishwashers. They consume more time and resources by far than just washing your dishes by hand. Think about it. For virtually all commercially available dishwashers, you have to handwash the dishes ANYWAY, then put them in the machine, run the machine with all those associated additional costs, then, most likely hand dry most if not all of them ANYWAY, then put them away.... You haven't saved anything. You have wasted a lot of time and effort and the only real difference is that, for a little while, your dishes are warm....)

                                            The question in terms of the context of Scion is whether the pantheons of ancient cultures are going to change over time. There is some evidence either way, but the balance of the evidence in RAW is that the pantheons are going to remain static representations of some view of their source culture. The Virtues, Fatebinding, etc. all tie directly into that. Where change could have been manifested, e.g. in places such as some gradual shift of the attitudes in the gods over time, like, say, the Greek portrayals of the Dodekatheon vs. the Roman portrayals of the same, that idea was never addressed. And though negative evidence isn't as dispostive as positive evidence, it is still evidence. [Caveat: I do know that things are/might be changing in Scion 2.0, but for those of us out here in the wilderness hearing only the whispers of the songs being sung at the designers' campfires, RAW and discussions of House Rules are all we've got to work with until we get something playable.] It's definitely a viewpoint that has some solid backing on both the practical and most esoteric sides of academics investigations into the same issues. Pantheons and religions are the place where the most fervent viewpoints on cultural values are steadfastly held to. They are the core of cultural identity. As the founder of sociology (and arguably, one of the founders of anthropology as well) Emile Durkheim said, religion is the mirror where a culture goes to look at it's perfected self (both paraphrased and translated from French).

                                            If you, in your game, want the pantheons to adhere to Western cultural norms, kudos. Go for it. I admit that, even after decades of training in cultural relativism and past cultures, when I really think about Aztec, Greek & Roman, Norse, etc. varieties of human sacrifice, it seems pretty horrible to me. Particularly the accompanying cannibalism among the Aztecs (and the sheer, almost industrialized SCALE, of their sacrifices). Also, the institutionalized incest among Egyptian royalty. (But neither is worse in any way than the blood-drinking in Vampire, overall.) For me, having those be totally acceptable things to your character's parents is part of what Scion is about; the struggle between generations. (Also backed up in the books by having the LAST generational conflict flare back up.). But that doesn't have to be the way you do it in your own game. If you are playing in someone else's game and they are hewing close to the rules, you need to be upfront about it, because there definitely will be the kinds of problems that tear bands apart and turn them into arch-nemeses who will do anything to destroy their former comrade(s) and all their works. (And how appropriately mythic is that? A whole ton of a lot...)

                                            And, once again, if you are going to RUN a Scion game, you are going to need to put this into your House Rules and be pretty explicit about it, as it is a change in direction from the base game. Someone who just reads RAW might or might not clue in otherwise and you might have some very disappointed players. I've already identified some of the areas you are going to have to change to make your personalized version of Scion based on how you perceive the core conflicts of the game - change the Virtues to the point of eliminating them, reconfigure Fatebinding so the gods don't end up being forced to be the kind of jerks you want them to have "evolved" away from, etc. You might even want to institute some kind of Humanity stat so you can punish backsliders or those who choose the older and darker ways. I dunno what else might work, but it might be deserving of a thread of it's own, separate from the issues of this one.
