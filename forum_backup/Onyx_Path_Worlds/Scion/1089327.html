      Two gods get together and interact. Sometimes they have sex, sometimes they just mingle together, sometimes one ejaculates on the other and the other brushes it off uncomfortably and it spawns a new entity on its own, sometimes the heavens and the earth meet and things just happen. Almost anything you can reasonably think of can result in a new god.

      How gods become supernaturally connected to ideas depends incredibly on the culture they are representing (or trying to emulate). Some gods are just born connected to an idea, while others have a personality that is close to an idea and they get connected by coincidence or circumstance. Others are outright given their ideas and told to manage them, while some have no supernatural connection to ideas at all.

      So it basically comes down to "However you want. Just make it an interesting story."

          Well Scion, at least with regard to "the World" setting, doesn't work like American Gods setting. It's not about belief just creating beings wherever some form of worship is directed. There needs to be a narrative, a Legend. So as Incendax, said its usually via some form of creation by existing divinities.

          Keep in mind, you're unlikely to see a Pantheon like the "New Gods" of American Gods in "the World" as not only the metaphysics work off of Legend, not Worship, but also the "Old Gods" have adapted much better than in the American Gods setting. You don't need a new God of Media when Hermes has adapted perfectly well to the modern age, for example.

          But of course, that's only in "the World", a setting Shard with a Pantheon of Gods of Modernity facing off against the ancient gods as an American Gods homage is totally plausible!

              Originally posted by Crowley View Post
              Been talking with some friends after i introduced them to Scion and American Gods.

              So i been thinking, how does one Idea becomes a God? or rather how a concept would evolve into a god within the Scion game?
              In a Scion game? Well, the way it would work in Scion 1e is that a existing Deity would have a Scion child, and that child could attach themselves to a modern concept, and uplift it along with themselves during Apotheosis.

              Scion doesn't use the idea of belief uplifting concepts into Deities independently, as we see in American Gods. However, a Scion could very easily adopt modern concepts and bring those into the divine fold rather easily. A modern War God could bring in the concept of Artillery, Special Forces, or Submarines. A Scion who is an artist could become a literal Rock God, etc.

              To use an example from American Gods, but a very background one as to not spoil anything for folks watching the show, I'll use the Railway Gods eluded to a few times. They could have been Scions of any Deity, it doesn't really matter, but let's say Hermes since he is a well known Traveler. One of these Scions could have been a Railway Engineer, another an Immigrant Worker (I don't know a ton about the American railway, but up in Canada they would probably be a Shen or Tuatha Scion depending on which side of the country they were on), and a third a corporate railway baron.

              The Railway Engineer has Epic Intelligence, Forge (not actually in RAW, but a common houseruled Purview), Fire, and Water. They bring about huge developments in the world of the steam engine, and through various complex adventures eventually reach Legend 9 and undergo Apotheosis. Now you have a God of the Steam Engine.

              The Immigrant Worker has Epic Stamina, Epic Strength, Death (since so many people perished in the construction and were buried around the tracks or inside side tunnels), and Psychopomp (since they came from far-afield and help others travel). They carve tunnels through mountains, hammer railway spikes in with their bare hands, lift entire rails by themselves, and gently bury the mortals alongside them who perish during the construction of the Iron Road. For this, let's say they are Shen. They undergo Apotheosis, and join the Celestial Bureaucracy, being appointed a position within one of the myriad ministries of Heaven, and are appointed as the Deity in charge of Railways.

              The Corporate Railway Baron is another Scion. They have Epic Charisma, Epic Manipulation, Wealth (not actually in RAW, but sometimes houseruled in), and Psychopomp. Their industries forge railways across the entire continent, and are an industry leader. They provide massive numbers of jobs, mostly poor paying, taking advantage of immigrant laborers coming to North America. They reshape the nation under their thumb, wielding tremendous influence upon the State, and go on many adventures which leads to their Apotheosis upon reaching Legend 9. They join their Pantheon as the God of Industry, or Railways, or in general probably has a wide 'Divine Portfolio.'

              So that's how it would work in Scion! A concept doesn't independently become a God in Scion, but a Scion can 'pick them up', and become the Deity of that.

                              Gods in scion are actual honest to god (ha), fire and brimstone Deities.                                                       I would hardly call the comic books deserving of respect when it comes to heroes. Classical heroes got the job done and made sure the villains stayed down. Modern day heroes only treat the symptoms, not the cause. And the villains keep coming back to cause more mayhem. If a comic book hero tried to seal away the Titans again, chances are Fate would have them break right back out and you'd have to do the whole process over again. Then they'd break out, you'd put them back in, they'd break out, it would be a never ending cycle of pain and misery.





                                                      Personally, I'm a fan of the trope of Gods being sustained by the power of belief, but humanity largely being unaware of it. It gives the possibility for stories in which mortals finally realize they're sick of being pushed around and try to rebel against the divinities; and actually having a fighting chance at doing so. In settings where the Gods are eternal and elemental, it means that mortals are pretty much stuck being their playthings. Makes it hard to empathize with or care about the plight of mortals if a Scion's life is objectively more valuable than theirs.

                                                          I don't know, things are a bit more complicated than that.

                                                          For one, when the Superheroes stop a major story arc, they generally stop it. I.e., if the Superheroes manage to stop the Alien Invasion, the Aliens are still out there but the Invasion Attempt has been thwarted and it won't come again in exactly the same form. Hell, that "in the same form" is another important little rule,-- but let's get back to that later,-- because really the "Alien Invasion" rule is kind of where the Titanic-level Threats would rise. So, Superheroes COULD stuff Titan's back in Tartarus. They'd still be there, ready to cause trouble again in some other form, but they could 'save the day' in the current threat. The real issue is generally more in terms of the day to day rivals of the Superheroes, their "Rogues Gallery", who Fate will conspire to ensure they're not permanently taken out of the picture. But, this is more likely to be other Gods and what not as opposed to huge world-ending threats.

                                                          Another important point, honestly, is the one that I brushed up against earlier. Mainly, threats that a Superhero defeats don't tend to come again in the same form. Once you thwart a villain, it doesn't matter how successful that plan was or how much damage was done, the next time he acts? It'll be in a novel fashion that doesn't really draw on past attempts. If you stop a villain from doing this one specific thing, you don't have to worry about them just... doing it again.. even if logically they really, really should. This is important for the big stories, like titan's breaking out of Tartarus, and also for the smaller ones, like the Joker kidnapping and maiming one of your followers.

                                                          Last, and perhaps most important, the core conceit of Superheroes is less that they don't actually solve problems or anything. No, it's the fact that their lives tend to be on repeat, that eventually things are going to circle back around and you're going to end up telling the tale all over again from the beginning. Superheroes don't get old, they don't really reach any real climax in their tales, they just kind of run on for a while and then start all over again. They're forever trapped in the beginning stages of their "careers".
