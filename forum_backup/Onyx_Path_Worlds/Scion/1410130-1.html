    Originally posted by Kyman201 View Post
    Wyrd if you want to play Mage, you can play Mage, you don't gotta come in here and go &quot;Okay but how do I make Scion into Mage?&quot;

    I don't go into the WoD threads and piss and moan about the mythic inaccuracies of all of the various splats, because then I'd be the asshole and people would quite rightly go &quot;Buddy fuck off and go back to Scion if you like Scion so much&quot;

    Edit: Some Cosmologies have Overworlds, and they're a thousand times more interesting than how I've heard these Astral Realms described. I recommend just using THOSE, because they'll inevitably make for a better story than trying to take something from a different game line and trying to beat it into shape to fit a different line, tone, and setting.
    I would note that whether you're talking about Mage: the Ascension or Mage: the Awakening, there are a number of conceptual parallels. For example, if I were combining Scion 1e with Awakening 1e, I'd probably say that Awakening's Supernal World and Scion's Overworld are the same thing; it's just that Awakening focuses on Watchtower Realms while Scion deals with Godrealms. Both Awakening and Scion have changed considerably since their respective first editions, so I'm not sure how this would work if trying to mesh their second editions; but I suspect parallels could be established.

    The Ascension parallel is in some ways more fundamental: Scion's “all myths are true” concept is similar to Ascension's multifaceted reality, as demonstrated by the way that Motifs in Scion 2e serve a similar role to Practices in M20. More generally, the mythological constructs that Scion 2e calls Pantheons are very similar in concept to M20's concept of the Focus, or what earlier editions of Ascension called Paradigms.

    With all that in mind, I wouldn't necessarily have a problem adding an Astral Space to Scion. But I'd want to tie it to a Pantheon where the notion of dreams and mindscapes having a reality of their own is a thing. I don't think any of the traditional Pantheons fit the bill; instead, I'd consider this to be part of a Scion variant (similar to Dragon or Mythos) where Scion is adapted to represent the likes of Santa Claus, Paul Bunyan, and others of that sort: beings who, unlike gods, do derive from and are sustained by human imagination.

    If combined with Scion proper, this “Realm of the imagination” wouldn't be “the space between the Godrealms”; it would effectively be “another Godrealm” unto itself.

                            The original supposition for Scion 1e was pretty simple in many ways, it was about playing Heroes, Demigods and Gods, and it was absolutely based on Greek mythology, there were other pantheons, we know that, but it was build (including the way the other pantheons worked in general) on a Greek mythology structure, with gods having children or adopting them from other gods, and Titans fighting against gods.

                            It offended many people, most Hindu and modern religions, as the way it portraits their gods were simplistic and completely centered on the Greek paradigm of gods.

                            Then the second edition came with the proposition of fixing the broken system AND the way the mythologies would be faced in the game.

                            The problem, as I see, is, some people don’t think Scion is Just a Game, as it deal with real beliefs it must be true to the beliefs, while most of the players see Scion as just another game that you can go from mortal to god, not different from Deities and Demigods from D&D or The Primal Order (Old but gold, worth the money! Check it).

                            Honestly, this conflict just throw people away from the game. I am here trying to spread the gospel (intended pun) of Scion, but it really gets hard to do if the community acts aggressively against the casual player.
                            When people come to this forum asking for help or suggestions for a crossover, they are looking for people that know the game and played enough to help with the mechanics and conversions. If they ask any Scion mechanics question in any other forum I will not see, because I don’t go around CoD or WoD forums often, and so will not be able to help.

                            People like Watcher and Sacerdos (there are many others that know a lot, but they Are my “Myth-source” of choice), spread the ideas of how the several mythologies can be better portrayed in your game. And I do enjoy how many of the more “hardcore players” value the theme of Scion, but when we get to a new player (and I include myself here) we don’t need to know everything to play a game just because there is a lot to know.

                            I, for example, like mithology, and Scion pushes me to look for more, but I just can’t deal with all the information to play 5 different pantheons in the same game and still keep fidelity to any of them. I just want to have fun and tell a story about heroes becoming gods, or maybe how gods live in a world that don’t give them value, or how heroes defeat monsters (and I really don’t want to go back to D&D for personal reasons).

                            Well, that’s it, hope nobody got offended as I called Scion “just a game”...

                                Originally posted by Mateus Luz View Post
                                Well, that’s it, hope nobody got offended as I called Scion “just a game”...
                                I don't think anyone would be offended at that, but, I think the important thing is to not use 'Just a Game' as a way to put down deeper criticisms of a text. I think the reason that the phrase sets a lot of people off is because it often is used to try to stifle debate or discussion, when something being a game doesn't diminish its importance or value. Be it a TTRPG, a video game, a movie, music, a novel, all of these things are texts that can be read and evaluated by the audience. Just because something is intended for entertainment doesn't give it a pass, or reduce its importance, or the potential dangers of these things perpetuating bad material.

                                Scion is a game, and just like any piece of media I choose to pay for, I want it to be worth the money I pay for it. Additionally, I want to think about what I spend my money on. Scion being a game doesn't magically free it of responsibility for its contents, or diminish the value of us from reading critically into the information it gives us.

                                I think ultimately people can do whatever they like with a piece of media, though there are some things I would consider tasteless (whitewashing characters, bi-erasure, etc) that people do when consuming texts. Scion is somewhat more of an interesting issue, as it is a mirror of real life faiths that deserve respect. I think the ultimate issue here in how people interpret things can be summed up in this idea of the mirror quite well. I am interested in the reflection (the myths, both in the factual material that can be found in the reflection, putting a critical eye at flaws in the reflection, and creating my own reflections and attempting to create the best reflections I can) where other people are interested in the mirror itself (the system, the mechanics, what the book tells you, the fictional structure put together to create the reflection). I find the mirror itself to be ultimately unimportant save for how it effects the reflection, while other people find the reflection to be unimportant save for how it expresses the function of the mirror. Both of these ways to approach Scion are fine as long as you are trying to be respectful, because it is the least we can do when enjoying these wonderful stories from various cultures.

                                Now, I will also just add, I'm not actually a guy, though no worries or concern there about that. But also, while I doubt people are going to be offended that you called Scion a game, they will probably not be cool with you saying "oriental" which you might want to change as that is actually a not-cool word to use.

                                On the topic of the idea of Astral Realms, I'm pretty sure that it is an actual religious concept from the last few centuries, so there should 100% be an Astral Realm in Scion. It is just that Pantheon's specific concept. This is a good example why I don't really find a need to add stuff to Scion outside of the base premise, there is probably a version of it that already exists in the setting somewhere. It is what I love about the game.

                                P.S. I don't only hold Scion to these standards. It isn't unique in that regard at all. If it helps, I actually have very serious criticisms of Deities and Demigods and the Druid Class as well. Like, massive. I think the first publication of it is responsible for the largest reaching piece of misinformation regarding the Celts and Celtic religion in the last century that has caused such a high level of damage to the cultural conception of these things I think it will be at least a century until it can be resolved. The new Netflix series about Excalibur I critique for trying (badly) to write Welsh in Ogam, messing up both the Welsh and the Ogam. The television show Brittania (I think?) I can't stand because it perpetuates flat out super-bad depictions of Celtic peoples, drawing on the 'Noble Savage' and all that. Nothing is above critique, especially when dealing with history and the like. What makes Scion slightly unique is it makes a claim to history, there is a claim that the books are drawing on actual material which gives it a sense of legitimacy and people consume the texts and absorb the information. It markets itself with this idea of a faithful depiction of these texts, and they hire scholars to work on the projects, which gives people confidence in the information they are consuming because the product has presented itself as working with authorities and trying to 'get things right' which is great! Up until an entire chapter is 99.9% made up but frames itself only as partly fictional.

                                        I'm not in favour of negative representations at all, and I think Scion has gone a long way toward being respectful of the faiths contained within. My concern is that many threads seem to focus on maintaining fully accurate depictions of gods and pantheons, while stifling any variation in the name of a good/fun game (read: enjoyable to participants while still maintaining at least basic respect to cultures and beliefs). "Just a game" means, to me, "not a speech by a world leader" and "not a paper by a scholar"; it won't change mass opinion or mislead nations, but should entertain the group within the genre you are playing. I don't get "play Scion but hide the Gods" or "play Scion but feature Christianity/Islam instead", but those are choices for a Storyteller to make, not me. If you want to create your own Pantheons, or have those in the game change in the decades/centuries/millennia since they were actively worshipped by societies in the real world, you should feel you can, and feel you can discuss those ideas freely here, on the Scion forums.
