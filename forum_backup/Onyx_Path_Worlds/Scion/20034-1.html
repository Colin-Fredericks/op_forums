                                Originally posted by Jen View Post
                                Hhmmm, I'm trying to make a write-up on Táo Quân, the Vietnamese Kitchens God, except that he is originally one single guy in Chinese Taoist but split into 2 guys and 1 girl (it's a triangle love kind of thing) when the Vietnamese people adopted his myth. Should I try to explain his paradox origin or just choose one origin and pretend the other one don't exist.
                                Gods and goddesses often have conflicted backgrounds depending upon when they originated and who assimilates them into their culture. Instead of looking at this as a paradox, I'd view this to be an evolution of the god's story and focus on providing rules for his current form. You can figure out a catalyst (e.g. some sort of bridge) later. Ancient history is cool -- but is it relevant to your game?

                                For a holiday-related pantheon, I'd put together a collection of lightbringing gods and goddesses. The sun (or son, if you're Christian) coming back into the world is a big part of how this holiday evolved. Gods like: Mithras, Saturn, Apollo, Ishtar, Marduk, and Osiris. The lightbringers do tend to be male-dominated, though! Wheels turning, calendars changing are also good ones to look at like Janus or the Holly/Oak King and Osiris/Isis myths.
