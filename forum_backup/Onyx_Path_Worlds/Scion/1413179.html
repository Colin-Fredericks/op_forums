    Scion x VtM: Antediluvian Pantheon

    09-12-2020, 04:06 AM
    Hey everyone, it's me, that one guy whose WtA conversion sparked some contreversy, to say the least. While I have yet to finish my edits of it (and to be honest, my enthusiasm for it has been drained from all of the arguments which occurred, valid as they may be), progress IS still being made, albeit at a very slow pace. In between that and this post, I've started up a new campaign with friends, and I've been taking a look at some of my other conversions with a greater focus on retaining the sanctity and respect towards real world faiths. This conversion, of the Vampire the Masquerade Antediluvians, is actually the 2nd version, as the first was less than great. It is my attempt to completely rewrite the lore of VtM to better fit the fluidity and mystery of Scion, and as such you'll notice quite a number of differences from the original work. Any discussion is welcome, but please keep it civil. I cannot stress this enough, taking into account what occurred in the thread of my last conversion. I do not want the Mods having to peer in because of drawn out arguments or personal attacks. With that being said, here is what I have thus far:

    Path Skills: Subterfuge, Survival
    Virtues: Humanity vs. The Beast (Humanity is the remnant of human understanding and consciousness a Kindred still possesses. The Beast opposes human feelings and failings, and instead desires only that the needs of the body are satisfied. This virtue in particular is antithetical to humanity, and it is little wonder how many of the Antediluvians have fallen into a state of Titanhood with such urges taking hold of them.)

    Background: The Antediluvians, the pantheon of the Kindred, are dying. No better words sum up the current state of the Vampire Gods than these, and the fault lies not with the few faithful which remain. The pantheon’s losses are due to two intertwining factors: Desertion and Falls into madness. At least three former members (Arikel, Haqim and Sutekh) have vanished and presumably been welcomed by other groups, while six others have delved too far into their purviews and become Titans to haunt mankind for the rest of its days. Because of this, only five of the original thirteen retain their positions and sanity, with two new members forcing their way in through divine thievery. Such numbers are insufficient for the final conflicts approaching. Gehenna is nigh, and there seems to be little the Antediluvians, the Kindred from before the Flood, can do barring a miracle. For unlike the Aesir, they have no idea of what the future holds. Unlike the Devas they know there is no cycle of life and death they can cling onto for safety. And unlike the Teotl, they do not have nearly enough sacrifices to provide for themselves. The last stand of the Vampires is upon them.

    History: Who were the Antediluvians before, well, the Deluge hit? Little historical records exist of that time. While the legends the Kindred tell speak of two cities, one run by the Biblical Caine, and the other by the Vampire Gods themselves, historians have yet to corroborate this story to any satisfactory degree. As soon as some piece of evidence is found, it slowly gets lost, damaged or vanishes entirely, “washed away” like the sea erodes at stone. This is believed by some to be an ongoing curse laid down by the Flood which drowned the first vampires in its depths, content now to consume history rather than ashes. Whether this is really the case or not is unknown, but it would certainly help explain the lack of worship this pantheon has.
    Here is what is claimed by the Vampires, at present: Long before the world was civilized, in the prehistory of man, an individual committed the crime of familicide, and was punished by some force or entity, transformed into the First Vampire. In the modern era, almost all believes corroborate this figure with Cain, the first Murderer as told in the Book of Genesis. However, all those who remain Gods of the pantheon are noticeably cagey about the subject. Typically they refer to this vampire as “the Dark Father” rather than by any given name. Regardless of titles, this man would take over a city, known as “the First City”, as its ruler, and would turn 3 others into his species. From them, the original thirteen members of the Kindred would be created.
    For a time, the First City was a paradise, for the vampires at least. They had all that they desired, alongside their divine immortality. While this longevity came with the curse of consuming blood to survive, and being unable to have natural children, the benefits were considered to outweigh the downsides. Something had to change, and sadly the Flood was that change. A rush of divine water, sent by an unknown God, Titan or Primordial (some speculate it as being either the Abrahamic God or Enlil of the Anunna, though neither side is sure of this) crashed through the First City, killing the second generation of vampires, nearly all of the fourth generation who were just being created, and all of the humans and other creatures living within its walls. Those same walls were leveled to the earth alongside every building contained within. While the Dark Father and the thirteen survived, questions have been asked throughout history about how the latter managed to survive where their predecessors, the three, failed. Some think the three were the primary targets. Others (especially the remaining Gods) say that they were able to get to higher ground quickly enough.

    Some wonder whether the thirteen cannibalized the three to save themselves.

    Whether any of these opinions are true or not is irrelevant, as once the Flood had ended, the Dark Father removed himself from the presence of the thirteen, leaving them to their own devices. In emulation of their old home, most of the thirteen tried to recreate the First City with their own encampment, the Second City, but it never reached the same level of prosperity or fame as the First. Eventually, every member left of their own volition to pursue their own efforts, and the Second City disappeared beneath the waves of history.

    The disparate gods wandered the earth, founding their own cults here and there, and trying to worm their way into other pantheons to retain their place in the world. Arikel was the first to succeed at this, either creating a new identity whole-cloth or merging her identity and Mantle with the goddess Inanna and cementing her station within the Anunna. Sutekh then followed, doing the same with the god Set of the Netjer. For millennia, none of the others managed to create a place for themselves in another pantheon or religion until Haqim, around 700-800 AD, vanished entirely before reemerging as the famed Assassin and instructor Hassan-I Sabbah in defense of Islam. While this is the truth, it remains only speculation among the remaining Antediluvians who observe familiar character traits amongst these “newer” gods. Others in the pantheon have attempted the same, such as Troile and Ravnos, but these efforts have borne little fruit.

    Something sinister would lurk beneath the surface of these independent goals, sadly. While the Vampire Gods were always fairly predatory in their behavior, referring to these instincts as “the Beast,” they were still fully cognizant entities. They still had individuality. With time, this was no longer the case. Tzimisce is considered to be the first case of this, having shed his godly nature to embrace a new existence as a network of plants and flesh. Absimiliard, consumed by his hatred of his hideous face, was next to dive into Titanhood as he took his wrath out on anything and everything he could find of good physical countenance. Troile, having failed to protect her patron city of Carthage from the invading force of Romans, embraced her rage to a monstrous degree, forcing Ventru to intervene and seal her alongside Theoi Scions into a prison of salt. Finally, Ennoia, Lasombra and Malkav followed on Tzimisce’s path and embodying their chosen Purviews. By the end of this, six new titans had been created, and only five Gods remained to combat them.

    Though new Gods would join the group, neither did so in a friendly fashion. Tremere, a Scion of Hermes, pursued the death of Saulot so as to complete his Apotheosis, and while he did ascend, he failed to permanently kill the God of Healing, who would Incarnate and come back a few centuries later. Augustus Giovanni, a Sorcerer who commanded the spirits of the dead, was instead Visited by Cappadocius in an a scheme to allow the Antediluvian to leave his Fatebindings behind and be free to take on another deity without consequence, but this failed and now both remain as Gods, though they cooperate with clenched teeth and fists. Ventru, noticing the few numbers the Kindred currently possessed, gathered what few of his sane brethren remained to create a power bloc to protect their dwindling worshippers. He also invited the lost three to this discussion, no reply was sent back. Though all parties agreed to his plan in some form or another by 1493, tensions would still exist in the framework of the organization as personalities and responsibilities clashed. None but Ventru would dare invite Tremere and Saulot, or Augustus and Cappadocius, into the same room for anything except the direst emergencies even now.

    The Current State of Affairs: In today’s World, the Antediluvians are trying to create as many Scions as possible in the hopes of new Demigods undergoing Apotheosis. This has been less successful than it perhaps seems, as with the loss of “traditional” child creation, other methods such as breathing life into corpses or bleeding ichor into the mouths of their chosen must suffice. Even then, it sometimes does not take effect, making the Vampire Gods the only Pantheon known for having “failed” Visitations due to chance alone. Why this is the case is yet another topic of hot debate.

    Even as they do this, they must watch out for their fading legacy and their potential descents into Titanhood. Humanity seems almost supernaturally compelled to forget the works of the Kindred, and while this can help at times, it frustrates the efforts of those pursuing Godhood. With the loss of tales and evidence, the Mantles of those who remain are becoming fractured, risking that any divine member could fall and lose their sanity in favor of their Purviews. To make the best of this situation, the Kindred make it a point to try and recruit as many Titanic Scions currently running about onto their side as possible. So long as they do not work to further the goals of their Titanic Patrons any longer, they are embraced with open arms. Conflicts between “uncles” and their “nieces” and “nephews” cannot be afforded in the present situation.

      I don’t think the Antediluvians are a proper pantheon for a few reasons, even if you consider them god level creatures (what I don’t).

      Fist, They don’t work as divine creatures, like the totems do for WtA, they work more as cult leaders than reason for cults to gather. They were never described as gods, maybe as godlike, but not real gods, in opposition to Totens that work as minor gods for the Werewolves.

      They are not immortal from the divine perspective. They don’t age, but they are not really immortal.

      Their level of power is of a demigod, they are powerful, but they are not god level. A 3rd generation can affect a large number of people and have followers to extend their power over countries, but they can’t affect directly that many people. For example, that image of a golem miles tall are beyond the power of any Antedeluvian, even the most physical ones, but demigods face them.

      If I were to crossover them to Scion, I would make them as Denizens (with a Vampire path/calling) and make them legend 8, maybe 9 if you want to make them godlike.

      Well, that’s my opinion, if it matters.

                  I was debating whether the Pantheon Purview of the Antediluvians would be related to drinking blood, but I realized that 1) The Teotl already had their Sacrifice-centric Purview, which might overlap too much to be worth making, and 2) It works just fine for a Motif. Instead, I decided to create something related to the "vanishing" of Kindred history which they believe is tied to the Flood in this conversion.

                  Pantheon Purview: Deluge

                  Motif: “By the drinking of another’s blood can new and strange power emerge.”

                  Innate Power: While your character’s presence does not innately fade from the minds of others, their offenses do to a degree. Whenever your character suffers a negative condition as a result of insulting, harming, or abusing the power of a Fatebound figure, such as a Guide, a Cult or even just a normal Fatebinding, reduce the unit of time used in the duration of the Condition (Season -> Arc -> Episode -> Act -> Scene, etc.). This is simply forgetting your error, rather than forgiving it, as such you cannot benefit from this effect for the same figure again until you have performed an appropriate Apology Deed or suffered the same condition again at its full duration.

                  Cracked Mirror
                  Cost: Imbue 1 Legend
                  Duration: Indefinite
                  Subject: Self
                  Clash: Composure + Legend vs. Cunning + Legend
                  Action: Reflexive

                  Rather than tarnishing the memory of the living, you instead scrub away the data present in the machine. While this Boon is active, impose a +3 Complication on any attempt to use recorded information to identify you, to implicate you as a culprit or to catch whatever you say. Even after you stop using this power, the recording is still useless as long as it captured you while you were using this power. This power Clashes against a computer or device created and maintained by an entity of the same tier or higher, or a computer with a conscious that is of the same tier or higher. Note that this power has no effect on living entities unless they use some sort of electronic media to substitute for a sense.

                  Ties That Don’t Bind
                  Cost: Spend 1 Legend
                  Duration: Instant
                  Subject: One Fatebinding you possess
                  Action: Simple

                  If the creation of a fortuitous tie can help restore the mythical energies a Scion possesses, then surely the opposite is also possible. After spending 1 point of Legend, you reduce the Strength of one Fatebinding you possess (which has less than 5 Strength) by two levels. If this would reduce the Fatebinding to Strength 0, it immediately ends.

                  While Fate is willing to overlook casual use of this power by Heroes, it has become too invested in the stories of higher beings to forgive such a denial of narrative. Whenever a Demigod uses this power, they suffer an Injury Condition as if from a Bashing-tag attack. Whenever a God uses this power, they suffer an Injury Condition as if from a Lethal-tag attack. No effect can negate this backlash, and Health Boxes granted from Hard Armor cannot be filled with this Injury Condition.
