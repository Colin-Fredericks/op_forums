                        Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                        There's also the unavoidable reality that Scion's default cosmological assumptions do emerge from an Indo-European tradition (hence the Titanomachy being the degree of "thing" it is), so even when writers do a great job with the Shen or Netjer or Orisha there's a degree to which they are crammed to fit a paradigm that the Deva (particularly the older Vedic gods) more easily slip into.

                        Without question though 2E is doing a WAY better job with it than 1E.
                        Actually I think they have been firmly doing away with those assumptions, and quite a few other pantheons don't subscribe to that point of view, The Orisha being a good example of this. Indeed it seems the farther away one gets from the middle land mass (i.e. West Asia, Northeast Africa, and Southeast Europe), the more likely the mythology doesn't subscribe to the paradigm of titanomachy from the previews we have gotten so far. What should be telling is what the Teotl and Kami think about it, since they think in a very different way to the Orisha and their priorities and thought process has not been revealed so far. I would guess that the Kami are in a similar position as the Shen regarding their alliance with the Deva but agreeing in principle with the Orisha point of view. But the Teotl I couldn't guess at.

                        But the reality is that there are linguistic limitations when attempting to portray the Netjer, Shen, and Teotl because their is just not enough English material on them to reference or use. There are a lot of Chinese texts on local gods that I know of that the writers could probably never reference since their is no English translation of it. I actually fear that the Kami may be interpreted in an animistic way as defined by Western scholars, but I hope that is not the case.

                                                        Originally posted by Ostarion View Post

                                                        Edit: I see it mentions belligerence but not necessarily titanomachy, I guess we shall see later.
                                                        In most general terms the Deva, Teotl, and Theoi are the Titanomachy hardliners, with the Deva as the groups' self-assumed leadership.

                                                        The Aesir, Netjer, Tuatha, and Shen are moderates. They nominally believe in the Titanomachy but default to keeping the Titans in-line or assimilating them rather than killing or imprisoning them.

                                                        The Orisha and the Manitou are the doves. Not that the Manitou aren't willing to throw down with their own Titans when they have to, but they don't view it as a great cosmic struggle involving other Pantheons. And the Orisha don't even believe in the god/Titan distinction.

                                                        The Yazata are confused because they are Titanomachy hardliners by inclination, but they thought the Deva were Titans! And the Deva think the same thing about them! So it's hard for them to be involved with the militant camp.
