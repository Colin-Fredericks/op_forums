    Has anyone put any intense thought into using Xibalba in their cycles? I like the myths of Xibalba, and was wondering what sorts of ways others have used the 9 or 14 lords, the epic ball courts, the trials, etc etc.

      I did something like that. To make a long story short, one of my heroes' companions died and they had to go to Xibalba to get her back. Technically, they would've been to go to Mictlán instead, but I found Xibalba all the more intriguing so I decided to go with that. Way more bizarre stuff to pull off and what not.

      First, of course, the group had to enter Xibalba, which was an ordeal in itself. They couldn't simply commit suicide, lest they'd end up in totally different underworlds. And while our Anubis-Scion might be able to convince his father to let him go to Xibalba for a rescueing mission, they chances of our Loki-Scion to pull off the same stuff with Hel was... less than minimal. So after some research, they found out that a cave system in Verapaz, Guatemala, was believed to be an actual entrance to Xibalba. Well, it was their best shot, so off they went.

      Actually entering the caves without nosy people was easier than it would seem since the Aztec Scion of the group was a politician with considerable influence. One simple call was enough and they had the whole cave to themselves for a day, more than enough to check every possible nook and cranny with Magic. Finally it turned out that a small carving depicting a human sacrifice doubled as an Axis Mundi, allowing them to enter Xibalba without all the dying business.

      When they entered, they were greeted by darkness, a pungent smell and the faint noise of what sounded like rice being thrown on a giant drum. The noise quickly turned to be out the bustling of the first obstacle, a river full of scorpions, endlessly deep and wide. After that, a river full of blood awaited them and then a river full of fetid pus. Thankfully, they had an Izanagi Scion with them who had dabbled in the Sky Purview and thus were able to cross the rivers safely and without soiling their clothes.

      Next, they entered a section of four roads, all leading in different directions. To make it more clear what was going on, each road had a cloaked figure standing next to it who tried to convince the Heroes that their and only their road would actually lead them to Xibalba. I was looking forward to making this a fun little puzzle, dealing with an extended version of "One gatekeeper lies, one says the trugh" sort of riddles, but I underestimated our Aztec Scion who has a bit of a temper. After about five minutes of beguiling and riddles she simply grabbed one of the ghostly figures ( (The Death Purview really pays out in those situations) and broke its arms, threatening to break every other bone in its body unless it showed them the way. This lead to one of the most unusual rolls I ever did in any of my cycles. How often do you get to roll (Strength + Presence)?

      With the right road, they eventually entered Xibalba itself. Sitting atop a throne a really curious individual greeted them. Or rather, two individuals.
      Hun Came and Vucub Came, the two rulers of Xibalba, greeted them as one, a foul-smelling mountain of flesh with two bodies who were conjoined at their arms and legs. Whatever sentence one of them said the other finished and even though they were clearly mocking the Scions, they did so in perfect brotherly unison. The rest of the Lords of Xibalba looked no better, rashes gaping, flies buzzing around them and their bodies all warped to grotesque proportions, approrpiate to their names and descriptions:

      Xiquiripat and Cuchumaquic were large, broad-chested beings with frog-like faces who were covered in dark red blood which constantly dripped from every pore.
      Ahalpuh and Ahalgana had swollen bellies and limbs, covered in pus or discolored by jaundice respectively.
      Chamiabac and Chamiaholom were thin, skeletal beings who hung on to one staff made of bones and skulls like to a walking frame.
      Ahalmez and Ahaltocob were not seen directly, only two pairs of red eyes glowing with intense lust to kill, from mountains of mold and dust.
      Finally, Xic and Patan were small, imp-like beings with sharp teeth who constantly told each other "jokes" with violent and nihilistic punchlines.

      To quote one of my players outgame: "What the f*** were those people smoking when they came up with their mythologies?"

      Of course, the adventure didn't end here, but I'll have to go through my notes to tell what houses the heroes visited exactly. Memory doesn't serve me well on that one. I also have a set of rules for Pok ta Pok somewhere, seeing as they had to play it. So there's more to come once I get to sort through my notes a bit.

          An I continue, finally!

          Thankfully, the gods decided to go straight for the main course, not pulling their usual game of deception. They gave the group one simple task: Survive three nights at three different houses in Xibalba and we will give you the soul you desire! Of course, those houses weren't simple lodgings.

          The first one was the house of Jaguars, actually a giant jungle filled with equally giant Nemean Jaguars. Those beasts stalked the heroes everywhere they went and wreaked havoc upon them. They didn't get spared, of course and the only one to survive it was a cute fuzzy baby Jaguar who waddled off into the jungle.

          The second was the House of Frost which was a huge mountain that had to be climbed. Or rather the heroes decided to climb it to warm up their limps because the chilling cold really took its toil on them, giving them 1 unsoakable Bashing Damage for every minute they didn't spend heated up in some way. Of course, they also were attacked by restless souls who had been frozen to death in those wastelands. I actually took Mami Watas Water Zombies and adjusted them a bit.

          Finally, the House of Bats hosted them with an actual place to sleep - only catch it was overrun with Camazotz. A particularly fierce one by the name of Huapahuatl decided to try and kill them and used his superior Manipulation to try and make the Scions fight one another. They managed to outwit him, however, and had him ripped apart by his own men. Chaos for the win!

          Naturally, the gods weren't too glad to have lost that easily and thus decided to challenge the heroes to a game of Pok-ta-pok. Since there are no clear rules I had to improvise my own, which looked like this:

          -One court, two rings, the rings aren't sorted by team. Whoever gets the ball through one of the rings, makes one point for his team
          -It is forbidden to touch the ball with your hands, everything else is fair game
          -Before passing the ball through a ring, you have to play it so that it bounces off any wall twice and to another player of your team in a row. Only then you are allowed to score
          -Which team gets to 5 first, wins

          Naturally, with a combination of Epic Dexterity, Chaos and Illusion, our Scions won, if only by a hairs breadth. Of course the ball had been tempered with, leaving it deadly to everyone who touched it and didn't have the Death-Purview.
