    EPISODE FOUR: I’LL HUFF AND I’LL PUFF… (Continued)

    Act Four: Sweet Victory

    Less than an hour later, the Scions are at the athletics field. They chase away some kids hanging out in the bleachers. Marshall spots the places where a sniper could shoot at them and makes sure they are not occupied. The other Scions look for other dirty tricks as best they can. They don’t find any.

    As a final preparation, every Scion carries a bear-shaped squeeze bottle of honey — all but Gene, who carries the actual ambrosia. Exactly why they trust him with it is not entirely clear. But then, this happens a lot with Coyote and his kin.

    Bolvarg shows up on time. A panel truck drives onto the field and stops about 50 yards from the Band. The driver, Lars, gets out. He is another Nordic military man. The Scions note with some concern that he carries grenades as well as a shotgun. He covers the door as a third Nordic soldier (Ole) gets out, rifle at the ready, to cover the exit of Bolvarg himself. In the light of day, the Scions can see the fenrir wears an ornate golden torc. The two thralls spread out a bit from Bolvarg, but they keep their weapons ready.

    Bolvarg gives the Scions a final chance to give him the honey from heaven. They jeer and join battle… and Bolvarg starts his boom box to playing “Final Countdown.”

    Some of the Scions take a while to reach the battle, but everyone gets their moment of spectacle. There were too many stunts to list (or remember — it was a stunt-heavy combat, enabling the characters to spend Legend, Willpower or both on almost every attack.)

    • Lars starts by throwing hand grenades at the advancing Scions, but he cannot score a direct hit. Indeed, Jack deflects one grenade with a thrown rock so it bounces back at Lars. Jack doesn’t score a direct hit, either, and the tough, armored thrall is barely hurt by the blast. Jaeda races past him, though, and she manages to pull a pin on one of his grenades while yelling, “Yoink!” Lars goes boom.

    • Holly, of course, tries using the Evil Eye on Bolvarg. When she does so, the torc briefly glows. Holly guesses that the torc cancelled her spell. (It did, but only in part: Instead of –5 dice on subsequent actions, Bolvarg was penalized only 2 dice.) So, she waits and looks for a chance to coordinate.

    • Ole the rifleman sees Holly trying to curse Bolvarg, and he takes a shot at her… successfully. Fortunately, guns do suck damage against Scions, so Holly is not badly hurt. Marshall spends much of the fight shooting at Ole, whittling him down.

    • Gene, a true Scion of Coyote, decides to keep Bolvarg occupied by annoying him. He races forward using Lightning Sprinter, throws his lasso’s loop into the fenrir’s mouth like a bit, and swings onto Bolvarg’s back, whooping, “Giddy-up!” Bolvarg slams Gene against the truck, injuring him a fair bit, but at least the fenrir can’t bite him and is too pissed to go after the other Scions. As the battle progresses, Gene decides that staying on Bolvarg is not the brightest thing he could do. He rolls forward, wrapping the indestructible Relic lass around Bolvarg’s muzzle as he does so, leaving the troll-wolf briefly unable to bite anyone.

    • Gus uses the opportunity to try grappling Bolvarg. He finds the fenrir is as strong as he is and more dexterous. He can’t hold Bolvarg for long, and this makes him the fenrir’s preferred target once Bolvarg frees his muzzle from the rope. Bolvarg manages to bite Gus twice, but the first time scrapes off the Scion of Sobek’s hide (I rolled super-suck damage for Bolvarg) and the second time was just a nip. Gus mocked the fenrir both times. On the other hand, Gus managed to punch Bolvarg in the face while using one of the pepper spray cans as a fist-load, crushing it in the process, and Bolvarg didn’t like that one bit. Gus also managed to rip away the torc.

    • Jack uses his homemade javelins against Ole. Jaeda moves from the scattered chunks of Lars to Ole and kicks him in the nuts. Marshall shoots from a distance. Ole goes down.

    • With Lars and Ole gone and everyone closing on Bolvarg, Holly gets her chance to coordinate an attack. Bolvarg had already taken a number of wounds. Now everyone lays into him at once, going all-out with stunts, Legendary Deeds and Willpower channeled through Virtues. The results are spectacular. Gus clinches Bolvarg again and does a “Vidar,” wrenching open the fenrir’s jaws so he can’t bite anyone. Jaeda runs straight at Bolvarg, drops to slide under him, and uses the First Flower to gut him from below. Marshall follows, diving to thrust his M-16 (and hands, and head) between the fenrir’s jaws to empty the clip down the fenrir’s throat. This is quite enough to kill Bolvarg, but right as Jaeda and Marshall scramble away, Gene runs over the dying fenrir with the beast’s own truck.

    By now, the Scions know a bit more about titanspawn in general, giants in particular, and fenrir in special particular. Jaeda and Gus skin the fenrir (Jaeda wants to cure the pelt and use it as a bedspread), but first Jaeda cuts out the heart and drains the fenrir’s eitr into some convenient emptied pop bottles. (The others think this trophy is yucky, but we’ll see if anyone is willing to use it.) Gus, Jack and Marshall wrench out the fenrir’s teeth. Gus thinks he’d like to make them into a set of brass knuckles, to make his punches Piercing. After that, the Scions dispose of what’s left of Bolvarg’s corpse.

    Aftermath

    The Scions are left with many strange and violent events to explain away. Barbara starts the process with the story she and Delia cook up to explain the six injured students out in the woods, who babble about serving a giant talking wolf. The two Scions plant the notion that the kids were obviously kidnapped by a drug gang and subjected to some sort of experimental designer drug hallucinogen that left them raving mad. Then a rival gang beat them up, or maybe there was a falling-out among gang members. The other Scions think this sounds pretty good. The six young thralls will spend varying times in the hospital, and more time likely in a mental institution, but they remain a loose end. Thralldom doesn’t wear off.

    It helps that Nils, Lars and Ole all racked up long and violent criminal records during their thralldom to Bolvarg. Everything can be pinned on them, and Lars at least is not going to disagree. The Scions are not willing to execute the other two thralls in cold blood, but Nils and Ole apparently keep clam.

    (Barbara further reports, however, that Leo wanted to take the blame for beating up the six thrall-kids, out of guilt for nearly killing one of them. Delia blew her top at this plan and gave Leo an amazing tongue-lashing. “Little Miss Prettyboots has a mouth like a Marine drill sergeant. Who knew?” Oddly, Leo seemed to like the verbal assault, even as he crumpled.)

    The Scions then call in Laine and Derek. They can see that Laine is a little apprehensive, but she doesn’t try to stop them from restoring Derek’s eyes. Holly is a little put out to learn from her Guide that her Epic Intelligence won’t help her use the ambrosia: It all depends on medical skill and raw divine force. (I.e., the roll is Legend + Medicine.) This is not great for any of the Scions, but Jaeda turns out to have the best chance. She feeds most of the honey to Derek, anoints his eye sockets with the rest, and bleeds on him a bit for good measure. (It’s an Atzlánti thing — her way to channel her Loyalty and spend Legend for a Legendary Deed.) It works, and Derek’s eyes are miraculously restored.

    Which leaves the Scions with another strange event to explain away. Once again, Barbara has a suggestion: Create a story that the earlier report of Derek’s eyes being ripped from his head was media exaggeration, and that actually his eyes were merely injured. Change hospital records to match. The doctors who attended Derek will know better, but who will believe them? Derek’s parents will know better, but will they argue with a miracle? Not if the social Scions persuade them that they don’t want to be the focus of a media circus. This sounds good to Derek, but it’s up to the other Scions to decide whether to back the plan. (Note: They did.)

    Also: Much to Laine’s relief, Derek seems to find her as beautiful as before. The Scions still haven’t said that Laine was the monster who maimed him in the first place. And Holly’s Guide commends the Scions on finding a good use for the honey from heaven, after it has caused so much trouble over the centuries.

    On a not-so-warm and fuzzy note, Marshall stakes a claim to the Trophies from Bolvarg. He argues that since he and Jaeda dealt the most damage and were the ones who actually killed the monster, they should receive the Trophies and disburse them as they see fit. It is up to the PCs to decide how to respond to this claim.

                            Gene's player was not available for the next adventure. So, I made that the center of the adventure. I'd already read the "Coyote Stories" section from a book on Pacific Northwest native mythology, so that was convenient as well. No specific myths were referenced, just a general style of trickster tale.

                            EPISODE FIVE: COYOTE STORIES

                            Old Business

                            After the battle with Bolvarg, Marshall made himself annoying by questioning the division of the spoils. He argues that he and Jaeda should receive all the fenrir’s trophies (including the enchanted torc) to distribute as they choose, because they struck the killing blows. The other Scions aren’t too keen on his high-handed attitude, but they reluctantly agree they need some system to decide who gets loot from their adventures. They agree that if one person clearly defeated a particular foe, that Scion gets the enemy’s stuff. Where a trophy is highly divisible, the people involved in the victory get roughly equal shares, if they want them. Most of the Scions also point out that by Marshall’s reasoning, Holly should receive the trophies because they couldn’t have slain Bolvarg without her coordinating their attacks. Holly, however, doesn’t want much.

                            The Scions have nine doses of Bolvarg’s heart’s-blood. Some of the Scions don’t want any of it (they don’t see much use for an exceptionally yucky Berserker Potion). Jaeda and Marshal get two doses (they inflicted a lot of damage, it’s blood, they’re Atzlánti, it fits their idiom). Gene gets two doses, since he was the first to engage Bolvarg. Holly, Jack and Gus take one dose each.

                            Gus ripped the torc off Bolvarg, so he holds it for now.

                            Gus also receives Bolvarg’s fangs. While he has visions of gloves with ripping claws, a la Wolverine, he finds that isn’t practical. He can, however, fit the fangs into a set of brass knuckles that give his punches +2 damage and make damage Piercing (against all soak, because they’re a relic weapon).

                            Jaeda claims Bolvarg’s pelt to turn into a rug, or maybe a bedspread. Nobody argues.

                            Marshall clearly wanted to get more. The socially perceptive Scions can tell that he feels uncomfortable and under-appreciated as part of the nascent Band. He expected to lead because of his parentage and pre-Visitation combat training, and he isn’t. On the other hand, he doesn’t have an established leader to follow, either. He also hasn’t been the clearly superior fighter that he thought he’d be. (Guns just aren’t so useful against the supernatural.) Atzlánti warriors expected to receive promotions and honors for their victories. Plus, well, he’s a jerk. But he accepts the settlement of Bolvarg’s trophies.

                            In other matters, Holly works on acquiring a kevlar trench coat (chic and bulletproof!) and a Wild Kingdom-issue trank pistol. She also builds a dossier on Leo and his mortal parents. As expected, they are very conservative members of the very evangelical and very political local megachurch, the Glory of God Ministry. (It’s the sort of place that shows movies of American fighter jets during the church service.) Mr. Lefebvre doesn’t just put a fish symbol in his Yellow Pages ad, he advertises specifically as a “Christian Electrician.” Poor Leo.

                            Act One: Family Matters

                            A few weeks have passed since the events of “I’ll Huff And I’ll Puff…” The Scions have planted the story that Derek’s eyes were wounded, not removed. They have changed hospital records and convinced many key people to doubt their own memories. Derek, his parents and Laine know better, but they are all grateful for the miracle. Derek rejoins the swim team; he and Laine are still dating and seem very much in love. Meanwhile, the six students whom Bolvarg turned into thralls undergo therapy at a secure facility.

                            The Scions feel pretty good about themselves. So, naturally it’s time for something bad to happen. Their parents come to town — all on the same day. At first, this doesn’t seem like it will spoil their Saturday morning…

                            GUS gets a call from his dad Sobek, saying he’s passing through the area on the way to a job and to meet him at the liquor store in Etna. Sobek quizzes Gus about school, friends, how he’s adjusting to being a Scion, and progress in forming a Band. He also asks Gus if he wants something from the store: Gus asks for two of the strongest-tasting liquors (Jalapeño Vodka and Garlic Schnapps, or something like that). They continue chatting in the parking lot.

                            Gary Vetterson cruises by in his convertible. He spots Gus and does a fast turn into the parking lot and accosts the Scion. He demands to know where Gene is, or where Reese is, that he still owes the Scion of Coyote a beat-down for the events of Homecoming, and he is not letting some filthy Scion make time with his sister!

                            Gus plays it cool. He swears he doesn’t know Gene’s or Reese’s location, and he wouldn’t protect Gene if he did. He then offers Gary a swig from his bottles — both at once, of course, if he’s a real man. Gary accepts the challenge, takes a long pull from both at once, and smiles. He tells Gus that he ought to try drinking something strong, not this Kool-Aid. Which means Gus has to drink them together too. The supernatural jocks part, having established their mutual machismo.

                            As Gary drives away, Sobek wants more information on Gary. He didn’t see Gary as a giant. He’s never heard of giants hiding their nature from Gods that well, but he promises to ask Thoth about it. Sobek also asks why Gary isn’t dead. Gus says that for a giant, Gary and Reese aren’t bad. Finally, Sobek asks if Gus would take occasional small jobs for the pantheon if anything should turn up in the region. Gus says he’d be glad to. Sobek drives off to do wetwork in Russia. Gus thinks a moment and decides he’d better tell the others that his divine parent was in town. Oh, and Gary’s on the war-path over Gene and Reese.

                            JAEDA gets a surprise visitor when her mother Tlazoltéotl walks into the trailer Jaeda shares with her father, Mateo. Who is stunned. Tlazoltéotl ejects Mateo for this mother-daughter talk. Her gone-to-seed former lover now clearly disgusts her.

                            Tlazoltéotl quizzes Jaeda about school, friends, how she’s adjusting to being a Scion, and progress in forming a Band. They are briefly interrupted by a phone call from Grace, one of the kids in Drama Club with Gene. Grace says Gene didn’t show up for a read-through of a play and asks if Jaeda knows where he is? Jaeda does not.

                            They are interrupted again when they hear Mateo say, “Hey, you can’t go in —“ SMACK, and a bump against the side of the trailer. And then Nergal walks in. He wants Jaeda to bed his son Leo, and offers to pay. Tlazoltéotl, however, tells Jaeda never to let a man think he can buy and own her. (A man paying for the privilege of sex is another matter.) Nergal sneers and says that’s rich, coming from her. Turning back to Jaeda, he says that if she doesn’t want money, he can pay in other ways — relics, slaves, etc.

                            Tlazoltéotl does not like being brushed off — and when Tlazoltéotl gets mad, she gets sexy. Really sexy. She turns her Epic Appearance up full-blast and aims it at Nergal. His eyes flame with lust, and he reaches for her. (Jaeda finds herself thinking impure thoughts about her own mother, just from the sidesplash.) Tlazoltéotl says that this time, she didn’t tamp down her divinity. She pouts prettily at Nergal, strokes his face and coos, “Oh, go down for me, honey,” and pushes. The Lord of Lions folds like a cheap suit until he lies flat on his back on the floor. Tlazoltéotl puts her foot on his face with her spike heel in his mouth. “This is how you deal with men like him,” she tells Jaeda, as she dials down her Epic Appearance again. (Jaeda thinks, “I have got to learn how to do that.”)

                            Nergal exits, fuming. Before he goes, though, he tells Jaeda that his offer stands… and that he is better to have as a friend than an enemy.

                            Jaeda worries at that. As they bring the unconscious Mateo into the trailer, though, Tlazoltéotl assures her that while Nergal might have some of his minions harass her, he won’t attack her himself. First, his pantheon keeps him busy on the front lines, fighting the Titans. Second, in this time of crisis his pantheon can’t afford to open a war on a second front against the Atzlánti — and that’s what’ll happen, if he murders an Atzlánti Scion. Her mother then tells Jaeda to bed Leo Lefebvre if she wants him or thinks it’s the right thing to do, but it should be her choice, not influenced by Nergal.

                            JACK lounges around home, enjoying a late breakfast while his mother goes shopping. A sudden gust of wind shakes the house and the roar of a motorcycle engine resounds from the kitchen. It’s Hunter S. Thompson, a.k.a. Lono, astride the sort of insanely fast Italian motorcycle that nobody’s crazy enough to drive unless they’re hopped up on amphetamines, or a God. (The real Hunter S. Thompson loved them.)

                            Lono wants breakfast. Jack finds the tag end of a box of Wheaties; Lono pours the cereal in a bowl and douses it with whiskey from his hip flask. While they eat, Lono quizzes Jack about school (“How do you like math?”), friends, football, how Jack’s adjusting to being a Scion, and progress at forming a Band. He notes that Jack has never consulted the Polynesian warrior whom Lono gave him as a Guide. Would Jack prefer some other Guide? Lono insists that Jack needs a Guide to teach him the skills he’ll need in the Atua, both for fighting the Titans and establishing his place in the political hierarchy. (Academics for laws and ceremonies, Melee for close combat, etc.) To Jack, this all adds up to “More Homework,” and worse, “A Career Plan.”

                            They are interrupted by a call from Drama Club guy Josh, looking for Gene. Jack can offer no help.

                            Lono finishes his cereal. Jack promises he’ll invoke his Guide and try learning some new skills. Lono mounts the motorcycle, guns the engine, and vanishes in a blast of wind that makes a mess of the entire house.

                            HOLLY enjoys another excellent Saturday morning breakfast cooked by Moominmamma when she receives a text from her mother, Louhi. Holly is to present herself at Gate 6 of Sea-Tac International Airport at 11 a.m., sharp. Holly is there on time. A large, unsmiling man in a very well-tailored suit escorts her to the private jet of Louisa Northland, owner and CEO of Northland Holdings LLC, the Witch of Wall Street, officially worth around a cool billion, actually worth at least ten times as much.

                            Over wine, cheese and caviar, Louhi quizzes Holly about school, friends, how she’s adjusting to being a Scion, and progress at acquiring minions to exploit. The Crone of Pohjola finds Holly’s answers satisfactory. She is also intrigued by the unusual items of power the Scions have collected. Louhi offers her daughter a trade: a single use of a very special assassination service in return for each relic of interest that Holly provides. She would like to start with the Gorgon Blood and Bolvarg’s torc. Holly says she’ll see what she can do. Louhi tells Holly to expect more supernatural creatures to visit Avalon, and more chances to acquire relics: “The vortex of Legend is building, as I foresaw, and it draws in more of itself.” As she leaves, Holly sees Nils and Ole (Bolvarg’s surviving thralls), strapped to gurneys, being wheeled to the plane. The Crone of Pohjola has use for experienced, supernaturally-touched killers, and a little thing like the U.S. legal system won’t keep them out of her hands.

                            Driving back to Avalon, Holly receives a call she did not expect. It’s Gary, flushed, angry and mortified at asking a Scion for anything, wanting to know if Holly knows where Reese is. Holly doesn’t. Gary presses: He knows that Holly can find out where Reese is. Holly admits that she can. (She does not bother asking how Gary knows this. Gene knew, and it is no stretch to imagine that Gene might have told Reese, and Gary might have learned that way.) Holly tells Gary to meet her at her house. Along the way, Holly receives the phone and text messages from Gus, Jack and Jaeda, telling of their divine parents’ visits and, btw, where’s Gene? Holly (no fool) realizes that Something Is Up.

                                EPISODE FIVE: COYOTE STORIES (Continued)

                                Act Two: Rendezvous With… No, Not Destiny

                                At her house, Holly throws Gary off balance by immediately agreeing to help him find Reese. (And he had some good bluster and threats prepared.) She also gets background: Reese was off to meet friends of hers at Hooverdale Mall, but she didn’t show up. They called him; he called everyone else Reese knows; he suspects Gene is having his vile Scionish way with her.

                                Holly calls the others and has them join her and Gary. She also calls Marshall and gets a quick, strained, “Not a good time now!” Barbara is also occupied. She doesn’t bother calling Delia or Leo. Gary doesn’t like any of this, but he’s over a barrel.

                                The gang piles into assorted cars to start the trail in Etna. Holly casts Ariadne’s Thread. They follow Reese’s path almost to the mall, where it detours off to a bar (where the barman saw Reese, Gene and two Perfectly Legitimate IDs) and, in time, to the El Sleazo Motel (Cheap Hourly Rates — Magic Fingers in Every Room). Gary’s fury grows. Some of the Scions feel less respect for Gene for taking Reese to such a place.

                                The motel room door isn’t locked. Well, it isn’t locked after Gus pulls it out of the wall. Inside are the incongruous remains of a very elegant and romantic luncheon (grilled sole, a bottle of fine wine, a box of Godiva chocolates, etc.) There are also Reese and Gene on the Magic Fingers bed, just about to get to the good part.

                                Gary roars with rage and leaps forward before anyone else can act. (He used a Legendary Deed and channeled Willpower to absolutely definitely win the Join Battle roll.) Gary also briefly flickers into his 8’ tall, ice giant form as he pulls Gene from the bed and slams him down with all his strength across the lunch table. The table becomes splinters, which is to be expected. The grilled sole becomes half-eaten Fishwiches, the wine becomes Night Train, and the chocolates are just Reese’s Pieces. (Everyone mentally groans at the pun.) And Gene turns into a coyote who rolls and is out the door fast as lightning, yipping in panic.

                                Yup, Gene got a visit from his divine parent too… and Coyote decided to get some of his son’s girlfriend. Once she realizes what happened, Reese is nearly as angry as Gary.

                                Only, what happened to Gene?

                                Gary doesn’t care. Reese does, some, but lets Gary talk her into going home.

                                Holly sighs as she realizes she must spend another Legend point to track Gene. His path starts from his home and ends in the Avalon High parking lot. More precisely, it ends at the side of the parking lot in an area of beauty bark and some scrubby little pines.

                                There are tracks. The Scions examine them, apply a little Epic Attribute super-sleuthing, and reconstruct the events: Gene and a man wearing cowboy boots came to this place. The man walked several paces across the beauty bark and then suddenly went to one knee, scuffing the bark. Gene stepped forward. Then, Gene’s tracks stopped — but re-start from the place the cowboy boots were, and leave the bark. But Holly knows from Ariadne’s Thread that those are not really Gene’s tracks. From what happened at the motel, they must be Coyote’s, heading out to intercept Reese for a little naughty nookie. The real Gene seems to have vanished.

                                Jack decides that Holly shouldn’t do all the mystic mumbo-jumbo and activates Sense Tapu. He finds that yup, there’s something still magical at the point where Gene’s trail stops. In fact, he knows exactly what it is, because there’s a magical prohibition: It’s a portal to somewhere else, but it’s closed and not to be opened except by the right people, in the right way.

                                Jaeda wonders if she can’t use the First Flower to re-open the portal because, well, it’s magic and it cuts stuff. So she stabs the ground where Gene’s footsteps end. To her surprise, it works. Whatever tapu Coyote put on his dimensional portal, it isn’t stronger than a blade that sacrificed an entire pantheon.

                                The Scions have come this far. Down the rabbit hole they go!

                                    EPISODE FIVE: COYOTE STORIES (Continued)

                                    Act Three: The Even Wilder West

                                    The Scions land in another copse of scrubby pine trees, but these are older than the ones at school. The air is also hotter and thinner; the sun is brighter, though it seems to be the same time of day. Looking around, they see mountainous country covered with more pine and chaparral. Gus looks for tracks and sees, yup, Gene was here. He mooched around a while, then wandered off to explore his surroundings.

                                    Jack, meanwhile, checks with Know Tapu to find how they get back. The Boon seems to work, but the answer is insane: They need to climb up a hollow reed. (Must be a big reed…) He and Holly put their Obscure Trivia Master heads together, though, and remember that various Native American cultures say their ancestors came to the present, Fourth World from their former habitation in the previous, subterranean Third World — now the Underworld — by climbing up the inside of a hollow reed. To get back, the Scions must find a hollow reed, stick it in the air, and invoke their divinity (i.e., spend Legend) to recapitulate the myth.

                                    The Scions follow Gene’s trail. It soon intersects the tracks of a group of horses, at least some of them with riders. These people seem to have forced Gene to travel with them. After another few miles, though, the trail of hoofprints becomes a chaotic muddle, suggesting a struggle — that left blood. They don’t have to wonder what happened, though, when a Native American-looking dryad steps out of a tree and asks the Scions who they are. They are not real people (i.e., Native American — though the dryad isn’t sure about Jaeda). And, they feel sort of godlike but not like real, proper Gods. As the suspicious and hostile spirit eyes them, wolves and bears stalk out of the trees and bushes.

The Scions tell the giants to surrender, but the Sarsens are too angry or too stupid to do so. So, Jack pounds on the First Flower’s hilt with his war-club, and at last Dave collapses on the floor and begins crumbling into mud and gravel. Once Reese has fled with Acadia and Gary is unconscious, the Band finishes off Don in the same way. Gus doesn’t mind this, because the Sarsens initiated lethal force by throwing the sarcophagus-lid and did not surrender when they had the chance. He lets Gary live, though, as the frost giant was unarmed and never tried to move past fisticuffs.

                                                The shaft in the sarcophagus leads down to an identically-shaped crypt about 25 feet underground. This vault, however, is not a fake like the one above. Inscriptions in Greek, Cuneiform, Mayan and Egyptian Hieroglyphics cover the walls, interspersed with images of various gods. There’s another sarcophagus, though the designs are Mayan rather than Egyptian. A few stone blocks of the wall are pushed a little out of place, cracking a few paintings and inscriptions, while a few statuettes of gods fell over.

                                                A clay figure — a shabti — starts talking to the Scions. It introduces itself as the ghost of Michael Balboa, Scion of Mictlantecuhtli. Michael notes how much Gus looks like his old comrade Randolph. He also asks what the hell the Scions are doing, bringing the key so close to the gate.

                                                Michael explains that the only way his Band of Demigods could seal the cult’s gate was for one of them to sacrifice himself and become its eternal guardian. Michael had the right supernatural powers, so he volunteered. It’s his body in the sarcophagus. His corpse became the portal to Xibalba; it must stay there, undisturbed. Michael spends most of his time battling the spectres that try to slip through and can leave that duty only for short times. The damage to the crypt weakened the wards that assist him, though, and so several spectres managed to slip past him and into the World. The spectres were looking for the key. Fortunately, the Scions found it first, right where the earlier Band hid it.

                                                The only way to stop more spectres from forcing their way past is to repair the vault. Michael feels pretty sure that if the stone blocks are pushed back into position, the paintings touched up and the Greek, Mayan, Mesopotamian and Egyptian funerary prayers recited by Scions of the proper pantheons, the wards will repair themselves and lock the portal shut again.

                                                Gus and Jaeda can handle the Pesedjet and Atzlánti rites. The only Dodekatheon and Anuna available, however, are Delia Bright and Leo Lefebvre. The Scions call Leo: The Scion of Nergal isn’t happy at receiving phone calls in the wee hours of the morning that he can not explain to his mortal parents, but he is easily persuaded to help keep damned souls in Hell. (He’s not quite so happy that they want him to perform an Anuna rite.) Delia is a harder case. To the argument that the Xibalba-gate is a threat to the entire World, she retorts that then the other Scions should be willing to give her just about anything for her help. The Band bargains her down to Gus and Jack playing below their full capacity for the remaining few games of the football season: not throwing the game or humiliating themselves, but letting Warren shine as the star player while the college recruiters are watching. Gus very reluctantly agrees.

                                                The Scions spend an hour or so repairing the tomb chamber. Gus, Jaeda, Leo and Delia perform the funerary rites. Jack and Holly confirm that the wards are indeed repairing themselves.

                                                That still leaves the problem of what to do with the Vettersons and Acacia. They could still re-open and wreck the crypt to let spectres loose again. Even if the Scions were willing to kill all three, what if they already told someone else?

                                                Conferring with Michael, the Scions decide to fill the shaft with cement so the only way down would be to dig in with heavy machinery — which could not be done quickly or inconspicuously. Gus and Holly have money: They hire a cement mixer to prepare a truckload before dawn and deliver it to them, no questions asked.

                                                Jaeda volunteers to take Gary home before he regains consciousness. The Scions load him into her car, then dump him on the Vetterson front porch. Jaeda rings the doorbell, jumps in her car and speeds away.

                                                Finally, the Scions quickly fashion a copy of Sir Dance-A-Lot’s sword for the display case. Hopefully, no one will realize that the silly prop of wood and foil is not the same silly prop that was in the case the day before. What to do with the real sword is a problem they leave for later.

The Knights are undefeated; the Titans lost only once, to the Knights. In the week before the big game, Coach Reagan is near frantic with anxiety, driving the players to practice longer and harder, so intense is his desire to finally win the championship from the Titans. Nevertheless, Gus and Jack find time for a few other things; so do the other Scions.

The two young hrimthurssar also must have mixed Virtues — some Dark Virtues of the Titans, some Divine Virtues. This probably makes them less reflexively malevolent, but no less dangerous.

He asks Gus, again, if he’s heard anything from the disappeared Sheena or can remember any hint that something was wrong with her, that she had enemies, etc. Gus says he does not. His answers seem to satisfy Mr. Delisle. Sheena’s father says, though, that he remains convinced there is something to be discovered in Sheena’s disappearance, and he isn’t waiting for the cops: He’s hiring private detectives. If anyone harmed his little girl, he will find them, and they will pay for it.

He has no better luck than Jaeda in persuading the Scion of Nergal that being part pagan deity is a good thing; but he tells Leo that whatever Nergal really is, God or demon, Leo doesn’t have to be like his father. He can assert himself and be his own man. Leo’s resolve hardens and says he has an idea how to do this.

The dog’s barking woke her up. She looked outside to see what riled the dog and saw it killed by what she insists was a leopard. Animal Control says it must have been a cougar (they sometimes come out of the nearby hills and forests and attack pets, and no big cats have escaped from local zoos). Jack moseys over to Mrs. Wickersham’s and applies Instant Investigator. He comes away convinced that was no cougar, and that the putative leopard consciously slew the dog. He can’t tell why, though.

Do they understand? Warren does. Once they leave Coach’s office, he asks Jack if he’s coming along to prank Etna High.

Along the way, though, Warren makes no secret that he resents being overshadowed by Gus and Jack, and he thinks they must be juicing — some new designer steroid. Warren also saw how Horndog Bob and the other thralls came back to school significantly bigger and stronger than they were before. He tells Jack to ‘fess up and say what he and Gus are using, because Warren thinks he’ll find out anyway.

Warren is bummed despite Jack’s attempts to buck him up with praise of Warren’s hard training and discipline. Warren can see that no matter how hard he trains, he will never equal Jack and Gus. (What are they, mutants? Aliens? “Something like that. Does it matter?” No, not really.) But, Jack stresses that he and Gus will be team players. In fact, they will do their best to keep Warren central to the big game.

Not that he intends to fight with a knife, but he wants to know how to defend himself. They meet in the school gym after classes. Jaeda shows Leo the proper ways to evade and disarm. While they practice, though, Leo suddenly twists to hurl his back against the First Flower. Jaeda tries to pull the knife away, but the God-slaying dagger sinks deeply into Leo’s back. Leo falls, almost mortally wounded.

Before shock sets in, Leo explains that he was trying to cut the tattoo Nergal put on his back as a way to sever the lion-demon’s connection to him. He thinks it worked but he… kind of… overdid it… ow!

Yeah, it only looked like it went clear through his body and punctured a lung…

Leo can’t stay wounded forever; he has to take the threat seriously.
