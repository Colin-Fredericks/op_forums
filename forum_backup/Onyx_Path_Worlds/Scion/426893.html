              A lot of inspiration out there.

              It's not like original Star Trek didn't do Greek gods. Dr Who did some riffs on Egyptian myths. Patricia Kennealy Morrison did a series (or two?) of space borne Celtic mythology. I didn't like it very much, but she kept getting book contracts.

              I think you could spin the Egyptian and Aztec and not go too Ancient Astronauts on it. I remember some sci fi back when I was kid in a solar system where the planets were named for the Egyptian gods and the story was wrapped around some of the stories. I wish I could remember who wrote it. It was VERY long ago and the patina of years has fallen across it.

              There's ALWAYS Zelazny's Lord of Light. Among other other sci-fi books that feature gods or beings who have taken on godhood.

              I don't think there is a pantheon that can't be taken into space in a compelling way.

                                              Definitely the Vikings. They are all about melodrama. They were sailors and pirates, something that suits the ideal of travel between planets and maybe finding new things, if not they can be smugglers with ease. At home individual heroes were well known throwing themselves all in and going against whole armies but also being sneaky.Viking heroes also love melodrama, they eat it for breakfast, Lunch, and Dinner. I mean Brynhilda is a figure of Opera for a reason almost seems wrong to leave them out.

                                              My next bet would be the Yazata due to all the adventures and Good vs Evil that they nail so well. They also love stars. If not Greeks, they have all the family drama of every myth ever, also adventurous and also pretty well known by any group.

                                              China would be better for a lighter side. I mean it is all built up. Journey to the West could be made into a space opera. You have this government like a federation (Celestial Bureaucracy is almost how starfleet might describe themselves lol), you have trouble makers who we cheer for as they steal everything and good hearted marauders. You have prisoners traveling around doing good deeds to shave time off a long sentence, I mean there is a lot to do with it.

                                              The Force was kinda inspired by Buddhism even while very different from them, as written many are even converts so Celestial Bureacracy and Vikings, preferably against each other (but just a bit the federation is allied with those darn Klingons) and Yazata always being the group that is unaffiliated because they are too busy doing outlandishly cool things for apparently no reason, but also explain and mentor the heroes (the Jedis) any drama you need just milk Viking and Greek mythology for all its worth. (I mean Oedipus invented secretly a family member to the Soap Opera line up, and no one does needlessly star crossed lovers or people dying tragically and then coming back like the Vikings)
