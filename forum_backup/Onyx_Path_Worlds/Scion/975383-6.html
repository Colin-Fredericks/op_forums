            Brigid not having Forge was a mistake on the part of myself and the author, since it was only decided to be included as a core pantheon rather late.

            Some Gods have more Purviews than others; this is a function of both total Boon investment into Purviews and Legend. Some Gods have the bare minimum for a Purview to be "associated" with them; others have every single Boon in the Purview, and then some. I think, currently, Sun Wukong has a truly ridiculous number of Purviews associated with him, but in the current playtest build this means he doesn't have investment beyond the Demigod level in anything but the Epics, and those just barely.

                                                            Originally posted by Neall View Post
                                                            He's one of the ones mentioned most often, yes. Important?
                                                            Important? Check this out! Goibniu was a badass war hero for one of the most important events in Tuatha history.

                                                            1. When Nuada gives Lugh the Kingship to help with the impending Fomori invasion, Lugh immediately holds a secret meeting for an entire year. Who does he invite? The Dagda, Ogma, Goibniu, and Dian Cecht.

                                                            2. They talk about what they can bring to the war effort. Goibniu makes the most impressive boast -- he guarantees that he will outfit an entire divine army for seven years, replacing every spear that breaks with weapons that "will never miss" and that "no skin which it pierces shall taste life afterwards."

                                                            Then he talks smack about Dolb, the Fomorian Smith, and points out the enemy smith can't do any of that.

                                                            3. After the fighting starts, the Fomori realize they are losing the entire war for two major reasons. Dian Cecht is going around ressurecting almost everyone who dies on the Tuatha side, and Goibniu is replacing their broken swords, spears, and javelins with super lethal murder versions. In fact, they even talk about him needing only "three turns" / blows to craft a weapon.

                                                            These two almost duohandedly turn the tide of the entire Fomorian war.

                                                            4. Ruadan is an assassin and spy for the Fomorians, and he is handed the job of murdering one of the Tuatha. He could have picked anyone, but he decides that Goibniu is the single most important person to the entire war effort. Even over Dian Cecht.

                                                            5. Even more badass? Ruadan gets his hands on one of Goibniu's own murder spears and impales him. Goibniu not only rips it out of his own chest but launches it at Ruadan and kills him with it like something out of a Hollywood action movie. Goibniu then walks off getting impaled and gets healed by Dian Cecht.

                                                            6. I just wanted to throw this in because Irish Mythology has such fun weapons: After the war is won, Goibniu gets sad about his cheating wife and makes Nes, a weapon that causes anyone struck by it to either erupt into boils, or be burned alive like fire.

                                                            7. Some time later, Manannan is trying to help the Tuatha get used to their defeat at the hands of the Milesians. He calls in a favor and asks Goibniu to cook the feast.

                                                            Goibniu always takes things up to 11. So what does he do?

                                                            He creates food and beer that not only make the remaining Tuatha AGELESS but UNABLE TO DIE! Which would have been crazy helpful previously when Nuada got killed, but oh well.

                                                            8. If you like connecting the Tuatha to more modern religious figures, Goibinu continues on as Saint Gobban, or as the folklore figure The Gobán Saor.

                                                            The Gobán Saor does things like give obscure wisdom to his son to get his son to find a wife through selling a sheep. He builds the world's greatest castle quickly for an English King, tricking the king into sending his heir away to The Gobán Saor's house to be kept hostage to make sure nothing nefarious happened to him (it was tradition to kill your architect so they couldn't build anything better for someone else says the story). He built five castles of Kings or great chiefs, could make a spear shaft before you counted to five, make the spear head in three strokes, shows someone how to make a roof if you only have three joists, takes a king's challenge to build a bridge that needs no pegs or nails to hold it together, completing it with a few sticks and a hatchet, with the bridge becoming stronger the more weight that is put on it.

                                                            Definitely one of the heavyweight Tuatha.
