    Mother Goose

    05-01-2015, 11:50 AM
    If Baga Yaga is a Goddess, then Mother Goose is at least as much. She's been around Western Europe for a very long time. Marina Warner says she's probably Saint Anne, Christ's grandmother. She's very likely the Grandmother of some god or gods even if she isn't Christ's grandmother. We know that she rides ganders to travel the world and owls guard her door. German peasents once said she caused the snow by cleaning her bed. She has worked her little rhymes into many languages and most homes, for most people they're the first poetry they ever hear. She's a subtle goddess, and very likely a powerful one.

    I think Mother Goose probably has Epic Charisma, Epic Manipulation, and Epic Perception, these would help her get her rhymes out there and work her will on Mothers and Children, the people who seem to be her focus. Animal (Birds), Magic, Psychopomp. and Sky seem very likely too. Given how common the moon is in Mother Goose Rhymes, I'd bet on her having that as well. given how often old ladies are treated violently in Mother Goose Rhymes I'd guess Epic Stamina belongs on the list.

    What's your take on Mother Goose? A western rival to Baga Yaga? Or maybe a Western Mask of the same? Could she be a well known Goddess in disguise? Is she a member of a well-known Pantheon? Could she be a lone survivor of a past pantheon?

    Given how long she's been around and her stated concerns Epic Charisma, Epic Manipulation, Epic Perception, Epic Stamina, Animal (Birds), Moon, Sky, Psychopomp, Health, Magic, and Mystery, doesn't seem like an outrageous list.

    What's your list?

    Does Mother goose still take Scions?

      Fairy tales and nursery rhymes are riddled with the remnants of myths and legends. Sometimes from lines of actual descent, other times from recapitulations of the same thematic elements in a culturally similar form. Such as, whether or not you believe that Little Red Riding Hood is a re-capitualtion of a "lost" myth involving Fenris, Iduna and Tyr, it's still fun to think about using it in a Scion context. (Particularly since it adds something to the lexicon of all three, particularly Iduna & Tyr). If you dig into some of the books of the Bible, particularly the Psalms and Job, you get a really different take on Yahweh as a monster killer, a la Marduk.

      If Mother Goose is a face of something older (and she very well could be, probably is), I would want to tie her to that figure, whether she is a well-known figure in mufti or someone so old we don't have any real idea of who was worshiping her. If I went out a limb, I'd say you could tie her to the Hallstat Culture that developed into the La Tene (which is the archaeological manifestation of the original Celtic peoples). They have a TON of duck and goose statutes associated with ritualized contexts. And lots of quirky ideas about why they're there... "water fowl are transitional beings associated with both earth and sky... yada, yada". So I'd look at the Celtic folks to see if you can find her.... Maybe she's some version of Rhiannon (or, more accurately, something much older of which Rhiannon is another iteration)? Maybe she ties into the numerous swan mythic figures (and the ton of related fairy tales)

          Originally posted by Ajax View Post
          Such as, whether or not you believe that Little Red Riding Hood is a re-capitualtion of a "lost" myth involving Fenris, Iduna and Tyr, it's still fun to think about using it in a Scion context. (Particularly since it adds something to the lexicon of all three, particularly Iduna & Tyr).
          I've never heard of the story linking to Tyr and Fenris or Iduna, but I have heard it linked to my own namesake, the Úlfhéðnar ( http://deadliestfiction.wikia.com/wiki/Ulfhednar ). A rampaging man wolf stomping throughout the forest and assaulting travelers (In the original, his intentions with miss Hood are less than pleasant and far more Man like....) is more or less an actual religious tradition of the Úlfhéðnar where they would remove themselves from society and live as bandits for a period of time as an initiation rite. Its easy to see how the story is a christianised tale (Perhaps originally a warning) of something that was actually realistic to the inhabitants of dark age Germany that was over time turned light hearted and bastardized into its present form.

          In a setting in which the older gods actually do exist as a categorical fact, I would be careful about using folk tales in an unaltered state as setting cannon for the simple reason that it was often something else entirely, and really steps on the toes of established historical deities and religions, and Gods in Scion are not created through an act of belief. That said, it wouldin't be hard to make them all true in some metaphysical way ala American Gods if you really wanted.

          As for Mother Goose herself, ( http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Goose#Identity ) its apparently thought that she was an actual person (again, thats why I wouldin't use folk tales and nursery rhymes) but you could reasonably turn her into a scion or a byname of a goddess. Ajax's idea for a Celtic connection is a fair one, I might suggest connecting her to Freya (Or Frigg) of the Aesir, both motherly figures with lots of goose symbolism who often advised Men and gods.
