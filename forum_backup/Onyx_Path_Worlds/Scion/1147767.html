    How to make Mobsters a threat to Scions

    10-07-2017, 10:57 AM
    This a continuation on my Punisher Scion thread. If i get Scion 2ed i plan on doing a story-line which involves this character as a NPC and his " Holy Crusade" against the city's Mafia Families. My question is this, how can you make the Mafia a threat to Scions?

      If you're playing at Hero level, they should still be a threat. Even in 1e, a squad of veteran soldiers were still dangerous for a Scion to tackle. Plus, the title "Hero" goes well with the crime fighting aspect you're trying to convey in the character. Street level goons probably aren't going to put up too much of a fight; but as they work their way up the ranks of the mob, they'll have to start encountering the professionals who know what they're doing.


      In fact, you might say that some crime lords are part of a Titan cult and have "legendary" hitmen specifically hired to kill Scions.

          Originally posted by Killerclown View Post
          This a continuation on my Punisher Scion thread. If i get Scion 2ed i plan on doing a story-line which involves this character as a NPC and his " Holy Crusade" against the city's Mafia Families. My question is this, how can you make the Mafia a threat to Scions?
          This question is a bit tricky since we don't know the 'power scale' that will be present in Scion 2e. By that I mean just exactly how powerful a Legend 2 Scion is compared to a Mortal, for example. Assuming that it will be somewhere roughly similar to 1e though is probably a safe bet, but, you know. Keep in mind all of this is speculative and could be entirely wrong depending on how Scions measure up to Mortals in 2e.

          In the Physical department, mortal Mafias could present a challenge to a Scion by being numerous. A Scion is powerful, they can rip, maim, sunder, destroy at a whim, but they can't be everywhere. (With an exception to that statement given to Conall Cernach.) A Mafia family could present a really legitimate threat to a Scion by going to ground, and simply presenting too many fronts for the Scion to deal with at the same time. Target the Scion's family, and friends. Put pressure on any local community members the Scion sees as allies, swap to operating in a cell format so no one who the Scion can grab on the street level will know where the upper levels of the family is. Ambush the Scion, poison the Scion, manipulate mortal society to keep ever-present pressure on the Scion. Parking tickets, random citations, random searches on the street by corrupt cops to confiscate Relics (since walking around with a severed head that whispers the deeds of dead men is probably illegal). Try to get the Scion arrested, just get rid of the problem by any means. Put explosives in their car, in their mail, anything. Killing a Scion is hard, but possible.

          A mortal mafia might also be able to try to bring in some non-mortal backup. If they are an actual mafia family, I would suggest looking at the Theoi, or perhaps the Elohim for ideas. There were Greek colonies on Sicily, the Carthaginians had a presence, and later it was folded into the Roman Empire. They would probably have a hard time getting any 'big' guns, but entities like Satyr, Centaur, maybe a Cyclops or Giant, things they can bribe, and make deals with.

          In the Social department, a mortal mafia has no chance sadly. If 2e is similar to 1e, a Legend 3 or 4 Social Scion could run rampant through a mafia family. As an organization made up of people, their weakest point is those people, and a social Scion is an expert of people. They are charming, friendly, scary, capable of convincing the street level thugs that they are right, that the Scion should be the new head of the family, that under them, everything would be so much better and street level thugs wouldn't be sent after a superhuman being. The Social department also cuts out the mafia family's ability to use corrupt local institutions against the Scion, if they get arrested (if they can't manage to talk their way out of the arresting officer) they will talk their way out of whatever charges are put on them. If the family tries to go after the Scion's family, that gives the Scion a chance to talk to their street level people, and that can poison their organization to the core.

          The best way for them to deal with a Socially focused Scion would to kill them quickly, and without any actual people being present. This, however, does not make for a fun story. The PC opens a letter and they die from an explosion. Gas leak, explosion. The Mafia would want to effectively find 'instant kill, no people involved' tricks, and those are just not fun for players. It gets very 'rocks fall, you die' even though that's exactly what a mafia family would need to do to win.

          However, they probably wouldn't want to do that. A Social Scion is likely to manage to win the family over to their side, and make short work of the situation. They can't kill everyone, but they can rehabilitate mortals in the blink of an eye, change their ways, convince them to turn themselves in. Essentially Social Scions are very, very dangerous for any primarily mortal based institution, especially one that operates like a mafia family where social interaction is a major part of their internal culture.

          The Mental Scions are trickier. They don't have the natural defenses of a Social Scion where the mafia family is likely to change its mind about them very quickly. A mafia family's best bet against a Mental focused Scion is just to take them out asap. Mental Scions are not quite as scary as the Social Scions, but they can run absolute house. Audit every member of the mafia family in an afternoon and report them to the IRS? A Mental Scion can do that. Hack into every family member's online presence, and air all their dirty laundry to cause internal strife? Really easy for a Mental Scion. Track down exactly where you are hiding? They can do that. Smell the bomb or poison you planted from a mile away? They can do that. Reverse engineer that same trap and return it in a better hidden, more lethal way? They can also do that.

          The most dangerous thing for a mafia family about a Mental Scion is they are a threat if they are a block away from you, or on Antarctica in a research base. They can ruin you ten ways from Sunday from anywhere in the world, or even on the ISS. The best bet to deal with them is simply just killing them as quickly as possible, targeting their friends and family, a very similar tactic to the Physical Scions. The only difficulty is that the mental Scion might be totally out of reach.

          ---

          So, in sum, you will probably want to augment the mafia family with supernatural beings in some way. Pure mortal families will be twisted to a Social Scion's side with minimal effort. Even families with Supernatural backing will have issues with Mental Scions since math, taxes, and hacking are their world. Physical Scions, well, assuming they don't just batman into your lair in the middle of the night, kill the family head, and leave unnoticed, are more vulnerable to mortals.

          Potentially the best way to make mafia families a threat to Scions however is just to be too big, too everywhere, too omnipresent for a Scion, or even a Band of Scions to deal with all at the same time. Too many crises at once, too many things to stop, force them to chose between bad decisions, put them under stress, and hope to hell none of them snap in a bad way and go Achilles or CuChulain on you.

              Have you ever heard of a book series called the Dresden Files? It features a mafia boss by the name of “Gentleman” Johnny Marcone. Despite being 100% pure vanilla mortal, Marcone easily holds his own in a world of wizards, vampires, Fey lords and ladies and other supernatural heavy hitters.

              A lot of this is because Marcone keeps himself very well informed on the capabilities of the entities he deals with and their weaknesses. Knowledge is power when dealing with the supernatural. Lots of magical beings have weaknesses and restrictions, and if you know how to take advantage of them you can help even the odds.

              It also doesn’t hurt that he has a Valkyrie for a bodyguard. In a setting like Dresden Files or Scion 2E’s “the World” sometimes you just have to fight fire with fire, supernatural with supernatural. You can still have 90% of the mafia be human. They’re not really the ones you have to be worried about. It’s the supernatural beings that the boss has surrounded himself with that are going to give you trouble. The moment a mafioso realizes he crossed paths with a Scion out to get him, he’s going to hire some magical protection.

              Although, at the end of the day, Marcone sometimes just proves that he’s not to be trifled with just by busting out the big guns. In Dresden Files, fire is often called the great equalizer. There aren’t all that many supernatural beings that can’t just be incinerated. At Hero tier, even a Scion is going to have to respect the harm a grenade launcher (or two or six) is going to do to her. Sometimes the only way to get rid of a problem is overwhelming force of arms, and if a Scion proves troublesome enough then there’s a good chance the mafia might just do that.

                  If they're smart Mafiosi and, as "Gentleman" Johnny Marcone from the Dresden Files will show, they won't ever get up in the Scion's face if they have something better.

                  Many Scions get caught up in the powers of their characters, and how they can lift this, punch a hole in that, and convince anyone to do what they want, and forget that that's really only affecting their immediate vicinity. When a good guy Scion gets a phone call saying that they've snatched his very human girlfriend, or child, or best friend or what ever, and that they're going to be hung onto as a hostage until the Scion performs a service all that strength or the power to summon lions isn't going to mean much.

                  A good mook-bash is always fun for a good romp, but a good and switched on mob-boss like Marcone is never going to be stupid enough to engage the Scions face to face if there's any way around it.

                  Not a bad way to remind a Scion who's a little too proud of his super muscles or invulnerability that he doesn't exist in a bubble, or the danger of getting too close to mortals.

                                                      Many organized crime outfits developed from community support operations. One way to make them challenging to deal with is to return them to their roots. What if the Mafia is actually the thing that's tying a real community together? What if, in addition to blackmailing the rich and shooting their enemies, they actually contribute to a struggling demographic's well-being? Making them morally complicated means that a Scion can't just wipe them out and hope for the best; they have to figure out how to replace them and improve upon them. This kind of struggle is why I included Triads as a Follower choice in the Shen writeup.

                                                      Beyond that, a mob draws strength from numbers and loyalty, right? Remember that a mafioso is pretty much never alone. This isn't just important for overwhelming a Hero with numbers; it also means that any group of gangsters will probably have a diversity of strengths. Instead of only using mooks for this kind of thing, make mobsters work in groups which allow different members to compensate for each other's strengths and weaknesses.
