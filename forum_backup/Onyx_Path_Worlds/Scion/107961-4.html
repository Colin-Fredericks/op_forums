        It's taking the topic in a new direction, but it's my belief that the Virtues are bound to the Scion by his relation to his pantheon. I think extrinsic vs. intrinsic doesn't really work as a way to describe them. Intrinsic - sure. "The ichor seethes". Sounds pretty damn intrinsic. Extrinsic - even more important. Virtues should not be subject to the whims of the character. It's not what I have Conviction in, it's what the Aztec gods have Conviction in - the need to keep the universe going by oiling the gears with the blood of sacrifice. Which can demand some things that are pretty damn unappealing to modern sensibilities. Which is different than what the Egyptian gods have Conviction in - keeping to the universal principles of Ma'at. Which is more in line, but can lead to some serious issues as well. (Just not so bloody in most cases). I can't be an Aztec Scion and opt into the Egyptian world view anymore than I can be an Egyptian Scion and opt into the Aztec one. I certainly can't substitute my own belief structure. In some cases my belief structure can inform my Conviction, but it can't replace or even modify it substantially. It's pretty much always about what the Virtue is about, not me. Otherwise, why even have them? They aren't constraints at all. I will deliberately keep myself ignorant of the law to insure that I deal with Order as little as possible. Etc. Etc.

        I don't have an issue with people using their Virtues to test their surroundings. Why not? Is that building full of art. Dunno. I'll start putting together a plan to torch it. OUCH! There goes my Expression. Even though I'm getting a warm fuzzy feeling from my Intellect. Is it breaking the law to do X? Start doing X and see if your Order goes off. Those would be brilliant out of the box uses of the Virtues. I'd applaud the PC who thought of it and probably give them a stunt for it. Does it imbalance the game at all? If so not very much. How useful is it to keep trying more and more different things to suss out the local law code? It's trying to prove a negative and it's effectively endless with no real way to achieve your desired outcome. Reminds me of a villain from the weird Vertigo Doom Patrol days. He had every power you never thought of. So people start the fight listing the standards - flight, superstrength, etc. His first move - "You didn't think of the power to trap negative energy entities in glass bottles".

            Hence my argument that the view of each virtue is not bound by the mortal world or human law, but the much older and more stable view of how that particular pantheon views the virtues.

                            Originally posted by Iry View Post
                            Virtues react to what you know, but not to what you think.
                            I guess this where we disagree. To me, Virtues don't react to what you think and they don't react to what you know, they react to what IS. A Scion is as much a vehicle of his/her Virtues as you and I are of our genetic code. That's not to say that all the Scion is is a bundle of his/her pantheon's Virtues as perpetuated generation after generation via ichor, anymore than a human is a bundle of genetic traits.

                            With that in mind, as a Storyteller, I don't have to worry about whether or not a Scion goes out of their way to read up on the local laws every time he or she changes jurisdictions (which is pretty much every time there's a change of local ordinances, perhaps even neighborhood association rules or bylaws of the group meeting). She'll just KNOW she's in danger of doing the unlawful thing and 1) choose not to do it (or do it, depending on the law in question), 2) roll the Virtue and let her ichor decide her action or 3) pay the Willpower.

                            Though now I love the idea of the Order Scion completely ENRAGED that the house down the street is painted RED when the association rules clearly say that's a violation. In a very goofy comedy game of Scion...

                                                I completely disagree with handing decisions about Virtues over to players. That doesn't work with the way I see Virtues, character management, player involvement, game balance, having the Storyteller do his/her job or even role-playing in general. I don't know whether you have experienced full on power gamers and rules lawyers or <shudder> the dreaded combination of two, but I have for over three decades. And they have often been close friends. Using the rules to keep the game on an even keel is not handling my tasks as Storyteller incorrectly. I have players who have played with me for literally decades. Sometimes even these players, even the ones who are not power gamers or rules lawyers, end up trying to maximize the power and ability of their character vis-a-vis a situation they encounter beyond their limitations. That's one of the reasons the rules are there. Even in games like Amber Diceless, where it is possible to say, "my character can do anything, so she does X" there are rules that allow for adjudicating character to character and character to NPC and character to setting interaction. Virtues are definitely an important way of doing so in Scion. Enforcing adherence to Virtues is pretty much the same as enforcing the consequences of taking damage. To me, it's a fundamental part of Scion and should be handled that way. I trust my players to play their characters. They trust me to run the game. Virtues are a limitation that also provide some occasional advantage. I don't ask my character's to work hard at limiting their characters, I'll do that in 1000s of ways. I want them to have fun playing their characters within the constraints of the environment. If they want to overburden themselves with how they play their Virtues, I'm cool with that. I don't demand it. But making sure that, in the heat of the great idea, the guy with Order is okay with killing a cop as part of the plan realizes that he's about to do something against the very core of his bring? That's MY job.

                                                If I my players are investing heavily in the "disadvantaged" Abilities and I regularly call for rolls of those abilities, then with regard to my game, the use/purchase of Abilities and the way Virtues are handled, empirically, those Abilities are not disadvantaged. Just because the same effect can be gained in an alternate way does not diminish the usefulness in gaining that effect through a better, cheaper and more efficient way. Analogies - It's not the same to pull out your handy well-honed knife to cut a slice of bread as it is to stand in a dark room in which somewhere, there is a table and somewhere on that table, there is a loaf of bread and you have to use your dull skewer and randomly stab around trying to cut the bread. I can use the handle of screwdriver as a hammer, but, man, I'd rather use a hammer; I'll use the screwdriver if that's all I've got, but I won't expect it to work as well as a hammer.
