            Arcanum Magicae,

            You are very welcome. I think that the African Pantheons are very important (They all are, in their own way). Not everything can be fit into the main book, and any attempt to create a "Book of Pantheons" would result in a multi-volume creation. Fortunately, we have the Scion Community that can pick up where the Devs have to leave off (Not picking on the Devs. Some Very hard decisions have to be made, and I understand and fully support that. Heck...new Pantheons can be added later).

            I for one intend to add non-traditional Pantheons pulled from the imaginations of the Human Populous into my game with 1e (I know this won't win me too many fans...but what the heck...). I did this in my own Scion game, and it added some depth and breadth to the game (They weren't a "big" factor -- not on stage, all the time...but they showcased a growing world to the players...).


            Some ideas (Not asking them to be developed "Officially," mind you):

            The "Holiday" spirits:
            - Father Christmas (Olde Style, replete with a Sword. Defender of Children, Protector of the Innocent)
            - The Spirit of Easter (Renewal, rebirth, healing)
            - The Hallowed Lady (Protector of the Dead, Avenger of Martyrs, Guide of Hallows)
            - The Married Couple (Protectors of Marriage, Mother's, Fathers)
            - Mother of Spring (Patron of Groves, Bringer of Spring, Birth)


            (And others...) -- most of these are very specific, of limited scope, or a cover a limited period of time. They didn't play a big part in the game, but were cool for background flavor, and showed the players that "Hey...there are other thing's going on besides the 'Big Guys, from before.'"

                                While I’m in favor of there being something in the setting to support the notion that worshippers of the Abrahamic faiths aren’t utterly delusional (i.e., simultaneously denying the existence of gods that absolutely do exist in the setting while worshipping a God that doesn’t exist in the setting), I don’t think that the Elohim should be included for that purpose. If they’re included, it should be because said Pantheon’s mythology is interesting in its own right.

                                All that said, I wouldn’t mind the Loa being replaced by the the Orishas in Scion: Hero, with the Voodoo Pantheon specifically being held back for later inclustion in a post-Scion: God supplement devoted to Pantheons and the like that are connected in some way or another to the Abrahamic faiths: the Canaanite Pantheon that Abraham’s God waged war against, the Arabian Pantheon whose human followers were nearly wiped out by the rise of Islam, the Carribean religions that syncretized African and Christian beliefs, and so on: in short, all of the Pantheons that get awkward to talk about without referencing Abrahamic faiths.

                                I’d also like to see the order of introduction of Pantheons reflect the books involved: Scion: Hero should feature Pantheons that are either still actively worshipped today (e.g., Indian and Japanese Pantheons) or that have a significant presence in popular culture (e.g., Greek, Norse, and Egyptian Pantheons), while Scion: God should introduce Pantheons of more ancient pedigree who no longer involve themselves all that much with the current affairs of the World but are still significant presences in the Overworld.

                                My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
