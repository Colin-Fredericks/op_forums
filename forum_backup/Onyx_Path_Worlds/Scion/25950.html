    Pantheon sepcific books

    12-08-2013, 03:23 PM
    Not only do I want I new version of Scion: Ragnarok for the new edition, I want several pantheon specific books covering the major pantheons. Obviously of the ten realworld Pantheons outlined in Scion so far, some wouldn'd be able to carry a book of their own for financial reasons, so the less popular Patheon might be placed in a book together. It wouldn't be as good as a full book of their own, but it would be very useful.

    The main use of each of these books should be to make the myths and stories of that pantheon easier to roleplay. The kinds of problems and styles of stories should be a major theme in each book.

    Also as each pantheon has different types of supporting casts and monsters, different groups will need to be stressed in each book. Examples, both the Greek and the Japanese gods have large groups of allied gods. But the Greek gods have the Teelchines as Frenemies of a unique sort. Both the Loa and The Tuatha have many closely allied pantheons that would need discussion.

          While I think the idea is nice - it was a shame that Ragnarok was the only book that got into that kind of detail for a Pantheon - I'm still a bit hopeful that the 2e approach will do a better job at presenting the Pantheons in the first place. Better initial representation means less need for this sort of book.

          This would mean you could do books that are less Pantheon specific (which ultimately reduces their appeal to the whole audience) and build books more around themes. A book about "The End of the World." Lets you talk about the included mythologies that have strong end-times elements in some detail, but also discuss the generally mythological concept and how to use it in your game that would be useful regardless of which Pantheons you've got going. A book on "State Religions" lets you look at the Pantheons in the context of ones that were focused around the large ancient empires but have long since fallen out of practice, the ones that have only recently fallen from that position, the ones that are still dominant religions, and the ones that seem like they might be edging towards a come-back. This is again going to have a lot more inherent material for some of the Pantheons than others, but it can apply to almost any game that is going to deal with the repercussions of the Pantheons mixing with human affairs on a civilization level.

                  Mm. I'm not sure of this. While a good initial presentation of a pantheon would make it easy to roleplay them, a well-done pantheon book would let us look "under the hood", so to speak - explore the worlds they call their own, the various ways they engage with the modern World, the internal relationships and politics within the pantheon, etc. In short, getting a better, more in-depth understanding of them and their worlds.

                  As to relevance... to be honest, at the level of a splat, I don't think that's so much an issue. There's a place for an indepth exploration, as with a splatbook, and a wide-ranging exploration, as with themed books. In the best of both worlds, we'd see both. (There likely _is_ a cut-off point for how relevant a sourcebook is to a buying audience, but I think a splatbook is a good distance from that point - eyeballing WW's various splatbooks on DriveThru, they all seem reasonably popular.)

                      The splatbooks are usually fairly successful (but the number of them isn't directly correlated to how popular they were, lots of cWOD ones have pretty bad reputations), but they're also a lot shorter than something like Ragnarok. Things like the cWOD splat books, the Exalted 1e splat books, and the Requiem Clanbooks all clock in ~100 pages. Ragnarok is ~240. The Order books for Awakening and the Covenant books for Requiem are the only books that come close to that kind of detail on a single splat (well not counting the Exalted core books for non-solars); but the Orders and Covenants both were in serious need of expansion and played a much more important role in the day-to-day for the PCs.

                      The problem with individual splat books for the Pantheons is that there's going to be a lot of them. If we see twenty of them, that's a lot of books. If we see thirty? That's also as many as VtM, and WtA individual Revised splatbooks combined (13 Clan, 3 Sect, 13 Tribe [Auspices were one book]). It just seems like more of a pain to the authors, as they have to write all those books, and the customers since you're going to end up wanting to support all the Pantheons in the main books. I'm not confident that there's the support on either end for enough splat books to double what any other game line has done in a single edition.
