    Scion 2.0 Project - Enech

    05-15-2014, 10:56 PM
    Enech

    Hello Scion Forums! From the fine people working on the     Scion 2.0 Project, we would like to introduce our first Pantheon Specific Purview rewrite. We decided this should be Enech because we are obsessed with all things Irish, and JSR is closing down before they got to work on their own version of Enech.

    Enech is essentially life in hard mode. From the moment you chose your first geas, you are throwing yourself into the insanity that is the Tuatha’s daily experience. It sets you apart from the other gods and opens you up to all the glories (and pitfalls) of Social Face. Yes, the Tuatha Gods basically embody all the gory fun of American High School politics.

    Enech is powerful, but this ability comes at a steep price. When a Tuatha fails to maintain their social prowess among their fellow Irish, they soon discover that their mastery over Enech has abandoned them as they lose the confidence they need to wield their powers.

    Additionally, due to the massive undertaking that our unofficial Scion 2.0 Project has become, we have begun to move all of our houserules, purview edits, knacks, birthrights, pantheons, and everything but probably the kitchen sink over to a website which we will be unveiling at the beginning of June.

    In short, this revamp of Enech serves as a teaser for what’s to come, as we are excited to share our system overhaul with you. If there are any questions, we will be happy to answer them in this thread; and that includes the generalized Animal Purview, or the content of the website, or anything else Scion-related.

    Enjoy!

    Aynie, Iry, and Watcher.

                      Originally posted by Ajax View Post
                      Should Enech be fixed? Is that even possible? We've just replaced it completely.
                      A glance into the linked document would reveal that while we've kept the name and the flavor of the geas and the Irish need to brag, the purview as a whole flows differently. Gone is the Irish Legend Economy (that never ran out) and we've detached geas from how RAW ran them because removing geas from the Irish is like the Greeks without their Arete.

                      Geas now provide a social bonus towards the Irish (and only the Irish) and are the keys to unlocking and maintaining one's level of Enech (or social face). If a player breaks their geas, they're immediately barred from using the PSP until they have made amends. Later on, they can take on additional geas which in turn increases the maximum amount of "Face" they can build, but it's a lot more trouble than it's worth.

                          Face benefits you as a representative of your social status among the Tuatha, encourages you to respect the culture of the Tuatha, serves as a gateway to keeping your PSP, and grants you additional legend with Bragaire.

                          Luigh Geasa is a sneaky boon because many of the benefits are social. Having a geis is considered a great honor in ancient Irish culture. If you offer to lay a worthy geis upon someone who cares strongly about Irish culture, then they would lose status if they turned you down. You could even use this to manipulate the gods themselves if you could think of a geis that is worthy of a god, and if you have devious intentions then your options open up even more. You also have the choice to lay geis upon your own band members, which might be the difference between the Tuatha or their allies refusing to give that person aid or relics.

                          From a mechanical perspective, being able to raise your maximum Face by +2 (or more if you are crazy enough to take several geis) means you can resist one more Tuatha Virtue before you start losing access to your PSP, one more satire you can endure, two more Bragaire you can fail, and two more points of legend every time you succeed at Bragaire.

                                      Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                      Being praised or insulted should provide a variable modifier to face, a skilled bard satireing you is worse than a cheap insult.
                                      Well, at the moment it is phrased as "someone in authority", not just anyone. The difficulty about giving it a long scaling effect depending on the societal rank, and the specific action taken to either build you up, or tear you down, is that it would become an incredibly complicate list. You would have to break down the different kinds of satires, the Glam Dicenn, mundane insults, mundane insults dilivered in rhyme, what is the specific rank of the Bard in question, for the list is incredibly long, and so on.

                                      It is something I thought about for a while when we were designing the face system, but it was far too complicated and would require an excessive amount of book keeping on the part of the Storyteller to know what specific rank of Bard this person is, if this was a Glam Dicenn, if this was a legal situation to use the Glam Dicenn in, and so forth. So, in order to prevent things from being too complicated, we shortened it to being "someone in authority".

                                      I am normally a stickler for being totally and utterly accurate, and if it was possible to do without needing a chart with about thirty different modifiers, I would totally go for it and try to work out the mechanics of it with Iry and Aynie. But, to do it any justice, it would be incredibly complicated and require excessive book keeping, something which the goal of Scion 2.0 is to reduce to allow people to run games without having to keep multiple binders full of information to reference.

                                      Now, what is important about the "someone in authority" tag is that it is someone who the Irish would believe is in a place of authority. So, Marduk, as a king, is in a place of authority over you as long as you are not the Ard Ri or the Chief Ollam. But, the Minister of Rivers and Floods from the Celestial Bureaucracy is not above anyone, since there is no equivalent in Irish society.

                                      I hope this makes sense as to why we do not have a large expansive system for the different kinds of insults. What I would personally suggest is for different bardic figures to take their Bard Rank as a Relic (in our system, Relics need not necessarily be something physical. If you are one of Niamh's Scions, you could take your Sidhe Blood as a Relic. Though you must always pay for it.) and give yourself a modifier to how many points of Enech you can remove or add to someone. But, it must be kept in mind that doing so illegally will have you killed. There are incredibly strict laws involving insults for the Irish.

                                                  Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                                                  So what's the point of Molfach?                                                   There is no strict mechanical benefit to having Molfach, but not every power should be strictly mechanical. One of the great difficulties as a Scion is that you are going to be meeting people who have no idea who you are, for you are a minor little thing, not even entirely Divine. And on top of that, you're probably also a foreigner, meaning that Gods, Goddesses, and Legendary Beings from other Pantheons are probably going to have no idea who you are, or care.

                                                  What Molfach lets you do is force those people to know who you are. If you tricked Isis into telling you her true name, the true name of Horus, and the true name of Osiris through a cunning plan, you could have used this power to make sure everyone knows just what you did. If you manage to disarm Thor, and shave his beard while grappling him to shame him, coming away from it unharmed, you can have even the Deva know what you did. It gives you a tremendous amount of Social power, both making sure that others know just who you are, how amazing you are, and being able to use it to shame others by their horrible defeats at your hands.

                                                  Now, the reason it is not lower is because every Boon before it fits incredibly well into the Rank they have been slotted into. The order of things has been highly considered, and everything fits nicely into the slot it has been given both mechanically and mythically. The order of Boons is to attempt to build up a Scion and their player into taking the Irish mentality of taking great risks, and having both great rewards, but also failures. The Boons before this allow the Scion to build themselves up to the point where they can take these great risks with the ability for great reward, but also failure. So, the placement of Molcach at Rank 5 is to continue building on that theme, before moving into the more Divine aspects of Enech.

                                                  Does the value of the Boon make sense to you with that explained Epimetheus? I can try to further explain if it does not.

                                                  Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post

                                                  All powers have to be designed to prevent people from intentionally breaking the game, people will abuse the rules if they can. This prevents someone from defeating Ra when he is Avatared down in a contest and forcing him to blink, thus ending all of reality.

                                                  But, to explain it from a mythological point of view, yes, requests had to be sensable. If you asked someone to bring you the moon, everyone would agree that you were being a twat for doing so. People would lose respect for you. But, if you were cunning about it, as Lugh was with the three brothers who killed his father, he made an entirely reasonable request, "Gather these items for me,", but managed to twist it around so that the items they had to gather tired them out and wore them down so they could be killed by the final tasks, you would be honored.

                                                  Irish mentality is quite difficult to grasp in the modern day, but just assume if you are being direct, and exerting your power over someone else in a way which is cruel, or mean, even if you have a right to, people will look down on you, and you will be shamed. But, if you manage to be cunning about it, trick them, make a safe situation suddenly become a cruel or mean one, that's totally fine and you will be praised for it.

                                                          Oh, no worries. I'm glad you are asking questions, though I am not the perfect person to answer them. Iry is our mechanics person, I just produce ideas and work on our site. But, I will do my best.

                                                          To set the stage mechanically, we still use the Epic Attribute Autosuccess scale, so as a Legend 11 God, you will have 46 Autosuccesses at base to work with. We have, however, slashed many of the ways to gain passive bonus dice, or Autosuccesses to things. Birthrights may not give additional Automatic Successes, or Dice, to give an example. We have also slashed most of the Boons which can give you bonus Dice and Autosuccesses, though each Purview has at least one, though it is minor to be sure. Virtues, at God, can give you Automatic Successes, and so can Legendary Deeds, though they are quite limited.

                                                          But, onto the topic of Bua itself.

                                                          Bua is actually not designed to be used at just the perfect moment after you have fought through the opposing army to reach its general to strike them down. You, of course, can use it for this function, but it would not be quite as powerful as possible, for you are worn down by that point. Bua is designed to encourage you to be horribly, horribly reckless, to do something amazing, but stupid. As is, actually, the vast majority of Enech. We want Irish Scions to be encourage to take colossal gambles, take massive risks, and have the potential for both awe inspiring success, and bitter loss.

                                                          So, to encourage that theme, Bua is most effective when you go all in on a single thing. You put every ounce of yourself into making a single play, and you will be better at it than anyone else could ever have been (unless they used an Avatar or Ultimate, but I will get to that in a moment). And, while you will Succeed, if you played your cards wrong, if this victory did not win you the war, you are now horribly weakened with zero Legend remaining, and you will have to make it through the entirety of the Story, desperately Stunting, doing more and more reckless things to regenerate your Legend. (No just going to the Teotl and asking for Legend. We are already giving Itztli some looks and poking at it.)

                                                          Furthermore, Bua is incredibly versatile. A Legend 11 Bard, without a Dot of Epic Strength, could pick up a stone and throw it through the eye of Surtr, just as Lugh did with Balor long before. Horribly maiming the Fire Giant, without a doubt, if they use it when they have a large amount of Legend left. A Warrior could sit down and discover the way to save Lir's children, and bring the ancient Titan back into the fold of the Pantheon, likely throwing Midir under the bus to do so, but hey, he's stupid. A blind man could find Airmed's herbs, and bring Miach back to life with them. But, all of these things will leave the young God who did them, if they threw every ounce of themselves at the problem, quite weakened, So, you must chose very wisely.

                                                          Additionally, on the topic of Ultimates and Avatars. A Scion who uses Bua will never be as good as a fellow Deity in one of these states. A God using Ultimate Strength will be stronger, a Goddess using The General will be better. But, what this power does is fly under the Mutually Assured Destruction bar that Ultimates and Avatars carry. When one being uses an Avatar, or an Ultimate, everyone else jumps into one too, to ensure some safety to themselves, or to combat the immediate threat in the only way possible. If you use The General to challenge Bres to single combat, Bres smacks you over the head with Ultimate Appearance. If you use Ultimate Charisma to win a peace between the Yazata and Deva, Shiva pops The Void as his role as The Destroyer, and then Vishnu pops The Sentinel, and it just becomes a mess.

                                                          Bua, not being one of the Ultimates or Avatars, however, does not cause this immediate panic from all the other Deities. You have the ability to effectively acomplish a goal uncontested, unless it is something someone is willing to blow an Avatar or Ultimate over and start a chain reaction, with the only issue being that you are going to be horribly weakened for the rest of the Story. You have to take a horrible risk to be utterly amazing, and that's what being Irish is all about. Bad choices and being awesome.
