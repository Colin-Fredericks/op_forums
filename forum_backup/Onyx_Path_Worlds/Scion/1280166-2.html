        If I may put my two cents on this whole point:

        I think, as it was announced, that was a poor choice. I am all up to create a pantheon for Cthulhu, but after all books are released and they go on and say: "Okay, we are making a brief pause of deeply researched and historically sensitive pantheons to make something fun and easier." and make an extra book, absolute off canon, with a few fictional pantheons, like publish the Mythos with the Tolkien Pantheon, Basic D&D Pantheon (Pelor and so forth) and Disney Pantheon. They might even make like a public vote to see which pantheons they will choose.

        Create those pantheons, but make they separated form regular world. Like a fun, uncomplicated way to play Scion in different Tones: wanna play it darker and hopeless, go for Cthulhu. Wanna play it more fantasy like, go for D&D pantheon. Wanna play it all fairy tale, go for Disney. I have no idea of how they would rebuild those concepts to fit in a working world, but I have all faith they could do it, and would make less people pissed off because those would be separated, self-sufficient worlds. And we would not need to compare the validity of the Mythos with the Greek Gods, for example, because it would be published very obviously as non canon, together with the Great Goddess Ariel, ruler of the Sea and Mistress of all that Sings.

        But right now, saying they want to do the Mythos, while fans are making their own pantheons books because they really wanna those pantheons but they are not famous enough to be on the top of the priority list (By the way, thank you Watcher, Svarogg and all of you guys), specially considering the whole implications of comparing real-life and fictional pantheons, sounds like a bad idea. Plus, if they make it before Demigod and God gone out, they will need to finish it in other supplement.

        About the racism, they can sanitized it, like every other media. The racism is present, but if you change a few things, mostly minor, the racism is just metaphorical enough to not be too noticeable. Most people who did not study about Lovecraft will not notice, and people who did will never be able to forget anyway. Specially because the Older Gods are everything but racist, as they do not care about humans, no matter where is your ancestry from. Which by itself creates a problem for why would they create Scions, but that is another topic.

        Im not commenting on what was said, as I have not heard the podcast, just the idea to start working on a Mythos book right now. I also will not comment the "a pantheon gone bad", because I would argue that no pantheon has gone "right", in the sense that they are not all good people. If they mean bad as in, "Evil, wants to destroy the world", which is an interesting concept, just fake a pantheon. Say that an Ancient population had those gods, the population was extinct and just the Gods remained, and now they want to destroy the world because of it. Hell, just create a variation of the Atlantean Pantheon, say that that the Atlanteans all died for whatever reason (war, earthquake due to Poseidon, Hubris or whatever) and now the Atlanteans Gods are all evil.

        --No one will be comparing fictional to real mythology, as the Atlanteans are already a fictional not-really-canon Gods that everybody is okay with,

        --No racism to sanitized,

        --Still fits the previously created cosmology and tone in a way that is easy to insert/remove then,

        --Has precedents, as the Nementedevos is also a Pantheon which population has mostly disappeared,

        --And, at least in my view, is actually more interesting than the regular Atlanteans. I never cared about the Atlanteans before, but if they were an evil Pantheon lurking around to recreate evil cults and trying to destroy all Pantheons, I would be far more interested.

        And before anybody says that some people still wants their Atlantean Pantheon, I say: just create a variation. Then we have good Atlanteans for those who want, bad Atlanteans for those who want, they can try the "Pantheon gone bad" concept without pissing anybody off, and everybody is happy. Then make the Mythos later, as a fun "not-canon" supplement, so they can also have the appeal of putting the Mythos into the Scion Series.

In fact, I came across some information years ago talking about how there is a particularly avid group of fans who are trying to make The Force a fully recognized faith. So does that mean that we should include the likes of the Celestials, or Abeloth into Scion, as there are actually people in our world who worship such things?

                                    From an atheistic stand point, all religions are fictional. They haven't existed since the dawn of time, somebody had to go out of their way to create them from the ground up. A long, long time ago, some people who didn't understand the world came up with whatever excuse they could think of to explain the nature of the universe and preached these ideas to those around them. Eventually, those ideas grew to become the religions they are known for today. All it takes is time, and enough people willing to agree to the idea for a religion to form.

                                    In fact, one could argue that it's already happening, as more and more people are starting to take inspiration from Lovecraft's mythos over the years, both in mainstream media, as well as the less popularized works.


                                    And on that note, I would say the idea of a religion founded on existential dread and nihilism is totally relevant to the world we live in today. Far more relevant, in fact, than praising a shape shifting rapist, just because he killed the embodiment of time and took over his throne. People are smarter in this day and age than they have ever been, and along with that newfound intellect comes a host of knowledge that both inspires and terrifies us.

                                    Theories like the Big Crunch, and Heat Death warn that the universe is ultimately finite and will eventually come to an end, along with all life within it. While the impossibility of FTL travel means that humanity will most likely never colonize beyond our solar system. Lovecraft had it right on the money, humanity is insignificant. Nothing we do matters, because the universe is more vast than we can possibly fathom, and anything we create will ultimately crumble to dust in time.

                                    That's the major point about Lovecraft's work that I find so fascinating. Regardless of what his political and cultural views may have been, his philosophy on cosmic horror makes perfect sense to me. And I am very curious to see how White Wolf plans to implement such concepts into Scion going forward.
