            Something that's struck me in this, is that there's a giant issue with transplanting modern concepts of gender and sexuality on ancient cultures.

            We have no idea what Zeus felt about gay people, because the ancient Greeks did not view sexuality in the terms we do. Zeus being confronted with a gay man by modern ideals is like Zeus being confronted with a cell-phone. He'd need to understand a completely different world to have any idea of what he's dealing with.

            If we assume that the modern concepts of how we think about what sexuality and gender mean in the World exist then the gods must have adapted because otherwise it's the same as if they never adapted to changes like technology or political systems.

            Another thing is that there's a lot of talk about the cultures that inspired the Scion Pantheons, and the assumption that how those cultures felt about these topics is the same as how their gods in the setting felt/feel about them. Human religions are often more conservative than their holy texts. "If you can't eat pig, it's easier just to not own pigs at all, then you never have to worry about eating them." And certain gods were often very popular in a fashion that could skew things. Thor was very popular at certain points in Norse culture (he's portrayed as being the Aesir that was most dedicated towards protecting humans directly), and probably influenced their attitudes around manly behavior because of it, even if Odin, Tyr, and Heimdall don't really give a shit about those things.

            Personally, I would look at each deity individually. How much are sex and and gender a big part of that god's story and role? When it Tyr every portrayed as caring about what's between your legs or who you like to have sex with? Tyr's about glory in war, honor, etc.

            That said, I do think there's going to be variety among the gods associated with sexuality or gender roles about them. Hera's focus on marriage and motherhood means she's probably more conservative about gender roles than Hestia, Athena, or Aphrodite would be. A goddess that's really big on childbirth is more likely to side on gender essentialism than a goddess that's focused on happy marriages since a happy LGBTQ inclusive marriage is still a marriage.

            Some of the gods are going to care in a way that's probably not LGBTQ friendly. Most probably don't actually care that much because it isn't the part of humanity that they reflect. Others are going to be for such issues because it expands what they're about to more people. The nice thing about Pantheons... is that the members can have conflicting goals.

                                            Thank you, Manbat, for raising this question. And for approaching it the way you did as well.

                                            There is probably lots of tension between "things as they are expected to be" and "things as they are in practice", because well, gods are individuals, and therefore subject to change, while pantheons as systems are all about stability.
                                            So, each pantheon is intimately connected to a culture (in many cases said cultures either died out or changed drastically, but ancient things are more numinous, or feel that way at least). And, naturally, to keep the whole coherent, parts of that whole (individual divine beings) will be forced to compromise a lot. And, since at one point in time they managed to find some common ground and establish certain principles that allowed them to cooperate, oficially introducing new principles or getting rid of existing ones is likely to generate too much internal strife for anybody's liking.

                                            Which does not mean most (or even many) gods are stuck in the stone age, or whatever detractors imagine.

                                            Zeus as the patriarch of the Theoi will proclaim that women are inferior to men, yes. But does anyone really think he does not respect Athene? Or would Artemis need official sanction to take revenge for a wrong done to her lesbian faithful?
                                            Ares might just laugh and say that victims of injustice need to "stop crying and take it like adults", but hey, sometimes it might be the only way.

                                            Among the Aesir ergi might be srs bsns. But don't expect Odin the allfather to put social norms before efficiency. And the Vanir likely never subscribed to the whole "masculine vs. feminine, dominant vs. dominated" schtick in the first place. They are nature, and nature is much more complex than that.
                                            Loki? Geez, everybody's preconceived notions of "normality" and "appropriateness" are his to play with as he wishes. With Lodur, you're the one who has to keep up.

                                            The Teotl might represent a culture that had a lot of unreasonable restrictions (and some surprisingly reasonable ones). But they also probably realize that a new epoch/sun demands new approaches, even if Huitzilopochtli is not amused and would like the "good old days" back.
                                            Also, do note that those "good old days" were the days of Nezahualcoyotl and Cuauhtemoc, so not all that is old has to be inferior.

                                            And no matter how misogynistic Japan used to be (and there still are traces of that, afaik), Amaterasu Omikami still reigns supreme. Nobody dares question her ability, not after that one incident... you know what I mean.

                                            ​ Sumerian texts outright say that mortals were created as servants and even slaves for the gods, but that never prevented proud Inanna from falling in love with earthly men, Enki from saving his creations even when it meant going against the decision of the other gods, or Utu from taking an interest in earthly justice.

                                            Traditionalists that they are, the Devas are still not made of stone, either. Shiva fell in love with Parvati precisely because she is his equal, and Vishnu is happy with whatever helps keep human society, and the world as a whole, healthy, and of course equality of gender, race, varna, etc. does. Why would he object to new ideas that actually help mortals become better and reduce his workload? Consumerism, on the other hand... not so fun.

                                            And the Tuatha are actually likely to be even more progressive than the average modern European, tbh. Most of them are incredibly dynamic beings, and if sometimes they get too carried away and solve a dispute via decapitation... we could all name a few politicians who could use that treatment.

                                            Basically, gods are complex. They are neither perfect archetypes who can do no wrong, nor straw bigots who need to be educated by the great and wise modern scions. Inflexible? Sometimes. Some of them. But there might be a better reason behind that inflexibility than "they are self-centered tyrants who would keep humanity under their heel".

                                            Hubris is not about talent. It is, at least in theory, about not understanding/accepting your natural place in the grand scheme of things.
