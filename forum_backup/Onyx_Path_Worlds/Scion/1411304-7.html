                        So, I have sat on Titanomachy and tried to figure out what isnt sitting right with me.

                        Warning -- a bit ranty --

                        I'm really hoping the development team does another major pass on this book. Titanomachy feels like it could be a lot more sophisticated (and probably should be, considering the material).

                        My biggest issue is that it doent do a lot to define the nature and motivations of the Titans writ large (outside of the new Callings, which dont get a lot of attention or discussion). "Titanism" seems to basically be a term of ostracism or a political decision - but why? I really want a LOT more wordcount on what makes a Titan a Titan and why they are a threat that requires a "cold war." Id like more exploration on their motivations and relationship to humanity... because right now, as bad guys and challenges? They fall flat. If anything, many of the Titans are outright sympathetic rather than nuanced antagonists.

                        The reader is expected to simply accept the imperative to fight the Titans because they have been ostracized and labeled them as the "other" by the Gods (that in itself is a bit troubling). Titanomachy doesnt give us a sense of the need or reason to oppose the Titans -- especially when we see time and again that the Titans are basically the Gods-but-not. A few examples of the havoc or destruction the Titans have wrought would be helpful as well. I want to know the consequences if my PC scion doesnt fight against the machinations of the Titans.

                        Titanomachy's entries on the various Titans is super useful -- but if we dont know what binds the Titans (across pantheons) then we dont have an understanding of the nature of the conflict. This really irks me because this was THE major failing of 1e in my opinion. In 1e the Titans were described as bad guys, but rarely explored.

                        Likewise, how many Titans do we have that are various iterations of "they want to be the Joker and watch the world burn." Okay. But why? Scion's modern takes on the Gods (and Titans) is a great opportunity to explore modern sensibilities and philosophy in terms of villains. I want more than just motorcycles as stand-ins for horses and guns as stand-ins for axes. I want to see more discussion of the modern vs ancient and how that impacts the Titans -- especially as that sort of disconnect could be a really cool motivator.** Instead of wordcount dedicated to stories I can pull off of wikipedia, give me something I cant find on the web. Help me understand what makes the various Titans tick in the modern world and how their old school motivations interact with modern sensibilities. That would be super interesting and useful.

                        I really hope the Dev team cuts about 10k words from the generic parts of the antagonist section and applies that wordcount to talking about who and what the Titans are. Help the players and Storyguide to understand why there is a driving imperative to oppose the Titans. Tell us what makes a Titan a Titan. Tell us why a cold war is necessary (especially as the Titans as presented are disorganized and hardly feel like a legitimate threat to the established pantheons)... and why a hot war may be devastating.

                        Oh, and the listing of antagonists really needs to be organized better. Throwing Titanspawn, Titan-aligned Scions, nemeses, and assorted and other sundry entries in alphabetical order isnt terribly handy. If a Storyguide wants to quickly pull up an antagonist in a game.. this is going to be too obtuse. Alphabetized entries leads to Wendigos and Nipahâhkatosow in different areas with no clear connection. Considering one is an extension of the other, I think the alphabetized entries needs to be dropped.

                        Reorganize into types of antagonists. It would also be super helpful to have each antagonist's pantheon listed. Knowing their home region would also be helpful. I speak five different languages, have lived around the world, and am pretty well educated in the legends of the regions I have lived in... and even then there are a lot of entries I couldnt place.



                        **were it me working on defining the nature of the Titans, I would riff off the disconnect between the old and the new. Titans would be the Gods/Ancient Entities who reject the modern world humanity built (and, by consequence, much of humanity). They are artifacts of a bygone era, with thinking that is so old as to basically be alien and largely incomprehensible to modern humans and the Gods of the pantheons. This divergent thinking makes them ihostile to a world and reality that clearly rejects their vision. In turn, Titans reject modernity, and each seeks to rebuke the modern world in some fashion (either actively or passively). All Titans see the Gods as sellouts and are, ultimately, resentful of the Gods' relative success.

                        Take something like the above, give it 5k words to explore, and suddenly the Titans are worthy foes that need to be opposed.

                                ...and if Titans are just as likely to be allies or opt-out of Godly politics -- then where does the "cold war" theme come from? So, the leaves us with a one-sided conflict (and, perhaps, a bit too much nuance).

                                Also, if the Titans are meant to be allies as much as enemies -- why call the book "Titanomachy?"

                                                        Originally posted by Nervaqus987 View Post
                                                        They should still have Titanic Virtues(Fecundity, Rapacity, Dominance, and Submission), but I'm not sure if those are going to be staying.
                                                        When I picked up Titanomachy, I assumed that this was the new paradigm for Titans going forward, now that Titanic Scions were playable: no more Titanic Virtues, now they’re just defined by Titanic Callings and pantheon opposition.

                                                        And then Demigod’s manuscript drops with all the Titans mentioned in the Pantheon writeups using the Virtue system, which both makes Titanomachy the odd one out and makes them not playable for Titanic Scions in the way that Titanomachy makes its Titans valid.

                                                        Our options, generally, are as follows:

                                                        #1: Leave things as they are. Titanomachy is the odd book out that defines Titans through callings and purviews, all the other books in the line define them through one (1) purview and virtues.

                                                        #1a: Origin/Hero/Demigod/God define Titans by Virtues. All other books define Titans by Callings. Chaos reigns.

                                                        #2: Edits are made to Titanomachy to include Titanic Virtues for the Titans therein, Titans are defined by deviation from their Pantheon’s virtues.

                                                        #3: Edits are made to Demigod to tweak the Titans therein to be more like Titanomachy, we now define Titans by Titanic Callings.

                                                        Personally, I think the best option is #2, because that aligns Titanomachy with the books that have previously been released while still building on them, despite the fact that in a world where the Scion writers are only writing for me they’d switch to Callings as the mark of Titanhood.

                                                        (In this world, there’s also a sidebar about Tamamo-no-Mae, Daji’s infamous Japanese mantle who even tangled with the Devas, in the Shen writeup, or at the very least a shoutout to her somewhere.)

                                                        #1 is, unfortunately, probably the most likely, given wordcount and production deadlines, as unsatisfying as it is.
