    Who exactly imprisoned the Titans?

    07-29-2016, 02:12 PM
    Did the gods do it? If so, which pantheons (bearing in mind that some pantheons, such as the Egyptians and the Norse, exist thousands of years apart from each other)? My personal theory is that the Titans are the Yozis of Exalted and were imprisoned thousands of years before recorded history. What is the canon answer to this?

      The canon answer from 1e is that there was a first Titanomachy, much like the one described in Theoi myths, wherein all of the gods of every pantheon, fought all of the Titans of every pantheon and imprisoned them. But honestly you can table rule whatever works best for your story.

          TheGraySoul is exactly right. Scion 1E simply rules it that all of the Pantheons fought a war and imprisoned the Titans. Despite the huge chronological issues in this, that is the reality that Scion 1E presents. The 1E canon answer is exceptionally Theoi-focused (most of 1E has a focus on the Greeks or Norse in the core systems of the game. It's a bit problematic.) such as even naming the Titans Titans.

          The way I get around this is use the idea of the Titanomachy like the Iron Age, in that it took place at a different time all over the globe, and in binding the Titans, both time and space got a bit confused and the world became what it is today. For example, the Tuatha fought a war with the Fomori some time before 500 BCE, while the Teotl would have founded The Fifth Sun later then that. It also explains why places like Hyperboria, Tir na nOg, and Olympus are not... there. Just how Time got fuddled by the removal of the Titans, places that were primarily legendary folded out of reality until the Titans are freed once again. It helps with the 'All Myths are True' problem as well.

                      The best explanation is "Schrodinger's Cat Theory"

                      The 1st edition book explains that all of the pantheon's creation myths are simultaneously true and false, which is why multiple gods can take credit for the same contributions to humanity. You've got Apollo driving his sun chariot across the sky, and at the same time, you have some Norse goddess being chased by wolves across the sky, and at the same time, Amaterasu is the one moving the sun, and so on.

                      So in a way, all of the pantheons are responsible for sealing away the Titans. And I agree with your idea that this happened in the days before recorded history, which is why there are conflicting time periods.

                                  Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                  Hey, since clearly no astronauts have reported seeing a giant man holding the planet over his head, could that mean Atlas is stuck holding Terra over his head? Would Scions wishing to visit Atlas have to journey through Terra to reach him?
                                  Ah! That's a really common misunderstanding of how Atlas works. Renaissance artists depicted Atlas holding the planet, but that's mostly because depicting his actual situation is sort of difficult, and somewhat less impressive. It's sort of caught on as the main 'image' of Atlas in cultural depictions modernly, but it doesn't make a lot of sense cosmologically for the Theoi. I'll give a quick rundown if anyone's interested!

                                  Atlas stands on Gaia, and holds Ouranos over his head. Sort of the easiest version of Greek Cosmology before the Philosophers got picking away at it and getting weird ideas is that reality is two bowls on top of each other forming a sphere. The upper bowl is Ouranos, and the lower Tartaros. This sphere of bowls is half filled with water, and Gaia rests at the center of this. Sort-of-floating, but there's also a connection running between herself and Tartaros evidenced by the fact Hades bridges the two. The water around the edge of the world (since it's essentially one colossal island) is Oceanos. Atlas stands on Gaia, somewhere by his daughters' garden, and holds Ouranos up above his head. I think somewhere around The Pillars of Heracles, by the Atlas Mountains is where he's noted to be? Can't remember for sure.

                                  Helpfully, they do also give us scale for this. If one dropped an anvil from the Heavens, it would take seven days falling to reach earth, and then another seven days to reach Tartaros.

                                  Edit: On the topic of space though, I just rule that just like the world, sealing the Titans also made that go a bit odd. Each Pantheon effectively has a different 'Outer Space', sort of like infinitely overlapped Terra Incognita, except they are all real, and our perception of Space's probably some sort of bad echo caused by all of these places existing on top of each other.

                                  For example, if a Theoi was sitting in the ISS, they would be floating in Chaos (the Primordial, they're not actually 'chaos' but more like 'the stuff outside Ouranos' IIRC. Nothing chaotic about them.) Helios, and Selene would be floating in the sky circling the bowls housing the earth. Ouranos is transparent, but from the bottom, Tartaros has been bound in... bronze? Yeah, bronze. Both the sun and moon are the same size as the other (to explain eclipses.) All the various figures made into stars would be 'out there'.

                                  If a Anunna Scion was sitting in the ISS, they would be floating way, way up really close to the first layer of Heaven. They would be able to see Sin, and Shamash, but would also have an exceptionally clear line of sight to the planets and stars around them. I'd probably give them a huge boost to Prophecy, and generally working out the will of the Pantheon.

                                  That is, of course, assuming the Titans had broken free. If they hadn't, it would just be the normal, mundane space. If multiple Scions or Legendary Beings were present in the ISS at the same time, highest Legend determines the dominant cosmology, or if a tie, roll Legend.

                                              As Watcher states, there are really serious problems trying to pin down mythic events to history and pre-history as much as there are issues with trying to pin mythic or pre-modern cartography. You can't really even say something like "The Titans were imprisoned in the Iron Age/ or at the Bronze to Iron Age transition". Not only is that a moving target depend on where and who you are talking about (to the point it might not even be a relevant concept at all), a lot of the myths seem to point to other and much more widely separated times. Comparative mythologists, even before you start taking it in the direction of the fantastic for a RPG, are all over the place.

                                              For example: Do Odin, et al. taking out Ymir represent the end of the Ice Age? (Which is intimated and posited in RAW.) That's a long, long, long time ago, if so. Does the war between the Aesir and the Vanir reflect the conflict between the ancestors of the Germanic and Norse peoples intruding into the territories of the previous cultures (which are more closely related to the later Celts in a very simplistic way of looking at the prehistory of Europe)? Maybe. It's as good a card toss into the hat as anything else. But, trying to do any of that pinning mythic events to chronological events seems to get counter-productive and really thorny (I mean, we're not even taking into account the whole development of these various characters through various iterations along various paths from an initial source...) Unless, of course, you want to use that idea for your game, e.g. the Vanir are part of the "over-Pantheon" of Celtic cultures that include the Tuatha De and the gods of the Welsh (and the Gaulish gods about which we have no myths). If that's something you want. then other pieces can fall into place... the aes sidhe and the alfar fit together, etc. But that's not the way it has to be (just because it's decent comparitive mythology) or even should be, if it doesn't suit your game where the "mythic space" of the Tuatha and that of the Aesir don't and or can't overlap.
