    2nd Generation Pantheons

    11-27-2013, 04:58 AM
    The premise is this: the Gods have not been keeping on the sidelines during the past couple hundreds/ thousands of years. Instead, our world and the worlds of myth continued to be inextricably linked. Not only that, but what happens on earth is mirrored in the Godrealms. When a people falls, so does their pantheon. When a culture becomes dominant, so do their gods. While this is evident in history books with stories of gods and men interacting up to the present day, it is most obvious in the effects on the pantheons themselves.

    The Challenge: pick a moment in history, such as the European contact with the Americas, Portuguese dominance in the Indian Ocean, the War of the Roses, etc. Trace out corresponding effects on the most closely associated pantheons. This is assuming that despite the intervention of the gods, history pretty much remained the same, with one major exception: Monotheistic religions never gained ground. Those cultures still rose to prominence, but under the auspices of their more polytheistic heritage.

    This could include the death of old gods or the apotheosis of Scions. Some may have become new Titan Avatars. Pantheons could mix as their patron cultures migrated and changed. Entire new pantheons would have emerged.

    I'm excited to see what can be imagined

                          Hellenism Ascendant! Assuming only a few relatively minor changes, the world becomes a very different place indeed. If Alexander survives even just a little bit longer (perhaps with the help of Zeus, or perhaps the Pesedjet; maybe both!) he might go on to conquer Arabia and the Western Mediterranean, perhaps even making incursions into sub-Saharan Africa. Even if his empire shatters amongst the Diadochi as it did in real life, stronger Hellenic states might still emerge. Pyrrus (an unfortunate Scion of Ares? Or Athena?) might form an alliance with the Carthaginians, or Mithridates VI (an interesting character, and a good candidate for being a Scion too; perhaps of Mithras or Bahram, but Zeus works too) might route the Romans in Asia Minor, or Marcus Antonius might defeat Octavian and preserve the Ptolemaic line (the Pesedjet again). Whatever the case, Rome never rises, and a vibrant new era of Hellenistic culture spreads from the Pillars of Hercules to the Indus Valley.

                          Obviously, the Dodekatheon ride high on their success. Greek language, art, architecture and theatre thrive, and pagan philosophers and mystery cults manage to co-opt any nascent monotheistic groups. The Scions of Zeus, Hades, Poseidon, Aphrodite, Ares, Dionysus, Artemis, Athena and the rest party, drink, fight and generally have adventures across the Mediterranean, Near East and beyond. But this also brings the Dodekatheon into closer contact with other Pantheons, for good or for ill. Following Alexander's declaration of godhood by the oracle of Siwa, and the fascination with all things Egyptian, the once insular Pesedjet spread their influence across the Middle East and Mediterranean. Isis in particular enjoys widespread appeal. Their rivals the Yazata have not done so well, re-building their power base in Seleucid Persia as native Greco-Persian dynasties rise and fall. Further east, the Devas are much as they always have been, but the spread of Buddhism has allowed them to move even further east, into the mandala states of Southeast Asia... and west, into the Greco-Buddhist kingdoms of Bactria. Soon Scions of Shiva, Vishnu, Kali, Surya, Ganesha and the other Indian gods are turning up in Alexandria, Sinope, Rhodes, Cheronesus, Ctesiphon, Babylon, Syracuse, Tyre, and even Athens!

                          Meanwhile, on the far fringes of Europe and Africa, the Tuatha and the forebearers of the Loa remain exotic and mysterious. They have encounters with the Dodekatheon and their Greek (and Hellenized) followers but they are a mixed bag. It is too early to make anything of them one way or another. The Babylonian Pantheon never falls either. Its still around and kicking, as are the related Phoenician, Assyrian and other Near Eastern Pantheons (though they cannot rival the majesty of the Dodekatheon and their Egyptian and Indian allies, of course). And beyond the Pillars of Hercules? Who can say...

                                                  The Middle Kingdom Beset By Barbarians (Mid 19th Century to Beginning of 20th)

                                                  The Celestial Bureaucracy had a particularly rough time during this period. Western imperialism (and later the influence of a modernizing Japan) in the World sought opportunities as the Qing Dynasty was facing the Heavenly Signs of natural disasters and economic turmoil... then the British smashed open the floodgates with the First Opium War. The Qing military was completely behind in technology, and suffered catastrophic defeat.

                                                  Likewise, the Manchus that dominated the dynastic power structure were foreign and hated by the population. The Amatsukami, Dodekathon, Aesir, Atzlanti and other Pantheons likewise seized the opportunity to extend their influence upon the borders of Tian, pressuring the Bureaucracy for concessions on all sides. The shen were more able to rebuff the foreign gods, but were heavily riven in disputes as to how to respond.

                                                  The situation was set for Hundun to light the spark, which the Elder Chaos did very well. The avatar Chiyou took on the cover of Hong Xiuquan, a failed civil service candidate who claimed to have visions of Heaven and the true lord of the cosmos (his lesser brother indeed), igniting what history records as the Taiping Rebellion. The massive following and armies behind the rebellion crushed government forces, bolstered by black calamities and chaos hounds. Likewise, other shen were tempted into service of Hundun and proceeded to wreak havoc in Tian. Naturally, other titanspawn flocked to the strife to make matters worse.

                                                  Both World and Overworld of the Middle Kingdom suffered horrendously, Chiyou letting his role of Hong fall into the background and the Taiping Heavenly Kingdom sabotage itself through corruption, British and French soldiers giving the Qing the edge at last.

                                                  The Celestial Bureaucracy was not saved from the fallen Wǔ Tiě Mén (Five Iron Gates - a dissatisfied minor god of military command),
                                                  Yānwù Bìng (Ill Smoke - the disreputable shen assigned to the purviews of opium) and Róuruǎn Desīchóu Fūrén (Soft Lady Silk - under abuse by a higher ranking male shen) by their own efforts, but by the combined efforts of Thor, Apollo, Ares, Vidar, Susan-oo, Set and Sobek.

                                                  The extreme debt that the diminished Celestial Bureaucracy were forced to acknowledge (bound by Fate) crippled the pantheon to the level of a third-rate power, and not until the most recent years of Guan Yu's term as the Jade Emperor has any sign of recovery appeared.
