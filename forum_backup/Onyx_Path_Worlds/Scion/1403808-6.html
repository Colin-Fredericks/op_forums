                                            I can only repeat that no group is ever isolated and there will never be anything that happens at your table that will not have "real life" repercussions. You and your players are not magically excluded from society and its weave for the duration of your game session. And you're right, it is not Samudra's place to tell you what you can and can't do. It is, however, absolutely his place to tell you you are behaving badly, insultingly, egregiously if you butcher a religion that is sacred to him and people he cares about for a personal idea of "fun." In general, there's a law of proportion - the more important a Pantheon or, to use your example, the ethnic background of a character is, the more research should advisably go into that. If your plot hinges upon an idea from a specific religion, I'd consider it common decency to look up if you're portraying that idea respectfully, or at least to address and improve your mistakes if you only realise them later. And that's what this is really about. Not free speech, not policing people's private lives, it's us saying "Yeah, you can do this, but we will consider you a dick if you do." Same for those NPCs. It's good if your Desi character is not a delivery guy with a funny accent, and if they make a one-time appearance, that's probably enough. If they are integral to the plot, it bears fleshing them out with some real-world experience instead of just your fantasies. And I think none of this is asking too much; like I said, if it is, if you simply don't have the time or resources for that kind of research, that is totally okay, and there are a number of Pantheons that will not cause you conscience issues using "blindly." I have sorted out the examples people gave on that previously.

                                            Two points I still want to address. One is that everyone here touts Rick Riordan as the patron saint of doing what you will with mythology, when he is really quite the opposite. This is a man who consciously stuck to mythologies that are considered part of the "Western" heritage to various degrees and opened a whole imprint of its own so authors who were actually from non-White cultures could have a platform to write books about their mythologies themselves. This is a man who has repeatedly apologised for what mistakes he made and has sought to improve in later books. He even apologised for parts of his books being found disrespectful by Hellenic revivalists, which is a plain rebuttal to the earlier example of him not being obligated by an "authority" or "ownership" on Greek religion. So really, to all the people who have used him as an example here: You are misrepresenting this man. He is not the idol of your artistic license on other people's heritage, he is the respectful and circumspect creator we are asking you to be.
                                            Lastly, there is the repeated line of "This is a very complex subject," which says very little and only makes me think that people want the discussion to end as "too complex," as impossible to find a solution. While there are, indeed, intricate complexities at play here, those can be resolved through debate and reasoning (the stuff we're doing here) and most of all by listening to the people affected (the stuff people accusing Samudra of human rights violations have not been doing). And at the core of it all are quite simple questions of respect and contempt, of right and wrong, and I really don't think we should try to muddle those up as somehow grey and impossible to decide.
