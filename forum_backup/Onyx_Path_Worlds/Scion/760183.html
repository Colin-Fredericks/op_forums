      D10 only, yes.
      You need Hero to start, and yes, Hero covers the core rules. The stuff you need to know at Demigod level is Demigod, and stuff you need to know when you reach God-hood is in God. Ragnarok is an in-depth campaign book for the Norse gods, and contains the Frost and Illusion all-purpose Purviews. Companion has some expanded rules and boons for all levels, as well as 3 new (fully formed) Pantheons, their Godrealms, Underworlds ​and their Titan enemies.

Some people on the forums commented they didn't know anything about certain pantheons, which leads me to believe the book isn't very descriptive. Personally, the only pantheon I'm really familiar with is the Dodekatheon. All others on the list I've only got second hand knowledge about.
                  Shadowstripe is correct: you can still do a LOT of Trickster stuff with Epic Socials. The Illusion Purview is definitely a bonus, but it's not absolutely essential. The good news is that the Ragnarok book is possibly the best book out of the Scion series, so if you can get it, you won't be disappointed.

                  The books do a good job of laying out the basics of the Pantheons. Each god gets a basic write-up that gives you some idea about what you're dealing with. Certain Pantheons, however, are better written than others. The Dodekatheon and the Aesir are pretty accurate, but more than one expert has complained about the poor writing of the Loa and the Atzlanti.

                  The books are descriptive enough that you can get by, but if you feel inclined to research the mythologies of the pantheons, you can do this for free thanks to the Internet. Almost all the pantheons and their stories can be found online, so it's not that hard to familiarize yourself with them.

                  As others have said: Hero, Demigod, and God are essential if you want to explore all the tiers of power in Scion. As always, however, the caveat must be repeated: Scion's system works fine at Hero level, but at Demigod and upward, things begin to break down. By God-level, the system is pretty much a destroyed mess. I haven't met a Scion group yet that plays the game without substantial house ruling.

                          World of Darkness is what got me introduced to WhiteWolf in the first place. While I do enjoy the supernatural horror setting of that game, I think the idea of a super hero theme environment is also appealing. And let's not pretend they aren't super heroes, the whole Fate Binding thing practically screams "Secret Identity" for players looking to avoid such things.
                          It's not just that mortals stop being a credible threat to you at the higher levels. The system itself falls apart, even when you're fighting giants and dragons and other gods. I'm not as system-savvy as others on this board, so I'm not the best qualified to talk about the system's inadequacies, but it's been almost universally acknowledged as a real problem and one of the main motivators for the 2E reboot.

                          Among the biggest problems are the fact that Dex pretty much reigns supreme among the combat stats. If your character doesn't have Epic Dex, he/she won't hit anything in combat that does have have it. You also can't evade incoming attacks from Dex-heavy characters. So if you have a character design that doesn't feature extreme Dexterity, then that character is going to be in big trouble in combat. That's just one example.

                          And Scions are totally superheroes. If Scions want to avoid Fatebinding, then they do need to try to maintain a low profile. On the other hand, Fatebinding can offer benefits as well as drawbacks, so a Scion who chooses to throw caution to the wind isn't exactly doomed either.
