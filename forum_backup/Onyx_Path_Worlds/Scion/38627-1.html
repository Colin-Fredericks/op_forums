                Mao Zedong is considered Shen right now Or at least there are, so I'm told by journalists on the China beat, actual offerings made before pictures of him just as if he were a god.

                As Telgar says, you have to cherry-pick your mythic elements and maybe hammer on them a bit to turn them into a somewhat-coherent and playable game setting. The trick, I think, lies in developing an imaginative sympathy for the mythology: To creatively misunderstand the details (to use critic Harold Bloom's notion of "creative misprision") while trying to stay true to the emotional core of what the stories mean to the people who tell them. Though it can be difficult when you look at a very big room through very small windows.

                For instance, I had some arguments with other writers about the CB's Virtues. Now, Virtues are a pretty rough way to encapsulate the essential attitudes of a mythology and the culture that creates it, but it's what Scion gives us. One of my first decisions was that the selection of Virtues that Scion: Hero gave to Tommy Li (p. 252) was just plain wrong. You can make a case for each of Duty, Harmony, Loyalty and Order, but the whole package is too one-sided. It seems to be a common image of China, but as I looked at how the heroes of actual Chinese legend and literature behave, I saw that they just aren't so stuffily Confucian. The Handsome Monkey King is Chaos incarnate. Nezha is an impetuous punk. Guan Yu begins his career by killing an oppressive landlord/local official and going on the run as an outlaw. There's something wrong if the CB Virtue set doesn't represent this rambunctious quality of so many Chinese heroes. So, Order goes and Valor takes its place.

                As a starting-point for a version of the Devas that doesn't suck, would anyone venture an opinion about what the Deva Virtues should be? If you feel ambitious, go down the list of Virtues and explain how each one does or doesn't apply to Hinduism and its heroic tales.

                Dean Shomshak

                    WARNING: Wall of text.

                    Conviction: Conviction implies not only a 'I'm right' feeling but also a subtle 'you're wrong' along with that. As such, personal needs must bow to Duty. Not a good choice.

                    Courage: Kali is the only real berserker in the Pantheon, and her rages are always things that eventually need to be stopped. Most of the Deva prefer to deal with their enemies through Intellect, like Vishnu's many escapades and Sarasvati's punking of Kumbhakarna. Even in war, an ideal set of rules is supposed to be followed, it's not a free-for-all.

                    Duty: The Big One. Dharma trumps most everything in Hindu myth and culture. Bheeshma and Drona in the Mahabharata fight against their most beloved students because of Duty. Ekalavya cuts off his own thumb, rendering the best archer in the world unable to ever pick up a bow, because of Duty. King Dashrath agrees to send his favoured son into exile because of Duty. Hanuman fights his own son because of both their Duties. The list goes on.

                    Endurance: While the image of the sadhu meditating in the mountaintop is probably enough to award Endurance to the Deva, it should be noted that the Deva display more than just the asceticism part of Endurance. They also display the Norse type of Endurance, of the non-stop party variety. Sure, Parvati spends eons without eating and Bhagirath meditates for many an age, but Ganesha eats and eats until his stomach bursts open, and Indra has epic drinking binges. Sarasvati holds marathon concerts, and Shiva dances till he drops (he doesn't drop). Endurance is also a shoe-in.

                    Expression: I'm really not sure. India has a long tradition of arts and music, but then so do many other cultures. We do not, however, have a dedicated role in society dedicated to Expression like the Norse skalds or Irish bards. Then again, neither do the Greeks. Overall, I would not oppose it, but like I said, I'm not entirely sure as to what goes into awarding a Pantheon Expression.

                    Harmony: Split down the middle. The Vedic Deva, being almost all nature deities, are heavily invested in Harmony. The post-Vedic Deva, however, not so much. I mean, the Trimurti and Co. Regularly allow their worshippers to hang out in the Overworld, despite the natural place for them being the Underworld. However, Harmony with nature is still an important part of Hindu culture, and I think this Virtue is a strong candidate, especially as a nod at the most ancient ideals of Hinduism.

                    Intellect: An important one. Respect for knowledge and those who have it is an important part of Hindu culture, and even the Gods prefer to deal with foes with Intellect instead of brute force. This is to the point where sages and wise men regularly curse even the Gods in many myths. I'd say Intellect is quite appropriate.

                    Loyalty: Like Valour below, Loyalty is a Virtue that is considered ideal, but ultimately bows to Duty. Vibheeshana in the Ramayana is lauded for his decision to go against his brother Ravana. Brothers Indra and Agni, and erstwhile best friends Drona and Drupad fight in the Mahabharata. Not an overall good choice.

                    Order: A confusing one. For one, the upholding of laws and authority, while considered a good thing in the culture is not a giant symbol of it, the way the Pharaoh of Egypt or the Code of Hammurabi are. However, the concept of Universal Order, Rta, is quite important to the Vedic Deva, while not really much of a priority for the Trimurti and Co. As yet another consideration, almost all the Gods in charge of maintaining Rta (Mitra, Bhaga, Aryaman, Asura Mahat Varuna himself), are almost all Yazata. Overall, I'd say there are better Virtues.

                    Piety: Hinduism is possibly the only religion that discusses atheism and considers it an equally valid way to enlightenment. As such, the belief in the superiority of your own Pantheon is non-existent, and Piety can be safely ignored.

                    Valour: While fair, gentlemanly fights are considered ideal, there are a number of cases in Hindu myth when the heroes are willing to break the rules. The Kurukshetra War in the Mahabharata is a prime example. As such, Valour is not an ideal Virtue for the Deva.

                    Vengeance: Not a Virtue often exhibited often. The only ones I can think of are Bali in the Ramayana and Gandhari at the end of the war in the Mahabharata, the former of which is always painted in a negative light. A Virtue that can be easily skipped.

                    Going by all that, I'd say Duty, Endurance and Intellect are definitely Deva Virtues. The choice of fourth one is difficult, but I'd go with either Expression or Harmony.

                                        India's greatest contribution to the World, the invention of zero, seems to me to fall firmly in the realm of Intellect. That being said, I am increasingly beginning to feel that Expression might be the better choice. Telgar is right: A number of the Gods are mentioned as being expert artists. I mean, there's the obvious, Sarasvati, but Shiva is supposed to be an excellent instrumentalist along with the Lord of the Dance. The image of Krishna playing the flute is pretty central to his followers. Both great Epics and Kalidasa's Shakuntala are on the list of 100 greatest books ever-written, and Indian music has had some popularity on the world stage through Pt. Ravi Shankar's collaborations with Yehudi Menuhin and George Harrison. And perhaps most importantly, the arts were/are pretty important parts of the fabric of Indian culture. Mythology describes music as a gift of the Gods to Man and a vehicle for enlightenment, to the point that certain melodies are considered out and out capable of magic, kind of like Finnish mythology.

                                        At the end of the day, if you're going to focus purely on the Vedic era, then a case might be made for Harmony, but if you are going to include the Trimurti and other Gods of the Classical era, then my personal choice for Deva Virtues would be: Duty, Endurance, Expression and Intellect.

                                                A pantheon gets Expression if poetry, music, literature, etc. are vehicles of power. It isn't just that the culture has art -- everybody has art -- but that art is itself valued so much it becomes a form of power. Sounds like Hinduism qualifies.

                                                Biggest example of Endurance I can think of is Shiva's throat. At the Churning of the Ocean at the beginning of the world, there's a flood of poison so deadly it could destroy the nascent universe. So Shiva drinks it -- but he can't swallow it. It's so dangerous it would destroy even him! He holds it suspended in his throat for all time, neither out in the World nor reaching his stomach. Shiva's throat is pictured as tinted blue to show the presence of this poison. Presumably Shiva does not find this pleasant, but the Supreme Ascetic will hold the poison there forever so the World may continue to exist.
