    Most of the Titan Callings have associations that can be drawn to 'normal' Callings, which can make for some easy-ish substitutions'.
    • Adversary to Trickster, as pointed out above
    • Destroyer to Warrior is harder, but doable.
    • Monster shares more with Hunter than I thought, but is easiest the weakest link among the associations.
    • Tyrant to Leader, obviously.

    Primeval has no equivalent.

    So if one needs too, we have some options to 'godify' or 'titanify' entities.
    As for Prometheus...the sample born scion of his in the adversary section (NOT THE MARRIED COUPLE) kinda implies a recent Mantle change. From a peace advocate who disappears for years to take care of 'family matters' to someone who shows up to conscript his own child into a war. And if Mantles are in play, there maybe a God Prometheus and a Titan Prometheus, who could even be the same person.

                I think the looking for correspondences thing is not helpful for making these Callings actually work. Rather, one should invest that thought into how they are distinct from the already existing Callings. I still haven't taken time to read that section thoroughly yet (I have been hung up on my discontents with the Pantheon-specific sections), but my surface-level impression of the mechanics is favorable. Destroyer, Monster, and Adversary all seem like good design options for me to flesh out not just Titans, but also Gods like Shiva or Sobek and of course their potential Scions, too. A lot of what is now contained in Titanic Callings until now overburdened existing Callings, specifically Liminal, which has to take care of underworld people, travelers, and all manner of outsiders. A lot of antisocial figures can now be expressed more specifically, I like that. It makes for powers that fit individual characters better. I agree, however, that Tyrant is the most difficult to distinguish from an earlier counterpart, namely Leader. Conceptually, I'm playing with analogies like "Leader is rule through charisma, competence, divine right, etc., Tyrant is rule through usurpation and force," but I'm not quite happy with that yet either.

                            Sacerdos is following the same line of logic I am here. The idea I had with the parallel Callings is provide ideas for alternate Mantles. Heck two of the sample Tuatha De Domnu Titans ARE Nementondevos deities. Even this book plays a little loose with definition of Titan. Remember, Callings are an out-of-game concept, unlike Purviews. Gods don't technically know about Callings, though it is naive to say they haven't noticed certain behaviour groupings. The Titan Callings are a bit more removed from a healthy human mindset (much like Titanic Virtues), which is one of the indicators I assume the more scholarly deities use to identify Titans.

                            Mind you Titanmachy appears to focus on the Titans that want to oppose the Gods, without erasing more...passive Titans. Danu certainly has a Primeval Calling in one of Mantles, Shiva has got to have a mantle with Destroyer Calling somewhen/where. Neither are considered Titans. Even with these new Callings, 'Titan' is a political term. It is just Gods can spot behaviours associated with these Callings; they know not to get too close to an Adversary, they worry about protecting their things/people/etc from Destroyers, recognise the disassociation that occurs with the Primeval Calling, they abhor Tyrants and as for Monsters...well, many Heroes make their names through monster-slaying.

                            I DO think there SHOULD be Titans without one of the new Callings. But that is such a minor issue, I'm not planning on reporting it as an inconsistency; I'm content to house rule it.

                                                Originally posted by Sacerdos View Post

                                                Why was it decided this figure needed a spelling difference for splitting, but e.g. Cernunnos didn't? Is there deeper meaning to that?

                                                Most Titans and dragons have multiple names and Mantles, making it possible for a Storyguide to assign one variant to a Titan and one to a dragon, at least in out-of-game conversation. Referring to the dragon only as Apep, and the Titan only as Apophis, makes it immediately clear to which entity the Storyguide refers when she says, “Apep’s followers were responsible for that bombing last session, remember?”
                                                For those Titans and dragons who have only one name and no Mantles but exist on both sides of the aisle, Storyguides may wish to assign a color signifier, or simply make it explicit to which entity they refer by calling them “The Dragon [Name]” and “The Titan [Name].”
                                                Titanomachy, pp 80-81
