    Okay, I clearly missed the fact that the 3 maximum on a attribute applied to player characters as well. Also, thinking back to it you also have the ability to spend 5xp to change your favored approach from the xp cost chart. I'm not going to lie, I dont see the benefit of a favored approach or arena as its being called here at all. I also absolutely hate mechanics that make people pay xp they will never get back simply to change their character which is likely intended for story purposes. It really hurts the player trying to make an organic character while promoting staying steadfast and unchanging. I've always encouraged the spend the xp temporary idea in those cases... spend 5 xp but gain it back 1 or 2 xp a session for free until you are back to the same xp total as everyone else.

    I really think this 3 cap on stats + favored approach needs to be scrapped... or atleast could you please give us some insight into why this is useful from a gameplay perspective? We don't need mechanics that feel like a quick creation tool. This game is designed for intelligent people who care to delve into the intricacies of mythology... we need options, not simplicity. Especially when defining our characters through their attributes and skills.

    Neall, Could you please give us some insight into why this system was chosen? As well as the benefits you have seen? Thank you.

    EDIT: Personally, id rather see the 1-5 dots in attributes and 1-5 dots in skills like we are used to and we know works. Rereading it it sounds like we will have a certain number of points to simply divide among Physical Mental and Social and all attributes within those categories are set to the same number. 3 Physical for instance giving you 3 Might, Dex, and Stamina and then you simply getting a +2 in whichever approach you chose. So a force approach would then yield 5 Might, 3 Dex, and 3 Stamina.

    Is that all correct? Or is an approach simply +2 and you can still raise your attributes to 5 through advancement? To be honest, this is giving me ptsd flashbacks to how confusing the damage system was on the first try as well... and we still haven't been able to see how that looks now.

    Not trying to be overly critical... I just don't want to see this game become so narrative based that you lose diversity in character builds. Narrative Crunch Hybrid with a little more emphasis on narrative is awesome... but 80% narrative will push alot of fans away. In my opinion, 2nd Edition is something that would benefit from being something that pays a good amount of honor to the 1st edition but advances it to the more modern narrative approach that is popular at the moment. A blend of things for the old and new gamer.

    EDIT2: If ultimately the system has many optional rules that allow us to get something more akin to the 1-5 attrib and 1-5 skill then there really won't even be a problem. Any idea when we will get to see those optional rules? I'm also particularly interested in seeing how all things combat, damage, and armor works.

                    Regarding NPC generation... I basically said exactly what you did the first time I saw Chronicles of Darkness and it was a big reason why I switched very quickly from traditional d20. If a npc in a scene needs to do something I just think of what their attribute value for that action would be (1 = deficient, 2 = average, 3 = above average, 4 = Exceptional, 5 = pinnacle of man) then think of their skill level, (0 = untrained (add a penalty), 1 = novice, 2 = could make a living off of it, 3 = stands out among their professional peers, 4 = Outstanding skill, could likely lead entire teams, a company, or make developments in the field, and 5 = a god damn legend in the field that really leaves very little or nothing to criticize) then add 1-4 if needed based on additional skills/specialties/unique circumstances and apply any 9 or 8 again properties based on how routine the action would be for them. That forms the dice pool and then I roll it. Simple.

                    The whole process takes no more than 5-10 seconds if you put even a mild amount of thought into the npc before the session or while the players are talking among themselves.

                            Well, with the rules as written and that we know of it is impossible to make a guy with 4 Might 2 dex and 5 stamina. (EDIT ADD IN: Admittedly, this already required me to allow them to use power, finesse, and resistance as the 3 categories for attributes in CofD... but even something like that isn't present in StoryPath as we know it despite that house rule's popularity in storyteller.) A super tough and exceptionally strong guy who isnt deficient in dex but isn't great either. So mathematically it is already impossible to say it dones't reduce character diversity. With the system as is, id have to go with Physical 3 with a focus in resistance to even get close to what I wanted. That would give him 3 Might, 3 Dex, and 3 (+2 on rolls) stamina. Id have to then pick and choose among knacks or whatever else is available to try and make him stronger or seemingly stronger. That's more confusing then just giving me more control over my attributes. It's been mentioned that there is an option to make favored approach be a single attribute... but that doesnt fix that issue. Maybe if your favored approach was made up of any 3 attributes to more personalize your character but even that is needlessly limited and couldn't represent the character stat array above.

                            As the system is, your attributes within each "arena" will all be the same base value before favored approach is applied. So we have people talking about what about the strong guy whos not smart... but what about the exceptionally manipulative person who has no social presence and otherwise wouldn't stand out at all? I really wish there is an optional rule to place points more freely and eliminate favored approach. I am really worried that favored approach will be referenced to by knacks, boons, and other aspects of the system and that it will be very difficult to phase out without rewriting alot of things... which would put us right back where we were with 1st edition in terms of adding additional work.

                            Trust me, I get it. Storypath isn't storyteller. Onyx Path wants their own system to base games off and show their vision to the world. This is awesome. But my concern is that they are focusing too much on this painting characters in broad strokes approach due to the epic scale of the settings attached to the system. The negative to this approach is that mortal games are going to feel goofy. Mortals who are painted in broad strokes are likely going to feel uncomplex/bland and tropey which will reduce what you can do with the setting.

                            I'm still holding hope that they don't want to limit diversity and that the information in the rest of origin and hero will allow you add the layer of diversity you need to separate characters but at the same time im nervous that with the delay we had already that the system is basically set in stone and may not have accounted for some things. There is plenty of time left though, Onyx Path will have plenty of time to create optional rules to help out players who don't want character creation to be painted with broad strokes but if favored approach is basically referenced everywhere in the first two books then im worried its going to be difficult to add in alternatives for people who don't like it without giving them a whole lot of homework. This is further frustrated by the fact they have been keeping system details so secretive since the delay.

                            I just want a system that is going to be diverse and have lasting potential. I want to play Scion for ridiculous amounts of years in the future. I want to be able to tell intricate stories within all the tiers of the World. This isn't a post trying to complain to the devs and make em feel bad. This is more so a post of concern from a person who may be too big of a fan of Scion's setting for my own good.

                            Even a minor post detailing some info into the design perspective that determined these mechanics would help get a grasp of things and sooth people's concerns I'm sure. If this is about them having serious passion for their vision of the game then I'm going to respect it alot more. We need to let Onyx Path embrace their proper vision of the game so that they have the passion to support it for the long haul. I wholeheartedly believe that. More transparency on the reasons behind their decisions to use these particular systems would go a long way. I feel confident that I can houserule the system enough that my group will love it, but what about the groups that don't have the experience with system editing that I do and never buy any additional books in the line? That's going to hurt the future of Scion.

                            Hopefully this long post adequately describes my (and some other's) concerns.

                                                        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

                                                        If a starting character defaults to 1/2/3 in Arenas and +2 across a Focus, that's the same as each Attribute starting at 1, with 15 to assign as you desire.
                                                        Actually, it isnt. The sum of the number might be the same, but the array of possibilities is widely different.

                                                        I made a fast math, and I got that the Storypath, in their Attributes only, have 18 possibilities. The Storyteller has 16200 different outcomes in attributes. Dis-considering the ones that let a character with a 1 in an attribute (for regular human sake) there are 324 different outcomes. (if I mathed wrong, please someone tell me in PM, and I edit here). The fact is, the Storypath system allow very little room for variation in the attributes part. But this is easily fixed without altering the Arena/Approach rules. If we have the option to start with maybe flaws or merits that affect specific attributes in a more roleplaying way (like a flaw that make your full dexterity only work out of combat, due to lack of training or something) then that wouldnt bother me so much.

                                                        The situation I see here is that the game is made with a certain kind of character in mind, so the rules for attributes seem to be made to specifically accommodate that kind of character. That is not a problem, at least not in my eyes, as it allows a more focused gameplay that facilitates the game for that kind of character. We had the inverse problem in Scion 1ed, where a system for mortals tried to fit with Gods and Monsters, and became cumbersome. My problem with the actual attribute system is a simple one: why is the Approach/Arena rules are better for the Scion game?

                                                        I dont see much advantage to compensate to the lack of freedom in the creation of the character. Maybe they decided that is the best way to interpret mythological beings, maybe they have the whole system build upon it and it worked so well that the lost of freedom on the attribute part seems worth it. As we dont know the whole game yet, I prefer to refrain myself from saying that it is a bad system, but I'm already thinking about how to house rule it. Hopefully the system will be great, with enough perks and different accessories to make each character unique and different, and all this house ruling wont be needed.

                                                        Or they could explain to us why this attribute system choice instead of the regular Storyteller system. That would be very cool and would end this discussion, at least for me. But I trust they are trying what they believe to be the best for the game.

                                                        About the rest of the system, I liked it. All seem very organic, very natural, but still better than Storyteller to tell a story. There is less focus on "grit and dark reality", and more in the story telling aspect of a game that I like. The complications seem more natural to the character, the Compensation (or consolation, I dont remember now) is a great meta-idea for the game. The different levels for different characteristics of different beings is also a great idea, helping a lot to make a God fight in the same game as a Satyr. Even the little hinted about the damage in other updates seem surprisingly creative.

                                                        I would be lying if I said I didnt like it very much, even if the Attribute section still bothers me, and I'm really expecting great things from this edition.
