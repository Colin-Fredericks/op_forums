                        In comparative religion, "Monotheism" refers to the belief in a supreme deity who is unique in power, but not in existence. Nobody in the world believes in only one deity-level supernatural entity. Christianity, for instance, has a spectrum of power from God Almighty through Satan, the Virgin Mary (at least for Catholics and Orthodox; not so much for Protestants), and various grades of saints and angels. (Not even bringing in the subtleties of Trinitarian doctrine.) Plenty of other mythologies and religions -- the Afro-Caribbean faiths, for instance -- posit a Supreme Deity as well; but in many of them, the Supreme Deity just sits around being Supreme while the subordinate deities do the interesting stuff and receive the active worship (because they might actually do something for you).

                                            One thing I'm anticipating about 2e is that it's going to have a much more flexible mythology framework. Yes, we're still going to have the Hero/Demigod/God “spine” with a pantheon of gods at the top, for the most part; but unlike 1e, it won't stop there. Origin will present priests, prophets, and sorcerers; scions themselves will have a variety of geneses, not merely literal children of gods; mythological beings will be a thing beyond being “Birthrights” for Scions; 1e's concept of “Greater Titans” is being stripped of its inherently antagonistic elements and expanded into the concept of Primordials, which can arguably serve as “Godheads” for the surprising number of pantheons that include such a concept. Not every “pantheon” (by which I mean mythological constructs) is going to use all of these elements; and you can get some very different results by picking and choosing which elements a given mythology emphasizes.

                                            The Abrahamic faiths should be able to fit within this expanded mythological structure quite well, albeit in a very unorthodox manner: the traditional “spine” I mentioned earlier is likely to be all but nonexistent (Orthodox Christianity allows for exactly one Scion, while Judaism and Islam don't even allow for that; though some “spin-offs” like Mormonism allow for something closer to the Scion concept), with just about everything being handled outside of that spine: priests and prophets, possibly a rare Chosen Scion or equivalent (see Sampson), angels as what 1e called Lesser Immortals (a term that I hope 2e abandoned it implies an inferiority that isn't necessarily there), and a “Godhead” Primordial heading it up.

                                            If 2e keeps the notion that Primordials transcend mythology (as evidenced by the mythology-specific Avatars each Greater Titan manifested in 1e), I could see a single Primordial serving as the “Godhead” for several monotheistic faiths, including the ancient Egyptian episode where Aten worship was elevated to exclusionary monotheism status. But I'd want to emphasize the Mantle system that allows one god (or in this case, one Avatar) to belong to multiple Pantheons, taking on a slightly different face in each; and I'd definitely want to emphasize the fact that Primordials aren't inherently evil the way 1e's Greater Titans were.

                                            As for the Order of Divine Glory: this, I'd like to see restricted to a Shard that's designed specifically to pit monotheistic faiths against Pantheons: in this Shard, the idea is that the Order's Fate-Binding has the potential to retroactively exclude the Pantheons from the World, something that the Pantheons obviously have every reason to want to prevent. But I'd only want it to a Shard: in the default setting, I'd prefer the supernatural backing for Abrahamic faiths to be henotheistic rather than monotheistic, even if the Origins level still speaks in monotheistic terms.

                                            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

Especially gods from different faiths, who their holy books decry as being false and blasphemous.
