    Tips and tricks for a new campaign, 2nd E

    07-18-2020, 08:50 PM
    Hello everybody,

    we are starting a short story in Scion 2nd edition. I'm an experienced ST in Exalted (2nd and 3rd) and wanted to do something more grounded and thus an idea for a iron/bronze level story was born while reading through the corebook.

    The story is a shallow rewriting of a German myth about the "Drachenfels" near Bonn that allows for some research and action packed scenes. More of an action movie approach to the setting than a philosophical one. But that is just fine for my group. Now a brief summary.
    A cult prepared a ritual lasting 3 times 7 days to bring a beast back to life that was known as a dragon back in the day. Maybe it was, maybe it wasn't. The "cult" are those that share the blood of said dragon or at least think that they do. The ritual involves several blood sacrifices and lifes taken in specific fashion. The players are meant to be the last sacrifices as they - as well as others - carry the blood of those that killed the dragon in the past. They meet before that though, as they always meet, when the murders happen. A corpse is drifting in the river? They are there. Somebody jumps from the 4th floor of a building? They all witness it. And some people start suspecting things.

    The PCs are origin level.

    Now, is there anything that would be better to know before running the game? Are there any special quirks of the system that I need to be aware of?
    I'm not exactly looking for adice on how to tell the story but more on running the system side of things.

    Thank you in advance.

      I've got some general advice for running a story.

      So your premise sounds fine, sounds like fun. Sounds like a great Origin campaign.

      Now, and I cannot stress this enough, make sure you and your players are on the same page. Maybe have a Google Drive that all of you can access where players can write down their hopeful Deeds, since those generally will shape the campaign, as well as tell you what the players want to do.

      In terms of mechanics, my advice? Do NOT try to front-load. I find things like Antagonists in Scion 2e to be easier to work with than the QC of Ex3 and the like, but still. Lowball your players. It's easier to throw weaker foes at them and spend Tension to upgrade them than to throw something too strong at them.

      Also, part of "Do not front-load" would be just breaking the mechanics into digestible chunks. Don't expect your players, or you, to know all the mechanics at once. Gate parts of them off depending on what you're playing. This feels like a Mystery focused game, so start more on the Investigation play, with research montages, maybe bring up Stunts in the context of what extra successes mean when you're in the library looking up court records. Focus on those at first. Then maybe lead into Action sections a couple sessions in, when they ask the Wrong Questions and have to do a Chase scene to get away, or maybe end up surrounded by mooks and have to fight their way out.

      Disclaimer: In favor of fun and enjoyment, but may speak up to warn you that you're gonna step on a metaphorical land mine

              I wouldn't put a complication on everything, and I personally really dislike the Complications of "If you don't pay this off, you fail" even if there are a couple in the books. I prefer complications to be "You succeed, but-" in nature. Example: Your players are asking around at a shady bar. Maybe Difficulty 2 for the Intel gathering, but put in a 1 point complication where if they dom't buy it off, then they're overheard asking questions and may encounter some thugs later. Don't make Complications happen everywhere, make them crop up from time to time in ways to make the story more interesting.

              As for successes, the base 1-5 difficulty spread will do for Origin. Your PCs won't be getting a huge number of successes on every roll at Origin, given that Enhancement isn't as widespread at Origin.

              Remember that Enhancement caps at 3 without magic, 5 with magic, and that Scale breaks the Enhancement cap. Also Scale is scary and I would advise not using it very often at Origin tier, especially not before you get the basics down.

              As for stunts, in Scion 2e Stunts are ways you succeed more, rather than Exalted's "Describe something cool but you may fail"

                  Thanks again for the advice.

                  So we had our first real session and everything went well enough. I think I really like everything concerning Paths. Momentum is fun. Deeds are very handy to create common ground.

                  I also quickly realized that complications should be used sparingly. But when they fit well into the game they are fun. I also allowed complications to be bought off even when the difficulty wasn't reached. I honestly don't know if that is intended but it worked out well as most complications were used in a social context. "Persuade the officer to reveal additional informations" -> Dif 2 but Comp 1 to not arouse additional suspicion. It enabled the player characters to fail in a more controlled way and everyone liked that.

                  Stunts I need to get used to. I find it surprisingly hard to think of additional degrees of success on the fly while also pricing them appropriate. For now I went back to an Exalted approach. Let the player describe the action. If I get a good idea for an additional stunt I communicate the cost/effect. If not I ask if there is something more he'd like to accomplish with that description and if it fits it gets it own stunt. That may make mixed actions a bit redundant or at least less clear, but it worked.

                  We had real problems with determining what constitutes a roll for "awareness". That was a major discussion we had because nothing really fits. After that I looked around here on the forum and found a similiar thread that made a few thinks clearer. But I still think that everything concerning "senses" isn't done well in Scion 2nd E.

                  We also had a fight against a single enemy. There were 5 player characters but only 4 were actively engaging in the fight because the last one was trying to keep others away while also double botching his rolls.
                  The enemy was some kind of shadow doppelganger that tried to shove one of the sacrifices out of the window. It had special rules for movement (couldn't be moved outside of the victims shadow and always moved with the shadow) 3 Health, 2 Defense, 1 Soft Armor and a dice pool equaling the victims dice pool + enhancements depending on the time of day/length of the shadow (in this case 6 dice +2E at the start and after a smart player idea down to +1E).
                  The fight ended with 1 bruise condition on a player and a bruised condition on the victim. In a battle where they don't need to protect the victim I'm pretty confident that they could at least take 3 Professional Archetype enemies without serious injuries. If they play smart and are not in a bad position they could probably take out 4 to 5 without a problem.

                  But before testing that assumption I'm gonna do a battle with mooks + leader in the next session to see how superior numbers change the flow of a fight.

                  Scale I'm probably not gonna use at all. Maybe if the decide to crash a car at 200 km/h into an enemy or something. But I probably won't use it for encounter design. I want to see how the "base" system plays out first.

                  I believe we had a single roll with 5 successes. Most rolls had between 1 and 2 successes. So my current ranking is 1 for "easy" 2 for "medium" and 3 for "hard" difficulty. Enhancements are a bit weird though with that raking. They don't really help to succeed in the first place with very easy rolls but if the character succeeds he does so very well even though the action in itself feels weirdly difficult because the chance to fail is still quite high. But maybe that is an Exalted thing because I'm used to TN 7.

                      Scion 2e has made me rethink my idea of a General Awareness Thing and honestly for the game feeling, I don't think it's really needed.

                      Like, it even went back to thinking about if I should make these things rolls? Say, if your game depends on players making a roll to spot tracks or notice something, then should you even make it a roll? And from there, don't think of it as "What's their awareness?" and think of it more as "What are they trying to notice?" Are they trying to spot someone trying to look casual before launching a Sneak Attack? That could be Empathy or Subterfuge. Are they noticing that the gleam of light isn't from a window but is from a sniper scope? Firearms, because they know guns. Are they noticing that the forest has gotten too quiet? That'd be Survival.

                      As for Stunts, well, the ones in the book are pretty handy. In a Hero game I had my Tuatha facing down an Asura who had quite a few weapons, and while he was rampaging to avenge his Bandmate, my character was able to do quite a bit by not only injuring quite brutally, but also making good use of the Sunder stunt to disable the enemy's arsenal.

                          I'm currently preparing a larger battle against the Vodyanoy from the Origin core book (p. 160 - 161).

                          They have the Shroud Flair which states: "All attacks made against shrouded characters suffer a +2 complication to the Inflict Damage Stunt."

                          A complication does not need to be bought. So it would go kind of like this?

                          - Attack
                          - Attack hits
                          - Attacker buys "Inflict Damage Stunt" for 0 (because there is no actual soft armor for archetype enemies as everything is folded into the defense value)
                          - There are not enough successes after the attack to buy off the +2 Complication from the Shroud Flair.
                          - Complication causes something detrimental to the attacker.

                          I don't really have a good idea what this complication could be. Especially considering that this could easily happen multiple times in the fight.
                          Maybe something like this?

                          Condition: Shrouded in fog
                          Effect: You were lured into the enemy's mist and are open to attacks from close range. Suffer -1 to your defense value against all attacks from the attacked enemy.
                          Resolution: Move a range band.

                          Then to change it up a bit, maybe something like this:

                          Condition: Stuck in the swamp
                          Effekt: You were lured into a small but deep part of the swamp. Movement based actions and defensive dodge actions take a -2 penalty and you need 2 movement actions to move a range band.
                          Resolution: Pull free. Might + Athletics against difficulty 1.

                          Is this as intended? Too much? Not enough? I have no idea.
