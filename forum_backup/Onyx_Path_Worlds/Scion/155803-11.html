                    The Animal Purview is actually complete, it was either the first or second Purview to be totally translated over. Is there anything you are looking for in an Animal Purview revision that it doesn't have? I could talk to Iry about it later today if it is.

                    For emotional manipulation, that is broken up across various Purviews and Epic Attributes. Darkness and Negative Epic Appearance have some Fear and Terror elements. Sun, and Charisma have overwhelming Hope and Adoration in there. Fire has some 'Supercharged Emotion' powers. Epic Manipulation and Epic Charisma are the 'core' pieces for playing with emotions though. The Emotion chain of Epic Charisma Knacks are my personal favorite.

                    If you want to play someone who works like Cupid, or other Love-Causing entities, the way we have normally addressed it in our games is through very powerful Relics altering how Engender Love works. Making it cheaper, potentially free when targeting mortals, making it permanent (that would be up there in power with The Helm of Darkness or Lugh's Spear though) and other such little twists to the power.

                    Originally posted by Manannansun View Post
                    And just one last question. When are the Aesir due to be completed? I apologize if I seem rude, but I found it funny that one of the most popular pantheons remains unfinished.
                    At one point the Aesir were someone I was looking at revising, but they have since fallen to the wayside for two big reasons which I can explain.

                    First off, we are waiting for Scion Second Edition to come out. We want to get our hands on the new system and see if we want to make a swap, and if we do, we need to decide how we want to go about using our updated Pantheons. We have not stopped working on Scion though, myself and Sacerdos have been stockpiling research, making in-depth outlines, doing all the 'fluff,' the cultural notes, potential Deity lists, that sort of thing for Pantheons. With luck, when Scion Second Edition comes out, we will be able to get three or four Pantheons up and running far quicker than normal.

                    And that ties into our second reason. When we finished the Kami, Sacerdos and myself spent some time talking about what to do next. The Kami were a 'step up' from other Pantheons I had prodded at previously. It was a massive undertaking which I wouldn't have been able to complete by myself for a ton of reasons. I want to keep up that level of writing and research, I want big, stupidly long Pantheons with cultural notes, talking about fun little bits of cosmology, everything. I initially was thinking of looking at the Aesir, or the Shen, but we decided to not revise a Pantheon in favor of creating entirely new ones.

                    These new Pantheons we have stockpiled research for are intentionally all non-European, we want to widen up the choice for people.

                    The Shiunesh, the Pantheon of the Hittite Empire are amazingly fun. A Pantheon who frequently almost brings total extinction to their mortal population for failing to honor them correctly. They really serve as a nice little addition to the Eastern Mediterranean, the Pantheon having a real-world long time friendship with the Netjer. Gotham By Night had started looking into writing up the Shiunesh before they closed their doors, so we thought it would be nice to quench anyone's thirst that had been kicked off by that.

                    The Abosom, the Pantheon of the Ashanti Empire, are also awesome. Where the Shiunesh are very 'classical Scion' with a Titan War, imprisoned Titans, all of that, the Abosom are a big jump away from standard Scion. They have a Supreme God who makes sure that mortals have a nice life more or less, a very friendly cosmology. They don't have any imprisoned Titans, or have a ton in that department at all. Nothing openly antagonistic on the scale of Apep or even just a random Fomori. They are the cultural group who has Anansi hanging around with them who is also really awesome.

                    The Sgā’na Qeda’s, the Pantheon of the Haida First Nation are also great. They are again a slight deviation from your standard Scion Pantheon. They have a Supreme God, but they are nowhere as involved as Nyame and more or less just stay distant from the world and even the Supernatural Beings themselves. Xhuuya, commonly known as Raven, is from this Pantheon and he is a ton of fun. They have three 'groups' of Divine Entities, rather than having one single 'group' as many Pantheons do. Essentially, they have three different Overworlds each with its own internal hierarchy. The Hlk’yaans xàaydaraay, the Supernatural Beings who reside in Diidguii, The Forest. The Tsaran xàaydaraay, the Supernatural Beings who reside in Siigee, The Sea. The Sgā’na xā’-idAga-i, the Supernatural Beings who reside in Qō’ya-iqā’gAn, The Sky.

                    The Enduri, the Pantheon of the Manchu, are the group we are still digging into. I am helping Sacerdos with them, he has the reigns for them, and is doing a great job. We have both been very busy with finishing our final years of our Undergrads, and working, so they are not yet finished. But, with luck, I should have some free time coming up to hammer into them and actually read the articles and take notes for Sacerdos. They are a really cool Pantheon from what I have seen so far, almost all Goddesses, and they have an amazing creation myth.

                    Scion 2e Homebrew Projects:
                    The Šiuneš, The Enduri, The Sgā’na Qeda’s, The Abosom, Lebor Óe In Dea, The Zemi, Nemetondevos: Revised, and Mysteries of the Otherworld.

                                            Originally posted by Manannansun View Post
                                            What sort of psp would you give the Plant Annwn or the Plant Don?
                                            Hm, a particularly tricky question. I have done some course work on Welsh literature, but they are not the region I am best at. The Irish and the Gauls are the two Celtic regions I am best versed in, the Welsh, despite having read a bunch of their literature, is something I haven't explored as deeply as Ireland or Gaul.

                                            I can think of some concepts that might be fruitful for a Pantheon Specific Purview however. The one that I have batted around in my head the most is the idea of Fate, but in an entirely different way than the Aesir approach it. What Welsh literature is particularly valuable for is illustrating a perception of Fate which is rather unique compared to the other Western/Northern European groups. Where as the Aesir have a predeterministic Fate system, that What Will Be, Will Be, the Welsh can be seen operating on a different wavelength. Llew Llaw Gyffes and Math fab Mathonwy both have 'Fate Clauses' on them. Math fab Mathonwy must have a virgin footholder when he is not at war or he would die. Llew Llaw Gyffes has a really weirdly specific series of clauses relating to his death. This idea is most evident with the 'Death Clauses,' but they exist in other areas, such as the curses Arianrhod places on her son Llew, all of which are conditional which allows Math to help skirt around them.

                                            The exact mechanical system for this would probably really depend on the Fate system someone is using, but I have some rough drafts of a revised Wyrd I was fiddling with a few years ago for the Aesir that I can plunder for ideas. I would probably make it very hard to die if the specific clauses were not fulfilled, perhaps impossible to die if they are not. (which may sound like a strong power, but I would rate it middling at best. Not being able to die just means you'll get your head cut off and carried around like Bran) I would include ways to place these conditional effects on people, both for good and bad, and a series of Boons that increase the mechanical function of your own personal ones.

                                            Now, this may sound like it has the same problem as RAW Geasa, as 'Fuck it up and Die,' mechanics are just not fun for the Storyteller or Player. Thankfully, Welsh mythology shows us that while these clauses need to be fulfilled to kill someone (if it is a death-conditional and not like, 'You will never have a name least it come from my own lips' sort of thing) it doesn't mean that the person will die. Llew has all of his clauses filled, and almost dies. He becomes mortal, it is possible for him to have died, but he manages to pull through, though he is in disgusting shape from the wound he was given until he heals up. Really brought him to the brink.

                                            From the little digging I can do, I think this concept is called 'Tynged,' but sadly my University isn't big on medieval Welsh literature, so I don't have a massive amount of resources on hand for this one. I can find the concept mentioned a few times alongside Wyrd as a Fate system though in some articles in passing, so I think it might be the right name. Wikipedia for some reason links it with the Geasa, which I don't entirely agree with. It feels fare more like the Norse concept of Wyrd than the sacred pledges / taboos of the Irish.

                                            I would probably start the PsP off with a Boon that places one of these Tynged on the Scion, I would probably start off with a Death Clause one, have the player outline one situation that needs to be filled for them to be mortal. (Not invulnerable) The exact breakdown of what the Boons would be would depend on the Fate system in play though since this PsP would probably build very heavily off that mechanical skeletal structure.

                                            While I have only given a few examples of Tynged in the literature, I am fairly sure there are several more floating around in the other Branches and perhaps some of the other literature. I think it could be tied into the Arthurian themes of the later stuff as well, "Whomsoever Pulls This Sword," sounds like a Conditional Clause not a Predetermined thing. The rule isn't "Arthur Pendragon Will Pull This Sword Out and Be King of all Britain," but "Whomsoever." I think there are several Tynged-esque ideas in the story of Culhwch and Olwen as well.

                                            I hope that proves somewhat helpful!

                                                            Originally posted by Manannansun View Post
                                                            Sorry to bother you again Watcher, and I know it isn't your forte but can you offer some suggestions on Purviews for Arawn of Annwn? I am currently stumped.
                                                            Sure, I can suggest some Purviews. I assume you've got the Epic Attributes and Abilities sorted out for him?

                                                            Illusion is a big thing for Arawn, disguising himself and Pwyll flawlessly as the other for an entire year. That is a particularly potent use of Illusion. Lasting a long time, not getting noticed, functioning at great distances, all of that. So, I would be entirely comfortable giving him the Illusion Purview.

                                                            Then, next I would suggest considering the Animal Purview, specifically Animal (Pigs, Hounds). If you are looking at using the 2.0 rules for this, then the Animal Purview includes the conceptual branch of the 'Hunter' as well as the 'Beast' so this would be appropriate for Arawn. The major time we see Arawn in the texts is when he is on a hunt with his strange otherworldly hounds. Later on in folk tradition, these hounds show up as creatures hunting across the land, being turned into Hell Hounds rather interestingly. Whatever their function, most likely as just hunting hounds, they are a decent part of Arawn's myth. Normally I wouldn't give Animal for such a reason, however there is something else. Arawn gives pigs to Pwyll's son Pryderi as part of a continuation of friendship. From what I remember of the text, these are not 'just' magic pigs. These are the first pigs anyone has ever seen, and they're insanely valuable because of that. So, having some animals doesn't give you the Animal Purview, even if they are fancy animals. However, giving humanity a new species of animal to farm, that is really 'up there' in the Divine-tiers, so in conjunction with the other bits, I would give him Animal (Pigs, Hounds).

                                                            After these two, I would consider giving him Justice for three reasons. Firstly, he is the king of his own special domain. This alone doesn't really give someone Justice, but having an important tie to Kingship does help a bit. Secondly, when he rules Pwyll's lands, everything is perfect and generally awesome, suggesting that Arawn is a really good king, which again, alone doesn't justify the Purview, but is a bit of a help. Lastly, and most importantly in my mind, is that he tested Pwyll's morality. He had not expected Pwyll to refrain from sleeping with his wife while disguised as himself, a theme that will show up many times in the future of Welsh literature (both people doing so, such as Uther, and people not, such as Gawain-kinda). He is so impressed by this, he and Pwyll forge a friendship that will outlast Pwyll's own life, and Pwyll is given high honors. The morality test part of this, especially since this is the genesis point of a very important cultural theme, and the kingship bits I feel does a fairly good job justifying Justice for Arawn.

                                                            The big challenge you have organizing Arawn's set is the nature of Annwfn / Annwn is. There is no evidence of it being an Afterlife until later on when Arawn starts getting linked up with Satan in medieval folklore. From what I know (which, again, not my area of expertise at all) is that the later reconstruction efforts painted Annwfn as an Afterlife, drawing evidence from the connection with Satan, and the place being underground. The Satan connection is, in my mind, not really evidence of much at all. He is conflated with almost anyone, no matter if they actually have anything to do with each other symbolically, or through cosmological function. I would say that the conflation was likely just rooted in an attempt to fit Arawn into the folk-christianity of the time, that sort of thing happens a lot. The 'Underground' image of Annwfn, which is sometimes used to explain it as a Afterlife, I disagree with for two reasons. Not all Afterlives are underground, and somewhere just being underground shouldn't flag it as an afterlife. Secondly, the whole 'Underground' bit only started with the conflation with Satan, and therefore has no root in the original stories. The realm is described as a otherworldly kingdom, on the surface, and on the Welsh peninsula somewhere. So, I personally would not give Arawn the Death Purview. However, if you want to run Annwfn as an afterlife, in which case Arawn should have Death.

                                                            So, in sum. Animal (Pigs, Hounds), Illusion, Justice. At least, using Scion 1e. I expect that it will be far easier to flesh Pwyll out in 2e looking at the Purviews names they have given us.

                                                            I will also add that this isn't how I normally go about giving Purviews. I feel that it's really important to have a grasp on the whole Pantheon and what everyone is up to before handing out Purviews and Epic Attributes. I treat each Pantheon on their own terms, so what might justify Epic Strength for one of the Tuatha wouldn't justify it for one of the Theoi for example. Further, I consider it to be important to work out what each Pantheon has 'going on' for themselves. For example, writing up all of the Theoi in a vacuum, and they will all end up with Epic Appearance. Writing the Theoi up in the context of their Pantheon, then you will see that while everyone is beautiful beyond mortal comprehension, some are more beautiful than others, and they are the ones who get Epic Appearance. Let each Pantheon set their own bars essentially, it helps keep things working.
