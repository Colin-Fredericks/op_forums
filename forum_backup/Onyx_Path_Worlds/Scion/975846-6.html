    Where did I trivialize acts of heroism? I am disagreeing with the concept that the existence of scions would have a dramatic effect on history, and I'm doing so by pointing out that, even in the mythical times of Hercules, Odyssyues, and the Argonauts, the acts they performed were Epic and meaningful. If any scion could do what they did it wouldn't be legendary because every scion would start off doing it. And that the PCs can do the same is proof they are destined for great things. PC scions aren't more powerful than NPC scions because of the magic of metagaming, they are stronger because they are fated for great things, to reach heights even most other Heroes never reach.

    And my other point about actions going unnoticed. That doesn't mean they aren't epic and meaningful, it just means that when your band stops a small army of fire giants from setting off the yellowstone super volcano that most people aren't going to know that. You still saved millions of lives, and people in the supernatural community are probably going to hear about it, but unless the scions come forward with some irrefutable evidence humanity at large will never know or believe.

                    Originally posted by vonpenguin View Post
                    I see the art of fire giants and tidal waves in the middle of a city to be the once, maybe twice a millennia event. Most scions don't reach that point. They die as the rent a cop that singlehandedly stops a bank robbery of ten guys with body armor and AKs and then dies on the table afterwards. Even among scions the PCs are exceptional. In 1e most scions didn't even get birthrights before the titans escaped.

                    The example of a mortal gunman against a physical scion is also ignoring things like onslaught penalties, coordinated assaults, and piercing damage. Heck most of those examples are more poor game balance than intentional awesomeness. Look in the 1e adversary section, a experienced cop is noted as being something a hero level scion should be concerned about tangling with. A swat team can and will pose a problem to any hero that isn't a legend four combat monkey.
                    Given that example art is usually intended as a thing players will get to do, I somehow get the feeling it isn't intended as something that maybe happens once a millennium, especially given that the tidal wave power is a starting ability for Heroic Scions. Even if it did only happen that rarely, though, that is well and truly enough to reshape history. The further back you go, the more it reshapes it.

                    You're also making the assumption that, because we used militaristic Scions as an example thus far, they're the only Scions that can change the world and that simply is not the case.

                    Consider, for example, the Printing Press; a deceptively simple invention which reshaped the ancient world into the modern world in the span of a handful of centuries. China had the printing press centuries before Europe ever did but made little use of it because of the complexity of the Chinese alphabet. Yet we have Scions; the superior geniuses whose brilliance can turn out radical new inventions. What if one of them looks at a wine press and skips Gutenberg by a few dozen generations? Imagine widespread writing in the 4th century. One little tweak and the world becomes unrecognizable.

                    Or what about charismatic Scions? Consider, for example, the fiery Martin Luther, who bent European religion over his knee without even intending it. What happens when the pre-Christian religions have their Peters? Their Pauls? What happens when these charismatic evangelists cause a spiritual reshaping and revival of their old faiths? And then, what follows when all the religious wars that shaped the modern world took an entirely different shape?

                    What happens if a Catholic Scion kept the Dutch Republic Catholic with their fanatical speeches? Without that, there's no Eighty Years War, no Protestant arm besides that of England trying to force the Peace of Westphalia, no safe haven for Lutherans and Calvinists, no birth of the Dutch Enlightenment...

                    And even if you consider those to be the "big", rare Scions, I'd say you only need one of the above examples and the world is a completely different place.

                    But what about the small ones? Consider, then, all the small parts played by people in history that have added up to big deals. Consider, for example, how history might have played out if Simon Bolivar's would-be assassin in Jamaica had possessed the spoiled Hunter Knacks that let you better track your target. A knife in the man's heart and the South American resistance to Spain literally dies in its bed.

                    Or perhaps the physician who has that little bit of magic that keeps a suitably important person from dying, like, say, Nicholas Hartwig. A Scion who can save a man from a heart attack in 1913 prevents the First World War singlehandedly and, with it, the entire reshaping of the map as we know it today.

                    History is such a big, messy thing. I can't see how introducing the possibility of giants smushing cities or people with the power to raise the dead into the mix can't utterly twist and blend it beyond recognition.

                    I can't stop you from seeing that as trivializing. Me, personally, I would see that as a character-defining moment that causes them to realize just what kind of creature they've become; the moment where they feel divinity coursing through their veins and ride whatever powerful, heady feelings surge through them. Maybe they break down and cry, thinking they're a monster; maybe they elate and laugh madly as they savour this adrenaline-fueled sensation of victory. Maybe somewhere in-between.

                    But in the end, epic things are what Scions do because they are Scions. It might take them a little while to get there but it is emphatically what they do. Scion is a game of Epic Storytelling and epic stories include things like Achilles standing naked on a hill, shouting at an army in rage and grief. Prince Rama hunts demons as a teenager. Samson beats thirty men to death because he's feeling a little pissy and a thousand when he truly gets mad. Hercules slays a lion with his bare hands as a warm-up.

                    Do these stories trivialize these events? Goodness, no. They build up to them first but they all happen. That's the point. Scions are supposed to do this sort of thing.

                    If you envision a world where Scions don't do the very thing they're supposed to do, what sort of thing do you envision happening? Scions don't need to start out perfect to do these things (though many of them are already exceptional by dint of divine blood), but they will do them with time and they will just keep getting better in the process.

                                        The one thing I want to point out to everyone: we've seen what Heroes can do in 2e in at least one regard.

                                        Changing Tides

                                        Cost: Imbue 1 Legend or Spend 1 Legend
                                        Duration: One scene or Instant
                                        Subject: Body of water
                                        Range: Long
                                        Action: Simple
                                        You exert your will over water, controlling the tides or currents that move through it. You can calm all water out to long range, making it still and placid even in the face of a hurricane, or change the direction that currents flow in, letting you reverse the direction of a river or cause a riptide to drag lost swimmers back to shore.
                                        Alternatively, if you spend Legend instead of imbuing it, you can call up a great wave to crash down on your foes. You roll this attack with (highest Power Attribute) + Occult and Enhancement 4. It has the shockwave tag (p. XX), letting you attack all characters in a single range band within long range, as well as the bashing and pushing tags.
                                        So for anyone saying that Scions should all be reshaping history and massively wrecking things... it takes the only dot of Legend a starting Scion has to make one pretty big wave. You can do a lot with that, sure, and it's definitely a noteworthy event... but it's also less destructive than a single artillery shot. Heroes are largely bound to the realm of the extraordinary but largely human with what they can do, and by the time you get up to the real heavy lifting that's when Fate starts slapping you down for making so many ripples.
