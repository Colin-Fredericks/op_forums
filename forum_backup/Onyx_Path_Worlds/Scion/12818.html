    Scion 2e mechanical balance

    11-17-2013, 02:10 PM
    So, I am curious to know if the folks working on 2e have taken a look at the math on rolling multiple dice against a fixed target number to receive a fixed target number of successes. there are quite a few examples of this in the system (combat comes to mind). The bits that I've done shows a pretty big cliff once you reach a certain ratio of dice/difficulty.

    My own experience with it has shown that it is exceptionally difficult for a GM to balance these between characters, leading to very tight ranges (3 or less dice in pool size) of ability that work in a way that might seem reasonable. Anything that hits those pool sizes has an extremely large effect one way or the other (assuring success or failure).

    I think I'd really like to see something a little more linear and predictable.

          Rolling d10, seeing if it is a 7-9 to acquire 1 success or a 10 to acquire 2 successes, means that on average you get 1 success for every 2 dice you roll, but your change of not rolling a success on a given dice is 60%. Which is neat, given that you should only roll for things that might actually fail.

          The problem with this system is that many dice on a single roll mostly means that you'll get half that amount of successes, which is linear. And automatic success that exceed the dice pool make the rolling mostly pointless, which is fine from a narrative standpoint.

          One cool system that I found was rolling a pool of dice and counting pairs. Like roll 5d10: 5 3 3 7 7 would mean that you got 1 pair of 3 and 1 pair of 7, which in fact gives the player 2 results instead of 1 (the usual number of successes). This cold mean that on combat the attack stroke at hit boxes number 3 and 7 once, or the character performed an attack on the timer 7, then something might have happened before the character's second attack on timer 3. Many things may use this method to see how well and how fast a given action was performed. The problem is that at 2 dice you have 10% chance of a pair, 3 dice 28% of a pair, 4 dice 49.6% of a pair, 5 dice 69.76% of a pair,... and only goes up and beyond 100% if you roll more than 10 dice.

                  My biggest problem has been in a group with a non-frontliner (no str, no dex, some sta), a big tank (str, sta, no dex), and a dex monkey (no str, moderate sta, a lot of dex).

                  When you put in something that will challenge a non-frontliner, it has very little chance of affecting the other guys. Not like "10%", like 0.1%. On the other hand, you have something that can impact the dex guy, it will obliterate the non-frontliner. He can't take the damage spill over from the extra successes.

                  I did up an anydice to help show the radical drop - http://anydice.com/program/2dd7

                  Essentially, you put in a 15 dice guy that can hit the dex monkey at 33% (lets say he's got a defense of 9), it's hitting your non-frontliner with a defense of 4 nearly 100%of the time, and getting extra damage dice 87% of the time. He's getting _five_ extra dice 33% of the time. Dang near enough to kill him, even if he has some soak.

                  so now you are forced into an arms race where you now must compete on Dex (or whatever thing it is)

                  Then you toss in the triangular scaling (factorial, but additive) of epic successes and you start having shifts between levels of epic stats that are bigger than the entire drop in the curve... meaning that you have +1 epic stat over a guy, and poof, no chance at all, not even with rolling. Add in things like Untouchable Opponent (the ever house-ruled) and now you can shift from "meh, maybe he'll hit me" to "no, not a chance, ever, the end" in a legend point.

                  This does happen in other areas of the game, but quite frankly, it's most seen in combat for a number of reasons. It happens with the same math for the strong guy with no dex...sure.. he can hit like a brick, but he can't ever hit the guy that the dex monkey can hit 1/3 of the time. I've seen with mental/social things as well, although those tend to be more "on/off".

                  I think the important part here is that if you are going to have number of dice over a target number vs a static number of successes, you can't just have them work off the same scale (attribute + ability) without some kind of modification, and you have to be very aware of _any_ modifications to that scale. If they are going to have things to allow them to hit better, it should have an analog on defense that gives the same scale of bonus, with the note that defense against _everyone_ in a round is far better than attack against against any one target... you only get the bonus once on the attack, but you can get it on everyone on the defense.

                  it's not that multiple dice are bad per se... you just have to constrain the numbers that can be had very strictly. After all, +1 die can give you upwards of 15% chance of success.

                      I'll add in another post because the first is a little long.

                      I might say that if you are going to have Defense be a static number (I think this is alright for the sake of not having to have an attack and a defense roll), the Defense should be more like the average of the Ability and the Attribute, or (if Abilities are constrained to a value less than the max of an Attribute), start off by halving the Attribute, or averaging it with another. But the range should _still_ be less than the expected number of dice that are being rolled against it.

                      Consider Mr 9 Defense and Mr 4 Defense above. Mr 9 has 4 Dex, 3 Athletics, 2 Epic Dex. Mr 4 has 2 dex and 2 athletics. If one was to say Defense was (Dex + Athletics/2) + Epic dots, it's now

                      Mr 6 Defense (Assuming round up) and Mr 2 Defense. More manageable, and one could use a 7 dice guy and not have quite as many issues.

                      Bear in mind, these are just examples, so I don't know that that's actually the right choice. My feeling is though that a group of players should all have target numbers of successes within about 3 of each other, whether to hit or to damage or be hit or dominate someone or what.
