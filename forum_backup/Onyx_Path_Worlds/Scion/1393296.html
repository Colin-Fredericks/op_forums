                                                          Waking up today to do work on the Finn Cycle and instead I'm being thrust into the Book of Invasions to establish dates. Okay.jpg

                                                          (I'm joking, I love to answer questions, this just might be long-winded, sorry in advance)

                                                          So, to start with, that Changeling: the Dreaming stuff is interesting and I might go looking into that later (The War of the Trees is a Welsh thing, that's an interesting choice to use) for my own research into the Celts in Pop Culture, but as Eld. says there, it is totally off from the actual material. While the source material is the same, as you point out Penelope, the WoD stuff there mostly is just reusing names at random rather than using the actual material.

                                                          Further, just a little bit of current research. The Celts never 'moved into' Ireland or anything like that. Through genetic analysis we know the Irish are genetically the same from modern day all the way down to the ice sheets leaving, there has been no major disruption of the population. This causes issues, no doubt, with language development and the move of Proto-Indo-European peoples and the Westward Expansion of the Celts theory which we have not resolved yet, but we're working on it. This is a very contentious topic academically right now, like, so contentious that I was at a talk by one of the main scholars in the field at the main formal conference for the field, and the dude beside me was swearing about the presenter because he disagreed. Like, this was incredibly heated and contentious. Just, like, to provide the context of the 'we dunno'.

                                                          Normally for Scion I suggest not using actual dates and times for Pantheons because things are wishy washy. Tragically, the Túatha Dé actually have a very specific time-scale for everything. We know the specific dates of all the invasions of Ireland even, and I think possibly The Second Battle of Mag Tuired, the war with the Fomorians you are talking about. See, the Irish placed their sagas and tales in the wider context of a Christian narrative, and as such, the timeline is linked up to that. For instance, the first invasion was shortly before The Flood, Conchobar mac Nessa dies when Jesus dies, and the Finn Cycle is told to Patrick.

                                                          And, lastly, for sourcing of this I am using The Book of Invasions / Lebor Gabála Érenn, the only text that actually covers the invasion periods in detail and so on and so forth. The Annals of the Four Masters, a medieval Irish Chronicle will augment this, as it has a King List which lets us work out the dates of things. The way we can use this to establish dating is the description of the time periods that specific kings ruled and working backwards from historic kings. So, for instance, we know when king X ruled, and The Annals of the Four Masters says the probably-fictional person before him ruled for 13 years, and the one before him for 6 and so on and so forth all the way back to the first kings in Ireland, the Fir Bolg.

                                                          So!

                                                          The Fir Bolg kingship starts with Sláine mac Dela comes to power in 1934 BCE, with the fall of the last Fir Bolg high king (and a very good Order Titan tbh) Eochaid mac Eirc in the war with the Túatha Dé who were invading to conquer Ireland is in 1897. So, we can firmly establish that the Túatha Dé invaded Ireland in and around this year. Then we have the Túatha Dé king list which we can look at in conjunction with The Book of Invasions to establish the events during these periods of history. We know that Lug is given temporary kingship by Nuadu for the battle with the Fomorians (it was less of a prolonged war and just one really big battle), so we can use this to establish the date of the battle as 1870 BCE. The three Túatha Dé kings who ruled Ireland in conjunction are overthrown by the invading Mílisians (who, by the by, descendants of Ramses II) in 1700 BCE.

                                                          So, yeah. Those are the specific years these things happen. If you want to be really specific, I think The Book of Invasions gives the specific day and month for the Túatha Dé invasion of Ireland as the Kalends of May, so, the first of May. Though, I'll be honest, that is going from memory of the opening line of the Túatha Dé section in the manuscript, so I could be fucking that one up.

                                                          Hope that helped!

                                                          EDIT: Also, yeah, everything we know about the Túatha Dé come from Irish-Christian monks. Ireland arrives in the 2nd-4th century in Ireland (again, bit of a vicious debate), and the sagas are not recorded until the 8th. The story of the invasion of Ireland isn't even recorded until the 11th.

                                                          Honestly, there is less alterations of the text for Christian reasons and more political reasons. The entire Ulster Cycle was doctored by the O'Neill dynasty to pull some fancy political and propagandist tricks against the previous Ulaid dynasty (see: 'Fury Destroys the World': historical strategy in Ireland's Ulster Epic), and the Fomorians were not always antagonists, but made into antagonists in roughly the 10th century as Norse-expies (see: Myth and Mythography of Cath Maige Tuired). Originally Fomorian was another name for the Túatha Dé and there was no division. This is why the family trees are so intertwined, because they weren't two different family trees originally.
