    Scions With Lesser Immortals?

    02-01-2016, 08:00 PM
    Is it possible?

    I'm curious because the books describe the lesser immortals as being some form of gods, but their legend is too low to produce Scions. With that said, if one of these lesser immortals grew powerful enough, would it be possible for them to have Scions of their own?

    For example, the Nymphs are fertility goddesses, while all / most of the Kami are technically deities. The Amatsukami just happen to be at the top of the hierarchy.

      RAW, Legendary Immortals are unable to produce Scions, though this is something that is occasionally hard to reconcile with Mythology. Achilles is one of the most famous Scions, yet his mother, Thetis, is technically a Nymph (she is one of the Nereids who are something like a sub-species of Nymph) and not a Goddess (While the Nymphs are Divine, they are not quite 'Godesses' as that is more like a species in Greek Cosmology than a rank of being.) which would mean by RAW his existence is tricky.

      What is probably sensible is to make rulings as a ST Lesser Immortal by Lesser Immortal, culture by culture. For example, while the Dwarfs of the Norse are Lesser Immortals, as far as I know we have no evidence of them producing Scion-like entities (more immortality, they wouldn't be Aesir due to not being part of the Divine Tribe), a Nymph from the Theoi isn't an unreasonable entity to have as a Divine Parent as there are in-universe examples. All Kami also technically should be able to produce Scions, but many wouldn't have the inclination to.

      Giving Associated Powers and Abilities for these entities, however, is exceptionally tricky. I would be tempted to give a Scion of one of these Lesser Immortals no Associated, or to keep it very minimal only giving a single Power (Purview or Epic).

              The Greeks are really weird with their perception about who is, and who isn't a God or Goddess. Technically, Prometheus, Atlas, Epimetheus, Hecate, Helios, Selene, Eos, and a bunch of other Titans are the same 'generation' as Zeus, but they are cast as Titans. They are, at the same time, the same generation roughly as Thetis who is neither Titan or 'God'. And even on top of that, we have the Giants which are a cousin species of some of the Nymphs, but are not Nymphs. The Hundred Handed Ones which are not Titans, ect.

              The Greeks are really tricky when it comes to making sense of this system. The way I normally explain it is that it's sort of like 'species' where the Giants, the Titans, the Olympians, the Primordials, the Hundred Handed Ones, both types of Cyclops, ect are just different 'sorts' of Divinities. They are all Divine, but the Greeks decided they went in different 'boxes'.

              What people should probably do is treat everyone on an individual level. Not all Titans, Gods, Giants, Nymphs, ect are equal to each other. Thetis probably should be more 'powerful' than one of the super minor Theoi, like Hebe for example (except that Hebe has a big political backup in the fact she's one of the few legitimate children of Zeus and Hera IIRC). I'm even always tempted to want to shuffle the Titans up in 'power' internally as well, and separate the Primordials / Protagonoi, but the system doesn't really work perfectly if you start doing that.

              An example from the Greeks of this going the opposite direction is at least one Titan who does have Scion (Helios - Thersanon). The Greeks do not play nice with the way Scion sets up, well, Scions.

              EDIT: Think I got off track there. What I mean by Thetis not being a Goddess is the Greeks didn't view her as being the same sort of entity as Zeus + his family. From our perspective, however, Thetis looks to be similar enough. It's really just the Greeks being exclusive, and I'd suggest ignoring it except IC. Thetis should be a totally reasonable Divine Parent option, despite being 'only' a Nymph. The Theoi though would probably be a bit snooty about it though.

                  Well the Scion books describe the Nymphs as being fertility goddesses. Then again, they attribute Circe as being a Titan Spawn on some pages and a Demigoddess on others (not sure if that was a typo or not). It also says one of her parents was a Nymph.

But the books do consider them to be Gods, albeit of low status. So I wasn't sure if it was possible or not for one to conceivably grow powerful enough to have a Scion.


                  One example that I think fits this is the story of the Kitsune taking on the roll of "fox wives" in Japan. According to the mythology, they disguise themselves as humans, seduce men into marriage and then have children with them. Once the child grows old enough, the Kitsune reveals its true identity and leaves the man she married. That, to me, sounds pretty darn close to a Scion origin story.


                  Western Dragons are also attributed to be very sexual and often take virgins to be their lovers / servants. Such dragons are also known to disguise themselves in human form, much like the rest of the gods who attempt to hide among mortals. And since Dragons are considered a class of God in Scion, this also plays into the possibility of them creating Scions.

                      Yeah, the Scion books are a bit... tricky. Nymphs are defiantly, like, sort of local spirits of geographic features (Dryads for trees / forests, Nereids for waters). Some Pantheons would look at that and go 'Deity-like-entity', like the Kami do. Where as the Greeks are super divisive about the whole matter. As far as I know, the Greeks did not internally perceive Nymphs to be Deities like the Olympians, but it just becomes one of those tricky situations where getting a culture to fit into Scion's systems is difficult. Their entry on Circe is actually technically right however, as she is the daughter of a Titan (Helios) and a Nymph (Perseis). She's technically a Demigod (Greek version of saying 'Scion' since Helios has Demigod-progeny), Titanspawn (Daughter of a Titan) and the daughter of a Nymph all at the same time. Obviously, this really doesn't play nicely with Scions systems.

I'd do it all based on the culture and the specific sort of Lesser Immortal rather than make any big flat rules for it though. Like, Fand (Sidhe), Thetis (Nymph), and probably some more examples I can't think about since it's a bit late here, should probably be able to have Scions.                               Beings with lesser Legend cannot sire Scions, at least not with mortals, rule-wise. However, if two people with Legend (say a Hero-Level Scion and a Kitsune) have a child, this child automatically becomes a supernatural being. It could be a Scion, a Kitsune or a full-fledged god, this is mostly up to Fate and, thus, the GM. A notable exemption from this are Amazons, who always sire other Amazons whose stats are buffed by having a father with high Legend. If you want to have a Scion of, lets say, Nephele, you could work with this and just say that her Legend is appropriate to do that job on its own.

                              Maybe those "Scions" don't have more than one Legend initially, but this still suffices for them to be adopted by a god. And this isn't too unusual among Gods nowadays, adopting promising Scions to make sure they make the most out of their birth.
