    EPISODE 18: MODEL STUDENTS (Continued)

    Act Three: Confusion To… Well, Everybody

    Once the Scions are safely away, they wonder what the hell is going on? Bennie can fill in some of it. Guibourg abducted them both and told them to summon Tatenen again. They didn’t have the scroll, but Guibourg compelled Bennie to remember the words. Guibourg tried using a new uas staff but that didn’t work, so Guibourg had undisclosed agents steal the stone staff from Jack’s house. Then he talked Zach into making a new shabti of Holly and animating it, calling Tatenen — well, an avatar of Tatenen — back into the World. It wasn’t hard for Tatenen to seduce Zach into a pact, selling his soul to the Titan.

    Bennie adds that Guibourg captured him and Zach days ago, but one of the first things Guibourg had Zach do is create shabti doubles to take their place. Zach decided on his own to kidnap Landon just so he could brag about how he’s become powerful and cool. Zach never knew that Landon is a shinobi. (Actually, Bennie still doesn’t.)

    Holly decides it’s time to use Mystery and find what’s going on. In hopes that she can suss some pattern to all that’s going on, she channels her Intellect, invokes a Legendary Deed and asks the inscrutable, all-knowing power of Fate:

    * Is the Gus-shabti still active? Yes.

    * What are Tatenen’s current intentions? To crush the Scions.

    * In all, what shabti-doubles did Zach create? Zach himself, Bennie, Gus, Holly, and two other kids the Scions don’t know. (Their names mean nothing to Holly).

    * Which are still active? All of them.

    * How does Picinich resist Gus’ Social-Fu? His badge is enchanted.

    * All this seems like a distraction. A distraction from what? Nothing. False assumption.

    * What are Suttung’s intentions with bringing the new giants to town? Matchmaking. To kill all the Scions, if he sees a chance.

    * What is Tatenen’s current base of operations? Guibourg’s house.

    Well, at least it doesn’t look like everyone is working together in a Master Plan…

    The Scions decide their next step should be to take the Relic badge away from Lt. Picinich. It’s not hard to guess that Guibourg had Zach send out the shabti-Gus and protected Picinich as a way to harass Gus without physical violence, and maybe even force him to leave town. Gus declines to play along. He’s going home, where he knows Picinich will arrest him again. He will play dumb, that as far as he knew his release was legitimate. And then tomorrow, when Picinich is no longer protected by his magic badge, Social Fu and the Sobecks’ lawyer will get him out.

    The scenario plays out as Gus expects. Picinich is waiting at the Sobeck house, doing everything he legally can to show Gus’ father how much he hates the Sobecks. He arrests Gus and takes him back to the police station lockup. The drunk-and-disorderly who shares the cell does not try to trouble the strapping football hero. That night, Landon slips into Picinich’s home and steals his badge. It’s easy for the young shinobi. The Scions don’t worry much. Even if the cop wakes up and sees Landon, what’s he going to do? Say that a ninja took his badge?

    The next morning, Gus appears before a judge for possible arraignment. While the Circuit City surveillance video is undeniable, the evidence that Gus couldn’t have been there is pretty strong, too (and the judge finds Gus’ testimony completely believable). There’s also the fact that the police in Monroe found the stolen goods when they responded to a disturbance at an abandoned house. The judge orders Gus released while the cops investigate further. Gus is back at Avalon High before lunch period.

    Meanwhile, Jaeda and Holly decide they’d better deal with all the shabti doubles they can find. Between classes, they find the shabti doubles of Bennie, Zach and the two other students, lure them out of sight from the other kids, and slit their throats, turning them back into clay. Only Bennie can immediately resume his life. If the two Scions regret adding to the roster of students who’ve disappeared, they feel they cannot leave potential tools of Tatenen running loose.

        EPISODE 18: MODEL STUDENTS (Continued)

        Act Four: Zombies Against Tobacco Use

        While the two Scions hunt shabti kids, they encounter Alex Totenbush. The swim team’s resident kushtaka glares at the anti-smoking posters in the halls. He’s offended: Tobacco is a sacred herb, after all, even if ordinary smokers impiously abuse it. But the Scions find they have more important concerns.

        Holly uses the Unlidded Eye as the Scions search for and dispatch the shabti doubles — and sees that dozens and dozens of students seem to be imbued with some dark force like nothing she’s seen before. As she studies the students passing in the halls, a girl asks her to join Teens Against Tobacco Use. All the leading kids should set a good example for the rest in health, as well as beauty and fashion! It’s Vanessa, the Fashionista who tried to humiliate Mannie Salkind and Holly, and on whom Holly took terrible revenge. And she too is possessed by the strange force. Vanessa reaches for Holly’s hand, so earnest is her appeal… Holly recoils, intuitively certain that she must not let the Fashionista touch her, and makes an excuse to be elsewhere.

        Holly immediately suspects some form of contagious curse or possession. So, who is Patient Zero? Her first suspicion is Mr. Sycamore, the Health teacher who runs Teens Against Tobacco Use and is a notorious projective hypochondriac. (Every class he teaches, he diagnoses two or three students with Hodgkins lymphoma, cystic fibrosis or some other lethal disease. He has never once been right.) When Holly peeks in on his class she sees that he is infected, but in the same manner as the kids she’s seen. So, Holly finds her pet computer genius Bobby Kilmer and has him search the school records for TATU.

        As she already suspected, very few kids stay in the club for long. They participate for one quarter to fulfill the school’s community service requirement, then quit. In the last week, though, membership jumped so that almost 10% of the student body belongs to the club. And one student, Sally Wright, stands out for having belonged to TATU since the start of the school year. Holly phones all the other Scions to say they have a humungous new problem.

        Jaeda, Gus and Holly get together and head for the class where Sally Wright should be. Jaeda first stops at her locker to get her pack of clove cigarettes, just to be obnoxious to TATU. They find Sally, but also the rival Band and a bunch of other students. “Join us,” they say. “It’s the right thing to do.” “You’ll feel much better,” says a smiling Leo. They advance on the Scions in friendly menace.

        Just because she feels pissed by all this earnest zombiedom, Jaeda pulls out a cigarette and lights it. The possessed rival Scions recoil like movie vampires from a cross. “Take them!” hisses Sally from the midst of the mob.

        The rival Scions are nowhere near the power of the PCs, but they do their best to grapple. Barbara and Delia try using Overt Order to make the Scions stand still, but the Scions have every reason to believe that’s a form of suicide — it’s an unacceptable order. On the other hand, the Scions don’t want to crush a bunch of innocent possessed kids. All the while, teachers are emerging from classrooms to demand what’s going on and order the kids back to their classes. Possessed students grab them, and a few seconds later they join the mob.

        Jaeda, however, thinks she knows the answer. She lets Leo grab her so they stand face to face. “Sorry, Leo,” she says, and leans forward to kiss him on the mouth… and blow smoke into his lungs. Leo coughs, releases her, staggers and asks, “What’s going on?”

        Holly catches on at once. She takes a deep drag from Jaeda’s cigarette to immunize herself from the horde. Then she runs over the heads of the massed TATU zombies to race through the school, calling out to the un-possessed kids, “Smoke ‘em if you got ‘em! YOUR LIVES DEPEND ON IT!” A pulled fire alarm increases the confusion and gets the remaining kids out of the school and away from the zombie horde.

        In short order, the Scions all have cigarettes from Jaeda. The rival Scions are freed and join in corralling the zombies. The Scions advance slowly, blowing smoke on the kids one by one to free them from the curse. Eventually, they reach Sally. The teachers are freed, too.

        Holly questions Sally about how she became the vector for possession. Sally turns out to be an intensely earnest girl who found A Purpose In Life through TATU. During Spring Break, her family visited the park around Mount Saint Helens. Somehow, something got into her. Sally felt it growing stronger over the weeks, until she knew she could share it, and her dedication, with others.

        With her incredible erudition and super-genius, Holly finds this actually rings a bell. Pacific Northwest tribes had a legend about evil spirits called Seatco that dwelled in Spirit Lake. (In fact, the lake was named for them.) The Seatco were shapeshifters who lured people into the lake, then killed them and turned them into more Seatco. Only, the eruption of Mount Saint Helens about 30 years ago destroyed Spirit Lake. Maybe, somehow, the Seatco became whatever possessed Sally. At least, Holly doesn’t have any better explanation.

        That’s when Gary calls Jaeda. Things have gone crazy at Etna High. Crazier. A lot of the students seem, well, possessed! The principal — whom the Scions know to be Guibourg — has barricaded himself in his office. The Scions say a variety of bad words as they remember that Mr. Sycamore teaches Health and runs the TATU chapter at Etna as well as Avalon High.

        The Scions leave Holly (now out of Legend) and the rival Band to finish exorcising the Avalon student body. Holly suggests that saying the whole thing was guerilla theater might make a good cover story. In a few minutes, Gus and Jaeda are at Etna. Reese is among the possessed. Gus can’t resist sweeping her into a kiss to blow smoke into her lungs. Gary glowers. “Hey man,” Gus says. “You’re in no position to complain about a Scion kissing a giant.” Gary snarls that he’s in every position to object about Gus kissing his sister. Gus shrugs, smiles and says that as far as he’s concerned, Reese is still Gene Blackforest’s girl, and he doesn’t poach.

        The task is easier at Etna than Scion: A lot more kids there smoke. At last, the Scions break down the door to Guibourg’s office. It is the first time they have seen their nemesis. He is a tall, distinguished-looking man in late middle age, the very image of a Dedicated Educator. He’s also dressed in a ceremonial robe and setting a variety of weird and grisly relics around a circle chalked on his office floor, clearly about to call up some dire entity from beyond. The Scions immediately snap pictures on their phones.

        Guibourg hastily cleans up the evidence and locks his magical tools in a cabinet. He doesn’t exactly thank the Scions, but he compliments them on their quick thinking. Jaeda shrugs in elaborate nonchalance. It was only an infestation of mutant Native American lake specters, after all. It was kind of obvious how to deal with them. Guibourg smiles thinly and suggests they should return to their classes in Avalon. The Scions do so, satisfied that they have saved Avalon from political correctness run amok.

        Epilogue

        The Scions find no real explanation for the TATU possession. They guess it’s just another symptom of the force of Legend growing in the area.

        Tatenen is still on the loose. The Scions haven’t found the Gus-shabti, either. At least they know Tatenen is staying near Guibourg. She seems unable to animate powerful shabti, since she had Zach animate the Nemean beasts. At a guess, though, Zach was never really the source of the power that animated the powerful shabti: His use of the spell was just a sort of beacon or conduit for the real Tatenen, and the real Titan cannot channel its full power through a mere shabti copy of itself. Apparently Guibourg couldn’t take over the job either, and Zach was bound as the only person who could release Tatenen’s power into the World. With him dead and the rod that was originally used to invoke the power back in the Scions’ hands, the shabti Tatenen should not be able to create anything more powerful than the shabti warriors. They hope! The Scions place the rod in a safe-deposit box, just as they did with the Scroll of Tatenen and the Sword of Xibalba.

        The Scions debate whether to spread the pictures of Guibourg in Satanic Sorcerer drag as a way to get him disgraced and fired. For now, they decide it probably wouldn’t work. Guibourg is no slouch at mental and social manipulation. Anyway, he could discredit the pictures with one word: “Photoshop.”

        The experience with shabti creatures helps Gus learn the Shabti Boon (Heku *** from my rewrite of the Purview.)

        The Scions agree to send the Scroll of Tatenen to Louhi as a way to make sure Tatenen can’t get it and use it to create mightier shabti creatures.

        For some time, Jaeda has felt bad that the only way she seems able to help people is by killing stuff. She especially wants to help Leo stop hating himself. Leo’s Nature is Wretch: He loathes himself, thinks he deserves to be treated like crap, and recovers Willpower when people do so. Based on a talk with her mother, Jaeda decides to try using Social Fu to (in game terms) change Leo’s Nature. She plans to keep hitting him with Blessing of Importance and Lasting Impression to make him feel good about himself, with Willpower points as the game-terms reward for doing so. Jaeda hopes that by, in effect, simulating him having a different Nature she can make it so.

        Finally, Gus wonders about Picinich’s beef with his family. All his father can say is that brother Gav had wild adventures when the two of them were young, and sometimes it got them both in trouble. At that time, Picinich was a young cop in Avalon. Picinich could never make anything stick against Gav, though. Apparently, he still holds a grudge. Gus, however, suspects that something more personal lies behind the cop’s hatred. What did Gav do to the guy?

            EPISODE 19: Bad Neighbors, Part One

            Act One: An Interesting Evening

            JACK: While eating lunch in the cafeteria, Jack notices Barbara frequently checking her watch. Naturally, he butts in and asks why. Barbara is evasive, but after checking her watch one last time she accosts Vice-Principal Stone about some bit of student government business that they need to take care of. She leaves with him, Jack trailing behind. As they pass a janitor’s closet, they hear as small scream. Stone opens the closet and sees a tearful Delia Bright, clothes in disarray, and an English teacher named Mr. Renfrew with his hands on her. “He forced me — He threatened —“ Delia sobs, and glares at Renfrew. The teacher backs away, spluttering incoherently. Stone grabs Renfrew and drags him away, in front of dozens of witnesses.

            Jack asks what that was about. Barbara explains that Renfrew is a witch that Guibourg recruited and initiated into the service of the Titans. He’d been playing nasty magical and mind games with his students and their parents. Barbara and Delia worked out a sting. Delia — suddenly dry-eyed — produces Renfrew’s Relic, a key chain bearing a small, black glass egg. Barbara says it might not be enough to put Renfrew in jail as a sexual predator, but he’s out of the school and his capacity for mischief is reduced by loss of his Relic. So, does Jack still want that date she promised him? She’ll drive.

            To end his grounding, Jack must promise his mother that Barbara is a good girl. Responsible, in student government, top student, etc. He dresses up nice. His mother grudgingly gives permission. So naturally, Barbara turns up at the Thompsons’ door wearing a gumberoo-leather catsuit and a studded leather collar, with a riding crop in her hand. She archly tells Ms. Thompson not to worry, she knows how to deal with naughty boys. Jack hurries to the car before his mother recovers the power of speech.

            For their date, Jack decided to take Barbara to Seattle’s Basque restaurant, one of only five or six such restaurants in the US. (Yes, it’s a real place. It’s a converted home with several small dining rooms.) To exercise his burgeoning super-genius, Jack learned Basque because it was one of the most difficult languages in the World, and by the Gods, he wants to get some use from it! The staff are so pleased that Jack speaks Basque that they ignore Barbara’s garb and give the couple a private dining room. Jack’s wits and savoir-faire are put to the test as Barbara plays with her food, asks embarrassing questions, pinches the waiter’s butt, and generally does her worst to make this the Date From Hell. When the dinner’s over, the staff asks Jack to please come again. Alone.

            On the way home, Barbara stops at a mini-mart for refreshments. Naturally, there’s trouble — but she and Jack expected there would be, so they both brought their Relics. Inside, one of the town’s new frost giants is losing his temper with the store clerk, who is trying to get his shotgun from under the counter. The adult giant (the aptly-named Jokull) tried to buy beer and food using (stolen) plastic, but he doesn’t know which card to use, or how, and is about to trash the store. Jack decides he doesn’t really want a fight, though. He pays for Jokull’s groceries, chivvies him out of the mini-mart, and tells the clerk that it’s really dumb to antagonize someone that big over beer, chips and cocktail pepperoni.

            Back home, Jack’s mom has recovered the power of speech. Jack needs all his powers of persuasion to convince his mother that he and Barbara went to a costume party — he dressed up as an accountant! She barely believes him. All in all, Jack thinks it was a pretty good date.

            JAEDA was also in the cafeteria that day, but she left early because Leo wanted to talk to her in private. When they’re alone, he nerves himself and asks Jaeda if she needs help getting away from Gary. He knows she’s dated him, but he also noticed how she smiles at him and seems to care about him. And he cares about her, too. In fact, he… (GULP) loves her. And if Gary is threatening her in any way to make her stay with him, he’ll do anything to help her get free.

            Jaeda recognizes that her attempts to alter Leo’s personality using Blessing of Importance and Lasting Impression have not worked the way she hoped. She also recognizes that Leo has just tossed his fragile, spun-glass heart to her, and the slightest fumble will crush it, and him. She replies that she does care about him as a friend, and if she hadn’t started dating Gary she’d be proud to become more than friends — but she loves Gary (Jaeda realizes this is true; it’s the first time she’s said this even to herself), and she can’t be true to Leo if she’s false to Gary. Jaeda pushes all her Epic Charisma into her declaration, plus channeling Loyalty and applying a Legendary Deed: This matters immensely to her. Leo smiles, says he’s lucky to have such a good and honest friend, and leaves feeling better about himself than, likely, ever.

            After school, Jaeda works her shift at Big Cups and returns home to the Motel 6. She, her father and the town’s new giants (the Eismanns and the Hrimsteds) are now the only residents. Maybe the giants’ barbecue pit and the beginnings of a crude blacksmith’s forge scare away new guests. The Vettersons’ big SUV is in the parking lot, though, and Jaeda hears loud, angry voices coming from the Eismann’s suite. She sneaks closer to hear Suttung Vetterson chewing out the Eismanns — something about paying for their damages, and more to keep the matter quiet, and this is not why he brought them from Canada! He stomps out with a parting snarl of “Get a job!” and slams the door so hard it breaks. He notices Jaeda but just grumbles that at least she works.

            The Eismanns call in their neighbor Starkad Hrimsted (the blacksmith) to fix the door. Jaeda tries to sneak close enough to eavesdrop on the giants talking without being seen, but does not notice the Hrimsted’s daughter Gunnlod approaching. (A botched Perception roll.) Gunnlod dresses Jaeda down for eavesdropping and warns her to stay away.

            HOLLY ends her lunch hour by heading for the signup sheet for the school’s talent show, due to take place that Friday. She intends to win it. At the signup sheet, though, she encounters Delia Bright — fresh from getting Renfrew fired — who also intends to enter and win. Delia has a favor to ask. In fact, she’s cashing in the favor Holly owes her for a bit of Prophecy several episodes back. Delia asks Holly not to enter. Holly smiles and says of course, anything for a friend. And silently vows revenge!

            As soon as possible, she meets Jack and drafts him for her plan: to meet all the genuinely talented performers in school and convince them not to enter. Delia will win, but her victory will be meaningless. What glory in flaunting divine talent, when there’s no competition in the first place? Jack agrees to help, sensing that to refuse could be bad for him.

            After school, Holly decides to take a look at Guibourg’s house. So that any of the warlock’s minions who might be watching won’t spot her, she disguises herself using her new Illusion Boon, Stolen Face (channeled through Santa Troll’s heart — obtained in Episode 12). She has the Unlidded Eye running while she strolls past the ordinary-looking, two-story house. As she expects, Guibourg’s house carries a Ward to keep out Scions.

            GUS has a less than pleasant day. It starts with Coach Reagan expressing his concern about Gus’ recent arrest. Coach warns that even for a player of Gus’ caliber, repeated arrests could be too much of a scandal and jeopardize his becoming team captain.

            Later, Gus sees Marshall blow up at Barbara. The Scion of Huitzilopochtli growls that he’s sick of taking orders from civilians and girls, and stomps off. Gus tries talking to Marshall, and Marshall adds that the other Scions disgust him for tolerating the Vettersons. In fact, he calls Gus a traitor for attacking him in order to stop the rival Band’s attack. And now there are six more giants in town! Way to go!

            Finally, Gus’ Fashionista-assigned girlfriend Lindsey says she feels neglected. Gus needs to take her on a nice, high-visibility date as a way to flaunt their status to the other students. Gus considers himself still honor-bound by the pact between Bands (and their respective social sets) that Holly and Barbara worked out, so he agrees to take Lindsey to the Public Pool two nights hence. But these are mere irritations.

            At about 3 a.m., Gus awakens to pounding on the Sobecks’ front door. Looking out the window, he sees cop cars. Approaching the door, he hears Lieutenant Picinich demanding that someone open the door or he’ll break it down.

            Gus makes sure he reaches the door before his father, and opens it. Picinich screams for Gus to get down on his knees with his hands behind his head, he’s under arrest! His gun is drawn. Picinich raves that Gus has finally done himself in, he has pictures of Gus trashing Picinich’s car less than an hour ago! Gus protests that he was home asleep the whole time. Picinich sneers that Gus could have snuck out past the house’s burglar alarms, reached the cop’s home, smash up the car with a baseball bat and returned in time to slip back inside with no one else the wiser.

            Gus decides he’s had enough of this harassment. He glares at the cop and hits him with Game Face. No longer protected by a talisman, Picinich drops his gun, gives a strangled cry, and runs out the door. The cops he brought as backup complete the arrest, though they permit Gus a few minutes to get dressed.

            At the police station, Gus sees the pictures Picinich took. Sure enough, the pictures look just like him, beating on Picinich’s car. They put Gus in the lockup with a street person who wakes up and makes sure to stay as far from Gus as possible.

            Gus’ father joins him at the station and says he thinks he knows why Picinich hates the family so much. It was more than the way Gus’ uncle Gav kept getting in trouble, but Picinich could never pin anything on him. Picinich had a fiancée. A week before the wedding, the young cop caught his fiancée in bed with the underage Gav. She dumped Picinich and left town a week or so later (though not with Gav; Gus’ father doesn’t know what became of her, or even remember her name). It was enough of a scandal that it played a part in Gav’s decision to join the Marines instead of finishing high school. Gav is long dead, but Gus looks an awful lot like him. Gus assures his father that despite what the pictures suggest, he did not trash Picinich’s car. Rather, he suggests that Picinich is losing his mind.

            Gus also gets an unexpected visit from Guibourg. The warlock principal of Etna High has a warning for Gus: Leave town. In fact, Guibourg wants all the Scions to leave town. Guibourg knows he lacks the power to kill Gus — but he can make life hard for all the Scions. He can also do very bad things to the mortals that the Scions care about. Gus defies the warlock.

            Through heroic effort, the Sobeck family’s lawyer manages to get Gus in front of a judge the next morning. Since Picinich is still hiding in terror somewhere, Gus and the family lawyer have no trouble getting Gus free on bail, but it’s expensive and the charges still stand: Gus doesn’t have the Social Fu to make a judge flat-out ignore evidence and ignore the law, no matter how sympathetic the judge feels. Gus is released in time for the second half of the school day, but there will be a trial.

                EPISODE 19: Bad Neighbors, Part One (Continued)

                Act Two: Additional Shocking Revelations

                The next day, the Scions agree they must do something about Guibourg, the shabti double of Gus, and Tatenen — but what?

                The realization that at least two Scions have doubles running around prompts Jack to reevaluate Barbara’s challenging behavior the night before, and her subdued manner and avoidance of him today. He asks Holly to check Barbara using the Unlidded Eye, and Holly confirms that it’s really Barbara, but she also finds fading traces of magic on the Scion of Chango. It feels… almost Pesedjet. Holly remembers Gus telling her that Heku includes a power to alter people’s personalities, but of course Gus would never do such a thing for mischief. Tatenen might, however, and the Titan-shabti already showed she had the Heku power to create low-power shabti of her own. Hurrying, Holly finds similar traces of magic on Marshall but not on any other Scions. In particular, not on Leo. Jack tries talking to Marshall about this, but the Scion of Huitzilopochtli refuses to concede that he said or did anything he did not really mean. Barbara, however, quickly remembers a meeting with Holly that the real Holly did not attend.

                Immediately after school, Holly turns to the power of Mystery to learn more about the warlock’s capabilities and plans. As usual, Holly channels Intellect and makes this a Legendary Deed. She learns:

                * What connection or coordination is there between Guibourg and Suttung Vetterson? None. They are working separately.

                * What is Guibourg’s current objective? Recruiting and initiating witches and warlocks to serve the Titans.

                * How many has Guibourg initiated? Ten. Uh-oh…

                * Which Titan does Guibourg personally serve? Darkness. (The Scions guess he and the Black Bodhisattva are buddies.)

                * What creature is summoned by Guibourg’s death? A Gyuki — another monstrous creature of the Darkness Titan.

                * Where is Tatenen’s current base of operations? Guibourg’s house.

                * What is the current location of Gus’ shabti double? Guibourg’s house.

                * What defenses are on Guibourg’s house, besides wards and Tatenen’s shabti warriors? Baka — a spirit of darkness from Voodoo legends. It looks like a black dog with horns.

                * What is Barbara’s favorite music? ‘Weird Al’ Yankovic. (Jack slipped this question into the list and Holly permitted it.)

                * What made Tatenen leave Guibourg’s house? Tatenen’s use of Bind the Heart on Barbara feels improvisational to the Scions — something done on the spur of the moment because she saw a chance to make trouble, rather than a deep-laid plan. So what was her real goal at the time? Sure enough, Tatenen’s primary goal was… seducing Cole Thomasun.

                Oh, crap.

                The Scions agree that for a start, they must save Cole from Holly’s imposter before he does something they will all regret. Then they must do something about Tatenen before she uses imposters to ruin all their lives. But how can they attack Tatenen when the Titan-shabti can hide behind Guibourg’s ward? Well, they know someone who’s pretty damn tough but isn’t a Scion…

                A few quick calls bring Gary Vetterson and Cole to a meeting in Hooverdale with Gus and Jaeda. Down below, they find Suttung, Hyndla and Gary, much battered and chained up with fetters too massive even for them to break. They are attended by the three male giants: Norfi, his father Jokull, and the giants’ blacksmith Starkad. Starkad is torturing the bound and gagged Hyndla to make Suttung talk. Norfi is torturing Gary with a blowtorch, just because.

                                                Battle is joined. Jack starts by throwing his Shinobi Shuriken at Starkad, wounding the giant but not by much. Gus pounds on Jokull and nearly rips his face off with a clinch from behind. A powerful blow from Starkad’s hammer nearly knocks out Gus, but Self-Healing puts him right as rain. Then Gus takes on Starkad, clinching the giant blacksmith from behind and pinning him with his own huge hammer. It’s easier because Holly has cast Evil Eye on Starkad. Jaeda engages Norfi. Holly quickly thinks to cut Hyndla’s gag as well: Both of the adult Vettersons know Overt Order (the reason the Canadian giants always kept three in the room and only one Vetterson able to talk). But the Vettersons are gravely wounded and need a moment to collect their wits.

                                                Seeing the odds, the Scions call for Jaeda to join them. She does so — but the female giants follow her, one by one. Still, Gus, Jack and Jaeda are now incredibly skilled combatants, and Holly keeps Overt Ordering one giant to attack another, and catching them with Serpent’s Gaze. Norfi manages a lucky attack with the blowtorch, but he soon falls. Jack knows that he is nearly impossible to hit when he combines Untouchable Opponent with Fight With Your Head, so he draws the giants’ attention to himself: He makes a big production about drawing his katana, hands shaking as if the blade carried terrible power, while intoning a tongue-mangling invocation (“Mary had a little lamb; its fleece was white as snow…” — in Basque). He play-acts like the sword moves by itself and drags him along, as a stunt and to unnerve the giants even further. (And of course he imbues the blade with Warrior’s Mana.)

                                                When Suttung and Hyndla start Overt Ordering giants to attack each other, drop their weapons, etc., and Reese joins the fight with Gary’s axe in hand, the battle ends soon after. Gus delivers the final blow to Norfi.

                                                Jaeda helps release Gary. Suttung glares as he sees the way they look at each other, but is too tired and thrashed to object, even after healing himself with water and Legend.

                                                What to do about the wounded, unconscious Canadian giants? Nobody really wants to leave them alive. The Scions and Reese just haul them to the bathtub one by one and start chopping.

                                                Jack realizes that he’s made a good beginning at turning his ordinary sword legendary. Using it to fight giants is legendary, right? Someday, maybe he can have enough legendary deeds associated with it that a God can turn it into a Relic! But a legendary blade needs a name. Considering his play-acting in the fight, he decides that “Lamb of Lono” is unfortunately appropriate. It sounds cooler in Basque.

                                                The Scions have a talk with the elder Vettersons. Gary is firm: He shall continue dating Jaeda. Suttung doesn’t like it, but he owes the Scions his life and the lives of his family. The truce will continue.

                                                About this time, the Scions notice Reese is gone. So is one of the slain giants’ hearts. Holly is out of Legend, so Hyndla casts Ariadne’s Thread to find her daughter. The Scions hadn’t tried collecting eitr before because they thought it would be deeply rude to do so in front of the Vettersons, but with the prospect of fresh trouble Jaeda quickly collects the last three doses from the melting hearts.

                                                The Scions and Vettersons find Reese a brisk five minute’s jog away, in a clearing in some woodland. She has just squeezed the last eitr out of the heart while calling, “Acacia! Acacia! Acacia!”

                                                And Acacia rises out of the ground, whole again — remember how the Tomb-Spawn ripped out of her, back in Episode 10? — but the Scions know she is not alive.

                                                The crazy Goth witch smiles a crazy Goth smile at seeing Reese and the Scions. Then she sees the faint light in the East from the approaching dawn. Acacia promises them the fun is just beginning. Then she dissolves into mist and vanishes.

                                                Reese explains afterward that while she carried away the dying Acacia, her friend told her that supernatural blood might be able to bring her back. (She’d been full of eitr and other stuff when she died, it was a very supernatural death anyway… worth a shot, anyway.) Reese finally had a chance. She missed her friend. Sure, Acacia was nuts, but she was fun to hang with and she was a friend. Reese doesn’t abandon her friends.

                                                So, the Scions eliminated two families of giants (and without any mortals seeing them do so). On the other hand, they can now add a vampire to their list of problems. And they still have Gus’ court hearing and the talent show to look forward to.

                                                The fun just never stops at Scion High!

                                                            EPISODE 21: Goodbye, Avalon

                                                            In which the saga of Scion High ends a little earlier than expected.

                                                            Act One: A Busy Friday Night

                                                            Fighting the Canadian giants and other business left the Scions almost exhausted of Legend and Willpower, but they feel better by Friday. Good thing too, because that’s the day of the school talent show!

                                                            Delia’s attempt to win by blocking Holly from entering deeply offended Holly, who sought revenge by making her victory blatantly hollow. Unfortunately, Jack (not knowing this) challenged Gus to appear in the talent show. They quickly signed up for the show. Gus, as always, plans to do his best. His idea is to flaunt his athletic prowess with a display of parkour moves, general acrobatics and martial arts, topping it all by smashing a cinder block. But blocking Holly’s revenge triggers her Vengeance all over again… Holly schemes to sabotage Gus by supplementing his music track with a softer second track of Right Said Fred’s “Too Sexy For My Shirt.” Realizing that Gus has no sense of humor where his reputation is concerned, though, and not wanting to blow up the Band at this moment of crisis, Holly grits her teeth and quashes the impulse.

                                                            Early Friday, Jaeda also has a brainstorm. What if she and Leo reprised their spectacular sparring match for the talent show? When she suggests this to Leo, he gets a trapped, miserable expression. He’d like to do it because it would make her happy, but he can’t. He just can’t. This disappoints Barbara, who very much wants to see Leo strut his stuff (preferably shirtless to flaunt the ripped physique he’s developed with his Epic Strength, Dexterity and Stamina). She suggests that Gus could deal with Leo’s stage fright by using Bind the Heart on him. Leo’s a little creeped out by the thought of someone magically altering his personality, but for Jaeda… He accepts. Gus agrees and gives Leo a new Nature so he enjoys showing off. Then Barbara uses Social Fu to put Leo and Jaeda on the program even though registration was supposed to have closed days ago.

                                                            The discussion of altering Natures leads Jack to a revelation. Jaeda has worked to change Leo’s Nature; but he thinks she’s changing her own. She isn’t as needy and clingy as she was at the start of the school year, more about caring for others. (In game terms, her Nature is changing from Addict to Caregiver.) Jaeda is surprised, but she hopes Jack is right.

                                                            The Scions are the last acts on the program. Jack goes first. He comes out on stage dressed in Shakespearean doublet and hose and declaims in a Richard III squawk, “Now…” and pauses. And pauses some more. Then whispers loudly, “Line?” A hook pulls him off stage.

                                                            Gus then does his acrobatics/martial arts routine. In his competitive zeal, he strives to make it a Legendary Deed. Between that and his Epic Dexterity, he does unbelievably well. (19 successes! Yes, more successes than dice.) Jaws drop.

                                                            Next come Jaeda and Leo, announced as “The Lady and the Tiger.” Jaeda and Leo wear tights, with a bikini top for Jaeda and some stripes painted in Leo’s hair and across the lion-demon tattoo displayed on his bare back. Perfect Partner and an accelerating, rhythmic piece of music turns the bokken duel into a dance, and then Jaeda’s Epic Appearance and Presence turn the dance into a seduction. The duel-dance ends with them pressed together, crossed bokken between them, looking like they are about to kiss. Jaeda and Leo also turn their performances into Legendary Deeds. The result is not quite so unbelievable, depending on how you parse the result (17 successes for Jaeda’s Appearance + Melee roll, 14 for Leo’s Dexterity + Melee roll to complement her), but they too receive a standing ovation, and a hundred camera phones record the performance.

                                                            Delia ends the show with a display of cheerleading to accompany the school song. She actually has Art (Cheerleading), as well as Epic Dexterity, Epic Appearance and Center of Attention. She also Deeds her performance, for 12 successes. The last act in a talent show enjoys an advantage as the freshest in people’s minds; talent show tradition is to give first prize to a girl who sings; and “School Spirit” is always a winner… but not after Gus, Leo and Jaeda.

                                                            The judges, blasted by three such performances, are still ready to give Gus the prize. Looking over at Jaeda, though, Gus sees how much this contest means to her. And, the player revealed, Gus has been in love with Jaeda for quite some time. (Gus said, “I do love you, Jaeda,” after the fight with the giants, but she smiled and misunderstood the way he loves her.) Gus plucks the first prize announcement from the presenter’s hand and names Jaeda and Leo the winners. The student audience is happy enough, and the two Scions take a bow to thunderous applause. Once they leave the stage, Barbara grabs Leo and hauls him off to a broom closet for smoochies.

                                                            Immediately after school, though, Gus has his court hearing. Before heading for the courthouse, he creates a shabti of himself and sends it out to rob the same Circuit City that was robbed before by the double created by the late Zach. He doesn’t try using Social Knacks on the judge, prosecutor or Picinich; just sits quietly and lets the family lawyer do the talking. The judge sets a trial date; Picinich gloats that Gus will get what he deserves; Gus smiles slightly.

                                                            By the next day, the cops and prosecutor have the store video footage of Gus causing trouble at the Circuit City while Gus was in a courtroom in full view of a judge. Picinich sputters that Gus has an accomplice made up to look like him. The prosecutor avers that that may be, but the trial goes on indefinite hold for further investigation. Picinich’s captain orders him to take a long vacation.

                                                            Holly, meanwhile, copes with the unstable emotions of her pyromaniac minion, Maynard Salkind. Since the Gyuki is a creature of shadow, bright, hot incendiaries are the obvious weapon to use against it, just as they were against the Lucifuge. Maynard can build such devices. By Friday, however, Maynard is an emotional wreck. While Holly was busy working Social Fu damage control and fighting giants, Vanessa the Fashionista unexpectedly decided to apologize to Maynard for setting him up for humiliation with Holly (back in episode 11). She apologized so much that she gave him a happy. Then she was disgusted with him and told him so, eloquently. Holly catches up to Maynard not far from Vanessa’s home in Rowanfield. He’s wearing an incendiary suicide vest and is not ready to listen to reason, so she catches him with Serpent’s Gaze and disarms him. Then she exercises all her skills at calculated, manipulative niceness to talk him down. It isn’t easy. Still, she now has a supply of the incendiaries she wanted…

                                                            Jaeda also has a not-so-pleasant coda to her talent show triumph. During her shift at Big Cups, a Native American man in a black Subaru drives up to the coffee stand. He wears an all-black suit with a black shirt and black tie. The man says for her to pass the word that if Louhi attempts her plan, the Manitou will take it as an act of war. See, it won’t just be Avalon and Etna that disappear: The towns and the Titan will crash down into the Ghost Dance Country, since Coyote was careless enough to create a weak point leading to the pocket world the Manitou created for the tribes and their spirits. That will become the new Tartarus. The black-clad figure concludes by warning Jaeda that Louhi isn’t the only God who can steal the sun. Jaeda promises the God — whom she correctly recognizes as Raven — that she will do her best to pass his message to Louhi and other Gods.

                                                            For his part, Jack works another depressing shift at Stick It. Special Agent Cross stops by to update him on the campaign to debunk the video of the Scions’ fight against Tatenen. A number of Gods have joined Holly and Barbara in using Rumor Mill to spread the “It’s Just Photoshop” meme. Unfortunately, parties unknown (but probably including Guibourg) are using Rumor Mill to spread a contrary memes that It’s Real and proves that Otherworldly Forces Are Among Us. As for the town’s ambient Legend, killing a bunch of giants helped lower it — but the spectacular displays at the talent show raised it again.

                                                            (Jack, incidentally, wonders if Agent Cross is real or if he’s going nuts as various genius mathematicians have done. After all, none of the other Scions have seen Agent Cross.)

                                                            The Scions decide they had better try convincing Louhi her plan is unworkable. But how? They guess that since Holly is not the only agent placing Louhi’s mystic resonators, someone else is probably keeping an eye on her and the town. To draw out that agent, they start collecting the resonators Holly already placed, and from the locations Jack deduced.

                                                            The Scions soon notice that not all the resonators they collect (disks of wood, metal and bone inscribed with runes) are the ones Holly placed. On two of them, some of the runes are drawn wrong and are, in fact, decals covering another symbol: a stylized canine head. A coyote?

                                                            Before too long, a Nordic-looking man accosts the Scions and warns them to stop interfering with Louhi’s plan. The Scions point out that Coyote has interfered already, and they pass along Raven’s warning.

                                                            That done, the Scions round out the evening by framing Guibourg. The big problem with debunking the Tatenen video is that a house really blew up, and a huge brick tetsubo was left on site. You can’t explain away a massive explosion as Photoshop.

                                                            So they come up with a story that “Principal Borgen” is involved in shady financial dealings and blew up his own house to collect the insurance money. He had the “fake” video created as a distraction to send the cops on a wild goose chase. Holly, Barbara and Gus know enough about business to know what information and fake documents they need for a hoax that will survive examination for at least a little while. Jack makes sure the numbers are correct. Bobby Kilmer, Holly’s hacker minion, breaks the video down into separate tracks and fakes up CGI rough drafts of Tatenen-Holly swinging the brick tetsubo.

                                                            In the wee hours of the morning, Landon breaks into Etna High with a jump drive loaded with digital fakery. Gus creates a squad of shabti warriors to protect Landon. As the Scions expected, Guibourg warded his office against Scion entry. He also warded it against shinobi entry: Oops, he knows about Landon. However, he did not ward it against shabti entry. Landon send a shabti in with the jump drive and talks the animate statue through the process of loading the false data into Guibourg’s computer.
