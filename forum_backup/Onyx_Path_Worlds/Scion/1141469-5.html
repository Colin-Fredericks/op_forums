                    Originally posted by Samudra View Post
                    Speaking of which, I meant to discuss this earlier but it completely slipped my mind... but I prefer to treat the Puranic Chandra and the Vedic as either separate deities or significantly different Mantles of the same deity... Soma in the Vedas is primarily associated with the Soma plant, with his Lunar associations being a distant second... he IS a Moon God, but that's barely mentioned and/or relevant to the Vedas, which are significantly more interested in the Sun.

                    Chandra, on the other hand, is a Moon God first and foremost, and a God of astrology and the calendar... he has minimal associations with Soma, though he came out of the same Churning of the Oceans Episode as Amrita. They are treated as the same God, with Chandra being called Soma and Soma being a byword for Moon, but to be perfectly honest Soma is practically Titanic in the Vedas... concerned only with his Plant self and it's properties and use in sacrifice... which should surprise no one since Soma of the Deva and Haoma of the Ahura are probably the same person (even if the plant they represent might not have been). Chandra by contrast is a lively figure who is a ladies man, being married to the 27 lunar asterisms and carrying on a torrid affair with the wife of Brihaspati, with whom he had the Planet Mercury as a son.

                    So yeah, if I were designing it, I'd treat Soma as a Titan of Fertility and Moon, with possibly Deception (for the possible hallucinogenic effects of Soma) and Chandra as a God with Lover, Sage and Healer as Callings, and Moon, Fortune, Order and Health as Purviews.
                    A god who has massively changed over time sounds like exactly what Mantles exist for.

                                                        Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                                                        Samudra, I've read reference to Agni and Soma (specifically in the lunar role more than the plant, so pretty directly translating to Chandra) being in a sexual relationship. Do you have any familiarity with that tradition?
                                                        There is a passage in the Shatapatha Brahmana that refers to Agni and Soma as a productive pair, but also applies the same name to Indra and Agni, so the exact nature is debatable. I have heard this theory many times, but don't know of any specific source for it (which doesn't mean there isn't one, mind you, as A. I hardly know every piece of Hindu literature there is and B. This is Vedic era myth, which is a somewhat hazy subject to begin with, so I can't give you a definitive answer). However, Vedic era Hindu religion essentialy consists of offerings, primarily of Soma, into Fire, in the hopes of generating wealth, food and progeny... in this context, Soma has been identified with semen, Agni with the womb, and the resulting wealth with the child. This metaphor can be taken literally as well... it is a valid interpretation to say that the Divinities of Soma and Fire are likewise in a sexual relationship.

                                                        However, interestingly, the same passage of the Shatapatha Brahmana does explicitly say that Mitra deposits his seed in Varuna on the new moon night and nature rejoices. Other passages in this or other texts refer to the positions being reversed. I wonder what Varuna thinks of his erstwhile boyfriend being a Yazata now.

                                                        Also interestingly, there are multiple versions of the story of Kartikkeya's birth where Agni swallows Shiva's semen and is either impregnated himself or passes on the seed to another being, so clearly the 'Agni has sex with other guys' theme has staying power.

                                                            Originally posted by Center-of-All View Post
                                                            Hmmm... would Ushas be perhaps an appropriate additional Deva? And I still think Hanuman would be an appropriate divine parent.
                                                            Ushas and her sister Ratri are both potential God candidates, though Ushas at least in the Vedas gives off a bit of a Titanic vibe... unlike Ratri who is more a Loving Guardian figure who protects against the dangers of the Night, Ushas is a more remote, awe inspiring figure of Light and even more importantly Order, who is often said to 'diminish the days of men'.

                                                            Ushas in the modern day has to some extent been combined with Saranyu, Goddess of the Dawn and Clouds, and wife of Surya. The festival of Chhath explicitly identifies Ushas and Saranyu as one, so like the erstwhile Soma becoming Chandra, the Vedic Ushas may have beome the Puranic Saranyu. Interestingly, Saranyu once created a clone of herself from her shadow... this clone called Chhaya (literally 'Shadow') is a goddess of shadows and darkness and can be seen as a sort of Puranic era Ratri, who sadly has next to no worship in the modern day.

                                                            Hanuman, like all Avatars, could may be treated as a separate God for purposes of patronage (I say patronage and not parenthood since many traditions describe Hanuman as a sworn virgin, so he is unlikely to sire Scions). It can just get very difficult sometimes to decide which figures in Hinduism should be individually written up, and which treated as Mantles or Incarnations of others (I mean, depending on whom you ask, Brahma Vishnu and Shiva are all Mantles of the same Primordial deity).
