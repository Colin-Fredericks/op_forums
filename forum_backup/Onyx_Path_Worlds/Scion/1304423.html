      I know little about Africa Pantheons, but one thing I learned about religion is that it’s quite hard to pin point pantheons when the cultures are not settled as a big empire.

      For example, the Celts used to have a set of gods they follow, but as they were not united, the beliefs where different in different villages, so it’s hard to build a proper pantheon from this disperse believes, even if there are a few of them that have a common name and even some correlation, like Tutatis. The Greeks on the other way, had a core belief that was established during the classic era, and even so there are several of the gods that are walking contradictions, like Aphrodite that have different origins depending where you look for it.

      Than, Eastern Africa has a long story, but the way it developed and the fights between the various groups didn’t rise a proper pantheon. Bantu is a umbrela the same way a Celtic pantheon would be.

      Same way, Yoruba pantheon is quite different in different places in the world, the African religions in Brazil are a mix of several religions the slaved people brought from Africa when kidnapped. Most of them where from the Western Africa, so there are several connections and correlations, but still it was not a single pantheon, not as we have now. It’s interesting for me, as I have access to lots of information in Portuguese, to read what is written about the Orishas, because there are lots of contradictions, changes in values and small things, just because it’s based more on the African version of the Orishas and less on the Brazilian version.

      Not a big help to you, just an advise after trying to do some research on Etruscan and Celtic pantheons myself...

          In addition to what Mataeus said, Eastern Bantu religions tend to be heavily henotheistic bordering on monotheism + ancestor worship, with the ancestors in question being personalized to each community and family. They’re extremely hard to cram into the Scion cosmology.

          That said, the Nyanga gods as appear in the Mwindo are one of the more fleshed our full Bantu pantheons. The Congo may be more westward then you were thinking, but it’s still nominally Central African. I’ve been using the Swahili (as the lingua-franca in the region) ““Miungu” for said Pantheon.

          Also the Zulu tradition gets you at least three or four deities. They’d be the oNkulunkulu.

              West Africa, besides being a vague and ultimately colonialist designation, is a hotbed of cultures. My own list for the region includes, besides the Yoruba and Ashanti, the Gbe (Fon and Ewe), Igbo, Efik, Hausa, Mande (specifically Bambara), Dogon, and possibly Mossi and Serer peoples. Due to this region being a unique cradle of trading empires all the way up to its fateful involvement in the slave trade, it has remained unusually diverse even in the face of proselitysing, whereas it is equally true that the North, Centre, and East of Africa have largely been converted to first Christianity and then Islam very early on, leaving us with few records of the original religions of many of the peoples there. Nonetheless, there are some possibilities to work with even when excluding the huge complex of the Bantu, as you suggested. There are the various Kushitic peoples including the Maasai and also peoples like the Dinka. They tend towards native Monotheism, but that makes them no less workable than other monotheistic or henotheistic religions already included in Scion (e.g. the Yoruba or Hindu Pantheons, or my and Watcher's own upcoming Ashanti Pantheon). The situation is similar for Ehtiopian minority groups like the Oromo, which have a similar emphasis on a single High God surrounded by lesser "angels", with the added fact that they have adapted many Christian stories into their own myth. And lastly, there are the pre-Bantu inhabitants of Central and Southern Africa that you've already pointed towards yourself - the Khoekhoe, San, and Mbuti peoples. I've personally sadly found nothing on Mbuti religion so far, but there are rich accounts of the Khoekhoe and San - too rich even, maybe. As you'll remember, Watcher and I were working on a San Pantheon for a while, but it has been sidelined for now since we found ourselves unable to resolve certain problems with the ethnic affiliation of certain myths etc. They're not impossible, they're not out forever, but they're certainly hard to do. Well, that much as a little overview from my side on non-Bantu, non-West or -North African Pantheons. Have fun with it

                          One group I'm aware of but not an expert on is the Kalenjin ethnic groups in Kenya. While almost all of them in the modern age are Christian or Muslim, their older religion had the usual all powerful righteous sky god, as well as a lesser thunder god and two rival lightning gods (one evil and one good). Asis was their primary sky god. (They also have a fairly cool regional monster, the Nandi Bear [though it sounds more like a giant hyena in its physical description], which eats the brains of its victims, usually disobedient children.)

                          There's also the Dinka [religion/folklore/mythology?], of the South Sudan region, which apparently has a small pantheon of gods, including the supreme sky god and creator, Nhialic, and his primary agent Deng, a god of rain and fertility. Also a goddess of gardening and womanhood, Abuk. Again, I don't really know anything about them, other than having heard of them.
