            Originally posted by Bioagent View Post
            Frankly, I am not a super fan of divinity being is so well known. It throws my head for a loop because the setting would be very different from a historical perspective. But that is where "suspension of disbelief" comes in I guess.
            I'mma quote Neall here on the whole lack of a Masquerade with the Divine:

            "There isn't [a Masquerade] in Scion 2e because there wasn't one for most of human history"

            And that just kinda made a lot snap into place. Because for a good majority of human history, these Gods WERE real. They influenced the lives of their worshippers, guided cultures, shaped history.

            Without ever actually appearing in person, arguably.

            That's why I kinda side-eye people insisting that history would be COMPLETELY different if the Gods were real. It may be a little different, but things could also shake out similarly.

            As for the rest, there's a reason why country lines in US Comic Books still look ROUGHLY the same, even if they have their names changes. Yes, the presence of Metahumans in the setting's history would probably change history about as much as Scion's history. But the Superhero Comics still map out... ROUGHLY the same. Why? Because it feels more familiar.

            :/ I'm not a historian, I'm not gonna try and speculate alternative histories that are maybe 10% actual insight and 90% bullshit butterflying. I'm not gonna redraw the national borders of the world.

            And if you change The World of Scion too much, it doesn't feel enough like our world, which would defeat most of the appeal of the game. And I don't know about you, but I find "The Gods exist, nobody doubts this, it looks similar to our world because it has to" easier to swallow than 1e's "There's a big Masquerade, the Gods pretend that they don't exist at all because shut up, their overblown egos allow them to hide, nobody catches on despite there not being any handwave of any sort, and you are one of their Children but nobody will believe you about this despite the fact that you'll be flying and throwing around fire, also your growth in power will be called Legend and vaguely tied to fame and worship but nobody will believe that the Gods exist because shut up"

            Edit: As for the whole "Edges of the World"... Maybe, but I still don't think they mesh THEMATICALLY. And like... Okay, not a huge Lovecraft Scholar, but I'm under the impression that the big central point of the works is like...

            "You are small. You are weak. The universe in its infinite vastness doesn't really care about you. There are beings out there that are so far above you that it's incomprehensible. Nobody actually cares. You're not special."

            That, from my understanding, is the central theme. Like if you had to enshrine the thing that puts the Horror in the Cosmic Horror of the Mythos, it would be that.

            And it's instantly defanged the moment that Cthulhu takes an Astra shot to the forehead from Brahma.

            So putting the Mythos in the greater context of the World feels not only jarring because of my personal disinterest in the Mythos, but it feels jarring because it's not a thematic fit. Everything about there being other Pantheons of Gods who actually give a shit and will protect humanity from the great darkness out there makes the very central theme to the Mythos fall apart.

                            Originally posted by yukikaze View Post
                            To be honest, I don't see why the lack of a Masquerade seems to be such a dealbreaker for people. If you can accept that as a reasonable suspension of disbelief in, say, the MCU movies, why can't you in Scion?
                            Agree with that. There is no need of masquerade, all you need is a reason to avoid using the powers near mortals, like a paradox or Fatebinding:

                            Gods are far more dramatically affected by Fatebindings. Not only can they become bound to entire peoples and cultures, Gods find themselves (or at least, their Godly Mantles) changed by their Fatebindings just as much as those they’re bonded with. You can see this in the character of Ares/Mars, who is variously a vainglorious coward, a rampaging brute, or the model of martial virtue, depending on what Mantle he wears and what societies (e.g. Athens vs. Sparta, Asia Minor vs. Rome) those Mantles are Fatebound to. It’s for this reason that Gods are reluctant to engage directly with the World, preferring to act through intermediaries like their Scions.

                                                    If you want to have your Scions do horrific things for the greater good, you don't need the Mythos to force them to do it.                                                     But I guess ultimately even beyond the Thematic Reasons, there's a big reason I object to the Mythos being put in the same setting as the other Gods.

                                                    I am really really really uncomfortable trying to imply, even slightly, that the rantings of a New England racist are on the same level as these very real religions that very real people worship. YES I realize that the Mythos has grown beyond its origins, but like... I just find it very weak. And dull. And unengaging. On multiple levels.

                                                    I don't find it scary because unlike Lovecraft, my self-worth isn't so tied to the idea that I'm Super Special due to my breeding that the implication of not being special fills me terror. I don't find the image of a universe with no greater plan and no higher loving power scary. Because that means I can just make my OWN fate in the world.

                                                    I don't find it interesting because it's just "Grah big monsters in space" with a dusting of the aforementioned stuff. I don't find Cthulhu scary beyond "Oh no big monster that can eat me", which you could replace with a T. rex or a Rathalos from Monster Hunter. This may have been because I first knew of Cthulhu as a meme back when I was in high school. That was over a decade ago, and I've only found him less engaging since then.

                                                    And if you're going to write them up as Titans... Why give them Divine Callings as though they'd be Patrons of a regular Pantheon? The Callings are meant to be archetypes that echo aspects of Humanity that the Gods themselves reflect.

                                                    How does a Pantheon that doesn't protect have a Guardian? How does a Pantheon that only kill have Healer? How does a Pantheon that doesn't lead to a bright future have Leader? How does a Pantheon that cares not for our mortal laws or justice have Judge?

                                                    Just... Yeah, to repeat my point: The Mythos Pantheon REALLY doesn't work in a setting where the other Pantheons exist. They add nothing beyond some pop culture points. They're really not interesting to me, and the game that they support isn't really in-line with the main assumptions of the game.

                                                    I am not saying you are wrong. The Mythos is a tough nut to crack. It all depends on what angle they are going to approach this.
