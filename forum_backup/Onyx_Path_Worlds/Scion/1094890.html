                                          Originally posted by racain23 View Post
                                          My understanding was that the sythe was a pre-Christian holdover, but I have nothing concrete to back it up. I have read through revelations, but I've always struggled to make coherent sense of it.
                                          I would actually suggest the Scythe not being pre-Christian, but a later Christian change in their iconography. The Four Horsemen have a very interesting relationship in their depictions over time, with Revelations being about third century CE, and being carried through Christian popular culture through then. I don't know of a lot of places where a Scythe developed into a piece of death iconography asides from The Horsemen, not anywhere in Europe until the Black Death at least where Death picks it up in popular depiction.

                                          For example, there becomes a divide in Western and Eastern Europe over the identity of one of the Horsemen. In Western Europe, Conquest is replaced by Pestilence, where Eastern Europe keeps Conquest. There is about a thousand years for popular religious belief to change and dance over this period, and I don't think the Scythe shows up on Death until the arrival of the Black Plague in Western Europe. At this point, Conquest is faded out for Pestilence, and I believe the idea of Death clearing lives like wheat becomes more common, and thus the Scythe. Of course, all of this is in essentially 'Pop-Religion,' and it doesn't always become cannonical. For example, Pestilence still is not one of the Four Horsemen in traditional Catholic systems, but the general public never remembers Conquest.

                                                  Originally posted by danelsan View Post
                                                  I guess some might prefer Pestilence because Conquest feels kinda redundant with War. Of course, they all lead to Death, so it is not much of an issue.
                                                  Revelations is so incredibly symbolical and weird that even theologians have enormous trouble interpreting it. Some make a distinction between Conquest as a process of domination, and War as all blood and gore. Some say Conquest is war between nations, while War represents civil war. Some say that the horseman on the white horse is Jesus Christ, re-conquering the world spiritually on Judgement Day. And what the hell does "all Hades following Death" mean? Information is slim, with the Horsemen just covering a few lines in that whole big book, then never appearing again.

                                                  That said, I'm always a fan of making the horseman on the white horse Pollution

                                                  For more Abrahamic mythology on Death, one could also consider the well-known Islamic depiction of the angel of death, Azrael, a terrible being with innumerable eyes and tongues. Whenever he claims a person's life, one eye closes, and when all his eyes are closed and the end of the world arrives, Azrael himself will die. Then there is the death of the firstborns in Exodus and some other passages in the Old Testament alluding to a personified Death - in Exodus, it actually says that God himself walks through Egypt to bring death, but as with Jacob wrestling with God, Jewish and Christian commentary later explains that as an angel having acted as representative of God in these situations. The Book of Job then mentions a whole class of minor angels of death and fate, called memitim in Hebrew. So whether one wishes to make a composite figure out of that or several separate ones, there is enough material for sure.
