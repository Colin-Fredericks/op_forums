    So, you probably noticed that the video in general is a bit exaggerated, and doesn't cite any sources itself, but they make an important distinction - the wives aren't expected to suicide when their husbands died, as you put it, but are expected to commit suicide after their husbands already committed ritual suicide. And that puts us in a whole new realm of complexity. The video generalises about a millennium of historical development on samurai practices into 7 minutes - and the same goes for our short culture entries here. Both present only the gist of an issue, and, in case of the video, the most "horrifying" elements of it. As you can tell from our culture entries, all these practices changed and developed over a long time (if you want more detail on any specific era or stage of development, maybe rather PM me before I start dragging this whole thread into a drawn-out discussion).

    Generalising again, I can say that a wife committing suicide alongside her husband was not common practice - as I've mentioned, committing honourable suicide was actually a way for a retainer to force his lord into providing for his family and to exempt them from collective punishment. But, of course, there was never a standard case of seppuku, as there was never a standard case of any cultural practice. As we've explained, seppuku was often forced and used as punishment, and in such cases, family, friends, or servants of the condemned could be forced to join in on the belly-slitting, and yes, in such case a wife could be affected, too. But it was in no way the usual procedure.

    Such collective punishment of the (innocent) family members of a criminal alongside themselves was very common in all of East Asia, and was supposed to act as a strong deterrent against crime, although it was of course mainly used in political cases. Before the beginning of the age of samurai, Japan was actually quite queasy about capital punishment in general, but collective punishment was carried out widely in China, and it is, sadly, used in North Korea to this day.
