        I'm doing some digging into the Nyanga pantheon - lets call them the Milungu.

        Nyamurairi: God of fire, earthquakes, and volcanos; king of the subterranean realm of the gods

        Mukiti: Serpentine river god

        Muisa: God of the dead

        Kahindo: Goddess of good fortune, daughter of Muisa

        Yana: Bat god, uncle to the culture hero Mwindo, he & his bats are the forge-workers for Nyamurairi

        Kitundukutu: Cricket god and spy-master/collector of all gossip of the gods

        Mweri: Moon goddess

        Kubikubi: God of stars & the sky, in reversal to most pantheons, secondary to the subterranean fire god Nyamurairi, though lord of his own domain

        Kiruka: the rain goddess, and old woman, and mother to Nkuba

        Nkuba: the vengeful lightning god

        Kentse: sun god

        Ongo: god of those yet to be born in his subterranean realm, molds the human form in the womb

        Mwindo: Ascended culture hero

        Mpaca: Possibly a Titan, but could also be a god of nocturnal dangers hostile to mankind

                    Originally posted by No One of Consequence View Post
                    I'd enjoy seeing the Zulu pantheon. (I'm not sure exactly how separate they are from other Bantu cultures.)

                    They're distinct, but I'm having a hard time finding that many deities. Bantu traditions tend to pretty focused on single creator deities + ancestors, which is why I was so surprised to find the Nyanga pantheon. Like Watcher said, most Bantu traditions are heavily henotheistic.

                    The pantheon name for the Zulu would be the oNkulunkulu (which is the plural for divinity, with Unkulunkulu or uThixo being the primordial creator).

                    Pantheon members include Umvelinqangi as the sky father figure often conflated with Unkulunkulu, but more on what Scion would treat as the "God" level (though interestingly he sometimes precedes Unkulunkulu, creating him from the reeds of Uthlanga), Mamlambo the mother of rivers, and Nomkhubulwane, the agriculture and beer goddess of the rainbow.

                    Uhlakanyana could be a deity. He's a malevalent trickster dwarf who likes to challenge humans to fights and contests. Defeat means death, but he hands out magic powers to those who win. Some sources seem treat this as a race though, ala faeries, rather than an individual.

                    There's also Uthlanga, a swamp that is also a deity and might be a primordial or titan, that Umvelinqangi wed and bore Unkulunkulu.

                    Unwaba is a chameleon who's a divine messenger, but honestly that guy gets all over Africa in different names. Even Yoruban tradition has Olorun use a chameleon as his divine messenger.

                            Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                            @Watcher

                            Who do you see as a viable Divine Parent/Patron for the Abosom beside Tano and Anansi? I’m assuming Nyame and Asase Ya are your typical Sky Father and Earth Mother Primordials who many Pantheons claim descent from?
                            The four of them would be totally viable Divine Parents or Patrons, but there is also the Ancestors, and the Ntoro Deities who would make viable patrons, and Divine parents.

                            Nyame and Asase Ya look like your typical Sky Father and Earth Mother at a glance, but they're actually really-really different. Nyame doesn't really have a super-duper Virile Father, he is just a really benevolent Creator Deity who happens to live in the sky, and control its functions. He isn't actually a personification of the sky a-la Ouranos, it's just his place of residence, and his primary area of control. Essentially, think of it like he created the Abosom to delegate out the functions of reality, but he didn't delegate out the functions of the heavens, and thus manages those himself. Asase Ya on the other hand isn't personified, she isn't anthropomorphised, she just is the earth. She doesn't have any of the Maternalistic angles, instead being the creator of the Underworld (she houses the dead within her body), protector of humanity when both dead and alive, and involved with various taboos about farming. So, at a glance, the pair look very 'Standard Gaia-Ouranos situation,' but they're actually really different. Nyame and Asase Ya are also not a couple, Nyame has a bunch of wives, but she isn't one of them.

                            While there is the phrase, "All are the children of Nyame, none are the children of Asase," this is less about Nyame being an All-Father and instead discussing Nyame's caring, fatherly love for his creations, both Abosom, and humanity. Nyame is the creator of all of the beings in reality (except Anansi, and possibly Asase Ya) not through your 'standard' "had sex with a rock," or, "ejaculated on the dirt," sort of creator, but instead very much a physical creation. Nyame doesn't have the virile angle going on, he just 'makes' stuff.

                            The other potential Divine Parents and Patrons are the Ntoro Deities, which are... sort of really complicated. Okay, so, if the Ashanti were an Imperial Group, there were sub-clans which were the Ntoro groups, a lot like an Irish tuath, or the Haida Eagle-Raven family groups. You can't have a child with someone who is part of your own Ntoro group for example, and the Ntoro has a whole aspect about the soul, and reincarnation, and the like. The Ntoro Deities are the patron Deities of these various Ntoro groups, providing their mortals with the capacity to procreate, and serving as their patron. Depending on who your Ntoro Deity is, you will have a collection of various taboos. The most well known of these is Bosommuru, a great python that rests beneath a lake. Rather nice fellow. There is a whole host of these Ntoro Deities.

                            Then the Ancestors. They would make viable Patrons to a Scion. Ashanti minor and major Kings (rulers of an individual clan, or the entire empire) are invested with the divine force of the clan or empire through special stools. Black Stools which important ancestors linger around for the clan groups, or The Golden Stool for the entire empire itself. These minor and major Kings would be perfect Scions, except they pick up a ton of taboos that would make playing the game really difficult. They could be awesome NPC Scions, or if a player wants to take a really big challenge that would put Geasa to shame, they could give it a bash. These are my general notes on the subject of this challenge.

                            Ignoring the fact that a Chieftain or King would have their weeks consumed by ritual practices in specific geographic locations, making adventuring slightly difficult, these taboos cripple the capacity of an Ashanti Chieftain or King to be a viable player character: The King of the Ashanti must never touch the ground, must never stumble, be hit, or hit another. They must not eat in public, must not drink in public, must always be in perfect health, remain physically unblemished, and must go to bed early every night. If these taboos are not kept, the King is divested of their Kingship, their divine nature vanishing, and ceases to be of any significance. Chieftains can similarly have their power ripped from them through a political process where they are removed from their office, for reasons such as failing to perform a ritual, violating one of their taboos, cowardice, and a myriad of other reasons. While these positions within the Ashanti Empire can be viably replicated with the Scion template, due to the fact one can spontaneously cease to be a Scion and due to the many taboos, it would be exceptionally difficult for a player or Band to cope with this. Further, there already is currently a King of the Ashanti who has sat upon the Golden Stool, and there can only be one at a time.
                            The two Titans might also make okay Divine Parents to a player who wants to do something super weird, but, who knows. After the Scions of Titans rules are published, we could go back through all of the Pantheons we have done and see if we could implement the system. There are also just sort of random Abosom who are Divine Parents in folklore. The Abosom of a palm tree is one I believe.

                                    There definitely aren't eight more for the Abosom. We did a fair bit of research on them, and apart from those four, everyone else is just so incredibly local and minor that we couldn't flesh them out at all. A minimum of twelve is very unwieldy for the majority of the world's pantheons, I think, since most pre-modern cultures show only a very small, hard core of important deities with a huge number of localised cults. Of the pantheons we're currently working on, we're seeing 7 major gods for the Manchu pantheon, 10 for the Hittites, 4 for the Taíno, 8 for the Haida, and, as mentioned, 4 for the Ashanti. The only ones out of these you could somehow construe to have 12 gods at all, including minor ones, would be the Hittites.
