                        On the topic of the Orisha perception of other Pantheon's Titans Lula, a lot of what we see in the preview is mention of the... Sapient I guess is the right word? The Sapient Titans. Some Titans are not really 'Peope-y' and more colossal creatures. Hadi, Skoll, Apep, Jormungandr, Typhon, Namazu, those sorts of Titans. Ones who are just horrifically powerful animals more or less without any real stated capacity to communicate with others. How do the Orisha get along with them? It feels as though their, "Titans are marginalized Pantheons," angle is workable in many cases, but it feels to be a bit unsteady in the face of the less human, the less conversation, and the purely wild. Of course, not all Titans who happen to be in the shape of animals are this way, Fenrir can speak and is cunning, Illuyanka is a giant snake but is probably a more reasonable authority figure than Tarhun, just some of the incredibly bestial Titans seem to not fit into their world view.

                        My other question is how do they relate with the... I guess you could call them Flatly Antagonistic Titans. Izanami, for example, who sews death through the mortal world. Balor of the Evil Eye, a sea-faring, colonizing, conquering slaver. Isfet (Probably actually a Primordial to be fair) the opposite of Ma'at. Angra Mainyu, a great cosmic evil. (Also probably to be fair a Primordial) I can see how the Orisha might want bring peace with the more affable Titans, but the... 'Cosmic Evil,' figures, or you know, just Balor being an oppressive colonizing slaver. Are the Orisha still trying to 'make friends' there? Or do they sort of 'get' that some Titans are just not nice people. Not because they're Titans, just because they're personally cosmically antagonists, or just mean people.

                        All well and good to make friends with Titans, I love the angle, gives the Orisha a rather unique outlook. But occasionally one of them's literally a personification of Evil. Not all the time, but, they crop up.

                                                I have some mixed feelings on this one, to be honest. For the most part its pretty good and I can ignore the typos - I assume that it will get triple-checked before printing to add in the ocassional missing word - but ever now and then there's a sentence that I have some doubts about. The aforementioned Godwin's Law issue is one of them, but also sentences like 'In the Americas, the Night Doctors creep about under cover of darkness, abducting black folks American society forgets. They then experiment on their captives, transforming them into monsters, infecting them with syphilis to see what happens, or pumping them full of deadly experimental super-soldier serum.' also make me slightly nervous, for lack of a better term.

                                                I don't have a problem with real world stuff having a magical explanation in-game, but when you start getting into conspiracy theories I'm going to have some reservations, especially the "The CIA invented aids and gave it to black people" type of conspiracy theories, which is what I first thought of when I saw that. I don't have a problem with dealing with/addressing real world issues in game - its kind of necessary, especially for pantheons who are the patrons of people who face these issues like this one - but I feel like it should be handled a little bit more carefuly than this one did.

                                                Ultimately, though, I liked this one. Good work.
