    Separate Roman Pantheon?

    01-22-2014, 05:03 PM
    I was thinking, with the promise of several new Pantheons in Scion 2e, there's something that's been on my mind: should the Dodekatheon represent both Greek and Roman gods, or should they be separate pantheons this time around? I personally think so, because contrary to popular belief, the Romans didn't just take the Greek religion, change the names and call it their own, but instead, upon learning of the Greek gods, basically said, "Hey, Mars and this Ares guy are pretty similar, maybe they're the same god". Besides, there are plenty of Roman gods with no Greek equivalent, like Janus and Pamona, and even the ones that do have some glaring differences. For example, the Romans saw Mars as a god of honourable combat and the spread of civilization, a far cry from the Greek depiction as Ares as a crazed, bloodthirsty thug (and besides, Mars was also the god of farming, and I can't exactly imagine a Scion of Ares plowing fields as his Calling).

          I think a separate pantheon is splitting hairs. Romans took a lot of deities from various other pantheons. (Mithra is the usual celebrated example) But for reference: Interpretatio graeca Some deities dating to Rome's oldest religious stratum, such as Janus and Terminus, had no Greek equivalent. Other Greek divine figures, most notably Apollo, were adopted directly into Roman culture, but underwent a distinctly Roman development, as when Augustus made Apollo one of his patron deities. In the early period, Etruscan culture played an intermediary role in transmitting Greek myth and religion to the Romans, as evidenced in the linguistic transformation of Greek Heracles to Etruscan Her[e]cle to Roman Hercules.

                      Every storyteller has to make a personal decision about which influences to include, and which to put aside. For example, within my own game setting I prefer the idea that many of the Greek gods evolved into the Roman gods. Ares was literally a belligerent teenager who matured into the far more responsible adult, Mars.

                      Another storyteller might decide that they are the same person, but fatebonds force them to act a different way when they are speaking to different people. A third storyteller might decide that Ares is the only real god, and the stories of Mars are nothing more than humans getting carried away. A fourth storyteller might decide that they actually are two different gods who may or may not appreciate the syncretization.

                      Similarly, I prefer the idea that Brigid (being one of the few Tuatha to survive) became embroiled in Christian politics and embraced the role of St. Brigit.

                              As Glamour says, the problem is that while there are some differences, primarily of personality, Greek and Roman gods tend to have the same stories. Ares and Mars are very, very different personalities, but they do the same things. They have the same stories. They have the same Legend. So how can you meaningfully split them apart? There are some, and actually not all that many, uniquely Roman Gods. ...who may actually be Etruscan instead... that you might be able to split off. And the same actually goes for the Etruscans themselves, who also got lumped in with the Greco-Romans. It's because all three societies did a lot of trading and mimicking, with the Romans being the "latest" of the three and thus tending to "absorb" the other two.

                                              The whole multiple-instances-of-Gods works really well in a world where Gods are created by mortal belief. The Romans envisioned Jupiter this way, prayed to him that way, and they created Jupiter as the figure they wanted him to be. The Greeks had their own ideas and created Zeus to fit those ideas. Two Gods, one for each culture. Their Legend is the same, but their worshippers are different and since worship creates the Gods, then there are two different Gods.

                                              Iron Druid Chronicles, American Gods and the Kate Daniels series all go with this idea. It's very popular in urban fantasy stories because it makes the Gods fit more nicely into a consensual reality setting where the mass will of humanity is the strongest element of the setting, putting humans on top of the pile. Gods might be stronger than individual humans, but Humanity is the chief power of the cosmos through Specialness.

                                              Scion doesn't have that element, so it becomes considerably more difficult to have Zeus and Jupiter sharing the same Legends. If both of them did the exact same things, which one was doing it? All Myths Are True, but how does that work? Did it all happen twice? Or three, or four, or seven, or infinite times, once for each slightly different cult view? Massive, homogenous, organized religion is pretty dern spankin new. The Athenian Ares was already different from the Spartan Ares, does that mean there are two or more Areseses, in addition to a Mars and a Laran?
