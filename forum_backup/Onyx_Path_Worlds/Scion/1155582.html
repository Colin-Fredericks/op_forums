    Therianthrope Basic Template?

    11-03-2017, 07:54 PM
    After reading back through Scion: Demigod's antagonist section, I realized that Therianthropes don't have a basic template like the Undead (i.e Vampires) did. It just gives three examples of werebeast characters with a mixed assortment of powers and weaknesses. Tommy Kehanu, for example, gains access to a Tough Hide in his selkie form, which adds +4 to Lethal and Bashing soaks. Where as Werner Krieger is the only pre-made Therianthrope who is listed as having Epic Attributes. Hayden Marsh appears to be a very bland Therianthrope, who's only real powers include shapeshifting into her hyena form and gaining some bestial enhancements while in that state.

    This, I realized, makes it kind of difficult to come up with a basic premise for what kind of abilities a Therianthrope should have based on their species and level of power. Since all three examples appear to be focused on a specific individual, rather than a generalized grouping.

    So I'm asking around some of the more experienced players, to see what kind of template or model they think would be suitable for designing Therianthropes as mass produced characters?

                      Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post

                      For convenience, let's start with a basic template for Werewolves, seeing as they're the most iconic type of therianthrope.

                      I was thinking basic werewolf mythology type. i.e person meets a certain trigger and changes into a beast / man-beast form.

                      Now that I think about it, the idea of Selkies shedding their skin could also be applied to Werewolves. I've read about stories involving witches who can change into wolves by donning their pelts, and there are several movies in which werewolves assume their beast form by shedding their human skin.

                      Essentially, what I'm looking for is an idea on what immediate benefits would your average mortal receive after becoming a therianthrope. Does being a therianthrope carry any inherent bonuses to skills / attributes that they didn't have as a normal mortal, or do those come through training and practice? As I said, Werner was the only pre-made therianthrope in the book that had Epic Atrributes and knacks. While Tommy and Hayden appeared to still have mortal level capabilities, aside from their beast form enhancements.
                      Is there a specific sort of Werewolf you are looking for? Like, Berserkers, the children of King Lycaon, and the werewolves we see in Irish laws would probably all be different templates for example. If you are looking for the idea of the Anthropomorphic Wolf-Man from Medieval European Folklore, that's totally possible. The most common sort of Werewolf you get in European mythology is people who are transformed, or transform themselves, into just normal wolves.

                      The Folkloric Anthropomorphic Wolf-Man is pretty easy to organize into a template though. Starting off, lets break down the basic features of the entity. They are physically powerful, can, but are not always, frightening, might have better senses, and sometimes have regenerative capacities. At base, the main feature of these entities is physical power, so let's tag Epic Strength as the 'Key' feature of this entity. They are going to gain Dots of Epic Strength when shifted. Since it is their primary feature, let's say that they always have it as the highest value for their Legend, so they have Epic Strength at a set value of Legend-1. They should have two different sorts of attacks, both dealing Lethal, their claws, and their bite. Let's say Claws use the Seax template for the Claws, and use the Labrys template for the Bite.

                      Now let's get into the sub-variations.

                      Some of these Folkloric Werewolves are really scary, so some of them will have Negative Epic Appearance, but since it isn't a primary feature, even of those who happen to have it, let's say it's at Legend-3. So a Legend 4 Werewolf would have 1 Dot. Enough to scare mortals, but not enough to really throw a Scion. Since the Howl is a big feature of the frightening aspect, let's give them a special alteration to their Negative Epic Appearance that lets them use it with their howl, even if not in line of sight. So a Werewolf could use their Appearance + Presence to intimidate mortals even when not in line of sight, as well as using Knacks such as Dreadful Mein.

                      Having heightened senses is not always a common image for these sorts of Werewolves, but probably just because a lot of their stories don't have us spending a lot of time with the Werewolf 'on screen,' since most of the time they are the antagonist. Let's just assume that the 'better than human' senses carries over even if it's not in all of their folklore due to the nature of the stories. Since it's a part of their folklore, but not huge, let's say they have Legend-2 Epic Perception.

                      The idea of Werewolves having regenerative capacities is not super duper common until modern times, but let's throw it in here because it's iconic to these Anthropomorphic Werewolves. The obvious solution is to give them Epic Stamina, but, would that disrupt the image of the Werewolf since it just tanks their Soaks? Werewolves do get hurt, and get hurt badly, what's important is that some of them are able to recover quickly. So, let's not give them Epic Stamina, but instead give them a really nasty regeneration factor. Let's say they regain their Legend in Lethal, and remove all Bashing at the start of each of their rounds, even if unconscious. Silver will cause Aggravated damage to get around this, and just dismembering them by forcing Lethal to roll over into Aggravated is still also an option. Maybe also give them some extra Health Boxes if you want them to be more tanky.

                      Add in a splash of Malice, Rapacity, or some other Virtues depending on what sort of Werewolf you are trying to depict.

                      I would say that's your basic template. So a freshly turned mortal will have 1 Epic Strength. They then might have some extra health, and might be able to regenerate 2 Lethal and all Bashing at the start of each of their turns, with the sub-note that they take Aggravated from Silver. If they get to higher Legends, they may develop Epic Perception, and Negative Epic Appearance with some special clauses about use depending on what sort of Folkloric Werewolves you want to use as a Storyteller.

                      The details such as the Curse, or Disease, and the transformation trigger can be left to the individual Storyteller as well since those change quite often. As well as leave a note that there might also be some other bans a Storyteller may want to incorporate such as issues with consecrated ground, or church bells.

                      Just to show how there would be different Templates for different sorts of 'Werewolves,' I can quickly do up the Berserkers as an example.

                      Berserkers would retain the Legend -1 Epic Strength, but would lack the Negative Epic Appearance, the Epic Perception, and Regeneration. Instead, they would have some sort of Epic Charisma-esque effect where their infamy travels before them, reflecting the position of the historical Berserks in the Icelandic sagas. We can also give them a feature where their Berserk state gives them Animal Aspect (Wolf) or (Bear) to kick in on them, push them into a Courage Virtue Extremity, and give them some Dots of Courage, and let's say Malice since the Berserks are never regarded as particularly nice sorts of people you want around.

                              Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                              I'm not familiar with the cultural variants. The only werewolves I've ever heard of were simply described as the kind who turn into wolves / wolf-men and then proceed to go on destructive rampages until they return to human form. Although modern pop culture has given rise to lycanthropes who retain some measure of self control in their transformed state, most of the classics had them devolve into blood thirsty savages.
                              Oh yeah, there's a massive amount of variation in what we would call a 'Werewolf' in European tradition. The Wolf-Men idea is very recent, coming to popularity in popular culture in the Victorian period. Up until then, the 'Werewolf' is mostly just an individual who becomes a wolf, not necessarily even a powerful wolf. The notion of 'The Bite' is also super recent.

                              So, like, the Berserkers from Norse tradition are a group who sometimes get identified as being a Proto-Werewolf, humans who in a Battle Frenzy (PIE motif/theme) become more creature than human. Of course, by the time we see large scale literature emerging out of the Norse, the Berserkers have long-since passed their hayday, and function more like outlaws than anything, so we don't really know their pre-Christian 'mechanical' function. If it was a blessing of Odin, or the animal-aspect angle is just a poetic way of making reference to a rage-state.

                              King Lycaon, one of the... Silver Age? Humans from Greek Mythology who tricks (or tries to trick) Zeus into consuming the flesh of one of Lycaon's own sons after Lycaon slaughtered and had him made into food. He is cursed for testing the knowledge of Zeus, also for murdering a child of his, and trying to have someone commit cannibalism. He runs off into the wilds never to be seen again. This notion of a 'Divine Curse,' leveled upon someone becomes more common under Christianity, potentially having a root in this story of Lycaon. He doesn't shapeshift or anything, but his curse is to live as a wolf.

                              Then in Ireland, we don't have awesome records in a literary sense, but werewolves, at least, female werewolves show up in the legal tracts. They are regarded as people who wear the skins of wolves, though it's not super clear if this is a literal 'donning a mantle of wolf fur to become a wolf' or 'changes shape and thus is wearing wolf's skin' sort of thing. They're not held legally responsible for their actions while in their wolf-shape, but are also exempt from anyone needing to provide them hospitality when injured since they're regarded as dangerous or at least unpredictable. So they are shapeshifting, but it seems to be willing, and just into the form of a wolf, no wolf-woman situation.

                              Then later in the Medieval Period of Europe, you get a 'development' of the werewolf folklore, very regionalized, so the Loup-Garou of France is very different from what you would find as the image of a 'Werewolf' in the Eastern Holy Roman Empire for example. The idea of a 'Werewolf' as a entity is super-recent, same with 'Vampire' or 'Elf' even. They are modern constructions of earlier Myth and Folkloric traditions, distinct different entities merged into a single being, with aspects removed, or added, or changed in the modern (mostly Victorian) retellings of the stories. This is why the notion of just 'what is' a Werewolf is hard to explain, they're not actually one 'thing,' the Loup-Garou, King Lycaon, the Berserkers, the Wolf-Woman of Eastern Europe, the Irish Werewolves, they're all distinctly different entities, with different 'powers,' with very vague connections. The idea of not being in control of their actions is also not a common feature, the Berserks sort of are, King Lycaon is for sure.

                              I am glad I could be of help though! It is a very interesting topic, Werewolves become a medical issue in the Middle Ages of Europe, it's a really an interesting area of medical history.
