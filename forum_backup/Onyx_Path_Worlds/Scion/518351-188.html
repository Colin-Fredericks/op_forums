    Both, really, in a way.

    Zurvanism was a Mazdayasnian heresy that enjoyed some prominence during the Sassanid Empire, but otherwise disappeared after the 10th century. Zoroastrian Mazdayasna was always the dominant strain and remains as such today. It's like representing Pelagianism as the dominant strain of Christian theology despite it definitely not being the case ever. Zurvan is always represented as subordinate to Ahura Mazda, not as the primordial force, in the Avesta. Basically, whomever wrote the previous Yazata book seemed to not have knowledge of Zoroastrianism except through those Greek sources that thought that they were all Zurvanites back then.

                                                Originally posted by xhepablo View Post
                                                Is the conflict between the Devas and the Yazata stated in the Devas preview only between the Vedic Devas and Yazata? I mean, there are instances of Parsis in India worshiping Ganesh as a Yazata, and here's one recent example that is heavily attended and organized by Parsi Zoroastrians: https://www.indiablooms.com/ibns_new...d-ganesha.html

                                                The critique is mostly of the Vedic devas and how they used to be violent, sacrificing, caste-imposing deities, not really many of the modern Hindu ones.
                                                Ganesha has already been stated in the Preview as being very open to working with people his Pantheon might in general be wary of... in addition, the largest communities of Parsis in India live in Mumbai, which is Ganesha's city... they likely have his explicit protection, and I do not doubt that the community's interactions with him, both IRL and in the World, were significant factors in his viewpoints towards working with Asura. The Preview also mentions that no matter what the Deva think of the Yazata, they strongly disapprove of taking any of that out on the Parsi community.

                                                All that being said, the modern day Deva are just as involved, by and large, with fighting Asura as the Vedic era Gods, and the conflation of Asura and Ahura (and the same for Deva and Daeva on the other side) ensures that even the modern day Deva are likely to be wary of an entire Pantheon self-identifying as Titans (and vice versa).

                                                Of the Deva, Indra is definitely the most likely to blow up against the Yazata and take action against them... he's entire fame as a Vedic deity is in his exploits against various Asura like Vritra and the Panis...and even his more modern myths have him constantly coming into conflict with them (sometimes even completely innocent ones whom he works against for no other reason than that they are Asura).

                                                Durga is an interesting case due to her function... as THE Guardian Goddess, Durga's myths only ever feature evil Asura because her myths need to feature her saving the World, unlike Indra's myths which focus primarily on his glory in combat (Vedic era) or his insecurities about his throne (Puranic era)... therefore, while Durga acts against Asura all the time, she never acts against innocent Asura like Indra does... and it is noteworthy that her myths always mention her starting the battle with a warning to leave and let these people be, and she will let the Asura go unharmed... they always reply with 'Hey, pretty lady, have sex with me!'... only following which does she ever enter battle. So basically, Durga is unlikely to ever start trouble... she's just very, very likely to finish it.

                                                Among the other Gods, Brahma doesn't care much, and has blessed countless Asura in myth before... Shiva is also known to be fairly impartial, and Vishnu has explicitly had notable Asura devotees like Prahlad and Bali whom he has blessed. Lakshmi and Parvati don't interact with Asura much outside of a few obscure myths a piece where some Asura or the other tries to kidnap/seduce them, and likely don't have strong opinions on the matter, while Sarasvati on the other hand, is a Vedic era Goddess who is described in the Veda as riding into battle against Asura... so she may have more negative reactions.. on the other hand I can't imagine her not getting along with Anahita.

                                                Agni is pretty much a Vedic era Ganesha in his job as bridge between worlds, and likely would be open to Yazata relations... Surya probably toes the party line in a general 'why not' way without being too invested personally, and Varuna is probably still pissed that his boyfriend Mitra left him for the Yazata (I'm not getting into the whole 'is Varuna Ahura Mazda' can of worms). Yama is too busy for this shit... also he MAY be Yima over in that Pantheon.

                                                Kali will probably just do whatever the fuck she wants to, unless the Yazata attack one of her sister selves.

                                                (Btw, I know I'm using Asura and Yazata interchangeably in that piece... I am fully aware that they are not actually the same groups in the modern day, whatever they might have been in the depths of time... but the Deva still see them that way, much as the Yazata see the Deva as the same as the Daeva, so I was writing it from their perspective).

                                                Out of curiosity, we see a fair few stories of Asura who are good and often devoted to the Deva in myth... are there any similar stories of Daeva in Persian myth who are good and/or convert to the worship of Ahura Mazda and the Yazata?
