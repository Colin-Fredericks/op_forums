    Unveiled

    08-04-2014, 07:28 AM
    Presume some Scions do something so obvious and so over the top that it is impossible (given the information age) to keep it under wraps. What then would the reaction of the world be? We're talking about giving the whole world conclusive, empirical evidence that not only do the old gods exist, but they have power, and power that is more immediate and effective than anything we've seen in thousands of years.

      The everyday person's reaction depends on how the revelation came about. Most people who are too old to read Percy Jackson won't believe you're a child of Zeus no matter what you do. They'll either believe that you're powerful but have a different explanation for your powers or that you're a fake. If the revelation was on TV, most will believe it's faked.

      As for large groups, it also depends on how the revelation occurred but will probably be more predictable:

      Governments: move to defend themselves from the new powers by seeking out powers of their own. Since this is an action game, some might initiate pre-emptive strikes.

      Churches: publicly denounce the new false gods. Since this is an action game, some might decide to burn these witches themselves.

      Corporations: how does the revelation affect their bottom line, and can they benefit from it? Since this is an action game, some might decide that further research is necessary and that the so-called gods' rights are less important than scientific and financial progress.

      Scientists: immediate attempts to understand and catalog these new beings. Well, right after they manage to prove experimentally that it isn't a hoax. Since this is an action game, some might find ways to enhance powers while others find ways to dampen them. Still others may seek to capture scions for experimentation and even look for ways to bend the godlings' ichor to their own ends.

              A one-off thing can be written off as a fake, but the more often it happens, the more publicly it happens, the more likely it is people are going to accept there's _some_ reality to it - after all, extraordinary evidence is being demonstrated on a repeatable basis.

              Then there's everything that comes with the Scions - followers, creatures, titanspawn...

              And in a 2e campaign, you're also going to have sorcerers, oracles and holy people demonstrating people can develop certain powers, that they're drawing from the myths, and that there's something powerful associated with the myths.

              It'll get to the point people acknowledge something above and beyond current scientific understanding is going on here, that people are suddenly gaining superhuman powers, that mythic creatures and monsters, and modernised versions of same, are crawling out of the woodwork... The effect on the sciences alone would be cataclysmic, as what we know gets turned upside down.

              You may get a number of general viewpoints:

              * All of this is inspired by mythology.
              * All of this is the inspiration for mythology.
              * All of this _is_ mythology updated for the present day.

              You're going to get the mercenary, seeking to make use of these powers; the researchers, seeking to understand what's going on here; the spiritual seekers, looking for faith or meaning; the suspicious, wondering if this is all it seems; the believers, who accept this is what it appears to be; the believers with a grudge, who want to know where the gods have _been_ all this time; the media, looking for something that's going to get more eyeballs - 'cause you'd better believe this'd get eyeballs by the plenty, since it's good spectacle; and yet more besides.

                          It all really depends on how active the Gods, magical creatures and other Scions of the setting are. With Epic Socials, it's child's play to sway mortals. Spin it however you want and the world dances to your tune. Call up a few key people, Social them into your brainslaves and then go on TV, use some Knacks and boom. Positive press from every direction.

                          My players are currently teetering on the edge of revealing the supernatural to the world. They're opposed by Agents, tall, skinny, sunglass-wearing suited people that seem unusually resilient when faced with supernatural power, are armed with Legend-draining weapons and can detect "Aberrant Entities" via their special sunglasses. The Agents claim to work for the US government. It isn't just the spooks that are opposing them, though. One of the PC's is against it, because of the chaos it might unleash. Several NPCs have expressed doubts about it.

                          What will happen if the supernatural is revealed? It's hard to tell and it depends entirely on the actions of the PCs.

                          At least, that's how I think most Scion games should work.

                                              How the world responds to the revelation that A) The "pagan" religions are actually pretty freakin' relevant beyond revivalists and academia, B) That these gods are having children who are pretty much badasses destined to become mythic themselves and C) Not only are there Gods but really nasty Monsters that wanna eat you -- all of this depends on the type of Chroonicle theme you're trying to run.
