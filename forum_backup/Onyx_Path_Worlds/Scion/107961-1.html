                Originally posted by TwitchTheRat View Post
                She has to either spend a willpower or roll to save that person.There are such a mind blowing number of laws for most places, not even Order 1 is going to keep you very safe.
                Unless there is a law that says she has a duty to help that person that is equal to or greater than the the duty to not jaywalk. Usually affirmative obligations outweigh negative obligations. It's not hard to be the kind of person who has that duty (Good Samiritan laws, having such a relationship that not making the attempt is tortious conduct, being a person of appropriate status - emergency personnel, etc.)

                But you don't need to be a lawyer to make sense of it. A ST can make the determination that a law that gets in the way of the plot is superseded by another law. There is a good defense he or she can pull out of her back pocket anytime necessary in the US - a law can be Constitutionally vague enough that it's enforcement is inherently problematic and subject to too much discretion on the part of the officer making the decision. For example, a law against vagrancy that doesn't define what a "vagrant" is.

                There's also an interpretation of where the Scion is getting the urge to follow the law from. It can't be personnel knowledge. Ignorentia lex non excuse est. So it's more visceral than that. The Storyteller should be able to tell the Scion when she is about to violate the Virtue. That throws it back in the lap of the ultimate decision-maker. In my game, an Order character was trying seduce a mortal (effortlessly) and the natural consequence followed through. She met the same guy again. The first time she was in Cuba. Adultry isn't illegal. A good time was had by all. The second time, she was on a US Military base. The Uniform Code of Military Justice says adultry is illegal. She now had an Order issue.

                For me (and my players who all know I'm a lawyer) this is fun. For most Scion games, it's a judgment call by the ST.

                    Originally posted by TwitchTheRat View Post
                    You were doing good right up to this point. You cannot selectively enforce some laws but not others. While mortal authorities have to prioritize because the do not have the manpower or financial incentive to pursue every law, your Virtue makes no such exceptions.                     Yes, you can. That latitude is actually written into most legal codes. Have your Order character become an officer of the law. Then there are a lot of things he can do as a consequence of his position. As the black-letter law with brightline definitions is the standard you're applying, there are a TON of exceptions to the duty to obey the laws based on exigent circumstances. The big one everyone knows about is self-defense. There are a lot of others. And, as I said before, the character shouldn't have to consult their inner tome of laws that apply to the place they are standing right now, they should be getting gut feeling when they are about to do something wrong (and what the right thing to do would be to a degree). So, if the ST thinks Order shouldn't apply, the character shouldn't get that gut feeling.

                    A lot of what Scions do is exigent or even outside the bounds of the law. If something isn't addressed in the legal codes, then there is no punishment for doing it, even if it feels like there should be. (There is a very ugly case in Wisconsin involving some despicable grave-robbers that illustrates this perfectly).
