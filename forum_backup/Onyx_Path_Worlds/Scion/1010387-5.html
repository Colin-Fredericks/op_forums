    EPISODE 12: SOLD MY SOUL FOR CHRISTMAS (Continued)

    Act Four: Santa Troll Is Coming To Town

    The Scions’ job is not done, though, for they learned that another threat was building. When Jack talked to Moose about the skip day, the jock (and loyal Follower) worried about his grades and not doing what he was supposed to. That isn’t Moose! So Jack activated Sense Tapu to find if Moose was under some supernatural influence. He wasn’t. Well, not exactly. Jack found a Moose-shaped hole in the ambient supernatural substrate of the World. Jack had never sensed that substrate before, because he had never experienced its absence.

    Sense Tapu, however, is only a yes/no detector for any supernatural influence beyond mystic prohibitions. So, Jack calls in Holly. With Unlidded Eye, she can diagnose the void more precisely: Moose has lost his soul. Jack remembers how blasé Moose had been to Vanessa’s breakdown, and mentally kicks himself for not recognizing the plot hook at the time.

    Once the Scions have dealt with the immediate threat of Reverend Stringer, they can look for Moose’s soul. Jack already knows to start looking at the mall. What did Moose do between griping with Jack and ignoring Vanessa? Why, he found a Cuddle Me Eggbert on sale at a seasonal store called Santa’s Little Helpers.

    Cuddle Me Eggbert is the “fad” toy of the season. It’s a foot-high plush egg with googly eyes, a smile and a computer chip and speaker inside. It asks to be hugged, rocked, etc. and rewards these actions with coos and gurgles. There aren’t enough to go around, and parents are clubbing each other like baby seals to get them.

    Alas, the Scions are scattered at the time. Jack phones everyone and tells them to get to the mall PDQ. Jack and Holly get there first; Gus will be delayed by picking up Jaeda at the motel where she and her father stay. So, Jack and Holly head for Santa’s Little Helpers themselves.

    Santa’s Little Helpers is a nexus of concentrated, sugar-coated kitch. It sells holiday-themed candy, tree ornaments, house decorations and toys — and, every day, a few Cuddle Me Eggberts. The employees are all short, elderly men in elf costumes. In the back of the store, kids line up to sit on Santa’s lap, tell him what they want and have their picture taken. Holly, using the Unlidded Eye, can see that the employees are really goblins and Santa is a huge, grotesque troll, hidden by illusions. And one hassled but happy mother is about to buy a Cuddle me Eggbert.

    Goblins are bad news. They are minor creatures in most ways, but they possess one great magical power: the Deal. A goblin can seal a deal with a curse of bad luck on the person who breaks it. What’s more, goblins can take intangible qualities in payment, such as memories, skills, personal beauty, luck… or souls. They are also extremely cunning and persuasive salesmen. The Goblin Market is one of the enduring evils of the supernatural World, and it has come to Hooverdale Mall in the guise of Santa’s Little Helpers.

    The two Scions waste no time. Holly shouts that a toy store at the far end of the mall has Cuddle Me Eggberts for sale at reduced price. That clears out most of the shoppers. Jack moves in and stops the mother from selling her soul for a toy, but draws the attention of the goblins and Santa Troll. They recognize him as a Scion and attack.

    The five goblins use props in the store as weapons. Two of them grab big candy canes and use them as maces. (They hit like maces, too.) Another two throw handfuls of entangling tinsel. The fifth mans a gumball machine and makes it shoot hard candy like a machine gun. Santa Troll, his cover blown, rips out a section of support pillar and tries to swat Jack with it.

    Fortunately, Jack is now extremely hard to hit, what with Untouchable Opponent and Fight With Your Head (used to counter the troll’s high Epic Attributes) and he brought his new katana with which to fight back.

    Unfortunately, the number of opponents means he must turn his back to someone and give a goblin a free shot.

    Fortunately, Jack can pick which one, so he is only struck by candy cane-maces, and the goblins aren’t strong enough to hurt him much.

    Unfortunately, the troll finally realizes that swatting Jack with a heavy object might not be as effective as trying to grapple.

    Fortunately, Holly cursed Santa Troll with the Evil Eye.

    Unfortunately, the goblins know the Evil Eye as well, so they can’t be cursed with that spell, and they can curse right back, reducing Jack’s chance of hitting.

    Goblins are also tough enough that Jack can’t kill one of them with a single blow. Jack realizes that if he doesn’t get backup, the goblins and troll will eventually get lucky or he’ll run out of Legend despite all his stunting: He’s burning through huge amounts of Legend, what with using two Legend-intensive Knacks every action.

    Holly gets Santa Troll off Jack through the simple expedient of using Overt Order to make the troll run away. She herds him to the nearest mall entrance and holds him there through repeated use of Overt Order, but she can’t force the troll to run into the sunlight (which would turn him to stone).

    About the time that Jack is spending his last Legend points, Gus and Jaeda show up. Gus shoulder-blocks the troll out the door of the mall. The troll turns to stone, falls over and breaks open. Since the troll is still dressed as Santa Claus, this traumatizes several onlooking children. At the store, Jaeda knifes a goblin or two.

    When Gus and Holly return, some goblins try to surrender. The most severely wounded goblin runs for the back room, but Jaeda kicks the door open before the goblin can lock it. The door smacks the goblin in the face and flings it against the wall. He, too, surrenders. The goblins all grovel and whine piteously, pleading for mercy — in fact, using the Power Groveling Knack.

    First, the Scions want the souls back. The goblins plead to keep them: Souls are incredibly valuable in the right markets. Can’t they just cut the Scions into the scheme? Jack reminds the goblins that this time, the Deal is: Give the Scions everything they want, swear to leave Avalon and never, ever return to Washington State. Otherwise, the goblins all die on the spot. This is not a negotiation.

    The goblins hand over the souls. These turn out to be white moths trapped inside little glass balls, packed in egg cartons. After suitable prodding, the goblins explain that the way to return someone’s soul is to break the ball near them (20 feet or so) and tell the soul to return to its proper home. As a final piece of good fortune, the goblins kept careful records of whose souls they bought.

    The Scions also think to ask about the Cuddle Me Eggberts. They were goblin-made knock-offs, after all. Even here and now, the goblins can hardly keep from hugging themselves at the ingenuity of their scheme. See, the Cuddle Me Eggberts were magical, too. They will talk to the children, befriend them… and persuade the children to sell their souls as well! And there’s nothing, nothing, more profitable than a child’s soul…

    Jack tells the goblins to get gone, now, before he reconsiders the Deal and kills them anyway. The goblins fade away.

    So, the Scions — well, mostly Jaeda — spend the remaining nights before Christmas breaking into people’s houses, returning souls and stealing Cuddle Me Eggberts, saving at least three dozen children by ruining their Christmas.

                        EPISODE 13: Odds and Ends and New Beginnings

                        Another short episode. Gus’ player couldn’t game, and there were no huge plot points to pursue, so the remaining PCs dealt with various issues in their lives, wrapped up a loose end and learned a little more about the dark forces gathering around Avalon and Etna.

                        Act One: Post-Christmas Blues

                        Winter break is mostly quiet for the Scions.

                        GUS and his family leave to visit his grandfather in Florida — and, Gus hopes, learn more about his uncle Gav and great-uncle Randolph. Stories from his grandfather and other relatives leave Gus even more convinced that Gav and Randolph were both fellow Scions of Sobek.

                        HOLLY takes pity on Jack and lets him break up with her or, more accurately, publicly ends their sham relationship. (He asks to break up; she replies, “Run free, young stallion.”) This leaves Holly free to make Cole her public boyfriend. Though she doesn’t fill her parents in on these details, she does introduce Cole as “a friend” when he comes to visit her. Edgy Biker Guy Cole is on his best behavior to Holly’s parents. Holly’s father, meanwhile, reveals that he enjoyed motorcycles when he was young, asking Holly’s mother with a smile if she remembers how they met. Holly’s mother blushes.

                        Cole’s purpose in visiting, though, was not merely social. He tells Holly about the latest Etna High student to disappear. Supposedly, he and his parents moved out of town suddenly — but Cole thinks there’s something fishy, seeing as how they left all their personal belongings behind. Cole says he’s asking around, trying to find more information, and he’ll let Holly know if he finds any leads.

                        Just before her parents return to New York, Holly’s mother warns her that the bank the family has run for 150 years is under siege. Holly’s father didn’t make foolish investments, but the bank did business with people who did business with people who did, and the dominoes are still falling. The Frosts will never be poor, but they might need to cut back: There’s no guarantee they can continue to rent Holly a house and servants. Holly decides to turn her formidable intellect to the problem of becoming independently wealthy.

                        Holly also ponders how the Moomin family can afford to return to Finland in legal, respectable fashion. The likeliest plan is a small catering or tailoring business, fronting for Moominmamma; Moominpappa is a skilled builder, but he would have trouble working as a carpenter without anyone realizing he is not human.

                        Barbara tells Holly that her band is going after Guibourg. Leo is furious at Guibourg for using Christians and Christianity as cover for his evil scheme. He demanded that Delia use her oracular powers to find more about the warlock principal of Etna High. Leo further let Delia, Barbara and Marshall know that if they did not back him, he would confront Guibourg by himself. Barbara would rather the PCs stayed out of this for now. If they all die, the PCs can avenge them.

                        On the first day back in school, Barbara tells Holly how the confrontation went. There was no fight. Guibourg was ready for them, meeting them in his office. The warlock said that his death would release a powerful demon that would ravage Etna. There would be no possible cover-up. Barbara and Delia think Guibourg was telling the truth, too.

                        “That man has a Blofeld complex like you wouldn’t believe,” Barbara said. “He did everything but stroke a white cat on his lap.” Leo was ready to try killing Guibourg anyway, but Marshall saw reason (for once) and dragged him away. Leo is not taking any of this well.

                        In a possibly unrelated matter, Holly dissects an earthworm in biology class. The intense young teacher, Mr. Clive, discourses a bit on the biology of annelids and notes that some close-minded fools in the scientific establishment still deny the existence of the giant Palouse earthworm despite more than a century of sightings by reputable witnesses. When his own researches are complete, though, he will silence the mocking tongues of the Academy.

                        JACK has a quiet vacation from school, though he continues working at Stick It. On the first day back at school, Dr. Demerit has a quizzybubble to see how well his students remember the subjects he lectured upon in the last week before Christmas. The quiz consists of 10 math problems for 5 points each, and one question for 50 points: “What is my favorite color?” Clearly, the man is taking petty revenge for losing a third of his class to the Scions’ “skip day.”

                        Holly realizes that she cannot possibly suss the math teacher’s favorite color from his clothes or other clues; he likely did not even tell the class the truth. She pulls out her mirror. Demerit instantly notices and tells her she does not need to primp in his class. Holly catches Jack’s eye. He gets the hint and begs Demerit to let him go to the bathroom. Demerit responds by pulling a bedpan from his desk and tossing it to Jack. Jack says that well, okay, but it’s Number Two… Demerit rolls his eyes and tells Jack to go. This byplay sufficiently distracts Demerit that Holly can perform a quick Mystery session. After asking what Demerit had said his favorite color was (“Aquamarine”), she asks some quick questions about Guibourg and Cailleach Sarsen. The most disturbing answer comes when she asks who Sarsen plans to attack next: It’s her.

                        Jack, meanwhile, is lucky enough to find one of Demerit’s students from an earlier class. To Jack’s chagrin, that class received a question about Demerit’s favorite piece of music (the Ricercare from Bach’s Musical Offering, if you must know). Jack grinds his teeth at being outwitted by the wily math teacher. Hoping to get a little of his own back, he writes the answer to the other class’ question. The next day, his test paper comes back with a notation of, “Not clever enough, Mr. Thompson.” But Holly gets 100% and a notation of “Impressive.”

                        JAEDA has a miserable holiday. Worst of all, losing the trailer seems to have broken her father’s spirit. They need work, but her father seems unable even to try anymore. Jaeda finds herself forced to take charge and look for jobs for both of them. She eventually chivvies her father into taking a job as a waiter at a Mexican restaurant. Jaeda herself takes the quickest, easiest, most immediately profitable job she can think of: bikini barista. Given her stunning beauty, she gets a lot of tips. Between the two of them, they manage enough to take a room at a Motel 6.

                        The only bright spots come from Gary. As part of his Solstice present for her, he takes her shopping to get some nice clothes instead of the police and Salvation Army donations.

                            EPISODE 13: Odds and Ends and New Beginnings (Continued)

                            Act Two: The Lion Snarls

                            While heading for the cafeteria, Jack hears a boy ask loudly, “Hey, Bible Boy, how’d your big rally go?” followed by laughter. Then a group of boys start singing, “E-vil, In league with Sa-tan,” followed by more laughter — abruptly ending in a squawk. Jack hurries to the scene and finds Leo holding another boy off the ground, in one hand. Leo is glowing as he says, in quiet fury, “God is not mocked.” The other boys are cowering. Gawking students fall silent. Jack realizes that Leo is on the edge of doing something very foolish that will expose him as a Scion beyond any hope of cover-up.

                            Jack defuses the situation the best way he knows how: He gets Leo to punch him instead of the hapless mortal boy.

                            Holly arrives a few seconds later and uses Center of Attention to distract the other students. Leo drops the other boy, hunches in on himself, and turns away. Holly and Jack run a quick scam on the kids, suggesting the whole thing was a trick that Jack arranged (planting a glow-stick on Leo, talking the boys into making fun of him, etc.) To cement the hoax, Holly tells the boy Leo lifted off the ground to admit that he was in on the plan. Compelled by Overt Order, the boy says just that. The crowd breaks up, muttering with disgust at how Jack has “played” Leo.

                            Jaeda arrives last to the scene with Leo. She follows him as he leaves and has yet another talk with him in an empty classroom. Possibly tired of coddling Leo, she bluntly tells him that he likes hating himself. A little probing about Leo’s home life leads to the revelation that he was adopted by the Lefebvres (and he has no idea of his birth-mother’s identity). More importantly, Jaeda is not surprised to learn that the Lefebvres raised Leo with strict discipline. In fact, she suspects most people would call it child abuse. Leo insists he deserved it all, and everything the Lefebvres did was out of love and concern for his soul. Jaeda ends the conversation no wiser about how to untwist Leo’s self-loathing, but at least she tells him that he doesn’t have to be alone in opposing the absolute, definite, no doubt about it evil of Guibourg and the Titans. Leo says he will fight them — even if it kills him.

                            Somewhat later, Jack has his own talk with Leo. Leo admits that he feels not just defiled by his parentage, but utterly useless. The Scions say he should try to use his Scion powers for good, but he can’t even fight Guibourg. Jack responds that none of them can fight Guibourg — yet — but they can fight lesser evils and do their best to be good people. Leo has to admit that while he cannot consider Jack a virtuous person, he has never seen Jack hurt another person on purpose. It’s at least a start.

                            Afterward, Jaeda thinks Jack may have been more successful than she was in getting through to the self-tormenting Scion of Nergal. (Maybe it’s a guy thing.) Holly, for her part, despairs of ever helping Leo. She believes he will explode soon, and all the Scions can do is try to contain the damage.

                            All this time, the Scions ponder how to go after Cailleach Sarsen. They feel pretty sure Guibourg is advising her, and that he likely has Prophecy with which to learn their plans. How do you fight someone who knows whatever you finally end up deciding to do?

                            Act Three: Bad Traffic

                            After school, Holly gets in her Lotus to drive home. Fortunately, she has keen eyes and sees the car approaching on a ballistic arc, silhouetted against the clouds. It barely misses her. It’s the attack by Cailleach Sarsen. Holly peels out and calls Jack and Jaeda to let them know the conflict has heated up again: The stone giant isn’t even trying to hide her attacks from other people.

                            Jack drafts the nearest one of his football buddy Followers, Gnu, to run the Stick It in his absence. Holly picks him up at the mall. Then they head for the espresso stand where Jaeda works. A quarter-mile from the stand, another car sails toward them. Holly slams on the brakes and bails; Jack grabs the wheel from across the front seat, steps on the gas, and steers the car away from the automotive missile. Hurled sections of Jersey barrier follow in quick succession.

                            Holly runs the rest of the way to the espresso stand to find Jaeda. She hits the motorists with an Epic Social-backed warning that the Crips are on a rampage and coming down the freeway, getting the mortals (she hopes) to drive away from the fight. Jaeda then joins her in running back to the confrontation. Jaeda does not like running through a very cold January night wearing nothing but a micro-bikini, but two dots of Epic Stamina mean she can endure it for the moment.

                            Meanwhile, Jack looks for Mama Sarsen. He finds her. He first tries a bit of automotive jousting, driving past her at high speed while slashing with his sword. It doesn’t even nick her. (Stone giants are tough.) Then he rams her with the Lotus. That manages to at least rattle her a bit. (Four levels of bashing damage.) The car, however, is crushed, Jack took damage too, and he failed to knock the giantess back. She responded by smashing down with the section of Jersey barrier in her hands, knocking Jack for a loop. Holly and Scion reach the fight just as Mama Sarsen smacks Jack again, knocking him out and breaking bones. (The bashing damage wrapped around to lethal.)

                            Holly concentrates on getting the unconscious Jack away from the wreck of her car. Jaeda uses the remaining concrete barrier and wrecked car as springboards to run up the giantess’ back and swing around to stab her in the eye with the First Flower. And… something unexpected happens. Jaeda made a necklace from the shuriken left when Dark Master Chiba died; she wears it now. She feels Legend flow as she attacks the giantess who destroyed her home, and she wishes that she had a sword instead of a dagger. To her surprise and delight, the First Flower deals more damage at once than Jaeda ever saw before. Cailleach Sarsen falls backward, unconscious.

                            Holly, using The Unlidded Eye, sees that Dark Master Chiba’s shuriken is more potent than the usual Shinobi trophy: It is also a Relic for channeling War Boons. Jaeda has just done this, spontaneously learning and using a new Boon that the Scions dub My Knife Is A Sword. Such investigations, though, come after Jaeda carves up Cailleach Sarsen to kill her and extract her heart and its freight of eitr. First things first! Mama Sarsen’s body quickly crumbles into a heap of clay and pebbles.

                            The three Scions decide they should leave quickly before the accidents caused by hurled cars and Jersey barriers brings the authorities. Jaeda hotwires an abandoned car, and they zip away. First stop: Holly’s house, so Jaeda can get something besides a microbikini to wear.

                            Some Slight Addenda: Jaeda calls Gary to tell him about the fight. She figures it’s best he hears about it from the Scions themselves. To her relief, Gary isn’t mad: Cailleach Sarsen attacked first, it was a fair fight, and what the hell was the giantess doing, showing her powers so blatantly in public? And Gary is disappointed that he didn’t get to see Jaeda fight in a microbikini.

                            Jack calls Gnu to check on the Stick It. Gnu says he had no idea running a Stick It could be so challenging. Aside from the usual difficult customers, Gary stopped by to do his usual hassling of Jack and saw no reason to let up because Jack wasn’t there. There was also a creepy old lady who, upset for some reason, croaked out a curse on the Stick It.

                            The gang drives out to the mall (it’s a good place to ditch the stolen car anyway). Holly examines the Stick It and finds no curse on it — just the mall’s new Fatebinding to Jack from all the Legend he spent fighting Santa Troll. On the way out, though, with Unlidded Eye still running, she sees that magic still pervades the retail space that held Santa’s Little Helper. A school and office supply store has moved into the space and is conducting its grand opening sale, but the place still carries some sort of Psychopomp connection leading beyond the mortal World.

                            Holly and Jaeda investigate. The store’s contents seem ordinary enough. As they look around, though, the pimply-faced teen at the cash register asks the manager, “Sir, What does this light mean?” The manager looks at the register, looks around, spots the two Scions and approaches them, thanking them for coming to the sale and assuring them the store can serve all their school supply needs, however unusual. Holly sees the man is a lesser immortal: specifically, an akh or one of the justified dead of Duat, the Pesedjet Underworld.

                            The manager leads them through a door to the back room where he keeps the special products for special customers: reams of authentic papyrus, ink and pens made from Nile water and reeds, laptop computers and PDAs in hieroglyphics, cuneiform, Hebrew, Ancient Greek, and other languages, and similar items. A cardboard cutout of Thoth waves cheerily. “Welcome to Scribal Depot!” the manager gushes.

                            The only item the Scions find that is actually magical is a box of Blue Books of Thoth, enchanted to prevent cheating. It is addressed to Avalon High. Holly buys some papyrus and a laptop with a Hebrew keyboard, just because. It all seems harmless, but it undeniably shows that the Avalon region is getting weirder.

More precisely, it’s a sub-basement decorated in Hammer Films Transylvania Classic, with a coffin in the center of the room, a crematory urn in the coffin and a winch over the coffin for hanging sacrifices. There’s also ornate plasterwork and red and black drapes.

                                                        Not being stupid, the Scions suspect traps. Jaeda searches the Fane (she’s the one with Larceny) and finds hidden wires leading from the coffin to two of the plaster panels. Jack knocks away some of the plaster, and Jaeda chips away the rest to reveal the claymore mines set to go off if the urn is disturbed. This would be quite sufficient to blow Scion blood throughout the room and into the vampire ash from the shattered urn.

                                                        Well, the Scions can’t leave high explosives lying around; someone could get hurt. They retreat from the room, and Jack throws something to knock over the urn and trigger the mines, sans Scion blood. After that, they figure they’d better leave quickly before the noise attracts the police. First, though, they collect the scattered ash with a Dustbuster. They make a quick trip to scatter the ash into the nearest running water, never to be retrieved.

                                                        Back at the mall, the three mortal boys are glad to hand the minions back to the Scions. Warren and Bull leave. Cole sticks around while the Scions discuss what to do with the minions… because that is a problem. The three men were ready to kill to revive their vampire master. The Scions suspect they may have killed before, hoping that mortal blood would be enough.

                                                        Holly asks her mysterious Guide if enthralled vampire minions can be deprogrammed. Her Guide says it’s unlikely, given that the three stayed loyal for almost 30 years. In fact, the Guide guesses that only their enthrallment enabled the men to preserve a façade of sanity all this time, and losing all hope of restoring the Master will break their minds completely.

                                                        Holly then asks if there isn’t something someone could do for the men. The Guide says that a broken mind is hard enough, but the vampire broke the minions’ souls as well. It’s the only way they could have stayed loyal so long. Even Gods would have difficulty fixing that sort of damage. At best, the men would have to be locked up for the rest of their lives, and their broken souls would make them sock puppets for other forces of evil.

                                                        “This sucks,” Holly says. Her Guide agrees. That’s why such evil must be stopped. Because some damage can never be fixed.

                                                        Sure enough: When confronted with proof that the Scions thwarted their plot and the Master isn’t coming back, the three men go catatonic. The Scions argue a bit about what to do with them. The only choices they see are to deliver them to a mental hospital or kill them, and death not only looks more expedient as a way to prevent other Titan servants from using the men, it might even be a mercy. Cole looks sick. He asks Holly if she’d think less of him if he left now and pretended that at the last moment the Scions thought of some brilliant way to make it come out all right. Holly says she would have thought less of him if he’d insisted on staying. Cole leaves.

                                                        The Scions load the comatose minions into the van the minions had intended to use to transport Warren, and drive to a remote location. Holly says she won’t have other people doing her dirty work. She takes a knife and thrusts it into a minion’s heart. Jaeda and Gus kill the other two. Gus summons his crocodile to dispose of the bodies.

                                                        All in all, the Scions found it one of the worst days of their lives. Holly seems especially bummed. Gus reminds her that they are merely at the beginning of their power. Someday, Holly will be a God who will work miracles that astound even other Gods. Perhaps he doesn’t realize that Holly is less broken up over the tragedy than over her inability to think of a solution.

                                                        Daughter of Louhi, after all.

                                                            EPISODE 14: Bad Options (Continued)

                                                            Act Three: Sex and Violence

                                                            As President’s Week break approaches, the Scions make plans for their week off from school. On a car-hunting trip with Gary, Jaeda learns that the Vettersons will be out of town. They spend the week on a skiing vacation in the mountains, enjoying the snow. Gary wishes Jaeda could come along, but with his father hating Scions… Of course, it turns out to be the same ski lodge where Mr. Sobeck is getting married.

                                                            The rival Band, it turns out, is also leaving town. Some of them are willing to talk to Jack, because everybody knows Jack is just a big goofball. The rival Scions are also going to the lodge on a… hunting trip. Jack manages to wheedle his way into the expedition, but he lets the others know about the rival Band’s expedition.

                                                            The PCs immediately figure out that their rivals probably intend to try killing the Vettersons. Frankly, they don’t think their rivals can take the giants. After all, Gary and Reese practically defeated Gus when they were all Legend 2, and the Scions know that Suttung and Hyndla are even more formidable. They also know that if any of the Vettersons are attacked, or worse killed, it’ll be war between the rest of the family and all the Scions. Suttung and Hyndla have only held back this long because they didn’t want to throw away their comfortable lives.

                                                            Mister Sobeck told Gus he could bring a friend along to keep him company at the lodge. Gus invites Jaeda. (The elder Sobeck smirks and says she’s a good choice.) Holly invites Cole to join her for a ski trip as well, though she reserves rooms at a neighboring ski lodge instead of the one to which the Sobecks and Vettersons are going.

                                                            Once at the lodge, Jack tries to talk Barbara out of attacking the Vettersons. He doesn’t think that his Band could win a fight with the Vettersons without losses of their own — maybe at all — and the rival Band is not as skilled at combat and magic as they are. The giants are tough. Gary has shown he can survive a shot to the head, and he’s probably not the only one of them!

                                                            Barbara thinks they can do it. She has no intention of attacking the Vettersons as a group. Delia has foreseen times in quick succession when three of the four Vettersons will be alone. They will ambush each giant. Marshall will start by shooting each giant in the head. Before the giant can recover, Leo will engage to keep the giant busy. Barbara will fly down and attack with thermite charges prepared by Marshall’s gun-shop mentor. Marshall and Barbara both think the plan has a good chance to work. And if they can eradicate a whole family of dangerous titanspawn, woohoo! The power of their Legend will grow!

                                                            Jack retorts that Gary and Reese are changing in ways Holly can detect with her magic. The two young giants are no longer pure titanspawn. If attacked, though, they will stand by their parents and whatever change they are in the middle of will be ended.

                                                            Barbara wavers. She says she has to think about it and talk to the other Scions. Marshall, she admits, is the one who’s most set on killing the Vettersons. The others simply want to do something, well, legendary. But Jack points out that he and his friends have not gone hunting for enemies to kill. (Well, Dr. Sosa was an iffy case.) They’ve simply allowed monsters and villains to come to them, or show that they were clear and present threats to human life, and then fought them.

                                                            Barbara also lets drop that she knows all about Jaeda and Gary. Weeks ago, Delia recognized that Jaeda was going steady with someone. From there, it wasn’t hard for the daughter of Apollo to figure out who it was. It is possible that Marshall has issues with losing the Atzlánti girl of his dreams to a giant.

                                                            Meanwhile, Jaeda has managed to find Gary without being seen by the other Vettersons. It’s not hard: The lodge is a flurry of activity with all the caterers, decorators and wedding guests running around, preparing for the wedding the next day. Suttung Vetterson, of course, is none too happy to find the Sobecks at the lodge, but he does nothing but glower.

                                                            They have a nice reunion. When Gus looks for Jaeda, he finds her in a back parlor with Gary. Actually, he hears Gary moaning, “Oh, Jaeda, there’s only so much a man can stand…” followed shortly by “This website is fantastic. A Shelby-customized Ford Torino Super Snake, it’s gorgeous!” Which gives him the confidence to look in on them.

                                                            Jaeda sits on Gary’s lap in a big overstuffed chair. They have a laptop that displays a picture of a muscle car. They also seem very at ease with each other. As soon as Gary sees Gus enter, his posture shifts and he grins a look-at-me-with-your-date smirk, but Gus saw there’s real affection between them, from Gary as well as Jaeda. It isn’t just sex. Privately, later, Jaeda says she’s sorry she kept it secret she was still dating Gary, but she was afraid he and the other Scions would be angry. Gus says he respects Jaeda’s right to choose her boyfriend, he respects Gary, and is neither jealous nor angry. He can’t imagine the others will mind, either, or feel it’s any of their business. But he does tell her about the rival Band’s plot to kill the Vettersons.

                                                            Holly and Cole come over from their lodge to confer with the Band. Holly uses Mystery to find when and where their rivals plan to ambush the Vettersons. (Since the rivals already made these plans, it’s valid information for Mystery.) Their rivals plan to attack on the next day — the day of the wedding — in the span of less than two hours.

                                                            Once again, they see no good way out. Jaeda is adamant: She isn’t betraying Gary. The Vettersons are all safe from her until one of them attacks her. The others aren’t too keen on cold, premeditated murder of someone who never attacked them or any innocent mortals as far as they know. On the other hand, they agree that tipping off the Vettersons to the attacks will push Suttung to murderous counterattack and spark the conflict they want to avoid. They aren’t too keen on fighting the rival Band, either. The discussion breaks up without resolution.

                                                            At least, without resolution from three of the Scions. Gus goes looking for Marshall and finds him about to head out on skis. Gus knows it’s the Scion of Huitzilopochtli’s first time on skis, and he offers to show Marshall some of the best views and slopes. He takes Marshall past one of the ambush sites, and spots how Marshall examines the terrain for places to hide and shoot.

                                                            Gus then casually mentions the most dangerous slope in the area, that only expert skiers may dare. It would be risky even for a Scion super-athlete if he wasn’t experienced. Gus has skied it, of course… Sure enough, Marshall takes the challenge and demands to ski Suicide Slope right away. They both race down the slope. Marshall would have easily taken a Winter Olympics medal with his skiing (5 successes on the Dexterity + Athletics roll). Gus far surpasses him (10 successes — super-hot roll). Marshall is so elated by the rush of speed and adrenaline that he doesn’t even feel jealous.

                                                            Well, that won’t do. Gus goads him, saying his skiing wasn’t bad for a newbie, admittedly he’ll never be as good as Gus. And trying Suicide Slope took more courage than Gus expected from a sniper who tries to shoot people in parking lots instead of facing them in honest, open combat. And so on. Marshall snarls, “I shot the wrong one!” — incidentally confirming Gus’ suspicion that Marshall tried to kill Gary. Before long, Gus gets the reaction he wants: Marshall pulls a knife and attacks him.

                                                            Marshall knows knife-fighting, but he’s a novice on skis and he doesn’t have Cat’s Grace to let him fight in thigh-deep snow without impediment. Gus quickly grapples Marshall, and Gus is much stronger. He grips Marshall’s arm and move it so the knife cuts a bloody line across Gus’ face. Then he beats Marshall unconscious and beyond, breaking bones. Marshall gets a few punches of his own in, but the Scion of Huitzilopochtli never has a chance of winning.

                                                            Gus then carries Marshall to the nearest aid station and gives the paramedics a story about how they tried Suicide Slope and it nearly killed them. Scion Mind Tricks ensure the paramedics believe the lie. Gus endures treatment for the cut on his face and a few bruises, then heads back to the lodge to tell the Scions that the problem is solved. Barbara, Delia and Leo can’t spring their ambushes without Marshall; Gus counts on them to see reason.

                                                            Barbara returns to the room where Jack waited for her once she talked to the rest of her Band. Bitterly, she tells him, “Well played.” Jack realizes that Barbara now believes his earnest attempt at persuasion was a ploy, and his sincerity was deceit that she failed to penetrate. Jack isn’t sure what makes him feel worse: that Barbara thinks he’s a lying bastard, or that she thinks he’s actually a brilliant planner.

                                                            Gus apologizes to Jack and the rest for taking action without consulting them, but he didn’t want to force them — especially Jack — to choose or reject a course of action that even Gus admits was pretty slimy. But, well, Scion of Sobek, the leg-breaker of the Pesedjet who gets his hands dirty so others don’t have to. There’s no chance now of bringing the two Bands together, though, and Marshall will undoubtedly set out to kill Gus.

                                                            The rival Scions leave early the next day. Barbara tells Jack that no doubt he will find alternate transportation home.

                                                            The wedding happens on schedule. The Scions spot Hyndla watching from a doorway. She may be a giant, but she still likes a wedding. Reese shows up long enough to dance with Gus at the reception. Gary and Jaeda furtively text back and forth about when they can get away.

                                                            That’s when the woman on snowshoes comes in screaming that animals attacked her husband and dragged him into the snow.

                                                            Act Four: Finally, A Nice Simple Monster

                                                            The Scions easily slip away in the uproar and put on their skis. Using Predatory Focus, they follow the woman’s trail back to the attack site. There’s a lot of blood melting a pit in the snow, with scraps of clothing and snowshoes. The largest remaining part of the victim is a foot still inside a boot. It seems that just as the woman said, a number of somethings jumped out of the snow and dragged the man down.

                                                            The Scions follow the trail. It leads them to a dell full of deep snow. As they enter, four bizarre beasts leap from the snow and attack, one beast per Scion. The white-furred beasts have heads like weasels or wolverines, but much larger. They have no legs, though. Instead, they swim through the snow like fish. Fortunately, although the beasts take the Scions by surprise and bite them, their jaws are not quite powerful enough to bite through Holly’s kevlar coat or the Epic Stamina of the others. (And the damage rolls sucked.) The Scions strike back when they get the chance, but the beasts’ strategy is to dive back into the snow as soon as they get a chance, to leap out again from somewhere else in hopes of getting another surprise attack. Once the Scions are prepared, however, they can dish out a lot more damage than the beasts can. In less than 20 seconds, the four beasts are all dead.

                                                            A bit to her own surprise, Holly has heard of these chimeric beasts. (Thank you Know-It-All.) They are snow wassets, whose only published description comes from a slim volume called Fearsome Creatures of the Lumberwoods, published in 1910. (You can find the whole book online.) As for what brought them out here and now, well, most likely the Fate-warping presence of eight Scions and four giants!

                                                            As she examines the bodies with Unlidded Eye, Holly sees the fading power of the chimeric beasts concentrating in their pelts and their teeth. She figures out that the Scions have a choice of trophies: Take the pelts and turn them into garments that grant immunity to extreme cold; or take a tooth as a Relic that would enable its owner to channel powers of ice and cold (i.e., the Frost Purview) — but not both. They take three pelts to turn into fur jackets, anticipating that they will be useful when Suttung eventually moves against them, and one tooth in case someone ever wants to learn Frost Boons.

                                                            The rest of the week is uneventful. Nevertheless, the Scions can’t fully enjoy their week in the mountains as they keep watching for Suttung Vetterson to drop an avalanche on them, or for more snow wassets to attack the people around them. All in all, they’re fairly glad to go home at the end of the week and prepare for school.
