    I have been summoned. Right, what's goin' on? Oh, The Morrígan? Right.

    I think Liminal, Lover, and Sage perfectly encompass The Morrígan in a merged form.
    • Liminal is obviously there due to her function as a spooky, something-to-do-with-the-dead-but-we-don't-actually-know-what, and mysterious function. The Scene in the Boyhood Deeds where Badb helps CúChulainn kill a spectre tie into this (since this form of The Morrígan is a merged entity as I will get to in a moment).
    • Sage I think is also very important due to The Morrígan's function as a knowledge source, helping the Tuatha De Danann during Cath Maige Tuired with information, as well as her stated skills with magic as she levies various sorts of odd curses upon the Fomorians during Cath Maige Tuired. Furthermore, while Sage lacks support for Prophetic Powers at Hero, that doesn't mean it lacks it at Demigod, or God. I would argue that Sage likely is intended to be used for Prophetic functions based on other people who have Sage. Apollo, for instance, is a totally useless adviser, but does hold an Oracular function represented by his Sage Calling. Dionysus also has Sage, and he's not very good at advice outside of his function as a Mystery Cult Patron, and thus we see Sage being tied back into this notion of spontaneous divine knowledge. Amaterasu has Sage due to the Oracular contest with her brother Susano-Wo, Hachiman has a function as a foretell victory with... doves IIRC, it's been a while since the Kami revision and I'm up to my eyes in Teutates currently. For the Tuatha specifically, The Dagda also has been given Sage with obviously is tying into his function as a source of Otherworldly Knowledge, so, yeah. I think it is safe to assume that while Sage may currently not support Oracular functions at Hero, probably does at higher Legends. So, I would say Sage is really solid for her.
      • Subcomment: If you have read Cath Maige Tuired and don't remember The Morrígan giving two really big prophecies, that's because they are something called rosc passages and are the most horrific, hellish thing in the world to translate. Cath Maige Tuired has three IIRC, two of her prophecies, and then there is one describing the fight between Balor and Lug. They are cryptic to the point of no successful well thought of translation from the original manuscript text existing. While we don't know the exact words, we do know that The Morrígan is giving a prophecy of some sort in these passages, and they appear to be incredibly dire ones.
    • Lover: So, Lover is really important for The Morrígan in this merged state since she has merged with Macha as evidenced by the presence of Beasts (Horses) and Epic Dexterity. The story of Macha and her mortal husband is a perfect story of a Lover, she shows up one day, takes over his household, decides she's married to him. Very Irish conceptualization of this sort of thing. In addition to Macha, I would argue that The Morrígan herself (the singular entity named The Morrígan, not the group entity) has the entire scene with CúChulainn which is seemingly intentionally connected with the same sort of interaction we see between himself, Lí Ban, and Fand in The Sickbed of CúChulainn. She is spurned by CúChulainn since her request is that he abandon his post and run off with her, also because he's sort of busy when she accosts him, but it is a very Lover feeling scene. Then you have the whole thing with The Dagda on top of that. So, Lover I'd consider almost foundational to the character.
    • Hunter: The Morrígan doesn't really... do anything like this. She doesn't spend time in the wilderness (the Tuatha are actually way more into clearcutting forest and leveling natural wilderness for use as pastoral lands than people give them credit for) and doesn't track anyone down at any point. I know in popular culture there is an idea of her being a Psychopomp, or a Death-Source, but it sadly unfounded in the Saga literature. So, Hunter doesn't actually have any basis in myth.
    • Warrior: Also another pop-culture misunderstanding. The Morrígan doesn't really fight, that's not her thing. She takes part in... I think one fight, but that does not make her a warrior like Lug, Ogma, etc. She is involved during warfare against the Fomorians, but mostly through strange magical functions such as the theft of the... what was it, courage of the liver? Something odd. The Morrígan herself is not a divine fighter, she is not there in the thick of it clashing with others, she is using strange magic, and influencing combat, but not partaking directly in any degree that would justify putting her on the same level as individuals who take more of a major role in fighting. She's got a chariot, but everyone in the Tuatha has a chariot, status thing. She helps in battle, but that's because literally all of the Tuatha help in battles. Cultural thing.
    So yeah, I'd say The Morrígan as presented is a solid entry. If she was broken up into her component parts she might have some different Callings perhaps? I can see Macha with Sage (Curse of Macha), Lover (explained above), and Creator (Mother function) for instance. Ultimately however, the rules are guidelines in any game. I don't think I would give The Morrígan Hunter or Warrior in any of my games since I don't think there's any proof of such a function in the Saga texts, but if other people want to do so, that's totally reasonable. Do whatever makes the game fun for you.

    About the general absence of Hunter with the Tuatha I can explain though. So, your legal and social status in early medieval / older Ireland was dependent on your tribe, and being inside your tribal lands. If, for instance, a Smith from Ulster was in Munster, they would have no legal status, and no social status. Forests, and other wilderness areas are therefore beyond the law, beyond status, and that is fundamentally important for the Irish at the time. Being a hunter, living off hunted game, would be one of the lowest status social ranks, below even someone who is a herder. You would have almost no social standing, respect, power, or legal status. This is seen with the Fianna, bands of young warrior nobles who lived in the woods to more-or-less work off the testosterone in a way that wouldn't damage the tribe, sort of. These Fianna bands were half-outlaw, half-helpful, raiding both their own communities, and outsider ones. They are hunters, they are people in the wilderness, and until they return home and become actual members of society, they held a very nebulious position in the social system. The only people who had any 'mobile' social status were the Fili, or the classes that spawned off of them as they seemingly broke up or decayed into separate groups, such as the bards. Everyone else needed to stay within their tribe, or lose everything that mattered.

    So, that's why Hunter is probably not represented very well in the Pantheon. There are certainly Irish characters who would have Hunter, Finn being the big one, but none of the Tuatha themselves short of nameless ones in the background since their numbers outclass the stars in the sky, waves in the sea, grains of sand, etc etc.

                                                I do, however, have very unorthodox basis for the Hunter Calling to offer up to you.

                                                Midir (Pronounced: Mithir)
                                                Proud, devious, and cruel at times, Midir the king of Síd Brí Léith, is one of the more confusing members of the Tribes of the Goddess Danu. He flits in and out of the stories, sometimes replacing a better known figure for a single retelling, only to vanish the next time. He has been The Dagda's father, his brother, and son, as well as the father of Macha, Ailbe, and Dorind at times. He is a wealthy man, happy to use his great wealth to acomplish whatever whim he has at the time, and while he has been a kind, benevolent foster-father to Aengus Og, he can fall to a furious black rage when wronged one too many times, bringing dread with his arrival at Tara when Eochaid had married a reincarnation of his love Étaín as he arrived in his full might and anger as Eochaid had the audacity to split open Síd mounds in a search for Étaín when she had been stolen away. He also had his primary wife, Fúammnach, killed by Aengus Og when she was seeking to kill Étaín. His love for Étaín defines him, though this love is tainted to a degree, exercising a concerning amount of control over her, even in the context of the time. However, he seems to have calmed down over time to the point that Étaín herself begins taking mortal lovers such as Eochaid Feidlech. (a different one than the Eochaid Midir clashed with)

                                                He once owned the cauldron of The Dagda, though it was stolen away from him by CúChulainn, and once owned three cranes of stinginess, though they were stolen by the mortal satirist Athairne Ailgesach. He provided a magical horse to Eochu mac Maireda and Ríb at one point, though the creature was rather... cunning in of itself, urinating to much in a great thundering stream that it created Lough Ree. Midir has died, like most of the people of the Goddess Danu, killed by Elcmar, who was then killed by Aengus Og. However, death hasn't stopped the Pantheon from meddling long after their deaths.
                                                • Callings
                                                  • Hunter: His search for Étaín takes Midir thousands of years, and despite that he never gives up his search. He is capable of recognizing Étaín when she has been transformed into a giant fly by Midir's primary wife (the Irish practiced polygamy with a 'primary wife' system) Fúammnach. Is willing to go to great lengths, pay massive prices, and cause others to commit colossal socitial violations to get his prize.
                                                  • Lover: Midir's largest myth, Tochmarc Étaíne (which you can read here!), is the story of his adoration for the woman Étaín, chasing her through a thousand years, and different reincarnations, loving her even when she was a giant (albeit very useful) fly. Willing to do horrible things for love.
                                                  • Trickster: Midir accomplishes his goals through manipulating, tricking, and decieving others. In his final gambit to win Étaín from her most recent husband Eochaid, he not only uses a word ploy to steal Étaín from him, but then in an act of furious vengeance, tricks Eochaid into having a child with his own daughter with Étaín after tricking him into thinking the daughter was Étaín. (This is a very big deal in Ireland, they don't like incest at all)
                                                • Purviews
                                                  • Deception: Magically tricks Ailill into falling into a Wasting Sickness for Étaín so he can disguise himself as him to sleep with her, may have been the source of Aengus Og's trick to take the Brug from Elcmar (probably Nuadu). His final lie to Eochaid is really rather complex, and results in Eochaid committing the large taboo of incest.
                                                  • Order: Midir is the Ríg (chief / king) of Brí Léith (modernly known as Ardagh Hill, Co. Cork. You can see it here! He rules the inside of it, the Otherworldly Síd mound) and is very concerned about the time he loses an eye which would remove him from kingship. However, Dian Cecht heals it, and he retains his authority. Commands his people to reshape Ireland itself as part of his payment to Eochaid to win Étaín from him. Further, his names has an entemological link with the Old Irish Midithir, the verb 'to Judge'.
                                                  • Prosperity: Midir is stupid rich, even for one of the Tuatha. The third section of Tochmarc Étaíne is him waving around his massive wealth to intentionally lose gambles with Eochaid until he lures him into a false sense of superiority to keep betting until he can raise the stakes high enough to get Étaín.

However, he seems to have calmed down over time to the point that Étaín herself begins taking mortal lovers such as Eochaid Feidlech. (a different one than the Eochaid Midir clashed with)

                                                    He once owned the cauldron of The Dagda, though it was stolen away from him by CúChulainn, and once owned three cranes of stinginess, though they were stolen by the mortal satirist Athairne Ailgesach. He provided a magical horse to Eochu mac Maireda and Ríb at one point, though the creature was rather... cunning in of itself, urinating to much in a great thundering stream that it created Lough Ree. Midir has died, like most of the people of the Goddess Danu, killed by Elcmar, who was then killed by Aengus Og. However, death hasn't stopped the Pantheon from meddling long after their deaths.
                                                    • Callings
                                                      • Hunter: His search for Étaín takes Midir thousands of years, and despite that he never gives up his search. He is capable of recognizing Étaín when she has been transformed into a giant fly by Midir's primary wife (the Irish practiced polygamy with a 'primary wife' system) Fúammnach. Is willing to go to great lengths, pay massive prices, and cause others to commit colossal socitial violations to get his prize.
                                                      • Lover: Midir's largest myth, Tochmarc Étaíne (which you can read here!), is the story of his adoration for the woman Étaín, chasing her through a thousand years, and different reincarnations, loving her even when she was a giant (albeit very useful) fly. Willing to do horrible things for love.
                                                      • Trickster: Midir accomplishes his goals through manipulating, tricking, and decieving others. In his final gambit to win Étaín from her most recent husband Eochaid, he not only uses a word ploy to steal Étaín from him, but then in an act of furious vengeance, tricks Eochaid into having a child with his own daughter with Étaín after tricking him into thinking the daughter was Étaín. (This is a very big deal in Ireland, they don't like incest at all)
                                                    • Purviews
                                                      • Deception: Magically tricks Ailill into falling into a Wasting Sickness for Étaín so he can disguise himself as him to sleep with her, may have been the source of Aengus Og's trick to take the Brug from Elcmar (probably Nuadu). His final lie to Eochaid is really rather complex, and results in Eochaid committing the large taboo of incest.
                                                      • Order: Midir is the Ríg (chief / king) of Brí Léith (modernly known as Ardagh Hill, Co. Cork. You can see it here! He rules the inside of it, the Otherworldly Síd mound) and is very concerned about the time he loses an eye which would remove him from kingship. However, Dian Cecht heals it, and he retains his authority. Commands his people to reshape Ireland itself as part of his payment to Eochaid to win Étaín from him. Further, his names has an entemological link with the Old Irish Midithir, the verb 'to Judge'.
                                                      • Prosperity: Midir is stupid rich, even for one of the Tuatha. The third section of Tochmarc Étaíne is him waving around his massive wealth to intentionally lose gambles with Eochaid until he lures him into a false sense of superiority to keep betting until he can raise the stakes high enough to get Étaín.
