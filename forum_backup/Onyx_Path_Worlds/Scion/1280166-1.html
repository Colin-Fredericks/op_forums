                                        Also, there is the whole problem of false equivalencies. Firstly, the "gods" of the Lovecraftian cosmos do not scale with the gods of world mythology. Whereas myths are always about humanity and what it can and cannot achieve in some way, Lovecraft's beings are intented to be creatures of pure science, even the science mysterious to and beyond humanity; they do not care and there is no magic to them, they are the stuff the universe, the tangible, scientific universe is made of. Depending on how you read them, they are either infinitely beyond the gods of mythology, being present in multiple dimensions and on far off planets whereas the gods of real-world mythology are confined to earth and its closest neighbours, or infinetely below them, seeing as both Elder Things and boats were able to throw down with Cthulhu whereas Vishnu or Olodumare would be literally omnipotent, omnipresent, and omniscient. And that's not even getting into how they fit into categories like God, Titan, and Primordial (spoiler alert: They don't.)

                                        Secondly, there is the ethical issue. Scion, especially in its vastly improved 2e incarnation, is a game that is about honouring the beings of world religions and world mythologies, even though it may be in an unconventional way. With the way particularly the Deva, Manitou, and Orisha were written, there is a flavour of decolonisation, of amending what has been done wrong in the past to it all. The mere inclusion of the Lovecraftian "deities" no matter how it is done spits in the face of that for the very simple fact that it creates an equivalency between the beings sacred to millions of people and the consciously fantastical creations of a single person, creations meant to inspire horror, not devotion, and to express his personal ideologies, not the shared values of grand cultures. And that is doubly horrifying because it creates an equivalency between the most sacred things of denigrated peoples of colour and the pet OCs of a man who was very much disgusted by these "inferior races", who wrote his stories with the express intention of warning about and exploiting for tantalising entertainment the perceived "degeneracy" of these peoples.

                                        Don't get me wrong, I love the Lovecraft Mythos, and I love the things that can be done with it. But here it does not belong. Here, it can only do harm. And whoever has conceived of this should know that not only will I never put money into a supplement like this, it makes me question putting money into the Scion line as a whole.

                                                I won't argue about the inclusion of a Mythos Pantheon and how it has very nasty implications about things, about how it puts legitimate deities of real world religions up against a collection of fictional figures many of whom are based off these real figures (Nodens, Dagon, etc) and were popularized by a man who thought that the people who worshiped these real deities were backwards, horrible, and 'savage.' There is no argument there, it's plain as day why that is just very.... much an issue. Maybe they could resolve it, but what I would instead like to focus on is the themes of the two genres involved.

                                                Scion is a game based on Heroic Sagas and similar texts, and its themes directly clash against those of the Mythos. Hopelessness, the inevitability of loss, the fundamental inevitability of failure, those are not the themes of Scion. Scion is a game not about 'Will You Succeed?' but a game about 'How Will You Succeed?' There is a fundamental thematic clash here, not even in the sense of 'these two Pantheons have a different thematic focus' but in a 'These are directly opposites' notion.

                                                The horror of the Mythos, the realization that you are but gnats in the face of uncaring beings of unimaginable power... well that's cut short by the other Pantheons. Nyame is here, and he is Omnipotent, and a very nice friendly person who wants to see you succeed. YHWH is around and while He has some days of being a bit eldritch, He tends to care either that you're being good or being bad. There is borderline a limitless continuation of this list, of beings that exist in Scion that just cut the legs out from the horror that the Mythos expresses. Sure, there's one Pantheon that is all about the hopelessness of humanity, but then you're a Mílisian and you can can kill Gods really easily so that.... hard to not have hope when you have Deicide in your blood.

                                                Sure, Azathoth is huge and colossal and when he wakes reality will end, but... that's like Tuesday for the Pantheons. If Atum-Re blinks reality ends, it's really not an uncommon theme, and honestly, you should be way more worried about Atum-Re blinking since Apep is actively trying to eat him every night than the possibility that Azathoth might wake up one day. And, because the Scion system is based on the three 'tiers' of Divinity, Gods, Titans, and Primordials, this means that Azathoth is equal to other Primordials. So throw Míl Espáine at anything from the Mythos, and he could cut them down like chaff, and he's literally a random human who brought on a deicide, the ancestor of humanity equal to Danu for the Túatha Dé Danann. Hard to not have hope. Especially when this is a 'One Pantheon v. Literally Every Pantheon Humanity Has Ever Worshiped' situation.

                                                Furthermore, if these Mythos Figures are intended to be an antagonistic Pantheon... why didn't they get their shit stomped? Because, as I have spoken about at length before, actual like reality-ending threats are not something any Pantheon wants. It's not something the vast majority of Titans want, or Primordials. So, if this one Pantheon is being actively malignant and opposed to everyone else... I mean, sure they're strong like any Pantheon is, but they're contesting against every Titan, Primordial, and God from every Pantheon ever. Countless numbers of omipotent beings would just turn on them.

                                                The idea of including the Mythos is an act of respect to not turn a real Pantheon into an 'evil Pantheon' is also totally missing the forest for the trees. You are legitimizing this 'Pantheon' as much as the Hindu Gods, you are making them equally true, and... like, if this is coming from a place of respect, that feels fundamentally misguided and very badly thought out.

                                                Now, there is a place for Cosmic Horror in Scion, but not this sort of cosmic horror. Apotheosis is a horror story for most Pantheons. If you are a Theoi Scion, you are no longer a 'person,' you are an abstract concept that most of the time wears a people suit. If you are a Netjer or Deva Scion, congratulations, you have lost your individuality. You are no longer a 'person.' A Netjer Scion will find their 'self' broken into shards and find that they never manifest in a 'pure' form, but always find themselves as an being comprised of at least one other 'person.' You are no longer Tim, you are Tim-Seth-Ra, but you are also Seth-Tim-Bes, and you are Kephri-Tim-Atum and you all exist at the same time and are having a conversation where you all visceral disagree with each other. For the Deva you are a shard of the infinite, and... you just aren't an individual any more.

                                                There is place for true cosmic horror in Scion, but it's not this without heavily compromising either the themes of Scion, or the themes of the Mythos. Lovecraftan material is cool, but this feels like literally the diametrically opposite place that it belongs. I hope this turns out to be some sort of internal miscommunication, as it goes against everything that 2e has done to be respectful and well researched.

                                                            Originally posted by karpomatic View Post
                                                            I was enthused when I heard this on the podcast. I'm interested to see their take on it. Some people here are forgetting that there's an Atlantean (sp) pantheon.
                                                            The differences are:

                                                            1. The Atlantean Pantheon is going to be non-canon, included in the Companion as a very much admitted up front fictional Pantheon that is very much optional.

                                                            2. The Atlantean Pantheon is going to be designed as Y'know actual gods. With character, worship traditions, and the semblance of a culture associated with them.

                                                            The crew are going to make an effort to have the Atlantean Gods fit into the tone that Scion has: That of modern myth and heroics, by way of crystal magical nanotech.

                                                            The Elder Gods and Great Old Ones exist as a metaphor for how uncaring the universe is, and how ultimately humans are utterly insignificant. That is what makes them scary to many people: Because they remind humans of how small we are.

                                                            This tone is completely at odds with what Scion does.

                                                            And even if you do put in an Evil/Uncaring Pantheon (why would they make Scions?) you have the other problem that Cthulhu and co make for very poor additions to a Scion game. Cthulhu has no real powers beyond "big" and "tough to kill... For a lowly human". Cthulhu doesn't demonstrate any kind of mythic ability. It's not a god, it's a giant squid thing. Correct me if I'm wrong, but I don't believe that the whole "to look upon Cthulhu is to be driven mad" is meant to be some kind of indication of a special power and more that humans can't comprehend the creature we're looking upon.

                                                            There's a reason why Call of Cthulhu's character types are priests, scientists, historians, and gruff salty fishermen. These are ordinary people, people poking at things their delicate minds can't comprehend. If they poke too deeply or learn too much, they're doomed. A shoggoth could take them out.

                                                            Sic a shoggoth on a Hero with the Warrior Calling and they'll tear it apart. In the context of comparing Cthulhu to Zeus, Law and Order and Lightning wearing a person suit, or Shiva with the third eye whose gaze can vaporize the world (but won't because Sarasvati took the world-ending flames and ate them), or even Ogma telling this squid thing to get off his lawn... Yeah, in mythic terms Cthulhu's a chump.

                                                            So yeah, like I said. Cthulhu? A puss-poor fit for a game like Scion, and offering little Call of Cthulhu fans will enjoy. Like part of the reason you play Call of Cthulhu is for the horror angle.

                                                            Scion would not work well for that.
