                    Having a separate Greek and Roman pantheons is a bit messy, but then again having more than one pantheon at all gets just as messy if you take it far enough. Just how many creation myths are there for instance? While some are specifically for one people, often as not a particular myth is supposed to cover all people everywhere and that obviously leads to compatibility problems. For that matter World creation myths do the same thing and obviously they cannot all be literally true at the same time if there is any kind of exclusive linear time. Gods who are simply variants of one another are a potentially more complex problem but not impossible to resolve.

                    If when I start the campaign I am working on I have players wanting to run Scions from both of the two or there was some other plot thread that required dealing with both pantheons in detail I would probably treat them as separate pantheons and gloss over the retread parts as suspension of disbelief (so far I do not have that problem, but the first campaign I am trying to run in the system is in very early planning stage so it might come up), otherwise I would simply leave it undefined until more was needed.

                    The same kind of problem comes up in oWoD when running mixed "splats" (which is the type I ran most) if one tries to get too literal with the cosmology of the various lines, and the solution is to concentrate on the player characters in the "now" and let esoteric details like that slide; it should work here to an acceptable level here too.

                        I remember tracing specific gods backwards, to see how far they went and who they were in the past. It was quite fascinating, and it makes me wonder if those predecessors still exist in Scion, and in what form. If you're going as far back as Babylon, for example, you can see some later gods who sprang up from the Babylonian mythos - while some Shinto gods were 'adopted' and evolved from Ainu gods or from Chinese gods, or from Hindu gods.

                        I've considered, at one point, taking all the gods and tracing them back to their earliest essences. THOSE are the gods. They've adopted different names and such over time, but when you encounter them, they're more like their primeval aspects instead. The entire idea of 'pantheons' is something humanity made up, and doesn't actually exist.
