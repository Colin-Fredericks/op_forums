                    EPISODE EIGHT: ROAD TRIP

                    Act One: A New Quarter Begins

                    The Big Game was also the end of Fall Quarter. With new classes and newly raised Legend, the Scions await new challenges…

                    In no particular order:

                    GUS has not had to clash with Burt, the school’s most incorrigible bully, and his cronies. They know better than to challenge him. One day, though, he sees one of Burt’s friends try to score brownie points by tripping Leo as the Scion of Nergal hurries through the hall. Leo, however, has Epic Dexterity and Cat’s Grace: He keeps his footing and doesn’t even notice the attempt. This annoys Burt and his friends. As luck would have it, Landon Smith passes by at that time. Burt remembers how Landon humiliated him on the first day of school. He and his buds suddenly surround Landon and start beating him down. Landon isn’t enough of a judo fighter to defend himself against three bigger, stronger boys attacking him at once and from surprise.

                    This offends Gus. He catches Burt’s two cronies by their thumbs and twists, forcing them to their knees. Burt stops kicking the fallen Landon. Gus notes that three on one is not fair, and he asks everyone how they want to resolve the situation. Landon says he’ll fight all three, one at a time, if that’s what they want; but it comes down to Burt vs. Landon behind the gym after school. Gus promises/threatens to act as referee.

                    The fight is pretty even: Burt is bigger and stronger than Landon, and driven by fear of humiliation before his cronies — but he has never genuinely trained at fighting or self-defense. Burt ends up lying on the ground with Landon’s foot on his throat. The three bullies slink off in defeat; Gus feels he has seen justice done.

                    JAEDA keeps an eye on the gym after school, watching for a day when Leo trains. When this happens, she enters to do some sparring. First, though, she belts Leo across the face and tells him that if he ever tries to throw himself on her blade again, she will make him regret it. Did he think for one moment what could have happened if he’d died? He could have started a war between the Anuna and the Atzlánti! Leo crumples and says he’s sorry… but Jaeda gets a weird vibe. She Reads Motivation and realizes that in a perverse way, Leo likes being punished. He thinks he deserves it, to the extent that this is the center of his sense of self. (I.e., his nature is Wretch.) He is the perfect abuser’s victim, and she has just reinforced this. Jaeda tries to get back to the impersonal practicality of training, but she realizes that Leo is even more messed-up than she thought.

                    (Though it didn’t really matter in this adventure, the Scions also found that Leo’s Legend rose to 3 as well, thanks to his nearly-suicidal attempt to cut himself free of his lion-demon Guide. Leo’s Legend may be one of torment and self-loathing, but it’s still a Legend.)

                    Jaeda also teaches a pair of Fashionistas not to mess with her. After PE, she opens her locker and finds that someone has stolen her customary trampwear and replaced it with the dowdiest, most old-fashioned dress imaginable. Two Fashionistas are instantly ready with camera-phones to spread the image of her with the dress, and to mock her with snide questions about who’s taking her to the barn dance.

                    Jaeda doesn’t play along. She glares coldly at Vanessa, the Fashionista who is closest to her size, and says she will take Vanessa’s clothes instead and Vanessa can wear the dress. And she will hurt Vanessa if she resists. Jaeda offers to return Vanessa’s clothes when her own clothes are returned to her regular locker. Vanessa decides that discretion is the better part of survival, and so she puts on the horrible dress. Jaeda then plucks up Vanessa’s phone, snaps a picture, and sends it to everyone on the Fashionista’s friends list. She finds her own clothes hanging by her hallway locker before the day is out.

                    JACK feels pretty good. Doctor Demerit gave his first “quizzybubble” (or pop quiz) of Winter Quarter. Holly competes fiercely with Jack to get better grades in AP Math — but this time, Jack surpasses her. Both answered every question correctly, did the extra credit… but Jack gave every answer in base 8 as well as base 10, for extra extra credit. (Doctor Demerit noted in the margin that for a real challenge, though, Jack should have used base 7.) Jack tells Holly not to feel bad at only getting A+ instead of A++; everyone knows that girls aren’t good at math.

                    Jack feels not so good when his mother tells him that now that football season is over, he should get an afterschool job. The bank has unexpectedly bumped up the mortgage payments on the house; Jack eats a lot; a community college salary only goes so far. Jack does the math (those classes from Dr. Demerit sure help) and says he’ll start looking right away.

                    Hooverdale Mall holds the greatest local concentration of entry-level jobs, and Jack knows where he wants to start: Stick It, in the food court. He’s already semi-famous for eating one of every item on the menu of Food-Like Products On Sticks in a single meal. Jack gives the manager his most heartfelt speech about how much he wants to work at Stick It. When the manager says he already has enough employees, Jack offers to intern: He will work free for a week, to show his skill and dedication.

                    Hyndla Vetterson walks by and hears this exchange. She stops and adds, in a friendly, supportive tone, that Jack’s almost certainly over that head lice infestation. Jack puts on his best bright, false smile, greets Mrs. Vetterson, and says the new girdle suits her, you can hardly tell how much weight she’s gained… Hyndla barely restrains herself from burying Jack in the rubble of the food court and stalks off.

                    Jack gets the internship. Woohoo, he gets to wear a hat that looks like he has a stick thrust through his head! And he’d better do well in the internship, because when he leaves the mall he finds a ball of crumpled metal that used to be his car.

                    HOLLY learns a few things.

                    Derek asks her if ghosts are real. Holly says yes. Derek says that’s a relief, because he’s seen a few. Yup, his divinely-regenerated eyes give him Death Senses. He is also now a legendary mortal.

                    Holly also learns about Jack’s new internship. Thinking this is just too rich, she buys the Stick It franchise at the mall so that she, in fact, is now Jack’s boss. Of course she doesn’t tell him. Jack gets his entry-level job. When the time is right, though…

                    Most importantly, Aaron tells Holly some bad news. Suddenly, his father is trying to sell the house and business. The Avalon Slasher makes the town just too dangerous, so he wants to move the family somewhere else.

                    Holly wangles an invitation to have dinner with the Bindahnas and arranges to be alone with Aaron’s dad. She tells him that she knows there’s some other reason why he wants to move the family, and she knows they are not what they seem. Mr. Bindahna blanches, and Holly can tell this is the sort of fear that can make people do violent, desperate things. She quickly reassures Mr. Bindahna that she is no enemy, declares herself as a Scion, and says she wants to help. Mr. Bindahna still won’t tell her much, but he says the spectacular game against Etna High will attract attention to the area — attention that could be very bad for his family. They are refugees — runaway slaves, in fact — living in hiding as mortals. He pleads with Holly not to tell Aaron or his little sister Janet the truth, as it’s safer for them not to know what they really are. All this reinforces Holly’s suspicion that the Bindahnas are a family of jinn. She accepts Mr. Bindahna’s reasons for moving the family, though, and says she won’t interfere.

                    Act Two: Let’s Try That Again…

                    Jack subscribes to a “WeirdNet” service that collects and distributes reports of strange events. During a morning class, his phone pings to let him know of a new, local WeirdNet report: a pink python seen at the sanitary landfill that serves Avalon, Etna and nearby regions.

                    Ever since the lindwurm attack at Hooverdale Mall, people in the area have occasionally reported seeing big pinkish worms or snakes. The Scions successfully deluded the authorities into dismissing the mall lindwurm as a bizarre stunt by a drug gang, intended to frighten people away from a major drug deal. Since then, opinion has been divided whether there is an actual “pink python” in the area, whether the reports indicate a continuing hoax by the same gang, copycat hoaxes by local people, or whether the whole thing is mass hysteria. Undeniably, pets and local farm animals continue to disappear.

                    In the ensuing months, the Scions did encounter hoax lindwurms (some kid paints a length of dryer outlet pipe pink, attaches a cardboard head, and poses it in some bushes). On the other hand, Marshall slew the lindwurm in the graveyard that held the Xibalba-portal crypt. So, Jack alerts the rest of the Band and they plan to cut the next class and hunt the lindwurm before it kills anyone at the landfill. Jack also notifies Marshall, since he wants to make nice and figures the Band can use all the fighters they can get. Holly, however, decides to stay in class: She’s sure the rest can handle a lindwurm without her.

                    As planned, the Scions leave school and rendezvous at the dump. (Jack needs to beg a ride with another Scion.) They find Marshall already there with his five army-brat buddies, and they’re already battling a lindwurm amid the stinking garbage. As the Scions hurry toward the fight, another lindwurm erupts from a mound of garbage and attacks them.

                    The fight does not take long, since the four Scions have a better idea about monster-killing than they did at the mall. Some attacks bounce — even, to Jack’s frustration, his new stone war-club (lindwurms have high Bashing soak). A few decent stunts later, though, Jaeda manages to strike the Nemean worm inside its mouth… and the lindwurm swallows the First Flower. The inexorable peristalsis of the lindwurm’s gut sends the knife cutting through its flesh from the inside until the knife drops out the worm’s side and the monster falls dead. (Jaeda’s last attack pushed the lindwurm into Dying health levels anyway; might as well make the death cool.)

                    Marshall swaggers over, prepared to boast about killing the lindwurm before the others showed up. The PCs point to the worm they just slew. Their debate on how to dispose of the corpses is interrupted by the arrival of Ms. Dortwade, the school district’s truant officer. Ms. Dortwade, a pickle-faced, middle-aged woman, smiles with grim delight at the chance to get Jack and Jaeda in trouble again. She is disappointed to see “good kids” like Gus and Marshall drawn into their shenanigans.

                    And then a third lindwurm erupts from the ground, right in front of Officer Dortwade, confronting her with a horror undreamed of in her narrow, spiteful world. She faints.

                    The five Scions (and five gun-toting army brats) make short work of the lindwurm, so it doesn’t get a chance to eat Officer Dortwade. Jack cleans her up as best he can under the circumstances and carries her to her car. He wakes her up and tells her she had a fainting spell, then asks if they should they call an ambulance. Dortwade still seems dazed but focuses on the stable, familiar and undeniable fact that Jack and the others are skipping class. She hauls them back to Avalon High. They get detention. “Big whoop,” Jaeda says. She gets detention almost every week.

                        EPISODE EIGHT: ROAD TRIP (Continued)

                        Act Three: When In Doubt, Add Ninjas

                        A few days later, Gus is giving his evening Self-Defense For Young People class at the Y. It’s the usual mix of people who want to learn to protect themselves and girls who want to spend time close to a brawny young studmuffin. Suddenly, the skylight shatters as a man in a trenchcoat falls through it. Thinking quickly, Gus grabs a wrestling mat and sweeps it over the heads of his students, protecting them from the rain of glass shards. “Don’t let them get the package,” the man gasps; he presumably refers to the package he clutches in his arms. Immediately thereafter, several ninjas jump down through the opening, swords drawn!

                        Thinking quickly again, Gus roars, “Run!” to the stupefied students. He then grabs the stranger and jumps up through the broken skylight to the roof, and starts following his own advice.
                        The ninjas leap back up and out as well. They hurl several shuriken at Gus, but they miss. Gus finds he can run faster and leap much farther than the ninjas, so he gets away.

                        As he runs, Gus wonders who in this situation are the good guys and who are the bad guys, the wounded stranger or the ninjas. He decides that ninja assassins aren’t usually the good guys. Besides, these ninjas made no sound at all, which is uncanny, and makes them seem even less likely to be on a noble mission.

                        At first, Gus thinks he should take the mystery-man to a hospital. The man protests that the “shinobi” who were after him would think to check every hospital nearby. So, Gus calls Jaeda, since she has some first aid skills, and takes the mystery-man to Holly’s house. Along the way he calls in Jack as well. The mystery man has a further surprise, though: He asks Gus, “You’re a Scion, right? Good.” Or, maybe not such a surprise. After all, a man chased by creepy silent ninjas is probably in the Big Game.

                        Jaeda stabilizes the wounded man and the Scions get as much of the mystery-man’s story as he’s willing to tell. Contrary to their first suspicion, the man who calls himself Riley Krieger is not a Scion. He is just a highly skilled mortal who works for various Gods, mostly as a courier. He has to be highly skilled, since anything a God would use a courier to deliver is something other people — or entities — would like to take for themselves. Krieger says he doesn’t know what he’s carrying: What he doesn’t know, he can’t be made to tell. He just picked up the package from one operative and set out to deliver it somewhere else. Krieger also explains that the ninjas are from a special cult that all other ninjas shun. The cult serves Mikaboshi, the Titan of Darkness, and its prophet, the Black Bodhisattva. These “shinobi” gain minor supernatural powers from their service, but they lose their souls.

                        Holly decides she needs more information before committing to anything, so she pulls out her mirror and applies Mystery. She learns that Krieger carries the Bell of Endless Night. Remarkably, Holly has heard of this! (Bunch o’ suxx on Intelligence + Occult gets you this.) The bell in some way reveals every place where the power of the Titan of Darkness bleeds through into the mortal World. It would be very useful to the Gods and a great loss to the Titans. Krieger is meant to deliver this powerful Relic to — Classified. Well, it’s someone powerful enough to block supernatural inquiries, and it would sure suck for the Gods if servants of the Titans could pull their secrets out of the ether… But Krieger is sincere, and Holly decides she has enough information to trust his mission. (For now.)

                        The Scions know they can’t just turtle up and try to fight off the shinobi. Krieger assures them that while only a dozen are after him at the moment, dozens more can come within a day or two, plus Gods know what else. He needs to complete his mission and disappear.

                        Krieger is in no condition to travel on his own, so the Scions offer to help him deliver his package if it’s not too far. Where was he going? Krieger says he was to deliver it to Jewel Cave in the Black Hills of South Dakota. The Scions check a map, do a little quick math and figure they can drive all night and reach the cave by mid-morning. It will of course mean missing a day of school before they can drive back, but what the hell.

                        Gus calls his father to say he just met two girls who are really, really big fans of his football exploits and he won’t be home for… well, who knows. His father accepts this with a smug “That’s my boy.” Jaeda’s father knows the deal. Holly does not need to ask anyone’s permission. Jack simply decides to seek forgiveness later.

                        Off Krieger and the Scions go in Jaeda’s Roadrunner. They switch roads frequently, choosing randomly at intersections, in hopes this may confuse any supernatural or divinatory pursuit. They know, however, that they have limited choices of passes through the Cascade and Rocky Mountains. If the shinobi can attack, they’ll do it at those choke-points. Krieger adds that the shinobi can’t act during the day, so the Rocky Mountains will be the supernatural ninjas’ last chance to take back the package.

                        The Scions get through the Cascades without incident. As they predicted, though, the shinobi catch up to them in the Rocky Mountains. (Specifically, the Big Belt Range, while they drive from Bozeman to Livingston, Montana.) Jaeda drives. (She’s the one with Control: Car.) As they speed through the pass a truck slowly pulls up behind their car, with shinobi in the back.

                        Jack decides to deal with the truck. He climbs out the window of their car and onto the roof. As the truck approaches, he jumps, already swinging his war-club at the truck’s engine. He strikes a tremendous blow: The truck’s engine starts sputtering. Jack uses the momentum of the truck and his impact to bounce him cartwheeling back up the road to the Scions’ car.

                        At the same time, two shinobi appear on the hood of the car, weapons ready. The Scions, however, are readier. Before the shinobi can cut through the windshield, Gus snarls at one and hits him with Game Face. Holly taps the windshield in front of the other to draw his attention, and shouts, “Jump!” through the open window — using Overt Order. By the revised “Unnatural Mental Influence” rules, they must obey for one action before they spend Willpower… The two shinobi bail. Their truck, meanwhile, falls behind. Jack didn’t wreck the truck, but he caused enough damage to slow it.

                        Holly suspects this won’t be the end of the shinobi’s attempts, even if the Scions get a chance to switch roads. (She guesses the shinobi cult might have Prophecy about what roads the Scions will take, or multiple people with Mystery working in sequence.) She calls on Smoking Mirror to watch the road ahead for further attacks, and she finds one: a car speeding toward them, with an eighteen-wheeler approaching at a more leisurely pace. The speeding car is almost certainly an attack; the Sea-Land truck might just be ordinary commerce.

                        The Scions ready themselves. This time, Gus climbs out the window and onto the hood of the car. If necessary, he intends to try pushing the shinobi’s car away from the Scions’ car. The shinobi’s car moves to the middle of the road. Maybe the driver is playing chicken with Jaeda, or maybe he just intends to crash his car into the Scions.

                        It doesn’t matter, though. Jaeda looks like she’s playing chicken right back, but at the last second she swerves to pass the shinobi’s car with less than an inch between them and two wheels up on the embankment. (Legendary success at Control.) The shinobi driver does not do as well. He swerves, trying to hit the Scions anyway, but loses control of the speeding car and misses the next turn on the mountain road. The shinobi’s car goes over the edge in best Hollywood fashion.

                        As for the eighteen-wheeler? Jaeda faces another challenge to pass by it at high speed on the curving road, but she does so with ease. The surprised truck driver also keeps control of his vehicle. It wasn’t another shinobi attack, just normal traffic.

                        The sun rises half an hour later, and the Scions hope they are in the clear. They arrive at Jewel Cave a few hours later.

                        They join the first tour of the day. The cave is well worth seeing — magnificently decorated with crystals and strange, gleaming gypsum formations. At a certain point, though, Krieger nudges the Scions and they leave the path for a tunnel opening that no one else on the tour seems to notice. They enter a cavern hung with thousands of slender stalactites that resemble long soda straws.

                        A human figure steps out of the wall, like a person-shaped silhouette or cutout made of the stone surface. The figure asks for the package. Krieger hands it over. The Scions tense for the sudden but inevitable betrayal or revelation that they’ve been played for suckers. It doesn’t happen. The figure merely thanks them.

                        Jack and Holly both try using their Epic Mental Knacks to find some clue to the unknown God’s identity. (They’re pretty sure it must be a God.) With his Instant Investigator, Jack spots the figure tapping his fingers irregularly against his thigh while he talks. It’s Morse code for, “Good work, kids. Say hello to Jaeda.”

                        The mystery God offers to get them back to Avalon. The Scions accept. The God opens a passage through the rock back to the surface. Krieger stays in South Dakota; the God takes the wheel of the Roadrunner. He steers it down into the ground. A few minutes later, the car enters a huge cavern. The God drives through the tunnels and caverns for about half an hour (and the Scions catch glimpses of strange things off to the side), then steers the car back through the rock to the surface and the Avalon High parking lot. He thanks them again and sinks back into the ground.

                        When Jack tells Jaeda what he noticed, Jaeda guesses the mystery God was Quetzalcoátl. He’s an intellectual showoff, has some earth associations, and knows about Jaeda — after all, she carries his knife. He’s also certainly magician enough to block anyone else’s use of Mystery. But the Scions can’t really be sure. The Scions go in to their classes. Everyone gets detention for coming in late without notes from their parents, but nobody much cares.

                                EPISODE NINE: P.S., This Is A Trap

                                A shorter episode this time.

                                Act One: Interludes with Gary

                                It’s been a quiet week in Avalon, at least up to now. The Scions go about their daily lives. So do their rivals. Sometimes, they intersect…

                                HOLLY is not especially surprised that Friday when Aaron Bindahna asks her out on a date. She knew he was getting a crush on her. (So are her Followers Manny Salkind and Bobby Kilmer, and half the boys in the Mathlympics, Chess Club and Knowledge Bowl.) She lets Aaron take her to a concert (small and arty, not stadium rock) and to dinner afterward at Olive Garden.

                                Gary also shows up at the restaurant with his current girlfriend — who won’t be his girlfriend any longer. Whatever they planned for their evening, they are now having a fight. Girlfriend is upset that Gary looks at other girls and wants more of his time. Gary says she’s clingy and needy (likely true), and that he can get what he wants from plenty of other girls (well… also likely true). A waiter tells Gary and Girl to lower their voices; Gary throws the waiter across the room.

                                Holly decides she really doesn’t like Gary. She walks over and tells him he’s a pig… so eat like one! Gary obeys the Overt Order, and promptly drops his face into the antipasto tray. Girl giggles. Aaron goggles. Holly decides they’d better get the hell out before Gary recovers. Seconds after they reach the street, they hear an angry roar from inside the Olive Garden and a whole table comes flying through the window.

                                Holly thanks Aaron for a nice evening and gives him a peck on the cheek. Aaron believes the evening was a disaster.

                                JACK now spends 20 hours a week working at Stick It. This means his football team Followers (Moose, Bull, Bear, Ox and Gnu) hang out there as well, hoping to score free food. (Sometimes they do, especially at day’s end when stuff would otherwise be thrown out.)

                                Thanksgiving is coming, so Jack’s supervisor shows up to train him on the holiday special, Thanksgiving On A Stick: turkey mini-sausages, cranberry jigglers, candied yam cubes, stuffing cubes and mashed potato spheres with a hard gravy-like shell!

                                Today, Jack and his pals are joined by Gary and his crew of football team thralls. They hassle Jack by taking forever to order, complaining about the food and wanting replacements, intimidating other patrons, etc. Jack’s pals are getting pretty steamed, and Gary and his buds seem ready to oblige them with a fight.

                                Jack decides to preempt things by pulling out a box of sticks, tripping and “accidentally” throwing them at Gary & Co. as a hundred little javelins. They stick in Gary’s thralls pretty good, and Jack basks in the cries of pain and surprise. For Gary, though, they are barely an annoyance. He starts talking lawsuit. Jack finds this an occasion for more insults. In a battle of zingers, Gary is truly unarmed, so Gary pithily responds by attacking Jack.

                                It’s not much of a fight. Jack is agile, but Gary lands a lucky punch that staggers Jack. (Luck plus Legendary Deed plus channeled Virtue, that is.) Jack hits back, but doesn’t do much. Then Gary switches to wrestling, so he can use his Strength instead of his Dexterity to attack, and immediately gets Jack in a clinch. He squeezes; Jack struggles to no avail and passes out. Meanwhile, Gary’s thralls duke it out with Jack’s pals.

                                Jack wakes up with mall cops around him. Gary and the thralls are gone, but they left broken tables and chairs and “SCIONS SUCK” written on the wall in ketchup. Jack has the dubious comfort that security cameras probably caught the scuffle and Gary will probably have to pay some kind of fine.

                                GUS is teaching one of his self-defense classes that evening. Instead of ninjas attacking, this time Gary swaggers in to interrupt the class. He tells the students that what a guy needs for self-defense is muscle, so work out until you can do THIS: whereupon he haymakers a punching bag, which explodes in a shower of sawdust. And for the gals… get a boyfriend who can do that.

                                Gary isn’t there just to be obnoxious, though. He challenges Gus to arm wrestle. Gus, of course, agrees. At first, Gary seems stronger — stronger than Gus remembers — but Gus grits his teeth and vows that no way is any giant going to beat him at anything! Legend spent, Virtues channeled on both sides… and Gus feels a surge of strength like he’s never felt before. And the table cracks beneath the strain. Gus not only slams Gary’s arm down, the table splinters and he flips Gary to the floor! Gus has gained his third dot of Epic Strength.

                                (Gus’ player rolled amazingly hot. In fact, he would have won without the extra dot of Epic Strength. As it happens, though, this coincidentally happens to fit very well with the Fatebinding between Gus and Gary…)

                                Gary is astonished. “You got a power-up too?” he demands. “Morning after the game. You too?” “Ditto.” They part, warily on Gary’s part, but Gus exultant.

                                JAEDA spends Sunday at the local wrecking yard, looking for spare parts for her Roadrunner. She encounters Gary and Kwame “The Cat” McMorris. While the giant and suspected were-leopard look for parts for Kwame’s car, they argue about what to do about Gus. Kwame suggests ambush. Gary protests that beating Gus won’t mean anything unless he wins fairly. “Yeah, that’s why you’re losing,” Kwame retorts. At which point they come around a stack of cars and see Jaeda.

                                Despite the argument and his defeat the night before, Gary seems to be in a fairly mellow mood. He Talks Car a bit with Jaeda and suggests parts she could grind down a bit to fit her Roadrunner — he thinks he saw one such car. Holly smiles, kisses Manny on the cheek and tells him he’s very sweet. Then she turns to Vanessa and the Fashionistas. Delia is already white as a sheet, eyes wide in horror. Holly’s smile and words to Vanessa are pleasant, but the more perceptive kids catch the venomous fury behind them and start edging away from Vanessa. The Fashionista knows her scheme to humiliate Holly through Manny has misfired, but she doesn’t know exactly how. Or what the daughter of Louhi will do to her for it.

                                                    She’ll find out.

                                                    Holly has a talk with Manny afterward, applying all her Social-Fu to let him down gently. He’s a good friend, and she appreciates his gesture of affection, but she’s already in a relationship, etc. Manny confirms what she suspected, though: Vanessa convinced Manny that he had to make a grand gesture to Holly, or he’d lose her to the handsome and glamorous Cole.

                                                    JAEDA has an unpleasant week. First, she comes to one of her afterschool training sessions with Leo to find Marshall already there. He tells her that he’ll take over from her, Leo is a member of his Band, not hers. Jaeda questions this (rather cogently turning Marshall’s own words against him in the process), but she feels she can’t push Leo too hard to defy Marshall. Leo doesn’t, even though — or possiby because — Marshall adopts a classic “You maggots!” Marine drill sergeant attitude to Leo. The situation also confirms that to Marshall, at least, the Scions have conclusively split into two separate and rival Bands.

                                                    A few days later, Jaeda sees Horndog Bob, the masterless thrall of the late Bolvarg, hassling a girl in the hallway. This is nothing strange. Bob relishes the size and strength he gained by becoming a thrall, and he thinks he’s now entitled to take what — or who — he wants. He’s cornered the girl, groping her and telling her to go along with it, she’ll like it. The girl clearly feels otherwise. So, Jaeda intervenes. Per her usual practice with Bob, she kicks him in the groin and punches him in the nose. Bob drops. At which point, Vice-Principal Stone arrives. He gloats about the chance to expel Jaeda, finally, but the girl and the also-arriving Janitor Mitch stand up for Jaeda, pointing out that she was protecting the girl from sexual assault (and likely saving the school from a lawsuit). So, Jaeda just gets detention (again, ho hum).

                                                    More importantly, Jaeda’s father receives another phone call about the reason for her detention, and it comes during one of his drinking bouts. It turns him self-pitying and maudlin. He cries in his tequila about how he’s failing as a father, Jaeda’s mother was the only good thing that happened to him and he screwed it up, etc. Jaeda finds it all very uncomfortable.

                                                    JACK, on the other hand, faces a simple, straightforward problem. He made enough at Stick It to afford a motorcycle (an elderly Kawasaki that definitely does not enhance his coolness). He’s riding it home from work when a Dumpster comes flying at him. It hits. He manages to skid with the blow enough that the ton or so of metal and garbage doesn’t hurt him, but it’s not good for the bike. Jack crawls out in time to see a huge, lumpish hag who seems made of stone charging at him. She carries one of those metal pipes set in a concrete plug that are used as movable dividers or fence supports, and she swings it like a club. Jack is unarmed. The giantess sobs about how the Scions killed her boys, and she’s going to kill them in revenge. Jack realizes this must be the Sarsen twins’ mother.

                                                    The giantess can’t hit Jack with the club (his DV is too high). When she gets wise and tries grappling instead, Jack remembers what happened with Gary and decides to run, though it offends his pride (and his Courage Virtue). Sometimes, though, discretion is the better part of staying alive. The giantess hurls her steel and concrete club at Jack, but she misses. She settles for crushing his bike and throwing that at him, too.

                                                    The other Scions are not too surprised at this attack. Actually, they are more surprised that it took this long for Mama Sarsen to try killing one of them.

                                                    GUS continues his neighborhood watch activities. One night, a mugger accosts him and the old lady he escorts home after dark. The mugger has a gun. Gus grins and prepares to make the mugger regret his choice of profession. Then a ninja drops silently from the trees between them. With one hand, the ninja motions for Gus to stay back. With the sword in his other hand, he slices through the mugger’s gun. Behind his ski mask, the mugger’s jaw drops. The ninja wiggles his fingers at the mugger in a “come and get me” gesture. The mugger, slave to every movie and TV show in which a dumb thug confronts a clearly superior hero, throws the remains of his gun at the ninja. The ninja darts forward, catches the mugger’s arm and judo-flips him to the ground. Then the ninja holds the tip of his sword to the mugger’s throat and waves an admonitory finger at him. The mugger lifts his hands and says he gives up. The ninja salutes Gus and the old lady, steps into a shadow and is gone. The ninja never made a sound.

                                                    Gus lets the other Scions know the shinobi are back in town. At least, there’s one shinobi, and he’s fighting street crime. Huh? None of the other Scions can make sense of it either.

                                                        Act Two: Revenge of the Ninja

                                                        While the Scions ponder these developments, Jack and Holly go on a date that Friday night. Like the rest of their relationship, this is duty rather than pleasure — something to cement Holly’s place among the Fashionista clique and Jack’s place among the alpha jocks. They go dancing at The Public Pool, the hottest (and only) club in Avalon and Etna. As its name suggests, The Public Pool used to be a swimming pool before city budget problems forced its closure. Now people dance in the empty pool, with the deep end as the mosh pit. Naturally, Cole shows up. Matters briefly get a little tense between him and Jack.

                                                        After their public appearance as a couple, Holly drives Jack home in her Lotus. Jack has his window down so the icy breeze on his face reminds his adolescent hormones that he really truly should not try anything romantic, no matter how beautiful Holly is. (Stupid hormones.) Which is why he and Holly hear the scream.

The shinobi are not trivial to defeat, but Jack outmatches them and Holly uses Overt Order to make them stand still and take Jack’s blows. Jack takes the sword from one shinobi and uses it against the rest. First one shinobi falls and disintegrates to leave only a black shuriken behind; then the second. The third grabs the girl who screamed and holds a blade to her throat. The Scions let him retreat.

                                                        The shinobis’ victim turns out to be Landon Smith. He is seriously wounded (a couple shuriken in him, which soon disappear) and unconscious. Jack and Holly perform what first aid they can and rush him to a hospital.

(In play, this timing got a little compressed; everything from the assault on Landon to the final fight should have taken longer than one night.) Holly runs interference while Jack slips in to talk to him. Jack activates his Know Tapu and detects that Landon has become supernatural, with some sort of ban against sunlight. It isn’t hard to infer that Landon has become a shinobi, and Jack soon gets him to admit this. Which leaves two big questions.

Master Chiba, the new sensei at the dojo Landon attends, led him in a special rite to gain powers of martial arts mastery, stealth and shadow. Becoming a shinobi hurt a lot the first time as darkness ripped Landon apart and remade him, but now he just needs to drip a little blood into a shadow to become a shinobi again.

                                                        And why? To make a difference in the world. Landon took up judo so he could defend himself against bullies, but he found he wanted to stop bullies from picking on other people too. So, he joined the neighborhood watch (like Gus). Master Chiba saw that desire and offered him a chance to become part of something greater: a secret order of supernatural ninjas that fight to save the world from evil in the night. Stopping a mugger was his own idea, though, as a way to test his new powers (and show off to Gus).

                                                        Landon won’t be doing any more ninja-ing for a while. The Scions leave him at the hospital and head for home while they ponder what to do next. First, though, they decide Landon needs a guard in case more shinobi try to kill him. The choice is really between Barbara and Leo. (Delia lacks sufficient physical prowess, and Marshall’s a prick.) While Leo would probably do it, Barbara volunteers: She has less combat skill than Leo, but she can fly Landon to safety if the hospital comes under attack.

                                                        For the next order of business, the Scions decide to learn more about Master Chiba. Holly uses Mystery to ascertain that Master Chiba serves the entity Shosa-no-Mikaboshi, AKA the Black Bodhisattva of the Endless Night Clan, and that he’s turned 20 local people into shinobi. The center of their power is a place called the Black Mirror Shrine, which they can reach through the dojo Landon attends, but this otherworldly shrine is an extension of the Black Bodhisattva: He can withdraw it from the World any time, and it disappears if the Black Bodhisattva dies.

                                                        The Scions then visit United Martial Arts (Judo • Aikido • Health • Sport • Defense) to see a class late that Saturday afternoon. Jack has Know Tapu active and finds that the receptionist is a shinobi. So are most of the students in this advanced class. So is their teacher, Master Chiba. (And yes, he looks just like Sonny Chiba). Shaken, the Scions leave.

                                                        Night falls as the Scions drive away in Jaeda’s Roadrunner. They argue whether they should try confronting Master Chiba right away or wait until morning when the shinobi won’t be able to use their powers. And a huge, shadowy claw rips the engine from the car, while hidden shinobi knock out the streetlights nearby. (The shinobi don’t join the fight, though.)

                                                        The Lucifuge is back. (Or it’s another one. Does it matter?) None of the Scions’ regular weapons can hurt it. Jack tries throwing the shuriken left by the two dissolving shinobi and hits with one of them, doing a fair bit of damage, but retrieving black shuriken at night in the middle of battle is slightly problematic. He then punctures the car’s gas tank, and Gus coats his hands in the gasoline to strike at the Lucifuge with flaming fists. (He’s tough enough the fire barely stings.) Jaeda gets flares from the back of her car and lights them to harm the shadow-creature; she even uses one like a knife. Ultimately, she ignites the pool of gasoline, sacrificing her own car to generate another light source with which to harm the Lucifuge. Holly commandeers a passing driver’s car to use its headlights as another weapon. For its part, the Lucifuge manages to bite Gus; its shadowy mandibles inflict deep yet eerily unbleeding wounds. At last they manage to kill the unholy thing. Between fire and the damage to the engine, though, Jaeda’s car is trashed.

The frost giant is appalled that the monster destroyed a classic muscle car. He tells Jaeda that he wants to help take down the sonuvabitch who sent the Lucifuge: This crime must be avenged! Jaeda accepts Gary’s offer with gratitude. Besides, it’s a chance to ride in Gary’s fabulous Mercury Cougar XR-7…

                                                            Act Four: Heart of Darkness

                                                            Gary drives the Scions back to United Martial Arts, with brief side-trips to collect weapons and to buy flares and gasoline for improvised Molotov cocktails. When the Scions reach the dojo, they start by throwing Molotov cocktails through the windows in hopes the light of the burning building will inhibit the shinobi — or at least prevent Dark Master Chiba from summoning another Lucifuge.

                                                            Then they all charge in. The students are running out a side door as the Scions (and giant) run in the front door; Dark Master Chiba is already gone, but Holly knows exactly which way he went thanks to Ariadne’s Thread. The trail leads the same way the students went — but they find 20 shinobi blocking their way!

                                                            Gary doesn’t care. When Holly says the trail leads to Chiba’s office on the other side of a workout room, he charges straight through the mass of sword-wielding ninjas. He gets past most of them unscathed… but a few of the shinobi manage to cut him with their blades, nearly killing him by the time he reaches the office. Jaeda is right behind him, and the few shinobi who try attacking the little girl instead of the charging giant miss her by a mile. Holly likewise slips past into the office. Gus and Jack battle their way through as well, leaving a trail of fallen shinobi behind them.

                                                            In the office, Gary’s first act is to grab the tank of Dark Master Chiba’s water cooler and crush it over his head. The water freezes as it drenches him (and for a few seconds he is visibly a giant), then cracks and falls away to show Gary partially healed from his wounds. (Gus mutters that it isn’t fair that giants can heal themselves better than Scions can.)

                                                            The office has a fire door to the alley outside, but it’s one of those doors that can’t close quickly, so they don’t think Dark Master Chiba left that way. Besides, Ariadne’s Thread points to a silk wall hanging. Sure enough, there’s a door behind it… though the Scions saw no door on the other side of the wall. The door turns out not to be locked.

                                                            Gus guards the door to the office while the others go through the mystery door to the Black Mirror Shrine. The shrine looks like the interior of a Shinto temple — a large, rectangular hall with a peaked roof — but the walls and pillars are all made of black mirrors set at angles to create a confusing kaleidoscope of reflections. The only light comes from twisting “black light” tubes whose weird, purple illumination conceals as much as it reveals.

                                                            At the far end of the hall, Dark Master Chiba kneels before a figure who sits on a throne on a dais. This figure rises to his feet as the Scions enter. He appears to be a man in robes of black silk edged with shards of black glass. His face is a black porcelain mask of a serene Buddha, with three eye-slits filled with opal. They have found Shosa-no-Mikaboshi, the Black Bodhisattva, master of the Endless Night Clan!

                                                            The Black Bodhisattva makes a desultory attempt at intimidation with the old “You have come to me as I planned so that I can kill you” routine, but the Scions don’t buy it. They and Gary attack. Dark Master Chiba dons a demon-face mask and fights at his master’s side as a high-powered (Legend 3) shinobi. The Scions, however, have sussed how to block the greatest power of the shinobi and their master: They light flares, filling the Black Mirror Shrine with light so there are no shadows through which Chiba or the Black Bodhisattva can teleport. Dark Master Chiba snuffs the first two flares with well-aimed shuriken, but the Scions promptly light more.

                                                            Nevertheless, it’s a hard fight. Jack manages to lose his shinobi shuriken (though Holly retrieves them). No one seems able to lay a finger on the Black Bodhisattva: He activates a War Boon, Warrior Ideal, that penalizes all the attacks upon him. The Black Bodhisattva attacks by slashing with the razor-sharp black glass shards of his robes, though he doesn’t have much better luck against the Scions than they do against him. Holly curses him with the Evil Eye, but they can’t tell if it’s working or not. Overt Order clearly fails, and Holly ascertains that the Black Bodhisattva is Legend 5 — a Demigod-equivalent titanspawn.

                                                            Holly’s powers do work against Chiba. The ninja sensei remains nearly as agile as his master, but eventually Gary grapples him and Jaeda cuts out his heart in revenge for her car. He dissolves, leaving a shuriken and his demon-mask.

                                                            Out in the office, meanwhile, Gus holds the door against waves of shinobi. Only two can attack him at a time, but they stagger their attacks so two attack every tick. Fortunately, Gus has cover from the door and an improvised shield, and he wears his new chainmail. The few shinobi who manage to hit him with their swords cannot harm him. Gus, on the other hand, finds that he can simultaneously punch one shinobi with his fenrir-enhanced cesti while using Game Face on another. Even with the cesti, he can’t one-punch the shinobi, but every use of Game Face means another ninja running in terror.

                                                            In the Black Mirror Shrine, Gary manages to clinch the Black Bodhisattva and tear off his mask. This seems eminently sensible, since, dude, creepy mask worn by creepy supernatural ninja leader? Gotta be a Relic, right? Well, yes, but the Black Bodhisattva responds with a chuckle and a statement that now the Scions get to see his true form… And Shosa-no-Mikaboshi swells into a gleaming, black-skinned horror bigger than Gary’s giant form, with huge claws and a mass of tentacles where its face should be! The horrific Thing emits a ghastly ululation, throws off Gary with ease, and resumes battle with the Scions. Fortunately, although Shosa-no-Mikaboshi is now much stronger, it seems not quite as agile as before.

                                                            Holly decides she can do nothing in this particular battle and goes to help Gus. About that time, the shinobi manage to cut the power to the building (or maybe it’s the fire), making shadows by which they can teleport into the office; but only a couple manage it before Holly enters and lights flares. Between Gus and Holly’s use of Overt Order, the few remaining shinobi are either knocked out or flee. Of course, the mind-controlled shinobi recover their senses within 10 seconds or so, but the fire is spreading through the building. The two Scions see one fanatical shinobi try to reach them by charging through the flames, only to catch fire and die, silently screaming.

                                                            Gary gives up trying to grapple the Black Bodhisattva and goes back to chopping with his axe. Jack has a shinobi blade and manages to cut the Black Bodhisattva a time or two. Jaeda, however, guesses that light is the creature’s true weakness. After wounding the thing in the head with the First Flower, she lights two flares and jams them into the wound, flame first!

                                                            The Black Bodhisattva throws her off and screams, a sound more horrific and terrifying than any the Scions have heard before. It affects them and Gary like Dreadful Mien, affecting everyone at once. They must flee in blind terror for at least one action; and so they all charge out the door. Moments after they leave, the door vanishes. They decide it would be prudent to leave the building and be far away before police and the fire department show up.
