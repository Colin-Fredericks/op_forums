            The developer and writers have already said one of their goals for Scion2 is to have multiple "settings" for the game. The National Pantheons could be such a setting, that's fine. So could a steampunk civil war setting, or a space opera setting, or anything else. It's a little harder to shoehorn in something like American Gods where faith is the fuel for deities because that would need some kind of system and it'd be pretty fundamental to the game. That'd be much harder to work out as a setting-specific trait than just different takes on what Scion can be and do.

            When White Wolf pitched Scion, it was ancient mythology awakens in the modern days. That's what got me, and a lot of other people, to play it. That's why we've stuck with it when the system is laughable, the books are terrible and the writing is sub-par. That idea, of classical myth in the modern age, is badass. Is it the only badass thing that exists? No. Can other things be equally badass? Yes. But they are not what Scion was claiming it was about when it was released. That's not what we bought into. Can Scion be expanded to include other ideas? Sure, why not? It just needs to be clear that those things are an alternate take on Scion. You don't sign up for a Werewolf: the Apocalypse game and then get handed a pile of material for the Wild West. That's what Werewolf: the Wild West is for. You don't crack open a copy of Romeo and Juliet to read about the battle between the Capulet and Montague crime families of California. That's when you go watch Romeo+Juliet.

            CAN Scion be all these other things? Of course. But it needs to be itself first. It needs to deliver on its original promise: ancient myth in modern times. Then there can be all kinds of alternate settings and game mods and everything else. Those need to come later, and after they get the original game right. This isn't a case of saying that Pizza Hut must ONLY EVER SELL PIZZA, it's saying that maybe they should hold off on trying to sell pasta, salads, desserts and roast chicken until they get pizza right. Cause if your flagship concept can't sail, what's the point of spending time on all the extra stuff?

                                As you yourself noted, you'd have a problem with a minor Greek god(dess) being on the same level as Zeus, but in Scion canon Persephone has associated Purviews and is therefore on the same level as Zeus and Odin.
                                Persephone is an actor in one of the most well-known myths in the world, she is an important Goddess in the Mystery traditions, she plays an active part in the myths of Adonis, Luece and Minthe, is an observer in the myth of Orpheus and is the Iron Queen of the Underworld. I do not have a problem with her at Legend 12. That said there are a number of deities in the more traditional Pantheons whose status I disagree with, such as Kebauet, Bragi, Idun and Quirinus, almost the entire Keepers of the World.

                                If the problem is that you don't want Uncle Sam to be as powerful as Zeus, then the simple thing is to remove the requirement that you need to be Legend 12 to have associated Purviews so that lesser gods don't all need to have Legend 12 just to have Scions that get associated Purviews. That addresses the much larger issue you've stated.
                                It would not solve all my problems with Uncle Sam, but I would welcome an official system for allowing lesser Gods to have Purviews associated.

                                The game is built around this assumption as being the primary path for the PCs: they'll achieve apotheosis and become gods.
                                That is the point: for the PCs. It is a fundamental conceit of RPGs, whatever form they might take, that the PCs are special. It's why the PCs are the only ones who can kill the dragon. It's why the prophecy always refers to a PC. It's why one PC can mow down armies of mooks. In Scion, it's why PCs can achieve apotheosis in a single lifetime when people like Gilgamesh and Theseus and the Pandavas could not.

                                I feel like you're shifting the goal posts here. Your complaint has never been that Uncle Sam has the specific Purview associations that he does and that they're just attributed poorly, your complaint is that he has them at all because of what rules say he must have done to have them. Since you agree he could have done what it takes to have them, the whole Osiris tangent seems entirely irrelevant. Both of them are written up in the rules based on what the authors felt was appropriate to the mythology around them.
                                I feel like I have been unclear, in which case I apologize. My line of reasoning was this: If you apply the advancement rules for PCs to NPCs like Uncle Sam and Columbia, then you needs must let it apply to all the Gods. Betsy Ross or John Henry, if built like a player was playing them, could definitely earn and spend enough XP to reach a Legend level on par with Vishnu with any Purviews they wish. The problem is, by the same token, why hasn't Osiris picked up Epic Stamina to heal his severed penis? For that matter, why hasn't his wife used her mastery of the Health Purview to grow it back for him? Why hasn't Thor taken the time to learn Water to help in his battles against the Midgard Serpent? Why hasn't Aphrodite not taken the time to learn a single Purview up to Avatar level? Why hasn't Athena bought some Epic Appearance if she's jealous enough of Aphrodite to start a war over it? The list goes on.

                                Now, any of those things might have happened in your personal game, but they cannot be a part of the official settings because that would imply that your divine parent doesn't really matter any more because you could argue that in the intervening time the Gods could have learnt any power the player deigns to have associated, because that's what they would do as PCs. Hence they cannot be treated as PCs.

                                The only way to avoid a chaotic free-for-all of associations would, therefore, be to stick to the abilities they display in myth or that are attributed to them in religious literature. Osiris does not have Stamina because he does not have it in the myths. Thor does not have Water for the same reason, though if they were played as PCs I do not doubt that both would happen.

                                Which leads to my problem with the National Pantheons. There is no myth where Betsy Ross heals the sick, or spreads plague for that matter. There is no myth of Columbia leading the souls of the dead to the underworld, none of John Henry commanding flames. Where are the hymns describing Robin Hood's command over the darkness, or the ones that call on D'artgnan to protect his followers?

                                Most of the powers given to the National Pantheons seem to have been given for thematic reasons, not mythic ones. My question is, why should they get this special treatment, when all the other Pantheons need to justify their associations (of course, a lot of the associations in Scion 1e are insane, but that is a discussion for another day)?

                                I hope that made it clearer. Again, I apologize for the earlier ambiguity, English is not my first language.

                                As a final note, I am not attempting to dictate to the developers what they should or should not do. I am simply putting forth my opinion, that keeping Scion focused on the initial core premise, that of ancient Gods and their modern children battling the Titans, would make for a better product.
