                        Originally posted by Prince of the Night View Post
                        its just "Special snowflake" Hate.
                        I don't think so. Particularly when the dominant thread of people's explanation is "it took up space that could have been used to detail another ACTUAL pantheon based on another ACTUAL mythology".

                        There is nothing particularly "special snowflake" about the Atlantean pantheon beyond the fact the authors made them up out of whole cloth. Scire fits right in to the general slop of PSPs. The deities didn't have any particular fancy side stuff that made them different from the already detailed gods. (No, "Atlantean gods get the PSPs of other pantheons" or "Atlantean gods get 12 abilities" or the like). There was some truncation in the conceptualizations of their Underworld and Overworld, admittedly, but, overall, those fit in with the core themes of the pantheon and it would be very easy to see similar ideas being applied to other pantheons with mythologies that don't fit so comfortably into the whole tripartate conceptualization.

                        They aren't even really the only "made up" pantheon, as the Allied pantheons are effectively "made up" particularly with regard to the idea of them being any sort of group. There's not much more than individual mythologies (if that). No actual "pantheon" mythology. There aren't many well known stories of Uncle Sam going out to get Paul Bunyan to fell a big tree that was killing all the crops in Virginia because it cast too much shade or Betsy Ross calling on Pecos Bill to go out and harvest some strange desert plant for a very special flag she wanted to sew...

                        The Nemetondevos are effectively another "made up" pantheon, since there is next to nothing in the way of individual mythologies much less anything that identifies them as a pantheon proper (except of course that they were, there's just no evidence of it). Not many people have the same feelings about the Gallic deities as the hate on for the Atlantean. Aside from the largely fictional nature of the end product, at least it's about actual gods some people worshiped at some point in time. (And really, there's not any other way to create a "lost" pantheon than making it up almost entirely.)

                                        Originally posted by Ajax View Post
                                        There are better fictional pantheons out there than the Atlanteans. Many of them on expired copyrights even or with holders willing to work with respectful fans.
                                        I'm aware. It's something we've talked about for 2e, given that it's no longer a limited line. Stretch goal pantheons may or may not include fictional pantheons, simply because I'm not liable to use a fictional pantheon in a core book.

                                        However, the Atlanteans have a distinct advantage, in that they're wholly fictional - and if we're talking about PCs creating their own pantheon, they remain a good example. By Plato's account, Atlas and his family are assuredly Scions, and might've ascended to Godhood. Due to the way Apotheosis and pantheon creation works in 2e, Atlantis is a good sample choice for other "what if" reasons.

                                        In Scion 2e canon, though, Atlantis is a locked-off Terra Incognita. Getting there via ritual or magics will probably get you to a spot that looks like a much bigger version of the island in Tomb Raider, except with lots of Grecian ruins instead of Japanese ones.
