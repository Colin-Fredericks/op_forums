                    There first thing going on here relates to mechanics. You can find information on object health levels located in Scion: Hero (page 201). We have gotten rid of hardness, but there are several examples of how many health levels various objects have, but in no way is it possible to represent every single kind of object. A character might need to break through a wooden door, a steel door, a stygian iron door, a shield door of Asgard, or a ten mile wide door leading to the depths of some horrible titan realm. Part of the passage reads:

                    "Inanimate objects can take a set number of health levels of damage before they are destroyed. When they have lost half of those health levels, they are considered “damaged,” which inhibits their natural functions. If a tool, it functions at a penalty. If a wall, it no longer keeps things out. The object is not a complete failure until it is destroyed—a damaged wall keeps the flow of intruders to a trickle, for example—but sometimes characters need do no more than damage an object."

                    The second thing going on here relates to arbitrary power levels. They exist. The storyteller decides how many health boxes her monsters have, what their defenses are, how hard they hit, and any supernatural effects they might possess. In this way, the power level of combat characters also depends entirely on the whims of the storyteller. The job of the storyteller is to decide how difficult certain challenges are, relative to the capabilities of her band, and which characters are participating in a scene.

                    The storyteller likewise decides how much Soak DV and Health any given object has.

                                            Scion also has absolutely no rules for what twenty successes on an Intelligence + Medicine roll even mean. My gut says no, but it's completely up in the air, depending on who your storyteller is. And every time I poke you to find out what your idea of the framework is, the answer is "that would be up to the ST to decide". You are not designing a game that way.

                                            Can you imagine a D&D campaign where the ST absolutely *has* to design the opposition, down to the hitpoints and hardness of structures by arbitrary decree or fiddling around with enemy statblocks from two or five levels lower? Or set the DC for trapfinding, tracking and discovering a secret door all by himself? D&D has a website with hundreds of free monsters all over the level curve, and unless your campaign is incompatible with sixteen different kinds of dragons, you can easily find something pre-made for the supported levels.
                                            My point is not that D&D is the superior game. My point is that in the design of D&D, the designers at least bothered to do their goddamned job.

With your current position, you just leave that to the improvisational ability of the ST.
