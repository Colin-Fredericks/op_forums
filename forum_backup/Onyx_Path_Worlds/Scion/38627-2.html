                                Going back to the original post - I have to agree - Shiva and Kali aren't evil. This is something that is reflected upon with Shinto, too - there are no evil gods. A god is someone who supports society, and acts in accordance to the natural world. A god of storms who wipes out a village doesn't do it out of malice or with evil intent. It is the nature of the storm to sweep in from time to time - natural calamities are seen as a part of the order of life at best, or a sign that there is an imbalance in some fashion caused by mortal activity.

                                According to Shinto (and from what I've seen, Vodou and Hinduism), good and evil are not something dealt with on a celestial scale. There's no cosmic good or cosmic evil. Evil is decided upon by society, and it is society's role to protect civilization and promote 'good'. It is not the purpose of religion to dictate what is Good or Evil, only what is harmonious, what brings spiritual balance. This has the ... perhaps uncomfortable... side-effect of stating 'you are responsible for your own actions'. You can't claim a higher force has 'forced' you to act in this fashion, if you perform an evil act, it is on your head, and nobody else's.

                                This also has the side effect of altering the perception of how the world works. A violent god is obviously angered by some action performed by mortal society. It is the job of the faithful to discover what angered the god, to appease the god, and then find the person responsible and have them show contrition. The god isn't in the wrong - not as we understand it. Instead, the god acts in accordance with its nature. As far as storytelling is concerned, it becomes a lot less about 'beating up enemy gods and monsters', as it is about 'understanding why things are happening, and doing what needs to be done'. Sure, with actual monsters, you'll see battles (Susano-O vs Orochi comes to mind), but the gods in these pantheons didn't wage war on one another, or on people, and none were 'evil'.

                                I think this is one of the benefits of the Shinto, Hindu, and Vodou faiths over some of the other ones (Norse and Egyptian come to mind). Being from a region which didn't see a lot of internal strife, there's no influence to make any specific god "evil" because they were revered by a rival faith (look at Loki and Set). I think if I'm going to run Scion, I'm removing the entire idea of the gods being 'good' or 'evil'. They are primeval forces, but ultimately, they're there to promote civilization and are a part of the natural world.

                                                It is perfectly acceptable

                                                This is an area where Hindu religion and Hindu mythology diverge somewhat. While the religion treats ALL Goddesses as manifestations of Shakti, the Supreme Feminine Energy of the universe, the mythology only pays lip service to this concept. There a re multiple scenes of the various Goddesses interacting with each other as separate beings, like the time Lakshmi and Sarasvati got into a quarrel over who is superior. Even Parvati and Kali, who are often seen as the light and dark sides of the same being, treat each other as separate, such as the time when Shiva teases Parvati about letting herself go and starting to look like Kali, and Parvati is horrified by the idea.

                                                I would say the ideal case would be to treat the older Vedic Goddesses (Ratri, Usha, Indrani, Svaha, Sanjana, Chaya etc.) as minor Goddesses in the Legend 9-10 range (the first two in that list might be bumped higher). All the post-Vedic Goddesses can be grouped as Avataras of the five main Goddesses of Hinduism, who are 1. Parvati (with Gauri, Annapurna etc. as Avataras), 2. Kali (with Chinnamasta, Tara etc. as Avataras), 3. Durga (with Shakambari, Katyayani etc. as Avataras), 4. Lakshmi (with Kamala, Tulasi etc. as Avataras) and 5. Sarasvati (with Gayatri, Matangi etc. as Avataras).

                                                Now, whether you treat the Avataras as alternate identities of these five, or as minor Goddesses in their own right, is an ST call. Both are valid interpretations, but I prefer the former.

                                                (Incidentally, if you're still interested in a Scion of Durga designed for 1e, who is very much her own Goddess in myth, her associateds would probably be Charisma, Appearance, Strength, Guardian and War. The abilities would be Melee, Control, Thrown, Fortitude, Presence and Empathy).
