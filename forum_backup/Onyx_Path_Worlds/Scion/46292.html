    What about Gods who are worshipped in more than one Pantheon?

    01-10-2014, 11:57 AM
    It's well-known that the Theoi have both their Greek and Roman personas and that their personalities and identities change a bit in their Greek and Roman aspects. At least some of them do. Ares is more the "noble warrior" kinda guy when he's Mars, for example.

    Guan Yin is worshipped both in China and Japan and, I believe, she's even revered in Hinduism. She's considered a member of both the Shen, the Kami, and she may even be welcome among the Devas.

    Mithra is a member of the Yazatas, and yet he was also worshipped by the Romans (Theoi) and even the Hindus (Devas).

    Isis was widely worshipped throughout the Roman Empire.

    There may be others that I'm just not thinking about right now, but that's enough examples for what I'm asking.

    How does Scion handle these Gods who technically belong to more than one Pantheon? Do they have a whole separate identity within each Pantheon? Or are they pretty much their "default" persona and just happen to be welcome amongst the Gods of their other Pantheons?

    Perhaps more importantly? Okay, the general rule is that Gods only get one PSP, but for a God who is considered a member of another Pantheon, can they somehow switch? I mean, Guan Yin is worshipped just as seriously in Shintoism as she is among the Shen. Does she have Taiyi, or Tsukumo-Gami? Or can she perhaps switch between those by switching between her Chinese and Japanese identities?

      The Gods you describe can be safely put into a number of categories and then dealt with appropriately.

      First, the easiest. Isis might have been widely worshipped in the Roman Empire, but she was worshipped as an Egyptian Goddess. No one tried to pass her off as a Roman deity, as far as I know. Thus, this case is not a case at all. Isis remains Egyptian and retains only Heku.

      Second, Gods like Mithra, who are worshipped in more than one Pantheon, but whose importance in one vastly outshines their importance in the other. Mithra is amongst the most important of the Yazata, but a Deva so unimportant that his very name is now better remembered as an appellation of Surya than a God unto himself. Mithra should therefore be a Yazata, and have Asha.

      Third, we have the eternally confusing Greco-Roman Pantheon. How this situation is treated depends massively on ST decisions and personal settings. If, in your settings, the Greek and Roman Pantheons are just one Pantheon, then no question arises. If, on the other hand, they are different, then two things can be done. Firstly, you may hold that Mars and Ares are two different Gods altogether, and give them their respective PSPs. Or, you may assume that the uniquely Roman Gods, like Janus or Pomona, are a Pantheon as a whole subservient to the Greek Pantheon. In this case, Ares/Mars should only have Arete. He is a Greek God being worshipped by the Romans, and his personality differences can be put down to personal quirks. Also, note that the portrayal of Mars is, obviously, more recent than that of Ares. His more mature personality can be put down to his having grown up from his Greek days.

      Finally, we have the actual problem children. The rare few Gods like Guan Yin and Quetzalcoatl who are honestly and widely worshipped as members of multiple Pantheons. I would put it down to ST choice. But I would say the best answer is: follow the mythology, not the religion. Guan Yin is widely worshipped across the Far East, but her most specific myths all occur in China, like her interaction with the Monkey King and the children of the Dragon King. Kukulkan was undoubtedly an important God for the Maya, but we have more mythology for Quetzalcoatl. Thus, they would be Shen and Teotl respectively.

      Hope that helped.

          This is a messy issue because it happens a lot. Pretty much any time cultures mesh, they'll appropriate each other's religions to some degree. With PC's, it's fairly simple. They started out as members of one Pantheon and then, later, acquired worshippers or Fatebonds that tied them to another Pantheon. You always know a PC's original Pantheon. No questions.

          It's much harder for existing Gods, because their origins aren't always clear. Some are! Epona started out Gaulish and was adopted by Rome. The Romans adopted a lot of Gods. They adopted Isis and Mithras. In cases like that, it's easy to say that they're still Gaulish, Egyptian and Persian, but they've developed cults and ties to the Roman Pantheon. It's harder with gods like Quetzalcoatl, who isn't quite the same as Kukulkan. Is he the same God with different names, or two different Gods that are just very much alike?


          I would run it individually in each case. It also depends on if you plan on your chronicle ever reaching God-level. If not, what does it matter if some Gods are able to be in multiple Pantheons and, thus, can wield different PSPs when in their different forms? Maybe when she's in Chinese territory, Guan Yin has Taiyi, but if she crosses into Shinto territory, she loses it and gains Tsukumo-Gami. That becomes problematic with PC's at God-level, since you have to run things for Gods according to the same rules that will be used for PCs.

              Nothing in Scion says people cannot worship -- and more importantly, tell stories about -- another culture's Gods. What's more, nothing in Scion says that Gods could not interact with Gods of other pantheons before the present day. So if there's a "problem" with Gods appearing in multiple cultural contexts and mythologies, it's a problem of us being neat-freaks and trying to place everything in tidy boxes, rather than a problem with the setting.

              I'd nominate Coyote as a prime example. Numerous Native American cultures told stories about Coyote. He even appears in lists of Aztec Gods. This does not mean Coyote has to be Atzlanti, or that there have to be multiple Coyotes. It just means that mortals really really liked stories about his highjinks, so those stories spread. And maybe the stories spread so much because Coyote got around and interacted with mortals of several cultures, and with their Gods.

              (I have no idea which Native American culture Coyote is most associated with, whose pantheon would be his pantheon of origin. It might not even be possible to discover, making Coyote's assignment to any particular pantheon rather arbitrary. So it goes when you try to fit messy reality into game categories.)

It's not dissimilar to how multiple Eurasian Pantheons have hot-headed thunder gods who can't keep it in their pants and (usually) have a love of drinking. The many Coyote Gods are no more the same person than Zeus, Thor, Indra and Perun are the same person.
