                I'm not talking about the general rules for automatically letting players succeed at things. I am specifically talking about the Skill Tricks of the "spend a point from the Black Pool to automatically X" variety. If you limit them to trivial tasks, you might as well remove them from the game, because nobody is gonna buy a Skill Trick and spend from the black pool for a trivial task.

                On the other hand, I'm afraid they might be too powerful as they are now. Why engage with the whole gambler's fallacy thing and accumulate a bunch of points in the black pool to try a massive roll, if I can just spend one point and automatically succeed?

                As for the Exalted comparison, just look at Lie Detector, which looks like it could be a problem for intrigue and mystery stories almost as readily as Exalted's Judge's Ears Technique. It is the same issue of investing character resources making a character great at something - thus indicating to the Director that you want your character to face that kind of challenge - and in so doing acquiring powers that just solve that challenge by themselves, without the player actually engaging with that problem in any really meaningful manner. It is one of the things the EX3 Devs explicitly set out to correct, and I hope Sardonyx* won't fall in that trap.

                Now, if you could get more information out of determining lies (why are they lying? Which part of that statement was a lie?) by rolling, or got a chance to do a superior attack (at the risk of missing) by not using Martial Artist, that might be enough to balance it somewhat, or maybe there is some other nuance of the system that I have not noticed or is not present in the previews so far that prevents the "press button to skip plot" issue of auto-success Tricks, but it still makes me weary for now.

                *By the way, the spell-checker here thinks that is not a word. Someone feed it a geology or gem-cutting book or something ^_^

                                            Originally posted by Tiresias View Post

                                            Because rolling a lot of dice means you can buy off more Complications and add more Effects to your roll.

                                            Not necessarily. It depends on what "automatically succeed" turns out to be. For instance, if it gives you enough successes to clear the difficulty, then you get to roll your dice pool for extra successes, that could end up with more than when rolling a bunch of extra dice, especially at high difficulties. Not to mention things like Lie Detector, which makes you auto-succeed and clears one Complication.

                                            I mean, OK, unless it turns out to be horribly designed, there will be times when spending the entire black pool and hope you get a fuckton of successes would be the choice over automatically succeeding. I was just exaggerating for effect (much like when I said nobody would buy a Skill Trick and spend from the black pool on a trivial task. It is likely that someone out there would actually do so). But depending on how auto-success works, that use of the pool will become a lot more of an edge case, turning the dynamic swings it would otherwise introduce into something that happens relatively rarely.

                                            That is a minor concern, though, as I don't think it will turn out that badly designed. I'm more worried about the auto-succeed Tricks resolving the plot instead of the characters doing so, which was one of the problems that complicated my Exalted games. I mean, there is nothing there like "I want my Eclipse to be a terrorist / revolutionary! I will take all these social Charms here. Ok, I wanna start by influencing the local artistic circle, fostering among them resentment towards the ruling family. After that I will join that guild so I can subvert it to my own ends and..." "NO, no. Look at your Charms, you just need to give a single pamphlet to any random peasant and BOOM! Revolution!" but I'm still seeing the potential for trouble, especially in the Procedural and Intrigue areas.
