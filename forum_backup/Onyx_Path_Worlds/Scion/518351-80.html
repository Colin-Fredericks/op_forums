                                            I think there is a difference between Mortal beliefs doesn't affect gods and Gods don't require mortal belief. Mortals affect gods plenty via Fatebinding. It locks them into various modes of actions and limits their choices.

                                            Gods don't require mortals is more along the lines of an independence of power separate from mortal prayer/faith. Just because no one has prayed to [god of your choice] in two thousand years doesn't mean that their power has waned to any point or become close to mortal like some modern stories are trying to conflate. Zeus is Zeus. He's was a Legend 12 (or whatever he works out to be in 2nd Ed) powerful god in Ancient Greece seven thousand years ago and remains so today. He doesn't require the belief or worship of mortals to sustain his power...although if he had worshipers it would do something for him (if I remember the conversations correctly he may get extra temporary Legend points/other beneficial effects).

                                                    Originally posted by Egg View Post
                                                    Do we have any information on what will the Gods of the Manitouk Pantheon be?
                                                    Some tentative research let me put together this list of likely candidates

                                                    Nanabozho (who is also Michabo, the Great Hare - Wisakedjak, and Gluscap)

                                                    Chibiabos (Wolf, god of the dead, possibly also Malsum to Gluscap, though that villainous legend is disputed)

                                                    Nokomis (Grandmother Woodchuck)

                                                    Kichi-Odjig (the Great Fisher)

                                                    Biboon (the North Wind)

                                                    First Mother (the Corn Maiden found in many native american legends even beyond the Algonquin groups; alternatively could be male to the Ojibwa and be Mandaamin - both cases sacrifices self to become corn)

                                                    Tshakapesh (Man in the Moon)



                                                    You've also got, particularly among the western Algonquin groups like the Ojibwe, Thunderbird (Animikii/Binesi) and Horned Serpent (Misingenbig). I don't know how Neall's planning to handle them other than he has plans. They're even more prominent among the legends of the Plains Indians, but obviously overlap through cultural exchange.


                                                    And then there's Gitchi Manitou Him/Itself. My guess is that He/It will be left a distant unmanifest divinity referred to by different names by different Pantheons.
