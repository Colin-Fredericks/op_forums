                                    There are actually two characters from Hindu myth one of we would today call transgender and another is probably genderfluid.

                                    Shikhandi from the Mahabharata was born with female genitals but identified as a man and was brought up as such (through Shiva's directives to his father), even being married to a princess and receiving male genitals from a friendly forest spirit known as a yaksha to consummate the marriage. Shikhandi had been the princess Amba in his previous life, who had sworn to kill the warrior Bhishma for kidnapping her. Through a boon from Shiva she was born as Shikhandi, who went to war with the Pandava in the Kurukshetra was (his father was Drupada, father of Draupadi, meaning he was the Pandava's brother-in-law). There he stood in front of Bhishma and Bhishma, who is apparently transphobic, said he would not fight a 'woman' and put down his bow, at which point Arjuna and Shikhandi shot him down.

                                    Ila, whose story is a minor tale in the Mahabharata, was born the eldest daughter (sometimes eldest child) of Vaivasvata Manu, was transformed to have male genitalia at birth by the magic of the Gods Mitra and Varuna and brought up as a boy, Sudyumna. Upon reaching adulthood, he entered a sacred grove to Parvati whose magic changed his sex again and Parvati modified the magic somewhat so that Ila/Sudyumna would spend a month as a man and a month as a woman with attendant sex change. And Ila/Sudyumna seems to have spent most of the next couple of years switching from male to female. Of course, ancient writers being what they are and not having the same understanding of sex and gender we do hir gender switches are linked to sex changes but I like to believe Ila/Sudyumna is honestly genderfluid. Anyway, Ila becomes mother to the king Pururavas by Budha (not Buddha) God of the Planet Mercury, a son of the Moon God Chandra. Pururavas would eventually become the founder of the Lunar Dynasty that the Pandavas and their cousins the Kauravas belong to, and Ila eventually attained enlightenment and attained oneness with the Goddess Durga.

                                    It is also to note that most male gods have feminine forms. Vishnu's Mohini Avatara is probably the most famous, though Vishnu also has Vaishnavi. Brahma and Shiva similarly have Brahmani and Shivani (also known as Maheshavari). Skanda has Kaumari, and Ganesha has Vinayaki. Indra has Aindri and so on. The Goddesses don't have any similar male forms usually...however, in some small strains of Hinduism Krishna is considered an Avatara not of Vishnu, but of Kali, so that counts (and Radha in this case becomes and Avatara of Shiva).

                                    All in all, while Hinduism doesn't entirely define sex and gender identities the way we do in modern times (like most mythologies) I think it's safe to say that the Hindu gods will not have much in the way of hang-ups when it comes to transgender or genderfluid characters.

                                                Then again, unless you intend to run a game where women, minorities, and LGBTQ characters (and players) are discriminated against, it's probably best to go with the idea that the various deities are open to gay rights, women's equality, and the rights of people from other cultures.
