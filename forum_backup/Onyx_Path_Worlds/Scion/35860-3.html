    Bear in mind that when I suggested the “distantly related” theory, I referenced the Mitochondrial Eve or one of her ancestors being a Scion — and thus every human being living on the planet today being “distantly related to the Gods”. The whole point of the Champions is that it isn’t any sort of unique heritage that empowers them, and anyone could turn out to be a Champion. There would probably be theories that Champions are more likely among the more immediate descendants of Scions; but there would be no evidence to support (or refute) such theories.

                                            In Telgar's defence, Scion's mythological background lends itself to an elitist feel the way Vampire encourages a horror one. World Mythology makes it very clear that the Gods are on a level above humanity. Humanity might (rarely) reach divinity, but most of the time it's by accident or because an already existing God took an interest in them. Even non-Scion heroes like Jason and Odysseus only succeed through divine help.

                                            Now, if this were a game like Mage, then a human being (eventually) forcing Zeus to wash his car or Isis to do the dishes would be totally in line with the core concepts of the game, and would be awesome. But Scion has a different source, and thus different themes. Of course, you can totally run Vampire as a Romantic Comedy if you want, and similarly you can run Scion as a game of humanism, but that wouldn't be entirely true to mythology.

                                                    Right; but as I see it, Legendary Beings (Santa Claus, Uncle Sam, etc.) would be “sushi”. Champions, Immortals, and Godborn are more like “grilled vegetables”, “salmon”, and “ribs”: they’re the sorts of dishes that you’d expect to find at a steakhouse, to be eaten with or instead of steaks as the customer sees fit. They’re not “extra settings”; they’re the same setting from different, complementary perspectives. And far from detracting from the development of the default setting, they would enrich it: a steakhouse that serves nothing but, say, roast beef would go out of business rather quickly: there would be a few diehards who want just that one dish and nothing else; but most people want more variety than that.

                                                    This isn’t something that has nothing to do with the scope of the game. Most of the ideas I’m presenting here are already in the game; they’re just not presented as playable characters. And the one that technically isn’t in the game fills what I consider to be a gap in the game: the numerous heroes of mythology who were not children of the gods. I keep coming back to Jason because he’s perhaps the most blatant example of a mortal hero who keeps stride with heroic scions; but he is far from the only one. Legendary Mortals don’t cover this, because they’re quickly outclassed by even Heroic scions; and the blood-brothers in Companion (I forget what they’re called) are explicitly in a subservient position relative to Heroic scions, which doesn’t fit the likes of Jason or Odysseus.
