    Originally posted by Crying View Post
    I don't really like this one very much, to be honest.

    I'm not overly fond of the humor and how some of the deities wwere portrayed, but those are small problems that can be ignored. The big issues I have are that I feel like it's overly positive - like every other Pantheon, the Shen can be huge dickwads and I feel like, other than mentioning that they are often egotistical, this isn't really represented - and the presence of... how do I put it... I can't think of the right word, but the fact that there are sentences like "...which Chang’e herself attributes to the eyes of sexism on a woman who knows what she wants..." or "...he’s realized how much toxic masculinity colors his actions and feelings." displease me.

    Maybe it's just because I lean further towards cynical side of the spectrum in regards to how the gods behave than most people on this forum seem to, but this one didn't sit right with me.

    I readily admit that it seems very light and romantic, but this is something that Chinese authors do as well. The past and myths are told in mostly positive lights in favor of the good deities over the disreputable ones, until they get better and then they are seen positively as well. Some contemporaries argue that harmony is just a nice way of explaining things we don't understand. Or that such order is actually deceptive of what is really a chaotic existence.

    But I don't mind the levity, even if it is deceptive, simply because it matches the human ability to do the same. And a pleasant face can be much more sinister when it hides an ugly heart.

    How can I know if what I claim I know to be true is rejecting the idea that there is something I might not know? How can I know if what I claim I don't know to be true is rejecting the idea that there is something I do know?
    -Zhuangzi

I ring the fortune bells, and purchase the dao prayer strips. I pray and acknowledge various deities according to custom. Chinese New Year is important so I have to avoid cleaning the house for that period. I grew up in a house with a shrine in one corner, so I have one in my house as well.

                So am I superstitious to believe in such silly notions and rituals when I should focus on the facts and logic of our world? Or perhaps I find nothing wrong about accepting them both at the same time.
