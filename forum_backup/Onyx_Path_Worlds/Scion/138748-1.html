    Ok! Sorry for the delay (damm you, real life) Anyway, here i am again, with news and reports about the Tuatha!

    As i was pretty excited with the new and fresh rework of Enech, i conviced our ST to run for us a brief Scion session in Nuada's court this weekend. Long story short, the plot was Dagda's uprising against Nuada in a critical moment: a reunion between the Aesir and the Irish Gods. The reason behind the encounter was the extermination of the alliance between the Fomorians and the Northern Giants. Morrigan had forseen the destruction of the Tuatha's King in the following battle, and so Dagda tried to dethrone Nuada, in order to assume himself the King's death.

    Anyway. The players picked both Tuatha's and Aesir Gods (all of them formerly Scions, having reached Apotheosis in the last Chronicle) to play. Without further delay, these are our impressions:

    Great things:

    -The new feeling of Enech. The Tuatha have now that Irish vibrant feeling. The new Enech contributes to the feeling of playing with characters larger than life, boisterous and passionate. It's much, much more energetic now (old Enech always felt somewhat oppresive if you get my meaning: sometimes it felt like it was more punishment than anything else) and, well, organic: the system of Face is really dynamic.

    -Right now Enech improves the social intercourse in a scene, and through the use of some Boons (specially Dúshlán Mór (••••• ••), we loved that one so much) the story developes by itself, something we really appreciate. It eases pressure for the ST when it's needed. Great tool.

    -I'm going to say it again: Dúshlán Mór: awesome. Whoever thought of it, you have our love. Take it.


    Doubts:

    - Feabhas (••): Can you use it in conjunction with 10, in order to magnify even more that last power?

    - Altram (••••): Feels more like a Merit than a Enech Boon. Maybe it could be better to link it to the transition to Demigod directly, sort of a xp-free, special Tuatha bonus?



    Details that could use a little love:

    - Maybe make some graduation between Geas. You can have anything as a Gea, so it would be nice if harder Geas had better rewards.

    - We missed one power that directly taped into Fate itself, as Irish have this powerful conection to Fate. I'll write more on that later.

    - Luigh Geasa (••••• •): Players that get Geas but don't obtain Face benefits tend to feel it as a punishment or limitation, rather than something to help them evolve their characters concepts. One suggestion here:

    - Maybe you could consider to give them a benefit (albeit a marginal one), apart from the special social bonus with Tuatha. Maybe the Geas this power creates could help to recover Legend, for instance, to Tuatha and foreigners alike. Thus, it would help to mitigate the heavy cost of Bua (••••• •••••), something we really feel necessary.

    - Mórgatch (••••• •••••): The specialization concept about this Boon was original and greatly appreciated. (One of the players, in fact, suggested that this could be done to all the Gods, or at least possess some Knack or Bonn so inherently tied to each God nature that, once reached Apotheosis, could become free). If you are familiar with Mage the Awakening, could be something similar to Attainements. In fact, if that power (whoever it would be, it surely should have some limitations like don't take a powerful and offensive boon, like in MtA.) Alternatively, you could discount as much Legend as the Legend Rating of the God. If the cost reach zero, that's it.


    - Bua (••••• •••••): Powerful and impressive. Once played like your opening gambit is in fact awesome. But there's a problem with it. Using it feels... unworthed. Alas, you can perform ONE impressive action... at the price of being unable to do anything later. That forced supression feels anticlimatic. Moreso, if things get twisted and everybody starts tapping into Avatar.... oh, you're fucked. No Legend, no Avatar. No Avatar... well. Oh and one doubt: please clarify that "such an action is written into Fate". Has some mechanical benefit to that? If not, maybe it should. We missed some Enech directly messing with Fate.

    - Our suggestion regarding Bua? Create some form of rapidly harvesting Legend if the action feels right enough, maybe via Luigh Geasa.

    Anyway, great work. Congrats, team, and thank you for the closeness and the involvement with us fans!

A Geas is something which has no up side except that you can show the world how awesome you are, for look at what great deeds you accomplished while at the same time imposing great limitations on yourself.

        Also, we are trying to get away from Legend regaining stuff in Scion 2.0. We are going to take a knife to Itztli, and you will only be regaining Legend back from sacrifices at God to better fit in line with the Aztec cosmology, since only the Gods receive the power from sacrifices. (Griffinguy will be helping us with it) We will still encourage sacrifices through other ways (the Scion regains Virtue channels, gains 'favor' with a God [not sure how we will do that one], supercharges their Willpower, and so on), but you won't be getting Legend back until Legend 9.

                Originally posted by Phersus View Post

                No worries, I can really understand the confusion. Old Enech made it seem that way, but they are very separate ideas that tend to collide only when there needs to be a tragedy. This is because of how dear the Irish held one's Social standing, so, therefore, having one's Geasa be the cause of one's doom was deeply cutting in the Irish mentality. So, it's not so much as them being connected, but the Geas being an easy medium to the idea of social standing, and causing the cruel Fate to be even more heart wrenchingly tragic.

                But, The Morrigan and the CuChulain situation was actually because of the prophecy that CuChulain bound himself to. CuChulain allowed himself have the prophecy of being the greatest warrior in Ireland, but to be doomed to die young (Very much like the Achilles Prophecy). So, in order for that to come true, CuChulain refused The Morrigan, who took it unto herself to ruin his shit. Leading to CuChulain killing his lover/foster brother, breaking his Geasa, and eventually dying. In the process, he showed the world that he was the greatest warrior in Ireland, but his death was separate form his Face for all facts but he broke his Geasa to further make his death a tragedy.

                The idea that if you break a Geas you will just die soon is a inconsistent one unfortunately. Sometimes people seem to have that looming over their head, and sometimes they don't. Some people break Geas and die, and some don't. So, in order to make it playable, we went with it just not spelling your immediate doom.

                What was likely the root of this idea of it spelling your doom is that if you broke a Geas, you would likely have been socially ostracized, and thrown out of your tribe/kingdom/ect, which is something that would spell your doom since in a Bronze Age wilderness, you really need a society to support you if you want to survive.

                        Alrighty! Sorry about the delay, I had a bit of a medical problem but now I'm back on my feet able to ramble about this stuff.

                        Okay, so. The status of women in pre-Christian Ireland is really complicated, as we don't strictly have anything purely from the period itself. Sort of the problem we always have. The Brehon Laws are one of the more trusted sources, but even they are not recorded until after the Christianization process has already been more-or-less finished. We do know that the Celts were not a Super Feminist, Peace-Loving, Nature-Worshiping, Classless Society as was the popular image of them circa the 1970-80s, but women had it fairly well? I mean, being a woman in the ancient world generally sucked, but Ireland wasn't the worst place. Upper-Class men did practice Polygamy, but it's kinda unclear exactly to what degree this was since the chieftians/kings were entering into sexual-religious relationships with local Tutelary Deities which means they are probably sitting at at least two wives each. Except one of them is, well, a Deity and probably isn't really around the place. One of the base units of wealth, the Cumal is a female slave, three of which is worth one cow, which doesn't speak particularly highly of the status of women.

                        However, at the same time of all of this, women seem to be doing some pretty awesome, radical stuff, especially in the literature. Queen Medb is a queen in her own right, using her sexuality to get her way. She is independently powerful from her husband, and wields so much influence she brings all of Ireland to war with Ulster, and emerges victorious against CuChulain even when he has The Morrigu (or, well, 2/3 of The Morrigus. Nemean and Badb are on CuChulain's side helping him in the battles, but The Morrigan ends up working against him) and Lugh on his side. (Technically, while she does win, the magical bull she wanted ends up getting killed like instantly after making the whole war pointless) She is a badass, powerhouse of a figure. She obviously has a huge amount of personal honor, wielding it with horrific capability. She shames others into helping, uses her own position as a political tool, is fiercely independent, everything.

                        At the same time, however, Medb can be seen to have direct control of her daughter's body. Medb uses her daughter Findabair's body as a tool to get other people to do what she wants. She destroys Findabair's own personal agency in the face of her own political goals, constantly offering up Findabair's body and promising to marry her to one person or another to get people to make the suicidal feat of trying to kill CuChulain.

                        Other instances of women with massive amounts of personal honor / Enech would be Macha, Emer, and Scathach. Macha's personal honor is why she ends up cursing the Ulstermen. She is challenged, her husband accidentally drops the fact that she can run faster than horses, and the King demands to see this. Macha, despite being nine months pregnant, can't just say 'no'. Sure, the king would kill her husband for big-talk if she didn't run, but that isn't why she runs. She does it because she is challenged, her honor is on the line. And, when her honor is then insulted by having to give birth in the mud before of all of the Ulstermen, none of which try to help I will point out, she levies a curse on them. The story of Macha is a story of Enech, her upholding her personal honor, and unleashing a massively powerful war-curse upon an entire kingdom when that honor is insulted.

                        Emer is slightly different. She is the best of woman, the peak, the height, the paragon of being a woman. (You will notice, being 'The Best' is a very common theme in Irish literature) In the story of Bricriu's Feast, we see Enech playing the main role. The trickster Bricriu manages to bring Ulster to its knees through manipulating the greatest heroes of Ulster into fighting each other over who is the greatest. He does the same with the greatest women of Ulster, the wives of these three champions, each of whom viciously argues and verbally spars with each other over which of them is greatest. Emer emerges victorious here, and she is considered without a doubt the greatest woman of Ireland. When Fand tries to carry CuChulain away, it is Emer rolling up with fifty of her handmaidens all armed to the teeth with knives that scares the shit out of both CuChulain and Fand. And after some degree of threatening, Fand leaves with Manannan saying that Emer and CuChulain deserve only each other as they are the perfect man and woman respectively. Even when Emer and CuChulain are, like, eight years old (because keep in mind, The Morrigan tries to jump CuChulain's bones when he's like... 12? 11? Really young, young enough he's not considered a man, and thus not targeted by the curse) Emer tests CuChulain, the pair of them speaking to each other in riddles, flirting with each other in a way that none of Emer's handmaidens / wardens know what they are talking about. I would argue that Emer is just as Honor-dependent as CuChulain is, the pair of them perfect for each other as they are the paragons of their sexes. (And Emer being the 'paragon' shows you a lot of what a high-status woman would be able to acomplish, at least from a literary perspective, which is a lot. Like, shaming kings, shaming Gods, threatening a Fairy Queen with a knife if she doens't step off her husband)

                        And then Scathach has Enech in a way that may be more familiar, she is a martial paragon. She is the greatest trainer of heroes, and has high standards for her apprentices. CuChulain is the Best Hero, so he has to be trained by the Best Trainer. That best trainer happens to be a woman, and the text doesn't really make any point of this past the sexual angle that goes on. Scathach has an enormous amount of Enech, her personal honor so high that she is The Best, and by being trained by her, CuChulain can be The Best. If he hadn't been trained by her, that would be a mark against his honor. So, I would argue that Scathach has a massive amount of personal honor, she is certainly swimming in Enech. She's not even a local, she's got so much honor that some kid in central-Northern Ireland knows that he has to travel to Scotland, through the Shadowlands (poorly explained past the name) to reach her. Her reputation travels far.

                        So, that is why I would argue that women are just as legitimate users of Enech as men. There might be slightly different standards of what being Excellent means if one wants to be a paragon of their sex, but if you want to be a paragon of a profession, your sex won't matter.

                        ----

                        Okay, now for the second question! Could a woman Scion of the Tuatha inherit things as a Deity. Sure, absolutely. The Tuatha might not actually obey the Brehon Laws, those were brought by The Sons of Mil. I don't know if there are any references to any of the Tuatha being Brehons. And, even if they are, people don't follow the rules all the time. If you are rich and powerful, you can do what you want. If you just became Ri, the normal task at hand is to kill all of your male siblings, and uncles, and nephews. Unless you like them, in which case just blind them or otherwise main them. The Brehon Laws don't want people doing that, but it was not uncommon, a good way to cement your power.

                        So, if, for example, Lugh had a daughter who entered... whoever your Storyteller is running as the current Ri of the Pantheon's hall, and gained a position in the tribal society, Lugh could totally give her whatever he wanted on his (second) death. She might need to fight for it since inheritance fights are normal, or Lugh might need to make it public knowledge that he wants her to inherit XYZ, but it would be fine. If you are big, powerful, and influential, you can ignore the rules more or less.

                        Women inheriting land was not super common, but we do have two examples I know of form literature. Medb, as discussed above, is granted an entire Kingdom to herself by... her father I believe. Her father or husband, but I think father. And then there is another example of a woman becoming a Ri... oh, what's her name. Macha Mong-ruiad, yeah. She rules as Ri for a while at one point after her father dies, and waging successful war against those who disagreed with her. So, for a Scion, it should be no difficulty at all.

                        ---

                        And for the last bit, some Fairy Queens are claimed to have some ownership of land most likely because they were probably not always Fairy Queens. They were probably originally local Tutelary Deities, local Soverignty figures for the regional chieftians, that sort of thing. So they owned the land just like Macha owned Ulster, or Eriu and her sisters owned Ireland as a whole, kinda-sorta-sorta-kinda.

                        I hope all of this answers your question though! I think it's totally appropriate for a woman to use Enech, just as much as a man. But, if you are still uncomfortable with it, I can try to help you brainstorm a new PsP for the Tuatha if you'd like.
