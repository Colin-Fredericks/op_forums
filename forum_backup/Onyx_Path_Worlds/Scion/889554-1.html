                Originally posted by Neall View Post

                I don't see how anything in Scion 2e is going to be difficult to implement in an online game, assuming everyone keeps track of their pools. Fate does just fine online.



                Scion 2e's rules aren't fully written yet, much less balanced out.



                I'm sorry you feel that way, but a patch wouldn't solve Scion 1e's core fundamental issues. Scion 2.0 is excellent and I admire that team's work, but they're building on a rotten foundation. You're not wrong in calling it Scion 1.5.

                Better to rip the whole thing out and rebuild it.

                Ajax, I'm sorry if some of the rules so far don't please you, but there will be a rules release around the time of the Kickstarter after some playtesting has been done, so you can judge for yourself. Online play is something I take pretty seriously since it's the primary way I play tabletop games nowadays too. If you've got concerns, feel free to reach out to me and say something or start a thread. A number of the playtesters I've talked to are online-only so far, so I think any issues with online play will get brought up so we can address them and smooth them out.
                In my experience and as far as I have been able to tell by reaching out to those I know, Fate doesn't do well as Fate online. It does Fate as a DRPG. (Which admittedly is a lot like what Fate is anyways.) The difficult to manage mechanic get dropped. Which is problematic if the mechanic is core. From the material I have read there is a grouping aspect to these tokens that would be effortless at a table FTF but is going to require some creative workaround to manage online. If I am not getting the rules involved or there has been a change since that bit was published, I apologize, but our group was looking through the bits that are out there and it kept coming up.

                Since I'm not a playtester or a developer, I can only address what you have posted for us to read. I'm going on that. It's not like looking at the bits of Wraith20 that come out. There's no context to fall back on. This is all new from the ground up. All I can understand is what you have put out there and the comments the developers make afterwards. So I know I'm not getting an accurate read, but it's the only read I CAN get. And rules like Consolation and mantles are worrisome from what I have seen. Consolation seems like a bad idea all around. It's not setting appropriate. What Consolation did Icarus get? Orpheus? Sigurd? Balder? Miach? Etc. Consolation seems to remove actual failure from the equations. Sometimes myths aren't about "failing forward". They're about failing. Catastrophically. Mantles might be the salvation of the game, but they could could just as easily make it very much like the worst aspects of Fate, e.g. he who has the best bullshit wins. The new Virtues seem so vague as to be be ethereal. Old Virtues had their problems, but they were an actual THING, with benefits and drawbacks that had an effect. The could be played up or played down, but they were THERE. The new ones? I don't see them as being anything other than a background concept that can possibly be referenced when it seems appropriate, but, really, not often.

                The core system of Scion 1.0 was a cobbled together mess. I agree. It was built on the White Wolf engine with parts of Adventure bolted on here, Aberrant spliced in there, Exalted threaded through some of the gaps like twine, etc. But that's different than completely rotten. I can't buy that. It's based on the White Wolff engine and, to one degree or another, that's been functioning through several game lines for over twenty years. If you want to go a whole other direction, that's fine, but that doesn't mean that Scion couldn't have been reworked from the ground up from the fundamentals, From my days as a lawyer, I can easily understand going another direction for purely IP reasons or taking that hat off, for purely creative reasons, but I don't think that it was actually impossible. Whether or not I agreed with everything they were doing, Watcher, Aynie, et al. were trying to do so fairly successfully.

                I'm not giving up 100% (though I have thought about about and have discussed it with those who have), but my eye is wary one at this point. I don't think my concerns are unreasonable. As you have said, this IS going to be a completely different game with, essentially the same name and some fundamental conceits in common. That IS a big change to make. CoD isn't really the NWOD, just like 2nd Ed. AD&D isn't really 3rd or 3.5 which are not 4th or 5th. Scion 1 and Scion 2 are going to be a similar shift.

                                RE: Tokens by Google - Messy. Unnecessary. Could have been avoided effortlessly. It doesn't appreciably add to the feel of the game mechanically or by achieving the goals of a game about living through the creation of new myths. Most myths when the demigods and heroes got together in groups aren't about the group's accomplishment in any case. It's about the amazing things each member does that contribute to the goal (vide the Argonauts or the 108 Stars).

                                RE: Virtues, New and Old - Just because you didn't use them doesn't mean others didn't and they didn't add appreciably to the games. They did in the ones I ran and in the ones I played in. They made the character of each pantheon very distinctive and something that HAD to be taken into account in gameplay. Having a sheet of those Virtues reduced to bullet points was MUCH easier than the proposed online token tracking system. The new Virtue system is going to move the whole idea into the background and color it with appreciable amounts of vagueness. More like D&D games where alignment was a couple of letters on a character sheet and rarely referenced. (Not that it COULDN'T be important, but it just wasn't almost all the time). Probably even more vaguel than that. More like Essence in Mage. That is out there but so intangible as to be hard to even bring into play without a lot of effort.
