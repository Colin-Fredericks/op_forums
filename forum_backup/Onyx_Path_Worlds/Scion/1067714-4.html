    Originally posted by Watcher View Post

    That would be because Anansi isn't actually one of the Orisha! The Orisha are the Pantheon of the Yoruban culture, while Anansi is a figure from a different cultural group which is best known for the Ashanti Empire. They have a very different religious system from the Yoruban peoples to their south, and are semi-Monotheistic with an Supreme God named Nyame, assisted by a host of other supernatural entities called Abossom.

    Interestingly, Anansi isn't a Deity, he isn't worshiped by the Ashanti, but he is involved in some versions of the creation myth, and is heavily involved in the folkloric Spider Stories. There is more than enough figures in the Pantheon of the Ashanti however to put together a nice Pantheon for Anansi to hang out in though! Myself and Sacerdos have done a bunch of research for them, and have some rough drafts of the Pantheon finished. They are a really cool group.
    There's no evidence Loki was a worshipped deity either, but he clearly plays the narrative role of a divinity in Norse myths. So as Scion's about myth and not religion, Anansi still strongly fits into the divinity role as a trickster culture hero child of higher divinities.

        Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
        There's no evidence Loki was a worshipped deity either, but he clearly plays the narrative role of a divinity in Norse myths. So as Scion's about myth and not religion, Anansi still strongly fits into the divinity role as a trickster culture hero child of higher divinities.
        Ah, I think I didn't explain myself well enough! Yeah, we don't have any evidence of Loki being worshiped, or Lugh, but Anansi is specifically not worshiped by the Ashanti. Their religious system has never died out, their King, Osei Tutu II, is still a Divine Monarch, he rules with the will of Nyame through the Golden Stool. The Abosom are still worshiped, their ancestral spirits are still worshiped, Nyame, and Asase Ya are still worshiped. But Anansi, very specifically, is not.

        So, for sure, Anansi will make an awesome Divine Parent, but he's not a God. Like, in the strict sense, not in the 'gameplay' sense. The Abosom probably don't like him very much, his role in the stories is a "And this is why you shouldn't be a bad person, look what happens to Anansi," sort of thing. But he'd be a totally viable Divine Parent. His cultural group just doesn't regard him as a God. Never was, and still isn't. So, when we get the Abosom all written up, Anansi will totally be a Divine Parent option, but the Abosom don't consider him to be a God. He's a weird... other thing.

        Technically, none of the Abosom are Gods, only Nyame is. But, Anansi is specifically not one of the Abosom, the beings who fill the role of 'Gods' in other cultures. Anansi's just his own thing. Very powerful, creaiton-myth powerful, tricking an all-seeing, all-knowing (sorta, it's tricky), all-powerful God powerful. But, technically not an Abosom.

        We just really feel like it's important to make note of the difference to be respectful to the Ashanti people since it's a living religion. It'll make more sense when we post the entire thing for sure, I have written a big long section on the Nature of Divinity for the Ashanti since it's a bit tricky.

                                Originally posted by Center-of-All View Post
                                I was somehow under the impression that the Haida raven was named Nankil'slas. Was I misinformed, or do they have two ravens? Or one with two names?
                                Absolute ton of names! Like, so many. Names have a special position in Haida culture, more names, more important, that sort of thing in basic. It is really interesting. NAñkî’lsLas is a name that Xhuuya goes by during the very end of the creation myth. I tend to refer to him as Xhuuya since it is his first name, and because NAñkî’lsLas is the problem-entity I eluded to at the end there. There might be another person called NAñkî’lsLas, or Xhuuya might also be that person, but in that case he was two different people at once, one of whom didn't realize he was the same person, and he was his own heir, except the other Supernatural Beings respect the elder-NAñkî’lsLas more than they do Xhuuya-NAñkî’lsLas which suggests they might not be the same person? (Another edit for clarity here. While this sort of thing may sound standard for mythology, people having multiple bodies, it's unprecedented in Haida myth, and the section where it comes up is super unclear in the different translations I have read.)

                                It's... really confusing, and there doesn't seem to be any real consensus internally. It's my current 'thing' I'm fiddling with and trying to research. Not much luck sadly.

                                (If you are interested in Xhuuya's other names, here is a list! NAñkî’lsLas (Nang Kilstlas, translated as Voicehandler) Nang Kilstlas Hlingaay (translated as Voicehandler’s Heir), Xhuuya (translated as Raven), The One We Speak Of (the Haida don't always give Xhuuya a name since the general consensus is he doesn't deserve the names he has. He isn't... seen in a very good light), and Wī’gît.)

                                Edit: Oh, and if anyone would like to read some Haida mythology, check out A Story As Sharp As A Knife: The Classical Haida Mythtellers And Their World, and Nine Visits to the Mythworld, both collections of traditional oral accounts from the turn of the 20th century, recorded with permission, and translated by Robert Bringhurst. Lovely reads.
