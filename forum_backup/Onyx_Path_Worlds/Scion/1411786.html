              My way to see the world is basically it’s the natural world as we know it.

              Terrae Incognitae are a bit tricky, most of them you need to know how to get into or have some divine interaction to get there by mistake. For example, to go to Alfheim you need to know how to climb Yggdrasil, and to do that you need get to some special places or a help from a god to reach it, while to get to Arcadia (the mythical one, not the region in Greece) you need to be in the right area and “get lost” in the right way.

              The god realms are another thing, they are not Terrae Incognitae, even when they have a physical location in the world (Mount Olympus) or in the cosmology (Asgaard), you need to get to the right place AND do some trick that only gods and maybe a demigod with the proper purview can do.

                  I'm going fucking bonkers with it. Myth is fucking bonkers. Embrace it. Yeah, there's hundreds of Gods that claim to be moving the sun. They're all right, from a certain point of view. Don't worry too much about it.

                  For most lay people, the World is much like our world. The thing to keep in mind is that like... Through the VAST majority of human history, the myths and legends and religions were known to be true. Ask the people of Hellenic Greece about Centaurs, they'd go "Yeah, centaurs exist. They're like, over there-" and gesture Off That-a-Way. They exist, everyone knows it, it's just kind of accepted.

                  And I am very much including the present in this. Like, ask any of the religious people today if they doubt their religions.

                  You'll probably get more answers than the number of people you ask. In the World of Scion, it's all true, at once. Athena at some point may have been a Hero who eventually achieved Apotheosis, but now she is a Goddess, and Always Has Been, because Fate and her Legend declare it so. Truth is mutable, the World runs on Story-Around-The-Campfire Logic (which is even more fluid than Action Movie Logic), go wild.
