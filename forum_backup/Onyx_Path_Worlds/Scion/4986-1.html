    So what are you calling magic? PSPs? Boons? Something else? Right now, each Pantheon's magic does represent their culture via their PSP, their signature-styled Boons and the Birthrights they bestow. If you're talking about something as complex as a whole second system of powers designed to reflect each culture's views of what "magic" is, I think that's going too far and making the game too complex.

    Too much diversity can be bad because it makes the game system cluttered and confusing. Scion already has a lot of issues, and I'd rather see a very strong, well-designed Purview system that works for all Pantheons and then can be customized on a per-character and per-Pantheon basis with unique, signature Boons and Birthrights. Don't forget that we also have Knacks and Epics in Scion that represent things almost as "magical" as Purviews, especially the Social Knacks and Mental Knacks since they often stand in for the lack of an Emotion/Love Purview and a Crafting Purview.

    Does Scion really need three different systems of powers? Rituals, Boons and Knacks all together? That seems like overkill to me, and just another thing to spend XP on. I agree, it'd be nice if Loa felt like Loa and Egyptians like Egyptians, but I think the ability to do that already exists, it just isn't being used as well as it could be. There aren't enough Boons, especially unique signature Boons like Fury of War and Riastriad. In fact, those are just about the only signature Boons because no one but the Norse got their own book. With more Boons added in each Purview, they can feel more "magical" and take over a lot of the things the Magic Purview used to do. With more Relics and other Birthrights, they can add more flavor.

    I want to see diversity and uniqueness, but I don't think a whole new system is the way to add that.

    I'm a professor! Why is no one listening to me?!

                Perhaps if the notion of Relics and Stunts were generalized a bit from their intuitive definitions (e.g., “Relics” that represent a type of thing that can be fairly easily acquired and lost, rather than unique items that you don’t dare lose, and “Stunts” that have nothing to do with attempting something spectacular), then yes, they could be used as ways of at least partially implementing a magic system. Even aside from the counterintuitive use of terms, I don’t think it would be quite enough (e.g., it doesn’t give a means for Origins' minimal-Legend sorcerers and holy men to practice magic); but it’s an interesting idea.

                Speaking of unintuitive names, I should probably speak of Practices rather than Rituals when discussing the “magic as Birthright” idea. Rituals are just one of several possible conditions that a sorcerer might need to meet in order to access a Boon through a Practice; and the selection of what sorts of conditions would need to be met would be a key part of defining the feel of the magic. I could probably write up the whole system I’m thinking of in one or two pages, possibly less.

                And those aren’t the only options. I’m sure the developers have their own ideas on the subject, and they are likely to have nothing in common with either of the proposals we’ve made.

This defines how you use your Boons: you channel them through your Relics. The suggestion I’m considering here is to treat the practice of magic as an alternative to channeling your Boons through your Relics. That doesn’t limit options; it adds options.

                        Originally posted by Telgar View Post
                        I would rather not see such a thing and leave it up to individual STs and players to determine how a Boon functions and consider any ritualized practices to be stunts and other situational bonuses, not an entirely new system of powers. Maybe the Loa doesn't want to use voodoo and he wants to practice techno-sympathetic magic via microchips and HTML. Leave that up to him.
                        “Magic works exactly how you imagine it works” is a Mage: the Ascension thing, as I see it. Given that Scion is all about fidelity to myths and legends, magic in Scion should work the way the myths and legends say it works1. Techno-sympathetic magic has about as much of a place in Scion as a Pantheon featuring Santa Claus or Uncle Sam.

                        Now, should a Loa be locked into just voodoo as her means of practicing magic? That depends on your views on mixing cultural elements in a single character. I could maybe see a Loa who has studied magic at the feet of a mentor who is aligned with the Aesir and thus adhering to the trappings and practices of Norse sorcery; but it shouldn’t be that she uses runecrafting instead of voodoo on a whim. Likewise, there’s the question of how compatible different styles of magic are: can a sorcerer practice both voodoo and runecrafting? I’d be inclined to say no; but I can see decent arguments for allowing it, concept permitting. Either way, it would be an implementation detail, to be decided by the developers and overruled by the Storyteller if he disagrees — just like the Storyteller is free to introduce a techno-sympathetic magic style or the likes of Santa Claus into his game if he so chooses.

                        Also, I’m not advocating an entirely new system of powers2; what I’m picturing would use the same Boons that everything else does to define what you can do when you practice magic. The only “new system” that would be added would be summed up in a Birthright write-up (or something similar) that would be no more extensive than Relics, and possibly less so.
                        1 Ideally, of course. In practice, complete fidelity is unworkable; but that doesn’t mean you can’t get reasonably close, or that you shouldn’t even try.
                        2 I’m also not opposed to it, if the developers can come up with one that’s suitably concise.
