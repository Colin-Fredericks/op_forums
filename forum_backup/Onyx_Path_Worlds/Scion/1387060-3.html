                Here is more from Scion: Companion preview blog ( from over 1,5 years ago ) on Myth Levels / Dials:

                Originally posted by Myth Levels preview
                CHAPTER ONE: MYTH LEVEL

                The Myth Level is five options along four axes of design, meant to allow the Storyguide to choose how many of Scion’s setting “dials” they want to crank. This chapter lays out what each of those dials looks like at each of the five Myth Levels. Those Myth Levels are:
                • Iron: Perfectly mundane. Very similar in nature and tone to Gaiman’s American Gods, where ifriti are cab drivers, Odhinn is a shiftless wandering con man, and Chernobog and the goddesses of dawn, midday and evening are poor immigrants scraping by in a shitty apartment in Chicago.
                • Heroic: Mostly mundane, but with room for great acts of heroism. Most supernatural creatures still have mundane manifestations here (that centaur is still just a biker here), but Scions and gods may appear as impressive and tremendous – Aphrodite isn’t some burnt out barfly here, she’s drop-dead gorgeous and probably on fashion covers. This is the level of the Iliad and the Odyssey.
                • Bronze: This is where the supernatural really comes into play. Creatures of Legend shed their mundane disguises in areas of Bronze Myth, and appear in their true nature. Magic becomes overt here, and Demigods wield their true power. This is Clash of the Titans territory, and there might just actually be dragons in that undetailed area of the map.
                • Silver: Past the threshold of urban myth and getting into epic fantasy, places of Silver Myth are deeply resonant with Myth. They are often fanciful places and hidden vales of magic technically found in the World, but not of it. The power of a creature of Legend is reflected in its appearance – the mightier the entity, the more impressive it appears. Zelazny’s Lord of Light goes into this area of Myth in its narrative.
                • Gold: Flat-out mythological, where symbolism is the same as its reality. Places of Gold Myth are not even of this World. Lots of Scion 1e went into this territory, with its otherworldly locales, and lots of fantasy that touches on extradimensional areas and the like qualify.
                The trick is this: you should be able to choose a starting Myth Level and then turn each dial up or down within that level to taste. So, for instance, as Storyguide I might choose to start at the Iron Level but crank the Titanomachy up, turning American Gods into a raging shadow war that you have to keep peeling back layers of the World to see.

                    And that's how I would make Lovecraftian Cosmic Horror work for Scion. The younger, more recently evolved Gods still have recognizable human motivations - but this will wane as time goes on.

                        This discussion is not actually about reconciling the Mythos of H. P. Lovecraft's paracosm with the beliefs and myths of various cultures around the world, it is a discussion about primacy. Fundamentally, this is an issue that shows up just as much when merging two pieces of fiction in a crossover fanfiction - which makes sense because P&P Roleplay is basically extended cooperative fanfiction with a conflict resolution mechanism - as it does now when we discuss this matter. Painting with a broad brush, it is not possible to ever reconcile two propositions that make contrary claims without giving primacy to one claim over another. Jürgen Hubert claims to have reconciled them, but this is only true insofar as he has implicitly given one of them primacy over the other, just like Samudra and Kyman201 have explicitly given the claims of divinities primacy over that of the Mythos.

                        What I mean by this is that Scion's default statement is loosely that all myths are true and that it is simply a matter of from which 'layer' one happens to view the world. In one layer, the goddess sang of the rage of the Peleïd Akhilleus and the countless ills he brought upon the Akhaians, in another layer the same is at most a post-hoc legendary figure whose legend became paramount in the Greek Dark Ages when the scattered chiefdoms of post-Mykenaian Greece looked back on a past they no longer remembered but in a haze and told stories of the great cyclopean walls and constructions left from those times. In this sense, Scion very carefully avoids giving any story primacy over others because what Scion deals with is not two (or eight or twelve) pieces of fiction to be reconciled, but the very real beliefs and traditions of real human beings, some of which have lived for a long time in a situation in which the primacy has been given to whoever happened to rule over them at the time. Whether these are the Parsi of India who had to explain our Mazdayasna in terms of angels and archangels or face persecution from the Church of Scotland or the Nahua of Mexico who became heathens in the minds of Spanish conquistadores who imagined the frontiers of the Virreinato de Nueva España as a new reconquista and the lands of the former Triple Alliance as a new Emirate of Granada does not really matter. A "reconciliation" of the Mythos of H. P. Lovecraft with the beliefs most mythologies in the world that states that the deities of the Mythos are simply the gods of older beings, strange and alien to nascent humanity, who once lived in foreign vistas amidst the stars before themselves being extinguished by the passage of time is not a compromise between them at all. It is an all but explicit statement that the gods are either wrong or lying, that they did not create the world or matter much beyond a simple lonely orb of land and water passing through the universe, the universe is fundamentally Darwinian and gods evolve and die as time passes and the universe did not have its origin in anything but a concentration of meaningless particles.

                        You can run a game built on such a premise and it could even work for certain pantheons, but if you were to tell me - a Zoroastrian - that it is fully compatible with the Mythos to have the world be created not by Ahura Mazda but through complete randomness and to have the actual truth of the world be one of meaninglessness and purposelessness rather than that of the threefold path of Asha and that gods are simply powerful beings and nothing more, I would certainly raise my eyebrow in confusion. It is valid and fine to choose to prioritize the atmosphere of cosmic horror and make changes based on that, but you have to acknowledge and accept that it requires making changes and I hope that you can certainly understand why many of us people of faith are little inclined to make changes to something that matters quite deeply to us in order to prioritize the vision of a New English guy from the 20th century who would with most likelihood despise us if we stood in front of him, flesh and blood.

                        EDIT: This came out a bit more aggressive than I'd have wanted to (and also I spelled a name wrong), so I do want to clarify that I like the works of Lovecraft even though I know he would most probably find me an abomination against God that weakens the blood of the white race. I also do not mean to imply any hard feelings for choosing to prioritize the feeling of cosmic horror and the Mythos for the sake of the game, I just wanted to make it clear that there was a choice being made and that this was not simply a matter of reconciling but of picking and choosing.
