    Originally posted by Mizu View Post
    What happens if a dragon tries to learn sorcery? And what happens if a dragon tries to gain the awareness stat after they spend some time tangling with Lovecraftian horrors?
    Dragons and Sorcery don't really mix well. You can use the "Denizens, Saints, and Sorcery" sidebar on p. 77 of Saints & Monsters to make it kind of work, though. After Inheritance 10, Dragons can just do whatever Sorcery Spells they want and pretend like they were Sorcerers the whole time.

    There's not a book answer for what happens to Heirs who seek Awareness, but I don't see a reason why you couldn't cross those streams. Heirs with Awareness would be in a tight spot where they're trying to lie low at the same time that they're trying to get the Great Old Ones' attention. Such a game would probably involve a lot of secret moves and plots within plots. So, it'd be a weird game, but Mythos games are automatically weird, so it's not too farfetched.

    By the book, mixing Awareness & Inheritance wouldn't work well unless all the players were Mythos Heirs; Heirs can't get more than 1 Inheritance ahead of their broodmates (Dragon, p.118).

    e.g. If a Mythos Heir completes a Deed set and raises their Awareness while the rest of the Brood increases their Inheritance, the rest of the Brood is locked out of picking new Deeds until the Mythos Heir completes another Deed set and chooses to increase their Inheritance.

    If you ignored that rule, then the Mythos Heir would end up lagging behind as they split their Deeds between the two resources, the same way as the only Mythos Scion in an all-Scion game would.

    If you ran an all Mythos Heir game, those problems would fall away, though. It'd still be weird, but as long as that doesn't get in the way of everyone's fun, I don't see a problem.
