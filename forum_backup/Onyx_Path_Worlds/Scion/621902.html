    Pantheon of Horrors

    08-10-2015, 01:51 AM
    I've been toying with this idea for a while but never worked up the nerve to actually go through with it. I wrote a pantheon that's based around popular horror character. I tried to avoid picking obvious ones like Jason or Freddy and instead went with classical monsters or those with a lesser known reputation.



    ----------------------------------------------


    The Horror pantheon are gods and goddesses found in monster stories. The core members of the pantheon are derived from classical movie monsters, but newer generations have begun to appear in their roster as well, as mankind's view of these monsters changes over time.

    Since these monsters are often attributed to the horror genre of media, they are collectively known as Horrors, or just simply Horror when addressing a singular member. Their PSP of "Cinema" allows them to manifest a variety of powers based on Hollywood special effects.

    The overworld of the pantheon is called the Silver Screen and is surrounded and besieged by Academia, greater titan of education and understanding. It is described as a realm of atmospheric suspense and dread, where nightmares and terror are as common place as grass and clouds. Their underworld is known as The Bargain Bin and is described as a realm without color, where fear is replaced with a sense of mild amusement due to abundant stupidity. It is said The Bargain Bin is the final resting place for failed Horrors.


    -------------------------------------


    History: One of the younger pantheons to emerge in the world, the Horrors are less than 200 years old, being a product of the modern age. Their first depictions came in the form of books and movies that were designed to scare people. However, as these monsters became more and more popular, they developed a cult fan base and began to take on a life of their own.

    The founding members of The Horrors are all classical movie monsters who have been around for decades and their stories having been re-imagined several times. However as their popularity grew, new interpretations and even spin offs to the popular legends began to make their way into the public image, allowing for new generations of monsters to be added to the roster.


    -------------------------------------------


    Nature: The Horrors were given life by mortals who love to be scared. So it is no surprise that the primary drive of the pantheon is to scare people. Sometimes this merely takes the form of taunting their victims, instilling them with nightmares before moving on to a new target. Other times these visits end violently, their victims being destroyed in gruesome ways.

    To those outside the pantheon, the actions of the Horrors may seem villainous, but as with all natures, appearances can be deceiving. The Horrors fight to stop the spreading of disbelief throughout the world. Mortals are rapidly becoming more advanced as they learn more about the world around them, and faith in the supernatural has begun to wane. If mortals stopped believing in the gods completely, there would be nobody left to keep the Titans at bay.

    The Horrors give mortals a reason to fear the unknown, and a reason to believe in monsters and magic. If a mortal starts to peer into secrets they were not intended to know, the Horrors arrive to set them straight. If things go according to plan, the mortal's belief in otherworldly powers is strengthened. If the mortal remains persistent, then their sudden end will serve as an example to others.


    -------------------------------


    Relationships: Understandably, the Horrors are not met with very high opinions by the other pantheons due to their age. However, their fixation on spreading terror and misery among the mortals probably doesn't win them any favors either. When they aren't disguising themselves among the mortal population, most Horrors resemble monsters in their true forms, which makes it difficult to seperate them from the Titan Spawn.

    Time will tell if the Horrors manage to improve their standing with the other established pantheons or not.


    -----------------------------------


    Members


    Count Dracula: God of darkness and the creatures who dwell within it. He is reigning king of the pantheon and sustains himself on the blood of mortals. He has cheated death multiple times, always finding a way to restore himself after his supposed destruction.


    Victor Franktenstein: God of the forge. He seeks the power to create life, but has so far only learned how to restore what was taken.


    The Wolfman: God of the hunt, as well as beasts. He has power over the primal urges within mankind's soul and is known to turn mortals into savage beasts.


    Pumpkin Head: God of vengeance and punishment. He was horribly abused as a child because of his physical deformity. Now, he unleashes his wrath upon the cruel and the selfish.


    The Blob: God of destruction. In its true form, it resembles an amorphous mass of organic tissue that devours everything it touches.


    The Mummy: God of the underworld. He serves as protector of the dead and lays curses upon grave robbers.


    The Leprechaun: God of dark comedy, he resembles a demonic imp dressed in Irish attire. Each of his golden coins have the power to grant 1 wish, but he will violently kill anybody who attempts to steal them.


    -------------------


    Secondary Members


    Count Orlock: God of terror and former scion of Dracula. His physical deformity and silent disposition only further enhance the fear he spreads in others.


    Monster: God of philosophy and former scion to Victor Franktenstein. His studies revolve around the nature of the soul and what defines somebody as being human. His father created him by assembling pieces from multiple corpses.


    The Fly: God of tragedy and former scion to The Wolfman. He was once a brilliant scientist, but one of his experiments went horrifically wrong, combining his DNA with that of a house fly.


    Ginger: Goddess of puberty and former scion to The Wolfman. She has a rebellious personality and is prone to emotional outbursts and sexual urges.


    The Plant: God of the harvest and former scion to The Blob. Resembling a giant, carnivorous plant, it hungers for human flesh. It also has a love for campy musical numbers.


    The Creature: God of water and former scion to The Wolfman. His more popular name is "The Creature from the Black Lagoon" among mortal society.


    Doctor Moreau: God of abominations, although his father is Victor Frankenstein, he was adopted by Wolfman due to his activities. He uses his advanced medical skills to surgically combine humans with animal DNA.


    The Claw: God of the sky and former scion to The Blob.Vaguely resembling a buzzard in appearance, it has been described as being the size of a battleship!


    Stripe: God of chaos and former scion to The Leprechaun. He resembles a gremlin with a white, fuzzy mohawk and commits widespread mayhem for his own amusement.


    The Thing: God of betrayal and former scion to The Blob. It is a shapeshifting entity with the power to assimilate others into its being!


    Kong: God of masculinity and former scion to Wolfman. He resembles a massive ape standing 25 tall, In order to stave off his wrath, he demands that his worshipers offer him sacrifices in the form of beautiful women.


    The Djinn: God of greed and former scion of The Leprechaun, he offers to grant you one wish in exchange for your soul. Unfortunately he has a nasty habit of corrupting the wishes to disastrous consequences.
