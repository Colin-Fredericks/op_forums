    Fujiyama Kazushi: The byproduct of an attempt to build relations between the Aesir and the Amatsukami (both warrior cultures), Fujiyama is part of a Scion exchange program. The Norse pantheon adopted a Japanese Scion, and likewise the Japanese pantheon adopted a Norse Scion. Before receiving his visitation, Fujiyama was born and raised in Japan, where he developed an interest in sumo wrestling during his high school years. His parents supported his interest, first as an extracurricular activity, and later developing into a career opportunity.

    By subjecting himself to the rigorous training regiment of sumo, Fujiyama grew into a big man. Exceeding 200 pounds by the time he finished highschool, and growing even larger as he dedicated himself professionally to the sport. This is when Fate decided to get involved and sent an agent to test him during one of his training exercises. A weak (yet still formidable) frost giant attacked Fujiyama while he was alone in his master's dojo and challenged him to a wrestle match.

    The sight of an 8 foot tall man of ice probably would have been more shocking to somebody who's country didn't have shrines dedicated to any number of nature spirits, but something inside Fujiyama compelled him to accept the challenge. In typical fashion, the giant thought himself the easy victor of any clash with a human opponent. But sumo wrestlers are able to lift, throw and push 400 pound men around, and Fujiyama's strength caught the giant by surprise as he was forced on the defensive and eventually forced out of the arena!

    With his victory over the frost giant, Fate decided the time was right, and that's when Fujiyama received a visit from Vidar, who claimed him for the Aesir. It would later be discovered that Fujiyama's replacement among the Amatsukami had been adopted by Hachiman, thus making the program an exchange between two Gods of War.


    ------------------------------------

    As both a sumo wrestler and the heir to giant's blood, Vidar's trademark paranoia has set in hard when it comes to his adoptive Scion. Fujiyama is already large for a mortal, and there's no telling how much bigger he'll grow if his powers are allowed to develop further. To prevent the possibility of Fujiyama going rogue, he keeps him under close supervision. He also seems to be a demanding task master who is hard to impress even when Fujiyama does a good job. But Fujiyama is accustomed to hard work and discipline, so he does not hold a grudge against his adoptive father.

    At least not this early on, anyways.

    ------------------------------------------

    Roleplaying Hints: Fujiyama is the tank of whatever band he's in. Huge, strong, and specifically trained to repel similar individuals, Fujiyama is ready to place himself between advancing enemies and those he wishes to protect. And yet for all his raw power, Fujiyama is not a testosterone fueled meat head and is quite capable of remaining calm under pressure. Giving him an edge that most warriors of his caliber often lack!

            Edgar Viste: in an effort to improve stability between two warrior cultures, the Gods of the Japanese and Norse pantheons decided to exchange one of their Scions. Edgar Viste is who the Aesir chose to give up, and now the Amatsukami are seriously wondering if they got the raw end of the deal on this exchange.

            Born in Norway, Edgar would grow up to become a problem child. He would constantly get into fights with other children his age, and the fact that he often won such confrontations did not improve his reputation much, either. Perhaps, in some part of their consciousness that the Aesir would never acknowledge, they hoped that the Amatsukami might be able to teach him discipline and self control. Regardless of their reasons, Fate decided that Edgar's family would be moving to Japan due to, as his parents put it, improved career opportunities.

            While initially unhappy with the sudden transfer to a country who's language he barely spoke, Edgar found a way to cope with his frustrations by joining the school's kendo club. Edgar felt surprisingly uplifted with a weapon in his hand, even if it was only made of wood and quickly began to establish himself as one of the best students in the class. Of course, this moment of respite only persisted during school hours. Outside of class, he was still the violent and unruly boy he'd always been. As he grew older and his skill with the sword improved, he would eventually go onto start his own street gang of troublesome teens who used their muscles and numbers to get their way.

            The Amatsukami decided enough was enough, it was time that Edgar Viste learned some proper discipline, and clearly he wasn't going to learn it on his own. Hachiman, the Japanese God of War saw the fighting spirit within Edgar, and decided to claim him as his own. It would later be discovered that Edgar's replacement had been adopted by Vidar, the Norse God of War, thus making it an arguably fair exchange.

            Hachiman's original intention was to groom Edgar into a proud, disciplined samurai who the Amatsukami would have been proud to count among their warriors. It didn't take, and Edgar Viste became a ruthless thug who seemed eager to draw his blade (now very much real) on anything he perceived as a threat. Edgar's been taking some history lessons, and discovered that all that crap about Bushido and honor was only created during the final years of the Samurai in a desperate attempt to remain in power. The real samurai were blood thirsty thugs who oppressed the population so badly, they inspired the greatest assassins in history!

            Today, Edgar still commands his street gang of violent delinquents. Although now that he's a Scion with access to super human abilities, he's begun to expand his horizons to worse crimes than petty theft and vandalism. He may be planning to build his own criminal empire, if he can safely avoid the Yakuza for now. Hachiman has given him veiled threats that some members of the organization share his special gifts, and they aren't very tolerant of rival organizations, especially not ones lead by foreigners!


            ----------------------------------------------------


            Since his visitation, Edgar has been granted the katana which has since become his most cherished possession. It enables him to channel the War purview and adds +2L -2 Speed to the Katana template. He has taken to calling this weapon Satsujin-sha, which means Manslayer.

            Additionally, Edgar has access to the gang of Thugs he managed to round up before his visitation. His gang measures 25 members in total (Followers 5)


            ---------------------------------------------------


            Roleplaying Hints: Hot tempered and violent, you make for an effective warrior to your band's ranks, though only because you're the most ruthless among them. You don't follow the code of Bushido, because you feel that it was bullshit to begin with; just a desperate attempt for the samurai to appeal to the government before they were disbanded. The gods gave you a sword, and the purpose of a sword is to kill people!

            Although you may have been claimed by the Amatsukami, you are not welcomed by them. Your belligerent attitude and criminal associates has brought shame to Hachiman who thought he could make you a better person. Instead of earning the Amatsukami's approval, you're only hope for continued advancement is to prove that your methods of dealing with the Titans are too effective to simply be cast aside. Sure, people might get caught in the cross fire, but if your manage to kill more Titanspawn than pedestrians, you're confident the Gods won't be complaining too much.

                                    Ash Sims: This noble savage is one of Artemis' adopted Scions. But while his motherly patron is known for hunting wild animals, Ash prefers to commune with them. A social introvert from an early age, he has trouble relating to human society and prefers to surround himself with the company of pets. The fact that his mortal family worked as veterinarians and ran an animal shelter only made it easier for him to spend time with his beloved animals. His parents just thought he was being helpful around the work environment, unaware he was using it as an excuse to avoid associating with humans.

                                    The reasons for Ash's anti-social behavior are unclear. Perhaps Fate intended for Artemis to claim him from the start, and orchestrated his behavior in such a way. Artermis is also known for spurning the company of men, so this theory is not as strange as it seems. Regardless of the reasons, Ash found that he was reluctantly forced to venture into the world following the events of his visitation.

                                    One night, a wounded animal had been admitted to the shelter, with his parents planning to nurse it back to health. Eager to get to it first, Ash visited its pen to discover that the animal was actually a newly infected Therianthrope, still reeling from the shock of its first transformation. Ash watched as the creature warped and shifted before his eyes and rammed the pen hard enough to twist the metal out of shape. Once free of its trappings, it tried to lunge at the startled Ash.

                                    As if by instinct, Ash ducked under the crazed animal, wrapped his arms around its neck, and rolled his body to pin the animal underneath him! It was a technique liked to use when he was playfully wrestling the dogs at the shelter. The technique worked, and the shapeshifter seemed to be completely bewildered for a moment. As Ash loosened his hold, the beast squirmed free and then jumped out the window, running off into the night. When Ash rose to his feet, he turned to see Artemis standing behind him with a bow in her hand and an expression that seemed a mixture between curiosity and disappointment.

                                    "Go catch it..." she said, before thrusting a weapon into his hands.

                                            Though I've gotten some flack on these forums before for this character's concept, I am hoping that Scion 2.0 will allow me the freedom to create this character the way I envisioned her.

                                            ​Barbrah "Babs" Morgan: raised as a druid by a Seelie father and an Unseelie mother (foster parents), Barbarah (though most call her Babs) knows that for every boon one receives, a price must be paid (though the price isn't always equal to the boon received). This is the way nature works... that beauty and brutality often go hand in hand... and that to heal a wound, healthy flesh must sometimes be sacrificed for the greater good. Never was this more true than the day she was visited by her mother... the Morrigan... where, to save her from the Fomorians that intended to kill her and her foster parents, Babs would have to choose whose life to sacrifice. Though the Morrigan had intended for one of her foster parents to die that night and seal Babs' path amongst either the Seelie or Unseelie... she instead chose to walk the line between by sacrificing one of her Fomorian attackers. While displeased with the choice, the Morrigan couldn't deny that her price had been met (as she never specified who must die so the others could live) and even admired her daughter's resourcefulness in finding the loophole in the bargain she offered. In addition to the items her foster parents bequeathed her, the Morrigan allowed her to keep the sacrificial dagger she gave Barbarah (her mother never calls her Babs)... as a reminder of the sometimes heavy price that must be made to receive or give a gift. Since then, Babs has wandered the land, helping those who need it... and can meet her price for her gifts; all the time walking the line between Seelie and Unseelie... the darkness and the light.
