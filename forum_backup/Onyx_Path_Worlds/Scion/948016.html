      The appeal for me (as someone who is coming to Scion with 2e and didn't touch the first) is it's urban fantasy that breaks away from the pop culture and horror influences more popular in World/Chronicles of Darkness, Dresden Files, Supernatural and the like, instead populating the secret world with the children and champions of the old gods of every pantheon mankind has ever known. Want to play a son of Zeus who carries some of that lightning in his trusty revolver, a servant of Anubis who returns the dead to their eternal rest, or an unwilling prophet graced with visions from Quetzalcoatl ? Scion is the game for you.

      Rules-wise, the new Storypath system looks to be in kind of a middle ground between nWoD/CofD and Fate Core, with d10 dice pools and a focus on cinematic quick play with a lot of storygame elements. It looks really simple and elegant.

          Hey! Glad you are interested in hearing about Scion. I have been playing for six years now, and there really is a ton of amazing stuff you can do with it. It goes in directions I have never had other Tabletop RPGs go before, and has a huge amount of opportunities for interesting stories and themes. Since Atamajakki touched on the characters you can play, I will talk a bit about the worlds and themes you can explore!

          Themes
          Culture Clash: Possibly my favorite theme in Scion is that of culture clash. In Scion 1E, you were always the half-mortal child of a Deity (from what Neal has talked about in the Ask Neal thread, this is getting opened up a bunch) which is really interesting. The Pantheons are these huge, monolithic societies that have essentially remained stagnant for X amount of years since their withdrawal from the world. These are beings who are not just set in their ways, but are in many cases physical embodiment of those ways. This creates great opportunity for stories, for example!

          - Playing a LGBT Teotl (Aztec Scion). The Aztecs in their (in Scion, very legitimate) desire to keep the Sun from being defeated, had huge laws in place against non-Heterosexual relationships as you were endangering the world by having sexual relationships that couldn't produce more children to keep society going. The Aztecs even have a Goddess who tempts people into violating the strict laws in this area to catch them in the act and rain fury down upon them. But, you are a Scion! Maybe if you're Huitzlipotchli's son, your dad is more concerned with you being a warrior and will enact a degree of protection on you as you rail against the system. Or, perhaps, you are an Epic Social character, and through sheer bloody mindedness, work to convince your Pantheon to change their ways.

          - Playing an Egyptian Scion in general. The Egyptian Pantheon is incredibly inter-entangled. Not in the Greek way, but in that everyone's married to a sibling pretty much. Congratulations! You are the Son of Anubis! You are going to marry your sister despite the revulsion both of you have to this! Will you get over your petty mortal morality? Or, will you both weasel your way out of the situation somehow?

          - Playing a Kami (Japanese) Scion. Your Pantheon is really into incredibly complex systems of proper behavior. If you're a modern person in their early twenties, you will be struggling to play catch up, learning a huge amount of complex systems. If you are unlucky enough to be a woman, well, it's not going to be fun unfortunately. But! You still are a Scion. You can tear this world down around your feet if you so desire. Will you adapt to this new world? Force it to change? Or utterly reject it?

          Power: You are the child of a God or Goddess. You wield a tremendous amount of power. Not just physical power, sure, you have a burning sword, neat, but, that's small scale. Even the lowliest Scion's parent is a Deity, a full fledged, world shattering figure. And, guess what? Most of the Pantheons have systems in place that involve parents keeping an eye on their children, to keep them under control, and to protect them. If your father is Thor, and someone is going to kill you, you best believe the world itself will shake when dad shows up furious and ready to kill everyone who set upon their child.

          Adding to this, Scions can acomplish anything with enough luck and learning. In WoD, that's normally the Mages' thing, but, it's totally different here. I'll give an example from a game. My Scion was being held by The Nuckelvee, and clutching a bomb inside the giant flayed centaur-sort-of's grip. Another player tossed a fire source onto this, everything goes up like a torch. I take something like two sources of 25 Lethal at Legend 3. This isn't a "well, I'm dead" situation, it becomes a, "Right, okay, so. How will I survive this?" as you look at your exp list and start glancing through powers. On a larger scale, Scions can do things like steal the sun, free Titans, kill Gods (sometimes, some Pantheons have true immortal Gods, or if they're the Irish Pantheon, killing them isn't... I mean, just normal mortals have killed them.) bring about the end of the world because you fucked up and slept with the wrong Deity, and then manage to save it by the skin of your teeth. The question is always how can I do this, because there will be a way. Kill a God? I bet there's a weird magical weapon from some Pantheon that would manage that, I wonder where it gets left laying about in Mythology? Might just be in a hill tomb somewhere unimpressive.

          Changing the World: Scions will change the world. Even if they do nothing. There is no Masquerade in Scion, but the world isn't any different. The Pantheons have just withdrawn so well that legend, and power have faded. But they're back and you are the herald of their coming. Everything that is, will be questioned, and change. The Sun is a thousand-thousand things, Huitz carries it upon his back, Apollo drives it across the sky, it is a torch carried by an Inuit woman fleeing her brother, it is a woman on a horse fleeing a colossal wolf, it is a lonely Russian woman searching for a child that was fundamentally Un-Made, it is Ra on his barge, it is Varacocha powerful and sublime, it is Amaterasu's glory, it is an opponent Mithra beat into submission. It is all of these things at once, and countless more.

          Countries will fall as the Pantheons return to the world, annoyed that it moved on without them. The Middle East will be rocked by the Great Anunnaki arriving, (several other Pantheons would be in this area as well, just simplifying) looking around, and being confused about where their cities went. They left them right here, they're sure of it. It was a lovely city, it had a Zigguraut. What happened to it? Did those stupid pestilent mortals, little more than a slave-species, break it? Bastards. They will be rebuilding it, all of it, Ur, Babylon, all of the great cities. The United States, and Canada will be rocked as a plethora of Pantheons return. Ireland will probably just be conquered as the Irish Gods and Goddesses come screaming out of the hills shouting about how the Gael are all bastards and will be put in their place. And, mortals can do nothing to stop this, their greatest ally will be the Scions, who can try to soften the blows, guide, and help mediate between the Divine and mortal.

          Research: As I am an utter dork, I love research. Digging through my University's frankly really odd Occultism section, flicking through journals to find some magical weapon mentioned once in the Irish texts so we can give Prometheus a smack about the head. Mythology is just so rich and fascinating. Scion actually got me so interested in Irish mythology that I am likely going to be starting my Masters in the Autumn of 2017 in Celtic Studies looking at these texts.

          Scion 2e Homebrew Projects:
          The Šiuneš, The Enduri, The Sgā’na Qeda’s, The Abosom, Lebor Óe In Dea, The Zemi, Nemetondevos: Revised, and Mysteries of the Otherworld.

              To commit a minor faux pas and double post, Scion's main draw for me is having fun with mythology and remixing those stories against a modern backdrop: Osiris is dismembered and then returned to life without his penis, and rules as pharaoh of the underworld and as god of life at once; what if that story, already so clearly about liminal spaces and paradox, was retold as a queer narrative? The king dies, and by losing what makes him masculine rules the dead as a queen. Is it likely to give a conservative mythology academic an aneurysm? Sure, but it's also an awesome reasoning to have a trans woman as a Scion of Osiris.

              It also has all the fun of a superhero crossover for a mythology nerd. Anubis protects graves and guides the dead into the afterlife; The Dagda revives men with the butt of his club. What happens when the two of them get into a spat over the sanctity of death, and send their children and champions to fight it out in the streets of your home city? What if Zeus and Thor get into a feud over who has mastery of storms?

                          Originally posted by LC1984 View Post
                          Thanks again for the information. How difficult is it to prepare for a new story, what enemies and opponents will the characters meet (mostly gods and titan)? Can the players characters come from different pantheons? What brings the different characters together?
                          Happy to help mate. Preparing for a new story is not too difficult, but, I have only ever run Scion, so I can't actually speak to the comparative difficulty to other games unfortunately.

                          Enemies for your players, that's really wide. Pretty much determined by the Story. There are a mountain of possible antagonists, from simple one off enemies fought in scraps, to large overarching antagonists. For example, Dionysus could be an antagonist for a story, say, he has shown up in downtown New York and has kicked off a colossal orgy of extacy, violence, canabalism, self-mutilation, and sex because... I don't know, let's say Hera cursed him with madness again, or his thought-to-be-cured madness kicked back off. The Band would probably face opposition from Maenads (mortal women who worship Dionysus), Centaur, Satyrs, panthers (it's a long weird story) of unusual power, normal mortals driven to madness, that sort of thing. Then on top of physical confrontations, there could be an equal or greater number of social challenges (though any of those could probably be solved by Social Scions with luck) and they could run into Pan, Cybele, various Nymphs. All culminating in managing to get to Dionysus and calm him down somehow. (can't kill the Theoi, only mortally wound them. Also, actually trying to fight a proper God's suicidal when that Pantheon's leading cause of death in Demigods is hubris)

                          Players can totally be from different Pantheons, though, what I see people do a lot is set limitations. For example, if I want to tell a story focusing on the North-Western European Pantheons + the Greeks in their Roman guises, I'd put a soft suggestion that they all pick from those Pantheons (Irish, Norse, Greek) rather than other ones. You can have totally open stories too, really, it's up to you as a storyteller. Some Pantheons are a bit weird playing with others, the Russians are the biggest problematic Pantheon, but they're not even written up so no worries there. The Kami (Japanese Pantheon) and the Teotl (Aztec Pantheon) may have serious issues ever working together, but their Scions still could.

                          And, getting the players together can be almost anything. Like, here, I'll give a few example from games I have been in and one I have run. The players are all living in New York, and one sunny, lovely afternoon, they all happen to be in Times Square to watch a giant serpent made of pure darkness rear up and swallow the Sun whole casting the world into total darkness (and killing / incapacitating all Sun Deities and Titans at once) an they have to scramble to deal with the immediate situation. The players could all be hired by some mysterious benefactor (a Deity or Titan in disguise) to have them do something shady. Or, simplest, several Gods and Goddesses from different Pantheons have a common goal, and shove their Scions together with a task.

                                      Hi there,

                                      I must admit, I was always curious about Scion, but never even got to see a single book from it. (Not even sure it got a release in my country)

                                      I am not a huge fan of Vampire and Co, as the rules never clicked with me (only Dark Ages was kinda nice), but I'm a big fan of Exalted.


                                      So, I heard Scion has some similarities to Exalted, but even more so to some of its source material with Titans, Gods etc.

                                      Why should I play Scion, what is its focus and in what direction is the new rule system moving? I am going to take a look at the preview, but as I am not familiar with a great part of the WW and OPP rule systems (with the exception of Exalted), I really, really would like to hear opinions and comparions from fellow gamers, who are a bit wiser than me?

                                      I was a big fan of mythology growing up, and Scion tries to stay true to the actual myths instead of adapting them to the more modernized interpretations. Scion can also double as a super hero role playing game if you find the idea of ancient mythology a little bit too extreme for your tastes. A lot of the Gods favor a more traditionalist approach, and ancient tradition supported mass murder, slavery, racism / xenophobia, rape, and other gross abuses of authority. It's understandable not everybody would feel comfortable portraying that for their characters. The fact that this all takes place in the modern world also shows why the players and their characters might feel uncomfortable.


                                      Unlike the World of Darkness series in which the odds are stacked against your character from the start, Scion focuses more on your characters rising to godhood and taking their place among the heavens. The books flat out advise Story Tellers to let the players win, but it still falls to them to make their victories seem like major accomplishments. Early on in the game, they might be dealing with a few thugs and monsters, but towards the end of the game, they're stopping extinction level threats and monsters so powerful it takes multiple Gods to defeat them.

                                      Unfortunately, one major problem people have with first edition is how absolutely broken the characters become starting at Demigod level. A character built around Dexterity is borderline invincible because nobody can touch them without having a similar Dexterity score. I understand they're trying to fix this with second edition, though.



                                      The vanilla game has six pantheons, with additional pantheons included in supplement material. It also gives you instructions on how to create your own pantheon, whether you're adapting one from real life, or customizing it from the ground up.


                                      The base game has the Greek, Japanese, Aztec, Voodoo, Norse and Egyptian pantheons. Scion Companion adds in the Irish, Hindu, and Chinese gods. The Persian gods are available for download on DriveThruRPG.

                                      Scion Companion also introduces the rather poorly hashed together Allied and American pantheons. The Allies are a grab bag of French, Russian and Brittish folk lore, while the American pantheon is likewise comprised of American folk tales and national icons. A lot of players didn't like how they were constructed and felt they could have been done better.


                                      Finally, Scion also introduces its own original pantheon based on the city of Atlantis. The pantheon is supposed to be extinct, but it gives Story Tellers hints as to how the pantheon might be resurrected, if that's what they wish to do with it.

                                          Originally posted by LC1984 View Post
                                          Thanks again for the information. How difficult is it to prepare for a new story, what enemies and opponents will the characters meet (mostly gods and titan)? Can the players characters come from different pantheons? What brings the different characters together?



                                          1 - The major challenge is knowing enough about the mythology you plan to use without coming across as incredibly racist and stereotypical. I mean, how often are you going to find a player who knows jack about Voodoo beyond sticking pins in dolls?

                                          2 - Practically anything from mythology can be used as an enemy, a servant, or a guide. If it's not already in the book, just write up the sheet for it and you're good to go. Although early in your Scion's adventures, they're likely to still be fighting mortals with the occasional monster thrown in. As they advance, however, they'll be tackling creatures worthy of Demigods and Gods.

                                          This is just a sample of what you can expect to encounter:

                                          Minotaurs, Cyclopes, Hundred Handed Giants, Shinobi, Dragons, Demons (from various cultures) Vampires (from various cultures), Werebeasts, Angels (yes, angels), Nymphs, Oni, Zombies, Spectres, Valkyries, Amazons, Colossi, and Krakens!


                                          3 - Yes, your players do not all have to originate from the same pantheon. Although the Gods have many rivals, particularly regarding foreign pantheons. It is both expected and encouraged that having cross-pantheon characters in the same group would result in conflicts of interest. Working around these conflicts is part of the fun.
