        I admit I object to adding Boons to Relics on principle. In Hero we have a hard limit on Boons you can know, making the ones you pick be very important and say something about your character, with the gaps filled in by Marvels.

        And if you only need one Relic Dot for a Boon, well one, piss to that idea I guess who needs Marvels when you can just kit yourself out with Boons?

        And also why are Boons cheaper than Knacks, XP-wise? This makes the opposite of sense. Getting yourself a new Knack is 10XP, while getting a Boon via a Relic Dot is 5XP.

        Also several of the other Relic Abilities feel mispriced.

        Aggravated takes 4 Relic Dots? Does this mean that Aggravated if used as part of the base weapon's tags should cost 4 points? I mean I can see it if the Tag got buffed like I heard but I'd like that spelled out. Same for why Shockwave, a 4-point weapon tag, only 3 Relic Dots?

        Why is being able to summon and dismiss it a 2-dot power? That feels more like a 1-dot power. Especially if it takes an action to do so.

        3 dots to give it a basic personality? That's just robbery, given that RAW you can instead invest 3 dots into making it a Guide that can give you access to a stunt AND maybe another Purview or an alternate Calling.

        (And yes I've submitted all this to Errata Feedback)

                            I think the problem with Scion is it throws most of the limits of the powers to the "common sense" of the Story Guide and the players. The Devs used a certain mindset that they didn't delivered in the book.

                            What I mean is let's say I have a Scion with a certain purview, for example War, War cover Strife in conceptual level as well as armed conflict. Can I use War to change someones biological sex? Its an conflict, a war between what the person feel and what its body forced to the person, so I could do that, same for Chaos changing the "natural order", or Fertility making reproduction possible, or... well... any purview if you have enough imagination (the idea of changing someones sex came from an old discussion in this forum, so, sorry if it offends you).

                            If you have players used to the more rigid system, like Mage (any of the 2), while they can bring a lot of ideas, they will probably stay inside the circles intended by the devs, if you have a group of players used to a more flexible system (like powered by Apocalypse, and other more modern systems) you can extend the powers beyond the usual.

                            I guess, Kyman201 is one of the guys that have a more open way to see the limits, the same (in this case I am sure of it) for Oxcelot, while I am adept of a more rigid system (more in pair with Trinity Continuum).

                            There are problems with Scion, as there are with any other game, but the main one in my view is how flexible it can be while we play a character that should be less flexible (we play Heroes, not gods, yet).

                                            It may be a bit tougher to do a Versatility Set of Dials, because yeah, Iron is "Very Rigid, No Adjustments, You Can't Use Metaphorical Fire With Your Fire Purview Even Though A Sample Boon Lets You", and with Gold being the basic gameline assumption...                                             "Be a little bit metaphorical"? "DM flips a coin to see if they allow this"?

                                            And if your setting assumes that you're only using these Purviews in their most literal definition (setting aside that it's gonna have to be something you and the players agree on) then you'll probably have to go through and shave like, a third to half of the Purviews off of each God in the Corebook, because a lot of them use their Purviews in more metaphorical ways. Brigid, for instance, has Fire but rarely has anything to do with spraying fire from her hands, and very much focuses more on the Homey Hearth, the Flame of the Forge, and the Fires of Inspiration. So are those all invalid under your assumption?

                                            Like, it takes precisely zero mechanical adjustment to have a more literal-minded group of players go "Yeah I'll use Water to break a water main and flood the street" all the time, but what's actually GAINED by a player who REALLY wants to do something like "Oh can I use Health to hit someone's pressure points and make them explode, Fist of the North Star Style?" and the GM going "I'unno, that seems a little out there"? Like, does going "No" in this situation make the game better? Does encouraging your players to have some fun with the Purviews make things better for everyone? (Note: I'd hesitate to allow a One Hit Kill with any marvel unless it's a Trivial target but I'd allow Health to make Attack Marvels and the like by striking pressure points)

                                            Maybe it's different playstyle but I don't see how just flat-out denying attempts at being creative when players want to be makes it more enjoyable. If your table is more comfortable with more literal Purview focus, then by all means, stick there, but even in this case does it hurt the game when someone goes "Well the moon controls the tide, so I'll use a Moon Marvel to imitate the Shifting Tides Boon"
