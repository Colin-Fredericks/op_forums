    EPISODE 15: Obsession

    Late February and early March are a depressing time of year in western Washington. It’s been cold, wet, dark and windy for months. Spring can’t arrive soon enough.

    But not yet.

    (Gus wasn’t along for this adventure. He was out of town. Dunno why… maybe the Pesedjet took him up on his offer to do “jobs” for the pantheon.)

    Act One: Jaeda Versus Horndog Bob

    As always, the other Scions keep busy in their own ways.

    JACK learns that his father Lono expects him to develop greater leadership skills. His Guide challenges Jack to lead his Followers in some sort of group project. It doesn’t matter what, so long as it takes at least a day: It has to be something greater than leading a bold expedition to Burger Trough for an all-you-can-eat special. Jack — possibly influenced by his Endurance Virtue — plans an overnight camping trip in the wet, cold Washington woods and manages to talk the Defensive Line into coming along.

    JAEDA talks to Leo about the problem of Landon, since Tlazoltéotl told her the Anuna were good with divine covenants and bindings. Leo replies that Landon sold his soul to the Titans and deserves what he gets. Jaeda points out that the Titans haven’t collected yet, and what would Leo prefer: Landon bound to a pagan god of dubious morality, or Landon bound to a Titan of absolute, definite, no kidding evil? Leo passes on what his lion-demon Guide says: If Leo embraced his Anuna heritage more fully, he could learn to bind Landon in a covenant… to Nergal. Leo won’t be able to forge covenants in his own name until he becomes a God. Leo suggests this is not much of an improvement for Landon, but Jaeda keeps it in mind as a last-ditch way to keep the young shinobi out of the Titans’ hands.

    HOLLY has talked Cole, Landon and Jack’s Follower Gnu (the smart one) into joining her in a junior vigilante squad, protecting people from muggers and minor supernatural threats and solving minor mysteries. (“It’s really old Mrs. Feeny’s nephew, trying to drive her crazy for her money!” “I would have gotten away with it too, if it weren’t for you meddling kids!”) She has her magic and her superhuman Intelligence and Dexterity; Landon has his shinobi powers; Cole and Gnu have skills and guts.

    Since no one could interest one of their divine parents in Landon’s case, Holly decides she has only one force that might interfere with an attempt by the Black Bodhisattva to claim Landon for the Titans: She casts spells on him repeatedly to Fatebind him to her for life. Landon doesn’t much like the notion, but he is willing to try just about anything to keep from becoming the Black Bodhisattva’s slave. Now they just have to find out what role Landon is bound to assume, in relation to Holly, for the rest of his life…

    All this activity means that Holly spends less time with Laine, Derek and other members of her coterie of mortal friends. Derek says that he and Laine missed her; they hoped she’d come to more of the swim meets. The boys’ swim team improved over the quarter: Some swimmers who seemed to have hit their limits suddenly improved. And the whole team will need to be at their best to compete with Etna. (Avalon’s great rival never had a strong swimming program before, but this year’s team is incredible.) Actually, Derek feels a little concerned about how much some of his teammates improved their speed. He suspects performance-enhancing drugs or… something.

    A few days later, Jaeda comes home from her shift working at Big Cups Espresso to find her would-be nemesis Bob the Thrall waiting for her. He wears a long overcoat. Jaeda says that if he flashes her, he’ll regret it. Instead, Bob rants about how he and the other thralls will take their revenge for the killing of Bolvarg, because the fenrir gave them power and now they know how to use it. But Jaeda won’t be the first one they hunt: No, first they’ll kill the Scion who shot and killed Bolvarg. She’ll be next, though, so enjoy life while she can! And Bob then throws off the coat, turns into a wolf and runs away — a bit to Jaeda’s disappointment. (She looked forward to beating him up again. Or maybe getting a wolf-pelt rug to replace the Bolvarg pelt that burned with her trailer.)

    Jaeda remembers that Marshall shot down Bolvarg’s throat at the same time she gutted the fenrir and Gene ran him over with a van, making them equally responsible for his death; but she guesses Marshall tells the story a little differently. Evidently Bob somehow heard that version. After a moment’s thought, Jaeda sighs and phones Marshall to warn him that a pack of werewolves are after him. Marshall manages to thank her for the tip without sounding too ungracious, and he boasts that he will find the former thralls easy to kill.

    Then Jaeda phones Jack and Holly to keep them apprised. Neither of the two brainy Scions ever heard of fenrir thralls becoming werewolves. They figure Guibourg somehow enchanted Horndog Bob and the rest. It’s moot, though. The next day, Marshall boasts to all the Scions about how he led the werewolves away from his home, shot and killed them all. Holly hopes that Jaeda helping Marshall will reduce the hostility between the two Bands.

    Act Two: Jack Versus His Mom’s Date

    The same day begins innocently enough for Jack. His mother tells him he’ll have to make his own dinner before his shift at Stick It begins, since she won’t be home until later that night. Jack asks why. His mother blushes as she admits that she has a date. It’s a man named George Skehum, whom she met through her work, and she hopes Jack will like him when they meet. Eventually. But not this night.

    Jack spends the day in distraction. (Doctor Demerit notices and comments on it.) After school, Jack drafts a convenient, confused kid to learn the exciting world of food service by taking his shift at Stick It. Jaeda, Holly and Cole help in tracking his mother to her dinner date with George at the local Olive Garden restaurant. Jaeda immediately notices George’s classic 1967 Chevrolet Camaro. Holly spies on the couple with the Unlidded Eye. As expected, George is supernatural — but not, as the Scions half-expected, Coyote. (Why Coyote? Well, the well-built, handsome George is Native American, and “Skehum” sounds like “Scheme.” Yeah, it’s kind of dumb, but sometimes so is Coyote.)

    Instead, to the Unlidded Eye George appears as a sort of otter-man: a kushtaka, a race of lesser immortals from Pacific Northwestern myth. The Gods appointed kushtakas to save people who drown at sea by turning them into more kushtakas. Unfortunately, the kushtakas got over-zealous and took to drowning people to make sure they would be on hand to save them. While kushtakas are not totally evil, they can be pretty bad. Holly uses Mystery to learn that George the Kushtaka intends to marry Jack’s mom, then turn her into a kushtaka.

    Jack’s first thought is to break up the date and possibly attack George then and there. Jaeda thinks that’s a great idea: They can kill George, and then take his car! Surely with their Social Fu they can transfer the registry to her… Instead, Jack has Cole jimmy the trunk of the Camaro so he can hide and ride along, able to jump out at a moment’s notice.

    After dinner, George drives to a place with a view across Lake Washington to Seattle. Jack is forced to endure some lovey-dovey small talk and smoochy sounds. After a while, though, the two agree they should call it a night as they both have lesson plans to prepare for tomorrow. George drives Jack’s mom back home (more smooching before they part; Jack grinds his teeth). Then George drives to his own home — and Jack pops the trunk to confront him.

    George reacts with normal outrage at this trespass. Jack sasses back, saying “Yeah, there otter be a law.” If George catches the pun — and Jack’s hint that he knows what George really is — the kushtaka chooses to ignore it. Once Jack lays out what he knows, though, George doesn’t waste any more time on bluster. He also immediately twigs that Jack is a Scion.

    Jack lays it out bluntly: If George harms Jack’s mother, Jack will kill him. There’s no place that’s far away enough to escape him. (Atua, you know. Great voyagers.) George protests that he wants to turn Jack’s mom into a kushtaka because he fell in love with her the moment he saw her on a dive, and he wants her to be with him forever. As a kushtaka, she won’t grow old! If Jack loves his mother, how could he deny her immortality? Jack is adamant: If George wants to court and marry his mother, the kushtaka must agree to live as a human from then on, never using any supernatural powers, and letting her live a normal, human life. George then throws Jack off-balance by promptly saying, “Okay.” They part, not liking each other one bit.

    Jack hurries back to Stick It. He finds $40 gone from the cash register and a store manikin taking his place at the counter. The exceptionally nearsighted (and dim) customer demanding service does not seem to have noticed.

        EPISODE 15: Obsession (Continued)

        Act Three: Holly Versus the Vettersons

        A few days later, Holly eats dinner while pondering how to get filthy rich. None of the Internet schemes she thought of so far seem promising. She decides that she’ll simply have to study business administration, investment and the like, and just invest the money she already has to make it grow. Between her Scion super-genius and careful use of Mystery (and Prophecy, once she learns it), she figures she can assure herself a steady, and steadily-increasing, income.

        Holly hears a scratching on the roof and smells a peculiar smoky odor from the fireplace. She immediately calls Cole. A wave of weakness sweeps over her, though, and the phone drops from her hand: Holly finds herself too weak to move, stricken by a poison in the air! At the same time, she feels dizzy and sleepy: Her dinner was also poisoned.

        Holly realizes who blew poisoned smoke down the chimney when a window breaks and a pair of Skinwalkers leap into the room. Navaho legend says that Skinwalkers poison people with a magic dust that they blow into victims’ faces or drop into fires. The Skinwalkers gloat a bit. The other shoe drops when Hyndla Vetterson tiptoes from the kitchen to stand right behind the Skinwalkers. She bangs their heads together, and the Skinwalkers fall unconscious. Hyndla then turns off Holly’s cell phone. Holly falls unconscious about the time Hyndla dumps her in the Vetterson family SUV.

        Cole, of course, immediately contacts Jack and Jaeda and tells them about the alarmingly-cut off call. They hurry to Holly’s house and find the Skinwalkers just waking up.

        Jack and Jaeda do not tolerate the Skinwalkers’ attempts to bluff and wheedle. They say that Very Bad Things will happen to the Skinwalkers if they don’t cooperate. The Skinwalkers explain that they used a hollow reed to escape from the Ghost Dance Country into the World. At first they thought they had a whole new world of mortals to terrorize and exploit, but the World has changed a lot since they left it more than 100 years ago. It’s scary, they want to go home, but they can’t re-open the portal Coyote made! They thought Holly could do the job for them since she is the witch among the Scions. The Scions say that no, actually Jaeda opens the nexus, but if the Skinwalkers ever want to see the Ghost Dance Country again, they will help rescue Holly. The Skinwalkers start by turning into coyotes to sniff around for scents. They report that Holly’s abductor smelled cold. The Scions realize that the conflict they tried to avert in the previous episode is happening anyway: The Vettersons have attacked them. The Scions, Skinwalkers and Cole race to the Vettersons’ house.

        Meanwhile, Holly wakes up in a basement kitchen, trussed up with duct tape. Hyndla has a cauldron on a stove. In it she mixes honey that bees made from graveyard flowers, rain water collected at the dark of the moon, and other such ingredients. The giantess cheerily explains that it’s nothing personal. Gary is going to college next year, and she wants to get his grades up. So, she’s making blood mead — the magical Mead of Wisdom from Norse myth. She doesn’t have Kvasir’s blood, but Holly’s should serve to impart academic skills and knowledge. Mortal blood would do for a temporary endowment of wisdom, but Hyndla wants to do it right. After all, if you don’t have time to do something right, when will you have time to do it over?

        And besides, for Suttung this is personal. The jotun paterfamilias stops by the basement to say that he’s wanted to kill the Scions for some time. Starting with Holly makes sense because she could divine his plots against the other ones. His reasons? Suttung wants revenge. Decades ago, the Gods slaughtered his clan, leaving him as the only survivor of his family. Now he’s going to kill the children of the Gods — any Gods. He doesn’t know which Gods massacred his clan, so he holds all Gods responsible.

        Gary also stops by, briefly, to protest that he doesn’t need magical mead of wisdom. He already has offers from colleges in Texas, and they don’t make football players take classes for real. He’ll have nerds to do his schoolwork! But Gary isn’t willing to defy his parents outright or admit that he doesn’t want to fight the Scions because he’s dating one of them.

        All Holly’s persuasion seems to be in vain. The older giants don’t care that they’ll have to fight all the other Scions; Suttung even says he has other plans in motion to deal with them. The two giants also scoff at Holly’s claim that Gary and Reese are changing into something no longer purely frost giant that might end the war between Gods and giants. Holly decides that her dignity matters less than even the slightest chance of survival and starts screaming for help.

        Outside, the Scions make a nasty discovery. Jack, with his Atua sense for supernatural prohibitions, senses the Ward around the house. Sure enough, the Scions find they cannot enter. But it’s only a Ward against Scions! Jack and Jaeda persuade the Skinwalkers to enter the house, try using their poison smoke powder against the giants (burn it and send the smoke into the house’s excellent air conditioning system) and rescue Holly.

        Down in the basement, Holly smells the smoke and tries holding her breath. Unfortunately, Suttung and Hyndla have too much Stamina, Resistance and Epic Stamina for the Strength-draining supernatural poison to affect them. Suttung notes the presence of the Scions outside of the house, comments that he wishes he had his bazooka handy, and heads upstairs. The home’s fire alarm sounds; Holly soon hears Suttung’s roar, a crash, and the yipping of fleeing Skinwalkers.

        Holly has a final brainstorm. She asks Hyndla what information she could provide to ransom herself. After all, she can find out anything. Like… Where do other frost giants live? They wouldn’t be alone in the World. Her children could find proper jotuns to marry. Hyndla wavers, but agrees to the deal. She returns Holly’s magic mirror. While Suttung chases Skinwalkers, Holly invokes Mystery (this is a different subject than Kushtaka George and therefore is a different story) and finds that the nearest colony of frost giants lives in British Columbia. Honoring the deal, Hyndla cuts her loose.

        Outside, the Scions are surprised and delighted to see Holly stroll toward them, safe and sound. The Skinwalkers jump out windows to join them. Suttung is dumbfounded to see Holly free. She shrugs insouciantly and says, “I have a pet shinobi.” Everyone skedaddles.

        The Scions keep their word to the Skinwalkers. After collecting the third Skinwalker (stoned in the shapeshifter-witches’ hideout — Skinwalkers are debauched, self-indulgent creatures), they hurry to the Avalon High parking lot. Holly confirms that the latent nexus point still exists. Jaeda stabs it with the First Flower, and it opens. The Skinwalkers jump in, eagerly, and are gone.

            EPISODE 15: Obsession (Continued)

            Act Four: Scions Versus Good Intentions

            Even though Hyndla Vetterson released Holly, Suttung mentioned plans to attack the Scions — and to owning a bazooka. The Scions fear the jotun might try to kill them by blowing up homes or cars. That might be enough to kill even the toughest among them; it would certainly kill their mortal parents.

            Holly’s parents are safe on the East Coast. Jaeda’s father knows the score: She has no trouble moving him to another motel. That leaves Jack. It’s Friday, so he borrows money from Holly to buy his mother a weekend getaway at a mountain resort. Jack tells her that he confronted George about dating her but was ashamed of doing so, and he arranged this weekend to make it up to her. And beyond that, he doesn’t want to know. Holly warns the rival Scions that Suttung might make a move soon and endures the resulting I-told-you-sos (mostly from Marshall).

            While they arrange all this, Derek Huckovich brings further concerns to Holly. His friend and teammate Alex Totenbush is trying to sell him on something that Alex says improved his swimming prowess and could help Derek as well, warning that Derek could fall behind the rest of the team and the Etna swim team — but Alex won’t say what this “something” is. He just says it’s great, it’s legal, and Derek won’t regret it. Derek is suspicious.

            So Holly turns the Unlidded Eye on the swim team and finds that half of them — Alex and five others — are kushtakas. Well, Alex is a kushtaka. The other five kids seem halfway there. She also takes a quick look at the Etna swim team. They are all half-changed kushtakas, too!

            Between her encyclopedic knowledge and super-genius, Holly figures out that the transformation from mortal to kushtaka does not always happen at once. (In one well-known myth, a great shaman prevented his own full transformation into a kushtaka and even managed to regain his humanity. In another story, a man’s kushtaka sister started enchanting his children into otter-folk, but reversed the spell at his insistence.) Holly deduces that the half-transformed kids were drowned and changed in swimming pools, but the change will only be complete once the kids enter salt water. Killing the kushtaka who changed them should break the enchantment. Unfortunately, the kids were genuinely drowned, and they will still be drowned when the enchantment reverses. Only immediate medical help can save their lives.

            Of the Scions, only Holly and Jaeda have any medical skill. What luck, though, that Holly recently gained the Instant Seminar Knack, so she can share her Medicine Ability with others…

            The Scions debate what to do about George and how best to round up the half-changed kids. Holly remembers a favor her mother Louhi gave her in exchange for examining Bolvarg’s collar (back in Episode Six). She pulls out the business card, phones the number, and orders a hit on George Skehum. She does think to add the proviso that the assassination must not harm anyone else. (Somewhere, Louhi smiles at this sign of her daughter’s progress.)

            The Scions realize they don’t know when George will die, and to save the Kushtaka Kids they need to have them all present at that exact moment. Holly grits her teeth and turns to the one person they know with Prophecy: Delia Bright. Delia agrees to perform the Prophecy in return for a favor to be named later. Holly agrees, and Delia gives a time down to the minute.

            The Scions use their Social Fu to bring all the Kushtaka Kids together, but apart from anyone else, when Zero Hour comes on Sunday. Holly coaches Jack’s Followers, the Defensive Line, on lifesaving. Suddenly, the Etna swimmers fall over, drowning. The Scions and Followers swing into action. In the group of Avalon swimmers, though, nothing’s happening.

            The Scions realize that George didn’t change them. Well, of course not! Once they think to check, the Scions find that George was the Etna swim coach, and he wasn’t about to help the rival school’s team. Alex did. Alex had a friend on the Etna team who told him about how George magically made them into super-athlete kushtakas, and Alex wheedled George into changing him, too. Alex then went swimming in Puget Sound, completing his own transformation. And then he started changing his friends on the Avalon swim team.

            Alex is outraged by the Scions’ outrage. He helped his friends. Being a kushtaka is great! They’re immortal now. And better swimmers (thanks to Epic Dexterity). They can learn other magic powers, too. But he promises not to drown anyone else.

            Alex is a fibber, though. He manages to hustle his teammates away while the Scions are still dealing with the Etna team. They steal a van. Holly uses Ariadne’s thread to follow them. It’s a race to see whether the Scions catch up to the van before Alex reaches the seashore. Alex has a head start, but the Scions have Jaeda’s driving skills and Jack’s math genius to find the fastest routes. Jaeda forces the van off the road barely a quarter-mile from the shore. The van rolls, banging up Alex and the other kids, but the nascent kushtakas take off running.

            There’s only one way left to save the five kids. Everyone chases Alex. Fortunately, both Jaeda and Holly have Lightning Sprinter. They delay Alex long enough for Jack to catch up and grab him.

            For all the harm Alex tried to cause, the Scions don’t want to just kill him. (Holly especially feels bad about the people they couldn’t save.) But they have a cunning — and very dangerous — plan.

            Jaeda has a Knack that lets her set limits to the damage she inflicts. (With Great Power, adapted from the Scion Wiki.) She slides the First Flower into Alex, bringing him exactly to his last Dying health level. The five swimmers fall down, drowning on dry land. And then Jaeda hits Alex with her new Health Boon, Anodyne/Affliction (Heal/Infect, renamed and slightly rewritten for clarity), turning the lethal levels into bashing. Alex is still unconscious (and with a few levels of lethal damage from bashing wraparound), but he isn’t dying anymore. Jaeda sprints to the other five kids, using the same Boon on them in turn, saving their lives while Holly administers conventional first aid for drowning. (And spending all this Legend in a short time definitely Fatebinds the swim team to Jaeda in some way, not yet known to her.)

            The Scions hustle the five now-mortal kids to a hospital. Despite the ministrations of Holly and Jaeda, they did suffer drowning: They will need medical care and convalescence from the physical trauma. The Scions hit the emergency room staff with whatever Social Fu they can muster, so nobody thinks too hard about how five kids drowned in a traffic accident on land.

            Then the Scions wake up Alex and give him an ultimatum. If he ever tries turning anyone into a kushtaka again, they will kill him for real. Alex still insists that he was helping his friends, but he understands the Scions are deadly serious. (Thank you, Benefit of the Doubt.) He promises not to transform anyone, and the Scions feel sure of his sincerity, or at least of his fear.

            Jack’s mom suffers an unhappy end to her weekend, though. She had a really nice day and a half with George. On the way back from the resort, though, George crashed his car. It burned. (Jaeda grieves at the loss of the Camaro.) The remains were so badly burned they almost didn’t seem human.

            Aftermath

            Jaeda hears from Gary. Hyndla convinced Suttung that finding more frost giants mattered more than killing Scions. Arranging a trip to the wilds of Canada will take about a week.

            Gary is bummed. He and Reese told their parents they’d be fine back in Avalon, but Suttung and Hyndla believe the Scions will kill the younger giants the first chance they get. So, Gary must go to Canada too. Maybe no cell coverage! Definitely no Jaeda! “They’re treating me like a kid!” Not to mention that he needs to graduate properly in order to go to college. Jaeda doesn’t like it either, and says she’ll try to find some way for him and Reese to stay.

            (She also tells Gary how the Band stopped their rivals from their attempted ambush of the Vettersons at the ski lodge, and that Marshall probably still wants to murder the family. Gary immediately growls that he doesn’t run from fights. If Marshall wants to throw down face to face like a man, Gary’s ready any time! And he’ll even let Marshall take a free shot at him with his rifle, not dodge or anything… if Marshall gives Gary a free swing with his axe afterward.)

            The Scions also worry that Suttung and Hyndla might use the week to launch further attacks. So, they arrange to meet the Vettersons under a flag of truce, bringing Barbara along to represent the other Band. They offer a deal: None of the Scions will attack any of the Vettersons, here or up in Canada, if the Vettersons agree not to attack any Scions. They promise that Gus is honorable and will abide by a treaty negotiated by the rest of his Band; Barbara says Delia has agreed to the treaty as well, and Leo doesn’t care. Marshall will hate it, but Barbara says that she and Delia can control him.

            Suttung is suspicious, of course, but the Scions manage to convince him and Hyndla of their sincerity. (Barbara has Benefit of the Doubt: When she tells the truth she is believed, no matter how implausible the truth may be. And Hyndla has Takes One to Know One, which detects lies.)

            The Scions can see the wheels turning in Suttung’s head as he realizes the Scions gave him a pledge to let him strike first. He and Hyndla agree to the treaty.

            The Scions spend the rest of the week looking over their shoulders, nervously waiting to see if Suttung attacks them. He doesn’t. The next weekend, Suttung and Hyndla load up their extra-large SUV and drive off to Canada to search for the secret settlement of frost giants.

                        EPISODE 16: Mictlán A Go-Go

                        March brings final exams for Winter Quarter, with the promise of Spring Break after that. It also brings statewide championships for the various sports. Avalon does poorly in boy’s swim & dive, since half the team is recuperating from a freak drowning accident. Only Alex Totenbush and Derek Huckovich do well: Alex because he’s still a kushtaka, and Derek because he’s a superb athlete who didn’t fall for Alex’s promises. Still, at least Avalon has a team: The entire Etna team withdraws from competition, for reasons unspecified. On the other hand, Gus and Gary compete to see who’s the state wrestling champion. The two are perfectly matched. This time, Gary wins the title for Etna. (I flipped a coin, because they really are perfectly matched in all their relevant Traits.) Gus takes it sportingly. He tells Gary he hopes their competition continues until their final battle to see who’s the last man standing at the end of the world. Gary agrees, that’d be cool.

                        The Scions also have some enduring concerns from their last adventure. Jaeda finds Holly’s scheme to Fatebind Landon slightly unbelievable. She goes to Leo with her concern and asks to speak to his demon: Since bindings and covenants are a big Anuna thing, she figures the lion-demon is the closest thing they have to an expert. The lion-demon says that Landon’s Fatebinding will not protect him from the Black Bodhisattva in any way. He’ll just become a slave of Eternal Darkness and Evil with a Fatebinding.

                        When Jaeda passes this discouraging news to Holly, she also brings up her concern about Marshall — concerns shared by Gus. The two Scions just can’t believe that Marshall Byrd took on four werewolves by himself and won. They ask Holly to use Mystery to find what really happened. Jaeda suspects that Marshall actually lost the fight and is now a werewolf, while Gus wonders if Marshall made some kind of treacherous deal to save his life. Holly confirms, however, that Marshall told the truth and is not a werewolf. Marshall led them away from populated areas, then shot them from hiding. The other Scions point out that Gus only defeated Marshall so easily because Marshall is not primarily a hand-to-hand fighter and didn’t have Cat’s Grace to let him walk on the crust of snow. Any scorn for his abilities really is not fair.

                        Gus also takes the opportunity to ask if Marshall wants to kill him (“Yes, someday”) or intends to kill Gary within the next week (“No”). Holly still has one question left in her use of Mystery, so the Scions ask the nature of the plans Suttung Vetterson had devised to kill them all. Holly receives the brief statement, “Car bombing.” After that, the Scions with vehicles all have Jaeda check them for bombs. None are found.

                        In other matters, Jack pushes Jaeda to quit working at Big Cups Espresso and get a weekend job as an auto mechanic. She has the skills. It bothers Jack that she’s taking the easy course as a bikini barista — and he feels the irony of giving such advice when he still works at Stick It.

                        Jack also has a new motorcycle: a Ducati, an insanely fast Italian bike. A Ducati normally costs about $5000, but Jack got a reduced price on a used and damaged bike because the previous owner’s next of kin wanted to get rid of it. Reese pulls Gus away from them and tells how the Vettersons have had two families of hicks as house-guests for the last week. The Canadian giants don’t know about cell phones and Facebook. Heck, they don’t know about showers and indoor plumbing. And Reese’s parents expect her to coach them on fitting in? The worst, though, is that she suspects her parents want more than for her to have fellow giants as friends.

                                                        At that time, Norfi finishes his food and wants more. The pimply-faced young clerk tries explaining that this was not an all-you-can-eat special and if Norfi wants more food, someone must pay for it. Norfi gets a look of stupid cunning and says Gus will pay for it all — now, and any other time Norfi comes to Stick It.

                                                        Gus finds this excessively generous. Norfi tries intimidating Gus into going along. Bad idea! Gus challenges Norfi to arm-wrestle for it: If Norfi wins, Gus will pick up his tab forever. Norfi takes the bet and Gus beats him with ease. The outraged Norfi realizes that Gus is not mortal and attacks. Gus decides that wrecking the mall fighting this dumb lunk is a bad idea, so he simply grapples Norfi and holds him until he runs out of steam. Reese, meanwhile, lectures the two newcomers that this is the 21st century and the USA, people try to get along. The commotion (Norfi’s kind of loud) attracts a mall cop, but he recognizes Gus the Local Sports Hero and is satisfied he has this troublesome kid under control. Reese drags Norfi and Gunnlod away — Norfi still glowering at Gus.

                                                        JAEDA knows about the new giants in town from Gary’s calls and text messages. Gary is not happy: The Canadian giants are more than a handful, and his parents keep him busy training them. Finally, Suttung decides to move them out of the house and into a motel — the cheapest motel he can find nearby. Naturally, it is Fated that this is the Motel 6 where Jaeda and her father live. Suttung and Jaeda see each other as he and Gary move the newcomers into their motel rooms. Suttung does a face-palm. Gary rolls his eyes. Then Suttung looks back and forth between Jaeda and the new giants and gets a nasty smile.

                                                        It’s still another day until Gary can devise an excuse to get away from his parents for a date. He and Jaeda park on a lonely Hooverdale side-street and make out. When they shift to talking about Jaeda’s progress in fixing up her Torino, a figure steps out of the shadows. It’s Kwame the suspected were-leopard. (And Jaeda suspects he was watching all along, the perv.)

                                                        Kwame wondered why he wasn’t seeing Gary so much any more, and followed him. Now he knows, and he’s angry. Gary’s doing a Scion? The enemy? And actually seems to love her? That is so wrong! But there’s one way Gary can make things right…

                                                        Friends share. (And yes, Rapacity is Kwame’s highest-rated Dark Virtue.)

                                                        Jaeda watches Gary to see how he reacts. (Jaeda’s gang background means that being passed around is not entirely a new concept to her, and she knows her mother Tlazoltéotl is not exactly big on monogamy.) Gary reacts by snarling, jumping out of his car, and ripping a small tree from the ground to use as a Kwame-swatter. Kwame backs away quickly. As Gary draws back to throw the tree like a javelin, Kwame blurts angrily, “I don’t know you, man!” before running.

                                                        HOLLY, meanwhile, plays Plucky Girl Detective with Cole and Landon. She figured out that surly Mr. Burton is hiding something nefarious in his machine shop, and she wants to find out what. The machine shop is locked up tight. Landon could use his mad shinobi skills to pick a lock and disarm alarms, but he just motions for the others to wait and shadow-walks inside. A moment later he unlocks a side door and bows them in. Holly sees a shuriken in the burglar alarm box… and Mr. Burton coming through a door, carrying a shotgun.

                                                        Holly acts first, of course. She starts by kicking Burton in the stomach, but she fails to knock him out. Landon decides to intimidate Burton into submission by cutting his shotgun in half. Unfortunately, this merely results in Burton firing a sawed-off shotgun when Cole tackles him. The shot barely misses Cole. The three athletic teens quickly subdue Burton. The shotgun blast alerted neighbors, though, and the police will arrive soon. The three kids beat a hasty retreat.

                                                        Holly chews out her partners with a mix of anger, fear and relief. She tells Landon that his stunt was worse than useless. De-shinobified, he hangs his head and apologizes that he’s still new at this. Then Holly rounds on Cole and asks what he was thinking, charging a man with a shotgun. He could have been killed! Unabashed, Cole says he was thinking that if he didn’t die, Holly would be so impressed (or relieved) that she would smother him with kisses. Holly admits that, well, yes, that’s coming, but she’s still upset and she doesn’t want him to die.

                                                        (As it happens, the newspapers the next day tell how the police responded to a call about a gunshot at Burton’s machine shop. The cops found the marijuana-growing operation in a hidden room, which explained why the machine shop kept drawing power when the machines weren’t running. Taken away to jail, Burton growled, “I would have gotten away with it too, if it weren’t for those meddling kids!” But he can’t identify Holly and Cole, and including a ninja is just crazy talk.)

                                                        Holly decides to bite the bullet and use Prophecy: What will happen if she continues her relationship to Cole? And the words that emerge from her prophetic rune-song chill her to the bone. She foresees Cole hanging himself, because he and Holly finally have sex — and then Cole discovers that he is Holly’s half-brother.

                                                        Oh, crap. Cole is bound into the story of the legendary Finnish hero Kullervo, who also had poor impulse control, banged his long-lost sister without knowing who it was, and ended up hanging himself for it. How is it that Holly has a half-brother? She doesn’t know yet.

                                                        The scene ends with Holly pondering what to do next. But she won’t simply tell Cole right away. No, the daughter of Louhi must ponder every angle on how she can use the situation…

                                                            EPISODE 18: MODEL STUDENTS (Continued)

                                                            Act Two: Feet of Clay

                                                            Gus finds he has more important things to think about than giants. Spring means football practice starts again and picking next year’s team. The day after Gus encounters Norfi, Coach Reagan calls Gus into his office to discuss a very important choice.

Jack is popular with the other players, but he lacks seriousness of purpose. Gus has focus, but he challenged Coach’s authority and Coach does not easily forgive that. So Coach Reagan puts the question bluntly: Will Gus back Coach in leading the team? Gus says yes, but does not back down from his earlier deeds. He says that with him as captain, Coach Reagan will never need to call for a dirty play, no matter who they play against. Coach Reagan grudgingly accepts that and provisionally appoints Gus as next year’s team captain.

                                                            Which makes this a terrible day for Lieutenant Picinich to pull Gus from class and arrest him for assault and robbery.

Robbing that Circuit City, taking game consoles, other high-end electronic gear, CDs and DVDs, punching and shoving anyone who got in his way — all caught on multiple security cameras — was the dumbest bit of arrogance Picinich
                                                            has ever seen. All the money in the world won’t keep Gus out of jail! But… Picinich might go easy on Gus if he informs on Jaeda. The cop clearly thinks Jaeda is the Avalon Slasher, or at least criminal in some way. Gus gives nothing.

                                                            Gus calls the family lawyer, of course, and thinks furiously. The robbery took place when he was at the mall, working out at Brawn’s Gym. Cameras must have caught him entering and leaving the mall, giving him an alibi. The lawyer points this out. Picinich counters with his belief that Gus somehow slipped out of the mall without being seen, sped to Circuit City, robbed it and sped back and re-entered the mall, again somehow avoiding cameras. He just needs time to prove it, and he can hold Gus for at least 24 hours while he looks for it.

First thing, Gus tries to point out that he could not have robbed the store because he was at Brawn’s Gym, using Benefit of the Doubt to make Picinich believe this true statement. Picinich hesitates in doubt — but then grits his teeth and ripostes with the theory that Gus somehow left the mall and returned while evading the cameras. Somehow, this mortal beat Gus’ Epic Charisma Social Fu, and that shouldn’t happen.

Holly and Landon discuss it as they leave school and head for their cars. The conversation is interrupted when a giant eagle swoops down, grabs Landon and tries to carry him off.

                                                            Holly darts to her car and pulls out the Colt Peacemaker she now hides in it. Marshall, just emerging from school, also races to fight the monster. Unfortunately, they soon find that guns are useless against the Nemean monster, while Marshall isn’t strong enough to grapple it. The eagle carries Landon away. Holly, however, has time to cast The Unlidded Eye to see that the eagle is really a powerful shabti creation, just like the Nemean Corgi the Scions fought in the previous adventure. She also casts Ariadne’s Thread so she can follow the eagle wherever it goes. She finishes by calling an alert to all the other Scions. Jaeda and Marshall pile into Holly’s Lotus and speed after the eagle.

She shows up and uses Social Chameleon and Epic Socials to present herself as a lawyer with the paperwork to release Gus. The Scion of Shango works fast and gets Gus out the door before the buffaloed cops notice the legal papers she gave them are actually forms she grabbed at random from desks. Gus and Barbara drive after the others, guided by cell phone. Unfortunately, Holly, Jaeda and Marshall have a long head start.

Most likely, she realizes, the eagle reverted back to a clay figurine. Feeling foolish, she re-casts Ariadne’s Thread with Landon as the target, and regains the magical guidance. The spell leads them to a foreclosed house in Monroe with a window (and part of the wall) broken out. The three Scions decide that time is of the essence and they can’t wait for Gus.

They also find Zach, Bennie and two horse-sized Nemean leopards. Zach holds the stone uas staff the Scions had left in Jack’s care. Holly can see he’s changed: Zach is now a person of Legend, a witch empowered by the Titans, as Acacia had been.

What are you doing — oh!” Holly experiences new heights of icy rage as she realizes Zach not only disobeyed the Scions’ earlier command about never using the spell again, he made another copy of her! Giving full reign to her Vengeance, she vows that Zach will die. But then Zach just points at the Scions and says, “Attack!” The warriors and the Nemean leopards do so.

Holly orders one shabti warrior to attack another, freeing Landon, and Landon grabs Bennie to try escaping the house. Marshall finds the huge leopards are no easier to clinch or harm by gun or K-BAR combat knife than the eagle was.
