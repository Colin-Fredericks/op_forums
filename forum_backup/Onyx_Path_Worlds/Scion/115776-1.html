        I'm thinking in Ancient Greek terms, legally, a divorce is a little different from separation. Running off and leaving Zeus doesn't make him less her husband, it simply deprives him of her presence. Divorce is ending the marriage permanently. I'd have to dig through some books to look it up exactly and, obviously, there are a ton of different legal systems that would come into play - by era, city, region, culture, etc. The laws of Sparta regarding marriage and the laws of Periclean Athens won't match up and both will be very different from the Late Roman Empire.

        If Hera wanted to get divorced, she would be so bound by her Fate that she couldn't use her own power to affect that? Not even Ultimate Manipulation? Even after spending 1500 years + staying away from mortals so she wouldn't have to be completely bound by their culture-wide Fatebonds?

        If so, that sucks for PCs who get to be gods. I would probably get a little rebellious in the game if I burned all that Legend to be an Avatar or for an Ultimate Attribute and found out that I was precluded because of what some mortals thought of me. Sure, it might be HARDER because of the weight of that mass of belief influencing Fate, but if I'm clever or persistent or whatever, shouldn't I have a chance?

Sure, it might be HARDER because of the weight of that mass of belief influencing Fate, but if I'm clever or persistent or whatever, shouldn't I have a chance?
                    The problem is that that selfsame power is what makes them capable of being awesome, is what limits them from breaking their own Legend. From memory (don't quote me on this), if a Scion wants to reduce their Legend, they have to make an effort to not use the power, and to act in such ways that are not consistent with the Legend. It's not definitive by any measure, but it certainly suggests that Legend and their power is closely tied.

                    So, Hera (in the example) couldn't use Ultimate Manipulation to break Fate's hold, because Fate/Legend is what gives her that power in the first place. And if she could somehow, she'd have to rebuild her Legend to incorporate that new aspect of herself, during which she wouldn't be able to access most of her power, if she didn't lose it outright.

                    Course, talking about how God-level powers interact at this stage is inherently problematical, since a lot of them boil down to "I win", with no clear restrictions. Which is all very fine and thematic, but it does raise the problem of omnipotence, at least in part.

                    ​I don't necessarily think it'd be a problem for PCs, though (the Legend-limiting bit). Assuming that these PCs have been being played for a while, their players have probably fallen into certain patterns. These patterns then form their Legend, and while they'd find it hard to break out of them in a metaphysical sense, most of the time they'd be unlikely to do so anyway because of established roles and suchforth. Plus, there's the whole 'role-playing' aspect, so as long as you discuss with your PCs what Godhood actually means, and stick to it, I wouldn't suspect it'd be an issue.

                        This is one of the things in Scion's cosmology that I'm not entirely on board with: the Supremacy of Fate. It is a very Eurocentric concept. In Europe we have tonnes of Prophecies and Fate deities and the general overarching belief that Fate trumps all else, as seen in the Moirai, Ragnarok, the many Prophecies of the Tuatha, the Tablet of Destinies and so forth. Outside of Europe on the other hand, things are different. The Egyptians don't seem to really care about Fate one way or another, The Orisha believe that Destiny is an inner potential to be achieved, not an outer force being forced upon you, the Deva believe Fate is an Illusion and one they have all transcended through Moksha. Explicitly making Fate the Ultimate Power That Is puts a downer on those alternate views.

                                Originally posted by Allan53 View Post

                                Hmmm..... To me, Hera's role as goddess of marriage, fidelity, whatever you need to be the kind of person to stay with Zeus, is still manifest in the modern world explicitly in all the laws and customs that are integral to marriage. Even where the laws of marriage don't have a lineal dissent from Roman Law (which is a chunk of the world), said laws have had a huge effect on the way marriage is handled throughout a chunk of the world (pretty much anywhere Christianity has triumphed, for example, though that's not the limiting case... English Common Law holds significant sway wherever the British Empire once did, and, even though a lot of the roots are Germanic, there is a TON of Roman influence on it from multiple different directions.). So, if that's the Fate that is shaping Hera, she might already have the ability to divorce Zeus, particularly for infidelity, though in a lot of places, no more reason is required than that she wants to.

                                So, I guess my postulation is post hoc. Hera has already been using her Ultimate Manipulation to create an escape clause in her Fatebound role for centuries by careful adjustment of public perception, supporting women's rights, etc. Perhaps even with the tacit assistance of other gods. Athena springs to mind. She might not be 100% on board with making daddy unhappy, but HER Fatebound role of being the Eidolon of Justice might influence her into Hera's sphere on this, knowingly or not. (There's that Ultimate Manipulation again... with a dash of Ultimate Charisma).

                                The other side of this is a generalized belief that Fatebound roles aren't 100% static. I think, if your going to have a Dodekatheon that represents both the Greek and Roman conceptions of the same deities, you probably also need to have a way to have those deities have a chance to shift around in their roles a little. My big example on this would be Ares to Mars. If they are the same guy, SOMETHING happened. The petulant mama's boy/mass murdering psychopath (moreso than other gods that is) of the Iliad is not at all like the guy the Roman Legions were so fond of. Ares had to do some growing up along the way. Yes, still the God of War, but different.

                                The only way I see around that is having Fatebound roles that can be changed based on who the god is currently around. "Oh, that's the Ares side of Mars/Ares... He's not like that when he comes here. We get the guy who doesn't froth at the mouth..." Even if that seems okay, then why can't Hera just use her Epic Manipulation to create a place/time/group where her role includes the idea of being divorced and staying there (or in that part of Olympus? or that wing of the palace in the uber-Dodekatheon multi-part palace (ya know, not the Mycenean throne room, the Late Roman Imperial throne room for today's audiences...)

                                The other way it go "splitter" and have multiple different pantheons with very huge overlaps in associated myths. In which case my arguments fall through. Fatebound roles are so strong that the gods can never change and Hera can't divorce Zeus because she never did. She can run away for a bit, but she's always Fated to go back to him. Same with Juno, who happens to have exactly the same myth, but it's about her and Jupiter (which is just a Roman contraction of "Zeus Pater" anwyays).
