            It's very, very common to lump all native North American cultures together and say they're the same thing. That the Cherokee are just a tribe of "Native Americans", along with the Lakota and the Navajo and the Zuni. Sure, they're from hundreds or thousands of miles away from each other, living in entirely different ecologies and societies, but they're all basically the same! They all have Ravens and Coyotes and Thunderbirds! That attitude purvades modern pop-culture and, unfortunately, a lot of scholarly research on North American cultures as well. It's a remnant of colonialism when lumping "primitive cultures" together into archetypes was super popular because actually trying to understand them was hard work, you guys.

            Just to repeat: the idea of a single "Coyote" figure existing across multiple cultures that had little or no contact with each other in pre-modern times is exactly as weird and crazy as saying that every European Thunder God is the same figure and that their name is Thunder.

            The only reason to consolidate every single North American culture together into one big Pantheon of Archetypes is because it'd take a lot of research to actually create a Pantheon for any specific culture. It's so much easier just to consolidate all the Coyote figures into one archetypical "Coyote" even though that ignores the dramatically different perspectives each culture has on that archetype.


            You can certainly make a Pantheon out of archetypical beings, but it wouldn't be a Pantheon that anyone ever worshipped at any point in history.
