                                    Originally posted by Mateus Luz View Post
                                    As God is coming (man, that sounded VERY prophetic), I have a question, not exactly about how it works, but if the book deals with it.

                                    Diferent cultures have different ways they see the gods among us, and I want to know if the book deals with it or if all gods can manifest equally, independent of their myths.

                                    To my limited knowledge, the Theoi can be any form they wish, and belong to the material world, where they were born and grew, while Olympus (the realm, not the mountain) is just the place they call home, while it is kind of anchored to the top of the mountain. Their avatars are part of themselves, manifested by their will as they can be anything, including many things at same time.
                                    The Aesir works kind of the same way, they are born in Asgaard, but they are as physical as it’s possible, they can do amazing things, but they are kind of locked in their physical bodies, and only can affect the place they are in (kind of… they have powers and relics to reach further) and they rely on golden apples to live forever (not naturally immortals).
                                    The Netjer are different, they don’t walk in the world with their bodies, they are powerful spirits that need people (occasionally animals) to have proper physical bodies, and their natural place is their realm, where they are more solid. Their avatars are part of their powers invested into people (for example pharaohs), and they can have multiple avatars, by splitting their power among multiple subjects.

                                    Basically, Aesir are human-like aliens that use magic and their natural powers to control the elements, Netjer are Wraiths that can manifest in the world by the use of their powers/magic, but still need to control someone else body if they want to be material, and Theoi are formless magical beings with the power to do lots of random things.

                                    I may have a very limited knowledge, and my description is very simplistic, but I am almost sure the way the many cultures see their gods can be described in many different ways that don’t fit the way it was described in Scion 1e.
                                    Pantheon-specific limitations on manifesting in The World or other realms is not something that's terribly feasible from a mechanical standpoint. Like, it's an interesting idea that would help better ground the various Gods in their cultural stories, but that'll create inherent inequality among the Pantheons. It would also require that reaching a consensus on what the myths have to say about how a Pantheon manifests among mortals, which would be a tall order, given just how many myths and versions of those myths exist.

                                    So, to answer your question, all of the Gods are on the same footing in The World, although getting to and from The World might pose special challenges based on where their home is.

                                                    Originally posted by Mizu View Post
                                                    What happens if a character who has Scent the Divine comes near a character who is actively trying to conceal their presence? Does it just auto-defeat stealth attempts?
                                                    2 important caveats with Scent the Divine:
                                                    1. Scent the Divine specifically says that it "detects" when such a being is nearby. "Detect" is not a keyword, so there's some wiggle room on what exactly that means.
                                                    2. Anytime two supernatural abilities interfere with each other, it triggers a Clash of Wills (Origin, p. 105)
                                                    So, no. Stealth isn't automatically defeated, but if the Scion with Scent the Divine active wins the Clash of Wills, they have at least a rough idea of where the divine being is.

                                                    As a personal rule of thumb, I'd say they know what range band the divine being is in and the general direction, gaining an Enhancement to any rolls to pinpoint that being. But, that's my interpretation of "detect," not an official one, at least not yet.

                                                        Originally posted by demonchilde View Post
                                                        Hi Hiromi,

                                                        I saw you said earlier that most of the Origin and Hero rules you've had to learn along with the players. I was wondering now that you're the line Dev, what your recommendation would be for handling some of the discrepancies regarding Virtues and Momentum.

                                                        I know the fix for the regular Virtue and Momentum system isn't too hard to tweak and there's a few popular options discussed on the Discord, but I'm curious what your recommendation would be. Also, I'm interested in what your fixes would be to some of the periphery systems.

                                                        Specifically the Innate power for Yoga, the Deva PSP. It currently reads:

                                                        "Once per scene, when you act selflessly despite hardships in order to uphold a duty or serve someone else, you may allow another player to spend Momentum on an action without having to draw on one of their Virtues. Every point of Momentum they spend also adds another free die, as though they had the Virtuous Condition."

                                                        This seems to contain some artifact text that implies you can ONLY use Momentum when drawing on a Virtue and then grants the Scion the Virtuous condition on top of that. What would your recommended fix for this be?

                                                        Similarly, I've already submitted errata for this as it's likely an error, but the Demigod backer manuscript has a section under Gaining Divinity Dice, which refers to any dice added from Virtues counting as divinity dice, including when doubled using Momentum, which also seems to be out of left field.
                                                        Yeah, that's some odd wording. I suspect that wording reflects an earlier version of Momentum and an earlier version of Virtue that both no longer exist.

                                                        I think the easiest tweak to Yoga's wording to bring it in line with the rest of the rules would be that instead of "without having to draw on..." if it said "as if they had augmented their roll with ..."

                                                        That way, you spend your innate for the scene, then your ally gets gets to add an additional die per point of Virtue per Momentum they're adding. Then, if they're not already at +2 (and thus already have the Virtuous Condition), they get another +1 per Momentum.
                                                        • Example 1: You use Yoga. Little Mao's at 0 Yin/Yang. Mendez spends 2 Momentum on a Close Combat + Dexterity roll that has nothing to do with Yin or Yang.
                                                          • +2 dice (Momentum) +0 (Yin 0 *2d) +2d (Virtuous) = +4d for 2 Momentum.
                                                        • Example 2: Little Mao's at 1 Yin. Mendez spends 3 Momentum on a roll that doesn't have anything to do with Yin.
                                                          • +3d (Momentum) +3d (Yin 1*3d) + 3 (Virtuous) = +9d for 3 Momentum
                                                        • Example 3: Little Mao's at 2 Yin. Mendez spends everything in the pool — 6 Momentum — on a Persuasion + Presence to rock the mic, even though that's not very Yin.
                                                          • +6d (Momentum) +12d (Yin 2*6d) + 6 (Virtuous) = + 24d for 6 Momentum.

                                                        So, my tweak would make it clear that you don't need to act in accordance with your Virtue in order to spend Momentum, but there's kind of an underlying problem that being Virtuous is really good.
