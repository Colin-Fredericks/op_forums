    Champions, Immortals, and Godborn

    12-23-2013, 06:48 PM
    Something I’d like to see support for in Scion 2e is playing characters who aren’t Scions. As I envision them, there would be three kinds of non-Scion protagonists:
    • Champions start out as (extra)ordinary mortals and have the potential to ascend to a Hero-like status despite their lack of any known divine heritage. They can’t go beyond the Hero tier on their own though. The purpose of Champions is to allow for heroes of legend and mythology who don’t have any known divine heritage.
    • “Immortal” is a catchall term for semihuman or inhuman beings such as those described in 1e’s Scion: Hero and Scion: Demigod. They exist on par with Heroes and Demigods, and those who start out Hero-like have the potential to ascend to a Demigod-like status; but they cannot achieve Godlike status without help.
    • The Godborn are like Scions, except that both of their parents are gods and they start as Demigods or Gods in their own right. They exist because most gods in mythology don’t fit the “Scion” model. Their stories ought to revolve around learning about the World from an outsider’s perspective.


    Scions would still be the default protagonists of the game, and the only characters who inherently have the full upward mobility to transition all the way from Origins to God. Champions and even Immortals ought to be “adoptable”, a process by which a God takes a particularly worthy Champion or Immortal and makes him just like a Scion (giving “virgin gods” like Artemis another option besides adopting another God’s Scions or violating her virgin status if she wants Scions of her own). Such a process should be easier for Champions than for Immortals, and should still take more work than just procreating.

    Conversely, there might be an option by which a Godborn’s parents can strip him of his power, reducing him to Hero or even Origin status: this is something of a postmodern concept, but is useful in that it allows he Godborn to participate in Origin- or Hero-level games.

    But for the most part, Champions, Immortals, and Godborn aren’t intended for games that transition more than one stage. A game about Champions may transition from Origins to Hero at some point; but if/when it does, that will be near the culmination of the Champion's story — whereas for a Scion, transitioning from Origins to Hero is pretty much where the story begins.

    Thoughts?

          With certain conditions, I don't have a problem with that. It's perfectly valid to run games that don't extend from Hero to God. It can be totally cool to run a Hero-only game, or a Hero+Demigod only game. God is really its own entire game by itself. Everything changes when you cross from Legend 8 to Legend 9.

          The first condition would be no adoptions. A Scion is part human, part God. That's the definition of a Scion. A dwarf can't become a Scion of Odin, ever. They can become an amazingly powerful dwarf, but they're always a dwarf because that's their Legend. The second condition would be this coming in waaaay down the line. Get Scion right first. Then do crazy stuff, if you must.

          I like Scion being a game about playing the children of Gods. That's what it said it was on the box, that's what I'd like to have inside the box when I open it. It didn't say "modern epic fantasy" or "urban fantasy kitchen sink", it said "children of the Gods". Cool, sold. And I think it loses something of its specialness when you turn Scion into urban-fantasy-kitchen-sink with angels, demons, dwarves, elves, saints, naga and Superman as playable characters.

As you’ve probablt noticed elsewhere, my own preference is to keep the focus on real-world mythology returning to the modern world, with the children of the gods at the vanguard of said return but not holding a monopoly on it.

                  Personally, I’d want Champions, Immortals, and Godborn in Origins, Hero, and Demigod, respectively; failing that, I’d want them featured in the 2e’s counterpart to Scion Companion, a fifth book in the series. It’s not something to be postponed for an arbitrarily distant “down the road”, like inclusions of Santa Claus or Superman would be; rather, it’s an essential part of the toolkit for enabling as much of real-world mythology as possible.

                                          Exalted is a giant mess of awful in its quest to be the big tent for all fantasy role-players to get their DBZ on under. I would rather not see something like that happen to Scion.

                                          The diluting of the core product is certainly not WW/OPP's sin alone, but they are perhaps the worst repeat offenders in the industry. Storyteller/Storytelling/Storyfap or whatever it is being called these days isn't presented as a system toolkit intended to support a wide array of games, it's presented as a group of mechanically-related but distinct games. Trying to fit more themes into a single setting inevitably causes thematic drift that hurts the overall tone of the game. ("Forever unclean!")

                                          With that said, I don't object to any of Dataweaver's proposals as possible ground for exploration. I think the game loses nothing exploring the more complicated sections of mythology, like ascended monkeys and fully divine children as playable characters. I also think that only a narrow handful of games and gamers would actually benefit from specific rules for handling those kinds of characters, however. You could probably cover the latter two concepts easily in sidebars, honestly.
