                                Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                                They made a movie about it:Windtalkers. ​Also, Navajo is NOT the correct name, that's actually Anasazi for "enemy". The correct name is Diné.
                                Navajo is the correct name in several important venues. For example, the US treaties are with the "Navajo Indians", not the "Dine Native Americans". Changing the appellation could (and has, for other tribes and nations and villages and communities) confused the issues (like land ownership, government assistance) in a very very bad way for the Native American group involved. Leadership in several tribes I worked with at one point were very definite on this point. So it's something that is very much a matter that has to be addressed on a case by case basis.

                                It's worth noting that the name "Navajo" isn't a pejorative term from nearby culture (like "Sioux" aka "those untrustworthy snakes" for the Lakota or "Eskimo" aka "raw fish eaters" for the Inuit). It's a Tewa word that means "Those guys from the wide valley".

                                "Dine" as the Navajo name for themselves is correct but not dispositive. It, or a very close variant, is the word for "people" in most Na-Dene languages, which includes a number of cultural groups from the US Southwest including, but by no means limited to the Navajo. It also includes many tribes in British Columbia, Yukon and Alaska as well. The Navajo call themselves either Dine or Naabeeho. The Navajo Nation, in Navajo, is Naabeeho Binahasdzo.

                                Anasazi is a world IN Navajo meaning "Ancestors of our Enemies" or "Ancestral Enemies" referring to the Ancestral Pueblo cultural ruins in Navajo Territory. The Anasazi aren't the ancestors of the Navajo. The Anasazi were the ancestors of the Pueblo tribes, including the Hopi and Zuni. We don't have any Anasazi words as the culture was effectively gone (well... moved) by 1350 AD. The languages of the Pueblo tribes are almost certainly descended from whatever the Anasazi spoke.

                                The Anasazi are the "bad guys" in some Navajo myths and legends. A mythical vanished people who fell from grace, were sorcerers, etc. etc. Which might have some back-up archaeologically. Some of the Anasazi practices seems to have been the sorts of things that many other cultures find troublesome. In a sort of the same ways the later Aztecs squick people out. (And they were in contact with Mesoamerican cultures with those kind of customs... evidence of chocolate and macaw feathers that were imported from Central America have been found. But so were boiled and gnawed-on human bones...)
