    Atlantean hate??

    01-18-2016, 01:48 AM
    I've noticed there's a whole lot of hate (or at least general distaste) for the Atlantean pantheon, and I'm sort of confused as to why. Is it simply because of the fact that it was published with the mention the developers had no plans for using them (which I can totally understand) or is it actual distaste for the pantheon itself?

    Because in all honesty, I find the pantheon fascinating, albeit pointed, and was able to use it as a building block for our very own self-esteem pantheon. Additionally, I didn't find much wrong with the pantheon or its purview, or at least not a lot more than other pantheons.

      In our very first multiple-story campaign, which mirrored many events of LRtH, my players were brought to the fallen Overworld of the Atlanteans by a demigod, where they encountered a preserved scion of Badarus, who, upon being awakened from cryo, agreed to help them with their quest if they helped him restore Atlantis.

They joined the young man, who was at this point a member of their Band, in finding the cause for the desolation in Atlantis, and beginning to reverse it, all the while encountering clues left behind by Badarus, whose far-reaching and nearly exact foresight allowed him to predict the plot of the quest.

              I imagine it's the same reason players were disappointed by the addition of Atlanteans as a new Pantheon in the Age Of Mythology expansion pack, as opposed to a faction based on an actual mythos. When a large part of your appeal is Zeus and Thor having a fistfight over thunderclouds, popping in with a wholly invented thunder-deity to act as a third patron lacks the same appeal – just as a fight between Captain America and Batman isn't going to draw much bigger crowds if you throw in Justice Bob, your new OC, no matter how marvellously he's been conceived.

              That said, in Scion's specific case it probably didn't help that the role of the Atlanteans – illustrating a Pantheon that had been utterly cast down and forgotten – could have been filled by other, real-world Pantheons who have been almost or completely lost to us. I imagine the next edition will approach that same idea from a very different angle.

                  People knew that Scion was a limited-book gameline, that it was only going to get a very few books. So there was a lot of feeling that the space given to the Atlantean and WW2 Pantheons was "wasted" space that should have gone to more real-world Pantheons. In a larger game line, I think that resentment wouldn't have been as strong. But when it's one with a very small number of books, there's a strong feeling that unwanted material is 'stealing' space from more 'deserving' material.


                  Which I actually agree with. Somehow an alternate-history WW2 setting with "gods" made up from newspaper cartoons was cooler than Babylon? Laaaaaame.
