            I think you're (perhaps willfully) interpreting the Order virtue a little over-zealously. An Orderly Scion isn't Lawful Stupid -- they still have the capacity to run ethical calculi, interpret events or situations under the totality of circumstances, and consider exigencies, mitigating, and exaggerating factors.

            They're not going to have to roll to shirk the Order virtue to jump in front of a bus to save someone because that would constitute jaywalking. It's an exigent circumstance in which, even if you want to play rules lawyer (or lawyer-lawyer for that matter), criminal (and civil) liability is exempt under good samaritan law. In fact, in some jurisdictions a scion with Order would have to roll to shirk to not "jaywalk" because reasonably aiding others is obligated by law. Using a weapon in self-defense would not require an Order roll similarly, as self-defense is a mitigating circumstance. Either way, it's a petty, nitty interpretation of a Virtue which makes things incredibly difficult and annoying for the player.

            Ask yourself this: do you have equally-stringent interpretations of other virtues? Would a scion of the Aesir have to roll Courage to not beat down a little kid who gave them a raspberry (as that constitutes A PHYSICAL CHALLENGE!)? Would a scion of the Dodekatheon have to roll Vengeance to not go full-on road rage if some idiot driver merged in front of them without signalling? No? then why the blue blazes would scions with Order be treated thus?

                                    Originally posted by Charles Red View Post
                                    I suppose you are right, which would mean the guy with Courage would have to respond to the challenge. Of course, you can be rather nice about it and gently put the kid in his place rather than exploding his skull.
                                    "...A failed Courage roll allows a character to: avoid the prospect of battle, resist a physical challenge, surrender to an opponent..."

                                    It doesn't necessarily mean a straightforward, plainly-spoken, challenge to duel or fight. An insult or taunt, no matter how slight, can be interpreted as the prospect of battle or a physical challenge, and to let that slide means the character is shirking their virtue. Likewise with the Dodekatheon example...note I didn't say the driver was rudely cut off; I just said the guy merged in front without signalling, you guys interpreted it as such.

                                    Which was my entire point. It lies in interpretation. Any of the virtues can be interpreted to literal, purely punitive, and chronicle-derailing extremes. What the OP is doing is cherry-picking one virtue and interpreting it as that extreme, without considering the big picture or relevance to a chronicle, while making that implicit concession to all other virtues. That's extremely fallacious reasoning.

                                    Consider another example: a scion with order is in a car chase with a Titanspawn. Is that scion going to stop at every red light, stay within the lanes, signal turns, and obey each and every rule of the road? No, by no possible stretch of the imagination would they, or even could they as pursuit and deliverance of fugitives to justice is pursuit of the virtue itself (and to not, is to shirk the virtue) -- breaking lesser laws in the pursuit of the higher goal in this case is waived due to exigent circumstances. An orderly scion would certainly make every effort to limit the chaos they cause in the chase, but the higher goal always wins in the long run.

                                    EDIT: Hell, I'll throw this one in too. Scions of the Dodekatheon don't go into constant virtue extremity between black Friday and New Year's, do they. They have Vengeance, and the entire holiday of Christmas is a giant fuck-you to the Dodekatheon. Christmas is a fusion of Yule (a Germanic, and therefore barbarian, holiday) and Saturnalia (itself a fusion of a holiday worshipping Sol Invictus -- i.e. Akhenaten -- and Saturn -- i.e. Cronus), coopted by Christianity (which in the Scion world is a faith of Titanic origin). Scions of Poseidon should be especially pissed, since ancient Greek solstice celebrations were in his name which is completely forgotten in the modern day.

                                                Exigent and mitigating circumstances, as they're typically known. Situations in which liability for breaking one law is waived in light of the totality of circumstances, usually in the case of emergencies or enforcing/prohibiting a greater offense -- a good (non-controversial) example from US criminal procedure is that a police officer can enter a domicile without residents' permission or a warrant (therefore trespassing, and violating the 4th Amendment) if they believe the life or health of someone inside the residency is in imminent danger.

                                                In Scion terms, let's say a hungry man-eating Titanspawn has performed a home invasion and has the family tied up in the basement. The would-be rescuing Scions must, by definition, trespass to save the family. Should that require an Order check? absolutely not, as first there's an exigent circumstance (the family is in imminent danger of being eaten) and second to allow it to happen (by action or inaction) is a direct, greater violation of Order (since the Scion would be allowing a family to be murdered and eaten, and for their captor-turned-diner to go free).
