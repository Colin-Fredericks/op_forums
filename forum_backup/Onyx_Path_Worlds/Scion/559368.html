    Missing Purviews!!! Or what new Purviews do we Want/Need?

    06-18-2015, 10:47 AM
    I like the 16 general Purviews in the main book. The Additions of Frost, Stars, and Illusion, in later supplements were welcomed. However some divine powers seem to be missing.

    Among those I think we need/want are.....

    A) Passion Sure the Epic socials allow you to manipulate your emotional connections to others, but they don't allow you to simply cause one mortal to fall in love with another. Or lust. Or hate.

    A Passion Purview would cover things like, creating emotional bonds between individuals and groups, raising or lowering the passions of a community, changing the nature of passions or at least for the express themselves in the world. The highest levels could remove a passion from the world for a time or create a new passion!

    B) Forge The Smiths of the Gods are a widespread phenomenon. A Purview that covers crafts and technology is needed. the Atlantean Purview Scire would suggest some of the powers that would fit this purview. This a power that deals with technology would fit the scions of Wayland the Smith, Hephestus, or Shango in his role as God of Truck Drivers (yes Shago is the God of truck Drivers and worshiped as such in several nations).

    C) Language/Communication This purview would cover Language, communication, knowledge, and symbols, important mythic catagories. Scire mentioned above has some ideas for this purview. At lower levels in would allow the enhancement or blocking of communication and learning., the gift of tongues, and the ability to decode/encode information. Higher levels could remove knowledge or even whole languages from the world for a time.

    D) Music/Harmony Just as we have the Chaos purview for divine disorder we need a purview of divine order. As Music is the oldest and most emotive metaphore of divine order it would be deeply tied to this purview.

    More later, times up.

    What are your ides?

          Time powers, like stop time, slow time, speed up time, move super-fast via time, travel in time.. that's all very much a product of 1900s fiction and relatively recent understanding of how time/space works. Ancient gods of "Time" are really more like calendar gods, or gods of the seasons, or gods of entropy. They don't freeze things in time. The nearest you'd come is to how sometimes there are magical places where a day passes in a thousand years, or vice versa. There's just not a lot to work with unless you bring in modern superhero type powers.

                                                      As someone who's adapted several mythologies and magical traditions for multiple game systems, I can say that the relation between the source material and the gameable adaptation is always a bit like the relation between a cow and a meatloaf. Scion is no exception, but... after you've slaughtered and butchered the cow, selected the cuts of meat, ground them, added seasonings and other ingredients, patted it into shape and baked it, you should hope it at least still tastes like beef. If the meatloaf still manages to be cow-shaped, that's a bonus. Scion has it extra-difficult because it takes a cow, a pig, a chicken, and an iguana, mixes them all together -- but at the end, people still want the meatloaves to retain their taste.

                                                      I am not sure the Purview system was the right approach. It's what we have until 2nd ed. comes out, though, and it's what Astromancer asked about, so that's what I'll discuss.

                                                      Forge is indeed the most egregious lack from the Purview list, though I would rather call it Artifice: Smith-gods aren't the only mythic artisans (and Craft is already taken for an Ability).

                                                      Other than that, my biggest wish would be to give Mystery and Prophecy the axe and make them Boons in a combined Purview of divination and knowledge. Call it Oracle, maybe. The various divinatory spells such as Ariadne's Thread go in Oracle as well.
