    There are a lot of rough areas that need work. but ultimately, since the heavy lifting of developing the foundations of the system has already been done (we have 2e to build from, and the difference shouldn't be anything like the step from 1e to 2e was), it shouldn't be impossible to put out the core books, containing all the needed mechanics, and THEN add complete supplements on the Titans/Mythos/Dragons/Sorcerers, expanded Pantheons, world books, and so on. The other main RPG companies follow the "core then expand" model (DnD, Pathfinder, Call of Cthulhu, Vampire, etc), so it isn't outrageous to ask that OP follows that example. I can say that I won't support a piecemeal approach at all - I got burned with 2e taking so long to get the core books out, and won't do that again.

                    As much as I want further expand on how different The World would be due to the "All Myths Are True" setting... Yeah, I can see that not happening. The history of the World's probably for the Storyguides to decide for their own game since a singular "canonical" answer will probably rub people wrong one way or another because they will each have their own idea on what's supposed to happen. And that's ultimately going to result in a setting completely unrecognizeable from our own that the developers might as well just invent something from the whole cloth and dump the characters in. And some of us are interested in player modern-day demigods in the actual modern world, not Generic Fantasy World #2599825295 with a completely original map, countries, cultures, geopolitical dynamics, etc., but the Gods remain as it for no explainable reasons.

                    "Oh, but the Gods being real should mean [particular tragedy] shouldn't happen!" Religious people still exist. They existed then and they exist now. Despite all the tragedies that have happened, sometimes to them, their people, and their cultures and countries, their faith still remain and they still believe there's a God (or Gods, or Ancestors, or Higher Beings, etc.), the God's existence is stronger than anything science could prove, and such tragedies still happened despite the God's existence. If they could wrap their heads around that idea and continue to live their lives, then you could sustain your disbelief long enough for a session.
