                                                  [Vaguely on-topic]
                                                  I briefly toyed with the idea of importing Scion's epic attributes into the nWoD... for use with the templates of that game (Read: I did not intend to have the children of gods in my game, rather I wanted an Epic Attribute mechanic for monsters like Werewolves).
                                                  I had several ideas, such as alternating target number drop and exploding dice drop for each epic dot, but most of them fell apart at high levels (sort of like Scion 1E does, I suppose). Recently, though, I came up with something that works well.

                                                  Lowering target number is the right way to go with epic attributes to keep them the closest to their original purpose (Mummy the Arisen taught us that). The problem is the ability to have epic attributes go so high! It is best to limit how quickly epics can grow, just like attribute growth is limited. I adopted a one-per-level-of-godhood system.


                                                  So here's the system.

                                                  At Legend/PowerStat 1, you may purchase up to 1 dot of epic attribute in any attribute. Each dot of epic attribute owned reduces the target number of rolls involving that attribute by 1--so using Strength 3 Epic Strength 1 results in a roll of 3 dice where 7s and up are successes.
                                                  For every three additional legend the character accumulates, her maximum number of epic attribute dots increases by one to signify her transition to the next stage of godhood. That is, a Hero has 1 Legend and can go up to Epic 1, a Demigod has 4 Legend and can go up to Epic 2, a God has 7 Legend and can go up to Epic 3, and a Patriarchal God/Matriarchal Goddess (Picture Zeus/Hera) has 10 Legend and can go up to Epic 4. Epic 4 reduces the target number to 4, which is tremendously useful for rolls with that attribute (but doesn't actually increase dice pools in the slightest, that's for regular attributes. Not quite as fantastic as Epic 12 in the Scion system, but you've got to tone things down for nWoD.

                                                  Each dot of epic attribute costs New Dots x 13 (or I suppose New Dots x 11 for the specialty epics; like I said, I wasn't going to use gods for this). Characters can purchase epic attribute dots EVEN IF THE PURCHASE EXCEEDS THE DOTS OWNED IN THE ASSOCIATED ATTRIBUTE. This allows for strange characters that appear inept but have bursts of incredible ability, usually when coupled with Willpower.


                                                  Like I said, I wasn't intending to actually use Scions with this system, so I don't have anything to say about Boons or Knacks. I just thought it might help to see one of the ways you might handle epics in nWoD.
                                                  [/Vaguely on Topic]



                                                  [Actually on Topic]
                                                  I was in a game where the Storyteller tried to cross Scion with the classic World of Darkness, with Scion being the 'graft' setting. I played a mage, we had one other, one werewolf, two demons, and two Scions. Right around the Demigod line everyone became outpaced by the Scions; the Storyteller tried to fix this by allowing the other templates to purchase epic attributes, but only the mages recovered. The other players wrote themselves out and made new Scion characters to compensate.

                                                  Interestingly enough, as we breached the God transition the mages began to outpace the Scions! Auto successes with attributes, coupled with our storyteller's interpretation of archmastery (which was the 'redirection' of paradox onto other entities) gave the two mages WAY more flexibility than any of the Scions could muster.

                                                  I got a little carried away with it, actually, making an army of squirrel ninjas and trees that grew fully cooked burritos on demand... among other zaney things. He DID make us go insane from the power, though, so I guess that's balance of some sort. For instance, in one scene my mage forgot whether or not he was allergic to bees when one flew into his face. To protect himself from death, he transformed the bee into a bear... because that was the most reasonable response he could muster. Common sense really doesn't apply when you can twist the world into ribbons without personal consequence.

                                                  So it CAN work, but it is going to require some clever footwork on both your part and the part of your players. You have to trust that they will not be as crazy as me. Also, that game's non-Scion characters were using oWoD mechanics, which made them slightly more compatible with the Scion system.
