    Godly Marriage and Divorce

    04-19-2014, 07:16 AM
    Me and my playgroup were joking about godlymarriage/divorce and we laughed a lot, then i had the idea what woul happen in scion:

    1) Two gods from the same/different pantheon/s are married, do one of the gods become member of the other pantheon or both become honorary member of the so?

    2) One of the gods dies, what happens to his belongins sons/daughters etc?

    3) the god who died comes back from the dead, is the marriage still intact or not(... till death do us apart)

    4) what could be cause of godly divorce? what WOULD happen in a divorce aside pettines and political fallout?

      Interesting question. Personally, I'm of the view that as someones Legend grows (ie, they become gods), they become constrained by said Legend. Ares can't back down from a fight, because his Legend binds his behaviour as much or more as it warps the world around him.

      So, in this scenario, they wouldn't marry a god from another pantheon, because they wouldn't...resonate? like that. And if they did, I suspect their marriage would carry a lot more metaphysical weight, so they wouldn't get a divorce. Hera and Zeus are still married, despite their deep antipathy: the marriage becomes part of their Legend, and this cannot be removed simply.

              I don't see why Gods of different Patheons might not marry. We have no such example in mythology, at least none I'm aware of, but that doesn't mean it can't happen in game, and certainly it can happen among player characters. In any case, how they are viewed by the two Pantheons depends a lot on the Virtues, and the circumstances of the marriage, and with how each Pantheon views each of the married couple.

              If they have kids when they are Gods, i'd imagine they would decide to which Pantheon they belong to, and that would be it. If they have Scions (by doing it in Avatar form), then their Scions will belong to whichever Pantheon activates them first, which will likely involve lots of political maneuvering.

              Death isn't an issue to all gods, so I don't think their marriages are "Till death do us apart".

                      1) Two gods from the same/different pantheon/s are married, do one of the gods become member of the other pantheon or both become honorary member of the so?
                      1) What we know about the actual Gods is that mortals keep them "alive" by still believing in their existances. If two Gods happened to marry each other, every mortal would have to acknowledge it to make it happen. The book states that Gods are made of mortals' expectactions which is legit since mythology was invented by Man. But everything is linked to Fate. In order for two Gods to be married, mortals are the only one in charge and I'm not sure that collective unconscious is powerful enough to mix any more ancient Gods together.
                      Without marriage, there are still gods and goddesses that appear in different mythology, especially the Wicca. Some Gods are borrowed and that's because it was wanted. Artemis might belong to the Dodekatheon, but if we take into account that the Wicca still exists, she might just wanna be Wiccan instead of Greek. If being known means to exist, two married Gods should join the pantheon that is most likely to be the strongest. I hope I was clear enough...

                      2) I don't really get the "what happens". If you're talking about heritage, I don't think it works that way. In order to become god, you have to build your Legend so they won't take his place until he fulfills his part of the deal or... Fate makes him God.

                      3) Technically, the bond was broken when the God died. And so the marriage. Coming back to live means he died once. But that is only available for Christians. Ancient Gods may be tied by stronger bonds (fatebinding) and marriage may be the ultimate binding.

                      4) Since no God ever got divorce, it's hard to tell. Still, I don't think it can happen because marriage is a sacred vow that cannot be broken. That's why Hera gets revenge on Zeus' offsprings or his lovers. And again, divorce is a too new concept for Ancient Gods.

                                                      While the other questions have been adequately answered from a variety of viewpoints, I'd like to point out that there is a case in mythology of a semi-divorce, in a sense. Now, obviously, divorce in our modern context is not something we find much of in mythology, there is a case where one God basically packed her bags and left with all intention to end the marriage, and that God happens to be Hera. Hera, tired of Zeus' constant philandering, left Olympus, and Zeus was devastated because contrary to popular belief, and contrary to our modern sensibilities, Zeus is very much in love with Hera. He moved heaven and earth to get her back, and when she refused to surface he resorted to announcing that he was remarrying. Hera heard this and instantly forgot her anger in her jealousy (because Hera is also, contrary to popular belief, in love with Zeus), and rushed to the wedding to find that the 'bride' was a wooden doll and Zeus was ready to remarry her.

                                                      I would also like to point out that Hera is technically not the Goddess of Marriage (That's Hymaeneus' job). She's the Goddess of Marital Fidelity, and hence can totally divorce Zeus without it interfering with her role.
