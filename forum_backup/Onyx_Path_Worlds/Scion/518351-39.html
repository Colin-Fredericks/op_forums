            The way I see it, the problems with 1e Scion was that the mechanics of the system got in the way of the fun... especially at the highest levels when the mechanics prevented a lot of things (like being able to hit something with a high level of Epic Dex without having a high level of Epic Dex to counter it).

            Rather than try to fix a broken mechanic, the new system is trying to deal with the problem my having a system in place that can handle everything from mortals beating on each other to Gods throwing planets at Titans (probably more figuratively than literally... but in Scion... who knows?).

            In Scion 1e, you had to adjust the concept of characters to what the mechanics could allow... Scion 2e hopes that the mechanics will adaptable to whatever concept a player can imagine for their character. You want a brute of a Storm God like Thor who basically uses storms and such as a blunt weapon... BAMM! Done. You want a more thoughtful Weather God who uses her power more as a fine tool (to give her worshipers a bountiful crop as well as to defend them with powerful storms)... that can be done too.

            Just as one can't use a Gun to heal a person of the wounds it creates, so too will the abilities of Relics be somewhat limited in what they allow others to do with them. Yes, if you take Giantsbane from Eric, you will be able to summon powerful storms... but a gentle rain is unlikely simply because Giantsbane was never intended to be that subtle (it is a weapon of war... not a gardening tool). If you want a Relic that can create a gentle rain... why would you try stealing Giantsbane in the first place? It isn't exactly known for doing such a miracle. It would be like stealing a nuclear weapon so that you can give people a suntan. Overkill at best... but much more likely to be a disaster.

            Does Giantsbane's inability to function in this way constitute a fatal limitation? Not really since Eric is not the kind of Character who would even think of using it in such a manner.

                        The problem with epics was that they more or less couldn't be fixed without changing the system... any attempt to do so would probably be temporary at best (sooner or later, the underlying problems with them would resurface). Working with a new system from the ground up allows for the fixing of this problem (or more precisely, making sure the problem doesn't happen)... and to deal with other issues that the old system also had.

                        Of course with this change, other things that used a similar system as Epics (namely Purviews that also used the 12 tier system) would need to be reworked to fit within the new system. In doing so, it could allow for a much more diverse and robust method of defining the powers of the gods... so that two gods of the same level of power and area of influence aren't mirror images of each other in what they can do. This hopefully allows a Scion of Thor to be a Storm God focusing exclusively on storms while a Scion of Quetzalcoatl can be a Goddess of Rains in general and can do much more than cause destruction with her powers. Before, the two would more or less have identical powers with the only difference between them being their fathers... at least in regards to the Sky Purview.

                        The simple fact is, the flaws in 1e was that the system was never intended to define such characters and the more they tried, the more problems doing so caused. The fact that virtually everyone who plays the game has had to make extensive house rules at some point to make the game playable means that the game is inherently flawed on some level and needs to be rebuilt. Will doing so alienate some players? Maybe... at least until the try the new system and see for themselves if it is better or not. Yes, there will be some changes that many might not be happy about, but that happens in every new edition of any game. If you aren't game to try the new system... that is your choice.

                        The end result is that the decision has been made. You can either deal with it and try to find something in the new system that gets your imagination fired up... or get left behind with your memory of the way things were while the game moves on without you. You are right about one thing... the new system may indeed have its own issues... but will they be as bad and as unplayable as 1e's were? Only time will tell.
