    Ainur preliminary thoughts

    02-14-2019, 12:49 AM
    Pantheon motif: Faith and determination can work miracles.

    Virtues Responsibility and Joy

    The Ainur are motivated both but the joy of Eru’s creation and also by the responsibility of their position as stewards. They fear to do more harm with their meddling than good and approach everything from the most cautious of attitudes. In some cases their particular joy may be frustrated by conflicts with their responsibility and they turn aside from their roles of steward to selfishly engage their desires as Morgoth and Sauron has. In other cases their responsibility may lead them to forsake the things that bring them joy for fear of destroying them. Withdrawing from the world as the Valar have.

    Cosmology - The Ainur are fated to remain with the world until it comes to an end. As a pantheon they have neither an Underworld nor an Overworld but live in numerous terra incognitae of variable greatness ranging from the former Rivendell to great Valinor, which can only be found by those who remember the way from when the world was still flat.

    Scions - almost all Ainur scions are Incarnate Maiar who have set about to trickle back into The World either to experience it for themselves or to attend to the will of Eru.

    Technically speaking those are the only Ainur. But some Elves could be argued to have reached God tier for such accomplishments as building the Silmarils or piloting a ship that holds such a jewel as a star in the sky. Also. Some humans have reached the Demigod tier but as they are not bound by Arda’s Fate all Men move on rather than become lasting cosmic figures of the world. Still it is possible to include some Elves and Men as Chosen, especially those that are educated by one of the Maiar.

    It should be noted that One who had no human blood was granted the Gift of Man, Luthien. So it is conceivable that a human could appeal to Eru in someway and turn aside from the Gift of Man. In doing so this person would no longer be human as the nature of their spirit would have changed. Humans are by definition free of the bonds of Arda so if a human becomes bound to Arda they are no longer bound. This keeps the God tier for the most powerful and cosmically impactful of Ainur and Elves but still allows for an PC that starts human to advance to God tier. Though they at this point would suffer the doom to see the things that bring them joy eventually and be replaced by things that would also later die off.

    Elves also have a reincarnation system and thus can be played as Incarnates.

    There is only record of one born scion in the Legendarium, Luthien, but her existence indicates that the Ainur and Eru don’t consider it a matter of cosmic importance. Also elves and men less touched by the lies of Morgoth may be closer to the original intentions of those people and be considered born scions. As a note, in order to have a child an Ainur has to Incarnate as either a human or elf and their children will be considered of the appropriate people.

    Created scions are a whole issue though. Trying to create life through means outside of the will of Eru is liable to lead an Ainur to fall. Aule managed it with the dwarves as it became apparent that he created them to add to Eru’s creation rather than usurp it. Created scions either have been similarly approved by Eru or else have severe issues resulting from their creator basically playing Frankenstein.

    Titans. Morgoth - Chaos. Fecundity, Submission. Frustrated by the constraints placed upon him by Eru, Melkor slowly became more and more twisted until he fell entirely. He started trying to replace elements of Eru’s creation with his own and we he failed in that he began to try and twist Eru’s creations toward his own designs. As each of these became more and more twisted and ugly, he finally just desired to torment Eru’s creations.

    Sauron - Deception (Forge suggested as better option). Dominance, Submission. Sauron fell through his love for Melkor and the greatness that he used to be. He applied all his craft and power toward achieving Melkor’s goals. Even as Melkor became Morgoth Sauron was unable to see as his liege grew more mad over time. When Morgoth was finally banished he had a moment of regret but pride prevented him from returning to face Manwe’s judgment. From this point on he grew increasingly insane until he was finally defeated. He exists now as a ghost of a ghost. Unlike Morgoth, whose Iron Crown was reforged into manacles, Sauron’s One Ring was destroyed and he had no way of gaining a new fana with which to influence the world. Though perhaps some sorcerer might bind him into a corpse or wolf and open the path for him again by accident.

    Ungoliant - Beasts(spider) (Darkness suggested as a better option. I also considered Passion(Hunger)). Rapacity, Fecundity. Little is known of Ungoliant save that age and her children seek to devour the force of creation. She is not one of Morgoth’s creatures and indeed grew to threaten him at one time. Some suspect that she is not even if the Ainur but something else entirely.

    Feanor - Passion(Vengeance). Rapacity, Dominance. Feanor is not Ainur but is an elf, though his impact on Arda places him here. An elf of great power and wrath who at one time battled Morgoth himself for a long time before being slain. Feanor and his sons are bound by oaths to retrieve the Silmarils and slay all who would stand between them and that goal. They are first kinslayers and while some tried to redeem themselves they often became as great evils as Morgoth’s lieutenants were. As elves it is possible that they could reincarnate anew and once again begin their campaign of destructive vengeance. For now, though their spirits remain in the Halls of Mandos.

    Pantheon Purview - Craft (filler name). Magic in Arda is a term used by the less educated to refer to the crafts of the learned and also the deceptions of the Enemy. The results of these skills seem supernatural to onlookers but in reality all that has been done is to allow nature to work in complete and total efficiency toward the desired of the end of the one performing the spell.
