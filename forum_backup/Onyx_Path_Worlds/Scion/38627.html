    Shiva and Kali aren't evil

    12-29-2013, 03:25 AM
    I can understand many of the mistakes that the creators made about Hinduism, such as Vishnu has been with many women not just Lakshmi as that is one of his allegorys about religion, but Kali and Shiva are not evil. In fact other than Vishnu they are some of the most popular Gods in Hinduism. Kali can be destructive, violently so, but she is a good god, and I have no idea how they decided Shiva was evil except that his part in the universe is destroyer of Worlds which isn't an evil thing at all if you understand how it is meant. yes Shiva beheaded his son Ganesh, but that was because he had no idea he was his son and thought he was actually hurting his wife who had told Ganesh to stand guard while she bathed. As soon as he found out about his misunderstanding, his tears and sorrow prompted an elephant to loan his head for Ganesh. Which has a religious meaning as well. I feel that Kali and Shiva were just portrayed with almost no understanding of who they are at all. Take a Kali, she is supposed to be frightening...sometimes. But she is known as a slayer of evil, a protector, a mother, and even sometimes one of the most loving goddesses. It felt like they felt they had to put some evil god in there and just assigned it to them.

                  I was born and raised a Hindu in Kolkata, which is Kali's sacred city and the centre of her worship. Kali is my Ista-Devi, which is Hinduism shorthand for favourite God (though it holds far deeper meaning than just that), so suffice to say that I have more than some passing knowledge of the subject. That being said, I don't find Companion's Kali write-up to be portraying her as evil so much as only showing one side of her. Kali is indeed, a fearsome, terrible Goddess...to her enemies. To her devotees Kali is the universal Mama Bear, willing to do absolutely anything to protect her cubs. I feel a more well-rounded description of Kali in 2e would help explain her nature to an audience that primarily knows her from Temple of Doom.

                  No, it is Shiva's description that I find insulting! Shiva is a God whose very name means 'The Auspicious One'. Out of the Trimurti, Shiva is the kindest, most caring and overall nicest. He is noted as being the God who is easiest to please amongst the whole Pantheon, and the one most willing to forgive those who transgress against him. He has a temper, it is true, but he is always quick to make amends when he calms down. Compared to Brahma, who actively lies and cheats to try and assert his superiority over the other Trimurti, and Vishnu whose stellar deeds include kicking his pregnant wife into exile because his popularity polls were dropping, cheating on Lakshmi with Goddesses like Prithvi and Tulsi, and forcibly ending, with trickery, the rule of a King so just he is still worshipped in some parts of India as a God, Shiva is a Saint.

                  And what does Companion paint him as? A mean-tempered tantrum-prone drug addict who enjoys cheating on Parvati!

                  In general, I will say the Hindu God chapter could use some revision. Personally, I find it shocking that a culture that stands with Dharma as its backbone does not have the Duty Virtue!

                                                      The still-living religions present another problem that makes treating them respectfully kinda hard: they're still around. That means they're still evolving, changing and you can't 'snapshot' them as easily as you can the ones that aren't active in their original forms. All respect to reconstructionist religions, but those aren't the original forms of the religion which, in most cases, we know very little about. It's a lot easier to look at dynastic Egyptian religion from a perspective of almost 2000 years after the end of contiguous native practice. There's no ancient Egyptians left to tell you you're doing it wrong.

                                                      There are still issues with looking at the thousands of years of religions evolution and trying to sort out what the gods would look like "now", with layers and layers of changes built into them. Do you say that anything after the start of Hellenization in Egypt 'doesn't count' for Egyptian religion? Sure, you can do that and get away with it pretty easily, but it's totally a judgement call on your part. Egyptian religion, like all religions, shifted its portrayal of different figures based on current politics and cultural perspectives. As one part of the empire grew stronger, the gods favored by that portion became more important, while the disenfranchised gods grew less important or were cast in a negative light. Sometimes Set was super awesome and heroic, sometimes he was a total bastard. It depends on when you're looking at, who you're asking.

                                                      Religion, which all mythologies are, is super complicated and never clear-cut. No religion is monolithic and codified, offering an exact map of the One True Way to read it. Every little sect of a religion has their own One True Way and they get really touchy about it being challenged. After all, it's their religion. Kinda important to most people.

                                                      The best Scion can hope for is to pick a period in time, snapshot the religion at that point and try to do a decent gloss-over on key figures. If they even get as accurate as a children's mythology book, I'd be pretty happy. That's just concerning accuracy of things like Associations, basic descriptions and Favors. Scion is, after all, trying to construct a single, cohesive world setting that incorporates 10+ entirely non-compatible cosmologies. It's going to take some liberties with reality, here.

                                                      I'm a professor! Why is no one listening to me?!
