            Originally posted by Ajax View Post

(Assuming of course, in said culture, you are supposed to honor and obey your parents, which is pretty standard offering for most cultures, particularly those with Piety associated).

            Every Virtue is a royal pain. In fact, some of the "virtuous" Virtues aren't. Virtuous, that is. A character without Loyalty can genuinely be loyal to their comrades easier than someone jerked around by very specific hardwired imperatives that only cover part of what actual loyalty and may actually discourage the character from following through on really helping a friend in need.
            I know that some people run Piety like this, but, I don't believe that is a very accurate way to run Piety. RAW, it is a violation of Piety to "Defy the wishes of family or a God," which can be an accurate interpretation of Piety for some Pantheons, but very much not all Pantheons. We all know that RAW is a bit iffy with a lot of things, from some dodgy mechanics, to some dodgy mythology.

            Piety is upholding the values of your culture, believing that your Pantheon is the greatest because you just are. Now, for some cultures, following the will of the Gods to the letter is an entirely acceptable expression of Piety, but not for all, not at all. A Tuatha Scion with Piety is going to have a very different Virtue then a Shen Scion with Piety, an Aesir Scion with Piety, or a Teotl Scion with Piety.

            The way that Piety could express itself for an Aesir Scion is that you worship the Gods, and strive with every fiber of your being to be the pinnacle of what the ancient Scandinavian (and Germanic) cultures that spawned them believed a person could be. For the Aesir, this would mean fitting into your gender role (no being like Odin and running around with women's magic), and being the best at whatever role in society you take up to be yours. Are you one of Odin's sons? You must be the greatest Prince (or son of the Chieftain) you can be. Are you a Skald? Travel to the far reaches of the earth and return with the stories of your Band. Are you a warrior? Pretty much just be Beowulf and you'll be fine.

            For a Tuatha Scion? Piety pretty much just means that you should be amazing. Be awesome, have no blemish on your social record. Be the greatest Druid, Bard, King, Champion, Smith, Magician, or whatever you want to be. (With a possible exception of women being Kings but the lack of women kings recorded may be because of Christianisation and the records being written three to five hundred years after Christianity arrived) If your Divine Parent shows up and goes, "Hey, stop doing what you want, and do what I tell you.", just buckling to what they want would be a pretty big failure on your part. You can do what they want, if that is what you want. If you want to tell your Divine Parent to shove it, go nuts, but you're going to have to live with the consequences of your actions. If you did it in a manor that was incredibly brave? Standing up for what you believed in despite a God threatening to kill you? You're going to get some praise from the Fili.

            So, while sometimes doing just as mommy or daddy tells you is a valid expression of Piety, for example, it is accurate for the Anunna, the Gods of Mesopotamia, since Humanity was effectively created as a slave race to do things since the Lesser Gods got mad they were being used as slaves for the Greater Gods. So, until you undergo Apotheosis, you know in your very soul you are not worth as much as a God by any streach of the imagination. But, all of that is just an expression of you fitting into your role that the ancient culture that the Pantheon came from.

            RAW Piety is kinda silly, in that regard. Doing what the Gods tell you is sometimes a valid expression of Piety, but defiantly not all the time. You should be filling your role in this ancient culture, being the best at it, and knowing that your culture is better then all these other, clearly lesser ones.

                    Originally posted by Ajax View Post

                    Laws of Hospitality were a Big Deal in Ireland. Like, a Really Big Deal. I don't think we have a single example of the Tuatha themselves violating hospitality, even the Formori don't break it to attack The Dagda. A breach of hospitality was something you could legally be ostricised over, you could be satarised, it was a big deal. Bres fell from power because he didn't properly follow the laws of hospitality involving Bards.

                    And, while there is not a lot of "those people suck" mentality, there is a very large, "We are the most amazing" mentality. I think Piety is one of the best fits for the Irish, really. I think Intellect is the biggest streach of their Virtues. I know what the writers were going for with it, they were trying to express the "being a clever shit" theme they have in their myths and culture, but Intellect doesn't actually express that. I would probably replace Intellect with Vengeance, since we have more examples of the Irish Gods being Vengeful rather then fufilling Intellect. Although then you get into a weird situation because while the Irish probably have Vengeance, it is not something they really approve of as a society. They had a system like the Wergild to prevent blood fueds. But, even though it is a bad thing, Lugh, The Morrigan, Aengus, Dian Cecht, they all have it for sure.
