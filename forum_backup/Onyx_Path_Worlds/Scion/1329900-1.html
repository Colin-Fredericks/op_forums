                                                To be honest, back in 1E, I was fascinated by the Atlantean Pantheon as being their own actual Pantheon rather than just a renaming of the Greek Gods in a similar fashion as Rome did. That they were given a unique culture (even if there were a lot of gaps in the fine details) rather than just a copy of Greek culture that has been done so many times previously was intriguing to me. Then came the Titan corruption of Atlantis and how it was the Scions of the Atlantean Gods that seemed to have been turned against them, transforming Atlantean culture into a dark reflection of what it was... and how the other Pantheons had to band against this transformed Atlantis to save the World. Yet even with this tragedy, some of the pettiness of the Gods from other Pantheons showed through on how they felt the need to not only stop the corrupted Atlantis, but to completely erase all traces of it, down to every last member of the culture (presumably even those who may have escaped its corruption) because they felt they couldn't risk the chance that anyone got away to undo what they had done. Over all... the story that was woven about Atlantis... its rise, corruption and eventual fall was as epic as it was ultimately tragic in my eyes... and gave me inspiration for me to work on a plot line of its eventual redemption (what can I say... I love stories of redemption, even if they end tragically).

                                                So I began scouring the information provided, looking for some plausible means to potentially bring this pantheon back for my own stories and games. It was made clear that any Scion of Atlantis would be different than those normally found in the game, since the Gods of Atlantis were presumably destroyed by the Titans (or at least imprisoned by their corrupted Scions and weakened by thousands of years of not receiving any worship). Then, in the adventure that was provided about Atlantis, I saw what I needed... the ship that had sunk and crashed into a submerged Atlantean Temple was what I would use to allow the Atlantean Gods to escape their imprisonment by their Titan corrupted children and gave them the chance they would need to redeem their Pantheon.

                                                To put it simply, the basis for the Campaign, the Atlantean Gods would use the last of their power to infuse their essence into mortals to be reborn as the last generation of Atlantean Scions (basically become what in 2E is Incarnate Scions). Part of this required tracking down the last traces of Atlantean bloodlines with in the world (even the combined might of all the Pantheons couldn't completely wipe out all traces of Atlantis, as some had escaped to other cultures before the corruption of Atlantis and had remained hidden within those cultures). This scattered the would be Scions initially, so as to escape initial notice of both the Titans and the Gods that still sought to keep Atlantis from rising (and potentially dooming the world again). These reborn Atlantean Gods would have to find each other, reclaim their lost birthright and avoid their enemies to regain the power they once had... starting off as Heroes. Not only would the Titans be after them (to corrupt the essence of the Gods they couldn't before), but also the other Pantheons too (or rather their Scions tasked with making sure that Atlantis remained but a forgotten memory). It was going to be an epic game, where the players would be tempted by power, hunted for crimes their ancestors had committed and have to choose whether to remain true to the Gods they had been or become something different (while they may have the essence of the Atlantean Gods within them, they were not those Gods themselves, but new incarnations, free to choose their own path, for good or ill).

                                                To be honest, once the Pantheon is reintroduced to Scion 2E in Mysteries of the World, I may try to adapt this idea to what is presented... as it was an amazing story that I would still like to continue with (never did get past the Hero portion of the original game). Regardless, I am looking forward to seeing what Mysteries of the World contains... especially in regards to Atlantis. I know it wasn't the favorite of many fans of 1E (though I think the hate was more from a very vocal few than any sentiment shaded by the majority of fans), but it was one that gave me a huge amount of inspiration. I hope that it will do so again in 2E.
