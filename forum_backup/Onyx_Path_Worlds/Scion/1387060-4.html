                        That's... not what Theocentrism means. Theocentrism is a measure of how 'Central' a particular Pantheon is to the World. Iron Theocentrism does *not* mean that no myths are true... it means that the Myths of only one Pantheon are True... thus, if said Pantheon were the Greek, Athena absolutely *would* have always been a God. If you choose a setting with No Pantheons, then there are *no* Gods, rather than false ones, and the PCs are limited to pre-Visitation Scions and Denizens, not Heroes.

                        You don't get to have your cake and eat it too... Either gods are *Gods*, in which case they have Scions and are the Truth of the World... or they don't even really exist and you don't have Heroes... this 'Gods are just powerful Wizards' style setting is completely at odds with the genre of Scion, which is ultimately Myth, and continually strikes me as trying to do a vampire setting that has no blood or life drinking... what even are you looking for in a vampire game then?

                            Here's my two cents on how to incorporate the Mythos into a regular Scion world. How I'd run it is that the World is like a conglomeration of bubbles somehow layered on top and within each other and generally allow for the others existences. The Mythos bubble is different. It's on the outside and it doesn't want to be integrated with the other pantheons. The Mythos deities don't really want to be fatebound to the Earth but they can't cause too much trouble or the bubbles will pop. The Earth Gods want the bubbles to be intact to prevent the Mythos deities from adding to the Titan problem and the Mythos want to avoid cross-contaminating with the Earth any more than necessary. Because in the original stories the Earth is just another rock in space to them. In Scion the Earth very important and it is aggravating for the Mythos deities to be bound to just a simple rock.

                            For a campaign this means that Heroes would mostly be dealing with anything that was summoned or slipped across the barrier between the bubbles. Demi-gods and Gods would deal with bigger threats but also would want to ensure the barrier between the bubbles don't get too unstable. Cause while some of the more powerful gods wouldn't have too much trouble against the Mythos deities not every god is all powerful. Not to mention the collateral damage.

                            Edit: you can even add in the idea that the Earth Gods are as strange and incomprehensible to the Mythos deities as the Mythos is to regular humans.

                                                Now I'm going to admit that I'm more familiar with Cthulhu Mythos deities than I am with the Deva so please bear that in mind.

                                                For me the Mythos is less about the absolute lack of caring gods in existence and the utter nihilism of Earth's place in the universe and more about the absolute strangeness and disconnect the Great Old ones and the Elder Gods have with what humanity knows about reality. Bast and Nodens/Nuada are actually considered a part of the Mythos, not sure when they were added but I know they were at some point, and are generally seen as having some interest in Earth's continued existence. How much they care depends on the writer.

                                                Now to me the whole point of the Mythos is that the closer you get to them the further you get from your previous understanding of reality. Take for example the Color Out of Space. It is a story where a meteor crashes onto a farm and an entity contained within poisons the well and leeches the life out of everything in the area. The horror is found in how strange this entity seemingly made entirely of light came from somewhere so far beyond the laws of physics that the light itself is a color never seen on Earth, which science says is impossible. So I'd say that rather than dismissing the power of the Earth Gods you focus on how incompatible the Mythos deities are. How one investigating them might become lost to almost everything, even death itself. And not in the escape from reincarnation Hindu way.
