          The distinction between a god and a ghost can be rather blurred in Chinese folk religion, so sure, why not?

          There are some choices that bother me because of personal preferences. For example, I prefer the version of the story in which Houyi became tyrannical after losing his wife, though the seeds were always there in his impetuous personality. Furthermore, I think it's odd that Erlang's write-up doesn't mention the story that his mother was imprisoned beneath a mountain for breaking the rules against god-mortal interaction, which adds new dimensions to his somewhat standoffish relationship with divine officialdom.

          However, there are also some choices that just seem strange to me. For example, the Queen Mother of the West is indeed one of the oldest members of the Chinese pantheon, but I have a hard time seeing her as a Primordial when she interacted so much with both gods and mortals on a regular basis. She's a regular participant in the feasts of heaven. Furthermore she's taken mortal lovers as recently as Emperor Wu of Han, who was well into historical times at around the same period as Marius and Sulla over in Rome.
