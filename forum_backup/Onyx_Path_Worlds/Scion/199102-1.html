                        Originally posted by Incendax View Post
                        Social characters tend to be the most powerful ones in the entire game, and often need to take a step back so that the other two character styles have an opportunity to shine. Mental characters are the second most powerful in theory, the way they can bring into existence creative and outside-the-box solutions to almost any problem (but are also the hardest to create challenges for). Physical characters are probably the weakest out of the three categories, but they are the easiest to construct challenges for by a pretty large margin.

                        But hybrid characters are almost always the best way to go.
                        Not sure I buy this, for certain values of buying this...

                        Derrick, son of Thor, kicks ass. We know exactly how well he kicks ass. Every one of his stats has a measurable result. He can lift and throw THIS much. He can do so THIS far. He can move THIS fast. He's THIS good at hitting. He can take THIS amount of damage.

                        What does it mean when Marie, daughter of Erzulie, gets 20 successes on a Charisma + Presence roll? How is that different than when someone else gets 15? Or 30? Or 10? If she's opposing the roles of Ohrimeo, son of Amaterasu when both of them are trying to influence the same person or group and he gets 17 successes. So what, exactly does Marie get for her 3 threshold successes? For that manner, how many people can Marie affect with her 20 successes? Is there a diffusion effect if she tries to affect 100 people vs. 10 vs. 1? What if 10 of the members of the group are Scions or Titanspawn and the rest are mortals? What if it's a one target situation, like they are both trying to convince a Titan's Primary Avatar to hear out their proposals? What if it's a Scion with a lot of Wits or Intelligence? What if it's a 3 yr old?

                        Then the mental characters get totally shafted. There aren't there really any decent rules for making stuff (witness the proliferation of fan-made Purviews to handle this) but, as the Good Baron pointed out, stuff is completely irrelevant except as fashion accessories (my words). (An Aside: How much does it bother anyone else that Mjolnir is really almost completely irrelevant? That, really, Mjolnir doesn't change Thor's abilities... he could use a Black & Decker claw hammer and get only slightly less effect, or a piece of drywall or his fists). Where Mental characters should shine, there is no system at all. Where is the ability to figure out really complete and effect plans of action? Anticipate an enemy's? Seek information? Act like Sherlock Holmes's intellect bootstrapped into a weakly god-like AI? Etc. Etc. Some of it gets clocked under Mystery and Prophecy (again, another area of dissatisfaction that gets Houseruled, though we always have made it work in my games), but all those Epics you invested in? Worthless...

                        So no. Social characters are primary if the Storyteller lets them be by fiat and in the hands of a dominant player. Mentals... I'm not buying that at all. Unless the Storyteller allows something like ex post facto legerdemain.... When the PCs suddenly realize that they need a portable armory, the Int character, who had never made any preparations for having that gets to say "Of course I have a cache of weapons in the trunk of my car. I acquired them by <insert clever story> in anticipation that this eventuality occurred..."

                        I do agree that hybrid characters are the way to go. Physical primary hybrids. Or a way. Purely physical works just as well or better. You get to be the hero of the myth that way, instead of his or her clever, smart or likeable sidekick.

                        Which goes back to the OP. So, for now, conditionally, I'll say "Yes, all Scions are monster-slaying heroes" or should be, as it plays to the games strengths, such as they are.

                                                            Also, i'm pretty sure the primary function of a seraglio, even one mostly composed of berserk ladies and titanspawn suitors, is not combat. Can't quite put my finger on the why...
                                                            As per the OP, I'm saying the Scion rules encourage the physical primary attitude. Anything else is not as well supported. HOW, within the context of RAW, as clearly and concisely and you can describe a combat action do you bring combat to a crashing halt and start negotiations using your socials? Similarly, how do you do so with mentals?

                                                            That's not an easy question to answer. A lot of the time that is by Storyteller fiat in an attempt to balance the various roles in the game. It's not supported by much in the way of mechanics. Which means it's like trying to hold on to an eel, very slippery. When using almost pure Storyteller fiat, you risk game balance.

                                                            This may not be important in your own game. You may have a great ST who is able to juggle eels effortlessly, but all too often, they slip and then the balance can go the other way. The social character takes over and combat only happens when and where she chooses. The mental character has it all planned out to the point that everyone else's volition is negated. (Of course, I knew that he would do that... so I had this stratagem in place). I'm not saying those scenes are BAD scenes, but all three should be balanced out. A character who maximizes investment should not be clearly superior to the other two almost all the time to the point that it doesn't take much of an investment in the other two to nearly equal (or even equal, due the vagueness of what X successes actually means) the social/mental characters.

                                                            The other issue is that, if your social character keeps preempting combats (balance going to social powers), the physical character will have one option to insure he will get to exercise free will. Taking out his social ally. Which is fine in some groups some of the time, but if the badly written rules interpreted in such a way as to give some oomph to the characters who got shafted tip things the other way...

                                                            That's what leads me to my answer to the OP. Yes. It's a monster-hunting/killing game. Make sure the characters have physical prowess. Then pick more physical prowess, some social prowess or some mental prowess....
