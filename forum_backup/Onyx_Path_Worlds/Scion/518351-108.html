                                                        Okay, I've got some time, I'll take a crack at it.

                                                        Okay, starting with Paths - Paths are your character's backgrounds and concepts. As of Hero level, you get three -

                                                        Your Origin - How you came to be. When referring to Scions, most likely who their parent is and how they were born. Like, say, Son of the Dagda for example.

                                                        Your Role - How your character came by the skills and abilities they have at the start of the game. So our theoretical child of Dagda may have grown up the clever child of poor working class folk, but he made it in Hollywood through raw talent and some very careful planning. So his Role would be "Cunning Actor."

                                                        Your Pantheon - How you relate to your pantheon. Our boy - let's call him "Connor McRae" - received a visitation from his father, and now uses his connections and encyclopedic knowledge of popular media to hunt overly dramatic monsters in Los Angeles. Let's go with "Sunset Huntsman."

                                                        Now, these three character backgrounds give us a fairly clear idea of who this guy is and how he operates.

                                                        This entry has gone on long enough for the moment, so I'll post it and collect my thoughts for the next post explaining Callings and Purviews.

                                                        Oh, and to give you an idea of how easy it is to come up with characters using this three background/concept idea? I literally just made this character up off the top of my head. And I am usually terrible with coming up with good character concepts.

                                                            Alright, part 2.

                                                            Paths affect some of the skills you start out with, without limiting what skills you can take in the future. From what I gather there are more benefits to this beyond getting an extra point in Occult or what have you, but Neall would be better at explaining the specifics than I.

                                                            Callings! Callings are archetypes your character may have similarities to that resonate with ancient myth and legend. Things like being a Warrior, or a Leader or a Creator. Heroes, Demigods and Gods get three.

                                                            Your Callings are chosen based on what would make sense for your Paths. Our "Cunning Actor" might be a Trickster or a Lover, but he's probably not a Guardian or a Judge. Important: At least one of your PC's Callings must be the same as one of his parent's Callings. PC's are not carbon copies of their parents, but they do inherit more than just their looks.

                                                            Connor's Callings would be: Sage, because of his father, the "Red Man of All Knowledge" (really digging deep into pop culture can lead you to acquiring some strange knowledge.) This satisfies the "one Calling the same" requirement. Next, let's pick Trickster for his Cunning Actor Path - he's too clever for his own good, and being a top-notch actor means he can make the most ridiculous lies sound convincing. Finally, he naturally has the Hunter Calling from his "Sunset Huntsman" Path. Creatures of the night, you better stay out of L.A. and Hollywood - nobody messes with Connor's town.

                                                            Callings aren't just labels, they give you access to Knacks - special tricks and qualities that don't fall neatly into out-and-out super powers, but are really useful to have. We don't know exactly what shape these will take in 2E yet, but an example might be Connor's Trickster Calling allowing him to buy something like "Bavarian Fire Drill" - Connor puffs himself up, puts on a lumenescent safety vest, grabs a clipboard and starts giving people orders, making them think he's a figure of authority. This offers a major boost to his attempts to deceive people into doing what he says when they normally wouldn't have any reason to.

                                                            Finally, Purviews. Purviews are your awesome super powers - Superhuman strength, control over the weather, the ability to forge items of awesome mystical power. Buying a purview gives one general freeform application, like the Fire purview would make you immune to fire damage and allow you to put out small fires instantly. They also allow you to buy Boons - more specific and potent applications of a purview's ability. For instance, Connor picked up the Levin Fury boon for the Sky purview, and now he can hurl lightning bolts as attacks.

                                                            You get access to one Purview for free from your parent's list. After that, you need Relics - magical items - to get access to more.

                                                            ...And that's all I got. If I missed anything guys, please tell me.
