    Specialties after character generation

    03-06-2018, 08:10 AM
    Is it too late to make it so that getting 3 in a skill, both before and after character generation, gives you a specialty? I assumed this was the case until one of the players in a Trinity game, who's wary of chargen optimization traps, noticed it and started to make sure that all his skills were at 3 so he didn't start with a deficit of potential XP. I checked to see whether it worked this way in Scion, and after an initially misleading sentence at the bottom of page 60, page 105 confirmed it was the same. It could be houseruled fairly easily, but I think having the canon rules set up to create XP imbalances at character generation is an easily solved problem, either by removing free character generation specialties, or by saying that everyone picks one up at the third dot of a skill.

                  +1 Enhancement is really strong. It's the equivalent of 3 extra dice, if you already succeed at something. The only downside is that they can't help you get that single success. Fortunately, that's where Momentum comes in, allowing you to add extra dice, allowing you to more reliably succeed. A character with enough specialties is basically always going to be getting an extra Enhancement on their roll, and that's really powerful.

                  And 7 skills is not actually bad - Most people can justify dropping a chunk of them as not relevant to their character concept. The bigger loss, IMO, is not having a few skills at 5.

                  The biggest problem though is that it seems to run against the design philosophy of Storypath. Previous versions of these games have been filled with hideous optimisation traps. Buying certain things at certain points was objectively better. It heavily encouraged min-maxing character gen, because the character who did was better right out of the gate, and was able to easily broaden their focus to be just as good as the generalist in a few sessions.

                  Storypath kills that dead. All levels of an attribute cost the same, whether the first or fifth. Same for abilities. There's nothing that's really efficient to buy at character creation, but inefficient later. Hell, the entire tweak system means that you can completely re-design your character's skills if you find that it's not doing what you want. Except that breaks down with skill specialties. A character with maxed specialties has 12XP (3xp per specialty, 4 extra specialties) that other characters don't. They are, no matter what else happens, objectively better off. And that will continue throughout the game - They have fewer skills at 5, but it doesn't take them long to fix that. They have fewer one dot skills, but how often do you need every member of a band to Pilot, or perform Medicine?

                  Also, it's mechanically messy. How does it interact with Tweaks? If I re-focus out of one skill and into another, what happens to my specialty? As best I can tell, they keep it (You only need 3 dice to gain a specialty, not to use it), even if they drop the skill down to 0 (Which isn't unreasonable - Over a sufficiently long campaign, you could absolutely justify losing skills entirely. Scions can easily go through multiple, radical changes. Even without that, a lot of skills will drop below 3. If I get a magic bow, I'm probably going to stop practising with my guns. If I go blind, I'm probably not going to be practising my piloting). There's no way to transfer it to the skill that you actually use, and for some reason you keep getting Enhancements related to your now defunct specialty.
