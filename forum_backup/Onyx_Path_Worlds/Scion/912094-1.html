                Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                And Prometheus was a Titan who represented the creative power of fire. He simply wasn't concerned about the applications of what he was creating, which made him come across as being a bit careless with his designs.
                Oh a soapbox for be to throw rocks at Prometheus, awesome. Sorry if this is a bit off topic, but I want to yell about Prometheus for a moment.

                Prometheus has the greatest PR of any Titan. People always talk about Prometheus being this martyr for the humans going against the will of Heaven modernly, which is a really lovely idea (which Prometheus in a Scion game could really monopolize on to get Scions to work for him) but I feel misses the core point of mortals being irelevent to the divine in Greek Mythology, and slightly misunderstands the order of events with Prometheus. So, I've got this slightly different way to present Prometheus trying to be more in line with the characters.

                So, the Titanomachy kicks off, and the Titans that stay neutral (Oceanus), or side with the upstart children of Cronus (Hecate, also possibly Helios, Selene, and Eos) are, in the end, not thrown into Tatarus (or otherwise imprisoned) by Zeus. For some reason, Prometheus and Epimetheus (who is Afterthought or Hindsight) are not imprisoned alongside their brother Atlas, and father Iapetus. So, thereby we can conclude Prometheus was either neutral or sided with the Gods (I think this is even outright stated in Prometheus Bound).

                Now, from here, there are two important parts of information that need to be remembered. First, Prometheus isn't a Titan of creative fire, or the like, despite him being presented so modernly. Prometheus is the primordial force of Forethought (its his name), and this is important to remember. Just like Oceanus is the rim-ocean, Erebus is darkness, and Nix is night, Prometheus is the literal manifestation of Forethought. Secondly, mortals are presented as being ultimately irelevent to the Divine. We are like mayflies to them (I swear this is a line in the Illiad or something, but I have never been able to find it. So I am slightly unsure.) and spectacularly irelevent unless they've decided they fancy us. So, with these two bits of information we can glean that Prometheus has thought out every single thing he is going to be doing and the consequences of his actions as he is the literal embodiment of Forethought. Secondly, that he likely doesn't care much for mortals.

                But, why would Prometheus preform The Trick at Mecone (the trick of getting Zeus to select the worse sort of sacrifices) then? We are never directly told in Hesiod's Theogony. So, we need to infer. Since Mortals are silly little creatures who have mayfly lives, he probably isn't doing this to help them. Prometheus' brother, father, and mother were all imprisoned by Zeus after the war, but Prometheus sided with Zeus. However, Atlas is being singled out, and punished in a very specific way (forced to hold the sky aloft) which in its uniqueness makes a good source of Prometheus' ire.

                So, Prometheus then tricks Zeus into accepting the worse sacrifice from mortals. Grecian sacrifice was fire sacrifice where you burned the offerings, mortals had a summit with Zeus at a place called Mecone where they wanted him to specify the sort of sacrifices he wanted. Prometheus killed an ox, and divided its body up into two piles. He took all of the ox's meat, fat, and otherly tasty bits the Gods and Humans both wanted, and shoved them into the ox's stomach. He then took all of the bones, and wrapped them up in the cow's hide.

                Zeus, looking at these two piles, is tricked by Prometheus into accepting the bundle which looks more pleasing, and resigning the Pantheon to sacrifices of bones and other waste from butchery while those blasted mortals get the tasty bits. (Hesiod presents this as Zeus being benevolent to the mortals, but this doesn't make sense if you realize what happens next) So, Zeus is mad, and takes fire away from humanity in punishment. (They can't preform the sacrifices without the fire, so Zeus is feeling a bit better about himself. Showed those mortals. But, this bit also sort of disproves that Zeus was being benevolent in picking the worse bundle since he instantly punished the mortals for it)

                Now, Prometheus looks at this and sees his trick is now ultimately pointless. The humans can't snub the Theoi without fire, all his trickery has been for nothing. Since mortals are irelevent little gnats, he's probably not doing this directly for their sake as well. So, he goes and grabs a fennel stalk, puts an ember from Hestia's hearth in it, and legs it down the mountain. He gives it to humanity, and woo, they have fire back, everyone's nice and warm, they can contact the divine through sacrifices, all is nice.

                Except Zeus is double pissed. Prometheus keeps showing off that Zeus is not omniscient despite him insisting that he is. So, Kratos and Ba (Strength and Force, two Titans who serve as Zeus bodyguards) snatch up Prometheus, and Hephaestus binds him to the mountain where an eagle rips out his liver and eats it every day. Now, keep in mind, as Forethought, Prometheus probably knew this would happen. Humanity, and Epimetheus both get punished as well for Prometheus' crime because, you know, Zeus, by the creation of Pandora. Bad stuff happens, humanity is thrown into chaos, all the evils of the world unleashed. Now, remember, Prometheus is Forethought, and probably knew that this would have happened.

                Then, Prometheus (in Prometheus Bound) actually directly showcases his capacity as Forethought. He lets it slip that he knows the identity of The Son of Zeus who is fated to overthrow him. Zeus, who was pretty sure he nipped this curse from Gaia in the bud when he ate his first wife Metis, loses it. He sends everyone to go try to get this information out of Prometheus, but Prometheus ignores it.

                And then, one day, Hercules arrives and frees Prometheus. Prometheus vanishes from the mythic record, and is never seen or heard from again. But, he does not like Zeus, and knows who The Son of Zeus will be / is. So, as the literal embodiment of Forethought, he likely is just hiding out, and biding his time.

                But yeah, round about way of saying Prometheus probably doesn't like humans, just really doesn't like Zeus. Ajax knows more about the Theoi than I do, but I think the characterization is pretty solid for this presentation of Prometheus. Prometheus as Forethought and not some fire Titan I find to be far more interesting as a story thread.

                ... I'll get off my soapbox now, sorry.
