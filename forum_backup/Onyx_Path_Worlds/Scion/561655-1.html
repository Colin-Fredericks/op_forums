The game already had guidelines for not breaking out the dice, and Superman + the Flash vs. 2 Shamblers is not a time to break out the dice. And then beyond that, the system has rules to treat zombies as a group, and it's really just whichever of the superheroes squishes them first. And then beyond that, either Superman or the Flash is going to have Focus, so as an example, the Flash squishes zombie 1, and then Superman steals the scene by grabbing and squishing zombie 2 right before it can bite the Flash who got distracted pulling himself out of zombie guts.

                Even with the scant preview we have... there's just a mountain of layers clearly baked into the developer's concepts to make sure this sort of thing will only happen if the Director wants it to happen; not because of some sort of fluke mistake in the rules.

                As as PaulChapman said, on top of this there's still going to be powers that might be able to impact action order.

                                06-22-2015, 10:11 PM
                                Originally posted by ArthurHansen View Post
                                It removes a style of play of 'fast squishy characters that act/react faster than their enemies'.

                                Take your two fastest squishy characters and a brick against one slow foe that has an area of effect attack.

                                Your faster characters roll a high initiative, your brick is slow. And lastly you your slow foe who rolled lowest.

                                Speedster A goes first and realized the danger of the slow foe with a damaging fire aura. He takes his action and retreats out of range. Slow aura burning monster goes, killing the second speedster, because he didn't get to go faster than the monster. The brick is a bit hurt, but finally gets to go, even though he was still a little faster.

                                It penalizes faster characters by not allowing them to react with their faster speeds before the the slow danger can hit them.
                                Actually, if the speedster goes first he has no way to know what the slow opponent will do. Speedster A goes, then the slow aura guy kills *him* because he can't react, then Speedster B has all resources available to evade the attack instead of blowing them in a prior action. So the true scenario is the *opposite*. And in fact, speed would be well-represented by the act of evading the enemy.

                                Previous games compensated for this by having everyone declare in reverse initiative order, then go, but the overwhelming experience of most groups is that this step was either omitted out of haste or forgetfulness, or highly disliked for slowing down play.

                                Initiative represents winning at acting first, but in most systems, *losing* at information acquisition. You go first without the benefit of being informed about subsequent actions. There are solutions, as above, but they're often clunky.

                                The other funny thing about initiative? It was never ever realistic. Combat is a set of relationships between people and objects that has too many dimensions to really portray with a single trait set. We do it to simplify things, but to understand its inaccuracy, go it up an old fencing coach. After he or she stop hits (scores in such a way as to interrupt your attempt to score) you over and over again, and after you go faster and faster, you may realize that the coach has a superior sense of timing separate from his or her raw reaction time to general stimuli.

                                Now put a gun in the coach's hands and pit him or her against a trick shooter, to see who hits the fastest. Fencing coach loses. Speed is often *activity-specific*. But we can't model that in a system where actions are assumed to be dynamic and complex, and we do have superhumans who *do* have a general quality of speed.

                                Fortunately, we have other tools to represent the various forms of speed. We gave systems for movement and to evade attacks. Reducing speed to the question of the order people go in (especially given that nobody really gets to go first or last after the first round, since rounds simply divide narrative time into chunks) doesn't really do it justice. We can produce powers systems for speedsters, and after that, use these other systems and trust in player control.

                                                            Arthur:

                                                            I think you're trying to break a system without the whole thing in front of you. It comes off as a bit silly.

                                                            Yes, there is a critical value in pushing the system to find out what its breaking points are. It isn't an inherently negative judgement because all games have them and it can be very useful to know where they are.

                                                            At the same time, such efforts are only really valuable if you approach the system with good faith. For example:

                                                            If the Director wants a ridiculously slow to act character with this initiative system... don't have him attack the first action he gets. Our giant is dimwitted and slow, and didn't realize a fight was breaking out around him. His first action is to grab his 2 ton club. Bam. All the PCs will get at least one action before he swings that baby at anyone.

                                                            The Director (or ST or GM or whatever) needs to design enemies that actually act mechanically how they are described.

                                                            Regardless of the initiative system, a character that is so slow and uses such and unwieldy weapon that they can only attack every other action, is going to feel a heck of a lot slower than a character that can attack every round. Who goes first in a round is far less meaningful than how much you can get done in one.

                                                            If you want a super-low giant that feels slow, you have to make him actually slow; not just slot him at the end of init.
