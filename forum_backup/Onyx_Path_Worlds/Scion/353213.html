    Unexpected Divine Superheroes

    12-28-2014, 07:08 PM
    This isn’t about Scion, but it is about mythic gods in a modern world, so I hope nobody minds. Besides, this forum has the greatest concentration I know of erudition about mythology and (I hope) willingness to creatively misinterpret those myths.

    I’m building a new superhero setting for my gaming group. Mythological gods becoming superheroes and supervillains has been a standard part of superhero settings at least since Marvel coopted Thor, so I shall include at least a few such heroes. But I want to look beyond the obvious warrior gods and heroes such as Thor, Hercules, Horus, Yi the Archer, etc. Such characters shall appear as NPCs but I don’t want to be limited by them. I want a few divine/mythic heroes my players won't expect.

    Example: I’m going to have Anubis appear as a fairly well-known NPC hero. Anubis? Why Anubis? Well, he has a connection with justice through his participation in the Weighing of the Heart ceremony. He’s a protector of tombs, tomb-goods and the dead, which could give him an interest in the multi-billion-dollar criminal trade in stolen antiquities, as well as people who have died without receiving proper funeral rites – such as victims of murder and massacre. Anubis solves the average murder as easily as Thor defeats the average mugger: the Jackal God just calls up the victim’s ghost and asks who did it… which means building stories around unusual murders. Anubis the Psychopomp also guarded the passages between the worlds of the living and the dead, suggesting stories about ghostly and demonic incursions, or battles with vampires and other undead villains. Between bizarre murders, museum robberies, military and supervillain massacres, and undead or otherworldly villains, I think Anubis supplies plenty of hooks for appearances as an NPC guest hero.

    Even for the standard warrior/hero gods, I hope to add a twist. Like, myths about the Mesopotamian hero-god Ninurta mostly deal with him battling demons and monsters, but he also has the divine title of Inspector of Fields and Canals. He’ll be my campaign’s analog to Thor, a big lummox who bashes enemies with his magical mace Expeller. (What does it expel? Consciousness, mostly.) But he’s also active with NGOs that dig wells, build water systems and improve crops and farming practices. Sounds harmless, right? Not to a corrupt Third World government that doesn’t want village folk developing any sort of organization independent of the State. So Ninurta has some rather worldly enemies, too.

    (I'm toying with Daedalus as a clockpunk gadgeteer, too, but probably not a hero. In the myths, He was kind of a bastard.)

    So that’s the sort of thing I’m looking for. Divine heroes who are heroic in ways you don’t immediately expect. Any suggestions?

    Dean Shomshak

                  You could have a god who wants to do something about their modern reputation, who's had enough of being seen as the bad guy. Set would make a good choice here. (Then there are his roles as god of the desert and storms, not usually paired together in Western cultures; imagine a divine superhero using sand and storm against you.)

                  Or take a page from Marvel's Loki comics (Journey into Mystery, Young Avengers, and Loki: Agent of Asgard, which're quite different from his film portrayal): have a god who uses his wits, cunning and trickery to bring down his enemies (or his pantheon's enemies). Hermes, Anansi and Eshu (hell, most trickster gods) would be good for this take.

                  Or Thoth as master of knowledge, law, science and magic, making sure that the world's systems stay in balance, that no force wins a decisive victory. Could be quite Sandman/Lucifer-esque.

                  Hm... looking at Nephthys' Wikipedia page, she represents divine assistance and protective guardianship, a companion to the dead who protects them on their journey through the afterlife. She's also a nursing mother to the reigning Pharaoh, serving as their ferocious and dangerous guardian, using her firey breath to incinerate the Pharaoh's enemies. In addition, she's a goddess of beer, preventing hangovers, and a goddess of healing. She'd be an interesting superheroine, to say the least.

                                  I know the Greek gods better than any others, so I'll stick with those.

                                  Venus actually had an obscure comic book series for a while. Mostly mystery/horror stories.

                                  With his prophecy/healing powers, Apollo could make a very interesting Wandering Hero in the style of Kung Fu & other such series. If you don't like him for it, Hermes would do just as well.

                                  I once did a superhero setting of my own where I took Princess Diana (Wonder Woman) as the current identity for Artemis, so she was more like one of the archer/weapon expert heroes, but with magic instead of tech.

                                  Athena could really rock as a supernatural investigator. Harry Dresden, but without the necessary learning curve of 15 books, plus Xena. Dionysus could be her Thomas Raith! Drunken party boy who's WAY smarter than he seems.

                                  If Daedalus is too much of a punk, Hephaestus would fill his spot. I'd read a comic book about a guy who's gadgets and preparedness outstrips Batman and Iron Man combined. Lesser known story, Aphrodite was cheating on him, but it couldn't prove it or catch who it was, so he put an invisible net over his bed to catch them. Not only does this start to show his "crazy prepared" potential, there's also this - he didn't just throw a spell over the net and say "invisible". He made the chain-link so small and fine as to make it nearly impossible to see, and it was unbreakable. Add to that his Vulcan aspects from the Romans as a god of destruction and fire, and he can be a formidable opponent for anyone.

                                  "Who knows what evil lurks in the hearts of men..................Erebus knows!"

                                  Heracles, Perseus, Orpheus and Achilles gets all the press. Jason, Theseus, Bellerophon, Castor & Pollux, and Odysseus don't get NEARLY enough credit for how badass they all are.
