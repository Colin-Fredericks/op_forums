            Originally posted by Neall View Post
            Like I said, the focus is on replicating the experience - but then, things are still in flux at this stage, and it's never wise to close a door too firmly when you might need to walk through it later. Exalted was about the Exalted, but God-Blooded, Heroic Ghosts, Dragon-Kings - they're fan favorites, and they even made an appearance in the 2e corebook. Dryads and nymphs and elementals and kami were the children of the gods, too. Aren't they scions by another name?
            I’m glad to hear this. Personally, I’ve always been a fan of scions getting center stage, but not being the exclusive protagonists of the game. To me, the main draw of Scion is the fidelity of its modeling of real-world mythologies, and the “children of the gods” thing is of secondary importance; and I don’t want to be told that I’m playing it wrong if I choose to center a chronicle around sorcerers, holy men, amazons, nymphs, kami, or other mythological beings.

            Besides, it’s a lot easier for a Storyteller who wants to focus exclusively on scions to forbid non-scion protagonists that the game otherwise permits than it is for a Storyteller who wants to focus on other character types that the game relegates entirely to the role of supporting cast to do so: the latter has to hand-craft the rules needed to do so; the former need only ignore the rules he doesn’t want to use.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
