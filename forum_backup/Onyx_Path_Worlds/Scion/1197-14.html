        Originally posted by Shadowflame View Post
        Would "radical fundamentalists" be a more appropriate term?
        Not really, because they're still "radical" about a set of beliefs that are NOT the "fundamentals" of Christianity. It's a semantics thing.

        I should point out that calling extreme Muslims "fundamentalists" is also incorrect. Islam has a lot more to say about tolerance and mercy than it does about killing infidels and shoving Islam down people's throats. These people read like two passages of the Koran and choose to believe that those statements are the entirety of the Koran. Same problem.

        As far as the topic at hand? Here are my thoughts.

        Yeah, I would LIKE a means to bring the Abrahamic faiths into Scion in a way that is respectful to both the Abrahamic faiths AND the other religions represented. That said? This thread, and many others like it, do a lot to prove that that's a very tall order. I have considered ways to bring the Abrahamic faiths into Scion and I haven't been able to come up with anything that works real well either. And if I, and the rest of the very intelligent people in this forum, haven't been able to come up with anything we can all agree on, then I have a hard time faulting the devs of the game if they can't either.

        And I definitely agree with Telgar and Samudra that letting the Abrahamic faiths be represented in an alternate setting kind of thing is probably for the best. People who aren't interested in bringing in the Abrahamic faiths can simply ignore them and those who are get their wish. Everyone wins. Now, it would be nice if the core books at least give some ideas about how to deal with the Abrahamic faiths in the core setting if one is so inclined, but I really don't need a full pantheon write-up in the core books. I don't think the issue should be completely ignored, but I don't think it needs the full deluxe treatment right out of the box.

        So my stance is that it would be nice to have, but I can survive if it never shows up. Letting it be an alternate setting is probably the safest way, and as long as everything is handled with respect, I can be happy.

                No problem. Happens to all of us.

                Yeah, you'll notice that when I typed the "offer a quick prayer" part, I capitalized the word "maybe." That's my way of emphasizing the importance of that word in the sentence. Yeah, God being okay with an actual act of worship to a foreign goddess is a BIG stretch. Showing respect and even deference to other gods in the interest of cooperation is probably acceptable to Him. But actively participating in the religion may not be. Now attending a festival or two in Amaterasu's honor would probably be okay. I'm not an expert, but I'm pretty sure most Shinto celebrations are relatively tame affairs that don't involve anything God would find truly offensive. Now, that may not be a standing rule, of course. I can imagine God would NOT want an Abrahamic Scion to even show up to a celebration in honor of, say, Dionysus? Big rolling orgy and "sex, drugs, and rock 'n roll?" Yeah, THAT may be going too far.

                The Abrahamic faiths, as others have pointed out, started out with a decidedly henotheistic world view. The whole "There IS no God but ours" attitude didn't really come into play until Christianity managed to take over Europe. The Tanakh (the official name of the "Old Testament") specifically makes mention of many of the gods worshipped by Israel's pagan neighbors and mentions numerous times when the people of Israel turned at least part of their worship to these other gods. Islam specifically mentions the Djinn of previous Arab religions as being very real, and just as capable of converting to Islam and achieving salvation. So the henotheistic core of the Abrahamic faiths is a very real thing and was part of the original world view. Within Scion's setting, it would be very easy to say that henotheism is STILL the appropriate viewpoint for the Abrahamic Pantheon. The fact that most of their worshippers have become hardcore monotheists doesn't really have to be an issue. Those people are simply mistaken. Thus? The Abrahamic Pantheon COULD fit into the core setting without too much trouble. Christians, like myself, who play the game will simply have to accept that the setting doesn't work if the Abrahamic faiths really are the only game in town. Seeing as how that wasn't the real, original attitude of the religion anyway? I personally don't have any trouble accepting this. I don't think too many others would either.

                Don't get me wrong, I stand by what I said in my last post: bringing the Abrahamic faiths into the core setting should NOT be attempted unless the devs are supremely confident that they've figured out a way to do it in a way that shows respect to all the religions represented. It probably does work best as an alternate setting, as you and others say. But it COULD be made to work even in the core if properly handled. That's the key phrase: "IF PROPERLY HANDLED." If the devs don't think they can do it right, then they definitely should not do it.

                And I'm not so sure that the Abrahamic Pantheon would rub EVERYONE the wrong way, if the Scion setting forces the henotheistic viewpoint. Yeah, the Abrahamic Pantheon is snooty and superior toward everyone else, but Companion tells us that the Devas can also irritate the other Pantheons just by pointing out the whole "Ha, ha! Our religion is HUGE!" thing. Oh, there's going to be friction, to be sure. But as you say, a LOT of the Pantheons have friction with each other. And honestly, that friction is exactly what makes inter-Pantheon interaction so interesting.
