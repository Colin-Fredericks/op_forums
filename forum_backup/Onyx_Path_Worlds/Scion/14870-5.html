                                    World Mythology is infinitely complex and does not lend itself to being shuffled neatly into a box where everything is the same. Myths aren't balanced, aren't designed to be played through as a game with a mechanical system. They're stories, and they're not even necessarily concerned with internal consistency within the same story, much less within the hundreds or thousands of years that the society existed who worshipped them. Stories that are concerned with getting across a point that illustrates a philosophical or religious tenant, generally. Trying to write a game that accurately reflects all of world mythology is crazy. It won't be balanced. It will need a different system for every single mythological creature, and often multiple conflicting systems for the same mythological creatures.

                                    I totally understand that accuracy is important, but a functioning game can't be so concerned with accurately representing or explaining every single myth and story that it forgets it's a game and it needs balanced rules and systems that are fairly elegant. It can try to come up with the most balanced and elegant system that it can, while keeping a maximum amount of flexibility. It can include accurate information in so far as it goes on the mythic figures included. But it's going to have to make calls and work things into the framework of the game's own internal mythology.

                                    There's no need to go making things up just to fit holes, like the Drowned Road, or get things objectively wrong for no good reason, like Crom Cruach, or shoehorning in weird suicide-bomber-angels that serve the Egyptian Sun Titan... but it's pretty silly to try explaining every single weird thing every single God does, ever, in terms of mechanics. They're Gods and they've got Stories To Tell. Sometimes they're just gunna break the rules. That's fine. They do that. The game can't, because if you just toss the rules out the window, you're back to "I killed you!" "No you didn't!" "Yes I did!". You can give the players some rule-bending with effects that turn mechanical conflicts into narrative ones, like Ultimate Attributes and Boons, but they still have to follow the rules. No one is using Ultimate Death to create a new species of animal, or cure a plague, or re-ignite the frozen sun. No one is using Ultimate Appearance to lift a mountain and turn it upside down into a floating island.

                                    I'm a professor! Why is no one listening to me?!

                                            I loved the Original Scion, unsable rules and all. I have run 4 different games and they all (with a lot of house rules) went great! I do think that playtesting is very important and will hopefully be done to a large degree. It was weird when ever my players would make a character and only a few games in were way better Scions than the examples in the books. At least at doing what they were supposed to do. I loved the example fluff for the most part also.

                                            The things I had issue with were- I didn't like that most boons were kinda worthless. I usually had to come up with inventive ways to intice players to get more of them when Epic attributes and knacks were the real power in the game. The creation of Birthrights was kinda weak. We had fun coming up with items because luckily my groups were fun and imaginative, but I would definately like some more hard and fast rules for that aspect. I thought Arete, while fluffy was a bit unfair to the other pantheons sometimes. I would like to see more fluff or maybe systems that explain or help explain how the world reacts to the scions? Obviously the Vatican would be a tad upset if the scion of Thor was on the Tonight show joking around with Jay. I loved Ragnarok, but felt it was limited by only being a book for the Norse. I understand why and still wedged my guys into it without much problem, but it would be cool to see that kind of book embrace other Pantheons. I liked the Fluff for Atlantis a great dead but rarely was anyone interested in playing a scion from there. While I loved rolling eight hundred dice the first few games, it gets a little old after a while. Maybe instead of adding more and more dice, maybe lowering the difficulty every so often. I could roll 18 dice looking for 7 ups or roll 5 dice looking for 6 ups or something. Or maybe 10s are more powerful on different teirs. I would like more Evil/ corrupted Scion options. I loved the action wheel for initative order, please keep that. I would like maybe some mention or mechanics of faith in respect to how people are nowadays. In other words, Thor is cool, but peoples worship of money is probably more powerful than half the gods combined.


                                            I will post more when I can think about it or talk to my players, but am super excited about this and cannot wait!
