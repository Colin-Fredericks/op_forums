    Pantheon Map

    04-26-2020, 09:22 AM
    I constructed a world-map to display the locations of as many of the pantheons as possible. It currently has the locations of 42 of them. I will update it in the future, and feel free to point out any errors. https://www.mapcustomizer.com/map/Ma...ld%20Pantheons

    Edit: It's updated! With 22 more pantheons!

    Edit 2: Made a few corrections in regards to terminology, and added the Pawnee pantheon, which I'm tentatively referring to as the Nahurac. I'll probably be picking at this for a while. 65 pantheons total!

    Edit 3: 21 more! 86 in total! Most of the new ones are pantheons I know very little about personally, but which I'm aware are options.

                      Well this is cool! It's interesting to see the diversity and distribution of pantheons around the world - especially how some regions are relatively crowded while others are almost empty. I guess that says something about Western scholarship and about which cultures we know (or think of) most/least. There's also a lot of new pantheons to discover that I've never heard about, so this is quite inspiring!

                      If you're interested in some more suggestions, there's a few pantheons I'm aware of that are still missing. Mind you, I know very little about most of these, so take them with a grain of salt.
                      - The Ayyaana (Oromo and possibly Somali people, Eastern Africa)
                      - The Benzhu (Bai people, mostly of Yunnan Province, China)
                      - The Hanitu (Bunun people of Taiwan)
                      - The Jinzami (Bakongo, modern day DR Congo & Angola)
                      - The Pangool (Serer people. modern Senegal/Gambia)

                      And some more complicated ones:
                      - The Orenda (the core book presents the Haudenosaunee/Iroquois pantheon as part of the Manitou, although I have some doubts if they shouldn't be related but separate pantheon)
                      - The Malakhim (the angels of the Abrahamic religions; I know they are quite contentious around the forum, but if you want to include them on the map, I believe locating them in Jerusalem might be acceptable to all three major Abrahamic faiths)
                      - The Aeons (emanations of God in various Gnostic beliefs)
                      - The Ascended Masters (of Theosophy)
                      - The Kingdom of Fanes (a royal family from Ladin literature that at least some people believe may have once been gods; located in the Dolomites of South Tyrol, Italy)

                      There's also a few cultures that I believe have (or in some cases had) their own pantheons that I don't have names for right now. Including the Dravidians, the Fulani, the Hungarians, the Kirati, the Meitei, the Tani, the Yi, the Zapotecs and the Zhuang.

                      Finally, I have some nitpicky suggestions for edits that I noticed:
                      - The Viet pantheon are called the Than, not the Thanh. It's a bit confusing because 'Thần' means 'god', while 'Thánh' means 'saint'.
                      - I believe the Hausa spirits are called the Iskoki, not Bori (which I believe is the name for their concept of spirit, or something)

                      Scion 2e Homebrew Pantheon:
                      The Thần of Vietnam

                                                          The Yibiás were a Tupi-Guarani pantheon, so they had gods of the Tupi and Guarani, which are two cultural lineages that are close together and share a bunch of myths, names, and so on. The Yibiás is a Tupi name (and means "Those who zeal for the World"), and since I produced Pindorama I have wondered if they wouldn't be better served being split apart. I still hold that they can fit together well as a somewhat concise cultural branch (or as concise as a disparate but related group of communities can be), but I have been tinkering about making a more Guarani distinction of them. If you want to keep them together as a single marking on the map you can put them as Yibiás (and more on a Central-South position in Brazil), but otherwise, you can keep them separate.

                                                          You can also mark the Tupi gods as Yibiás (closer to the Brazilian Coast), but a purely Tupi pantheon is... complicated, as the sources are few and disparate.
