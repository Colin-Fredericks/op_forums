    Scion: Germany - Campaign Preparations and Musings

    04-28-2023, 02:46 AM
    Soon, I will start my very first Scion campaign, and I am very excited. The basic premise is this:

    - The campaign will primarily take place in Germany and adjacent lands, with Kassel serving as the "home base" of the band - although they will travel a lot.
    - The campaign will be heavily inspired by my research on German folklore and folk tales, and I will always try to work local legends into the story.
    - The Scions will start at the Origin level. At some point they will rise up to the Hero level. Whether they want to rise to Demigod or further level will be up to them.

    Thus, to prepare for the campaign and in order to ruminate on campaign developments, I wanted to start a thread where I discuss aspects of the campaign in detail.


    Let's start by about the Pantheons involved.

    Germany is sort-of border territory between the Æsir, the Thenoi, and the Bogovi. The Æsir are the strongest in the northwest, the Thenoi in the south and west, and the Bogovi in the east.

    The Thenoi, just like Christianity, came with the Roman Empire, and the ruling aristocracy often had a strong affinity with the Thenoi.

    The Æsir got a huge boost thanks to the 19th century Nationalist Romantic revival. A lot of folklorists (including Jacob Grimm) saw hints and traces of Nordic mythology everywhere, whether valid or not - and others, including the Nazis, followed in their footsteps. Norse/Germanic mythology was often seen in contrast to the "foreign" Christian or Roman influences.

    Slavic mythology and beliefs were actively suppressed in German lands for a long time - there were several crusades in the early Middle Ages as Christianity tried to stamp out these beliefs. In The World, such attempts were obviously not as successful as in ours - but nevertheless, Western Slavic people were often forcefully assimilated into the German mainstream, barring some minorities such as the Sorbian people. This may have caused some resentment against other faiths. Nevertheless, old Slavic traces are everywhere in the Eastern parts of Germany, if you know where to look.

    During the Soviet Occupation of Eastern Germany, worshipers of the Bogovi saw something of a resurgence. Officially, the East German government discouraged all religions, but Christianity was more suspect to the state authorities than Slavic religious beliefs - especially since many Red Army members were also worshiping the Bogovi (and had an easier time practicing their beliefs than in the Homeland, where state security was watching more closely).


    There are also lots of remnants of old Celtic settlements and beliefs, but pretty much all the evidence is archaeological. Nevertheless, if the band delves into old Celtic fortresses and barrow-mounds - and they likely will, at some point - I need to figure out how to model their religious associations. Any suggestions? Variants of the Tuatha Dé Danann or the Nemetondevos, perhaps?​

          Frankly, any continental Celtic myth would have to be a reconstruction. Both the 1st edition and the second use bizarre reconstructions of Irish myth. The first used Yeats' Celtic Hinduism the second tried to capture the medieval Clerical take on the Irish gods. The second was arguably more authentic but the first was much more fun and highly playable.

          My suggestion is go with playable. Make up a small pantheon based on the broad themes of Celtic myth as you see it. Remember, the Ancient Celts tend to give their students psychological whiplash. They are the pluperfect example of the phase "Savages are cool, until they're savage." Although we don't get the jokes of the Ancient Celts (we do "get" Roman jokes even when they creep us out) we largely get their sense of humor. Then they do things we find unspeakably alien. Use that pattern. Alternatively charm and alienate they players.

                                      So, one of the first adventures I am planning is a treasure hunt of sorts, which is traditional in German folk tales.

                                      None of the PCs are really affluent, so this should be tempting for at least some of them. And if they keep their wits about them and succeed, they will end up with a classical chest full of gold coins.

                                      However, then their troubles might only start. In their part of Germany, the law states clearly that any such found treasures from previous centuries belong to the state. Thus, they will have to figure out how to actually spend it on things they want. If they only use it in the Mythic Underground, this might work out, although not all supernatural citizens are interested in cash.

                                      But if they want to spend it on mundane things, then they would first have to find a "trustworthy" black market dealer who is willing to exchange their gold into unmarked bills - or cryptocurrency, depending. And if they flout their new wealth, the authorities might become suspicious on just where they suddenly got all this money - doubly so if any electronic funds were involved.

                                      I'm looking for ideas on how I can handle this in the game, particularly if it is done in an amusing (for me) or embarrassing (for the player character characters) manner.
