    Episode XXXVI

    Six Underground Part I

    Ji-Woo's Visions

    Berlin

    Dear Yisheng, I’m writing to thank you again for delivering my absinthe. The way you ran in right after we finished defeating the Dreamer, bearing the bottle of absinthe as though it were the Holy Grail, was truly heroic. I must admit, though, that I’m glad that you at least waited until after Cybele showed up to thank us and turn me back into my normal self. Somehow I don’t think being a maenad would go over well with the Bureaucracy. Anyway, you can report to the Ministry of Wine and Spirits that the absinthe of Tian is absolutely exquisite. It restored all the color to my world and opened my mind once again to the beauty of life. I hope you’ve made god progress on submitting the paperwork commending yourself for a job well done, but I’m also glad you were able to sit quietly with me for awhile in Cybele’s temple as you enjoyed a glass of absinthe and rested a bit before heading back to Tian. You need a break from time to time too. In fact, if you haven’t taken a week off yet, you should get on it ASAP! I’m serious. Get on that.

    * * * * *

    I’m bored and frustrated and restless. Normally, I’d run off to Amsterdam or Ibiza or something, but I don’t really feel like going anywhere else right now. I’m also not feeling very creative. I think it’s because Pipa’s been gone for awhile on some task for Daji. I miss her. It’s weird, though, because I’ve never really MISSED anyone like THIS before. I mean, I’ve never been that attached to anyone except for my father, and I do miss him. But that’s different, and I don’t know how to deal with this. Sure, Hermione and Aoide and Zane and Edward are around, and I can easily distract myself by hanging out with them, but it’s not the same without Pipa. She’s up to something with Daji, and I’m sure it’s important, so I don’t want to bother her. But I hate feeling like this. It’s frustrating and a little scary.

    I have to do something to occupy myself until Pipa comes back. I think I’ll see if Zane wants to go explore the Black Forest through the portal in the dragon cave, the one Ulfa used to escape so long ago. We won’t go in deep; just enough to have a look around for an afternoon and then come right back. Maybe the mystical wonder of the place will brighten my spirits! But first, I have to see if it’s auspicious or not!

    Dun (Retreat) transforming into Guan (Contemplating)

    Well, that’s not good. The Yi Jing advises retreat. I should withdraw from the situation, which seems to mean that it is definitely NOT auspicious to go into the Black Forest. I should remain at home, which won’t resolve my current frustrations, but will at least teach me something. That’s just great.

    * * * * *

    Fuck the Yi Jing. I can’t hang around Berlin anymore. I’m getting Zane and we’re going to the Black Forest, auspicious or not.

    * * * * *

    I should have listened to the Yi Jing. Now Zane and I are lost. It’s not all bad, though. The Black Forest is amazing, and I’m rationing my absinthe. Everything is . . . epic. It’s like a fairy tale forest, beautiful and terrifying all at once. I’ve been sketching plenty of references for later. Honestly, even though we’re hopelessly lost, this journey has undammed the river of my creativity, and I can’t wait to get back to my flat and start painting.

    Getting back to the flat is turning out to be the problem, though. Yisheng, can you send help? Preferably more quickly than last time, with the absinthe delivery? Thanks!

    * * * * *

    Yisheng, we’re still lost, and I haven’t heard from you. We could really use a guide or something, because this forest twists even MY mystical insight. On the other hand, Zane and I managed to outsmart a witch with nothing but our wits, and then Zane outsmarted a big bad wolf with nothing but her fists. So at least being lost has been entertaining. I’m running out of absinthe though. So please hurry.

    * * * * *

    I just remembered, it’s your week off isn’t it, Yisheng? I guess that explains why you haven’t sent help yet. Oh well. I brought my pipa, so I guess I’ll use it to summon Pipa. She’s been around for centuries, so surely she knows how to navigate this forest.

    * * * * *

    Pipa doesn’t know the way out of the forest, so now she’s lost too. She was very cross with me for awhile, but she’s over it now. And at least she’s here! That makes everything better.

    Since Pipa joined us, we met a gorgeous nixie who was delightful except that she drank the last of my absinthe. And then we saved a fellow traveler from a hideous, mangy, leonine creature that spoke with the voices of its past victims and could shapechange into pleasing forms to catch its prey off guard. I could tell instantly that it was wearing a different shape, though. Still, that was probably the scariest part of the excursion. Unfortunately, the traveler we saved turned out to be a bandit. He tried to rob us and kidnap me, so Zane beat him up and Pipa ate him. It really is like we’re wandering around a Grimm’s fairy tale!

    At last! I’m finally home. All I had to do was retrace our steps through the forest. It wasn’t easy, and I wouldn’t have known what to look for, or even how to look, if Manannan mac Lir hadn’t shown up to help us out. He was charming as always. He asked after Andy, of course, and made sure I still had the absinthe spoon he gave me (it’s one of my priced relics). He also brought a fresh bottle of absinthe, which made the remainder of the trip flow much more smoothly. Honestly, the green fairy really helps open my senses when I need to see beyond what’s in front of my face!

    Anyway, I followed the signs and they led me back to the portal, and then Zane, Pipa and I were back in Berlin! We were gone almost a week, which is way too long to be wandering lost in a forest. But I learned a lot from the experience, including how to survive in such dangerous environments. Looks like the Yi Jing was right about that, at least, even if I didn’t exactly obey its advice.

    Anyway, Pipa’s back in town, so I’m going clubbing.

    Oh, Yisheng, you can ignore all of those requests for help in the Black Forest. Just toss them out if you haven’t already.


    Ireland - Gou (Temptation) maintaining

    Well, the Yi Jing indicates that our current situation is defined by temptation. I don’t think it’s addressing any specific action, but I think it’s making a statement about the theme of our current endeavor. And it seems like it’s a desire for power: mystical, political, and romantic. I hope our patrons in the Unseelie Court really do mean well.

    I should clarify what’s going on though. Andy asked us to join him on a trip to Ireland to meet the Queen of Air and Darkness. She and the Unseelie Court need someone to pull off a heist, and felt that their noble knight, Andy, would be the perfect person for the job, and of course they’re right. Unfortunately, Magnus was asleep and no one wanted to wake him since he’s been in a mood lately, so the rest of us—Edward, Zane, Pipa and me—flew to Ireland on Edward’s plane. It’s the same plane he used to fly us to Greece, but it’s been customized a bit since then. It’s very odd, but perfectly Edward. There’s also a charming nymph flight attendant who seems perfectly capable of handling her employer’s quirky personality.

    Once we arrived in Ireland, Andy led us into the hills to a faerie mound, and we descended into the Dark Court’s subterranean realm. I was anxious about it despite Andy’s assurances that his status among the Unseelie would shelter us from any malicious attempts trick us into some bargain that would leave us trapped in the realm for centuries. After all, the whole realm was underground, and I HATE being underground. But Pipa and Zane were with me, and besides, it turns out I had nothing to worry about. The place was so vast and spacious that it wasn’t like being underground at all. It was like wandering through a realm of eternal twilight. The darkness was lit by the soft blue and purple glow of luminescent plants, and a spooky yet gentle mist coiled around our legs as we walked through a forest of twisting gray trees. In the shadows between the trees, glowing eyes peered hungrily at us. It would have been scary if my friends hadn’t been there, but I knew they would protect me. And besides, the beasties in those woods would never get a chance to eat me because Pipa would eat them first.

    Anyway, eventually a couple of dour Unseelie knights on skeletal steeds came to greet us, and after Andy made courtly introductions, the knights summoned some more skeletal steeds from the bones of the earth, and we all rode off to the Queen’s castle. I’d never ridden a horse before, and didn’t really know how, so I rode with Pipa. Zane chose not to ride. She ran alongside us and kept pace perfectly well. I’m pretty sure the Unseelie knights were impressed, but they were determined to snub all of us but Andy, so they didn’t let it show.

    The Unseelie palace took my breath away! It was a towering cathedral of interwoven gray trees, their branches entwined to form intricate embellishments, majestic windows, and tall, slender doors. The architecture was stunning, and I couldn’t help staring at it as we passed through the entry hall and entered the throne room, where the Queen of Air and Darkness sat upon her ebony throne.

    She was just as majestic as the architecture, and she was attended by a dashing dark fae lord named Yrtalion. He seemed to be an advisor. After more polite introductions, the Queen wasted no time telling us what she needed us to do. To summarize, there is a powerful relic, a Black Cauldron that can summon an army of the dead. If the bodies of fallen warriors are placed inside it, it will produce an elixir that ghosts can drink to become powerful soldiers. The Deathlords of Helheim recently acquired the Cauldron from Crom Cruach, and they are searching for two other relics, both of which are required to make the Cauldron work. One is a magical stirring ladle, but no one knows where it is. And no one knows WHAT the third relic is, but we aren’t the only ones looking for it. The Queen believes it’s dangerous to leave the Cauldron in the hands of the Deathlords. They aren’t exactly good guys, after all, and while the Unseelie aren’t really good guys either, at least they aren’t in league with the titans.

    The Unseelie Court has not informed Hel or the Aesir of the Cauldron’s location because they don’t want Hel taking an interest. So part of our challenge will be retrieving the Cauldron without tipping off Hel. Fortunately, Hel is a bit distracted right now. She has captured a scion of Baldur and is trying to woo her, but the scion is very clever and has some mystical help from her father, so she’s managed to put off Hel’s advances so far. Good thing too. I hate it when the gods get all pushy! Also, Loki gave Hel a torque that is supposedly a powerful relic, but he didn’t tell her, or anyone else for that matter, what it does. This has also caused a great deal of concern among the Aesir, so it can serve as a nice smokescreen for our heist too. Of course, we have to be really careful not to implicate the Unseelie Court in anything we do. They have a rocky past with the Aesir, but right now there’s a bit of a detente, and it would be better for everyone if things didn’t erupt into open hostility between the two again.

    So, we just have to get into Helheim, find the Deathlords’ realm, and steal the Cauldron. The catch is that the Deathlords' realm is bitterly cold, so cold, in fact, that it drains the very life from one’s soul. Even Magnus couldn’t survive there for long, and the fact that he and I are both immune to mundane cold won’t protect us. However, Hel has a relic called the Dark Key that guards against the soul-draining effects of the Deathlord’s realm. So we have to get the key . . . or make a copy.

    And that’s the exciting part! Lord Yrtalion is a master of shadow magics, and he knows some deep, dark mystical secrets that will allow me to use my Taiyi to create a copy of the relic from my own shadow. Isn’t that fascinating? I can’t wait to try it, though I have to admit I’m a little nervous. I’ll have to tear away my own shadow, and that’s going to hurt. A lot. But it’s not every day you learn such an amazing trick!

    Anyway, Andy assured the Queen of Air and Darkness that we would take care of things. He is very courtly and noble when he’s among the Unseelie. It’s quite charming. Actually, even Edward was on his best behavior! Which is good, because once we were done at court, Lord Yrtalion escorted us to a teahouse known as the Weeping House. It was wonderful, with platforms suspended on the bowed branched of a huge willow tree. Luminescent white blossoms lit the place with a soft glow, and the atmosphere was both spooky and genteel. Andy recognized a few of the other patrons, and we joined them. It was instantly obvious that the three ladies, two banshees and a sluagh named Madb, Deirdre and Aoibhell, were deeply attracted to Andy, and who can blame them? There was also a brooding fae lord named Fionn, and he seemed to hit it off with Edward.

    Of course, I had to sample the local absinthe, and I found that it was remarkable! Yrtalion warned me that it contained the tears of children, but assured me the Unseelie had not been the cause of those tears. Zane seemed to disapprove, but I was too curious to pass it up, and I’m glad I didn’t. The absinthe was exquisite, and once I felt it open my senses, I decided to read the tea leaves floating in Deirdre’s cup, hoping to gain some insight into our upcoming heist.

    The tea leaves were a bit reluctant, but they offered a few nice revelations. I couldn’t divine what the torque really does, but I figured that information would he hidden behind too many veils of illusion for me to see it. It did become clear to me that Andy had a relationship with Madb, Deirdre and Fionn, though. He had slept with all three and was actively courting Madb and Deirdre, the banshees. He wasn’t involved with Aoibhell, but she certainly wanted to be involved with him. I didn’t need to read tea leaves to see that.

    On a more serious note, though, I learned that the stirring ladle is located somewhere in the Underworld, and that there is a detachment of frost and grave giants searching for it and the Cauldron’s other components. I suppose it was futile to hope there wouldn’t be competition, huh? That’s not really how fate works. Also, the tea leaves showed me that Hel’s security is tighter than usual right now, but it’s focused on the torque, of course, so we can use that to our advantage. Also, Helheim’s sentinel, Modgud, is jealous s of the scion of Baldur, so she might not be quite as diligent in her duties as she usually is.

    Of course, during our little tea party, Yrtalion was very charming, and clearly flirting heavily with both me and Pipa. I thought he was very handsome, and so did Pipa. He’s exactly the sort of guy I like sleeping with, because I know it will be a lot of fun, but there won’t be any commitment or attachment. So Pipa and I shared a look and decided that we would hook up with Yrtalion later. But first, he had to show me how to create a duplicate of the Dark Key. So Pipa and I returned with him to his elegant mansion of crystalline shadow while Andy left for some private time with Madb and Deridre. All in all, it seemed we were in for a very romantic evening. Edward, however, was stuck without a date, but he made the best of it by sharing drugs with Fionn and the other patrons of the Weeping House. Zane tells me that things got a little crazy there after the rest of us left.

    The process of transforming my shadow into the relic is fascinating. It involves some very touchy Taiyi techniques that come dangerously close to etching my soul, and the risk of damaging my spirit is very real. It takes a delicate touch but a firm command of Taoist techniques, and I will have to write new symbols with ink made from my shadow itself. In fact, that’s the part that will tear my shadow away from me: it will turn into the Key when the symbols become reality. I think I can handle it, but I guess we won’t know until I try. Still, it’s very exciting! I asked Yrtalion how he knew all of this about Taiyi, and he said he made a point of analyzing his chosen medium in other paradigms. Makes sense to me. I was a bit concerned about losing my shadow, but Yrtalion assures me it will grow back.

    Once the lessons were over, Pipa and Yrtalion and I spent the rest of the evening in his luxurious bedroom, wrapped in each other and in sheets made of pure shadow and startled gasps. It was delightful and dream-like and wonderful, especially since I was still drinking the Weeping Absinthe. Actually, I think I’m becoming addicted to these wonderful mystical absinthes. Between the Tian absinthe, the Weeping Absinthe, and the potent stuff Andy’s father gave me in the Black Forest, I’m becoming a bit bored with mundane absinthes. They don’t have the same impact the mystical liquors do. Yisheng, what would it take to set up regular deliveries of Tian absinthe?

    Anyway, our time with Yrtalion was over too soon, but it’s probably for the best. We have work to do. We’re flying back to Berlin to pick up Magnus. He might be able to get us some more information from Hriki and Sigrid. Then it’s off to Helheim!
