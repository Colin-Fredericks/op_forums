    You called? There are technically two kinds of Death in Hinduism, so you have two possible outcomes.

    The first is the Death overseen by Yama (or, in a more general sense, Indra and his court), which is the typical 'You die, you are judged by Yama, Lord of the Underworld, and given your just deserts'. What exactly this is, varies by telling. In some beliefs, you are punished for your sins in Naraka, or given your rewards in Svarga and then sent onto your next life. In others, the quality of your next life IS your reward and/or punishment. (I'll also make a quick note here that time is an illusion in Hinduism so your 'past' life could very well be from the future...so your punishment for evil in this life may be to be reborn during the time of the Black Death or something). In other tellings, part of the reward and punishment is handled during life itself, administered by Shani, God of misfortune and step-brother of Yama.

    As for what decides whether you get rewarded or punished, well that varies from 'don't be a jerk' to 'carefully and to the letter follow the rules and regulations set out for your caste and sub-caste till the end of your days to maintain purity'. There are descriptions of Naraka that are almost Inferno-esque, describing various realms dedicated to punishing various sins. Eating beef and killing cows is a popular feature on these lists of sins (of course Indra in the Vedas eats beef like there's no tomorrow but most Hindus nowadays really don't like to think about their holiest of holy books describing beef eating, but I digress). If you have ever eaten beef then you are probably on a one-way ticket to the absolute depths of Naraka Of course I'll probably be meeting you there for the exact same reason

    Those people living it up in Svarga getting rewarded are the Pitri, the honoured ancestors...Hindu funeral rites often end with a prayer that the departed soul join the Pitri. (Pitri, incidentally, literally means Fathers; no one particularly talks about what happens to the women getting rewarded, because orthodox Hinduism is nothing if not patriarchal).

    Now, the second kind of Death, is the Death overseen by Shiva and Kali (or, in a more general sense, the Trimurti and Tridevi), the death of liberation. It is tied not to morality or virtue, but to enlightenment. Through Yoga, be it the meditative practices of Ashtanga Yoga (a.k.a. the stuff most westerners recognize as Yoga), the search of knowledge and Truth that is Jnana Yoga, the carrying out of sacred duties (which again may be defined by caste roles but need not be) of Karma Yoga, or absolute loving Devotion to Divinity that is Bhakti Yoga, the seeker recognizes and understands Brahman, the underlying Truth of Reality, and becomes one with it...exactly what that means has a lot of variations and personal interpretations, but whatever it is it always includes being freed from the cycle of reincarnation...you are no longer bound by the Lie of Heaven and Hell, you have Ascended to the Supernal (to borrow some imagery from another gameline entirely ).

    If you can manage that, well, you're set.

            Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post


            Sorry, but I just don't think spiritual enlightenment is worth giving up steak and burgers!
            You won't have to As I mentioned in my earlier post, giving up steak and burgers is only a requirement for entering Heaven...not spiritual enlightenment. The goal of spiritual enlightenment is to transcend both Heaven and Hell and achieve Liberation. Eating beef is a Sin, and therefore tied to Morality, whereas Enlightenment is separate from Morality. Morality only determines whether you go to Heaven or Hell and what form you come back in...and both Heaven and Hell are Fail States as far as Hinduism is concerned.

            Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
            Also, I'm rather curious to know what constitutes as evil deeds in Hinduism, since there is a sect of practitioners who specifically focus on disillusioning themselves of EVERYTHING (including the consumption of human flesh and feces) in order that they might view the world without biased judgement.
            'Evil deeds' ranges from 'being a jerk' to 'marrying outside your sub-caste' to 'eating beef' to murder to 'all non-procreative sex' to theft to adultery depending on which Hindu you ask or which text you refer to... I'm pretty certain nobody's come up with a proper official listing, and Hinduism having no central authority there probably never will be. And again, this really doesn't matter because Sin itself is a part of the Lie of the World, of Avidya Maya, the Ignorance of Illusion. The Aghori that you're referring to ARE sinning as far as most Hindus are concerned...they just don't care because their focus is not Morality but Enlightenment. Funnily enough, the word Aghori literally means 'Not Terrible', and they took that name because they want to point out that the practices they indulge in are only 'terrible' through the eyes of civilized society, not in the face of Ultimate Truth.

            They're not the only ones, Tantrik mystics who worship Kali explicitly use what they call the five Ms (Matsya, meaning fish, Mansa, meaning meat, Madya, meaning alcohol, Mudrika, meaning parched rice, and Maithun, meaning sexual intercourse) in their rituals to honor Kali specifically because Orthodox Hinduism considers these five things unclean, and goes to great lengths to keep them away from temples and other 'holy' things, and by using them in the worship of God(dess) the Tantrik shows that he has overcome this limitation imposed by society. One Tantra inspired hymn to the Goddess even calls her Mahadevi, Mahasuri...i.e., she who is both The Great (Maha) Goddess (Devi) and The Great Demoness (Asuri)...or, to use Scion terms, the Great Goddess and the Great Titaness, with the implication being that both what orthodox Hinduism terms 'holy' and all it terms 'unholy' arise from the same Divinity.

            Interestingly, for all their otherwise bizarre practices, Aghoris and Tantriks are usually thoroughly opposed to the Caste System that Orthodox Hinduism is so fond of...all members of all castes including the untouchables are welcome, as both students and teachers (like most of Hinduism, these groups have strong teacher-student traditions). Tantra goes a step further, and allows women to be both teachers and priestesses, something Orthodox Hinduism does not allow, and in rural parts of India it is not unheard of for women and untouchables to join these sects as a way to get away from the mistreatment meted out to them on a daily basis. It's been getting better in recent years, but for a long time the only way a woman would have gotten any respect in a typical patriarchal village would have been living in the cremation grounds and covering herself with ashes while drinking out of a human skull
