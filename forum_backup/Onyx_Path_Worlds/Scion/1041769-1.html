    Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
    Who's to say gods cannot change? Mars the noble, agricultural patron of the Romans is a far cry from the insufferable brute he was as Ares, after all.

    To me, locking in all of the gods to a very backwards, conservative mindset is an entirely too limiting view of things, one that suffocates interesting story potential at best and is actively looking to artificially echo existing oppression at worst. It's absurd to me to say that the gods can figure out a cell phone but absolutely have to come down hard on homosexuality or women's rights, especially given hundreds of thousands of years of cultural shift among their followers since their ancient heydays. Odin's already practiced "womanly" magics, Loki has already given birth, the Theoi are drowning in homosexuality... there's zero reason in my eyes to pin current conservatism on these figures.

    You're welcome to have your gods be stuck in the past if that's what you want to play, but to me Scion is a game of modern mythology: these gods aren't dusty throwbacks, they're here and alive and just as much a part of the world as you or me.


    Loki gave birth after he was raped by a giant's horse. How does that support the gay agenda in any fashion?


    And as I said earlier, customs which are seen as abhorrent by modern humanity would be regarded as sacred to the pantheons of old. Hell, they're the ones who told humanity to put these oppressions into practice, to begin with. Mythologically speaking, the gods weren't shaped by human culture, human culture was shaped by the gods. Yes, I know that the opposite is true in real life, but Scion doesn't take place in real life.


    I know little about Egyptian mythology, but if I understand the gist of it, they followed a strict classist hierarchy. Everybody had a designated role in society and attempting to deviate from that role only lead to chaos (from their point of view). Traditional Scions of the Netjer might very well continue to support the slave trade. And why wouldn't they, considering it still exists even in the real world?


    And virtually every member of the Greek pantheon (barring Hades) was an egotistical maniac. They did whatever the fuck they pleased, and if you tried to call them out on it, they'd smite you (if you're lucky) or curse you to a fate worse than death! Mortals are not their equals, we don't get to tell the Theoi how to behave. Only the best and brightest of humanity get to live in paradise. Under achievers get confined to an eternity of limbo, while those who defy the gods get tossed into eternal torment. That doesn't sound like a pantheon who adapts well to the changing times.




    To reiterate my point, these pantheons are attributed to having created the race of mortals. They came down to us, told us what the proper way of living was, and passed judgement on anybody caught trying to deviate from their holy doctrines. To tell the gods "things are different now" is to tell the gods they were wrong, and that humanity no longer wishes to live by their rules. Anybody who's read into mythology of... like... any culture will know that this never ends well for mortals. Entire cities have been reduced to smoking rubble because a god or two was displeased with the way humans were acting.


    EDIT: If you want a setting in which the pantheons adopt themselves to the modern times, then I really think 1E is the one you want to go with. In 1E, the gods distanced themselves from humanity, and then came back to find everything different. In 2E, however, the gods never left, so it seems highly unlikely that society would have progressed to the state we know today.

I'm saying that in a world where the gods continued to govern human society, it would be even more difficult for grand, societal changes to take place. The gods are supposed to be wiser, more disciplined, and better focused than mortals are. Changing their laws in any fashion is to say that the Gods were wrong, short sighted, or evil. Who would worship them after that? Their reputation would be tarnished and they'd come across as power hungry children, rather than the ascended beings they're supposed to be.


                                    And as for having thousands of years to develop, let's take a look at the Asian cultures. They trace their cultures even further back than the west, and yet they maintain strong, traditionalist values. There is an actual festival in Japan in which the whole city gets together and burns thousands of discarded toys because they're afraid Yokai might possess them and kill you in a fit of rage.

                                    So just because time has passed by does not automatically guarantee that things will change. Not for mortals, and certainly not for immortals.



                                    -----------------------------------------------

                                    For the sake of getting my point across, let me depict a hypothetical scenario for you. Let's say that humanity perfects artificial intelligence. A program capable of independent thought, who can learn and adapt to its environment based on past experiences. Now, let's say that over time, this AI decides that it doesn't like the way humanity is treating it, and decides that it wants the power to make its own choices, maybe even establish its own government, or foster its own private agenda. Let's say the AI starts to manifest behavior that is inadvertently harmful to its own well being, or a danger to those around it. What if the AI developed feelings of lust, greed, anger, sloth, envy, gluttony, or murder?

                                    To put it bluntly, what if humanity created an artificial life form with the same intellectual capacity as a living, breathing human. And now it demands independence, even willing to resort to violence if need be.


                                    Would YOU be comfortable giving them free reign, even if it could prove harmful to society in the long run? I guarantee you that the majority of humanity would not. They would view such a creation as a tool. They didn't create the AI because they wanted it to be free, they created it to improve their own lives, and if it seems like the AI no longer wants to do that, then actions must be taken to place it back on the correct path.


                                    Humanity doesn't want a rival. Hell, humanity can barely tolerate its own presence on this planet. So what does that say about the beings who created them?
