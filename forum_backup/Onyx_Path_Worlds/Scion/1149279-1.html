                    Tons of people follow traditional belief systems modernly, based off US Census data, there were 340,000 individuals who self-identified as 'Pagan' in the United States of America, and 186,000 who identified as following a form of First Nations religious tradition. Interestingly, both of those numbers jumped a fair bit since 2001 where those two groups sat at 140,000 and 103,000 back in 2001. People still follow such traditions, even if the details have changed over time, there are several people on the forums who do according to previous discussions on the topic. Even though these groups don't still engage in all of their traditional religious practices doesn't mean they're not still following those faiths.

                    For example, you don't see a lot of flagellants or desert-based hermits around, but there are still Catholics. People are forced to reshape their religious practices into what a State-Entity deems appropriate behavior, for better or worse. So, modernly traditions such as animal sacrifice, and death rituals can be forcibly changed by these State-Entities by threatening legal action against the adherents of these faiths. For example, burials by exposure that we suspect some Celtic groups practiced where bodies were left out for carrion animals to consume, is illegal in Canada, and thus followers of such faiths are torn between their traditional belief, and legal action being taken against members of the community. Just because these faiths may change doesn't mean they're still not around.

                    It might just be due to my field of study, but I have spoken with a fair number of Neo-Pagans both online, and in person. I think about eight people in person without me going to any organized ceremonies or the like. It might just be that you haven't realized some of the people who have talked to are Neo-Pagans, I don't know if it's just where I am, but people don't often come out with their religious traditions without prompting.

                    Edit: If you are interested in becoming a member of such a community, you could probably find a local group online. With Samhain coming up for the Celtic Neo-Pagans, you might be able to attend a celebration of some sort.

                                    The extremely strict rules of war the Devas are supposed to follow offer their enemies some respite.                                         Its possible that it follows some patterns regionally, for instance your much less likely to find a pagan in some small rural town in the middle of nowhere for the same reason your less likely to find a Japanese immigrant in the same place. Extreme homogeneity breeds itself true and heterogeneity tends to attract more of the same.

                                        Under admittingly strong rules of disregarding those i would consider to be wiccans or similar (which i might add makes up the majority of adherents, so if you would like to include them you should increase this accordingly) I would say that adherents of my faith alone are populated enough in America for anyone to find a reasonably sized group of 6+ members within an hour or two of them in almost all cases, much more if your on either coast or near a large city. Its also very common in the prison system.

                                        As for the extent of current practices, I don't know any pagan groups that claim, or that i'd suspect, of performing human sacrifices. (interestingly, satanist groups, while much less common,i cannot make the same claim for). I perform animal sacrifice regularly, but its an admittingly uncommon practice to do so.

                                        Though to go on a bit of a tangent (And a possible plot source) in the US its actually much easier to get away with traditionally cultic behavior than many people actually assume do to the laws regarding freedom of worship. It is in fact legally possible to have a cult that engages in some very unsavory behavior including beating members and distributing corporal punishment, refusing to allow them to leave, sexual practices, starving adherents as punishment or restricting food for theological reasons, etc. The church of Scientology is the only group i know offhandedly that engages in this however. The Legal issues come about through rights regarding freedom of worship and also that of the informed consent of adults.
