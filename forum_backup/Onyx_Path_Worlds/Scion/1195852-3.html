                                As for the Order Justice thing... You'll be happy to know that nothing has been discarded Order is the Purview of divine authority and sovereignty, and the establishment of civilization in opposition to Chaos. Leader is the calling of kings and queens, those who lead while others follow. Judge is the Calling of those who enforce the rules and mete out punishment or reward or mercy as they see fit. A God with Order but no Leader or Judge, like Vishnu, is a force of stability and protector against disorder, but not much for actual laws. A God with Order and Judge, like Lakshmi, is both a force for stability and giver of sovereignty, and also a deity who constantly judges the works of mortals and rewards them with fortune and misfortune as she sees fit. A God with Order and Leader, like Indra, is a classical king who established and embodies the laws of the land, but has underlings to actually enforce them. And a God with all three, like Hera, is a force of divine sovereign power, a queen of the gods, and a judge of fidelity and marriage laws. So, as I hope you can see, nuance has been added, rather than anything being removed.

                                        There are three kinds of deities in Scion 2e... Gods, who are primarily involved with the World through the lens of Callings and humanity... Titans, who primarily interact with the World through, and are consumed by, their Purviews... And Primordials, who are those deities that are distant from and do not involve themselves with the World. Titans in 2e aren't necessarily evil, just completely caught up in their Purviews... Helios for example is a Titan that works with the Greek gods. Ao Kuang the Dragon King of the Eastern Sea works for the Shen... The Orisha as a pantheon don't have any Titans at all. Primordial deities are people like Oludomare of the Orisha or Danu of the Tuatha, who are vast cosmic creator deities that rarely if ever interact with humanity themselves. These classifications are not static... Ra was a Primordial who became a God after Isis stole his name, and Brahma was a God whose steadily weakening relationship with the World as he withdraws into himself have made him a Primordial.
