        Darkness and Moon are one of those really tricky things. Sun includes the theoretical "Day" powers, which has it opposing both Darkness and Moon at the same time. The way I look at it is that there are many figures who are linked to one, but not the other, which makes the two Purviews distinct from each other. For example, in the 'Primarily Moon' camp we have Selene, Artemis (though that one is just sort of tenuous sometimes), Sin, Mani, Coyolxauhqui, Bast (though I will admit the Egyptians are not my forte), and Chors. That host is directly representative of very specifically the Moon lacking direct connections to the 'Night' short of it being when they are around. For figures who are primarily 'Night', we have Nix, Apep (sort of), Nott, and Tezcat. For a Deity that unifies both Purviews, there is Tsukuyomi.

        So, cultures are dividing these concepts up pretty heavily. Why, I'm not particularly sure. it might be because you can occasionally see the Moon during the day, or because the Moon is a very obvious 'entity' in the night sky. On top of this, there are some pretty divergent 'subsections' that can exist within Darkness and Moon (just like how you've mentioned Fire incorporates Passion). Moon can have the obvious Lunacy, but also things for illuminating hidden things, and changing. Compared to this, Darkness can draw a connection on fear, and obscuring things (for example, hiding things from Mystery). Due to this, I feel that Darkness and Moon should probably remain disparate Purviews, cultures were viewing them as separate entities, and the both evoke rather divergent ideas.

        Wyrd is a really great basis in concept for the Norse. Our revised Norse PsP is probably similar. Jotunblut was neat, but it really required a lot of dancing around why Njord, Freya, and Freyr had it when they were not descended from Giants. Compared to that, the inexorable style of Norse Fate connects them all (with Freyr doomed to die, and Njord, well, doomed to live and return to his sister-wife without his beloved children) and is a divergent enough idea that it can make a really neat PsP.

        Stars was my personal nemesis for about sixteen months while I threw ideas at it until it finally started to work. I'm really looking forward to seeing what you lot have done to Stars to make it, well, sensible.

                That sort of thing's a lot like I ended up going with, I think. I've not had the chance to read The Wicked + The Divine yet, but Ishtar was one of the big points I drew on. Lots of being... not antagonistic, but divergent or separate from the Pantheon. Ishtar holding reality hostage (threatening to tear the gates off the Underworld) if her demands are not met, Radegast clearly being very important, being one of the Four Kings, but only interfacing with others when someone else drags him into the story (Stribog stealing his Cloak of Stars). Quetzalcoatl violating social norms and breaking into the Underworld to steal things. Aether being the Titan that is so distant it exists beyond Ouranos, more withdrawn than even the other Titans, Ame-no-Minakanushi popping into existence and then almost instantly leaving. All powerful beings, all important, and wondrous, but... far away, literally, or not. Also capable of making constellations and physically doing things with the Stars, but you can only make so many Boons out of 'fiddle with the night sky'.

                Either way if you guys did similarly or not, I look forward to seeing to how you vanquished that beast of a Purview. So important yet so poorly or inconsistently defined.

                        Oh that sounds neat. Planning is a very tricky thing to represent well using the toolkit from Scion, especially because a lot of the time you end up trying to play someone who is more intelligent than yourself if you're playing someone who is bothering to invest time and energy into planning. It becomes one of those situations where the character is limited by the player's own capacity. Manipulation and Wits both also end up having that problem sometimes, where if a player doesn't have the natural capacity for those things, it can be tricky for the character to really wield them.

                        Is Forge going to be able to do any work with... I guess non-physical works of art? Poetry, music, oral-storytelling, that sort of thing? Those things can be exceptionally tricky to represent in a Deity's repertoire. Especially when it is something that is obviously important to the culture (Susano-O inventing poetry [localized cult center myth, so that's one of the 'grain of salt' myths], Cairbre inventing Satire, Apollo serenading others with his music, Dionysus being a patron of acting) but not particularly mechanics friendly.
