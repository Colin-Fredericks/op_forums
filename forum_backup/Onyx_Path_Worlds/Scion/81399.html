    What will you Pantheons be like?

    02-28-2014, 12:00 AM
    Even though it may still be a ways off, it looks like we will be getting rules for how Pantheons form this time around (in Demigod 2e I believe). With that being the case, I'm curious as to what sort of Pantheon your Scion groups will form. Is there any unifying concept to your group that might be the basis for a PSP (not sure if the concept of these will make it through to 2e though).

Once I can get a new group together and win them over on Scion... we'll see what we come up with (though hopefully the new rules will be out by then).

          Forming your own pantheon would probably be a startlingly dangerous and difficult prospect for almost any group. So much so that I am curious what kind of mechanical benefits they will include to overcome the nearly overwhelming social backlash from such an action. I am also interested to see what metaphysical steps will be required to undertake such an action, given that it is currently only reasonable possible with the intervention of the Wyrd or Arbiter.

          On the old forums I did a poll to see how many groups ever created their own pantheons, and the result was 5%. I am curious to see how much this number changes once actual rules are in place to facilitate the event.

                                  Okay... I think we're straying from the actual intent... I'm not talking about making up Pantheons like Avengers or Justice League... I'm talking about the pantheons the Players are forming with their characters. Remember, the players are playing characters that will... with luck, become Gods in their own right.

                                  Many people tend to form bonds with those they associate with that are as strong as any familial bond... in some cases far stronger. Many band of Scions are formed from Scions of multiple pantheons... and most would rather trust their teamates that their own divine parents (and the rest of their families). So why wouldn't they form their own pantheon? Many groups tend to have their members specialized in certain areas, much like other pantheons, right? Of course, the interaction with one's original Pantheon is also a great source of adventure... but sooner or later there will be tension between what they want and what the player/character wants.

                                  Not all players will want to form their own patheons... some might want to take over and revitalize their current pantheon (especially those bands formed from Scions of a single Pantheon).                                           I agree with Iry on this. Liking your friends is no excuse for abandoning your family. Your parent Pantheons would go apeshit. Any Pantheon with Duty, Loyalty or Piety would freak out at the idea of one of their own abandoning them, and those with Vengeance will never forgive you for doing it.

                                          The Theoi would never even entertain the idea that anyone would want to leave their club of awesomeness. The Netjer would consider it an affront to Ma'at and Piety. The Aztecs know of many ways that such a Scion could donate to the war effort. The Shen would probably swamp you in official paperwork for the next couple of millennia. And so on.

                                          And, perhaps most importantly, Scions are created with one primary purpose: to be soldiers in the war against the Titans. Any God level Scion is expected to be on call to defend his Pantheon. And you're going to go up to Shango and say, I'm abandoning my post? You would be court-martialled and executed on the spot.

                                          All this is not to say that I don't want rules for players creating their own Pantheons. I do. The Deva-Yazata split shows clearly that it has happened before, and can happen again. But one should both have a good IC reason or it (as opposed to 'let's climb the mountain cuz it's there') and be well aware that they will likely end up dead, or wishing they were, by the end of it.

                                          Of course, if they can pull it off, they will be the talk of the Overworld for the next few aeons.

                                                  Hm. Some relevant quotes from Neall:

                                                  Current plan for Demigod is to have mechanics on how pantheons are formed or re-formed (''interpretatio romana''). Kind of stands to reason that PCs could take advantage of those.
                                                  Pantheons are comprised of gods and demigods (and culture heroes who exist on the hero level, but they don't do much, metaphysically speaking). When I mentioned them forming, I didn't mean to imply you, Jane Hero Smith, weren't part of a pantheon on the Hero level - you are. Demigod addresses how pantheons form as social as magical constructs within Fate, but you benefit from those constructs at every level, Origin included.
                                                  Interpretatio is going to be a thing in 2e. It's something all of the writers are keenly aware of, so we're taking that into account while designing the cosmology. As for PSPs, well. Those have changed a lot already. It's not fair or accurate to describe pantheon membership as a social club with fringe metaphysical benefits...but I wouldn't say it's fair to describe them as permanent mystical bloodlines anymore, either.
                                                  There is a plan for the new edition to more on religion as well as myth. I mean, people believed - and continue to believe! - this stuff. Blood was shed for it. The least we can do is acknowledge the difference between Jupiter Optimus Maximus and the King of Olympus (even if they both wield the three thunderbolts of Tinia, because they're the same dude). When I mentioned interpretatio, I meant it in the more general sense: how other cultures (and pantheons!) look at and interpret gods has an impact, if you consider that the mythology is partially driven by the acts of Gods and Fate. We're also talking about immortal, divine beings who exist over a continuum of thousands of years. People wear a lot of hats in their mayfly lives; how many more so do the gods wear, then?
                                                  Note that first quote, alluding to how the Theoi were reinterpreted in Rome. That is, becoming the Roman pantheon apparently involved some re-formation of the pantheon on some level.

                                                  Iry's argument appears to be based on the idea that you can't form a new pantheon without leaving your old one. I suspect that's not the case - that you _can_ form another pantheon without leaving your original. After all, it wouldn't be unknown.
