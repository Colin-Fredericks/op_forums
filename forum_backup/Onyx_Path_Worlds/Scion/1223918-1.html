        1. The setting material seems to imply that you presume worship in the Shiunesh is extinct or very nearly so, much moreso than implied for most pantheons in the new core. This seems especially blatant in the paragraph crossing pages 50 and 51. Was this divergence from the norm for this edition an intentional design choice on the part of your team, and if so, why did you make it? Or am I way off base?
        Yeah, we personally prefer to set our Pantheons in the world as we ourselves live in it every day. We assume that reframing this in the context of Scion 2e's World is easy enough; generally, we make up as little as possible, so you can just take the source materials as they are, and reframe them whichever way you want.

        2. Related to #1, if Shiunesh worship is so limited, why is Anatolia not an uninhabitable wasteland? This does not seem to be a pantheon that would take a lack of worship in good stride, and aside from that they're probably all hungry and smelly due to all the bathing and eating they're not doing as a result. Do you just assume that the Theoi/Yazatas/Tanrilar/whoever else I'm forgetting took up the slack (not with the eating/bathing, the other stuff)?
        That is basically my assumption. On the one hand, some of the Shiunesh went on being worshipped under other names (mantles) for millennia, including the Minor Goddess Kubaba, who under her Mantle of Kybele became not only a Major member of the castration-happy Phrygian pantheon (worshipped outside Phrygia as well into Eastern Anatolia and Thrace), but later also, by sharing her Mantle with Rhea/Ops, one of the Theoi. On the other hand, the Theoi and Yazata did all the godly stuff in Anatolia for the rest of Antiquity, later being displaced by however Christianity and Islam work (I'm not getting into that!). So yeah, don't worry, Turkey is taken care of, at least in that respect.

        3. So, you evidently elected to put out this work solely in Scon 2e. Is it then reasonable to presume that Scion 2.0 is a functionally dead project, at least as a ruleset? What made you decide to go in this direction for your stuff?
        Short answer: Kinda. We elected to go with the times instead of constructing what would essentially have been an unnecessary rival product to 2e. We assume that the majority of people is going to switch to 2e anyway, and so this is where our work will be more useful. Long answer: I'll leave that to Watcher.

        4. A bit of a practical matter: how would you distinguish Tarhun from other thunderous god-kings in terms of characterization? I'm a bit under-educated on some of these, and struggle on how to keep him well-separated from figures like Marduk and especially Ba'al Hadad.
        Oh, while there's huge overlap in function, they're very easy to keep apart in character. Tarhun, to put it mildly, is a dick. He'll do anything to stay in power, he'll blast his own children to shreds, he'll lie, he'll cheat - he makes Zeus look like a saint. He's a very war-like god who'll have his bulls graze in the ruins of destroyed cities. He's a fitting leader to the generally very aggressive and very demanding Shiunesh.
        Ba'al Hadad is the young hero, a figure full of sexual vigour and youthful energy, a monster fighter and a dying-and-rising god. He's also struggling in a complicated web of politics where half the pantheon secretely or not-so-secretely wants him dead, and so sometimes he does paranoid things like refusing to have windows in his palace.
        With Marduk, you should never forget that he's a composite figure, who subsumes a lot of the associations of the older Mesopotamian storm gods Ishkur and Ninurta. He has very strong associations with Water and even stronger ones with kingship than the other two - him vacating his throne is an absolute disaster, where for the Elohim, it's kind of just the result of the "scheme of the week". Thus, I'd characterise Marduk as quite haughty, even though he, too, is said to be of relatively lowly origins, having basically received his kingship as payment for defeating Tiamat. He's also definitely the most overpowered of the three, as the Royal Dynasties of Babylon just kept stuffing this their personal god with ever more attributes.
        So yeah, I think they're all quite easy to tell apart

        5. Is there anything interesting that you wound up cutting from the final pdf that you'd be interested in sharing or getting into more depth with?
        Not really. As you can tell from the length of this, we don't really do cuts At one point, I suggested doing a little Appendix on what became of the Hurrian Mantles of the Shiunesh in the later Armenian Empire of Urartu, but that never got off the ground for reasons of lack of sources on those guys.

        6. I seem to recall that at one point, Watcher mentioned that both of the competing lineages in Hittite myth had both gods and titans in their ranks. This obviously changed at some point during the research and design process (unless I'm completely misremembering, or course). What compelled this change, and what figures had a different status in the earlier design?
        Hm, that might have been before we clearly settled on 2e? Or might have been miscommunication? Because one line has to be Titans, for the reason already that Titan is such a political designation for "enemy". However, both sides have various Primordials on their side, so that might be where the confusion comes from? Anu and Aya side with Tarhun, Alalu and his folks with Kumarbi.

        and lastly, how is progress going on your group's next project? I'm really eager to see whatever pantheon is next on the docket for you.
        One pantheon is currently going through the last bits of proof-reading and should hopefully be ready to go soon. Two are still having their PSPs meddled with to make them easier to implement. And two are currently being researched and written, and promise to take a whole lot more time, because I swear, sources on the Taíno just want to make me sad.

        Thank you very much for your time.
        You're welcome! We're always happy to answer questions on our projects!

                    We are always more than happy to answer questions on anything we produce!

                    Our standards for Purviews and Gods has not changed going into 2e, the biggest change in that area is the dividing of Titans into Titans and Primordials. Our standard for what 'is' a God obviously changes from Pantheon to Pantheon, and the 'bar' for having a Purview or Calling changes similarly. We intend to allow each individual culture define themselves rather than forcefully fit them into different pan-Pantheon standards. We haven't changed to a looser standard for this or for future projects, and we intend to keep our little explanations for each Calling and Purview so people know why we made certain decisions.

                    In areas we will deviate from RAW, for instance, several of the new members of the Túatha Dé Danann I have written have less than the standard 3 Callings and 3 Purviews simply because they are hyper-minor characters I am including mostly for the completion of the áes dána section of Cath Maige Tuired.

                    The Primeval Gods (Hittite Primordials) by and large wouldn't work for Divine Parents as they are sealed away within The Dark Earth. The one exception is Aya who for some reason is still wandering around. The only issue about writing Aya up as a Divine Parent is that he is representative of a single concept: Knowledge. As Knowledge has no Purview (Him being an Order Primordial is a big stretch) he can't easily be made into a potential Divine Parent. If you really wanted him to be one, a Scion might inherit Sage from him, and maybe Order due to his Kingmaker role. Asides from that, he isn't representative of any other Purviews or Callings.

                                                    Which actually gets me thinking, now. Do we actually know much about Hittite(/Hurrian/Lwian etc.) religious practices in specific? You mention the tribunal-prayers and the personal care given to proxies, and that the royal family has religious functions around the empire as one of its primary responsibilities, but little in the specific of the unique points of worshiping Telipinu vs Kamrusepa, or how Inara worship differed from Kurunta worship, or what it really meant, religiously, when Istanu overshadowed Tarhun in the captial. Is this something that we have any real information on?
                                                    Sorry for taking a while, but I've been crazy tired yesterday. Also sorry because I won't be able to give a super-good answer, because you're asking for something that requires a lot of detail, and it's been several years now since I read those specific details. We do have a lot on Hittite religion, more than on their mythology, actually, because they were wonderfully bureaucratic and wrote everything down. A problem, however, is that what we know has undergone a process of random selection due to which sources have been preserved and in what form. As a non-religious example that I like a lot: We have lots of Hittite treaties with other powers - but only in copy. We possess only one of the metal tablet originals, and that one only because it was buried as a curse instead of being honourably put in a temple, where all of the others were stolen and melted down after the fall of the empire. Our situation with religion is similar, in that we have no religious images, very few temples with more than the foundations left, and that a lot of the ritual instructions that tell us how stuff was done allude to lost materials. Because being bureaucratic also means not writing everything down twice but saying "look over in folder F for this".

                                                    So, what do we know about Hittite ritual, especially in regards to differences between the specific deities? Basically, the Hittites have a lot in common with the Kami or Shen in that regard - both in that ritual orthodoxy is immensely important for your prayers to reach the deity in question, and in that all deities are essentially local. Most of the deities we present in our write-up are already amalgamations of various linked local deities, and as you can see from the lists of aliases, these connections and syncretisations span several cultures. So every one of these complex deities has numerous local manifestations with miniscule differences - for example, we gave Tarhun Fertility for his Luwian form's associations with viniculture, which his Hittite and Hurrian forms entirely lack. And every one of those 'Mantles' has its own local cult with its own cultic tradition which must be observed. It doesn't work if you try to change things! (Of course things changed anyway, but the change was slow and not very deliberate compared to what was going on with constructed uber-gods like Marduk and Amun in the surrounding nations). This means that a deity's Hattian form had to be prayed to in the Hattian language, otherwise they wouldn't listen, even if at home in Hattussa the same deity was being worshipped in Hittite. Then, over several great state festivals, the royal couple would tour the entire country, visit all the major cult centres of all the ethnicities and religions in their realm, and participate in all those orthodox and incredibly variant rituals. Like I said, I can't give you a lot of details off the top of my head, but e.g. in Telipinu's ritual, a tree was planted, Ishhara's temple had a ritual bed for her priestess to be ritually married on, etc.

                                                    Now, I think that covers your first question. As for the second one, the difference between Inara and Kurunta, that is more difficult. Inara's worship is mostly limited to the Old Kingdom of the Hittites, and becomes less and less prominent over time, whereas the male LAMMA-God (which you can associate with Kurunta). There's been a lot of debate in scholarly circles over whether they became actually conflated in Hittite times or not, and I can't give you a clear answer on that. If you want to look into the issue in more detail, though, there's a whole book on the LAMMA-Gods available online: https://oi.uchicago.edu/sites/oi.uch.../docs/as25.pdf

                                                    As for Ishtanu and Tarhun, it's like Watcher said, our ideas of that are heavily skewed by the religious policies of Tudhaliya IV, one of the last known kings of the Hittites, who was a huge fan of Hurrian culture, built a huge and beautiful temple to the Hurrian gods (Yazilikaya), and just generally left us a lot of sources - a lot of them, but obviously not the most representative ones. The primacy of Ishtanu is basically derived from the importance of that god's equivalents among the Hurrians and Luwians, and from the association of him with Arinniti towards the end of the Hittite New Kingdom. But we tried to go with the older tradition that still put the Hittites' own interpretations of their gods in a more central position, and that interpretation, I think, was also in vogue far longer than Tudhaliya's policy of promoting Hurrian ideas and worshipping the Sun God of the Sky (and even at Yazilikaya, the Storm God takes the most prominent position, so there's that ...)
