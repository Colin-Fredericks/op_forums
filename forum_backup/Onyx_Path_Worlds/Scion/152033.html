    The Spirit Nation

    05-28-2014, 11:10 PM
    Seeing the comments that a pure Native American pantheon would be good to represent America, I thought that it would be also a good idea. And then I remember the huge list of mythical creatures that start with A on that one website. After groaning, I thought about the stories that are based on Native American mythology. What I did remember was that the ones I recognized were about the Animal spirits. So I decided that having the stereotypical Great Spirit accompanied by the animal spirits of common lore. The reason I decided on calling the pantheon "The Spirit Nation" was that, given what I knew, the spirits of the country would aid and guide the tribes of America (or as they called it: Turtle Island). If there's something more accurate, please tell me, because I'm going off with what I know.

    The Gods: As I said before, I was thinking of the so-called Great Spirit being the all-father of this pantheon, with the animal spirits commonly told about in the lore as the other gods of the pantheon. As a picture, I could see them as basic Native American people with a part of their dress bearing a symbol of the animal spirit that they are representing

    Besides the Great Spirit, the Animal spirits that I know of commonly are thus:

    Coyote: This is a no-brainer because the deity was not only part of some trickster stories, but was also written up in the Scion:God fiction where he stole the Sacred Necklace from Yukiko during her search for the knob of Sun Wukong's staff. He was considered an alternative identity of Brer Rabbit, but I think in this case that Coyote being his own character would be good for this pantheon. Also of note, read Coyote Blues by Christopher Moore. In it, he places some tales of Coyote and emphasizes his nasty gambling habit, not to mention that the main story would be a good example of a Scion of Coyote. Also, if it's feasible, we can also have Rabbit in there as well, since he's mentioned in one of Coyote's stories (Apparently the animal spirits can change gender by removing the penis).

    Eagle and Raven: I heard about this story and it fits well into the global pantheon story of a solar blackout due to the sun missing. In this case, it was said that Eagle coveted the sun so he took it to his place and the world was cast into darkness. Raven, hearing about this, thought of stealing the sun for himself. Raven succeeded, but not without alerting Eagle into chase. During the chase, Raven dropped the Sun back into the sky and Day was restored. Something to think of.

    Other animals of note are Bear (thanks to the Brother Bear movies) and Wolf because he seems common enough.

    That brings to a total of seven gods total. Are there some Animal spirits that should be included to maximize the pantheon? Should the alligator be included to represent the swamp tribes like the Seminole and Okeechobee tribes?

    History: Since that it's fact now that Native Americans dealt with the Vikings beforehand and kicked them out. I was thinking that in the early days, after their Titan was sealed, the Spirit Nation had to deal with invasions from the Aesir in the East, The Chinese Bureaucracy in the West (I'm saying this because of the somehow dubious map that predated Columbus), and their bloodthirsty brothers, the Aztantl to the South, They were doing well until the titanspawn unleashed a plague that weakened the tribes, and the pantheon in kind. Because of this, they couldn't handle the resurgence of the Aesir and Aztlantl and the arrival of other pantheons as well, which ended in tenuous relations with the pantheons. When the Titans escaped, they teamed up to handle the return of their own Titan who renewed efforts to oppress their people.

    Relations with other Pantheons: Given the Native American relations with the Norse, I would think that the Spirit Nation would hate the pantheon for past transgressions. With the other pantheons, there would be an air of caution about them as akin to invading armies. The two pantheons that the Spirit Nation would deal with on a daily basis would be the Loa (some in the Nation would think that it was an attempt by the Orisha of Africa to claim a piece of their land as their own) and the Polynesian Pantheon (the pantheon which contains the Hawaiian Islands and the Phillipean Islands) since part of their area of influence (Hawaii) became part of their land.

    Pantheon Purview - I actually don't have an idea of how to make this. I don't know much about Native American terminology to get a name, much less to form the purview. Any help will be nice.

    Titan: Even though, the monsters in the Native American lexicon would make good antagonists or titanspawn, I don't know what to name the Titan and I'm confused as to what he/she is a Titan of. Again, any help would be nice.

    So this is the bare bones that I came up with about The Spirit Nation. Any help to flesh it out further and to clarify things would be great. If anything, I'm hoping that the suggestions will help Onyx Path to create this as a replacement to the Yankee Pantheon for 2E.

    So thanks for the help. Any suggestions?

              While your idea is one rooted in nothing but good intentions, I am sure, to address a large problem in Scion, it carries with it some rather large issues. Ranging from small, to incredibly offensive, though I do not believe you have any intent to do anything but try to solve this problem of representation

              The primary issue here is that you are attempting to combine an entire continents worth of peoples, with their own unique histories, mythologies, and ways of life, into a single group. That is incredibly offensive to these groups. This idea forces connections that do not exist, implies that all of the Natives of North America are effectively the same culturally, or if not that, religiously, and that is just not true in any way, shape, or form.

              To try to put it in a way you may better understand, this would be the same as saying the Tuatha de Dannan, and the Anunna of Mesopotamia are the same. Something which we know is entirely not true.

              Furthermore, you are playing into stereotypes of Native religion. Some, yes, are Animist, but some were not. Some had animal worship, but some too had Hero worship, or had entire Pantheons of very human figures. The entire root of this idea is one based on the Native American tribes being all the same, but that is a falsehood so colossal and great, I can't even begin to touch on how horribly wrong it is.

              You have Hunter Gather societies, Agrarian societies, Herders, Raiders, Urban, Nomadic, all of them different and unique from one and other. They may have once, in some long ancient past all come from an original root society that we have failed thus far to identify in any way shape or form, but even if they did and this progect attempted to play off this idea of the collective past of all of these hundreds, and hundreds of cultures, I would have to tell you that by the same logic, the Deva, the Yazata, the Tuatha, the Aesir, the Theoi, the Elohim, the Nemetondevos, and more are, by that logic, the same Pantheon for they descended from the same root culture.

              While, Jackalope, I believe that you had no intentions of this being anything but productive in attempting to solve this issue of a lack of representation, you have fallen into the same trap that the game's French developers, and many other members of the community have fallen into. This is the easy solution, but one that is fundamentally a bad one. It is an idea that has good merits for the desire to represent an entire continent of unrepresented peoples, but carries with it horrible implications of uniformity across numerous unique and wonderfully different cultures.

              If you wish to represent one of the Native American tribes through Scion, that would be wonderful. Maybe the Cree, Navajo, Iroquois, or any one of the other cultures. But, pushing an entire continents worth of peoples, making grand sweeping statements about their cultures, destroying any individuality, or uniqueness, that is not a good way to go about this.

              Now, of course, I can't magically stop you, or any others from working on this project, but, this is just my advice. If you would like to work on one of the individual Pantheons of one of these individual cultures, I am sure there would be many, many people on the forums who would be happy to brainstorm with you, and help you. But, this is one of those roads to hell, paved with good intentions.

                          There is a slight difference between the two though...that being that the Chinese Gods are mythologically part of the same Pantheon. For eg, Guan Yin is, in all probability, an import of the Hindu Avalokiteshwara mixed in with the Tibeto-Indian Tara. But she's worshipped by the Chinese people as a Chinese God, and has myths where she interacts with chinese figures like Sun Wukong, the Dragon Kings and the Jade Emperor.

                          On the other hand, no Haida ever went to the Eskimo and said 'Hey, Sedna's really cool, I'll borrow her for my religion', and no Hopi ever went, 'Hey guys, White Buffalo Woman is totally something we're worshipping now.' The peoples of Native America are unique cultures and their Gods have unique mythologies that simply do not interact the way Chinese mythology does, and they should be treated as such.

                          And before somebody asks, no Coyote is not a thing...there are a dozen different Coyote Gods across the continent each his own unique deity, as much as Zeus and Indra are unique.
