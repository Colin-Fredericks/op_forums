    Birthrights

    Relics

    The Fishhook - The fishhook that caused the quarrel between Ho-ori and Hoderi after the former borrowed it from the later and lost it. The fishhook is, however, not a particularly great fishhook any more, as the entire population of the ocean knows of it after Ryujin ordered them to find it. However, due to this, whomsoever possesses the Fishhook can extract a degree of respect and assistance from Ryujin’s servants, the fish.

    The Fishhook would be an appropriate gift for a Heroic Scion, possibly from Ryujin himself, or from Amaterasu, as the fishhook was owned by one of her descendents.

    Divine Birthmark - Like Hachiman, the Scion was born with an exceptionally notable birthmark somewhere on their body. One so perfectly positioned and detailed it can easily be mistaken for a tattoo. Auspicious birthmarks were often given importance in Japan, and they can provide a wide range of benefits related to what it depicts. Hachiman’s Tomoe birthmark for example likely helps the Deity wield War as the symbol is often depicted on their armguards.

    A Divine Birthmark would be appropriate for a Heroic Scion, and would be exceptionally difficult to introduce after initial Character generation unless something strange happens with a second birth or the like.

    Sacred Jewels - A common theme across gifts loaned out, or objects carried, and often an important part of Kami and Japanese Buddhist iconography. These Jewels carry within themselves powerful magical powers from simple to extravagant. For the Kami, some examples would be the Jewel of Resurrecting the Dead, the Life Inspiring Jewel, or the Jewel of Perfect Health and Strength. For the Buddhist Deities, these Jewels tend to be related to the Three Jewels of Buddhism (the Buddha himself, Dharma [law], and Sangha [community and clergy]) but they do not necessarily directly relate. Some of these Buddhist Jewels can perform such acts as purifying unclean water, warding off bad luck, or even granting wishes. The largest difference between the two religion’s Sacred Jewels are their shapes. Shinto jewels are often shaped like commas (Magatama), while Buddhist ones are often onion or ball shaped.

    One of these Sacred Jewels greatly depends on what it is capable of doing to determine what tier of play they are appropriate for. Generally, these are Relics with a single powerful Boon able to be channeled through it, meaning that a Rank 1-5 Boon would be appropriate for a Hero, Rank 6-8 for a Demigod, or Rank 9-10 for a God.

    Tide Pearl - The often loaned out by Ryujin, each tide pearl is an exceptionally powerful tool. Different pearls control different aspects of the sea, one for rising the sea, one for lowering it, for example. No matter which specific pearl a Scion wields, their mastery over the Water Purview in that specific area is heightened and using the Purview becomes far easier, while using the Purview to perform other unrelated deeds becomes more difficult.

    Though the Tide Pearls are loaned out frequently, Ryujin would likely only make a permanent gift of one of his pearls to an exceptionally powerful Scion, such as an in-law, or a child of their own upon their Apotheosis.
    Guides

    Tenjin - The God of Wisdom, and the Kami worshiped with that special brand of desperation and anxiety that students produce, Tenjin can mentor a Scion of the Kami, especially if they are a descendent of one of the high ranking Kami. In addition to academic studies, Tenjin can also provide lessons on how to act ‘properly’ and function within the delicate court life of the Kami, which can prove very difficult for modern Scions.

    Tenjin would be an appropriate Guide for Heroic and Demigod Scions, with the God instructing the Scion through one of his priests.

    Dragon Princess - Ryujin is exceptionally skilled at marrying his plethora of daughters off to important, influential, or interesting beings. A marriage between the Scion and one of the Dragon Princesses may have been organised before the Scion was even visited. A Dragon Princess could instruct their spouse in the use of the sea-controlling pearls of her Father, and could have great insights, many of them being exceptionally old beings.

    A marriage to a Dragon Princess would make an appropriate Guide for a Heroic Scion of a particularly important Divine Parent, or a Demigod who has proven themselves, or any influential Scion that happens to visit Ryujin’s palace.

    Fudo - The Japanese Buddhist figure could prove to be an exceptional mentor for a Scion of the Buddhist Japanese Deities. The figure of ultimate endurance, if tasked with mentoring a Scion, would likely spring upon them complex and brutal tests of their stamina, such as sitting under waterfalls, which could lead to a Scion becoming exceptionally tough, or possibly exceptionally good at talking one’s way out of the demands of a Divinity.

    Fudo would be an appropriate Guide for a Hero Scion of one of the Japanese Buddhist figures, or possibly one of the Kami with Buddhist influences, such as Hachiman.

    Amaterasu - The Empress of Heaven has long since had an empty position in her personal service since one of her handmaidens accidentally killed themselves with their shuttle when Susano-O threw a flayed pony before them. If a particularly noble or notable Scion achieved Apotheosis, Amaterasu could decide they were suitable to fill this missing rank. Here, the new Deity would be privy to a plethora of state secrets and hold an exceptionally privileged rank in court.

    Becoming a handmaiden of Amaterasu would be only appropriate for a female Scion who has achieved Apotheosis and has proven themselves to be a reliable and noble figure.
    Retainers

    Weapon Kami - A potent gift for any Scion would be an ancient Weapon Kami. Sword, Bow, or Spear, the weapon given to the Scion is more than what many Pantheons give their children. Due to being powerful Kami, one of these weapons can give advice, stand guard, move on their own, and even enhance their wielder's skill in battle.

    A Weapon Kami would be an appropriate Retainer given to a Demigod of the Pantheon. A Weapon Kami could also be a viable Relic or Guide for a Scion, possibly all three at the same time.

    White Fox - Inari gave one of their blessed White Foxes as a companion when they visited the Scion, tasking the Fox to watch over them and bring any message from the Scion directly to themselves. A noble and upstanding creature, unlike natural foxes, White Foxes are not particularly skilled in combat, but are an expert at moving undetected and sniffing out important clues.

    One of these White Foxes would be an appropriate Birthright for a Hero Scion of the Pantheon, likely one of Inari’s own Scions, or any Kami that Inari has a positive relationship with.

    Mischievous Fox - As Inari rules over both their own noble foxes, and the ignoble foxes of the land of plentiful reeds, sometimes Inari are needed to judge over their mischievous subjects. Something like probation, a Scion may be assigned one of these mischievous foxes to attempt to mentor them in good behavior. These foxes are exceptionally skilled in trickery and cunning, and the Scion may be held responsible for some of their ill deeds. In fact, they may even try to seduce their mentor Scion for noble or ignoble reasons.

    One of these Mischievous Foxes would be an appropriate Birthright for a Hero Scion, with the only caveat that Inari would need to believe them to be an upstanding and noble enough person to teach the misguided animal.

    Komainu - A creature ‘imported’ to Japan, learned Scions would point out that Komainu are exceptionally similar to the Chinese Foo Dogs, even though their name suggests they came from Korea. These large intimidating guardian creatures are likely to be assigned to watch over frail Scions of the Kami. While they may be frightening in visage, a Komainu will be a stalwart and gentle defender of even the most foolhardy Japanese Scions.

    A Komainu would be an appropriate gift to a Heroic Scion of one of the Kami. The Birthright can be improved by increasing the number of Komainu protecting the Scion, or having the original Komainu blessed by Hachiman to improve the grand beast’s protective capacity.

    Oni - These large rambunctious Ogre-like entities can be ‘converted’ into more noble entities through magic or mentoring. A particularly notable upstanding Scion may have an Oni dropped on their lap to tutor in proper behavior, which while possibly comedically difficult, can leave the Scion with a powerful friend. Possibly the trickiest situation that may arise with having an Oni as a Retainer would be their habit to, even when reformed, struggle against a desire to dine on the flesh of humans.

    An Oni would be an appropriate Birthright for a Scion who has somehow proven their capacity to mentor other beings in proper behavior, such as one who has taught a Band of barbaric Scions a degree of somewhat respectable behavior. If one were to reach Apotheosis and wish for their Oni friend to be equally empowered, it would have interesting political consequences. The Pantheon has had difficulty with powerful Oni, and they may need convincing before introducing another one to the Divine ecosystem. But that kind of story is half the fun.

        Cosmology

        Underworld - Yomi
        The most infamous of the Shinto afterlives, Yomi was the first Underworld of the Pantheon in the primordial era of creation. The realm is a dark and dismal one, ruled over by a mysterious group of Kami who go undetailed in lineage or even name. It is this realm that Izanagi entered to find his wife Izanami after her death, wishing to return her to the land of the living. In the darkness, Izanagi was unable to see, and Izanami asked him not to look upon her visage as she went to ask the ruling Kami of Yomi if she could leave. Failing this task, too tempted to look upon his wife, Izanagi found her to be rotting and filled with worms.

        Fleeing from Yomi, chased by his wife’s new servants, Izanagi took up a colossal boulder and slammed it over the entranceway of Yomi, forever locking his wife away within the darkness of the subterranean realm. Because this realm was sealed, and evidenced by the fact a plethora of other afterlives spring up after this event, Yomi fell into disuse as an Underworld and the Shinto dead travel to different Underworlds. Almost no souls rest in Yomi, with Izanami, the Yomotsu-no-Kami, and their servants being the only residents.

        Reaching Yomi is a curious feat for many reasons. The boulder that Izanagi sealed the entrance with is visible, one can go visit it in the Izumo province (modernly the Shimane Prefecture), and quite smaller than one would imagine it after reading the description of the boulder. To mortals, the boulder is large, but nothing exceptional for a boulder. However, the boulder is subjective, and the higher Legend a being is, the more massive and heavy it becomes. To one of the greatest Kami, the boulder dwarfs even the colossal one Amaterasu used to seal herself in her cave. To a Titan such as Izanami, it must be so large it reaches into the heavens.

        However, this subjective size makes Yomi an interesting realm because the more mortal one is, the easier it is to enter the realm while living. For a mortal, all it takes is shifting the boulder slightly and slipping down into the passage, a relatively simple feat when armed with chains and a truck. Though since this is a historic site, you would have to deal with mortal authorities. To a Hero or Demigod, the difficulty of shifting the boulder is equal to their Legend squared (equal to their total Legend pool) and must be achieved in a single Strength + Athletics roll. For a God, it would take Ultimate Strength or a creative use of another Ultimate.

        Yomi is so dark that without a light source, it is impossible to see, which is likely a blessing rather than a curse. Epic Perception still functions with every other sense, and is still valuable for finding one’s way around, but no amount of successes on sight can see further than a light source.

        The Home of the Yomotsu-no-Kami
        In the utter stygian darkness of Yomi, one is likely to end up tripping over the paved path that leads from the entrance of the Underworld, below the great boulder. The path is long and winding through the darkness, the same path that Izanagi fled from his sister all those years ago. At the end of this path is a building of some sort, made of wood as far as one can tell by touch and sound of the timbers underfoot.

        It is here that the mysterious Yomotsu-no-Kami reside, but just who or what they are is totally unknown. All that is known is that they hold the capacity to arbitrate if a dead entity can exit Yomi as shown by Izanami requiring to ask their permission. This is a gift that is almost never given, with the only being known directly returning from an outright and violent death being through the interjection of one of the first three Kami in existence, Kamimusuhi. Whatever Scion is brave enough to plumb the depths of Yomi will find these beings the ultimate rulers of the realm, with even Izanami herself under their authority.

        There is little else to be gained in this dark realm. Perhaps a questing Scion seeks some ancient piece of wisdom known only by Izanami or the Yomotsu-no-Kami, or they are seeking to make an army out of the countless servants that lurk within Yomi. In either case, corruption is assured.

        Izanami’s Home
        In the darkness of Yomi, Izanami made herself a place to live. However, it is not as grand as the first house she constructed with Izanagi when they arrived on earth. It is impossible to tell due to the darkness, but Izanami knows it is just slightly not as grand, and that infuriates her.

        The house is massive and sprawling, in fact, none of the Shinto Gods are quite sure how large it is since no one goes to check. Here, Izanami lives with her Thunder Kami servants, and the Yomotsushikome, fearsome underworld hags. Any Shinto Scion who dares to walk here is brave or foolhardy, since Izanami would love nothing more than to strike down any descendant of her faithless husband. She may be hesitant and willing to listen to Scions of Tenjin or Inari, or the exceptionally rare Scion that she has adopted as her own. While she has no reason to hate Scions of the Japanese Buddhist Deities, Izanami may treat them disparagingly just for being foreign.

            Underworld - Ne-no-Kuni
            When he was banished from heaven by Izanagi, Susano-O set off to the land of plentiful reeds in what would later be known as Izumo, with two wives in a home he constructed beside a river. We do not know what happened to this house, but he was later found in a different home beneath the roots of the earth in Izumo, named Ne-no-Kuno, which is where his son Okuninushi came to find him. Just what differentiates this realm from Yomi is not clear at a glance, but the Underworlds have two key different features. Firstly, Ne-no-Kuni is not gripped in the same stygian darkness, and secondly entering and leaving Ne-no-Kuni is simple, with no colossal boulder blocking the passage.

            It is here that Susano-O lives and rules over some of the Shinto dead, those that do not travel to Tokoyo across the sea. Just how the dead are prescribed to going to Ne-no-Kuni is not clear, but in this realm under the roots of Izumo Susano-O rules.

            Reaching Ne-no-Kuni is not exceptionally difficult for a Kami. In the province of Izumo, if one wanders into wooded areas, a Scion can quickly find themselves nearing the edge of Ne-no-Kuni, and slowly walking down into the earth below down a long wooded slope. The moment a Scion walks under the lip of the world high above, they are in Ne-no-Kuni.

            The Woods
            Walking down into Ne-no-Kuni, one will walk through deep woods abundant beneath the earth. Slightly withered and skeletal, these trees are not quite dead, but it is obvious they are somewhat struggling to grow here. Brushing against any of these trees, even slight accidental taps, will be immediately heard by Susano-O, even if he sleeps at the time. Navigating through these woods without touching any of these trees requires three Successful Dexterity or Wits + Athletics or Stealth rolls needing to meet or surpass 20 Successes, as the trees grow so close together and the ground is filled with roots that can trip unaware Scions up.

            This forest also has another danger, with some of the entirely dead trees hollowed out and turned into massive wasp hives, the same as those that Okuninushi was tested against in Susano-O’s home. These wasps are mean and unfriendly creatures that will try to swarm and attack any who get too close to their large hives. Navigating around these wasp hives makes the journey last twice as long and adds an additional three rolls to navigating the woods without making Susano-O aware. If Scions don’t want to risk this added time, they may attempt to bypass a hive by making a Dexterity or Intelligence + Animal Ken or Survival roll Succeeding at 20 Successes. Failing this roll leaves the Scions swarmed by these wasps, which are the following hazard.

            The Wasps of Ne-no-Kuni
            D20 15L 5P N The wasps of Ne-no-Kuni leave beings stung and swollen for ages. The penalty remains until the being leaves Ne-no-Kuni.
            These Woods also link to The Dark Forest, meaning if someone gets lost in Ne-no-Kuni’s woods they will find themselves in The Dark Forest, and could find themselves wandering into any of the other locations it links to, or the things and hidden places in The Dark Forest. On a slight positive note, you are likely to find other lost Shinto beings nearby in the forest, though if you wander far enough you may begin stumbling across Mesopotamian cedar forests and even more distant places.

            The Marsh
            Past the forest of Ne-no-Kuni is a sprawling expanse of marsh. Reeds as tall as an Oni fill the marsh, with areas of almost bottomless murky muddy depths hidden everywhere. There is a path through the mud and grime made from wooden planks built upon logs driven deep into the ground, but finding it can prove difficult, requiring a Perception + Investigation roll of 25 to discover. Otherwise, beings have to navigate their way through the disgusting corrupted waters filled with snakes both struggling to move through the mud, but forcing their way past their sharp and venomous fangs. Moving through the marsh of Ne-no-Kuni is represented in the following Hazard. For a Kami, wading through these corrupt waters is a violation of Harmony.

            The Marsh and Serpents of Ne-no-Kuni
            D25 5L 5P Y For beings that do not possess the Kami PsP, the difficulty of this Hazard is reduced by 10 as the overwhelming corruption and disgusting nature of the swamp doesn’t even come to mind.
            Susano-O’s House
            At the center of the Marsh is the house of Susano-O, or, technically, the second house of Susano-O, or third if you count the house he built somewhere on the surface of Izumo. Either way, the Uncouth Kami’s residence has long since been rebuilt since he destroyed it when Okuninushi trapped him with his own hair. Though, to rather low standards.

            The house is rotting and fetid, just barely managing to hold itself together. Or, possibly, it is exceptionally cunningly constructed to only appear as if it was a rotting hulk. The house is sprawling, and could be called a palace if it were not in the strange somewhat state of decay. Here is where Susano-O lives with his daughters and wives, and provided the visitor is not one of his own children announcing that they just married their sister on his front step, he might even be quite amicable as long as one wasn’t bothered by his uncouth and inappropriate, by Kami standards, nature. Visitors will be welcomed with feasts by Susano-O, and his wives will wait on visitors hand and foot, while the God inquires as to why someone would come to his realm.

            If a Scion wants something from Susano-O, they will likely be thrown through a similar series of potentially lethal tests by Susano-O like Okuninushi was in the past. Including having to spend time in rooms of Susnao-O’s house that are infested with the snakes of the marsh, the wasps of the forest, and centipedes that dwell here. They may also have to deal with his emotional outbursts, not intentionally harmful but outrageous and dangerous by coincidence.

            Attempting to move through Susano-O’s house without a guide also risks that a Scion will stumble across a room filled with these creatures, a roll of 20 Successes on a Perception or Wits + Animal Ken allows a Scion to notice that a room is infested before entering. For the particularly unlucky, sometimes these rooms have multiple groups of these pests present.

            Centipede Rooms
            D35 15A - N The centipedes of Ne-no-Kuni are especially vicious, but also carry with them the taint of the Underworld, causing wounds left by them to seep with blood for days. At the start of every Scene, as long as a Scion possesses the Kami PsP, they suffer a penalty to all actions equal to their Legend due to this corruption.

                Underworld - Jigoku
                Where Shintoism leaves the dead briefly mentioned, and their afterlives vague, the Buddhists of Japan did anything but. Jigoku, the Buddhist Hell of Japan, is one of the six realms of existence one may be reincarnated into after death. Unlike the other Japanese Underworlds, this is a place of punishment and pain.

                All souls that are neither saved by a Bodhisattva nor reach enlightenment are destined to Jigoku where they are to be judged and ordered to one of the six realms of existence. Though similar to the Chinese Buddhist Diyu, Jigoku is less structured, but the Buddhist Deities refer to the locations as being the same.

                The realm is ruled over by Enma-O, the Judge of Hell -- a large, violent, and frightening figure who resembles a fierce Oni. In Jigoku, there are no other Judges or being to question his rule, though Bodhisattvas can technically overrule his authority if they take an interest in an unfortunate soul. Some suggest that Enma-O is a form taken by Jizo, which would suggest that some tales involve Jizo talking to himself and convincing himself of things, but the truth of this is unknown.

                Reaching Jigoku is not a comfortable experience. The most common entrance is traveling to Mount Osore, the Mountain of Fear, a large dormant volcano in the north of Japan’s primary island of Honshu. The entire area stinks of sulphur, and smoke drifts through the air, almost concealing the many hot springs that cover the volcanic slopes. These hot springs are the method the still living can use to gain entrance into Jigoku, if they can just find a spring that has a small crevice by the bottom they can worm through (a Perception + Investigation roll aiming for 20 Successes). These hot springs feed into Sanzu, the Underworld river, and those who swim through the underwater passage from Mount Osore can find themselves surfacing in the Underworld, in Sanzu.
                The dead do not have to go spelunking to reach Jigoku. Though the living are unlikely to ever see it on earth, those in Jigoku can witness a burning chariot pulled by a bull-headed servant of Enma-O bring the dead from above and leave them upon the endless shore of Sai-no-Kawara. A Scion who is in good standing with Enma-O, or manages to trick the servant, might be able to ride back to the world upon the empty chariot during one of its return trips.

                Sai-no-Kawara, the Endless Shore
                The endless shore known as Sai-no-Kawara lies upon the far side of a great Triple Ford called Sanzu, though this shore is made of small pebbles rather than sand. Here countless children languish, trying fruitlessly to stack enough pebbles onto each other to form a tower. These are the Mizuko, children who died at birth, shortly afterwards, or were aborted. Having had no time to commit either good or bad deeds (though some count causing their parents grief through their death as a sin), they are caught in this limbo until they manage to complete a tower of pebbles - that is, forever, since these towers have no balance whatsoever. Their only hope is the Bodhisattva Jizo, who is regularly moved by grieving parents' prayers to descend to Sai-no-Kawara and lead the children caught there to rebirth.

                The dead children here will be as curious as living children to see Scions emerge from the river, especially if the Scion is sporting wings, antlers, or are otherwise fantastically inhuman, though some of the children are too young to do much more than stare. If a Scion is looking for Jizo, this is the best place to find him, as the Bodhisattva will inevitably arrive to gather some children to lead them away.

                Sanzu, the Triple Ford
                The dead are left upon Sai-no-Kawara and must cross over this river, where they are met by a demonic hag named Datsueba -- a horrifying naked hag that sits beneath a large tree, awaiting the dead who must give her their soaking clothes. Datsueba then reaches up and hangs them in her tree. Depending on how far down the tree’s branches droop, the dead being is forewarned about the severity of the punishments they will endure.

                The living who emerge from Sanzu and swim for the Jigoku side of the river will almost inevitably arrive by Datsueba and her tree, who will be very interested indeed to see living people in Jigoku who are not Bodhisattvas. The monstrous hag will offer to dry the clothes of these living visitors, and if done, a Scion can see how their deeds are measured by the Buddhists. While this is a good opportunity to see the weight of their sins, the living are safe from judgement for now.

                The Tribunal of Many Layered Towers
                After meeting Datsueba at her tree, the dead continue along a path lined with flames and looming trees as they approach the Tribunal of Enma-O. A great flaming iron gate stands before the Tribunal, manned by two large wardens of hell who resemble horrible Oni that bellow and laugh at the approaching dead. They will also open the gates for the living, but insist they go immediately to Enma-O. The grand tribunal stands tall beyond the burning gate, with a plethora of towers jutting from it, and even more towers sprouting from those to reach high into the air. The Tribunal grounds are guarded by even more Oni, wearing loincloths and brandishing massive weapons, who guard the dead as they move along the path and into the Tribunal itself.

                The Tribunal is constructed of purest gleaming gold and inlaid with seven bus-sized jewels that are grandly displayed on the more prominent towers, and above the main entrance of the building. Within the Tribunal, the dead find some relief from the terrible heat of the realm, and discover that they are within a massive room with a vaulted ceiling. A raised platform dominates one side, where Enma-O and his assistants sit upon the far end. Braziers of burning coals illuminate the area, and more Oni stand watchful of the dead, ensuring order is kept.

                Enma-O himself is a conflicting sight. He sits cross legged upon the raised platform and wears the grand and beautiful robes of a judge, while surrounded by several support staff who take notes and serve other bureaucratic duties. However, Enma-O is also a massive, monstrous red Oni-like being with a large beard, piercing eyes, who is fond of shouting at or generally abusing anyone in his immediate vicinity.

                The Judge of Hell carries a staff which is topped by sometimes one, and sometimes two human heads that whisper all sins that a dead person has done. While being judged, the dead person standing before Enma-O is tortured by two more Wardens of Hell just to make sure there is nothing to Enma-O. During this, the dead person being judged is tortured by two more Wardens of Hell, just to make sure that there are no hidden sins that his all-knowing staff has failed to mention. Upon hearing the deeds of a dead individual, the Judge decides what fate awaits them, be it a swift return to the world, or laborious torture. To his credit, Enma-O is a fair judge, and has unequalled knowledge of the laws and taboos he is investigating.

                The living who wish to speak to Enma-O must wait until he has finished judging the current soul, and then the wardens would allow them to approach past the line of dead. Approaching Enma-O while still alive will make the Judge immediately curious, and he will demand to know who they are and why they have arrived. He will usually wait patiently and listen to their story before commenting, and during this time his staff may begin to whisper details of the living person’s life thus far. Enma-O would always prefer that the living were not in his afterlife unless they are a Bodhisattva or one of his servants, but he will generally be rude but cooperative unless they mention wanting to gain access to a soul being kept in his Underworld. Should this happen, he will become paranoid and have the living searched for contraband Buddhist literature (see below) by the Oni.

                If the living has a good explanation, such as working for a Bodhisattva, or trying to accomplish something the cruel Judge finds interesting, he will have a number of his Wardens escort the living to wherever they need to go. However, these guides are more guard than guide, making sure the living are not going to run off, or sneak banned literature to a tortured soul.

                The most obvious reason that a Scion would enter Jigoku is to save someone that is being punished. Enma-O is used to having Jizo appear and argue in defence of a dead being, but he has no reason to be lenient with Scions. Actually convincing Enma-O to end the punishment of a soul and release them is a divine feat reserved for Ultimates. However, a Scion may always offer to endure the tortures instead of a punished being, which the Judge of Hell considers to be compassionate and admirable. Once he has agreed to this exchange, Wardens of Hell will lead a Scion through Jigoku to wherever the punished soul is being tortured, and have their tortures exchanged.

                There is another way to free the dead. If one can manage to bring a collection of Buddhist scripture to the dead the Scion wishes to save, the dead being is able to use these to free themselves. Bodhisattvas sometimes do this without the Judge of Hell being able to (or wanting to) stop them, because they are considered to be enlightened higher beings.                 • Chi-no-Ike Jigoku: A lake of blood in which women are forced to sit in punishment for... well, a lot of things. If Kannon is to be found anywhere, this is the location that they appear with a relative degree of consistency, saving women from this punishment by passing out Buddhist scriptures.

                    Overworld - Takamagahara
                    Takamagahara is the High Plain of Heaven that lies far above the realm of plentiful reeds, and is the abode of the Amatsukami. Much like a capital city, this realm is the center of Amaterasu's rule over the other Kami of the world, and serves as a central location for matters of state.

                    Takamagahara is quite literally above the earth, separated from it by Ama-no-Kawa, the Heavenly River, or the Milky Way. Because the Divine supersedes the observations of mortals, the night sky is quite literally a river of stars just above the Earth’s sky -- not quite the grand distance that a mortal scientist would suggest to reach the Milky Way.

                    The Overworld is a colossal plain edged with mountain ranges. Inside this plain are all of the residences of the Kami, some simple, some palaces. Miles upon miles of rice paddies and dykes stretch through the overworld, supplying the Amatsukami with food. Scions might note that the grand city is much like ancient Kyoto in layout, or at least, how it was planned to be lain out.
                    Spanning Ama-no-Kawa, connecting Takamagahara and the realm of plentiful reeds, is the Heavenly Floating Bridge, upon which Izanami and Izanagi stood when stirring the waters below with their spear.

                    Axis Mundi: Reaching Takamagahara is not too difficult, in fact there are two different ways. Those that can fly like the dragons may just ascend in Japan, racing skywards and swimming through Ama-no-Kawa, until they arrive on the Heavenly Floating Bridge and make their way into the Overworld.

                    Those who can’t fly must take the path that the Kami themselves took when Ninigi descended to the world to found the Imperial Line. One must walk to the peak of Mount Kirishima, a volcano, called Takachiho. Here, to the eyes of Scions and other Legendary beings, a path is visible that stretches up into the heavens.

                    This is the Heavenly Crossroads, and it is a long march. Scions may find the path somewhat surreal as they begin this march by walking up into the sky, yet somehow also traveling up a wooded path. They must navigate their way through the paths to find the Heavenly Floating Bridge. After one has been shown the way, this is no difficult task, but the unwise may wander along a different path and find themselves somewhere else entirely. Likely in one of the Pantheon’s Terra Incognita.

                    Following the primary path, travelers will eventually reach Ama-no-Kawa, the Heavenly River formed from the milky way, and the great bridge which spans it. Across this bridge is a paved road which leads into Takamagahara proper.

                    Hi-no-Wakamiya
                    As many know, the Ise Grand Shrine is modeled off Hi-no-Wakamiya, Amaterasu's palace in Takamagahara. However, like all attempts for mortals to emulate the Divine, their greatest efforts never quite manage to capture the wonders of the real thing. Hi-no-Wakamiya is a colossal complex situated on the northern side of the grand heavenly plain, the farthest point from the entrance to the Overworld. Behind it, tall mountains loom over the beautiful palace complex.

                    The complex itself is several times larger than the mortal reflection, though the general layout of the palace is similar enough that if one memorised one they could easily traverse the other. Ama-no-Kawa’s diverted flow surrounds the palace complex with a grand gilded bridge leading over to the palace-island itself. Predominantly wooded, the paths through the complex lead to several important features. The one most Scions will know about is the weaving hall of Amaterasu, the hole Susano-O tore in the roof long since patched. In this building visitors can find several large looms used by the Empress and her handmaidens. Unlike in the mortal world where the nobility would generate additional revenue by the women weaving silk cloth, such actions are obviously below Amaterasu. The Goddess creates beautiful and sacred clothing here, awarded to favored courtiers or Scions of the Pantheon.

                    A rather interesting feature of Hi-no-Wakamiya is the home of Izanagi, the father of Amaterasu. It is here that the elder Kami withdrew after the early events of the Kojiki, and the banishment of Susano-O. The Kami’s residence is ornate, for it is part of the grand imperial palace, but Izanagi rarely leaves it now, and almost nobody enters. The primordial father of most of the Pantheon tends to only emerge during meetings of the Pantheon, but other then that his face is rarely seen in Takamagahara. If a Scion were to desire to meet with Izanagi, it could surely be arranged, and it is here that they would meet.

                    The actual residence of Amaterasu looms over the entire imperial complex, standing taller than the many trees of the isle themselves. The residence of the Goddess is clearly designed to show the imposing power of Amaterasu to any visiting Kunitsukami, Marebito, or foreign Deity who has come to seek an audience with the Goddess. The Palace is extravagantly decorated in golds and beautiful artistic designs along the ceiling. A plethora of rooms for various purposes exist, be they visiting halls, kitchens, dining areas, and the like. A visitor to the residence would be lead specifically to the throne room of the Goddess, and be discouraged from wandering with impunity.

                    The Castle of Silent Illumination
                    One of the more fascinating locals in Takamagahara is the Castle of Silent Illumination, the residence of Hachiman. This tall, strong fortification is a metaphor, which might make some Scion’s heads hurt. It exists, that is for certain, but just how physical the fortress is can be up to debate. It is actually the tallest building in the Overworld, mostly due to castles being the only buildings that had sufficient foundation to allow multi-leveled construction in Japan. The Castle is west of Hi-no-Wakamiya, and commands a perfect sightline to where the Heavenly Floating Bridge touches Takamagahara, allowing the occupants to keep watch over those that enter the Godrealm.

                    The fact that the fortress is a metaphor doesn’t seem to have made it any less secure. In fact, the fortress being a metaphor has done quite the opposite, and has made it almost impenetrable. As a metaphor for the enlightenment that Hachiman achieved, attempting to breach the complex is almost impossible for those that have not also achieved the same enlightenment as the Kami, though it may be possible to subvert the metaphor in some way.

                    Doves, servants of Hachiman, sit on the eves and the roofs of the castle, often being sent down to the land of plentiful reeds by the God to show signs of his will. Inside the fortress itself, one can often find Hachiman in residence almost entirely alone. As Hachiman is no warrior, but the patron of warriors, the Castle of Silent Illumination is physically unmanned asides from Hachiman who is oft in contemplation rather than actually ‘on watch’ over the city. However, due to the complex nature of the fortification, only a fool would take this to mean that the Castle was not capable of defending the city of the Amatsukami with the competence only capable by the Divine.

                    Jingu and Hachiman’s wife, an unnamed Dragon Princess, also reside within the palace, bringing the residence up to three. The chambers of the late Empress and the Princess are a separation from the simple design of the Castle in general, and are appropriately luxurious for women of their station.

                    The Home of Inari
                    Away from the city of the Amatsukami, and settled in the foothills of one of the grand mountains that surround the Godrealm, is the oft-disused home of Inari. The winding path leading up to the home of the Deity is almost totally covered by red torii gates just like the shrine at Fushimi-ku. Looking out over the colossal rice fields that feed the Amatsukami, Inari’s home itself is almost always abandoned save for some of the Deity’s special foxes. These foxes are pure white, with many tails, and often carry sacred objects to bring to worshipers. Jewels especially are carried by the foxes, or sometimes inlaid on the tips of their tails.

                    On the rare occasions that Inari are in residence, the Deity is mostly holding judgements over foxes. Not their own, you see, as they are noble and upstanding creatures. The Deity judges mortal foxes who have wronged a mortal, who has asked Inari to punish the fox for its crimes.

                    Ame-no-Iwato
                    Not that Ame-no-Iwato, the cave that Amaterasu hid in, but another cave with the same name. Out in the mountains of the Godrealm, the cave is by the source of the Heavenly River, which flows directly past the entrance of the cave making entering it quite difficult. It is here that the God Takemikazuchi resides, along with several Sword and Thunder Kami, including Izanagi’s grand sword, which diverts the river at its source.

                    Reaching the cave is so difficult that when the Kami wished to summon Takemikazuchi, they sent a deer rather than personally attempt to make their way up the mountains and past the river. If a Scion wishes to reach the cave, they would need to roll Dexterity + Athletics and get 40 Successes, but if they would rather not, one of the deer in the Godrealm could effortlessly make the journey and fetch the god. However, most Scions would be dramatically out of line attempting to call forth Takemikazuchi unless they have been told to by another grand Kami.

                    Tenjin’s Residence
                    An echo of Tenjin’s shrine in Dazaifu, only grander and larger as all parts of the Overworld are. The home of this Kami is situated close to the Imperial complex of Amaterasu due to his previous rank as Minister of the Right. More affluent and grand than most of the other complexes in the city, Tenjin’s residence is filled with beautiful plum trees which the God still watches and writes poetry about.

                    An important Kami may arrange for one of their Scions to travel to Tenjin’s residence if they are in need of instruction. A proud and stalwart Kami, Tenjin may be able to provide beneficial tutelage and mentorship to a Scion in his home, however one must be sure never to disappoint or disrespect Tenjin lest he fly into a lightning spewing rage.

                    Those Not Present
                    As one might notice, despite being the residence of a large number of the eight million Kami, Takamagahara does not serve as a home for many of the most powerful Deities within the Japanese Pantheon. Kannon and Jizo both being Buddhist Deities do not reside with the Amatsukami. Kannon resides in the Pure Land, while Jizo spends much of his time wandering the realm of plentiful reeds, and working in the Buddhist Hell, Jigoku. Ryujin resides in his palace in Tokoyo, flying up to Takamagahara when summoned, and Okuninushi dwells within his special palace in the realm of plentiful reeds.

                    The Eightfold Crossroad of Heaven
                    Part of the Axis Mundi of Takamagahara, the Eightfold Crossroad of Heaven is somewhat more versatile than many other Axis Mundis. The giant crossroad on the way to the Heavenly Bridge leads to many places other than Mount Kirishima, as long as the traveller knows the way. The roads lead to every major and minor sacred site of Shinto inside the borders of Japan, and one is capable of ascending from those locations as well.

                    Once a being has traveled a specific path, they need not worry about finding their way again, however the first time they ascend to Heaven, or make their way down to the Realm of Plentiful Reeds from a particular path, they must roll Intelligence or Wits + Survival and score 20 Successes to navigate their way through the countless paths. If the traveller fails, they arrive at a random location mentioned above.

                    Sarutahiko can often be found at the Eightfold Crossroad itself. The frighteningly visaged Kami has been convinced before to assist others in finding their way along the divine roadway, and would likely be present to help guide a new Kami on their first ascent to the Overworld if they can amuse him. The Kami is likely to also assist any descendent of either Amaterasu or Okuninushi in finding their way through the pathways.

                    The Assembly Hall
                    Once, the Heavenly River flowed through the heart of Takamagahara, but it was dammed by the sword of Izanagi to create a large dry riverbed which the Pantheon used to hold meetings upon. Unlike many other Pantheons, decisions are not made by the unbridled will of the Pantheon’s ruler alone. When difficult situations arise, such as when Amaterasu retreated into the cave, or when the Amatsukami were looking to annex the land of Plentiful Reeds, the entirety of the Amatsukami (though now the Kunitsukami are involved as well) assemble to discuss matters.
                    These days, the great dried riverbed is still used when the Amatsukami wish to speak among themselves. While Amaterasu’s will is the final say, the Empress of the Kami does listen and take advice from her fellow Gods and Goddesses -- a practice that many other Pantheon Heads may want to learn from.

                    The first time a Scion of the Kami is brought to Takamagahara is likely when a situation arises that the assembly of the Kami is needed. If such an event occurs, word would be sent to Demigod or greater members of the Pantheon, insisting on their presence. Failing to appear would be a sign of great disrespect, even though the Demigod may find their opinions ignored during these discussions. They are one of eight million attending Kami, and they may be smart to keep their mouth shut.

                        Terra Incognita - Ryugu-jo
                        Deep under the sea dwells Ryujin, the Dragon King. Visiting it takes a skilled Scion, as one has to dive into the depths of the Sea of Japan and swim into the darkness there to discover the Palace. Others have normally reached the palace by being brought by a servant of Ryujin, but attempting to reach it without an assistant is far more difficult. One must achieve 25 Successes on an appropriate roll chosen by the Storyteller, relevant to the player’s chosen method to reach the wondrous local.

                        However, there are other ways to gain entrance to Ryugu-jo. Several bodies of water house entrances to the palace: Shinsen’en Garden in the Imperial Palace in Kyoto is one of these, along with a cave in Mount Muro. Some other entrances have been sealed off by corruption in the entrance waters, such as what occurred to a pond in Nara. If one was able to purify one of these entrances, Ryujin would likely be quite grateful.

                        The Palace itself, once a being reaches it, is a wondrous sight. A sprawling Japanese royal estate sits on an undersea plateau overlooking a deeper trench. The palace itself is created from the materials available rather than what one would expect, with wood replaced by beautiful coral growths. The buildings are so close together, and the pattern of the roofs interlocked, that visitors have noted that it appears like fish scales from above.

                        The palace is far more welcoming to land-oriented visitors than one would expect from an undersea complex. While the fish, turtles, and jellyfish that serve Ryujin and his family swim through the water filled rooms, visitors are able to walk through the rooms as if they were empty, and breathe entirely normally. The Dragon Princesses that dwell here with their father Ryujin have a habit of falling deeply in love with visitors and suggesting marriage rather quickly, which Ryujin is normally pleased with, as it extends his connection to grand family trees of the Gods. The Dragon King’s wealth is made apparent if a Scion agrees to one of these marriages, with the amount of gifts overflowing hundreds of tables.
                        The greatest danger of the palace is that time functions differently within the complex, with each day that passes inside equating to one hundred years outside, making visits exceptionally difficult and making Scions keep a sharp eye on their watches.

                        Terra Incognita - Tokoyo, the Land Across the Sea
                        A fabulous far-off land from whence the Marebito, the Visiting Kami come from is a large Terra Incognita somewhere off... one of the coasts of Japan. Just which direction one must travel to reach Tokoyo is never quite clear, but the commonality is that it is far from simple.

                        If a Scion desires to reach Tokoyo for one reason or another, such as retrieving a fruit from the seasonless fragrant tree for healing, one would need to organise a powerful naval expedition. It does not matter if one sails in ships suited for the seventeenth century, a modern cruiser, or an ancient simple boat made from hides, the difficulty will always be equal; Tokoyo is less distant in literal terms than figurative. The journey will take several days of combating wind, rain, storm, and occasionally encountering one of Ryujin’s curious children, possibly having to navigate a marriage proposition.

                        When a Scion reaches Tokoyo, well, that is where description of the land falls short. It is likely similar to Japan, but idealised, and filled with the Marebito. Some say it is ruled by Ryujin, but the details are unclear. Just what a Scion would find in this wondrous far off place is a mystery, short of a few known facts. The seasonless fragrant fruit tree grows there, which produces potent medical fruit. Some of the dead are housed here, though it may be underneath Tokoyo rather than on the surface, and just what causes one to reside here after death is unclear. Lastly, the land is eternal, with age not touching those that inhabit it.

                        Terra Incognita - Okuninushi’s Palace
                        When Okuninushi surrendered the rulership of the realm of Plentiful Reeds to the rulership of the Heavenly Grandson, his final condition was that the Amatsukami must construct for him a palace. A palace that had beams driven all the way to the bedrock of the Province and reaching all the way to the top of the sky.

A colossal ramp stretches through Izumo, several miles long, reaching up to the palace itself which stands on stilts, directly overtop the Izumo shrine. The Palace is so colossal that the roof of the single building brushes the undercurrent of the Heavenly River, and is so large that it is able to accommodate the Eight Million Kami for their yearly meeting in the building.

                        Scions of the Kami who have become Demigods are actually expected to attend this yearly meeting in Okuninushi’s palace, which lasts the entire tenth month of the archaic Japanese calendar, which when converted to the Gregorian Calendar is a mixture of October and November. For this entire month, Scions and full Deities alike must attend and explain what they know is occurring in the world, or be considered disrespectful. Only Ebisu is granted exception.

                            Ame-no-Minakanushi - The Center of Heaven

                            Powers: Stars
                            The first of the Kami to come into existence, Ame-no-Minakanushi is an ancient and long vanished being, becoming one of the Hidden Kami who disappeared from the world.
                            Just who Ame-no-Minakanushi is, is a mystery. They are the center of heaven, as their name implies, but just what this entails is never expanded upon by the writers of the ancient texts. Early Modern scholars postulated that Ame-no-Minakanushi retreated to Ursa Major, so possibly that is the center of heaven.

                            What is known of Ame-no-Minakanushi is that they were sexless, as it was not until Izanami and Izanagi that sex developed in the Kami. While Takamimusuhi and Kamimusuhi both seemed to become male and female retroactively, Ame-no-Minakanushi withdrew before this.

                            It is oft believed that it was Ame-no-Minakanushi who gave Izanami and Izanagi the task of creating the world, and they came to Ame-no-Minakanushi when their first child was born deformed and subpar.

                            Creatures: Ame-no-Minakanushi is a solitary and lone figure in their abrupt departure from the world. As far as anyone knows, they brought no other beings with themselves when they vanished. However, the other ‘Hidden Kami’ may have left to their halls as well, each a powerful Titan.

                            Realm: After Ame-no-Minakanushi came into existence in the first moments of creation, they vanished, becoming one of the Hidden Kami. Some assume that they retreated to the center of heaven as their name would imply, though just what this entails is difficult to discern. Some in the early modern period suggested that they now reside in the constellation of Ursa Major. If a Scion or a God wished to meet Ame-no-Minakanushi, they would have an odyssey par to none on their plate. They would need to first find out where the Titan retreated to, and then find a way to reach them. Just what they might find there is unknown, just what the temperament of this Titan is as unknown as where it went.

                            Takamimusuhi - The Right Hand of Heaven

                            Powers: Justice
                            Takamimusuhi was the second Kami to come into existence. Unlike Ame-no-Minakanushi, Takamimusuhi returned to the world after the initial withdrawal, and served Amaterasu as a right hand, convening the assembly of the Kami whenever it was necessary for a decision of consensus to be made.

                            A being of ritual and order, Takamimusuhi assisted Amaterasu in her organisation and subjugation of earth beneath her rule. For example, when the pheasant was slain by Ame-no-Wakahiko to conceal his rebellion, Takamimusuhi sent the arrow back from whence it came, killing Ame-no-Wakahiko in retribution. He served Amaterasu much like a Prime Minister in modern parlance, a secular ruler in charge of the government, subservient to Amaterasu.

                            For unknown reasons, Takamimusuhi fades from the history of the Kami. Initially, during the earliest periods, Takamimusuhi appears alongside Amaterasu when the Pantheon is called together in the riverbed of the Heavenly River. However, the figure fades away after the conquest of Earth, and it is unclear why this happens.

                            Creatures: If Takamimusuhi still dwells in the halls of Takamagahara, then his servants are similar to the rest of the Kami dwelling within the Overworld. Specifically his children with Kamimusuhi, including Yorozuhata-hime (the mother of Ninigi), Ame-no-Koyane (Priest of the Gods), Omoikane (Advisor to Amaterasu and God of Wisdom), and Sukunabikona (the Marebito who helped Okuninushi). If he has gone to distant lands, just what may serve him and his descendents is a total mystery.

                            Realm: As explained, Takamimusuhi has vanished from the records of the Kami, and just where he might dwell is unknown. He may still dwell within Takamagahara, and serve Amaterasu faithfully. However, he could just as likely be on some grand mission for the royal Kami, or simply hidden himself once more.

                            Kamimusuhi - Unbridled Life

                            Powers: Fertility, Health
                            Kamimusuhi, was the third being to come into existence. Like Ame-no-Minakanushi, she vanished and became one of the Hidden Kami, but unlike Ame-no-Minakanushi, she flitted back into sight on occasion.

                            Despite the first three beings in existence being made without counterpart, in time it appears she became female (as sex first appeared with Izanagi and Izanami) and had children with Takamimusuhi, such as Yorozuhata-hime and Ame-no-Koyane. Furthermore, a Kami from Tokoyo was born from her line.

                            Kamimusuhi wields a power par none inside the Pantheon: the capacity to restore life to the dead. When Okuninushi was slain by his brothers, and his mother wished for his resurrection, Kamimusuhi intervened and returned him to life. Furthermore, when Uke-Mochi was slain, she gathered up the grains that flowed from the dead Kami’s orifices and gave them to humanity.
                            Interestingly, Kamimusuhi is at times considered part of either the Amatsukami or the Kunitsukami. If another rebellion was to break out, just where her loyalties lie would be an important political issue.

                            Creatures: Kamimusuhi has a deep connection with the Earthly Kami, and especially Okuninushi, who she resurrected on the wishes of his mother. If, theoretically, Kamimusuhi was threatened, the Earthly Kami may schism over it. Furthermore, at least one of the Marebito from Tokoyo are from her line, and they may come to her aid if summoned.

                            Realm: Kamimusuhi vanishes, like Ame-no-Minakanushi, but there is no helpful hint in her name where she may have gone. The Titaness does appear sporadically, but never for long, and infrequently. Some might suspect she dwells somewhere in Japan, hidden away, or in the distant land of Tokoyo.

                            Izanagi - The First Father

                            Powers: Earth, Health, Fertility, Epic Strength, Justice.
                            The primordial father of the Amatsukami, Izanagi was the first male, and with his sister Izanami, brought forth most of the islands of the world, stirring the world with his great spear. Discovering the proper way to initiate sexual relations, he fathered a huge number of Kami with Izanami until Kagutsuchi was born, killing his beloved Izanami.

Against her wishes, Izanagi snuck a look at his dead wife, and was horrified by the rotting corpse so badly that he fled, enraging Izanami. With the Jeweled Spear of Heaven, Izanagi made almost all of the lands as well -- a creator par none within the Pantheon, even among the older generations of Kami.

                            Creatures: Izanagi, as the ultimate patriarch of the Kami, is in an almost unique situation for Titans. He has no monsters or beasts at his beck and call. Instead, he has an entire Pantheon. Every Kami in heaven and upon the earth would rise if their ancient father was threatened, with the possible exception of Susano-O.

                            Realm: Izanagi has not left the world entirely, he still resides in the mighty Halls of Heights by the Freshwater Sea. He is rarely involved with his descendents now, but musters with them when the Pantheon is called for their yearly meeting.


                            Izanami - The Dead Mother

                            Powers: Death, Epic Appearance (-)
                            Izanami, the horrifying rotting Titaness, was not always this way. Once she was the mother of the Kami, before Izanagi started multiplying their numbers independently. Along with her husband, she helped create almost all that is. But then she died giving birth to Kagutsuchi, and was bound to the Underworld. When her husband came to her, to beg her to come home with him, she begged him not to look upon her visage as she went to ask permission to leave. He failed this, and was so horrified by her corpse that he fled, insulting her deeply.

                            In a rage, Izanami sent out warriors after him; thunder deities who chased him as she swore that she would kill a thousand people every day in vengeance.

                            Izanami is no longer the primordial mother of the Kami, but instead a vengeful Titaness, rotting in the depths of Yomi and eager to murder any Scions who foolishly tread still breathing into her land. The colossal boulder that keeps her sealed is the only true stopper to her rage, making her only able to kill by proxy, but she desires nothing less than the destruction of Izanagi and his children.

                            In the depths of the Underworld, Izanami is now pure unbridled corruption, rotting, and filthy. The antithesis to everything the Pantheon hold dear. The only member of their ranks who does not hold their corrupt mother in vile regard is Susano-O, and one must wonder why. Did she whisper to him through the earth? Or, perhaps, Susano-O’s drive to chaos and resistance to the social norm pushed him towards her instead.

                            Creatures: Locked away deep in the Underworld, Izanami still has pawns yet. The Yomotsushikome, fearsome Underworld hags, and some unnamed thundering snake demons serve her. The Titaness also seems to occupy a soft spot in the heart of Susano-O, even though she is not his mother.

                            Realm: Locked away in the Underworld of Yomi by her husband Izanagi, behind a boulder so large only the strongest of gods could hope to move it, Izanami sits in darkness.


                            Kagutsuchi - The All-Destroying Fire

                            Powers: Fire
                            The Kami of Fire, Kagutsuchi ended the creation of the world by his birth from Izanami, burning her genitals and causing her death. Unlike many Titans, Kagutsuchi’s story ends here at his birth. Instantly butchered by an enraged Izanagi.

                            From the corpse of Kagutsuchi arise many Kami, sixteen in total. Kami of mountains, of volcanoes, of storms, and rock. Just what sort of being Kagutsuchi is must be riddled out from this myth, a source of fire so destructive it prematurely ended the creation of the world.

                            Kagutsuchi, if revived through some complex and probably gory ritual, would still be this ever-destroying Fire that annihilates all it touches and leaves nothing left. Who knows if he would hold a grudge against Izanagi for his murder? A question that is likely better left alone than attempt to discern the answer.

                            Creatures: For the moment, Kagutsuchi is a non-entity. He exists, kind of. Kagutsuchi was hacked into many pieces by his grieving father, and each piece became new Kami. Due to this, Kagutsuchi has no servants, or friends, due to not existing at the moment. If someone were to force those Kami back together somehow and return him to life... well, Fire needs no friends save fuel.

                            Realm: Possibly the most imaginatively imprisoned Titan, Kagutsuchi was killed and broken into many different, more benevolent Kami. The Fire Kami is now many, and is imprisoned in this way, each piece being a unique and distinct being with their own opinions and personality. The only way to free Kagutsuchi would to somehow stitch him back together, hopefully metaphorically, from his body parts.


                            Fujin and Raijin - The Storm Ogres

                            Powers: Storms, Epic Appearance
                            The two colossal storm Oni, Fujin and Raijin are all that is dangerous and uncontrolled in the sky. Fujin carries with him a colossal bag from which the winds of the world pour, whipped into typhoons that leave entire cities strewn around the province in pieces. Raijin carries with him colossal drums upon which he plays Thunder and crashing lightning by extension.

                            The two Ogres are mysterious, though few sit to ponder their unknown parentage when the pair roar through the sky bringing doom and destruction in their path. Raijin has been known to steal and chew upon the bellybuttons of others, but just why he does this is never clear. While this may sound like an innocent fable, such as stealing a child’s nose, when Raijin steals a belly button, he physically rips open the body and tears it away. This will kill you, though some traditionalists wear coverings across their belly to prevent this gruesome death.

                            The two Storm Titans are a force not to be reckoned with, for they are beings capable of terrible destruction, laying waste to cities in the blink of the eye. Laughing uproariously all the while in the sky high above.

                            Creatures: The Storm Titans have a familiar face in Japanese Mythology with the Oni. If the Oni are their descendents, or if they are particularly powerful Oni, no one knows, but the connection between them is obvious. If Fujin and Raijin decided that being tamed was not their thing, they may unify the Oni under themselves. Additionally, the Kami host several chaotic Storm Gods who may find more comradery with Fujin and Raijin than with their fellow Gods.

                            Realm: The twin Titans were, long ago, set upon by Kannon who calmed the raging storm and brought them to something resembling peace. If this conversion was a success or not is difficult to tell, as Storms continue to rage while they charge about and roar on a whim. If Fujin and Raijin were to decide that Kannon’s words were no longer sufficient, they would soon be dancing across the sky of earth once again, bringing catastrophic destruction in their wake.


                            Namazu - The Pinned Earthquake

                            Powers: Earth, Animal
                            The earthquakes of Japan in modernity can be devastating, but in the past, they were even more dangerous, frequent, and destructive. The colossal catfish Kami Namazu once swam around deep within the world causing earthquakes so massive they threatened to destroy the world. It is here that Takemikazuchi enters the scene, and pinned Namazu down by slamming a colossal stone pillar through the earth to pin the catfish Kami down by his head.

                            However, Takemikazuchi is not always there to subdue the Titan, and when the yearly month-long gathering of the Kami occurs, Namazu often will attempt to struggle against his bonds causing earthquakes.

                            Interestingly, the colossal catfish came to represent wealth as he destroyed the grand homes of the rich and gave the poor relief from their toil, as their masters attempted to rebuild. In modernity, he is still worshiped for wealth on occasion in special temples.

                            It is hard to say if Namazu could be reasoned with. The Kami represent many abstract features, and are entirely capable of communicating, but Namazu appears to truly be a colossal catfish in form and in mind. He may have the same perspective on the world as any other catfish.

                            Creatures: When Namazu was subdued by Takemikazuchi, Namazu’s lesser catfish earthquake spirits bowed down and groveled before the storm God. However, if Namazu broke free from the colossal stone beam, then they likely would once again rally behind him.

                            Realm: Beneath the soil of Japan’s Kanto region, Namazu is imprisoned. A colossal stone pillar is pinning the giant catfish’s head down deep underground, the top of which (called Kaname-ishi, or “Finishing Stone”) pokes through the ground at Takemikazuchi’s shrine at Kashima.

                                                    Suicide
                                                    Japan has a noteworthy relationship with suicide, having the seventeenth highest suicide rate by the World Health Organization. Though suicide does not appear in the higher cosmological myths of the Kami, the understanding and role of suicide is so divergently different from many other parts of the world. It is believed that suicide in Japan has roots in social obligation more so than mental illness as it is in the West, with these social obligations finding their genesis in both Buddhist and Samurai practice influencing popular culture. Buddhism brought to Japan self-immolation, a suicide which could benefit the karma of one’s community, self-mummification practiced by specific sects to achieve enlightenment, and self-abandonment, where one goes adrift at sea for rescue by Kannon. The Samurai on the other hand had a more famed and ritualised version of self-disembowelment, and though this practice (named Seppuku) is ancient, it was initially uncommon.

                                                    During the Middle Ages, rife with war, suicide amongst the Samurai became more common, though not ritualised, instead quick battlefield suicides were employed to avoid capture. As time moved on, this chaotic period ended, but suicide remained a part of the culture to the point that the Tokugawa shogunate classified Seppuku as a honorable form of execution of Samurai. This is the point where Seppuku takes a form more recognised in modernity, ritualised self-disembowelment, though the exact details of this complex practice will not be detailed here, only partly for concerns of length. The reasons for these suicides however is the important part, and fall into two large categories, willingly preformed ones, and forced ones.

                                                    In addition to defeat in battle (to avoid dishonorable treatment by an enemy force), voluntary reasons included self-punishment (on account of a personal failure), to protect one’s family (collective punishments for criminals was the norm, though the Samurai were able to save one’s family if they were to commit seppuku), to shame one’s lord, as proof of one’s innocence (or, more aptly, in indignation of a false accusation), or to follow one’s lord into death. Situations in which this could be forced upon a Samurai is both by an enemy (with the famed case of Oda Nobunaga trapped in a burning building with his loyal retainers), or as legal punishment.

                                                    While these notions may be seen as relics of the past, they survive into the modern era, though without the intense ritualisation. Salarymen burning out or cracking under insane pressure are often driven to it, which likely leaves the Kami with a somewhat unique outlook on suicide. To ancient Japan, suicide was an action of bravery, which may lead to a large degree of confusion with other Pantheons.
