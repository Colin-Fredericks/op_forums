    Game Breaking: How to House Rule?

    11-19-2015, 07:35 PM
    So It's been brought to my attention that the Epic Attributes can get pretty damn crazy down the line when the Automatic Successes come into play. I have been warned that once you get to Demigod level, anything with more Epic Dexterity than you is pretty much guaranteed to win a fight every time. In order to overcome this, people tend to use house rules and home brewed methods, but I don't know of any examples to help balance the game out.


    One possible idea I had was to simply downgrade them to 1 auto success per level (12 successes at max), but I don't know if this would dramatically reduce the effectiveness of these attributes from a story telling point. Say if a building needed 60 successes to lift and you only have a dice pool of 20 with 12 automatic successes. Technically your character might be able to lift the weight of the building, but mechanically it would be impossible to roll the successes needed to actually do it.

    So could anybody help me out with making the game more properly balanced so I don't have a horde of dex monkies running around smacking people into the dirt?

Now, with a mundane Strength of 10 and Athletics 5, one should be able to lift roughly 2200 pounds (over a ton). Epic Strength 10 adds 1,250,000,000 pounds to this total (625,000 tons), while the Uplifting Might Knack doubles this (1,250,002 tons).

                                  So, if the object is within this weight capacity (and at over a million tons, there would be very little that isn't in this capacity), he should be able to lift it without too much trouble.

                                  Now as a Feat of Strength that would require a roll, I would allow such an epically strong character to lift a capacity of his maximum strength times the number of successes he rolls, so with an Epic Strength of 10, even limiting them to 10 automatics successes, that is a minimum of 10 times this amount (over 12 million tons); not including any successes they get from rolling their base dice pool of 15 dice (for an average of another 5 successes, with a maximum potential for 30 more... if all the dice roll 10). Thus, a God at this level would have a maximum potential of around 50 million tons (give or take a couple of million) of lifting capacity as a feat of strength. Can you think of anything that would exceed this weight limit that a God might want to move (other than celestial objects like moons, planets and such)?

                                      What I was suggesting is to use your Attribute roll as normal (including the one autosuccess per dot of Epic), but to change what the resulting successes mean based on how many dots of Epic you have.

                                      As the rules are written, each success always gives you 25 lbs of lifting capacity, and that gets supplemented by a fixed amount that your Epic Strength provides: 500 lbs for Epic •, 1000 lbs for Epic ••, and so on. The only reason those initial 25 lbs per success mean anything at all is that you get a boatload of successes to work with: the official progression of auto-successes converges on a doubling of how many you are given with each dot, so Epic ••• not only gives you 2000 lbs of lifting capacity outright, it also gives you 7 successes-worth of lifting capacity on top of that, or an additional 175 lbs. Add to that another 25 lbs per success that you actually roll. Note that the auto-successes don't quite reach a tenth of what Epic Strength grants you outright.

                                      After a while, it becomes pointless to roll Strength + Athletics to lift something, because 25 lbs per success rolled is inconsequential compared to the tons worth of lifting capacity granted by the Epic. Dropping things to one auto-success per dot of Epic actually doesn't affect things all that much, as the amount of lifting capacity granted by the auto-successes as written never breaks even a tenth of what the Epic grants on its own.

                                      So, back to my suggestion: instead of each success granting 25 lbs, say that how much each success grants you depends on your Epic. Instead of a flat +500 lbs from Epic •, say that at Epic • every success that you get on the roll lets you lift 250 lbs; Epic •• grants +500 lbs per success instead of a flat +1000 lbs; Epic ••• grants +1000 lbs per success instead of a flat +2000 lbs; and so on. Now your dice pool actually matters.

                                      The other thing a linear progression of auto-successes does is to drastically scale back how much damage you can inflict. Three options here: stick to the existing system (meaning that if your Epic Strength exceeds your opponent's Epic Stamina by one at the high end, he's dead meat if you hit him); go with a linear progression (meaning that you won't be able to rip tanks in half); or go with a hybrid approach where you use a linear progression of one auto-success against significant opponents such as other Scions, but you use the published escalation of auto-successes when you're chewing the scenery. The last approach isn't particularly realistic; but it lets you be awesome without making a single dot's difference in Epic ratings literally a matter of life or death.

                                      A fourth possibility would be to downgrade the armor ratings of tanks and the like.

                                      For Epic Dexterity, keep the boost to Move and Dash as is, but reduce the DV bonus to a linear progression of one per dot of Epic. The wonkiness of the current system lies entirely in the geometric progression of DV and auto-successes, meaning that as you get higher in the scale a single dot of Epic Dex more than your opponent has means that he'll never be able to generate enough successes to hit you and you will always have enough successes to hit him; linearize those progressions, and the problem goes away.

                                      The advice I gave for damage-dealing Epic Strength also applies to damage-soaking Epic Stamina.

                                      Social and Mental Epics need no further tampering beyond putting their auto-successes on a linearity diet.

                                      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
