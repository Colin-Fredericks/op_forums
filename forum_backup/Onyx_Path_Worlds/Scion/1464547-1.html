            Originally posted by JSM3050 View Post
            Can you share any details on No Gods, No Masters (or point me in the direction of the relevant blog posts if I missed them)?
            Sure! The gist of No Gods, No Masters is that it's a pre-made campaign; it has a collection of linked adventures that center on the Keepers of the World. The Keepers are a group of Gods who were mistreated as mortals and, now that they're Gods, have an axe to grind. Imhotep — among others — have decided that the World will be better off without Gods. His goal is to cut the World off from the Pantheons and leave science and reason in charge of the humanity.

            Player Scions are free to fight the Keepers, join them, or find something even weirder to do with their time.

                        Originally posted by literatzi View Post

                        How does the Stun tag for weapons work?
                        Sure. That’s solid question caused by a combination of Storypath’s open-ended handling of tags and a bit orphaned text.

                        The text of the Stun tag reads:

                        Stun (1): When inflicting an Injury, this weapon can only be used to inflict the Minor Injury Conditions Bruised, Staggered, or Stunned, or the Major Injury Condition Battered. When a character is Taken Out by a weapon with the Stun tag, they are knocked unconscious.

                        We basically have 2 problems here:
                        • What’s a Minor or Major Injury Condition? &
                        • What do those Conditions do?

                        First, Minor and Major Injury Conditions don’t actually exist in the game. So, we don’t know what the terms mean. Rather than worry about orphaned categories, we’ll use the Injury Condition levels that are in the game (Bruised/Injured/Maimed). This gives us 4 Injury Conditions that we can slot into 3 categories.
                        • Bruised: Definitely Bruised level
                        • Staggered: This doesn’t sound like an injury, so it’s also Bruised level
                        • Stunned: Probably Injured level.
                        • Battered: Probably Maimed level.

                        Are these levels definitely correct? Not by RAW, but they work within the rules framework, so let’s call it good and move to what they do.

                        Injury Conditions are deliberately open-ended, which allows Storyguides to add whatever they want. There are general guidelines in Origin (the levels are -1, -2, -4), and examples (a Black Eye is a Bruised-level Condition that adds +1 Difficulty to Firearms actions). This loose framework allows Storyguides to make things up on the spot, creating really niche Conditions, like Impaled by an Award Statuette (Maimed level) or Stepped on a Lego (Bruised level). This open-endedness is really helpful for Storyguides who’re used to doing mechanical work on the fly, but sucks for folks who like concrete answers.

                        So, to help folks who want more Injury Condition examples, here’s some for the 3 undefined Injury Conditions for Stun weapons:
                        • Staggered (-1 Defense as they try to shake it off/+1 Difficulty to actions using Agility)
                        • Stunned (Limited to 1 reflexive action per turn & cannot take mixed actions/+2 Difficulty to all Finesse actions)
                        • Battered: (+4 Difficulty to all Finesse actions/Cannot take reflexive or mixed actions & +4 Difficulty to movement)

                            You can see this post on the forum ( http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...-scion-2e-play ) and the reviews on Drivethrurpg to get a sense of the numerous issues left.
                            I actually didn’t join Team Scion until after after Origin and Hero were out and didn't become a developer for Scion until this year; I had to learn the system in much the same way that any other fan of the game did. I’m NOT saying “I can play the game, so everyone else can, too.” I’m saying that the flaws that you seem focused on aren’t as big as you seem to think they are.

                            Do flaws exist? Sure. Everything ever published has its own problems. I don’t think the ones in Origin and Hero are substantial enough that they require producing a new version, which is more of a headache than folks realize. So, to re-answer your original question, we’re pretty unlikely to see an Origin or Hero 2.5.

                            That said, it seems like some of the problems you’re having with the game are less with the rules and more to do with the rules presentation. That is something I can and will do something about. I’m a former university lecturer, and making material more accessible is kind of my jam. I plan on putting out rules and setting info in more digestible forms, including videos. I don’t have an ETA on those, but the first few will probably come out in the next few months.
