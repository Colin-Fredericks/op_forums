      The Greeks are basically doing double duty. There are a very, very few major Roman Gods that are not also Greek... and they're not that major. Probably not Legend 12. Janus, Selvans, etc. The biggest difference is just the names, and a few Associated Powers. Mars, for example, would have Fertility and maybe Guardian, whereas Ares does not have either. Ceres probably has Justice, while Demeter does not. It's a very limited number of changes.

      I can point you to my Roman Pantheon-specific Purview, if you'd like to replace Arete with Civitas.

      There just isn't a point in splitting up Jupiter and Zeus when all their stories, personalities and traits are virtually identical.

                          The Areopagus proper is IN Athens. As far as I can remember, it's not a term for "any hill on which we do legal proceedings" in other cities. Take that for what you will, but if Athenian myth is the source of Ares as bad boy, there's a cognitive disconnect there.

                          Ares is DEFINTELY a bad boy in the Iliad, which is not Athenian and much more seminal as myths go for the Greeks, as, at it's core, it largely predates the whole Ionian vs. Dorian dichotomy. In the Iliad, Ares fights until Diomedes actually wounds him then runs home to mama howling and weeping. There's your definitely spoiled child following blood-lusting warrior type. Aphrodite does the same "spoiled child" thing, and her mythology per se is not defined as Athenian, as that was not a place where her cult was very strong. She had a lot of traction out in the isles. Which, like Athens were largely Ionian, so the same mytho-types largely predominated.

                          One of the definitive war gods of Sparta, which dominates the Peloponnese was Artemis. She was a homicidal bloody bitch who demanded that boys be whipped to death in her temple. That's definitely not something that gets picked up in Scion. She was less warrior, more hunter-ish in the Iliad, more petty than spoiled. And secretly saving Iphigenia after demanding her sacrifice is a little quixotic and not at all like the Artemis of Sparta. Then you take Artemis at Ephesus where she's a mother-goddess/goddess of the animals and plants/agriculture type, not AT ALL like any other Artemis (more Demeter or like the Macedonian cult of Rhea than Homeric Artemis). Then you take Diana, who is a weird synthesis, a goddess of the wild to be respected, but still pretty bloody-handed when you look at her King of the Grove cult at Aricia, which is very much core to how she was perceived in Rome.

                          There is a couple of ultimate questions important to Scion implicit in these discussions.

                          How schizoid are the gods in general? Do you meet a very different Diana in Aricia than you meet at Ephesus than how she is walking and talking in Athens vs. her behavior at Sparta? Are they all the same person or different aspects of the same person? Does she have any choice in how she is acting or is she somehow compelled to take on the Artemis of "around here"? Or is it incentivized somehow to act in accordance with your myths when and where they seem to matter?

                          Second, how much credence do you give to cultic practice when you are building the deity in question? How much do you build into the system? Which myths do you pick when you have so many to choose from and so many missing, lost, etc.? The obvious answer is "not much from cultic practice" and "not much that vary off the mainstream of how the god in question is commonly depicted in the modern world". Otherwise the game becomes less approachable and, ultimately, affects its success in attracting and keeping players.

                          But, thinking around corners is so much fun.....
