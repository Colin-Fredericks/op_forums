    Originally posted by Wannabe Demon Lord View Post
    I stumbled upon something odd concerning the Ashanti. In a book entitled The Complete Book of Devils and Demons by Leonard R.N. Ashley, in a list of demons/titans/evil gods, a being called Tando was referenced. He was described as being the primary god of evil of the Ashanti, and demanded human sacrifice, taking seven men and seven women at a time. A Google search brought me to a one-sentence article on Encyclopedia Mythica where it described him as a river god who was married to a malevolent goddess named Katarwiri. In the Guide to the Gods by Marjorie Leach, I looked him up and it confirmed both of those things, adding that he was also a god of storms, and would use disease, lightning, and floods to punish people. Katarwiri also had an entry but it didn't say anything more about her than that she was an evil river goddess that also demanded sacrifices. Watcher and Sacerdos, are you familiar with either of these beings? It might be bullshit, and you would know that way better then I, but that would seem to go against the Ashanti having a purely benevolent cosmology.
    Looks like Leonard R.N. Ashley was an English Professor at Brooklyn College, and wrote a series of Occult dictionaries. If I had one on hand I could give a better commentary of it, but, world-spanning encyclopedias tend not to be super accurate. No one can be an expert of everything, it's not really anyone's fault. It's why Dictionaries of Wold History tend to be mostly for public consumption since they will only be able to hit on the 'main points' and inevitably will make mistakes since you are talking about hundreds of fields of history at once. Similarly for Marjorie Leach who is an Independent Scholar. I'm not sure of the validity of her findings.

    If you want a good test of how valid something like this is, check out Gaulish Deities, and you can see how well researched into obscure areas it is. If Teutates is described as a singular Deity, then they are using an outdated theory which was revised in the 1930s - 1940s (it is a class of Deity, not a singular Deity) and if Cernunnos is described as a God of the Hunt or the Wilderness, it is behind archeological findings by about 30 years. (We can find no significant evidence of subsistence hunting in Gaul during the immediatly pre-Roman and post-Roman period, and the Wilderness thing is just speculative)

    But, I can somewhat explain what I think is going on. Within the Ashanti cosmological system there can't be God of Evil. Nyame is a Benelovent Over-Deity, and all supernatural power in the world has to be something he doles out, or doled out from one of the beings he has given power to. Not in a "I make you the God of Fire, now you are the God of Fire," sort of way, but think of it like channeling his raw power through a feudal pyramid. This is how Abosom, and mortals all can tap into supernatural powers. Due to this, you can't exactly have an Evil Abosom since Nyame would just not give them any power.

    This idea of Tando as a God of Evil is something that jumps out of me as one of two things. At a glance, it sounds like Christianization. Ashanti religion falls prey to Christianization in the period after we are looking at it, and Nyame becomes forcefully reshaped into YHWH. Colonialism does messed up stuff. I expect that the lack of any antagonistic entities necessitated the creation of ones for the converters since Christianity sort of needs that dualistic opposite for its cosmological system, and that's probably where Tando is coming from.

    The other option is that Tando is a foreign Deity who becomes part of one of the Witch-Cults. These are the only real form of semi-antagonistic Divine entities since they're not actually part of Ashanti cosmology, they are all imports, or foreign-inspired entities detached from the Abosom and traditional belief systems. Veneration and service to these Non-Abosom Deities is a method for people to gain malignant powers and use them for the detriment of a community. They're super-illegal, and the Ashanti priest class probably is not too keen on them. They become more popular in the early 20th century, which is after the period we are snapshotting. They at best don't really surpass Hero level antagonism however, not really the realm of Titans. They are entities that mortals are able to come into conflict with and emerge victorious.

    I quickly checked the Encyclopedia Mythica for the entry on the pair of them, and apparently Katarwiri is also from Tshi, and I suspect this is the source of confusion. The pair of them may be foreign Deities who were slowly being imported into the Ashanti regions after the period we are focusing on. Generally... be careful using Encyclopedia Mythica. They have a Sisyphean task to try to work on that impossible project, and don't list where their information is being drawn from. For example, they might be drawing their understanding of Katarwiri and Tando from Marjorie Leach's work, and thus it is just becoming a bit self-referential. Further, since the project can't have experts on literally every field, people working on the project don't necessarily specialize in the specific area in question. The author of both of those articles, Micha F. Lindemans, is a Classicist. No fault on them, they just don't have the resources to hire specialists on every culture known to humanity.

    Encyclopedia Mythica, for example, lists Balor as a God of Death, and that he killed Nuada with his eye. Balor's not a God, also the Irish don't use the God of X program, and while Balor did kill Nuada, in neither manuscript version of the text does it describe him using his eye for such. The section on Blodeuwedd has several explanations for her actions which are undescribed in any translations I have read. The section on Lugh has the misunderstanding that he is the patron of skills rather than being related to the Men of Art (not artists, it's the high-status professions) and thus a patron of the upper 20% of the population (think of Lugh as the God of a Class System if you want to use the God of X system) and... actually, that Lugh article is terrible. He isn't the patron of Lugodunum, that's Lug. Rosmerta is the consort (sometimes) of Lug, not Lugh. Lugnasad has no known ripening associations, that's fictional. I have zero idea where the Lynx association is from, and his exploits are not told in the Táin Bó Cúailnge, they are told in Cath Maige Tuired, Lugh functions as an ancillary character in the Tain, showing up in passing only two times.

            Originally posted by Wannabe Demon Lord View Post
            Do you guys think we should have a singular Bantu pantheon with the Milungu at the core and multiple sub-pantheons including the Zulu and Bunyoro, or keep them as separate as possible?
            That's the big question with them, isn't it? Similar to the Polynesians, the Bantu are technically just a language group, spread over an enormous geographical expanse, but with a lot of cultural and especially religious overlap. Watcher and I actually had a very short stab at the Balubare, the absolutely amazing gods of the Kingdom of Buganda, but quickly adjourned working on them as the larger Bantu issue began to arise. Now, the actual research we have done on the Bantu is minimal so far, but the general plan for working them as a pantheon has shaped up to be similar to the way we'll be treating the Polynesian gods: a core group of commonly-held gods, a number of important local figures, thorough explanations of how this shapes up as seperate pantheons as well, and a common PSP (Mana for the Polynesians, Ntu for the Bantu). However, this is a lot easier to do for the Polynesians, for the simple reason that they are polytheists who have a hard core of gods shared among almost all of the different Polynesian groups; the Bantu, meanwhile, are monotheists, with only one true and high God. Everyone else you might have heard about are, in fact, just very powerful ancestor spirits, and that is kind of the crucial issue with the Bantu: ancestors are enormously important to them, more important than God, actually, and their "pantheons" are normally actually the deified ancestors and lineage founders of the royal family. Thus, when Watcher and I get to doing our Bantu writeup, we will most likely largely conflate the different Bantu "High Gods" into one, and then outline a number of these royal ancestor-pantheons - so far, I have Bakongo, Buganda, Rwanda, Zimbabwe, and the Zulu noted down as candidates. So the solution we'd do is a combination of both, avoiding both the inaccuracies of a single Bantu pantheon, and the frustrating repetitiveness of separate ones. But that's just how we would do it when we get to doing it

            At the moment, though, Watcher and I have a number of other African pantheons on our plate that seem easier and quicker to do right now than the behemoth that would we the Bantu pantheon(s).

                    Just how many pantheons are you guys actually planning? You seem to mention more and more every time I speak to you. I know the ones you're focusing on in the immediate future (Ashanti, Haida, Hittite, etc.) but if you're okay with it, and there's not some reason you'd be keeping it to yourself, would you be willing to list all the candidates in your long-term plan?                     Well, it depends - how many do we want to do? All of them, all that are possible to do, and maybe then some. How many are we going to do? Depends on how many we can do, of course. And how many we are actually doing ... well, that depends on what you define as "doing". Some are in that very rough outline stage, like the Bantu, some are no more than a list of books that says "could be researched one day", some are already substantial collections of info, and on others, we are already implementing what rules for 2e we already know. What is very important to us right now is to avoid the pantheons that the Developers have already announces for the various installments of 2e. We don't want to offer a free alternative to any of their material too close to its own publication, so as not to impact sales negatively in even the slightest way. In the meantime, we are concentrating on pantheons that are more obscure, adding more to the already substantial variety of mythologies the developers have promised us.
                    You know that that latter group includes the Abosom (Ashanti), Shiunesh (Hittite), Enduri (Manchu), Sga'na Qeda's (Haida), and Zemí (Taíno) by now. Furthermore, there is a second African pantheon we're doing right now, but since we've spoiled so much of our work already, I think it'd be fun to keep you guys guessing with this one. I'll tell you as much: It has very many tales and very few gods, and all of them are pretty much psychopaths - all of them can be expected to kill people completely at random, without any reason. While their mortals have a sharp division between right and wrong, the gods do not, at all.
                    So that's what I'd give you - I hope it keeps you interested
