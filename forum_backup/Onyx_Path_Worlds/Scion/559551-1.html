                                    Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post

                                    More recently, Apollo tried zapping Superman right in the face. This backfired horribly, because Superman is solar-powered, and punched Apollo through a mountain. Nowadays, Superman Vs. Wonder Woman usually ends in make-up sex.
                                    The Apollo or Thor or Ares or Pluto in the comics usually doesn't resemble the most common amalgamation or archetype of the deity of that name. And I'm not talking in appearance (e.g. the superficial read that can end up with blonde instead of red hair). The power wielded by the gods in myths is transcendent. Transcendent power in comics becomes boring if it's not mostly offstage or its something that has to dealt with. Comic book protagonists who start demonstrating mythological god-like levels of power are de-powered. It's harder to tell an interesting story in genre when your protagonist meets your antagonist and wins without any conflict.                                     Apollo IS the sun. The sun is his chariot. Both are true at the same time without being contradictory. He is disease and healing and poetry and rational thought. At that's the short list of the highlights of what Apollo brings to the table.

                                                    Its worth mentioning that the introduction of deities to comic books in Marvels Thor was originally intended by Stan Lee (First of his name) to explicitly be better than Superman could ever be and frankly to kick his ass all over the place. The entire inspiration was "What could be more powerful than Superman? Someone who isn't a Man at all, but a living God".

                                                    Of course, this runs into reality when large segments of the populace are uncomfortable with letting their children read about and Idealize other Deities and the general fact that Superman is the most popular hero in the world, at the end of the day narrative reality bows to that other super powerful green item.

                                                    I'll also bring up the fact that Superman and many other comic book characters are religious in some vague manner (I think Superman might be a Methodist, the Thing is Jewish, etc). that means they acknowledge the presence of Deities, that they are more powerful than them and even deserving of worship from them. That kinda solidifies the issue in my mind as the Deities in Scion are not literary figures who happen to have taken an ancient name, but the honest to goodness Gods of the worlds religions. (Including the ones superheros might worship)
