                    The problem overhauling the Loa is the same problem you get separating out the Roman and Greek pantheons. (For that matter, you need to throw in Etruscan and Mycenaen as well. I discount Minoan for the same reason I discount a Gaulish pantheon. We just don't know enough mythology to construct a pantheon. All we got is art. You can have one, but it's going to be largely made up out of whole cloth with very tiny bits if story here and there. As in, less than Pecos Bill, for an explosive example. And the stories about the gods is a core concept to the game.)

                    Same for the Celtic pantheons too - Is Manannan mac Lir the same dude as Manawydan fap Llyr? Could be. (Seriously, how could he NOT be.) He just moves to the Isle of Man after he's done with all his shenanigans in the Mabinogien. Is Llew the same as Lugh? Harder sell.

                    Do you focus on Voodoo (and, implicitly hoodoo) or do you try to syncretize Voodoun, Staneria, Candomble and Yoruba religion as it's still be practiced? What do you gain and what do you lose? Is it worth it? It's pretty fine-grained to start teasing these guys apart

                    I'm interested to see what they do with the "pantheon variance" material in the new game. It's the answer to these questions. But you still have to have something functional in place and that's likely to be a pantheon that does not make these distinctions. Ogun is pretty much Ogun, but Ellegua isn't a whole lot like Legba, But are they close enough together for it to work on a coarse/get more pantheons into the books scale?

                                    As much as people hated the idea, I have to admit that I was intrigued by the Atlantean Gods. Yes, they were made from "whole-cloth" but then how could they not be? Still, a place as mythical and legendary as Atlantis begged for a Pantheon of their own.

                                    One game that I took part in (that unfortunately didn't last long) had the players portraying reincarnations of these long forgotten Gods. Stripped of much of their power, they had to take on the guise of mortal Scions and slowly rebuild their power. We were seeking out the long lost relics of our former lives, stolen and scattered across the world (their true nature and origin all but forgotten) to regain our birthrights (so to speak). We were also being hounded by the Scions of other Gods who were afraid that we would be turned to worship the Titans... and by Titanspawn who feared that we might join with the Gods against them. The game only lasted one winter (by spring things had picked up that most of the players no longer had set scheduels for weekly gaming), but we were close to reaching Demi-God level.

                                    While real-world myths and legends of ancient gods are a good place to start, there should be room for forgotten gods of legendary and mythic civilizations that have been lost to time. If the Legends of King Arthur are valid enough to create a pantheon, then why not a fictional pantheon of a land like Atlantis?
