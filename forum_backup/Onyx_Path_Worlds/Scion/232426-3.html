            It's a massively simpler game but OVA has a 'scale' factor. In that if you are superior in the current situation due to your scale you get a flat +5 (which is pretty big but not completely insurmountable).

            E.g.: You are piloting agiant gundam style mecha and get into a pushing match with a super strong superhero. By simple dint of being a giant mecha you get a flat +5, if you've got other strength modifiers those apply too. Conversely if you were trying to evade that giant mecha as the superhero you'd get a +5 to hiding amongst buildings and the winding streets of a city as the scale now favours you.

            I don't imagine that Scion will use as simple a system - but I;ve played games using scaling so know it can work.

                                                    Long time lurker, first time poster... Normally, I'm content to just read the the writing on the wall, but this is a topic I felt I had to speak up about...

                                                    So first, hi everyone, nice to meet you! I am so freaking excited about Scion 2nd edition that I can barely wait to spend all my slush fund on it.

                                                    Second, it was always my understanding that Obatala was the leader of the Orisha. He was the first Orisha created by Olodumare. He is the most powerful Orisha, the Lord of the Head, the King of Purity. He is recognised as the head of the Orishas, and he fathered many of the ones to come. He is also the father of humanity.

                                                    Shango, in some traditions, was the name of the third king of the city of Oyo and the fourth king of the Yoruba. He is the Orisha of thunder, power, raw masculinity and a terrible, terrible womaniser. He is not married to Erzulie (Erzulie comes from Haitian traditions, not Yoruba), but he is married to Oba, the Orisha of neglected wives. Shango has taken Oya and Oshun as his concubines and loves them far more than he loves his wife.

                                                    I'm not sure where all of this talk is coming from that Shango is the head of the pantheon, but anyway. All that I described up there I learned from Santeria.

                                                    Erzulie is ... not just Erzulie. There are two different ways to interpret Erzulie, though. Some people say there are many Erzulies - the most famous being Erzulie Freda and Erzulie Dantor. They are like sisters who cannot stand the sight of each other, but important to note is that Erzulie Freda is married to Damballah, to Agwe, and to Ogoun. Erzulie Dantor is married to Ti Jean Petro (or is his mother or is his lover, depending on who you ask). Others say that Erzulie Freda and Erzulie Dantor are two aspects of the same loa. One is definitely Rada (Freda), and the other is Petro (Dantor).

                                                    First Edition drove me absolutely crazy with how badly it treated the loa. Needless to say, I pretty much rewrote that whole pantheon for the games I ran.

                                                    Thanks for reading!

                                                            I'm not sure how to answer that question though. Yes and no. Yes, in that New Orleans Voodoo, Haitian Vodou, African Vodun, Santeria and all of the other Yoruboid religions are fascinating to the point of obsession for me, and I've been reading every website and book I can get my hands on for the past five years. I grew up hardcore, old-school Roman Catholic, and I had shrines to the saints that I lovingly built and maintained in my room. When I came upon voodoo, years later, it harkened back to the time of my childhood where religion was still something I fervently, lovingly believed in. Yes, the loa of Haiti and the orisha of Santeria used the images of saints to mask their true identities, so it's not quite the same, but in the traditions of the people, I found something I could very deeply relate to. For me, as a child, God was a more distant entity. He was often quite frightening too, given all the talk about Hell flying about. So as a kid, I clung more to the saints and to the Virgin Mary, revering them as friends and family who could 'get me in good with God.'

                                                            And a lot of that is echoed in voodoo (or vodou, vodun, however you want to spell it - I vary the spelling depending on which location I'm referring to).

                                                            The loa and the orishas are intermediaries between humanity and Bondye / Olodumare-Olorun-Olofi. The supreme being, the Creator god, is distant from all of humanity and earth, but the Creator has given power to the orishas and loa to help out humanity. So while vodouisants do not worship the loa and orishas as gods, they are certainly given their due and reverence. They are honoured with shrines, sacrifices, dances, and songs. But they are seen as family and as friends.

                                                            So yes, yes, it is pretty personal to me in that I want to see each loa and orisha given proper, due respect.

                                                            But also no. I'm not an active practicioner. I consider myself a student at best. I can't speak for any mambos or houngans, and I can't speak as someone who knows more than a beginning initiate. Do I believe that the loa / orisha actually exist? Jury's still out on that one, but I believe it's possible that they could. I don't believe anyone has the answers, least of all me.

                                                            I should probably stop rambling again here. ^_^
