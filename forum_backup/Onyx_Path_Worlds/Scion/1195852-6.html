                                    It's true that there is value in knowing the symbolism associated with a god, but there is space provided for it (and I realize this is an odd place to be picking nits, but since the discussion is ongoing...). There is plenty of room in the descriptions of the gods to explain the traditional associations and symbolism of their purviews. Poseidon is clearly described as a god of seas, earthquakes and horses, so players and STs who want to build symbolic systems around him have access to that information.

                                    The list of purviews (at least in Hero) is a piece of mechanical, chargen-focused information. We don't expect every facet of the god to be fully and exclusively represented by their list of purviews, so having that piece of extraneous information in that part of the god's write-up adds confusion more than resolving it. I mean, that confusion is resolved easily enough by having the rule clarified, so it's not a huge deal, but as a matter of style it's a bit of an irritant.

                                    Of course, in Demigod and God, when the gods are more near and active entities that PCs can interact with, the gods' purview specifiers may become relevant information, but they aren't yet.

                                    On a different subject, I also have a question about relic design: Where is the line between situation and general when it comes to enhancement bonuses. The specific example I have is a relic car with amazing handling such that it gives an enhancement bonus to Pilot rolls when you're driving it. To me, this feels pretty general, but it's significantly less general than, say, a pair of relic driving gloves that give you an enhancement bonus to all Pilot rolls you make no matter what you're driving (or flying). And if we say that driving the relic car is situational, does that mean that a relic sword that gives you a bonus to Close Combat rolls when you fight with it is also situational? Because that seems potentially even more abuseable.
