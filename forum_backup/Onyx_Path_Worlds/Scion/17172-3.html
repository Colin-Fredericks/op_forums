    Originally posted by Man In Black
    I am perfectly fine with people just buying what they want if that is what they want, I just want for those that choose to follow a given path to pay a cheaper price for the same thing the other guy just randomly bought. It can be as simple as "if you have a Boon rated 1 dot lower from the same purview than the one you want to buy, it is 25% cheaper (or any other percentage)".

    You can have the cake and eat it too.
    I don't think you can eat that cake and have it, too. Once you introduce an incentive to buy in order, you introduce the idea that there is an order and you're back to the idea anyone not doing that is 'cheating'. When you have this idea that there should be a progression, that Level 1 Fire Boons help you be better at Level 3 Fire Boons, then you get the mentality that someone who "dabbles" in Fire should be less good at their powers than someone who focuses on Fire. I do not agree. The person with 40 Fire Boons is better at Fire in general. Not better at any specific Fire Boon.

    Let us look at Huitzilopochtli, who quite obviously has Animal (Eagle) as one of his Associated Powers. He probably has a dozen or more Eagle Boons. Then there's Zeus over there, who only ever does one thing with Animal: turn into them. He has Animal Form (Eagle), but very little else. That does not mean Huitzilopochtli is better at Animal Form (Eagle) than Zeus. It does mean that Huitzilopochtli is better at the Animal Purview as a whole, but he doesn't need, deserve or get any specific bonus to that one, specific power. All his other Animal Boons don't help him be any better at using one specific power. So what if he can talk to eagles, grow eagle wings, make eagle-people, turn his blood into giant eagles or anything else. While all of that makes him an amazing God of Eagles, none of it at all helps him turn into an eagle any better than Zeus can.

    So what is Huitzilopochtli losing out on? He and Zeus paid the same amount of XP for Animal Form. They have the same benefit from it. Zeus should be just as good at it as Huitzilopochtli. Huitzilopochtli's advantage in contests of who-is-better-at-Eagles is that where Zeus only has one Eagle Boon, Huitzilopochtli has a dozen. That's all the advantage he needs.

    Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
    I would rather have Boons and Knacks providing the difference in power rather than attributes.
    I agree. I'd like to see automatic successes take a much, much smaller role in Scion2. They take so much randomness out of everything. Rolling dice is often useless because you already know the result before you roll. What does it matter if you roll 5 successes or 0 when you've got 21 auto-successes anyhow? There are so few situations where you find yourself facing an opponent with exactly the same number of auto-successes so that your contest really comes down to wits, proper use of abilities and the roll of the dice. Fights can basically be scripted out by comparing Epic Attributes and ... that just feels boring to me.

    I'm a professor! Why is no one listening to me?!

Indeed, Zeus might just be better at Vessel (the self-shapeshift part of Shaping) because he's spend a lot of time doing that and does it all the time.

            To my mind, it moves flavor out of the mechanics more and leaves the mechanical bits in and makes them the thing you pay points for.

                Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                The problem with the system is that it made fights that should have been cooler into one hit fights. It made specific challenges either impossible or a cakewalk and there was no in between. The exponential increase basically meant that unless you were equal in legend with equal stats you were either going to steam roll or lose. Which kind of makes a number of issues a problem. In my opinion, I would rather have Boons and Knacks providing the difference in power rather than attributes.
                I only experienced those problems early on. While the system has a vast number of flaws, the primary failing in this circumstance is not making the necessary options readily apparent and easily accessible. Some of those options were unbalanced themselves. It required too much work for your average Storyteller to create fair and balanced encounters, and this should be addressed in future editions. But it was entirely possible.

                The core features are being blamed, when in this case it is rules obfuscation that should be blamed.
                As players, you can expect your storyteller to go through the following combat phases:
                1. Enemies are way too easy!
                2. Enemies are way too hard!
                2a. The boring super-soak monster.
                2b. The demoralizing guy you can never hit.
                3. Not enough diversity in the combat (some players are left twiddling their thumbs).
                4. Too much enemy crowd control (some players are left twiddling their thumbs).
                5. Finally achieve the sweet spot.
