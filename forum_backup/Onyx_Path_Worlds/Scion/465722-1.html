    Pretty much.

    EDIT: To explain.. the example PC's and all the monsters are designed aesthetically. The big, tough, strong guys have tons of Strength and no Dexterity. Makes sense, right? Except... that in the combat system Scion uses, that means they will never hit anything. Sure, if they somehow did, it would be devastating... but they won't. Ever. It's not actually possible. They can try to run around that with the Clinch maneuver, but it has pretty serious drawbacks, and it means a Strength-based character (PC, giant, monster, etc) will always and forever have only one option in combat. Which makes that terribly boring for a player, and terribly predictable in NPCs.

    The example characters and the monsters are given all the dots they need to meet their concept... but none of the ones they need to succeed mechanically. There are very definite ways to build a character wrong in Scion, which is unfortunate. It should be totally viable to be a Strength-heavy, Dexterity-minimal character! That's a valid mythological archetype! It shows up all over, in all kinds of media. But it does not work in this system, because they will never hit anything, ever. It's similarly impossible to build a poison-using character, or a beast-master character, or any number of other very common character types. The system just doesn't support them. After Legend 3, it doesn't support firearms. You have to go with bows or thrown weapons, because Firearms don't get any Strength added to their damage. After Legend 3, that becomes a serious problem. Scion also doesn't support turtle builds, where you laugh off any damage. There are too many ways to break through it. Defense-centric is not a good way to go.

    If you already know Scion in and out, you can build a very powerful character... provided you go only for the Boons, Knacks, Attributes and Abilities that actually work. If you don't know which ones are good and which ones are bad, it's very easy to build yourself a really impressive character with tons of powers... that will never accomplish anything.

                        And keep it combat focused.

                        Social characters are either omni-powerful or nothing at all. And all the stats bleed together. There's really no difference between a Manipulation and Charisma character except which Knacks they get. Anything one can do, the other can as well. The difference is how they stunt/describe it. So social characters will step all over each other. And social characters can pretty much do ANYTHING social: Want to walk into the White House and make the President your best friend? Check. Want to talk the other players into being your willing slaves? Check. Want to go on TV and convince everyone that blue should be the same as green from now on? Check. Etc. All that is within reach at HERO and effortless at starting Demigod. And trust me, is sounds much much much better than it is. Also, no real rules for what happens when you have a "dance off" between two social characters. Add to that, that a physical primary character can effectively "minor" in social stats and be as powerful in the social arena as a character who specializes in it (since they only need to invest in one stat to cover all the same ground).

                        Storytellers and players can smooth some of that out with a lot of cooperation and hand-waving, plus some attempts to nudge things via some House Rules. But chances are good it will get irksome at some point.

                        Mental characters are pretty much nothing at all. It's not worth the time to try it. "Smart" gods are one of the archetypes that the game just doesn't support, or it doesn't really do anything to make them worth playing as there are no real rules about what Epic Intelligence, etc can actually DO and the Knacks are less than stellar. Oh, except for some combat adds here and there.

                            I haven't run into this in any of the games I've run. I use resistance rolls to resist mental traits and I have specific rules regarding the separate social and mental traits, and what they do. For example, there's a character who has ridiculous Wits and Integrity, so it's incredibly difficult to enter his mind and use the social machinations. There's also a Manipulation heavy character who is crap at being social, but can just manipulate really well, so the band just uses them if they need someone to be quickly interrogated using not-so-ethical means. As well, if you have an Artistry or Forge Purview, you can do some really cool things with Epic Intelligence and Academics/Art.

                            I've also found a lot of players will understand the limitations of their own character's personality, so a character who is really Charismatic and honest may not even think about doing something manipulative or two different mental characters use it in different ways, even when they both have Epic Intelligence - like one being a physicist and the other an archaeologist.

                            You can fix it and it can be really fun and rewarding for players who specialize in that.

                                I find it greatly rewarding to offer, uh rewards, to players who play to type or theme.

                                One of Thor's kids who wanted to be a 'baby Thor' and follow in dad's footsteps is going to have some trouble hitting. Obviously, if the player didn't want to take Epic Dexterity, something like Animal Aspect is going to help with that but that's just cherry picking a single boon in a purview they otherwise have no interest in and it's kind of discordant. If the player came up with an inventive way to level the playing field with the thematic powers and birthrights at the character's disposal (and there are some great ways to do that) I'd reward them for being inventive.

                                That also helps in the matter of social powers, which are terrifying. A starting character, right off the bat, with God's Honest and little else could probably get to anyone they wanted to with a little basic planning and some roleplay. At that point they can convince that someone (the president, Donald Trump, Zayn Malik) of just about anything. But if I know what the 'theme' of the characters are I can at least plan ahead. Not enough. I mean you pretty quickly lose any semblance of control of how the Scions walk in the world. But I can at least have some idea what to expect and I can make requests. Like 'Pleasepleaseplease, I would prefer that nobody makes characters who want to go straight out and mind control the president.'. Sooner or later the public is going to find out, and the social characters are going to earn their money in smoothing things along, but it's easier for me as a ST when they're the good guys and not some kind of divine occupying force. And those players who play to their theme I reward because it's like me saying 'Thank you for playing in a way I can kind of predict and thus design things with you in mind'.

                                                            This is supposedly not just directed to someone new to Scion as either a ST or player, but to someone that hasn't been a ST before, right? I thought that was what the OP was saying.

                                                            I am pretty confident from Charles's previous posts that he has scads of RPG game experience and StarkJr,, I haven't seen as much from you, but you are speaking with a voice that seems freighted with that same weight. I am pretty confidant that you two can both swing running a game that has huge functionality problems, either by handwaving away where RAW doesn't work, by adapting the House Rules of others and/or making up your own. I wouldn't reccomend a new ST put him/herself in that position. Handwaving away the rules is a difficult thing to do. And, for all the amazing work they did, JSR and Scion 2.0 (HR) are, essentially untested House Rules. Both groups have run great games and some of their stuff is damn good, but it's either a complete re-write that has uncovered and created new problems of it's own (take a look at the JSR Fatebinding rules for an example of something that is more detailed but VERY unwieldy). And, Iry, et al. have gotten a significant way into their rewrite, but it is by no means done. Hence, stitching together multiple rules systems. And, until there's a complete set of rules and some play-testing around with groups other than those writing the rules, it is, essentially a meticulously done set of House Rules/Labor of Love. (The kind of play-testing that Scion never got.) Using other people's idiosyncratic rules and adapting them to his/her own game is another thing that a new ST might have issues with. I have seen VERY experienced ST who love tinkering with rules just get up and walk away in disgust at certain points in Scion and done so myself. And that's after six very successful Scion chronicles I have run and one I have played in.

                                                            I LOVE the basic concepts of Scion. They are 100% in my wheelhouse. I scoured used book stores for weird myth books before Scion was a gleam in White Wolf's eye. My MA thesis and PhD dissertation proposal has massive comparative mythology components. Ask Telgar about how insanely into this game and the source material I am. (For that matter, ask Anne about it, where I was the "Hittite" guy - I actually have pictures of the cuneiform tablets with all the Hittite myths on them from Istanbul as part of my screen saver on my TV .)

                                                            For a new guy who wants to run Scion, which I FULLY support him doing, I would recommend the following to mitigate all of the above:
                                                            > Use the pantheon write-ups from Scion 2.0 HR (Iry, et al.) when and where you can. If you can get access to the JSR ones use those to plug the holes after Iry. I may have my minor quibbles with their choices here and there, but they are GOOD, SOLID and BETTER than the ones from RAW
                                                            > Use Scion 2.0 HR (Iry, et. al.) to expand the Purviews when and where you can. Same caveat about JSR there.
                                                            > Make sure every character has a combat function, even if they also have a non-combat emphasis. More "Athena" than "Hephaestus". Pure combat characters could easily dominate the party and/or the party could all be combatants. That plays to the strengths of RAW. And it obviates most of the need for the rest of the concerns. Plus it's a damn fun game all told. You might need to make sure you've got some decent "gun-fu" rules if anyone wants to play a gun-bunny or sniper type.
                                                            > Use the basic essential rule's fixes, like: eliminating Hardness, fixing Untouchable Opponent, maybe something to make ranged combat characters worthwhile (Perception adds is the fast and dirty way to do this), add in some kind of mental resistance. Those are the primary ones I can think of off the top of my head.

                                                            I think you could have a rocking good time with just those fixes up to the Demigod divide. And maybe there might be more official stuff out there to help out by then.

                                                            Or wait until the new Scion system is out and try that one out. Just run something else in the interim. We're doing that right now in my group waiting for Mage20. It sucks, but it is giving me loads of time to work on the next game and we are having fun playing something that we normally wouldn't (Vampire).

                                                            Honestly, I'd just TRY Scion, either RAW (that's mostly he way I ran the first one + of my Scion games) or with some of the fixes in place up front. But I can be relatively impatient.
