    I would not rule out Titianic Virtues out just yet, as they are used as a major indicator of Titan behaviour towards behaviour and negotiations in the Titanomachy book itself...despite the new Titans lacking listed Virtues and an attached Purview.
    I assume that the Purview connected to a Titanic Virtue is this 'dominant Purview' that is teased in Demigod, but that preview is as of yet, unreleased. Furthermore, the denizen book ("Saints and Monsters" iirc) is called out in the first Demigod preview as having the rules for Titan Scions, so we don't even have all of that yet.

    As for what 'makes a Titan'; one of the points made throughout 2nd edition, is that it is primarily political, though the behaviour of Titans does follow a general trend of being more removed from the human experience, humanities concerns and to a lesser degree, less empathic in general.
    The Adversary & Tyrant Callings strike me as particularly sociopathic. I'm not saying that having one makes you psychotic, just that other people's needs are a lower priority for you if you have one of these as the foundation of your identity.

    Admittedly, Adversary does require a certain degree of caring for others; it just does it in a "f@#$ you" kind of way.


    Edit: By contrast,the Primeval Calling is more about transcendence and being phenomena, so it is not exactly good for relating to things that aren't aspects of reality, ie Purviews. I think Helios has this Calling, and that is why he forgets that the sun can harm the mortals he interacts with occasionally.
    Likewise Monster is very much about being non-human and having quasi-animalistic traits. The condition that comes with the Calling reminds me of how Denizens are meant to separate themselves from humanity, so this Calling comes off as the most empathic...even if the Monstrous Urge is to trying make you eat people.

    It should be noted that Titanomachy isn't a book about most of the Titans, but the ones that are the most organized, active opposition to the Hero book pantheons. These are the people on the other side of the cold war. There is a reason Rhea, Nezumo and Elatha aren't in this book.
    While two of those people are on good terms with their pantheons, Nezumo is one I called out because (as far as i can tell) it isn't ACTIVELY hostile, rather it only accidentally causes problems while following it's Virtues (Fcundity and Submission - Earth) and it's Monster calling. He just is, and sometimes people get in his way.

                        Okay, let's do this. I got my hard copy out for referencing purposes.
                        • Nezumo is found on page 79 of the Hero book.
                        • Rhea is listed on page 151 of Book 2: Hero.
                        • Elatha is on page 163 of Hero.

                        They are all Titans in cannon.

                        Now I am not in anyway involved in the development of any Scion products yet, but I do think the reason the Titans in Titanmomachy are so different is that they are built as potential parents, rather than entities players may have to thwart. I do think they should have Virtues listed and their (Dominion?) associated Purview called out, but Titans can and do have more than one Purview in this edition. Heck Cernunnos the Titan has more Purviews than Cernunnos of the Nemetondevos!

                        Demigod Kickstarter Preview 2A mentions Dominion as a kind of communion with a Purview, and much like how I think Callings will scale up by tier, Divine Tier (4) Dominions are something I see heavily connected to Titans at God-level play. Neall calls out the expression of Dominion at Demigod level as 'casual Miracles'. If one can only have a single Dominion Purview, then that is where we get the Purview associated with the Titanic Virtues. So the 'single' Purview listed for Titans, is just the one associated with their Titan Virtues.

                        Titanomachy 'updates' a few Titans from the Hero book, but as noted above, it misses others. Additions have been put in here too.

                        The Aesir in Hero have a 'Notable Titspawn section, which lists Nidhoggr (the draconic spelling) and Vargr by name. Ymir is listed as a Primordial. Jord and Surtr are not mentioned, so they are introduced in Titanomachy.
                        The only Titan/Asura listed as such in the Deva section of Hero is Ravanna, who might be the mantle Saita is trying to claim, (as she is clearly still in the Demigod tier). There are hints in both books that an Incarnation may have survived. Ironically Rangda is the only Titan in Titanomachy can see the Orisha/Loa try to take down on their own. Of course I also see them willing to go after Baba Yaga.
                        Fujin, Raijin and Nezumo are the only Kami Titans listed in the appropriate section of Hero. In my rush to list Titans that aren't pa try t of the Titanomachy, I missed that Nezumo and Namazu are gender alternate mantles of the same myth. My mistake. Still, Goku and Princess Kiyo are Titanomachy only Titans. Although Kiyo and Ao Guang should also have Dragon mantles in my opinion.
                        All the Manitou Titans are listed in Hero and Titanomachy. Fantastic! Full write ups for everyone!
                        Same with the Netjer.
                        The Shen only list Ao Guang and White Eyebrow in Hero, so Kuafu and Su Daji are likewise without known Dominion Purviews and Virtues.
                        Coyoxauhqui is the only Teotl Titan in Hero and the one used in the listed example of why knowing a Titan's Virtues matter for roleplaying them in chapter 2.
                        The Theoi write up in Hero lists Cronus, Phoebe (who got erratta'd), Rhea and Typhon in order. So Echidna and Gaea are technically Titanomachy is their first appearances.
                        The Hero section on the Tuatha Titans lists Balor, Bres, Crom Cruach, (my first choice for an antagonistic Titan not connected to the Titanomachy, but Elatha was another example of a 'good Titan', so I mistakenly went with Nezumo instead) and Domnu. Fun fact, Hero lists Domnu's Purview as Darkness and her Titanomachy write up doesn't have it. Belenus and Cernunnos aren't listed in Hero but are mantles rebuilt from the dead Nemetondevos. So no Virtues for them either and another Pantheon of enemies to boot.

                        There are references to additional Titans in the texts (heck Prometheus has 3 scions in the antagonists section of Titanomachy) but that is less 'actionable'.

                                        The point on Titanomachy is that, if they want to respect the myths, the idea of a Titanomachy is bad because most of the pantheons have no such thing as Titans, they may have some adversary gods or an old generation of gods that are not part of the pantheon, or even an adversary pantheon that they may had a long story of adversity, but it’s hard to call any of them are Titans.

                                        The name Titanomachy is a reference mainly to the idea on 1e, that the gods are fighting a war against one powerful adversary that can face all of them at same time. This adversary never existed, the gods are there because they defeated all their adversaries or are waiting for the final war (or a combination of both).

                                        The resulting is a book about antagonists centered in the premise that some old gods (called Titans for simplification) are trying to take over the Gods. With that in mind you have the titanspawn (that was the general term for monsters on 1e), that are monsters aligned to the Titans.

                                        But a great point in the history is that the truth is told by the winners, and the gods are no exception, they are not good, they are not perfect, they are the ones telling the stories, and so, their enemies are the most horrible people in the world.

                                        The titans are the defeated enemies that may, or may not, be horrible creatures, that may or may not be willing to over throw the gods, that may or may not use their worst weapon to destroy everything as long as they win the war. That is what the gods are telling...

                                        If you want to make them the real monsters to let the players kill them with no guilty, ok. I personally prefer to make them proper adversaries that you may sympathize before defeating them or even side with them against “those warmonger gods”. I think those are a much better approach than it was in 1e, while it still gives a nice structure to those that like the good versus evil thing, with a bunch of “evil gods” and monsters to fight.

                                        If you go to DriveThruRPG and read the reviews of Titanomachy you have the other side of the complaining, that the Titans are forced to exist just to fill the gap left from 1e, that it goes against the myths, etc.

                                            If being a Titan is a political thing and the "war" is conducted from two sides of the same coin -- I think the way the Sabbat and the Pure are presented are great examples of how antagonists can be written with a lot of nuance (and playability) but still clearly be the "bad guys."

                                            The way the Pure evolved in WtF is a great example of this. Even though it took many, many books to finally get one dedicated to the Pure (and really make them playable), it was clear from the core what their motivations were, how they organized, how they self-identified, and, ultimately, the nature of their role as antagonists.

                                            Ultimately, you can have nuance and sophisticated enemies even if they basically look a lot like the protagonists. The key is spending the wordcount to present the case to the reader and making sure the authors all have the same vision for whats going on.

                                            Part of the problem I see here is that players are having to read between the lines and come to their own conclusions. People are already looking at rewrites. That really shouldnt be, especially in a book that is supposed to define the Titans and their threat. Some clear language right at the top seems appropriate.
