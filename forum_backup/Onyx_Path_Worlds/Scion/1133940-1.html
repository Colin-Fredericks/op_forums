                    I know very little about Andine cultures in general, but I think to go from "only deity of a pantheon attested by name" to "anti-god who killed all others" is quite the leap. But then again, paying more attention to the historical shifts in the World than to any speculative events among the gods is very much my personal approach and not an objective necessity. What I would look more closely at, though, is the question of how exactly Ai Apaec has come to be the only attested Moche god.

                    A quick look at some titles on Google Books gives me this picture of the situation: The Moche, like all South American cultures, did not have a system of writing, and their culture disappeared almost a thousand years before the arrival of the Spanish. Any names for their gods or kings or whatever are thus, necessarily, made-up. Many figures in their art are simply given descriptive names, but Ai Apaec is a title taken from a Colonial Era dictionary of the language spoken by the Chimú, the supposed cultural successors of the Moche (for whom a pantheon of their own seems to be attested, at least in outlines). It is an epithet meaning "Mighty", and has been applied by 20th-Century scholars to a corpus of figures in Moche art that is supposed to depict a highest, or possibly the singular, god of the Moche. The exact role and nature of this figure is contested, as is the question of which depictions are actually part of the corpus and which aren't. His by-name, "Decapitator", seems to be modern as well, and is related to the sacrificial rituals of the Moche, as well as some depicitions showing Ai Apaec with a cut-off head. I would liken this more to depicitions of the Aztec gods directly receiving the blood of their sacrificial victims, not to divine in-fighting. So, instead of Ai Apaec being the only Moche god still existing, I would rather ask: Does he himself exist at all? Or is he a scholarly construct?

                    But like I said, my knowledge on this region is limited, and thus I can't make any judgement calls on what is approriate and inappropriate either. I do think, however, that ascribing the worship of a tyrannical monster to certain "lost cultures" is certainly a trope that has been overdone. We are, after all, looking at what we assume to be the centre of a culture's religious life, not Cthulhu dead and dreaming. If you want to pursue the idea of Ai Apaec as a warrior eternally battling further, though, you could have a look at this digital museum exhibition, which attempts to reconstruct a myth cycle (of course entirely speculative) from the corpus of Ai-Apaec-depicitions: https://artsandculture.google.com/exhibit/QQW92VFa
