    Because comic books, like most adventure fiction [and mythology!], are based around larger-than-life characters. Why? Because it's not likely that a comic about Joe, the pizza guy, would reach a wide market among super-hero comic readers [readers of indie comics are a different thing, of course. They might love it!]. His stories wouldn't have the same level of drama and mythological adventure, and if they did, then you aren't really playing "mortal Joe, the pizza guy" anymore. And a game about the same pizza guy wouldn't require a game like Scion to simulate it; you would probably be better off using something more grounded in "reality". It would also be a completely different type of story than the adventures of the Scions of five Gods/Goddesses who are drawn together to prevent disaster and change the world itself.

    My point is, you don't need Scion-type rules to simulate this, if that's what you want to play. There's nothing wrong with playing it, if that's what you like - different strokes and all that. But why ask if we can get Scion-level rules for playing said pizza guy, or a mundane worshiper of some real-world religion? That's not what the game is supposed to be about, because it's being designed to enable players to take the roles of mythical Scions. And it's about the great religions of the past, and the myths and legends surrounding their Gods and Goddesses [and Scions, monsters, wizards, and others] - if this is the game you want to play, then the Abrahamic faiths should most probably be no more important than the other faiths, and possibly even less so. And if you want to play with angels, saints, and the real-world gods, that's an entirely different theme, and maybe Scion isn't the best foil for what you want.

As a game, Scion has to make some compromises in different situations to represent different Deities. Some situations are inherently too theologically complex to weave into a game line that has any semblance of balance, therefore the Gods in Scion are not purely the 'Real World Gods.'

                                    For example, a Deva, Theoi, Teotl, and Dia are all fundamentally different beings. Different cultures have different conceptions of what a Deity 'is' fundamentally. A Dia (Irish) is a being that is very, very capable of dying. A being that exists at a finominal level of power, but is dwarfed by many other Pantheons. Theoi are truely immortal, and a Dia is not. The Deva are hugely, cosmically powerful, while a Dia is very much not.

                                    However, for the sake of balance, all of these Pantheons are equalized effectively. All Gods are Gods, and that is something that is not true in the real world. Deities from one culture to another are fundamentally different, sometimes to a massive degree. Everyone who is Legend 12, be they Lugh or Vishnu, are roughly batting at the same level, and that is very much a construction of Scion as a game.

                                    So, I believe that is what Purple Snit was discussing with dividing Scion-Gods and Real-World-Gods. The Gods in Scion are beings inspired maybe 99% (probably more like 85%) from the real world, but the actual nitty gritty details can't be properly represented in the game if it is going to be balanced. (I have tried, and brainstormed ways to divine up the Pantheons more for accuracy, but it just breaks down. The big thing is the Dia and Deva exist on opposite ends of a power spectrum which just would not contribute to a fun environment for players)

                                    Purple Snit is also probably addressing the ideas that we get in the Scion Books of Gods limelighting as mortals sometimes, which, depending on the Pantheon, a God may never, ever, ever do. But, for the sake of the game, a compromise is made with the original 'Real World God' to fiddle with the cannonical truth of the Deity to make it work within Scion. Same with how Scion goes, "Er, sure, okay, the Irish Pantheon is kicking around and not (almost) all dead." just little deviations from the cannonical truth of the real world to make the game work.
