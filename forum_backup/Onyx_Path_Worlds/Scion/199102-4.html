                        To be frank -- and not to be flippant -- this is a role-playing game we're discussing. If conflicts are actually being resolved by players actually acting their characters, and doing it well...well, things are working exactly as intended. That's true whether a given character is mentally, socially, or physically oriented. If conflicts and plot points are being resolved exclusively on the basis of random and semi-random happenstance (i.e. dice rolling), there's something seriously wrong with the storyteller, their players, or the nature of the game being played.

                        Physical characters, meanwhile, are eminently capable of derailment -- just as much if not more than social and mental counterparts. Sometimes, all it takes is one stupid comment by a character that should have kept their mouths shut, or a player/character deciding it's time to start smashing/shooting/casting when it isn't, to completely throw an entire game session or story -- and, in a goodly number of circumstances, the consequences of physical characters screwing up a social scene can and do have higher consequences than the converse situation.

                        This is true regardless of game or mechanics. Scion for its systemic quirks and nuances is no exception to that.
