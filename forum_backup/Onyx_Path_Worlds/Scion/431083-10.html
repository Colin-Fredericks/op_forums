    171) So a relatively minor deity in your pantheon is trying to raise their public profile (and hopefully their Legend) through media based (loosely) upon their adventures. Movies, comics, and the like are certainly a viable way to get your name and face out there.

    Problem is, these works are starting to associate the god in question with powers and adventures that are rightfully associated with other gods. They're taking notice, and they aren't happy to share the stage with a "lesser" deity. Time to smooth ruffled feathers before things get tetchy (and by "tetchy," we mean "divine lightning bolts").

    172) Everyone knows that death isn't necessarily the final word when you're a god. Not that that overcoming it is a cakewalk.

    One of the long-deceased deities from your pantheon is in need of resurrection. The resident god of prophecy claims their presence will be vital to victory over your pantheon's foes. So, lucky you -- your "final exam" for advancement to godhood is to restore one of these deceased deities.

    No word about how you're supposed to do that, or what the dead god's former rivals are likely to do about it (note to self: do not invite Tsukuyomi to Uke-Mochi's welcome-back banquet)... if you want to be a god, this is the kind of thing you have to deal with...

                    176) The Muspel and the Jotun, two races who seem like they shouldn't even exist within the same city as each other have done the unthinkable, they had a kid together! Now, a brand new species of giant has been brought into the Nine Realms, with skin as strong as iron and blood like boiling water. The fate of this newborn "steam giant" is unknown this early in its development. But the Ragnarok claims the giants will destroy the Norse pantheon, so they don't trust the little abomination just on principle. Still, there may be some way to turn this to their advantage. Loki may be a god now, but he was originally born a fire giant. Perhaps the gods can accomplish something similar with this one?

                    Long story short, Odin is sending you to kidnap a newborn giant with unexplored powers who is being guarded by both a Jotun mother and a Muspel father. It also doesn't help that they are constantly surrounded by both fire and frost giants who anxiously want to get a look at the new hybrid. Good luck with that!

                                                            184) You have a half brother, a mortal who's known about your Scion heritage for nearly as long as you have. And while they outwardly put up a front of being supportive of you, your divine senses could easily tell they were always jealous of your, quite literally, god given talents. It's a sibling's job to compete with each other for dominance, but that was an impossible rivalry, given your backgrounds. That is until recently, however, when your brother announced their debut in a grandiose fashion. Somehow, they managed to get in touch with the giants and have been drinking heavy quantities of their blood in the hopes of being able to keep pace with you. And now, their binge drinking has paid off; your sibling has become a full fledged giant, a cyclops!

                                                            Now, they're on some kind of power trip, eager to prove they can be just as great a hero as you. Better, even, since they've been studying how you work and think you're a bit too sloppy. Unfortunately, Fate isn't content to just let this turn of events go unnoticed. It seems that Zeus is not pleased with your sibling, nobody should consider themselves equal to the children of Olympus, especially not some wannabe Scion turned Titanspawn.

                                                            The king of the Gods wants your brother in chains, enslaved in some forge under Olympus and forced to produce lightning bolts for use against Gaia's armies. But even though you and your brother have been rivals, there hasn't actually been a case of bad blood between the two of you. You have no reason to hate them, other than the fact they aren't pretty enough to meet Olympus' standards of quality.

                                                            So now you face a quandary; do you obey your king and enslave your own brother for the crime of doing what comes naturally to siblings, or do you attempt to persuade Zeus to give them a lighter sentence in hopes of preventing your rivalry from escalating into something more violent?
