    The Deva have rarely in myth gone about making pronouncements to the people at large... they don't even have a properly coded priest class... yes, the Brahmins exist but it's not like they have an official handbook or anything... the rituals and practices of a Brahmin in one village and another a few villages over can be wildly different, even with respect to the same God.

    Hinduism also has no official 'oracle' type role, no individual who channels and or interprets the will of the Gods. Hindu astrology is a thing that exists but that is less 'will of the Gods' and more 'effects of the planetary cosmic rays' and is geared more to manipulating those rays through gemstones for various benefits... it's a whole pseudo-science dedicating to telling the planets to screw themselves.

    Divine revelations are given to individuals, but almost exclusively to people who were looking for it in the first place... the Gods don't proselytize. People do, and there are stories of non-believing mortals being inspired by devout individuals and getting visions from Gods themselves, but the Gods don't go out of their way to convert people... they're Gods, they may enjoy the worship, but they don't need it.

    (This is a giant departure from Vedic era Hinduism, where the Deva are practically the Teotl in how much they need and want sacrifice and the reciprocal relationship between Gods and Man is paramount... but discussing the massive changes that religions go through and the drastic ways in which Gods change over time is out of the scope of this thread )

    Instead, because of the above, when Deva seek to influence the world, their most common avenue is Incarnations. Quite a few historical figures are considered Incarnations of various Gods, and while this is generally done to religious figures, I don't see why a Deva couldn't Incarnate as a politician or a sports star or a world renowned musician or scientist. This has an added advantage... the Deva as Gods are often expected to be paragons, to some extent detached from the foibles and bonds of humanity... Incarnations, being human, are expected to and even encouraged to be human and have human traits, which makes their stories more interesting than 'perfect God does everything perfectly'.

    As a side note, because Nyrufa brought this up, the caste system is totally a thing that exists, despite the government's ban on caste based discrimination long ago... for right-hand path Hindus, who believe that the caste system is God given. Left-hand path Hindus (myself included) gave up on caste based distinctions hundreds of years ago, also citing Godly revelation. Neither side has been hit with the wrath of God yet, so the only real reason to go with one over the other is how big a bag of dicks you want the Gods to be, and while it's fine if you want the Deva to be a bag of dicks, that position has no more religious validity than the other one... not to mention the many myths where Gods themselves don't care about caste, like the one where Lakshmi abandons Vishnu over her visiting an untouchable devotee or Sarasvati Incarnating herself into a Chandala family to make a point and eventually rising to Goddesshood again as Matangi. Idols of the Supreme Mother Durga must be made with a handful of dirt from a prostitute's courtyard, and the Tantrik followers of Kali (including yours truly) deny all distinction of caste and even allow women to be priestesses and spiritual guides, which most Right-Hand traditions do not. Most Bhakti traditions following Vishnu and Shiva also do not discriminate on caste lines or discard the system altogether, though being a Shakta I'm less familiar with them, so even mythically as well as religiously there is plenty of precedent for the Deva dumping the caste sytem in the cesspit it belongs.

    Speaking of cesspits, most priests in (at least my corner of) India don't use Ganges water anymore. For my father's last rites, the priest just spoke the names of the seven sacred rivers over a bottle of tap water and we used that for the ceremony. This is a modern innovation to circumvent the toxic water because, you know, we're not blind and know perfectly well that the water of the actual Ganges is a death sentence (also an example of how religions innovate). Also, instead of providing the priest a cow as a gift as is traditionally required in the sacred texts (since the apartment he lives in doesn't allow pets) I handed him a hundred bucks and officially announced to the Gods that the hundred bucks should be considered a cow in their eyes. And so it was done Religions change and adapt with the times, they wouldn't survive otherwise.

    Also, I'd just like to remind people that the Deva Virtues are Duty and Conscience after all. And I don't know if it was intentional or not but they do correspond pretty perfectly to right hand path and left hand path Hinduism. Given the level of research evinced by Mendez' work so far, I am tending towards it being intentional
