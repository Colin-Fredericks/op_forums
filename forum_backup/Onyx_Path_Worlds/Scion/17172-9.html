            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
            I think that's missing the problem most players have when presented with Mage significantly. One of the biggest problems in the Mage games is that players are immediately tossed into the deep end. Instead of slowly being introduced to freeform magic the system plops freeform magic in their laps and tells them to go figure it out. Awakening handles this better than Ascension by having a more methodical effect creation system, though it is more voluminous for it; creating a different problem of overwhelming new players with text.

            But that's also why I didn't just port Mage into Scion. As both Dataweaver and I have both stated (in different forms) Scion's tiered scale offers an excellent solution to that problem. Freeform gets introduced slowly.

            Heroes don't do freeform magic on the fly; they rely on Boons with a system for creating custom Boons for players that can't find one in the examples they like for their concept. In my hack I removed the Magic Purview (it become rather redundant) and ritual magic at Hero level was their entry to freeform. Thus Hero level play would rarely use freeform in the middle of action. Players would either create custom Boons outside of the session (reducing "deer in headlights" significantly by removing them being on the spot) or magic rituals would be a back-up mode of power that would get brought out when none of their accessible powers were capable of getting past an obstacle (giving the freeform use a very specific goal, making designing it in the moment much easier and because it would be time consuming in-character, players are generally more comfortable with it having to walk through it a bit slowly at first out-of-character).

            Demigods get to a more balanced place where magic-associated characters (aka characters that are opting into the freeform system early) can start accessing freeform power fast enough and of great enough effect that it represents a viable alternative to Boon based powers, but Boons remain a potent and viable focus for a character. Boon based Demigod builds can still fall back on freeform as it has scaled up, and when they do it becomes apparent that over investment in Boons is a bit of a waste of resources as they can freeform lesser effects as they think of them rather than having to buy a lower level Boon every time they have a new idea.

            Gods get into where Mage usually starts: freeform as the default mode of play with Boons playing the role of powers you are particularly good at. Your old Boons are still perfectly useful and solid powers, but you're unlikely to purchase new ones or design new ones; save for divine kids that might need a hand (and going back to Hero level play, NPC divine parents or birthright mentors can tutor a PC through crafting a new Boon allowing the ST to walk the player through the process at the same time). At this point you've have lots of exposure to freeform powers by either using them occasionally as needed or watching other players engage in the system to start wrapping your head around it. You're playing a god of fire, you want to do X with fire, so you use the freeform system to figure out the mechanics of accomplishing X.
            There are three parts to a freeform system to consider here: customizing existing powers (see Awakening’s Spell Factors), accessing powers that you haven’t specifically purchased (see the distinction between spells and rotes in Awakening), and creating new powers on the fly. The “creating new powers” aspect is the most problematic of the three.

            As you’ve pointed out, I agree that Scion has a unique opportunity to introduce freeform elements in a gradual manner, allowing players to ease themselves into it. For me, I’d keep most freeform elements out of the Hero tier; I’d introduce the ability to customize existing powers at the Demigod tier; and I’d add access to powers you haven’t purchased to the God tier. I’d reserve the ability to create new powers for scions who specialize in doing so rather than it being something everyone does; but I wouldn’t make that be a tier-specific thing.

                            To keep on the Awakening analogy:

                            Hero and Demigod use OOC design for instant casting, and on-the-fly for extended casting. Boons would be set powers either picked from examples or made with OOC design that are powers the Scions can call on immediately. Ritual magic allows for on-the-fly creation where the Scions can try to channel powers they haven't learned. The OOC designed Boons are not something the character is creating, but something the player/ST are creating because they want something not in the example powers. The on-the-fly rituals are something the characters are designing.

                            The primary distinction between Hero and Demigod would be that the on-the-fly ritual system at Hero level isn't competitive with Boons in terms of power and efficiency unless a character is designed heavily around making use of it and even then the practical restrictions make it limited in application. At Demigod, magic focused characters with their on-the-fly rituals can be more competitive with Boons.

                            To attempt an analogy from another WW game: At Hero level, on-the-fly is more like Thaumaturgy in Exalted, and a Demigod it gets up to being Sorcery in Exalted. Then at the God tier, the division between Charms and Sorcery is removed with on-the-fly becoming the default and known Boons getting scaled up to stay relevant.
