In the span of a single human life time (less even) Heracles went from infant to Olympian. The game itself is built very much on that trajectory. A player character is expected to be able to go from exceptional human (pre-visitation) to god (end of game level play) within a relatively short amount of time.                     I think the biggest and most obvious problem with the idea of Uncle Sam-as-deity is that historically he's never been worshiped. Nor has Paul Bunyan, or Pecos Bill, or any of the pseudo-nationalist figures in any of the made-up pantheons. This cheapens the overall nature of godhood and purview for some people, which can obviously have deleterious effects on their feelings toward the game.

                    It's worth noting that the Greeks had one of the most successful empires in history and as such would have to have had some of the most successful heroes in history. The patronage of their kings and war heroes tended to be re-attributed to a god if they became significantly great. Herakles was, by all accounts, significantly great. He sacked cities, married princesses, was a king, had 100 children, helped found a dozen more cities, and only the Christian cultists of the early eras bothered to try to squeeze all of this chronologically into a single human lifetime. The Greek sources are far less precise in measuring his period of activity.

                    Greek heroes underwent apotheosis with startling frequency, particularly if they were successful in founding a major city (Hercules actually kind of stands out as the only one never to have founded his own city).

                        From Scion: Companion at the beginning of the World at War Section -


                        If you’re feeling a little apprehensive about adding a historical setting to your game, we offer this simple advice: don’t sweat the details. Nobody’s going to test you on facts, you don’t need to memorize any dates and your high school history teacher isn’t going to grade your character’s back-story. For most groups of players, the fun will come from establishing the feeling of the war rather than from maintaining strict historical accuracy—and it’ll be a lot easier that way, too.

                        By the same token, this section is not meant to deliver an accurate representation of the historical war. World War Two is a vast subject which has been the focus of thousands of books, and there are simply not enough pages here to do justice to the real-life triumphs, catastrophes, heroics and horrors of the war. Furthermore, this section presents an alternate history—that is, history viewed through the lens of mythology. Just as when playing in a modern setting, Scion in the context of World War Two diverges from “real life” as we know it to follow the epic tradition, complete with heroes who perform amazing deeds, villains with supernatural powers and pointy-toothed monsters that would happily munch on any of the above.
                        Bolding is mine, for emphasis.

                        Seriously, it's not meant to be an accurate representation of history. So the fact that they are not worshiped is utterly irrelevant to them not appearing or Besty Ross never sewing a flag, nor her being on the same level as Kali. They literally made new myth out of it, and I think it's pretty cool. I totally get that it's not everyone's cup of tea, but to say it degrades Scion in some way is just simply a matter of opinion, and hardly universal. You don't like Vampire Dark Ages? don't use it. You don't like Forgotten Realms? Don't use it. Same here.

                        I mean, seriously, how is 5 Scion commandos sneaking into a Nazi castle disguised as no less than Himmler, Goebbels, Hitler, and their personal guards (complete with guy who can only say Nein! in German) to rescue 500 POWs any less mythic than the Trojan Horse? How is a Scion of John Bull with a mystic bulldog (mystic mastiff?) named Winston ripping the hatch of a tank and leaping from ship to ship under heavy gunfire less awesome than Odysseus taking down a cyclops? How is scion of Uncle Sam named Ulysses Samuel Adams who can summon forth his own personal commandos with the flag over Fort McHenry braving the North Sea and the power of a Soul-powered ship with nothing but his red, white, and blue swim trunks to sabotage it mythic and legendary than Perseus taking down a Medusa? How is a Scion of Robin Hood dodging the bullets of 100 Germans and making an entire battlefield look at him so that the rest can get into position not the stuff of legends? Just because they don't have worshipers in the Real World for a fictional game?

                        Maybe not in your world, but mine... totally happened, and awesome.

                                            I feel like you're shifting the goal posts here.                                             There's also some pretty faulty thinking that goes into a line of thought that comes down to being invested in the game supporting only your preferred modality of play. Did the Scion Companion's national Pantheons ever actually ruin anyone's game by virtue of existing?

Why can't Scion be run with the national Pantheons arising and that being the impetus for the ancient Pantheons to get back in the game adding another level of conflict beyond Titan smashing? Why can't Scion be run with Legend being closer to American Gods in how it impacts mystical strength allowing for a greater mix of modern and ancient gods?
