    Cyber Powers?

    07-25-2017, 11:24 PM
    If one thinks about it seriously, the online world has real power in shaping humanity. Mass media, disinformation, commerce, communication, archiving knowledge and ideas, etc. This is why I was left somewhat confused as to the lack of knacks or other abilities which deal with the internet in some manner. Its even more curious when one considers that at least two major deities are reputed to have at least some power over computers/programming/etc. These are Brighid and Hephaestus ( Possibly Ogun though I am not too sure about him, seems like a tenuous connection through metal working). I would also be inclined to believe that other knowledge deities would have some affinity for the online world (an Dagda, Athena, and possibly Thoth). So why isn't this accounted for among fanmade supplements? It might seem niche until you consider the type of damage hackers can do or Big Brother, or even the ability of criminals to disguise illegal activities within code. Just curious as to what you all think regarding this topic.

      What sort of powers are you looking for out of curiosity? There are some really awesome hacking Knacks in RAW, but they tend to get missed since they're not actually explained as being hacking or even technology based Knacks. From RAW, Math Genius and Cypher are amazing for being a hacker. In fact, just having those Knacks essentially lets you blow your way through all mortal security systems with almost zero effort. You can break any code in seconds, for no cost. The FBI, CIA, CSIS, everyone. Every mortal computer system you can break into at Legend 2 with one Epic Intelligence Knack. Mortal-built codes and encryption simply can't stand up to you, no rolls needed.

      Wireless Interface from RAW Demigod also couples amazingly with Cypher. Break into any computer you can see, no rolls needed, no physical access needed. All you need to do is look at it. This is possibly my favorite Technology based Knack honestly. Augmenting it with Relics can make it even more fun. For example, I had a Relic knife infused with a minor plague entity I would stab into any electronic system, infecting it with a virus as if it were a living being, and letting me use Wireless Interface on any system physically attached to the one I stabbed. So, for example, stab a security camera? Wireless Interface the computer it is connected to, use Cypher to break into their security system, loop the camera feed, and bam! Mortal security system vanquished. Add Speed Reader, a normally utterly useless Knack, and then you are able to hack at the speed of thought with Wireless Interface!

      For things like robots, AI, laser swords, that sort of stuff can be totally represented with Purviews like Artistry, Forge, Smithing, Creation, or whatever a table has called their 'Technology' based Purview. Sure, they tend to classically be for creating magical swords, entities like Talos, and etc, but they can totally work for more modern ideas!

      As for why these tend to be under-represented in houserules, I can think of some reasons. Personally, I like to keep my Pantheons in almost complete Stasis until very recently for my games. The Tuatha have been Underhill, or, well, dead. The Theoi have not left Olympus for more than a millennium. The Anunna totally focused on High Heaven, etc. Some Pantheons are more involved, the Shen are sort of unsure what Tian is up to, but are trying to work out its will to enforce on the world for example. But by and large, the Pantheons are not 'up to date' on the world. My favorite theme to explore in Scion is Cultural Clash, so I like throwing my modern Scions with their modern sensibilities up against someone who stepped out of the Bronze Age a handful of years ago, and are not totally sure what the mortals have done recently.

      Due to this, the Pantheons don't do a whole lot of fiddling with modern technology except for experimental curiosity. Some Pantheons who have... issues with mortals, or are traditionalists, stubbornly refuse to consider the new things the mortals came up with are worthy of their attention for example. (Leaving room for one of their Scions to argue the opposite) Hephaestus, for example, is slightly unsure about all this stuff the mortals have been up to recently. He looks at robots, and sees Talos, his Golden Servants, except... wrong. Not brought to life by the divine-technology of Ichor, but instead through complicated mechanics. He's not totally comfortable with seeing the mortals copying him in this way (Greek arrogance, he feels that the mortals of the Age of Iron couldn't have come up with the idea without just copying his divine ideas) but he hasn't started dropping 'That's Hubris,' bombs since he wants to figure out how they are doing it exactly before he properly decides if this is hubris, attempting to copy the Gods, yet.

      However, I know that my 'The Pantheons came back last week' angle is not that common, and certainly not exactly RAW. I think what generally discourages more technology based things in Scion in situations like this is simply that there isn't a mechanical model for people to use outside of contested rolls. The base system of Scion doesn't have any rules for hacking, or really interfacing with computers in any major way outside of Cypher which breaks it down to a back and forth of, "Who has higher Epic Int?" more or less. I have never seen anyone build a system to really expand on this, but I think there is totally room for that!

                  If you check the Hunter Knack Preview, it has a side bar that says:

                  Sidebar: The Digital Predator

                  Like Creator Knacks, Hunter Knacks do not need a literal stalked target. Figurative hunting -- be it chasing down a malevolent kami in construction plans or pursuing a black hat hacker through a mainframe -- allows you to use Hunter Knacks. So long as there is opposition, it’s sufficient for Hunters to pursue.

                  So I think at least as far as 2nd edition is concerned, you're covered. And Watcher already went through the options in 1st Ed. Also, as an aside, as the Goddess of all Language and Communication, Sarasvati will definitely have expanded to cover programming and social media into her domains. In addition, the Hindu craftsman God Vishwakarma has adapted to become the God of engineers... he is especially considered the patron of automobiles of all types; he is prayed to for averting car crashes and the like, and cars and buses etc. are worshiped as his divine manifestations on the day of his festival.

                              I thought there were Intelligence Knacks that dealt with manipulating technology, I found at least 2 canon examples from 1E.


                              Cipher
                              The Scion’s brain is his own personal Enigma machine. He can break any encryption and decode any message created by someone without Epic Intelligence, without the need for a roll. He can also design a one- time code for a speciﬁc recipient that cannot be broken by anyone without Epic Intelligence (regardless of how smart such a person is or what decryption equipment he might have on hand). Only a fellow Scion with Epic Intelligence can even attempt to break the code—calling for opposed (Intelligence + Academics) rolls. The person for whom the coded message is intended can automatically read the message as clearly as if it were written in his native language.



                              Wireless Interface
                              If the Scion devotes his total attention to doing so, he can mentally interface with an active computer without so much as touching it. He need only be able to see the computer, though not necessarily the monitor, in order to communicate with it. (Also, the computer must be turned on. Anybody can talk to a computer that isn’t on. Same thing happens too.) The degree to which the Scion can program the computer or access its ﬁles is the same whether he’s sitting at a keyboard in front of a monitor or just staring at its CPU, though, so it behooves him to at least know his way around an interface. Mentally interfacing with a computer’s programming absorbs the Scion’s attention, imposing a -2 distraction penalty on him unless he also has the Multitasking Knack.
