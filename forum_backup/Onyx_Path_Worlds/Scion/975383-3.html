    Scion 2e has to juggle what the ancients thought of their gods, what modern worshippers think of their gods, the needs of a role-playing game, the needs of popular culture, and the idea that the Gods of Scion are largely singular entities who change both by their own impetus and due to Fatebinding over time.

    We're striving a lot for accuracy, but it's a secondary concern. A very important secondary concern, but still secondary.

    Neall Raemonn Price

            Originally posted by herkles View Post
            [LIST][*]Aphrodite: I like this for the most part.[LIST][*]Eldest of the Theoi: that is not a title I have ever heard for Aphrodite. Now I have heard that title but it is for Hestia, who in most myths was the first olympion to be born.
            Well I can say that Aphrodite is considered the eldest in the Symposium, but that is a bunch of philosophers being playful. They all tell stories with their own way to make an ode to Love (Aphrodite) They reason she must be the eldest because Love (Their definition being looser than ours) must have come first for there to even be a Universe.

            As for Accuracy vs Not. The truth is that while accuracy is important, the nature of the game (if we are to tell modern stories) are the gods existing in a modern world and affected by the stories told. It would be more odd for the Gods not to grow towards their real myth. I mean, unless we insist that they only exist in their eldest forms (and thus not pantheons at all but warring gods representing different factions of the tribes/clans/city states nearby.) The truth is the myths we do have organically changed over time to fit the needs and beliefs of the cultures that worshiped them. A modern story of mythology must acknowledge that history changes Gods. One wanes and another gains pre-eminence, they travel, and yes, they change. If modern myths change then it makes sense that at least some part of them might acknowledge those myths. Why do their stories change who is related to whom? Why is Zeus not the same as Jupiter?

            I feel that is every bit as much part of mythology as the stories themselves, and that is at the core of telling modern myths. Of course that can only be done by respecting the original myths, but Loki being a Fire God is respectful and told respectfully as well.

                                    Originally posted by vonpenguin View Post
                                    Am I the only one that got a chuckle about the fact that in a pantheon full of gods killing, torturing, or turning people into spiders or statues or (anything else under the sun), it's Hephaestus trying to embarrass his cheating wife and her lover, and as I recall, being laughed at by the other gods at least as much, if not more than Aphrodite and Ares, that gets called out as vindictive?
                                    It's probably in reference to the throne he constructed for Hera specifically to torture her. In revenge for her abandoning him as an infant for being crippled by throwing him off Olympus (to be fair for this point, keep in mind the Theoi are 100% immortal save for two odd exceptions, so this isn't quite as bad as it sounds) he constructs an insanely elaborate throne that he sends up to Olympus as a gift for Hera.

                                    Hera sits upon this grand, beautiful throne, and it springs around her entombing her within the throne by invisible fetters. As the throne was constructed from adamant, no one could free her, so they had to go find the craftsman. Hephaestus refused to free his mother (keep in mind this is a culture where Matricide and Patricide, and all sorts of attacks upon a parent are seen as incredibly-incredibly bad) until the Theoi send Dionysus down to earth to get him absolutely wasted, where he agrees to free Hera in exchange for one of the thrones of the Dodekatheon.

                                    So, are the Theoi vengeful? Yes, it's a defining trait. But, Hephaestus is absolutely vengeful to the nth degree in their context of societal hate for crimes against one's parent. He also made that throne out of adamant, and almost nothing is made of that, only the most important and powerful things. So, Heph put a lot of time, and effort into his vengeance.

                                    Oh, and add to the 'crimes against the parent' angle that this is also a case of crimes against royalty, which are also pretty bad. Add to all of that, that the Greeks don't care what your parents did to deserve it, all that matters is you dared violate the sacred bond between parent and child. That gets Nemesis or The Furies after you.
