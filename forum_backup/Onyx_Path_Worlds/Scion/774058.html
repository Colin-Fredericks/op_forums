    Of Whom Was _______ A Scion?

    11-25-2015, 12:31 PM
    Inspired by a thread over in the Exalted side of things, who would the divine parents of assorted historical and pop cultural figures have been?

    On the What We Know wiki, Neall refers to Julius Caesar as a "militant Scion of Venus." That threw me for a bit of a loop - I understand the desire to shout out to Aeneas, but if there's one thing that Ceasar did not have a great deal of control over, it was his bedroom. Anyone who stays on bad terms with their wife, gets manipulated by Cleopatra, and somehow ends things so poorly with Servillia that her son stabs him is not getting any "love god" points in my book. I know it's a bit trite, but I think I'll shout out to Romulus instead, and assume Caesar was a Scion of Mars.

    That got me thinking about coming up with Scion statuses for his protegees. Marcus is easy - he always had strong associations with the sun (going so far as to name one of his sons Alexander Helios), so Helios (who might be a Mantle of Apollo now?) for him, too. Octavian gave me some pause. His greatest strengths are his people skills and use of information, and Rome doesn't really have an outright Thoth who embodies such ideas. ultimately, I decided that if he ultimately wins out over his many rivals because he has a better core of competent and loyal aides, then nobody shall do save the Goddess of Relationships herself - hail Augustus, son of Hera.

    ... that probably got a bit awkward after Julius became a Demigod himself. So it goes.

    "For me, there's no fundamental conflict between really loving something and also seeing it as very profoundly flawed." -- Jay Eddidin

                  Caesar spent so much time sleeping around that Cicero mocked him by calling him, "Every man's woman and every woman's man." Combined with his historically-attested beauty, Scion of Venus seems pretty appropriate to me, particularly since the Julii Caesares like to boast about Venus being part of their lineage.

                  I'm not sure what you mean about the rest. He seems to have loved Cinnilla, so much so that he was willing to risk death for her; he divorced Pompeia because of Clodius's Bona Dea-related shenanigans; and he seems to have gotten along fine with Calpurnia. Regarding Cleopatra, he needed someone to replace Ptolemy XIII. Who better than Cleopatra? Particularly since the other candidate was 12 or 13 years old at the time? Finally, what does his relationship with Brutus's mother have to do with Brutus murdering him? If anything, I'd argue that Caesar's tempestuous love life makes him even better-suited as a Scion of Venus, who is not exactly known for having a peaceful love life of her own.

                  Regarding the rest, I'd say that Antonius is a Scion of either Hercules or Bacchus. Hercules both because the Antonii claimed to be his descendants and because of Antonius's own physicality. Bacchus because Antonius liked to be worshiped as Dionysus. As for Augustus, I'd go with a Scion of Jupiter, though that's pretty tenuous.

                                                  Hades became god of the underworld and wealth. Claiming dominion over departed souls and all the earth's resources.
