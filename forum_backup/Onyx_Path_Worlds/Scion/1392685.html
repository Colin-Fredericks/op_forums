Were the glory days and later decline of for example Ancient Egypt and Greece due to increasing and decreasing involvement from their respective pantheons or were these golden ages mostly due to the actions and capabilities of humanity itself?

      This strikes me as a very "Every Table has an individual answer" question.

      As for how it goes at MY table... The line is tough to draw in a lot of cases.

      Like, at what point do you separate the actions of the presence of a God from their worshippers? Where's the line between "This was done by human hand without Gods" and "This was done due to Gods"?

      Like, the Deva. The Deva are everywhere, and it is through their presence at all levels that the universe functions. When a poet writes amazing prose, was that purely their effort? Were they blessed by Sarasvati? Did they enter a trance state and Sarasvati guided their pen? Or did Sarasvati just let a big of her power flow into the poet to grant inspiration?

      I remember at one point I saw someone ask a question that amounted to "If Aphrodite makes someone fall in love, is that real love or magic?" to which the answer is "Where does love normally come from?"

      Ideally I'd keep it mysterious and ambiguous where the potential line of Human Action and The Divine is.

      Also while Gods may be more popular in some areas, I don't often tie Gods to our modern concepts of nations. Any correlation is due to demographics, not due to arbitrary lines drawn on a map that most Gods probably wouldn't think much about.

                                  Originally posted by Astromancer View Post
                                  Most Chinese Americans are Christian, but there are Buddhists, Taoists, and others. Thus the Chinese gods are here too.
                                  And that's not even counting the thousands of Chinese Christian converts who are still on file, because it probably takes at least a few centuries until your records are properly disposed of.

                                  Going back to the original question, though, personally I tend to think of the history of the (mortal) World still as primarily driven by humans. And a natural part of that is that some cultures may come to worship one pantheon more or less regularly than another. Not because that pantheon is inherently better, but for any of the myriad of reasons that people both individually and as a culture adopt certain values, beliefs, preferences, etc. For instance, some gods are closely tied to natural locations (like the Netjer and the Nile), so anybody who lives around there already has a good reason to be respectful to them, maybe make a sacrifice to them now and then, and look, we've already founded a new cult! In the World, that's just common sense, really.

                                  That being said, my general view of the Gods in Scion is that they work best when they work on a) an individual basis (like blessing a single worshipper or cult), or b) a cosmic level (making sure the sun goes round and lightning is a thing). As soon as you go into the 'mid-level' stuff like social change, governments, and the course of human history, it gets messy. Because I feel like there are some seriously unfortunate implications when you think too long about the very bad things that happened to some of the cultures depicted in Scion. Like, what were the Deva up to while the British colonised India? To me, the answer is: a) they helped individuals and communities resist colonialism, and b) they made sure the laws of physics behave properly, and beyond that, err, don't think about it. There may be better answers than this on a case-by-case and pantheon-by-pantheon basis and I would love to know more about them. But we're not going to solve the problem of evil in a tabletop RPG.
