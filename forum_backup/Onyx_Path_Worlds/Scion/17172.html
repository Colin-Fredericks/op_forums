    Boons: Cloud vs Linear? CLOUD!

    11-24-2013, 01:26 PM
    This is something that's been a bit of a debate since the Hero came out. To set up my analogy, first let me mention the two paradigms that exist in other White Wolf games: Gifts (WTA) and Disciplines (VTM).

    In Werewolf, your powers are called Gifts and they're sorted out under your Auspice, Breed and Tribe. Each one of those things has a list of powers you can learn that rank from 1 to 5. Each set of powers has a general theme, like Wendigo Gifts being mostly based around battle and cold or Theurge Gifts mostly being based around spirits and shamanism. There's some overlap between sets of powers, with some Gifts appearing in multiple lists. That's fine because each Gift is a unique, independent power. There is no progression from any given Rank 1 Gift to any given Rank 2 Gift. There is no "order" to buy in. You never need to have any specific Rank 1 Gift before buying any specific Rank 2 Gift. You could, in fact, never buy a single Gift from your Auspice list until you cap out your Rank and decide to buy three of the Rank 5 Gifts from that list. This is the cloud power system​.

    In Vampire, your powers are called Disciplines and they're totally (mostly) independent from your Clan. Each Discipline is a self-contained progression of powers that must be purchased in order. From levels 1 to 5, there aren't even any alternate powers available. One power per dot of the Discipline, because that's all the options you have. Everyone with Dominate 1 has the same power, because Dominate 1 is that specific power. You can't buy out of order because knowing Dominate 1 informs your ability to use Dominate 2, which is just a more advanced, more evolved version of the first power. It's not possible to learn Dominate 5 without also having Dominate 1, 2, 3 and 4. It just won't work at all. This is the linear power system.

    Scion, according to the First Edition RAW rules, has a cloud power system. You do not have to buy Boons in order. You can pick up a level 3 Fire Boon without ever learning a level 1 or 2 Boon from Fire. However, Scion did a really crappy job portraying this system. They only published a single power for each level, which made the system feel linear. It felt just like Disciplines. Fire 1 was the same for everyone, Fire 2 was the same for everyone, all the way up to Fire 10. Even though the experience costs are designed for a cloud system, the rules suggest a cloud system and Scion lends itself strongly to a cloud power system (how often do Gods randomly manifest a single power from a Purview, but never anything else at all?), that was not how Scion's published material presented itself because of a limited number of powers. If there's only one Boon to pick at each level (the Companion did, randomly, add extra Boons to some Purviews, admittedly), its naturally going to encourage the idea that the power system is Discipline-like. It really didn't help when the only Purview that did have a reasonable number of Boons (Magic) was singled out for doing so, and turned into a Special Purview for apparently no reason except that it had "too many Boons". It didn't! It had exactly the right number! Give all the Purviews that many Boons and let players make choices about how their characters manifest the powers of Fire or the powers of Justice.


    So, I would really love to see Scion 2 do away with the whole linear power system idea and completely embrace the cloud system that Scion 1 tried to implement. Have a list of powers that fit the general theme of each Purview, with at least two Boons per level, preferably three per level. Please. Make it so that someone isn't buying "Psychopomp 4", instead they're buying Run Super Fast or Teleport Across The Room. If you want to really prove the system is cloud-based instead of linear, then cross over some powers. Give Earth, Water, Fire and Air all the ability to use the Summon Element Boon or something. Give both Darkness and Sun the Eclipse Boon. But really, don't skimp on the number of Boons and please don't turn Purviews into Disciplines. I don't want every Fire God to have the same set of Fire Boons. I want Pele to feel different from Hestia. I'd really like Zeus to have different Sky Boons than Quetzalcoatl does. And you know, maybe Zeus should have Animal Form, even though he doesn't have many other Animal Boons. In a cloud power system, he can do that.

    Linear power systems are fine, but they don't convey the flexibility and choice that should be paramount in Scion. Scions should be able to do all kinds of crazy things and break the mold. They shouldn't be limited to buying things "in order" when there doesn't have to be an order at all.

    I'm a professor! Why is no one listening to me?!

      There's also a lot of options between the two of these that increase the variation and flexibility you get with Cloud and keep the simplicity of Linear.

      Tree aka Exalted: Linear, but multiple options in a Purview and non-mutually-exclusive branching.

      Skipping aka WtF: Linear sets of powers, but you can skip powers. How Scion 1e essentially ended up working in practice.

      Multiple Choice aka HtR post Creedbooks: Linear sets of powers but you have a choice of options for each level. Even with two options at each level from 1-5, you have a huge amount of possible combinations.

      Name-Verb magic aka Mage: Linear domains became general magical competence in a specific area, with the ability to create custom made signature powers (rotes in Mage).

      My problem with Cloud and Tree, is that they end up with huge disconnected power lists. Lets say each of the three divine tier books introduces two options for each level of each Purview. That's 6 powers at each level. Assume the same set of general Purviews, that's 114 powers to sort through; on top of Knacks, PSPs and Specials. This is most likely why Cloud didn't get implemented sufficiently in Scion 1e.

      I don't think pure Linear is a good match for Scion though.

      I've always felt that something closer to Mage or Ars Magica suits Scion simply because the diversity of mythology makes it hard to just think of most deities as having a specific set of powers. You can always disconnect Boons from needing Purviews as a prerequisite (so if you just want one specific power, you can nab it and have it capped by Legend or something) while having characters that invest in the Purview get bonuses to Boons instead of them being restrictions on your Boons. Zeus can turn into animals by just buying that boon, but he wouldn't be as good a shape-shifter as character that's bought up all of Animal and a few key Boons.

          It's true, there are other systems. Cloud Power vs Linear Power are just the two biggest extremes.

          I'd like to see the Tree Power System from Exalted implemented for Knacks in Scion. That's already mostly how they work, but I think it could be made more clear. I'm not sure that it works as well for Boons, because it would shift around the paradigm of Purviews an awful lot more than going back to the Cloud Power System that was originally intended.

          Multiple Choice was also offered in Trinity with the Psi Modes. Each Mode was 1-5, totally linear, but there were at least 2 choices for each level and you could learn either, no difficulty at all. That is an option, but I don't think it's really flexible enough for Scion. There's still the issue of so many mythological creatures and figures just having one or two powers from a Purview instead of just learning a different set of powers.

          Guided Freeform Systems like MtA and DAM would be totally awesome! But they're incredibly complex and very punishing for players that aren't awesome at inventing new effects on the fly. I think if such a freeform system was implemented, it would be cool.. but it might put a lot of people off. I know that it did for Mage, because the idea of having to invent your own powers constantly scares a lot of folks that aren't really comfortable with that much system-crunching.

          Linear Skipping did end up being what Scion went with, but it creates a sense of "cheating" that I've had significant problems with in play. Someone that has invested heavily in Psychopomp feels cheated when someone "snipes" a high level power without investing in the lower level ones. It feels really awkward, especially when there's such a limited number of choices. You have the guy that's bought Psychopomp 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8... then the guy that just buys Psychopomp 9, without any of the build up. The linear-ness of the base system really builds in the expectation that linear is the "real" way to buy powers and anything else is cheating. I don't like that impression.

          A real Cloud Power System, yes, would need a lot of Boons. There are 19 All-Purpose Purviews, and hopefully we'll be getting a Crafting Purview to round things out to 20. 10 ranks per Purview, with two powers minimum, that's a theoretical 200 Boons. But, in a Cloud System like WTA, it doesn't have to be 200 unique Boons. Purviews can share certain powers, cutting down on word count required. Besides, it isn't like they won't free up a bunch of space by leaving out the annoying "Sample Adventure" chapters in each book and by cramming the base system into Origins instead of putting it in with Hero. 200 Boons isn't actually that much to ask for, and I'm not even asking for that many. 150, maybe.


          I'm not sure what you mean by disconnecting Boons from Purviews. How would that work?

          I'm a professor! Why is no one listening to me?!

                  Guided Freeform isn't always so scary. Awakening, while not something a lot of Ascension players liked, threw in a huge list of effects to the point where people that don't like coming up with custom effects can easily never bother. It becomes less of a necessity and more a cool thing you can do to get creative. It does get into the same issue as Cloud/Tree massive lists though.

                  And by "disconnecting" I mean that in a Guided Freeform instead of having a Boon require a certain level of Purview before you can buy it, anyone can buy any Boon from any Purview even if they don't have dots in that Purview. Instead, having both a Boon and the Purview it falls under makes it more powerful. So you reward Purview experts with more potent Boons, rather than gating access to Boons with Purview ratings. To keep things sane, I'd have the level of a Boon capped by Legend (so no Heroes with Demigod level Boons).

                  A possible example: For every dot in a Purview you have above the Boon rating, you gain a +1 to activate it if you've purchased it or a +1 to any roll to resist that Boon's effects if used on you (assuming there is one). So a Scion with 4 dots in Sky and the Storm Augmentation Boon is facing off against an opponent with Sky 0, and Storm Augmentation. The Sky Scion gets a +1 to activate the power (so it probably lasts them longer before needing to reactivate it) and a +1 to resist the stun effect, while the opponent with just the Boon doesn't have those advantages.

                  This also aids in scaling, as Boons would automatically get better the more you invest in a Purview and more Purview makes it harder for people that "snipe" powers to use them against you effectively.

                                                      If there is some advantage to having lots of Boons in a given Purview, I am curious how that interacts with adding additional choices for Boons.
