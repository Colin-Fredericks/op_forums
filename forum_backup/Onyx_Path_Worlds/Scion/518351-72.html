                                            I'm reading Rick Riordan's crossover story in which his Egyptian and Greek main characters meet for the first time, and the second story intrigued me as far as Scion goes.

                                            ​In this story, Annabeth (a Scion of Athena,) and Sadie Kane (not a Scion, but an Egyptian magician who knows divine magic) encounter Serapis. For those not familiar, Serapis was a god created by Ptolemy I after he took over Egypt. Serapis was intended to be a fusion of Greek and Egyptian religion in order to bring the Egyptians and Greeks closer together. For a brief time, he usurped the positions of several Egyptian gods in Egypt.

                                            In the story, Serapis claims that he was able to use the Great Lighthouse of Alexandria as a beacon that drew the Egyptian and Greek gods to him, whereupon he then began feeding on their essence, gaining their power. He claimed that since he usurped Osiris' position as God of the Underworld, he was able to compel Isis to serve him as his wife for a time (in the real world, Serapis was, indeed considered Isis's husband during his time.) He also claimed that he could use Sadie and Annabeth's connection to their respective goddesses (Sadie has a strong connection to Isis) to draw Isis and Athena to him.

                                            So, my questions are.

                                            1.) How would Serapis be handled in Scion? Now that we know gods can be members of more than one pantheon, would he still be around as a member of both the Netjer and the Theoi? If so, what would their opinion of him be?

                                            2.) This business of usurping other god's positions sounds a lot like Mantle play. Could Riordan's story actually happen in Scion 2e? Could a god usurp multiple gods and effectively conquer two pantheons? What about forcing Isis to serve him? Can just taking Osiris' Mantle allow him to do that?
