    Storyguide Chapter is out and as promised it does indeed have rules for using Free Assignment of Attributes and Skills.

    I'm incredibly glad these rules are *in there* but - and I'll be honest this is partly an issue I've been having with the StoryXing Advice chapters in OP/WW games for pretty much thirty years, I do find the tone a little bit condescending.

    Untethered Attribute Dots: Similarly, some players might prefer to simply have a pool of Attribute dots instead of having them assigned by Path choice. There’s a possibility using this method will result in characters who can’t actually accomplish things their characters should be good at, but if you and your players prefer a more freeform option, you can ignore the Attribute dots awarded by minor and major Paths and simply let the player allocate an additional 18 dots after putting 1 in each Attribute.
    Emphasis mine.

    Literally the whole reason I'd want to use Untethered Attribute Dots is that I want my character to actually be able to accomplish things she should be good at. The current system is not actually effective at letting characters accomplish things they should be good at unless you assume that what a character should be good at is overwhelmingly determined by which Family you pick.

    A Spawn of Vodnik, under the current rules, could be a Deep One who runs a used bookstore or a Selkie who is also a Navy SEAL (honestly didn't intend the pun but I stand by it). Under the default system, the game considers it safe to assume that those two characters "should be good at" mostly the same things.

                        Apologies for the delayed response, I haven’t had time to sit down and write this out until now.

                        Can we create both a Deep One who runs a bookstore and Navy SEAL Spawn of Vodnik who are good at their respective professions and different from each other? As you mentioned we’re looking for a minimum dice pool of 5-6, preferably 7-8, for what they are “good” at.


                        First some probability math. Using https://thecontractrpg.com/info/probability/ to determine the chance of a single hit on a roll, we determine how many d10s it takes to hit a percentage chance of a hit. For the sake of ease I’ll be rounding to the nearest tenth and only include percentages over 50%
                        • 3d10 65.7% 1 hit
                        • 4d10 76.0% 1 hit
                        • 5d10 83.1% 1 hit, 59.1% 2 hits
                        • 6d10 88.2% 1 hit, 68.1% 2 hits
                        • 7d10 91.8% 1 hit, 75.3% 2 hits, 52.9% 3 hits
                        • 8d10 94.2% 1 hit, 81.1% 2 hits, 61.3% 3 hits

                        To put those percentages into real world numbers I’m going to use a few sports ball statistics. In the NFL Drew Breese holds the single season completion percentage for a QB at 74.4%. The WNBA all time leading scorer Diana Taurasi’s best season field goal percentage is 46.1% (49.4% was her best NCAA year). I feel safe to say any dice pool above 50% counts as good and anything over 75% is very good, which is why I added the pool of 3 & 4.


                        Now, to keep with your dice pool range of 5-8 I’m going to shoot for each character to have at least one thing at 8 dice, another at 7, and then a handful more between the 5-6 range. We’ll start with Lineage and work through the character creation process to see how things shape up.


                        Primal

                        For the Deep One I’m going minor path for Lineage, major path for the SEAL. Our Deep One gets 3 dots between Athletics, Close Combat, and Survival while the SEAL gets 6. I’m going with an equal spread here, 1 dot each for the Deep One, 2 each for the SEAL. Minor Primal attribute split is 1/2/1 with major being 3/4/3. The Deep One I’ll go 1 Intelect, 1 Dexterity, 1 Stamina, 1 Presence, the SEAL 1 each Mental and Social, giving Dex 2 and the rest of the Physical 1.

                        Deep One

                        Athletics 1, Close Combat 1, Survival 1

                        Intelect 2, Cunning 1, Resolve 1, Might 1, Dexterity 2, Stamina 2, Presence 2, Manipulation 1, Composure 1

                        SEAL

                        Athletics 2, Close Combat 2, Survival 2

                        Intelect 2, Cunning 2, Resolve 2, Might 2, Dexterity 3, Stamina 2, Presence 2, Manipulation 2, Composure 2

                        With just one path we’ve got our SEAL with 3 pools of 4-5.


                        Spawn of Vodnik

                        Deep One goes major here getting 6 dots between Esoterica, Larceny, Ranged Combat, SEAL getting 3. For the Deep One I’ll leaning heavily into Esoterica with 4, Larceny and Ranged Combat getting 1 each. The SEAL doesn’t really need Esotrica so instead I’m going 1 Larceny and 2 Ranged Combat. Attributes the Deep One gets Mental 3, Physical 3, Social 4 while the SEAL gets Mental 2, Social 2. Yes this means at this point in time the Deep One is going to have one more Physical Dot while the SEAL has an extra Mental dot. There’s still another path to pick so calm down. For the Deep One I’ll do and even split Mental and Physical, 1,1,2 Social. The Seal is going Cunning/Resolve and 2 into Composure.

                        Deep One

                        Athletics 1, Close Combat 1, Esoterica 4, Larceny 1, Ranged Combat 1, Survival 1

                        Intelect 3, Cunning 2, Resolve 2, Might 2, Dexterity 3, Stamina 3, Presence 3, Manipulation 2, Composure 3

                        The Deep One is now 6-7 dice pool for Estorica, 3-4 dice every other skill

                        SEAL

                        Athletics 2, Close Combat 2, Larceny 1, Ranged Combat 2, Survival 2

                        Intellect 2, Cunning 3, Resolve 3, Might 2, Dexterity 3, Stamina 2, Presence 2, Manipulation 2, Composure 4

                        4 Skills have a pool ranging from 4-6


                        Role Path

                        Lets just use what’s in the book instead of coming up with unique paths here, I’ve got a laser tattoo removal appointment to get to. While Academic or Student could work for the Deep One, Occultist is the thematically obvious choice, gaining 3 dots between Athletics, Esoterica, and Medicine and Mental 2, Physical 1, Social 1. I’m going to split them 1 to Atheltics and 2 into Medicine, splitting Mental between Intellect/Cunning, Stamina (book store owners often are on their feet all day), Presence (make that sale). The SEAL really could go either way between Enforcer or Hunter, but I’m going to go with Criminal cause I’m cynical like that. So there’s Close Combat, Larceny, and Pilot getting an equal split. Putting 1 Mental into Resolve, splitting the physical between Might and Stamina, and adding another to Composure.

                        Athletics 2, Close Combat 1, Esoterica 4, Larceny 1, Medicine 2, Ranged Combat 1, Survival 1

                        Intelect 4, Cunning 3, Resolve 2, Might 2, Dexterity 3, Stamina 4, Presence 4, Manipulation 2, Composure 3

                        Esoterica Intellect/Stamina/Presence dice pool ranges 6-8, Athletics and Medicine 4-6, all others 3-5

                        SEAL

                        Athletics 2, Close Combat 3, Larceny 2, Pilot 1, Ranged Combat 2, Survival 2

                        Intellect 2, Cunning 3, Resolve 4, Might 3, Dexterity 3, Stamina 3, Presence 2, Manipulation 2, Composure 5

                        Close Combat + Composure pool 8, CC + Resolve pool 7, CC+Physical 6


                        Now the Deep One has our 8 dice pool in Esoterica + Intellect, which is arguably the main pool we’d look for here. Our Paths didn’t give the character Enigmas which is the Skill I’d argue is second behind Esoterica for our bookstore owner, so I’ll put 2 of the freebie Skills there and split the other 2 between Culture and Science. The Intellect of 4 covers getting and handful of dice pools in the 5-6 range.


                        The SEAL needs a little more work. We’ve got the handful in the 5-6 as everything except Pilot + Physical ends up there. Going 2 Close Combat and 2 Ranged Combat give a 7 and 8 dice pool for combat skills, which is what most folks think of when they think of SEALs.


                        So there we have 2 characters same Lineage and Family, who are both sort of good at several of the same things but excel in different places.

                            Thanks for putting the numbers together, I'm about to get extremely nitpicky but I want to highlight upfront that I actually do appreciate you engaging with this.

                            First nitpick: you can't roll Combat Skill + Composure in SPU. Or rather you can because you can houserule it but the official rule is that close combat is Might and ranged combat is Dex. This is a change from older editions. This makes the Navy SEAL worse in a fight than the average beat cop going by the Ashcan stats (Physical stat 3 plus combat stat 2). I appreciate you've used sportsball statistics to get a sense of what dice pools "should" be, but that's quite misleading because we don't actually know what the "difficulty" of those sporting feats is.

                            The next set of nitpicks aren't actually nitpicks so much as me unpacking why I found the original quote condescending.

                            You've successfully demonstrated that you the system as written lets you build characters who are good at the things the game designers think those characters should be good at.

                            But (and this isn't your fault because I can't expect you to be psychic) you've also kind of demonstrated that it does a terrible job at the things *I* think those characters should be good at.

                            The Deep One Rare Book Dealer has a good pool for Esoterica + Intellect, but no dots in Artistry or Culture, both of which are things I think are important for being a rare book dealer (I've got four discretionary points to play with, but we're looking at how well just the paths work). Their physical stats are lower than I'd like (I tend to imagine Deep Ones as being *superhumanly* strong) and their social Attributes are far too good. I don't need them to be able to fire a gun at all but they have to if they take Family Primary (which they need to if they want to be able to breathe underwater).

                            The Selkie Navy SEAL can do some of the things I'd consider necessary for the character as I'd actually want to play it, but not all of them (and again that's the outcome that the book is warning me will happen if I *don't* use their system). A huge part of being a Selkie is hanging out by the water's edge and luring people to watery graves, which I have no ability to do whatsoever with no good social stat except Composure. The Selkie doesn't need Larceny or Pilot, or Resolve 4 really (remember that the devs have said multiple times that you shouldn't be looking at what the dot ratings "mean" you should think of them as abstract qualities that just do what the dice do).

                            Once again I really am appreciative that you took the time to do a worked example but ultimately the question here is whether the existing system of locking most things to Paths is a more effective way of making characters that are good at the things I want them to be good at than letting me just put those points where I want and it's borderline logically impossible for that to be the case.

                            Even if I happened to come up with a character concept that we both agreed was perfectly reflected by the Path system, all that would mean that the Paths were as good as letting me free-assign. The core system really does have strengths--it's quick, it's thematic, it justifies the "storypath" branding and it's useful for people who find just being handed a lot of abstract numbers overwhelming and don't want to make a bajillion fiddly decisions.

                            But it's honestly *weird* for the devs to claim that it will somehow lead to characters that do the things I want those characters to do better than letting me design the characters myself.
