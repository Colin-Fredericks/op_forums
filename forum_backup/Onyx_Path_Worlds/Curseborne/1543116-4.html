                            Originally posted by Nobody Special View Post
                            A character with four or five dice in the thing they're supposed to be really good at doesn't feel really good at it. That was true in VtM in the 90s and VtR in the 2010s and it's true today. It seems disingenous to pretend that they do. The game *itself* gives its baseline antagonists way more dice than that and those are the guys who are meant to be little more than speedbumps.
                            Here's the thing, the group you play with defines "feel" a lot more than dice do. Look at your cheerleader character for a moment. She's going to get atop the pyramid. What happens?

                            One option is that she rolls her might plus athletics like you previously suggested. A second option is that she's not using might at all, maybe she's trying to dexterously climb the pyramid and is using dex plus athletics. Or maybe she's using cunning plus athletics. Hell, a different option is that she's not rolling by herself at all, it's a team work action because she's not getting to the top of the pyramid by herself. The rules can be used to support all of that, and thus enable the low might cheerleader that you insist doesn't exist.

                            But also, what happens if she fails? Maybe she gets close to the top, falls and cracks her skull open and dies right there in front of everyone... but probably not. Most likely she just doesn't get to the top, she had a false start and seconds later she makes it to the top when she aces her second roll. If failure is extremely punishing low dice pools "feel" bad, but when it's a few seconds of a setback and not much more, then having a dice pool of 4 or 5 isn't the worst thing ever and it's okay to fail the occasional roll.

                            That's not even getting into the whole "you don't have to roll a pilot roll every time you sit behind a steering wheel" argument.

                            And that's across the whole ruleset. Your cop example doesn't use "might" to determine their dicepools, so to get a matching dice pool while doing the same thing as the cop maybe a PC approaching someone trying to make an arrest would need cunning instead of might, or maybe the pc is approaching it trying to wear the suspect down and needs resolve.

                                Originally posted by ShadowcatX View Post

                                Flavor. You know a member of the Get will be physically powerful as the stereotype indicates they are. Now if having stereotypes be based on fact is in fact good, I'm not certain.

                                Balance. I think 10/6/2 is the most uneven spread you can force, as opposed to 12/6/0 with distribution. I think this is probably worth something, but it's not exactly a lot with it being 2 attributes.

                                Story shaping reality. The current rules enforce that both what you are and who you are influenced what you can do. If you're the whimpiest person in the world and are cursed into the Get of Lyla your body is going to change one way or another. Your pathes matter and help shape who your character is.

                                Are those things worth it? I'm unsure. Despite it seeming like I'm defending the system, I'm not sold on it either. I'm currently learning Scion though and I will say your choice of Path seems way less impactful when it's just "pick 3 skills."
                                Sometimes embracing stereotypes can be fun. Sometimes it can be fun to go against stereotype. The problem is forcing people at adhere to stereotypes. If someone wants to play a super physical viking combat monster Get of Lyka character, they can put their points into that. Then again, if they want to play a more social and cerebral Get character, they could do that as well with point distribution. Is it going to detract someone's fun if a player decides to make a werewolf character with only a few points in physical attributes? I don't think it would. It's not like free point distribution prevents anybody from playing into the stereotype if they like.

                                As for balance, 10/6/2 is still pretty minmaxy though not as bad as 12/6/0. Then again, distribute 18 with at most 9 and at least 3 makes it even harder to minmax and allows for greater flexibility. 8/6/4 forces even more balance.

                                The reality in a world can conform to certain trends without forcing those trends on players. I know that this issue isn't as politically charged gender based attribute modifications, but the idea of forcing stereotypes is similar. If someone says that in real life and in the reality of the game world, that men are stronger than women. For that to be a reality of the setting, does that mean that all characters have to adhere to that tendency? Tendencies, statistical averages, and true stereotypes can exist in the reality of a game setting (or the reality of the actual world for that matter) without forcing everybody to adhere to those stereotypes. Most Lykans can be very physical and still allow for players to be exceptions if they think that is cool.

                                I don't think that those things are worth it, especially with all of the added complexity and reduced transparency of the current system when 18 or 8/6/4 are options. Though in a similar vein as you say, I don't hate this system or anything and I like to stay optimistic about it. I still think that Curseborne seems like a cool fun game even if I have an issue with a few of the mechanics.

                                        Originally posted by ShadowcatX View Post

                                        Here's the thing, the group you play with defines "feel" a lot more than dice do. Look at your cheerleader character for a moment. She's going to get atop the pyramid. What happens?.
                                        Cutting most of this just because I think so the quoting back and forth is only making the conversation hard to follow at this stage.

                                        This is a super valid point and I don't disagree in general but I think the way I'd rather put it is to say that the way you play affects *how* the system affects you, not that it's the dominant effect.

                                        For example, one of the things your suggest is using alternative dice pools and that's definitely a valid call but it's also kind of an indirect way of dealing with the issue. It's essentially solving the problem of low dice pools making your character feel less competent by just never rolling your low dice pools. That's valid but it has downsides, it encourages min-maxing and can make people who just accepted their characters being bad at some stuff feel cheated.

                                        Limited consequences for failure actually does make small dice pools feel less bad, but at the cost of making all dice rolls feel less consequential. It also directly cuts against the suggested play pattern of the game, which seems to be based around an "only roll if there's significant risk of consequences" model.

                                        More generally, the big disagreement here seems to be what level 3 in an Attribute represents. You seem (and again I'm not meaingto put words in your mouth) to be coming from the perspective that it represents being *massively* above average. Like Might 5 means you're a finalist in World's Strongest Man, Might 4 means you're an Olympic powerlifter and Might 3 means you're not Olympic level but you're still seriously jacked.

                                        This *is* how it's often been described down the years but the problem I've always had with that interpretation is that it basically bundles well over 90% of people and far more than 90% of people you might actuality be *playing* outside of some very unusual games into 2 dots. This is even worse in Curseborne because the game is explicitly street level. One of the signature PCs is basically an ordinary high school teen.

                                        I tend to view Attributes much more generation and much more relative to the individual PC. Putting a start at 5 isn't saying "I'm literally the best person at this thing in the world", it's saying "this is my thing, I would like to do well when I take this approach to stuff".

                                        Of course as you say we haven't seen the whole system yet but we have seen the Ashcan and we do know that the devs chose to give the PCs enough points to have 3 dots in literally every Attribute. That at least *suggests* to me that they don't consider a 3 dot stat to be particularly remarkable.

                                                Potential double post because my last one got flagged as spam for too many edits and there's no telling how quickly it'll be approved, if ever.

                                                OK, setting aside the petty name calling and childish behaviour, it really doesn't matter what attributes represent in real life terms, the original point of this thread still stands. Currently it seems like too many major/minor combinations end up being the same, or with just one or two points shifted around, making it pointless to distinguish between major and minor paths other than picking which inheritance you get. Playing a Sorcerer Archivist for example. Sorcerer is 4/3/3 or 2/1/1, and Archivist is 5/2/3 or 3/0/1. They look pretty different, but when added together they both add up to 7/3/4, so why even bother having the major/minor thing for attributes. I still like the idea of having a pool of points you can distribute amongst attributes, with certain minimums and maximums. Then Lineages and Families provide bonuses based on their stereotype. Lineages and Families can even provide bonuses to specific attributes rather than just mental, physical, social. Make it like in Requiem where each clan gets +1 to one of two different attributes. It's better than the false variation we have now when all the major/minor lineage/family values adding up to the same stat distribution anyway.
