    The lack of a Supernatural Illuminati

    11-08-2024, 05:48 AM
    This is a discussion thread about one of the biggest differences of Curseborne from the World of Darkness and that's the lack of a supernatural Illumianti. There is no Pentex, there is no Technocracy, and there is no Camarilla. There's the Heirs and Premeires but they're not organizations so much as loose associations.

    This has a huge difference in the way supernaturals interact in the setting. I think Curseborne is more like Fright Night with its supernaturals than Blade. You don't have vast networks of power that supernaturals can draw from and the Curseborne may use their powers to get rich but the curses aren't really THAT suited to becoming wealthy. You're much more likely to have working day job Curseborne in this world.

    The supernaturals are also not able to exert their ideological will across the setting far more.

    I think it's the biggest way to distinguish the settings.

    How about you?

      This is one of those ways in which the curse metaphysic was a very sharp idea, in my opinion.

      Contrasting it against World and Chronicles, World had it's gothic stagnation where the old and the unadaptable kept a death grip on the world, all pretenses of power more a way of communicating an abiding fear that ends up killing the world, so an active Masquerade was a pretty powerful extension of those ideas, and that sort of level of conspiracy* would require a lot of coordination and organization to sustain. Chronicles started there but started to grow into this idea that the supernatural was actively harmful to most of humanity, and that that psychological toll was enough to render a broad veil, which worked for Chronicles also breaking down the setting convention to a more local level.

      Curseborne completes that transition where encountering the supernatural is very effectively stepping in it-if you investigate the things that go bump in the night, it tends to have a more immediate backlash and consequence because curses get embedded into the actions and places of the world and can seep from monsters even if they're trying to control it. The Accursed don't really need to police their hidden world because magic so readily screws over anyone trying to get into it-you don't see if there's a troll under the bridge because everyone knows it's immediately consequential.

      But that metaphysic cuts both ways, and the intimacy that comes with that curse foundation tends to both mean that local problems are a lot more present and that interactions between factions can sometimes have the feel of looking at the Gordian Knot like it could somehow be even knottier. It'll be cool to see how much more that will get explored in future.

      (That said , this does kind of broach on one thing I hope to see in future books, which isn't so much an Illuminati type as it is cross-Lineage/Familial organizations. The personal approach to organization does mean that it's very easy to heat up** conflicts, and that has immediate play value, but it feels weird to go back to a more World model for political structures when over in Chronicles the Contagion Chronicles and it's Player Guide felt like a deliberate kick to implied barriers of single-splat organizations, but that's all an aside unrelated to the thread.)

      But yeah, it's possibly the most organic way of doing the divide between the normal and hidden worlds in urban horror/fantasy I've seen in a long while, and a very solid way of bucking long held conventions. It's satisfying.

      *would honestly fail, but we're playing to the fiction.
      **Honestly, this is one thing that I think speaks the most to the differences between Curseborne and it's predecessors that I haven't seen anyone mentioned-Curseborne feels decidedly like a much "hotter" game compared to those two's "colder" approach on the whole.

          I have to admit, I do kind of miss the grand conspiracies, the shadowy manipulators, that we had in the WoD. I think that they made some degree of sense in a world where the existence of the supernatural could be so well hidden.

          It doesn’t seem like Curseborne will have that, though the setting of Curseborne itself is rather less concrete than WoD - especially the WoD that we know now after countless supplements that fleshed out these Illuminati.

          It may be the case that as Curseborne progresses that some more potential for high conspiracy will be introduced, certainly not in as strict a form as the Camarilla or Technocracy, but still in ways that can capture some of that fun.

          If you, for example, look at the kind of mind control that even an Heir with an entanglement of 4 can do, imagine entanglement 8 Heirs. Such a creature is going to be able to create its own conspiracies without some larger organization.

              I do feel international conspiracies of Accursed are going to be more political think tank and Mafia than Illuminati - they agree on the same things and trade intel and money regularly, but they aren't consciously coordinating with direct orders, they're a group of peers who are working towards a general goal and don't care about things that aren't under their remit. Which is good, because that allows players to break in as power players pretty easily, and makes it so that crews remain relevant at high Entanglement - a conspiracy is a crew that's grown long in the tooth and strong in the arm.

                  While there's no global spanning secret conspiracies, I do think the Curseborne setting could benefit tremendously from the idea of different secret societies, organizations, and factions that are cross lineage/family.

                  It's quite easy to see, for example... an Eco Terrorism faction made up of Outcasts, Primals wanting to protect unspoiled areas and going after the worst corporate offenders. Throw in some Dead who were killed by the despoilers and are now back for revenge.

                  By the same token, an organization of "Guardian Angels" who look hunt down human traffickers, whether they be human or supernatural. Freeing people in human slavery, saving children from evil cults as well as mundane abusers.

                  On the flipside, a group of Accursed dedicated to dealing with human beings as little more than Cattle.. Food for the Hungry, Emotions for the Dead, and sacrifices for the sorcerers.. would make a great organization to oppose and fight against.

                  In particular I think these types of secret societies and factions would work great for those who want to do something more than just street level surviving night to night play.

                      I do have some alternative takes, though.

                      I will note, though, that the "Families" designation is a lot more flexible than Clan or Bloodline, though. The Premier, for example, is probably as close to a Camarilla/Technocracy style organization as you's going to find.                       As a Sorcerer, you join up with that group when you want to be incredibly rich and powerful. Likewise, other Sorcerer groups are determined by your ideology as opposed to what type of supernatural you are.

                      I wouldn't be surprised if there's also a method of switching Families that we'll get more insight into the game goes on.
