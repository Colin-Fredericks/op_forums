          I thought that might be the case...

          Well, then, I feel I would be remiss not to share what I did come up with, for anyone to use (or modify) freely. All ideas provided below are intentionally limited to two tags - I'll get to the why in a moment. First up:


          Armor

          SPORTS GEAR - < No tags >
          Padded protective gear for high-impact sports.
          Dark Eras: cloth and leather gambesons

          WORK CLOTHES - Concealable
          Heavy, protective clothing for jobsite safety.
          Also used for: leather coats and jackets
          Dark Eras: work clothes

          HAZMAT SUIT - Exquisite Material
          A sealed suit to protect against nuclear, biological, or chemical hazards.

          BALLISTIC JACKET - Padded
          A basic military jacket composed of bullet-resistant fibers.
          Dark Eras: bone armors, boiled leather scale and lamellar armors

          BALLISTIC VEST - Concealable + Padded
          A low-profile bullet-resistant fiber vest wearable under other clothes.
          Dark Eras: chain shirts

          BUNKER GEAR - Exquisite Material + Padded
          A firefighter's suit, made to protect from heat and falling debris alike.

          RIOT GEAR - Padded (x2)
          A heavily-padded suit for riot control, resistant to repeated strikes.
          Dark Eras: chain hauberks, brigandine, coat-of-plates, metal scale and lamellar armors

          BALLISTIC PLATE - Impenetrable + Padded
          A ballistic jacket with added bullet-proof ceramic plate inserts.
          Dark Eras: metal banded and plate armors

          Updates: I moved around a couple "Dark Eras" items (mostly just the full-body chain armor and its equivalents being separated from the boiled leather and bone armors to match "riot gear"). The tag choices generally still follow the same logic here: Impenetrable for "hard" or bulletproof armors, Padded applied once for any actual armor as opposed to protective clothing and twice for the heavier but non-bulletproof items, Concealable if it is or can be worn under normal outerwear, and Exquisite Material for a couple items of modern protective wear designed against NBC or fire hazards.

          Old Notes: Looking at the Blessed Plate Relic, I noticed right away that the game seems to want mundane gear limited to two tags. The Relic provides Elemental Resistance (Fire) and Exquisite Material as "magical" benefits, and uses Impenetrable and Padded to represent a mundane suit of plate armor underneath the magic. From here, I derived an "upper limit" for mundane equipment, and worked my way down. I default to common modern items for naming purposes on each profile, but will note when alternative items (including pre-modern equipment) use the same Tags.

          To explain my thinking in the tag choices here: I used Impenetrable exclusively for "hard" armors - hard-plated articles that will stop bullets, even "armor piercing" bullets - while the first application of Padded separates "soft" armors from non-armor items which still offer some degree of protection from bodily harm. As for the work clothes and Concealable - the tag itself does specify "or indistinguishable from ordinary clothing."

              Projectile Weapons

              PISTOL - Concealable
              A small, easily concealed firearm.
              Dark Eras: blowguns, slings, pistol crossbows, pre-modern pistols

              SHOTGUN - < Variable tags >
              A long-barreled firearm capable of accepting a variety of ammunition.
              Variable tags determined by type of ammunition used:
              - Birdshot: Long Range + Wounding
              - Buckshot, Slugs: Brutal + Wounding
              - Beanbag, Rubber Shot: Stunning
              - Rock Salt: Painful
              - Magnesium ("Dragon's Breath"): Flaming
              Dark Eras: blunderbusses (buckshot only)

              RIFLE - Long Range + < Variable tag >
              A long-barreled firearm made for precision shooting.
              Variable tag determined by type of ammunition used:
              - Full Metal Jacket: Piercing
              - Hollowpoint: Wounding

              MACHINE PISTOL - Brutal
              A pistol-sized automatic firearm.

              ASSAULT RIFLE - Brutal + < Variable tag >
              A long-barreled firearm made for automatic fire.
              Variable tag determined by type of ammunition used:
              - Full Metal Jacket: Piercing
              - Hollowpoint: Wounding

              MACHINE GUN - Heavy Weapon
              A large, normally crew-served weapon, too heavy to wield in hand for all but the strongest.

              FLAMETHROWER - Flaming
              A weapon that sprays a line of burning fuel.

              TASER - Painful + Stunning
              A modified pistol that fires darts electrified by trailing wires.

              CROSSBOW: Piercing + Wounding
              A bow with added mechanisms to draw, hold, and release the string.
              Dark Eras: muskets (unless modern armors are available)

              BOW - Wounding
              A muscle-powered weapon using an elastic string to launch arrows.
              Also used for: sawn-off shotguns (loaded with birdshot, buckshot, or slugs; other ammunition uses the same tags as in a normal shotgun)
              Dark Eras: muskets (if modern armors are available), dragons (pistol blunderbusses)

              Updates: Many more changes here. Concealable is now restricted to weapons that can be *easily* hidden under clothing (in short, not a shotgun, even a sawn-off). Long Range is exclusively a property of precision-shooting rifles (ignoring arcing fire from bows). Stunning is now reserved for "less-lethal" weapons designed to briefly immobilize a target through "cushioned" impact or electric shock, while Painful is added for "less-lethal" weapons that produce a lasting "stinging" effect. Piercing is restricted to weapons which can *effectively* penetrate ballistic cloth (with muskets picking it up in Dark Eras where ballistic cloth isn't a thing) - this does leave no distinction in tags between pistols and pre-modern weapons like slings, but that and the issue with muskets are why I chose to move most pre-modern items to the newly-added "Dark Eras" notes.

              Other tags retain the same general logic as before: Heavy Weapon for weapons that begin to exceed personal scale (though it could also be used for fragmentation grenades to make purchasing Tricks for extra targets easier), Wounding for weapons with large or expanding projectiles designed for significant tissue damage, Brutal for personal-scale automatics (and buckshot), Flaming for incendiary weapons.

              Old Notes: Expanding from what I inferred for armor, I divided weapons into three general categories - blunt, edged, and projectile - centered around three tags - Stunning, Wounding, and Piercing, respectively. Not all weapons in each category carry the associated tag (knives, for example, lack Wounding to differentiate them from one-handed swords), and there is some crossover between categories (especially with Piercing and Wounding). Blunt and edged weapons use Close Combat (though do include thrown weapons with the Versatile tag), while projectile weapons use Ranged Combat. Brutal is reserved for especially large or hard-hitting Close Combat weapons, or for shotguns and automatics among Ranged Combat weapons.

              (I deliberately reserved Heavy Weapon exclusively for the machine gun, making it the only personal weapon listed to have Power Advantage. Everything else is absolutely intended to run into trouble against the Durability Advantage of medieval plate and modern ballistic jackets with ceramic plate inserts - not even a chainsaw gets Heavy Weapon, as will be seen later. For the combat-focused Accursed: learn Brutality. Use Brutality. Love Brutality. It's available to everyone at Entanglement 3.)

                  Edged Weapons

                  SHIV - Concealable
                  Any small, sharp object or sharpened piece of debris.
                  Examples: balisong, butcher's knife, flint knife, kitchen knife, pocket knife, prison shank, razor, scalpel

                  DAGGER - Concealable + Light Weapon
                  A small blade made to be used as a weapon.
                  Examples: combat knife, hunting knife, kirpan, keris, kukri, tanto
                  Also used for: push dagger (katar), sai

                  THROWING KNIVES - Light Weapon + Versatile
                  A set of several small knives balanced for throwing.
                  Also used for: chakram, shuriken

                  SLASHING SWORD - Light Weapon + Wounding
                  A one-handed sword designed for cutting or chopping strikes.
                  Examples: arming sword, cutlass, falchion, jian, khopesh, macuahuitl, saber, scimitar, shotel, spatha, talwar, wakizashi

                  THRUSTING SWORD - Light Weapon + Piercing
                  A one-handed sword designed to thrust between armor joints.
                  Examples: estoc, rapier, tuck

                  LONGSWORD - Brutal + Wounding
                  A massive two-handed (or hand-and-a-half) sword.
                  Examples: claymore, katana, odachi, zhanmadao, zweihander

                  SPEAR - Reach + Versatile
                  A long pole with a sharp tip, designed to be wielded in hand or thrown.
                  Examples: assegai, harpoon, javelin, pilum, trident
                  Also used for: atlatl, woomera

                  HALBERD - Reach + Wounding
                  A long spear fitted with an axe head below the tip.
                  Also used for: glaive, horseman's pick, lance, lucerne hammer, naginata, partisan, pike, poleaxe, tepoztopilli, yari

                  AXE - Wounding + Wrecking
                  A large, broad-bladed cutting tool, well-suited for use as a weapon.
                  Examples: battle axe, dane axe, fire axe, masakari, parashu, sagaris
                  Also used for: adze, ice axe, machete, mattock, pickaxe, scythe

                  HATCHET - Versatile + Wounding
                  A small axe weighted for throwing.
                  Examples: francisca, tomahawk

                  CHAIN WHIP - Light Weapon + Reach
                  A chain of small metal rods connecting a dart to a handle.
                  Also used for: kusarigama, rope dart, urumi

                  CHAINSAW - Deadly + Wounding
                  A large tree-felling tool, unwieldy in the hands of all but the strongest.

                  Updates: Fewer to note here, though axes now get the Wrecking tag to represent their function as tools. "Conventional" whips have been moved to the bludgeons, and the claws / bagh nahk has been relisted as an example alternative use of the knuckleduster profile; the "chain whip" listed here is the wushu (Chinese martial arts) weapon.

                  As for the tags themselves - Concealable is used for weapons that can be easily hidden in clothing, as with projectile weapons. Light Weapon is applied to weapons that specifically benefit from finesse (bearing in mind that we currently lack any means outside of magic to apply it to unarmed attacks). Versatile is used for throwing weapons (where the second tag can still be applied normally). Wounding is for weapons that produce large laceration wounds. Piercing is limited to thrusting swords which sacrifice their cutting edges in favor of a design that exploits the joints in hard armors. Brutal is used as both upgrade and replacement for Light Weapon on heavier longswords. Reach is used for polearms and longer lashing weapons. Deadly is still used exclusively for the chainsaw as a dubious "upgrade" over the longsword's Brutal, to make the chainsaw a terrifying "one-hit kill" weapon against unarmored targets, but just a *slightly* more dangerous improvised weapon against anything with any amount of armor (including "armor" provided by spells).

                  As an aside, depending on how your Storyguide reads the restrictions on applying the Deadly and Wounding tags with respect to armor, remember that it may be necessary to purchase the Sunder Trick before either can be applied to armored targets, even after their bonus injury boxes from armor have been exhausted. If your table uses that interpretation of these tags, Spells that provide the benefits of physical armor implicitly render the subject almost completely immune to both tags - the Red Death spell can get around this as long as Mental Fortitude is not active, as can Bombardment or Weaponmaster's Skill (by choosing Elemental Attunement) absent some form of armor with Elemental Resistance.

                  Old Notes: Throwing knives lose Concealable because of the two tag limit and the assumption that you are hiding more than one of them. Bayonets are listed as a variant of pike assuming they are being mounted on the barrel of a rifle or musket - some are, of course, designed to be separated from the firearm and wielded in hand as a dagger.

                      Blunt Weapons

                      CLUB - < No tags >
                      Any small blunt object used as a weapon, from sticks to police batons.
                      Examples: billy club, crowbar, jitte, nightstick, small hammer, sports bat or stick, tonfa, tree branch, walking stick, weighted flashlight
                      Also used for: most improvised weapons, pistol whip, rifle buttstroke

                      STUN BATON - Painful + Stunning
                      A handheld prod with an electrified tip.

                      SAP - Concealable + Stunning
                      A small baton with a weighted head, made for concealment and king hits.
                      Examples: blackjack, cosh
                      Also used for: war fans (tessen)

                      SLEDGEHAMMER - Brutal + Wrecking
                      A blunt tool with a heavy weighted hammer head.
                      Also used for: mace, maul, morning star, otsuchi, sali, shillelagh, war club, war hammer

                      STAFF - Brutal + Reach
                      A light weight but long stick weapon.
                      Examples: bo, gun, quarterstaff

                      FLAIL - Brutal + Light Weapon
                      A pair of short rods or a rod and weighted ball connected by a short chain.
                      Examples: nunchaku, three-section staff

                      WHIP - Painful + Reach
                      A flexible, stinging cord designed for pain compliance.

                      BOLAS - Stunning + Versatile
                      Hard, spherical bludgeons connected by a long cord, designed for snaring targets at a distance.

                      BOOMERANG - Brutal + Versatile
                      A curved club, designed to maintain an even flight path when thrown.
                      Examples: rungu, ula, waddy

                      KNUCKLEDUSTER - Concealable + Worn
                      A hard striking surface worn over the fingers.
                      Examples: brass knuckles, weighted-knuckle gloves
                      Also used for: bagh nakh, gauntlet, kakute, steel-toed boot

                      SHIELD - Shield + Stunning
                      A barrier carried in hand or strapped to an arm for intercepting attacks.

                      METEOR HAMMER - Reach + < Variable tag >
                      A length of chain connecting paired metal striking heads.
                      Variable tag determined by type of attached striking head:
                      - Solid head: Brutal + Reach
                      - Burning brazier: Flaming + Reach

                      Updates: Stunning has been removed from a lot of these, and is now restricted to electric / immobilizing weapons (in like fashion to the projectile weapons). Painful has been added for "stinging" weapons, and Wrecking for larger hammer-headed weapons to reflect more destructive uses as tools. Brutal picks up a lot of the slack from the removal of Stunning, and now serves as the tag to emphasize bludgeons made as *deliberate* weapons. Shields are no longer split by size, and use only one profile (as the buckler really couldn't justify having Light Weapon). The meteor hammer now has a variable tag to represent the different heads it might be constructed with.

                      Concealable, Light Weapon, Reach, and Versatile follow the same logic as with blades. Shield is for shields. Worn is for articles of clothing as weapons and weapons worn on the hands or feet (while I could see an argument for shields to have it, bucklers *don't* work that way, and larger shields should have Stunning for bashing foes).

                      Old Notes: The boomerang lacks Returning on purpose - apply it with magic, or swap the Brutal tag for it if you want combat boomerangs that somehow come back to the thrower. And I know Shaolin meteor hammers were never actually used in historical combat, but with the machine gun, flamethrower, and chainsaw all being a thing, blunt weapons deserve stylish options, too!

                      Final Comments: A huge thank you to Nobody Special and Sergeant Brother for challenging my thinking in making this list and pushing me to defend some of my original choices and reconsider others. You two are amazing, and even if neither of you ever use it, this list would be far worse without your input. This list obviously isn't any kind of "be-all-end-all" on the matter - I'm no subject-matter expert, just another hobbyist like (most) of the rest of us here, and the game directly tells Storyguides to let players make their own choices on tags - but I hope it provides people a useful starting point in choosing tags as a way of both (narratively) framing and (mechanically) informing the differences in what kinds of equipment their characters are carrying.

                                  Originally posted by Orphan81 View Post
                                  How combat and Damage works in Storypath is something I'm having some difficulty groking.

                                  I don't suppose one of you can break it down for me like I'm 5?

                                  It's strange there's no damage values to the weapons yet we have health levels of varying types.                                   Rather than trying to explain it like you're five, it might help if I explain it like you're somebody familiar with WoD/CofD and not with SPU (that's basically where I am so I think you'll wind up on a similar journey to me).

                                  So first off, context. My experience is that WoD/CofD has a massive Rocket Tag issue. I'm running Vampire the Dark Ages and while the PCs are fairly experienced by now it didn't take us that long to get to the point where the Brujah just deletes anything he wins initiative against. The SPU system seems explicitly designed to avoid that, which I think is good.

                                  The other bit of context is that this system seems designed to be cinematic: you fight harder as you get more injured, low-level NPCs only have one Hit Point, PCs can take a hell of a pounding before they get Taken Out and they only die if it's dramatically appropriate.

                                  As for how it actually works, this is definitely an unlearn-everything-you-have-learned moment.

                                  When you attack somebody you make a Close Combat + Might or Ranged Combat + Dexterity roll against a difficulty their Defence. This is always fixed for NPCs, PCs get to boost their defence for the full round by taking a Defensive Action (this means rolling dice equal to their Stamina). As with all rolls in SPU pretty much all the bonus effects you get come from buying tricks.

                                  If you successfully attack an opponent, which means if you get enough successes to match their Defence, you deal one damage. This is always the same no matter what you attack with.

                                  If you want to do extra damage, your only option by default is to buy the Critical Trick, which costs 3 successes (over and above the successes you need to match difficulty). This means you do 2 damage. Some Spells allow you to do more damage than that but they're extremely rare.

                                  Weapons never just do extra damage. Having a weapon grants an Enhancement on attack actions (this is just part of the general rules for equipment) and each weapon has up to two tags that let it inflict extra Status Effects.

                                  I think for me what made this make sense was taking it out of the white room and into the context of actual play; that is, it needs to be seen as integrated with the Antagonist rules and the prevalence of the Rocket Tag problem in the other games.

                                  Adversaries/NPCs in Curseborne all have a Template of either Shiver, Fright, Terror, Nightmare or Shattered Space. This template gives them either 1, 4, 10, or 12 Injuries (shattered spaces and terrors both get 10). Notice that this means:
                                  • A Shiver goes down to a single hit, nearly always.
                                  • A Fright goes down in 2-3 hits depending on how effectively you can buy criticals.
                                  • A Terror or Nightmare takes a long time to wear down and so you'll actually get mileage out of things like Bleeding and Poison. They're almost guaranteed to survive at least one round of combat.
                                  Consistently (and I strongly value consistency in game design), SPU prioritises how something works in play over what it's supposed to represent in the real world. It's not literally the case that a very experienced Venator or Sister of the Cross is somehow extremely resistant to bullets, it's that if the purpose of an experienced Venator or Sister of the Cross is to be a mechanical threat to the party, they need to not be trivially removable with a single gunshot.

                                  Of course there are massive personal preference issues here. Some players want to feel like an human-sized human-shaped target should always be killable with a single well-placed gunshot because they always want the option to do that Indiana Jones scene where he just shoots the guy. Others prefer there to be built in protection for pacing. Although I'll want to test to be sure I *lean* towards preferring the SPU method because I like that the game has thought about the alpha strike problem for me.

                                  Hope that helps.

                                          I liken it to Invincible, which is almost horror like in it's presentation. These Supernatural beings are capable of tearing mortals and one another apart quite easily.

                                          It's not terrible, but it certainly changes the way things feel if every fight that matters is a slug fest and there are 1 hit point "Goons" the PCs are supposed to be able to take in swarms.
                                          I think it depends a lot on how you imagine horror working, which gets to be a very complicated question when it's horror from the PoV of the monsters.

                                          I do generally think it matches my preferences. I don't think fights against significant enemies will turn into slugfests (although the quadratic effect of higher-tier enemies both having higher Defences and inflicting greater Complications might be an issue, although not one that *reduces* damage) so much as just be pretty guaranteed to not be over in a single round. Basically this and the strong reliance on Reaction Dread Powers (which I also have mixed feelings on) should eliminate situations where your big final villain either needs Fortitude and Celerity out the wazoo or else they're guaranteed to get one-round bodied without taking actions.

                                          Similarly I don't mind one-hit-point Mooks because I do think that in general the average supernatural should be able to violently dispatch the average mortal without much difficulty. I think I've joked in other threads about how common it was in Vampire (and it's a particularly noted issue with V5) for a hungry vampire to lose control and attack an innocent, only to lose the fight with the innocent because if you don't take the right skill and discipline combinations a vampire is just not very dangerous, especially if they're low on blood.

                                          Basically my preferred version of this kind of horror is one where regular mortals are extremely squishy (covered by 1HP shivers) but narratively significant characters don't just get wiped out by lucky dice rolls or bad initiative. So to me it works well.

                                          [Edit]

                                          I think the other thing I'd say is that I often find deadly combat an ironically bad fit for horror. To me the actual risk of game mechanical death is often less scary than it is frustrating. Obviously there are games where random, ignominious death are part of the horror atmosphere (especially ones that skew Lovecraftian) but I think to me I tend to find PC death in particular often leads to a bit of a "welp, better write up a new character" feeling which isn't super immersive.
