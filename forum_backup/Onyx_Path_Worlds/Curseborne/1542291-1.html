                                        The range bands will most often come up in combat. If you spend two of your actions fleeing and nobody stops you, then congratulations: you're out of range and just as you can't be hit, neither can they. There may of course be obstacles or obstructions preventing this movement, which is why white room playtests serve very little purpose.

                                        If you're trying to flee and your attacker is pursuing you, they get to move just as you do. So while it may sound strange to move up to medium range, if your attacker decides "I'm going to move up to medium range too" then congratulations: effectively, you've both moved the same distance.

                                            And are range bands meant to be measured individual to individual or are characters grouped into bands together? Like if I move "up to medium" am I now in medium with all the characters who were previously in medium? Or am I moving to medium relative to where I was? Or to medium away from another character who was previously at Close?

                                            [Edit]

                                            The way I *think* it works, just going by the movement rules in the Ashcan is that effectively any given character can be in one of five positions relative to your character:

                                            Close - You are near enough to physically touch them. If you want to attack them you can just use your action for it.

                                            Short - You aren't near enough to touch them but you can *get* near enough with a reflexive action. If you want to attack them you make a reflective action to move then a standard action to attack.

                                            Medium - Far enough away that you have to spend your whole turn moving to get into Close range. You *can* attack somebody at this distance but only by making a "rush" which is always rolled on Might + Athletics and seems intend to represent a kind of desperation body check not a considered attack with a weapon.

                                            Long - this is where I think it might get unintuitive for some people. As I read it, because range bands are effectively on a log scale, if somebody is a Long range then under default movement rules you can *never* get into Close range with them in a standard fight. Effectively you need to have a different sort of Scene to close the distance then so the fight afterwards.

                                            Extreme - See Long bit more so.

                                            To put it another way, the movement rules effectively divide characters into exactly two groups: the ones you can reach in a single combat round and the ones you can never reasonably reach.

                                            I think this is what makes the system traceable with multiple combatants, for any given person a PC might want to get up close and personal with there's only really two options: "you can't" or "you can get there in a turn". It's abstract but it means you don't really have to track anything.

                                                Adding this as a separate post because it'll probably be long, but I think it's a decent example of how this is meant to work and also what Matthew might be talking about when he says that the system's not designed for white room scenarios.

                                                Version 1:

                                                Storyguide: Okay there are three Zombie flesh eaters coming your way. Also, like always, you're in a totally featureless white void. The Zombies are currently at medium range.

                                                PC1 Okay, Zombies are pretty slow and we have guns and they don't. Standard strategy is retreat under fire. I move back 10 meters and shoot a zombie.

                                                Storyguide Right you hit the zombie but don't incapacitate it. Moving back 10 meters does nothing because you're still in Medium range, the zombie rushes you and attacks, take an injury.

                                                PC2 Crap, that didn't work. Okay I just take my whole action to move away from the zombie as far as I possibly can.

                                                Storyguide Right, that still leaves you in Medium range the Zombie rushes you and you take an injury.

                                                PC3 I look for a different game system.
                                                Now obviously you *can* run it like that and it *is* RAW but the game isn't designed to function well when run that way and that's not intrinsically a flaw in the game's design, most games don't work well if you don't work with them.

                                                Version 2

                                                Storyguide The three zombie flesh eaters burst through the door of the department store you're hiding in. They're at medium range.

                                                PC1 Okay I don't want to get rushed by these guys but I feel like if I have to actuality get something between us if I want to stop that happening. I'll move back. So reflexive action to move to a location in Short range, make sure it's behind a shelf or something, then shoot a zombie.

                                                PC2 I think we need to buy some time. If you give me the spotlight next I'll drag some clothes racks together to make a barricade then pull back to join you.

                                                Storyguide Remember that means the Zombies will probably go first next turn.

                                                PCS 1 and 2 Probably worth it,.

                                                PC3 Screw this, I run, scattering crap over the floor as I go to make it harder for Zombies to follow.

                                                Storyguide Okay, the Zombies approach the barricade and that kind of uses their actions because they're slow and it was far forwards. Then I'm going to have one of them take an action to start clearing the barricade then I'm going to pass it back to...let's say 3, because I don't want you lot to get six turns in a row.

                                                PC3 Cool can I get a recap of where everybody is?

                                                Storyguide You're medium from the Zombies, Short from your friends. They're Short from the Zombies, Close to each other, and all the Zombies are Close to each other too, what's your action?
                                                And obviously this is an idealised scenario where the players and GM all know the system, agree about how the system works, and are cooperating to make things come out appropriately, but that's kinda his the system is meant to be used (as far as I can tell) and the rules for movement are all the same as they are in the first example. If you want to keep a melee opponent out your face you can't just kite them, you need to give them something to use their Standard Action on that isn't Rushing you.

                                                    Second option is the one that make sense. The idea that they are always at medium distance is the main point here, if you can’t in a round go further than medium range, getting to long range takes some time, really long time for a combat scene.

                                                    Are they capable of running after you? Yes, so running will just buy you some time, they will eventually reach you if you stop to shot and they don’t. And that’s where the range bands are better, you don’t count steps, it is or not within range. If the challenge is to reach a safe position before they get you, it’s not a combat scene, it’s a chase, and chases have some interesting rules to play, like throwing obstacles to the chaser and better control of the “distance” you have from them, not measured in meters, but in successes.

                                                        The second option is clearly better, but that was the intent of the example. It was intended as an illustration of the difference between a white room scenario and one that actually engages with what Dungeon World would have called "fictional positioning".

                                                        I will add though that what it was *also* attempting to illustrate is quite how acute the problems get if you don't engage with fictional positioning and rely (as you can in many other games) on the abstract rules of the combat system to protect you.

                                                        In most games it's possible through *pure rules text* to put yourself in a position where you can shoot at a melee threat and they can't close the distance and attack you in a single turn, possibly for several turns.

                                                        It's not just that if your enemy can run and you back up shooting that they will catch up with you *eventually*, it's that no matter what you do, no matter how you move, there is no purely game mechanical way to stop an enemy reaching you in a single turn and making a melee attack.
