    How many Cursed in a city?

    10-08-2024, 08:53 PM
    When the game talks about family hierarchy, and family heads, is it talking globally, or per city? If each family has enough member in a city to form a hierarchy with it's own leaders, that must mean there are hundreds of Cursed in every city. Or will a city only have one or two of each family? Do members of different families group together to form organizations, like the Covenants/Packs/Freeholds/etc? Or does each family pretty much keep to itself?

      As always, the general rule is "However much is needed to make the game work." If Curseborne has learned any lessons from it's predeccesors, we're never going to get an exact answer for how many Accursed is in any given setting asides from the absolute most broadest generalizations

      Going into more specific idea, So far Chapter 1 and the Lineages have been a little vague about how they interact with increasing spatial scope-games are set for an idea of being local in the corebook, and that most of your shit is the result of dealing with the local iterations of Families and other Organizations, but Families like The Wardens, House Bathory, and Z.E.D & Colleagues are described as being not only large enough but centralized enough that it assumes a global setup, of which local versions are explicitly branches of the larger Family.

      I imagine future sourcebooks, including the undoubted ones focusing specifically on the Lineages, and I imagine we'll get touches on explanation in the Storytelling chapter.

      In the meantime, the general vibe I would say I've taken is that Families describe the most global, top-down view on them but that most Families that don't particularly mention hierarchy or centrality are mostly defined by their local branch rather than being solely driven by that view, whereas those that do mention hierarchy/centrality are much more uniform across various settings. And as taht relates to populations.

      Well, back to the "How many do you need?" answer.

                      Raccoons aside I think there's a couple of legacy assumptions baked into this, one of which CB explicitly rejects and one of which I'm hoping it will reject.

                      Assumption 1 is that there's any kind of *problem* with there bring hundreds of Accursed around in any given city. VtM was predicated on the Masquerade, he idea that nobody could ever find out that supernaturals are real or Bad Things Will Happen.

                      CofD and Curseborne assume a much higher level of background weirdness. If Curseborne in particular the assumption seems to be that most people have actually encountered the supernatural at some point, even if just in the form of a Liminal Space or, well, a Curse.

                      Assumption 2 (and this is the one I *hope* they hard break from) is that the City is the base unit of the setting. It only really made sense for VtM and got stranger and stranger the more dimension hopping the games started to involve.

                      By default, the game is set in the modern world. People can travel, use phones, send emails. Cities don't need to be these isolated fiefdoms. Even if there's only one Zed locally, for example, they can still get backup from the branch office two towns over.

                          As old CoD ST, when creating game, I just write the number of supernatural beings I need for stories and political reasons in area. ( At minimum it's like 20 NPCs, to cover at least one monster main types.) VtM numbers maybe good for more USA eyeballing, or first look, but in Europe it simply not work.

                          I live in City of Lublin, that is rather 9th largest city in Poland. ( We are capitol of Województwo, sorta like USA State area. ) Lublin has circa 340 000 citizens - it would mean we would have 3.4 of monsters by VtM rules. 4 if we round up numbers. Sorry, on 4 NPCs, even with 3-5 PCs I cannot make even smallest vampire domain or mages consillium.

                          If I remember my notes for game I prepared but not run - it was something like that:
                          • 7 vampires
                          • Circa 30 mages ( Lublin is Collages city )
                          • Circa 20 changelings ( we have weird paganism and folklore rules, but also some people missing in area )
                          Not counting werewolves, as I was not sure if wanted to use them in story.

                          And how will it all work in Lublin? Simply, CoD setting has many 'people are forgetting' mechanics for monsters - Masquerade enforcement for vamps, Delirium for wolves, Sleeping Curse for mages, etc. ALSO CoD directly assume EVERYONE had at least once in their life encounter with supernatural - only they repressed their memories. ( See CoD 2E corebook. question 'What you forgotten?' for new characters. )

                          I assume Curseborne has similar assumptions even if we do not see it yet in the text. Especially with much higher empathises on crossovers than CoD.

                                  Originally posted by Nobody Special View Post

                                  There's another problem (in the math sense) that was actually relevant in previous games, and I wish CB does it differently (idk, didn't read anything yet):

                                  Vampires are predators under pressure.

                                  However you frame them, both VtM and VtR vampires have a very important assumption about them: they need 1 unit of blood per night, every night. That, more even than any extra feeding to power their things, makes for an ecological pressure that’s hard to ignore, especially when you know how much this harms humans.

                                  All the calculations about how many critters live in an area started from this single problem. You can (and arguably should) ignore it, but you will have it cross your mind at some point, and will have people asking questions with this in mind. How many vamps before the city crumbles under pure ecological pressure?

                                  If the Hunger need to eat a minimum amount of blood every day, then the problem will stay. You can expect this question to pop out time and again and realism will linger on it. But if they don’t, and I think this works fine, then the whole debacle becomes irrelevant.

                                      Originally posted by Nobody Special View Post
                                      Assumption 2 (and this is the one I *hope* they hard break from) is that the City is the base unit of the setting. It only really made sense for VtM and got stranger and stranger the more dimension hopping the games started to involve.

                                      By default, the game is set in the modern world. People can travel, use phones, send emails. Cities don't need to be these isolated fiefdoms. Even if there's only one Zed locally, for example, they can still get backup from the branch office two towns over.
                                      That's...the exact opposite of how it worked? World of Darkness was a lot more globally oriented on the whole, and if you could get over the individual limitations of travels that normally afflict lower powered characters (assuming such were in place, like with Masqeurade), the mechanics and politics of the setting quickly reveal themselves to cover whole swathes of continents that you could try and work with, at risk of getting jammed in and chewed up by them. There's a reason why if you were a unaffiliated Lasombra in a Camarilla region, you'd basically got set to get fucked.

                                      Chronicles, by contrast, was the really city-oriented style of play, where in even characters that had mass-distance travel more readily baked into their character designs weren't personally invested in doing so-changelings from Lost could theoretically wrestle the Hedge into letting them traverse the world on a low budget and low time-sink, but most changelings want to get home, and even those less picky about their old homes don't want to have to handle the Hedge all that long, and there's more benefit in picking a corner of reality and holing up there. Vampires in Requiem look at the globalization that was starting to happen with the Roman Empire tend to take the Fall of Rome as they experieneced as a warning sign against the practice of being more globally centralized and organized, or the disagreements about what they would have to accomodate for in trying it again becomes the backbone for why they more or less stay fractured (also nevermind how the difference between Generation and Blood Potency relates to why you could have a global conspiracy of vampires in the one game and could expect such to always fall apart in the other). The most powerful splats of Chronicles were the ones more marked by being World-like in terms of global scales for casual participants, but even then the Awakened, the Arisen, and the Unchained still had more reasons to work on the city level than the global level in opposition to World.

                                      Anyhow, Curseborne's main mention of scope being personal, as mentioned in another thread, seems like a answer toward's the either/or approach depicted by the former two, because while that readily translates to a local frame, personal is flexible to the character. If you've got a world-trotting character, then so long as that attempt at world-trotting isn't an attempt to escape the personal scope, then Curseborne is happy work with you. It helps that the Families both seem to be structural enough that connections are easy to come up with without being so omnipresent that their presence or absence sets hard boundaries for anything else-you can design a local area however you want without feeling like it's divorced from the rest of the setting or needing to answer why there *isn't* a unified collective on the global scale.
