    Why No Accurate Nephilim?

    07-12-2026, 04:33 PM
    Okay, so over my decades of playing games, reading stories, and watching TV / Movies, I've seen the term "Nephilim" tossed around for quite a lot of characters. But never have I seen them be presented as anywhere even remotely accurate to the very creatures for which they are supposedly named.


    At best, they'll depict a Nephilim as being half angel, but that's as far as accuracy goes, before they veer off in a completely different direction. While yes, the Nephilim were the offspring of angels mating with humans, it was much worse than that.

    The Nephilim were a race of giants, who inherited the powers of angels and used them to tyrannically oppress the humans living on their land. They also had insatiable appetites which drove them to exhaust all available food sources in the land, before eventually turning to cannibalism in order to stave off their hunger.

    When God found out about their existence, he was so outraged, that he ordered their complete extermination.


    So why is it that so many fictional media, Curseborne included fail to get this bit of mythology correct? Even if the original Nephilim were wiped out, that's not to say that a new generation couldn't be created.

    Heck, that story could actually serve as a basis for WHY they became a family of outcasts in the first place. It's because their creation is now considered to be taboo, and the Outsiders don't want anything to do with them, lest they piss off a higher power.

              The idea of accurate Nephilim is nonsensical. Accurate to what? The Bible?

              Genesis 6:4 "The Nephilim were in the earth in those days, and also after that, when the Bene Elohim came in unto the daughters of men, and they bore children to them; the same were the mighty men that were of old, the men of renown."

              I didn't translate Bene Elohim because how you translate it stems from a lot of theological assumptions. But most importantly this is not a phrase that originally meant "angels" as those are divine messengers. The oldest meaning of Bene Elohim would be the deities of the Canaanite pantheon (as the Canaanites treated them as all the sons - Bene - of the high deity El), and pre-hard monotheist Israelite traditions would have also interpreted this similarly. It's only after the Babylonian Exile that Jewish theology shifted towards monotheism and the Israelite deity merged with El, and thus all of the early traditions of the Bene Elohim had to be reconstructed as servants of this new single deity.

              The phrasing itself is unclear if the Nephilim are their own thing, the Bene Elohim that mated with human women, or the offspring who we're supposed to then associate with demigods and mythological heroes from ancient epics (the mighty men and men of renown).

              Numbers 13:33 "And there we saw the Nephilim, the sons of Anak, who come of the Nephilim; and we were in our own sight as grasshoppers, and so we were in their sight."

              The context of this is that it's the end of the report by the spies sent to scout the land of Canaan before the Israelites end the Exodus and claim the land for themselves. This is where the idea of them being giants probably comes from. Though they're never called giants (and we have characters like Goliath later that repeat the theme of there just being some really big guys among the tribes already living there).

              And that's it. That's all the information you're going to get out of the Bible and older traditions. You have to jump centuries until you get to Enoch 1 and other late post-Exilic writings where we see interpretations of the Nephilim that actually give us any details. And those texts are not uniform in where the Nephilim came from, if they were even inherently bad, and what traits they had that separated them from normal humans.

              Claiming anyone "gets them wrong," is a far greater negative comment on yourself than it is on any author using them for fiction characters/creatures. If you think there's some authoritative source for an accurate depiction of the Nephilim, you need to do a lot more reading on the subject.

Now I'll say this in a RPG the term Nephilim being used for a half angel, half demon, half celestial as I have seen some games do it (Pathfinder 2E for one) is interesting and it leads to paths where much story can be added in but I see no reason why someone could't take these beings offered by the various game systems and "flavor" them however they want. If you want all Nephilim in your game to be more like the Biblical Nephilim that's cool just make a few adjustments, and you have power hungry, gluttonous monsters. I am a Christian and I don't really have a problem with taking some creative license with the word Nephilim because Biblically so little is known about them. To learn more that may or may not be true you have to go to the book of Enoch or other extra Biblical sources. It's kind of like Vampire the Masquerade using Cain as the first Vampire utter rubbish as far as reality goes but a nice storyline. One of the things we do when we decide to play RPGs in the first place is decide that we are going to kiss reality goodbye for a little while while we romp around in worlds or multiverses of pure imagination. So if something doesn't mesh with the real world or our view of the real world that's because it's designed not to. It's a feature not necessarily a bug.
