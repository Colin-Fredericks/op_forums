        I think you might be misconstruing what I mean by "play against type" and are being *slightly* overgenerous to the system in interpreting 6 in a spread as enough to really qualify as a focus. Sure you can max out one attribute but as you say that locks the others to being merely average.

        I think that's fine-ish for Mental because Intelligence, Cunning and Resolve (I believe those are the SPU terms) don't really go together that closely so there's a reasonable chance you might want to play somebody who is street smart but not book smart or very resolute but not otherwise "clever" in any sense.

        I'd say it falls below minimum for the Social and Physical spheres. Most physical or social archetypes kind of need a 4/3/3 spread to sell the fantasy. Most athletes for example, evening college level athletes, need notable amounts of both strength, dexterity, and stamina, ideally with an emphasis on the one that's most useful for their sport. Combat wombats need all three and again ideally an emphasis on their particular style so your bruisers don't look the same as your dex monkeys.

        The other thing I'd say is that for me "playing against type" means what you're bad at as well as what you're good at. It's currently impossible to create a Hyde, Ascetic or Chimera, for example without 6 points in Mental. But one of the major themes of the game is that you often *don't* get to choose your Family. I shouldn't have to fit on with my smart, cunning, resolute peers.

        basically the way I see it, 3 points in a spread is bog standard, 6 is notably above average but not great, 9 is excellent. I don't need any given splat to be able to go to a 3 or a 9 in every attribute but I do think a good rule of thumb is that they should always be able to go above and below 6.

        As for the D&D comparison. Firstly, these are very different types of game. If you play a Wizard in D&D you are ultimately choosing to play a character who does wizard stuff. By comparison if I choose to play a Lycan in Curseborne I could mean to play all kinds of things. I could want to be a frontline combat monster or I could want to be a cunning but cowardly fox shifter, or a prom queen who hates being a Werewolf.

        On top of which, D&D actually does let me put my Attribute points wherever I'd like even if it's not optimal for my class. If I want to build a wizard who wears armour and gifts with a sword and isn't especially smart I not only *can do that* but I will be about as good at fighting with a sword as most low level characters. Most importantly, I will be better at fighting with a sword than most town guards and bandits so by the standards of the world, my sword wizard actually works.
        3 is significantly above average. I can guarantee you if you saw the stats of every IRL athlete you'd see 2s and 3s far more often than 4s and 5s. So yes, I do think 5 in one stat while average in the other 2, or above average in all 3 is plenty to consider yourself good in a category.

        Now our of curiosity, outside of an entirely arbitrary "I want a certain number of dogs in a certain place" what do you think you can't play? Prom Queen who hates being a werewolf? Sure you can do that. Cunning but cowardly fox shifter? Yup.

                                                            Originally posted by ShadowcatX View Post
                                                            Um... no. The cheerleader isn't rolling might alone, nor stamina. The Cheerleader is rolling a dice pool consisting of an attribute plus a skill. That skill is, in this case, probably athletics, and they probably have it at a fairly decent level. Keep in mind a dice pool of 4 is going to succeed significantly more often than it fails. Also, keep in mind that a failure doesn't mean they're falling and breaking a leg it could be as simple as a false start attempting to get atop the pyramid.
                                                            A dice pool of *two* succeeds more often than it fails if you're only rolling against Difficulty 1.

                                                            I think this is the heart of the disconnect.

                                                            You seem to be looking at what the books (possibly not even the SPU books, possibly the CofD core rulebooks) say something represents and working from there. I'm looking at how it actually feels in a game.

                                                            If a cheerleader, a good cheerleader, one who could actually cheerlead for a living, is Might 2 Athletics 2 while I'm Might 2 Athletics 1 (since I don't take zero exercise) that means they, on average, only get 0.4 more Hits than I do on cheerleading tasks. They have a 26% chance of failing outright to do might-based cheerleading tasks compared to my (with *no cheerleading experience*) 34%.

                                                            A character with 2s in their physical stats and 2 dots in Athletics doesn't actually, in-game, feel athletic. They feel pretty mid. And it gets worse if difficulties go higher.


                                                            Again, NPCs don't play by the same rules as PCs.
                                                            They don't play by the same character creation rules, but a dice pool is a dice pool.


                                                            No, in your example an "average cop" (who has both special requirements and special training for their job) doing what they do best is 6 dice. That's not at all the same thing as "an average person doing what they do averagely is 6 dice."
                                                            But now you're suggesting that the special requirements and training of the average beat cop (which let's be real are not exactly stringent) require them to have dice pools of 6. So that's what we're aiming for.


                                                            You think it's impossible to get a dice pool of 6 without 3 in an attribute? You can have 1 in an attribute and still have a dice pool of 7 without getting into any supernatural abilities.
                                                            As far as I can tell in SPU nothing adds to dice pools directly (it adds Enhancements) so you need 1/5, 2/4, 3/3, 4/2 or 5/1.
