              Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
              Toon Force is one of the most hideously over powered abilities to exist in all of fiction.

              No, you can not play a Toon, because it would be impossible to balance!
              That's not necessarily true.

              Most of it is fluff, anything can happen to lead to a result, but a Toon is constrained by the result. The central power is only half a dozen effects that are strong, but not that hard to codify:

              - Immortality: nothing can kill a Toon.

              - Regeneration: a Toon won't stay out of action.

              - Toon Access: a Toon's access to an item is a binary state, either too easy or impossible.

              - Physics of Perception: a Toon must follow the same laws as everyone else, but must notice it.

              - Reciprocity: all direct contact follow the Laws of Toon Force – or not – without a choice.

              - Presentation: everything a Toon does needs a performance that takes time, effort, and visual.

              What this means as balance:

              1. Anything that can stop a normal human will in principle stop a Toon. It won't be for long, but it will work.

              2. The most powerful effect is not staying out of action for long, but not increased power.

              3. Everything else is two ways. For each effect that benefit a Toon, an equivalent hinder them.

              4. Surprise effect is strong, but vulnerability to genre understanding too. It's easy to explore Toon Force against a Toon when you're not in turn bound by its laws.

              5. It's profoundly anti-efficient. A Toon can't do anything the easy way, only the Toon way. That can and will get in the way almost constantly.

              What balances the fabled Toon Force is that it is governed by storytelling. It's unsettling as it won't follow a direct cause -> effect mechanic, but it does follow a meaning -> effect general law that you can interpret through the lens of a Universal Audience, the idea that they're judged by the very audience.

              Other games already worked this, but I'll bring to mind two where those laws have precisely to reconcile with mundane reality: Ravenloft and Changeling the Dreaming.

              What both make to ease things out is simply empower the ST/GM with the Storytelling God: the Dark Powers in Ravenloft, the Dreaming in Changeling, are external forces that judge what happens arbitrarily based on genre expectation
