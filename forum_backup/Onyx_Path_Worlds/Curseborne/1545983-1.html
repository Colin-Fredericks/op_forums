                        It gets tricky when we mix medieval and modern weapons. By medieval standards, a long bow is absolutely a long range weapon. In the Middle Ages, a crossbow is an armor piercing weapon. When you compare either to a modern rifle and neither is impressive.

                        My first inclination is to make hunting rifles have Long Range and Brutal, but I can see expanding ammo having wounding instead. That opens up the possibility of different ammo types having different tags.

                        I think that longbows should have wounding. In the modern era I don’t know if I would give them anything else. In that case, wounding would represent the arrow stuck in your body tearing your flesh and wiggling around making you have to be careful or remove it.

                        I could actually see giving some bludgeoning medieval weapons the Piercing tag. A blow with a mace or warhammer to the head may not actually make a hole in the helmet, but the impact could still concuss the victim. Should that mean stunning too? I’m not sure.

                        I could see breaking up pistols into heavy and light pistols. Light pistols are concealable and versatile. Heavy pistols are brutal and versatile.

                            The way I see it, unless you're playing a "Dark Eras" type game, as long as melee exists we're stuck with a mix of medieval and modern kit regardless - even with everyone else wielding guns, there's always a chance someone is going to ask for that katana (never mind the boot knives). It's in that particular light that I chose to make Piercing so common with ranged weapons - I know there *is* a difference in the armor-penetrating capability of a crossbow versus modern armor-piercing bullets, but it's a distinction I would consider significantly smaller than the one between that crossbow and a purely muscle-powered bow or sword, and the way tags work doesn't exactly offer us different "grades" of penetrating ability, just a flat "yes" or "no." Similarly, maybe kevlar vests and jackets could trade Padded for Impenetrable if modern soft armors are to behave significantly differently than medieval stuff, with the ablative plate inserts adding the Padded tag back on, but I personally wouldn't go that route - as with bow v. crossbow v. modern firearm, that kevlar vest's protective ability on its own is still closer to its fellow "soft" armors like medieval chain than to "hard" armors like those ceramic inserts or even just old medieval plate.

                            This actually cuts to the heart of why I wish there had been at least a few examples of mundane gear. It's all well and good to make declarations at our own tables - that's always going to have to happen - but each of these tags exists for a purpose, to shape the events playing out at our tables in specific and intentional ways, and it wouldn't hurt to have a ready-made illustration of that purpose at hand beyond just a disconnected dots-on-a-character-sheet type rule when we encounter the need to make those calls. (As more than a few writers have said many times on these very forums, fluff is crunch, after all.) None of the tags exist in a vacuum, and there is no point assigning tags to weapons without also considering the armor side of things (and I mean all of the different armors, and also how it will line up against every other weapon as a result, and...). Sure, some of the tags seem obvious in their application (Wounding), but as seen with the different ways we've each applied Versatile...

                            Speaking of, on the idea of using Versatile for pistol-whipping/butt-stroking, I can't say I'm on board with those kinds of attacks using the same set of tags that are applied when you pull the trigger and fire a bullet. That notion underpins my choice to use Versatile as the "thrown weapon" tag, where any other tag the weapon also possesses would absolutely be expected to still apply, in contrast with my idea of handling striking someone with the butt of a gun as if the character had used an entirely different weapon with fewer tags - or just no tags at all. Not saying you guys are somehow wrong to use it that way. I sure as hell can't claim to know exactly what each tag means more than anyone else outside the writing team. It's just that my read was that Versatile still applies all other tags, regardless of how the weapon is being used.

                            But yes, I do like the idea of different ammunition changing up certain tags. I originally already included it for shotguns - though maybe slugs could stand to be different from buckshot, if I'm going to take this farther down that route!

                            (Nobody Special, Sergeant Brother, I know this post might come off a bit more like I'm straight-up nitpicking some your ideas to death than my previous posts did - I'm sorry about that. I really do appreciate the back-and-forth, here, even when I find myself feeling, like now, as if I have to present a case for some of my own choices. Thank you, both of you. Seriously, you're both awesome. And ultimately, we all do still have the luxury if running it however we prefer at our own tables, and that will always be a good thing! For example: I do like the idea of having different sizes of pistols reflected, but I'm not sure if the tags make enough room for that while still preserving a meaningful distinction against the long guns. Though maybe light pistol = Concealable on its own, while heavy pistol = Concealable + Piercing or just Piercing on its own could work... though if the heavy pistol loses Concealable, the sawn-off will need to as well...)

                                            I agree that the default rolls for close combat might cut against the "pistol" interpretation of Versatile but that in turn might be a bit of a holdover from earlier versions of the system in which combat rolls had no default Attribute attached.

                                            Right now, as far as I can tell, it's not flat out stated that substituting Might + Close Combat --> Might + Ranged Combat for a thrown weapon requires any kind of tag at all (and the range band descriptions softly imply that thrown weapons go to Short not Medium) so I think you're just sort of meant to eyeball it either way. Although it also doesn't *say* the weapon has to be thrown.

                                            A general observation I have about the system is that it's very mechanised in some places (like having lots of tags and keywords) and then completely unmechanised in others (like not really saying what weapons can be used in what contexts) which leads to peculiarities like this. I think it works fine in play if you're willing to spot rule a lot of stuff, and spot ruling a lot of stuff is clearly how it's *designed* to work, so in that sense it's perfectly valid as a design choice.

                                                        Tags definitely are designed with narrativist approach in mind - Onyx Path typically approaches things from a perspective of "crunch" only mattering to the extent that it supports the intended "fluff," which I like about their games - but it's the "to the extent that it supports the intended 'fluff'" part that has me grousing over this list while wishing we had a list of examples for how the tags are applied (to both mundane and magical gear, not just the magical stuff) the way Exalted gave us (both 3e and Essence). A mundane, random kitchen knife untouched by any curse, to use an intentionally extreme example, shouldn't be blowing past even the highest quality armors without a care, so it's still important for those of us using the system to be offered a concrete idea as to whether the Piercing tag means "any thrusting weapon" the way D&D uses that word or "a weapon specifically designed to penetrate armor" the way Curseborne actually uses it. Unfortunately, not every tag is necessarily as obvious in application as Piercing. That's why I wish the book would have given us at least a few examples of items that would be common to almost everyone's games - at least enough to demonstrate the intended use of each tag. A set of reference points, to help new and experienced players alike navigate the application of these tags at their own tables.

                                                        I cooked up this list partly as a study in how the tags interact with each other mechanistically and what that will mean for any emerging narrative attempting to use them. (Hence why I used the Blessed Plate's tags, stripped down to create a two-tag mundane suit of armor, as my starting point. The example relic weapons, sadly, have a variety of tags that all make sense for them individually as cursed items, but maybe not so much when trying to scale them back to mundanity.)

                                                        Slings and slingshots - just Concealable like blowguns? Separate and give slings Brutal and Concealable in line with the changes to close combat bludgeons, while slingshots only have Concealable?

                                                        Crossbows and premodern firearms get separated from modern pistols and rifles with their variable ammunition.                                                             Breaking this way down into parts, I think there's a bunch of levers to pull here.

                                                            I think at the highest level, we need to remember that weapons have *up to* two tags, so I'd very much go with two tags for good weapons, one tag for mediocre ones, and none for outright bad ones (still better than punching because you get an Enhancement).

                                                            I think the other tags can kind of be broken down into a few categories: damaging categories like Brutal, Wounding, and Piercing; what you might call "form" categories like concealable, light, and Long Range; and then the weird stuff.

                                                            I think what you want here is:

                                                            - Obsolete weapons just don't have tags. You could maybe give flintlocks "concealable" but they're actually really bulky and unwieldy. If you're using Versatile for pistols, I'd give them that.
                                                            - Mid-tier weapons should probably get only their Form tags. Your knucledusters, your flick knives and the like. Concealable/worn and concealable/light for example are fine.
                                                            - Ranged weapons probably get a Form tag (concealable and Long Range most likely) and leave the damaging tag for ammo.
                                                            - Good melee weapons (yes that's a vague term) probably need a Form tag and a Damage tag, and generally I'd use Wounding for precision weapons (you don't get immediate stopping power but you might bleed out), Brutal for choppy or smashy weapons and Piercing only for things that explicitly defeat armour.
