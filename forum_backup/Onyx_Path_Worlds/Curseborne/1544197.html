    The Family Theme

    10-18-2024, 09:29 AM
    Is anybody else confused about the "found family" theme of Curseborne? The devs have made a big deal of it, even going so far as to cite it as a USP of the game. Even, and I'll admit to a bias here, going so far as to cite it as a reason to play Curseborne over Changeling the Lost, a game that's *really explicitly* about playing a group of people severed from their old lives and support networks who are brought together by shared experience and trauma. It's *really hard* for a game of this kind to be more about found family than CtL.

    The back of the Ashcan seems to sum up the game's approach to this theme as "Because Family You Didn't Choose Probably Sucks" and looking at that I started from the assumption that this was a fairly simple setup. Your Family (on the game mechanical sense) represents the family you *didn't* choose, whereas the player group (I think they call them crews in Curseborne) represents your found or chosen family.

    Except... you kind of *did choose* a reasonable chunk of the families. And a lot of the ones you didn't choose don't really feel any more like a Family than, say, the Camarilla. They're organisations with hierarchy and leaders and goals. Some of them are explicitly corporations or implicitly MLMs.

    So I kind of don't get how it's meant to work. To take the signature characters / samples from the ashcan as an example, you've got a Maven, a Nephilim, a Lykan and a Bathorite. Of those the Nephilim make a big thing out of traditional biological family, the Bathorites are a vampire bloodline, Issy explicitly describes the Lykans as a Family of Choice and the Mavens are kind of a group of dead people with loosely aligned goals.

    These characters are definitely a Crew, their character sheets include bonds to each other, but are they each other's "chosen family"? They don't seem to be. Eve and ... sorry, blanking on the lawyer...Cassie? seem to be on good terms with their Families and see the other party members as mostly acquaintances. Issy already *has* her found family in the Get of Lyka and seems fine with that. Sam lives like a typical Maven and that seems to be all the Mavens care about.

    I suppose from a certain point of view it can just be a thing you kind of bear in mind during gameplay but I don't see how it rises to the level of a USP for the game.

          I wouldn't say I'm confused about it, but I would say that I'm not sold quite yet on the execution. Next week's pair of chapter releases are gonna do a lot for dropping the pennies in the air I have on the subject of Curseborne, and I get the impression that where we're really going to see that theme/idea play is going to be in the individual Lineage supplements coming in future-the big question is if the core convinces me enough to look for it in those books.

          In terms of the found family aspect, I think that part I believe readily. The World of Curseborne is fucked, and it's clear that at any layer you want to address that, you don't want to just stick with your own for trying to resolve that. I don't need a whole lot for my buy-in there, I can see and grok "Fighting for the family you choose..."

          But the other half of it, "...Because the Family you didn't choose", that's got me wanting to see some teeth on it.

          Not because there isn't a vibe for it, mind. The Hungry have the familial paterarchy* baked in, because induction relies on the elders creating the young'uns', and that works in the way that it works in Vampire. The Primals also do really well with it, because you are basically stuck with your Family as the group of people who get and know your particular struggles, and how those particular struggles can also get you stuck to or otherwise constantly having to come back to that family. The Sorcerers could do with that being brushed up, but I can see how wizarding families build up and share a cadre of resources that's easy to express familiarity with, even when some of those families get a little more family business about it then not. The Outcasts feel a little distant from the subject, but that's because it just really hasn't had the room to get into the personal feeling of the consequences of grand-pappy wiggling the snake out in the garden or whatever manner of engaging with the divine/Outsides comes up, and I can see how quickly that can be brought into the regular interactions. The Dead are the only Lineage where I struggle to see it work in thematic and narrative tones, because not only do you not have any other person acting as a root relation for the condition, but you can far more actively switch between Families because the major emotion you get high on explicitly has text allowing you to change your mind on, but one out of five ain't bad.

          Buuuuuuut while the narrative and themes are there, I haven't seen as much for the mechanical or the really deep psychological hangups for it. Going back over to the Vampires of Yore for a bit, having ancestors in your blood curse wasn't just a nice way of saying someone wants to roleplay as your creepy grandpa, they had pretty literal metaphysical power over you-in Masquerade, enough so that an argument could be made that every clan basically just is a mass wayward extension of the founding Antediluvian, particularly since that's a big part of the mechanics for Gehenna. Requiem doesn't run the risk of it going that far, but blood sympathy mean that people in the lines of Embrace still had marked advantage and power in relationship to each other, often really supporting the elder members.

          Or in Beast the Primordial, poorly communicated and explored where it was, a big part of the conflict for them when it comes to other monsters is they feel and take their actions more personally than not (and assuming the Best's don't fuck up their Thicker Than Water and later Kinship benefits, the same goes for those monsters to Beasts, and keep that in mind for the rest), so what might otherwise just be a logical move that otherwise hurts feels more like personal betrayal, like actions are done specifically to them rather than just done, to a point where if the game was better executed, one of it's biggest problems is that no one gets to be a Antagonist so much as a Nemesis, which runs the risk of the game feeling more like checkers than chess.**

          Or, possibly best of all, in Promethean the Created, where in there's not strictly mechanical consequences (unless I'm forgetting some kind of mechanic from Second Edition or one of the supplement), but the issues and reasons that go into the creation of any given Promethean is just basically so utterly loaded with expectations(or even lack of expectations!) that there's no way that the relationship between Creator and Created isn't loaded with dramatic potential. Like, it's not hard to get angsty about why you were made, and that does a lot to that "found family" aspect that comes with trying to attain humanity.

          This is where I'm waiting for Curseborne to do something, because I can see the connections and the reasons for familiarity and personal feelings in the Lineages and Families (mostly), but it hasn't quite crossed the line into feeling consequential yet.

          *not really a word, but it's what I'm going to use for a non-gendered parentally-based hierarchy while my brain is waking up. When I get a better word, I will start using that.
          **But Beast's execution was botched enough most people can't even get to the Nemesis problem, so this really hasn't come up, yay for everyone.

              I think interestingly we might have opposite problems with the execution. I get the "fight for the family you choose" setup in theory except I don't know who the "family you choose" is meant to be. It feels like it should be the other PCs or general outside-of-Family connections you make in the game. But then why have Families like the Black Hearts who have an incredibly strong chosen family vibe.

              Similarly, I feel the "family" thing comes across clearer in the Outcasts than you do, and less clearly in the Primals and Hungry. You'd think "family you didn't choose" would be pretty much a shoe-in for vampire bloodlines, but at least one is *explicitly* a self-created family of people who have been kicked out of the other Families because their Hungers weren't appropriate (which feels like an almost *too* on the nose metaphor), then the Vorare and Ascetics both seem like you might just be able to join them voluntarily, along with *possibly* the Gaki. It only really seems to be the Heirs, Bathorites and *maybe* Iscariots who explicitly claim blood-descent. Otherwise Hungry Families seem like a bit of a mix of VtM Clan and VtR Covenant.

              For the Primals, it's explicit that only the Vodnici are blood-related so while the rest have an inherent quality that makes them identify with one another and seek out one another's company that again feels more like found family to me than "family you didn't choose". Issy even explicitly calls the Get of Lyka a family of choice.

              The Outcasts at least have purely hereditary traits from what I can tell, and even the ones that don't focus intensely on "family" in the traditional sense have an inherent emphasis on heritage baked in. I think there's an extent here to which the desire to leave things flexible and player-defined harms the theme slightly in that while in theory Outcasts have a shared heritage, in practice apart from the Chimerae and *sort of* the Nephilim there doesn't seem to actually be a defined origin for any Family so any shared heritage characters might have needs to be made up at the table. But at least that shared heritage is implied to exist and that's something I'm comfortable making up myself.

              I *think* possibly that the idea was for every Family to be a Family but in a variety of different senses, some literal and some metaphorical, but in practice that means that there's no consistent assumption you can make about what a PC's relationship to their Family will be, and not just in the sense that you might get on with your family or you might not but in the sense that your Family might be a fundamentally different *type of thing* depending on your exact splat and subsplat.

              Compare this to, say, Promethean where, yes, your relationship to your progenitor, and why they created you, and what that means was open to endless possibilities but it always meant something and the type of relationship was fixed.

                  I don’t know if I’m that attached to a focus on found family. Not that I have anything against the idea either, but with such diverse factions I think it’s appropriate also also have diverse ways for different characters to interact with their family, both literal parents and the group that the characters are officially affiliated with.

                  I think that the idea of a family being a literal family is cool. I also think that a group comprised of very diverse people from different backgrounds can be also be really cool. Likewise, it creates all kinds of role playing opportunities when a character has to balance loyalty to some greater organization with their alliance with other player characters.

                  I think it would help to have a better idea of how some of these accursed lineages become accursed. It sounds like the Dead aren’t a family in any way, they can join any family based on their own inclinations. Presumably, the Hungry have something like an embrace, which is in many ways creating a new family and pulling the person out of their old life, then again the vampiric family can then be rejected for the found family. There is a strong implication that many of the Outcast and Primal families have hereditary powers and in some cases are literal families.

                  The found family becomes a particularly important focus if they are the other layer characters. The crew in most Curseborne games is sure to include a bunch of different kinds of creatures. Why are they all working together? Because they are each others’ found families?

                      Some people are going to make it an important part of their game, others aren't! Our intention is for characters to be drawn together into their crews in ways that provide them companionship, understanding, and friendship, even when their Accursed nature is bleak. Your supernatural Family may be harsh or it may be kind, but ultimately they made you what you are and are probably pretty unapologetic about that. Your crew becomes your best family. Your found family, if you like.

                      Of course there are other characters who escape their mortal families and find that through being Accursed, their supernatural Family is what they've always sought. Now they have the opportunities, the power, the support network they always lacked.

                      It works on multiple levels. Curseborne is in a lot of ways a game about not belonging or feeling accepted until you find that there are others just like you.
