    101 Primal Families

    07-09-2024, 12:42 PM
    Realized that with Primals there is a lot of wiggle room for transforming beings besides Werewolves, Werecats, Jekyll/Hydes, etc...

    Name of the game is to post a Family idea and continue the numbering on from the previous post/reply. Like so:

    1. Mantis - Wild Form is chitinous with four arms.
    2. Fly - Wild Form is an amalgamation of Fly and a Human like an H.R. Giger abomination.
    3. Socialite - Wild Form is a extremely charismatic and suave, instead of getting the Physical boost it gets a Social boost, but the creature is about being seeing and any action that is not continuing a conversation or talking with people takes the penalty. (Think Steve Urkel's Stefan persona)
    4. Brainiac - Wild form is a bulbious brain that requires constantly solving Mental tasks, the Creature needs knowledge and cannot help be addicted to mental challenges. Boost to Mental rolls instead of Physical, but gets penalties for not doing Mental actions.

                      Okay! Now that we know Creatures are defined by a desire for safety at all costs and something else...

                      13. Arboreals: The source (or drawn from?) the legend of tricksy wood nymphs and related entities like huldras, Arboreals are were-trees and other plants. Sounds funny, until you realize how murderously competitive and patient plants are. Arboreals are born of a type of Creature that desires control over themselves and their immediate environs, and to reshape it into a perfect home - and don't mind watering the plants with blood and feeding them fertilizer now and again. That being said, they're insular and aloof, not cruel; an Arboreal enjoys "symbiotes" in their groves to help them thrive, and are often middle-managers and messengers for other families...and master trappers.

                                      14. The Horologians: A Family only slightly older than the Hydes, the Horologians are unique in that their Creatures aren't animals - or anything organic at all. They are spirits of metal and machines, originally clocks (as the name suggests), but as tech advanced, so have the Wild Forms of the Horologians - the majority of modern ones look like sleek cyborgs and humanoid war machines in their battle forms, though there has been a callback to steam(punk) tech. Their founder wanted them to be the best of human and machine, the passion of a human with the ruthless efficiency of technology - this backfired, as the Creatures of Horologians are just as proud and paranoid as their more organic counterparts, they just combine that with a more slow, logic-driven burn to their ire that lets them plan out (and draw out) their inevitable retaliation. The urge of a Horologian Creature is control - for everything around them to proceed to according to predictable, clockwork cycles of stability, and a fundamental part of that is understanding the world - and justice, to ensure the correction fits the pattern. Hell hath no fury like a Clockwork who both personally hates you and has a law or moral principle to cite for justification.
