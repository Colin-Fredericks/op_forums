    Some (hopefully) Constructive Criticism of Curseborne

    10-26-2024, 05:15 PM
    I've posted these on the Kickstarter comments, but wanted to come directly to the forum and place them more deliberately and get input from others.

    Let me start this off by saying, I really like Curseborne so far, and it's a game I definitely want to run. It has a ton of awesome story possibilities and great Roleplay. I'm also happy the Kickstarter has been so successful for Onyx Path, as personally and rather bluntly... I see them as having been robbed of their own original creations. Which really sucks. So Curseborne being a spiritual successor, a success, and bringing in a ton of new ideas that are a lot of fun is overall just awesome.

    But....

    Based off of the manuscript readings I've done, I still have some nitpicks and personal concerns for the game. Personal concerns that will if not addressed, see me basically writing a small document of "House rules and abilities" for my own table to really make the game into something I want...And I'd much rather prefer Onyx Path address these issues themselves so I'm not running my own version of Curseborne that is 25% my own rules meaning people coming to play with me might take umbrage and future Curseborne supplements become less useful, yadda, yadda.

    Anyway, let me get to the point.

    A couple years ago, I started writing my own "World of Darkness Heartbreaker." I was calling 'Children of the Night'. The long and short of it is, it looked a lot like Curseborne in concept, Multiple Supernatural Creatures, all with their unique inherent abilities... But sharing a Master Power List with different creatures having discounts/unique uses of that Master Power List.

    So yeah, clearly I love Curseborne since myself (and I assume lots of other gamers) have had similar ideas.

    My issues for Curseborne, however, primarily have to deal with... well the "Super" of the Supernatural... some of the powers and abilities, some that seem missing, and some that seem like they can be improved.

    So let's start right away with Lineages and the one that sticks in my craw as needing the most work, Primals and their Wild Form.

    Right now, the Wild Form is quite frankly.... very underwhelming. There's absolutely no difference in the Wild Form when comparing a Lykan to a Hyde to a Vodnick except for how you describe it.

    And while that might be enough for people who don't care about game Mechanics and favor fiction entirely, I'm not here to play FATE or Powered by the Apocalypse. (Nothing wrong with those games, but they don't scratch the itch for this genre) Quite frankly, I want the ability for players to give some amount of customization to their Wild Form.

    Lykans don't have claws and teeth..... Vodnicks aren't amphibious. One is a Werewolf, one is a Frogman/Deep One.... Yet mechanically they are exactly the same. Just as the Hyde is exactly the same.

    The only way they can get say, gills, or wings, or claws, or teeth... Is to buy the Practices that allow them to transform...

    And that doesn't feel like a person with a set "Wild Form" to me... it feels more like a Lunar from Exalted who buys charms for all the different Shape Shifting tricks they want.

    My own personal solution to this would be to give a small pool of customization points and let a Primal Player more or less "Build" their wild Form. You don't have to go crazy with it... But just having something like...

    *Weapon Tags for Natural Weapons like Claws, Teeth, Horns
    *Enhanced Senses to give Enhancements on certain checks, star light vision, blood hound nose, cat hearing,
    *Aquatic Adaption
    *Increased Movement
    *Inherent Armor
    *Increased Physical Stats

    Yes, there are Practices that do a lot of these things, but you don't need to make the Wild Form versions as powerful as the Practice. Let them stack with the various different spells of the Practices and the Wild Form's abilities become even greater. You could give an *Average* wild form build for every family and still let players customize to really make their Form unique, important and a feature of playing a Primal.

    Now in brief, the other things I wanted to see addressed were
    *More detailed info on how Feeding Works, since just saying "They eat Memories" is vague and unhelpful when there's literally a power designed for wiping memories and it would really suck if Ascetics feeding is more powerful or as versatile as the Entanglement 4 version of the Memory wiping power.

    *The lack of ability for creating zombies.
    We know Zombies are in the game. Why can't anyone make Undead Servitors? Multiple Splats in CoD and WoD were capable of this right out of the gate. Honestly there are a few Spells across all of the Practices that seem like they could be grouped together into a "Necromancy" Power Set... throw in the ability to create Undead with it, and it would be perfect.

    *There is no ability to gain Enhanced Strength unless you are "Hungry"
    There's no power right now that increases your Strength. No ability to have actual supernatural strength. Lots of "Do more damage with your attack." But going back to Primals in particular, and most likely some Outcasts and the Dead... why don't we have any ability for them to become strong enough to start flipping cars and throwing manhole covers around? It's a thematic thing that's probably even more important than the Necromancy thing.

    Anyway that's what is on my mind. I still love this game and most everything about it, but these stand out to me right now.

      I either fully or partly agree with pretty much all of this.

      I do think that an intended part of the game design is that some things are left up to individual Storyguides and I don't think that's an inherently bad thing but I'd like a lot more clarity on the kind of leeway that SGs have to make calls on this kind of thing and, perhaps more importantly, what kinds of things it's acceptable for players to ask their Storyguides for.

      Primal Wild Forms vs Hungry Feeding Preferences are perhaps the clearest example of the two extremes that coexist in the game here.

      The game mechanical effects of a Primal Wild Form are highly concretised while also being fairly minimal: you get a +1 Enhancement that stacks to physical actions while transformed and that's it. There's no indication that you're allowed to ask for or give any other kinds of abilities that might be appropriate and indeed quite a lot of abilities are actively gated behind Inheritances (like Vodnik water breathing) or Spells (like Lykans tracking by scent).

      By contrast Hungry seem to just be able to grant themselves quite potent supernatural powers merely by having that power described as their "feeding preference". Vorare straight up get the ability to consume souls. Black Hearts get the power to drain emotions. Ascetics get a memory-draining ability that from the flavour text seems more effective than the Tabula Rasa spell.

      Possibly the intent is that in both cases the SG has wide leeway to grant mechanical benefits to players but that in no way comes across in the Primal chapter, or anywhere else in the book. If the SG is meant to say "yeah of course you get Power Advantage on that attempt to flip a car, you're a freakin' werewolf" it isn't *mentioned* anywhere. Unlike the OP I don't mind if these things aren't nailed down but I *do* want them to be explored. Right now the manuscript contains a lot of unreliable narration and very little explanation.

      I know Danielle has said she thinks a lot of these issues can be addressed with small sidebars but I think they might actually need some *quite large* sidebars and preferably a good number of examples spread throughout the book.

                      I will say that in this regard it's a specific case of a more general issue which is that *every* way a character generates Curse Dice ultimately relies on a GM call and that isn't necessarily a deal breaker. Deciding if somebody counts as feeding or sacrificing meaningfully is ultimately no harder than deciding if "the whole crew came together to solve a problem" or if somebody "exemplified their family themes".

                      The Primal Damnation is actually a really good example for how this can be made a little more concrete in that it explicitly lists Conditions you have to inflict on somebody.

                                              After rereading the texts, my thoughts

                                              -god I thought the cofd books were "dry" by the absence of IC narration, but it must have been nostalgia giving me rose tinted glasses. The IC parts reminds me why I rolled my eyes and straight out passing over some IC texts in WOD books.

                                              ​​​​​This is urban fantasy, so we all know we'll need the "hey rookie, seems you're now supernatural and facing a reality you thought never existed, let me tell you how not to get yourself killed by next week" speech.

                                              But please, was it necessary for it to be spelled out by obnoxious, pseudo witty, pop culture referencing teenagers?

                                              And not just once, but five time, one for each family?


                                              Years ago, there was a guy attempting to make a vampire mmo solo (suicidal project clearly, and he did closed it eventually), he wrote a hell lot of IC lore dump and it's clear he was 100% influenced by the WOD.
