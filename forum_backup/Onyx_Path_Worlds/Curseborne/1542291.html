    Herding Cats (Range Bands and Positioning)

    09-16-2024, 11:28 PM
    I've been playing TRPGs for close to 30 years and I've noticed a shift from feet/meters/squares/fixed units to range bands. I understand the concept of fist/shotgun/rifle/artillery/ICBM range but how do you track positions?

    For example, Person A is Medium for Person B and Long for Person C. Person B is also Long for Person C.

    Person A moves from Medium to Short to Person B. How does this affect Person A's position to Person C?

    It seems like any time someone changes bands, you have to recalculate the new band distance to everyone, not just the active character and their destination/target.

    I'm sure there's a simple answer that I'm missing but after reading at least three different games' Core books, I haven't found one that explained positioning in a way I comprehend.

    Thanks in advance!

      Storypath Ultru Core Manual solves your problem - I'm not sure those rules will be printed in Curseborne core, but they are in SP Ultra one.

      Originally posted by SP Ultra Core Chapter 2
      Distance for Virtual Tabletop
      The distances described in the range table are narrative depictions to give some basis for the range bands. When playing the game using a virtual tabletop, narrative ranges aren’t as useful as actual distances. The following table gives hard distances and converts them to squares for VTT play.

      Range - Typical Distance - VTT Squares
      Close 0-2 meters 1 square
      Short 3-20 meters 2-10 squares
      Medium 21-60 meters 11-30 squares
      Long Over 60 meters to maximum effective range, ~1,000 meters 30-~500 squares
      Extreme By Situation (typically 1,000+ meters) n/a
      Out of Range Beyond maximum weapon range n/a

          My experience in SP is as a narrative game more than a table top game. I usually use a simple rule:
          Close = touching range
          Short = within the same room in a house or equivalent
          Medium = not the same room but within the same football field or equivalent.
          Long = further than a football field but you can still see clearly.
          Extreme = need a binocular or other helps to see clearly.

          Most of times the characters remain basically within the same range bands all the scene, except if they sprint around. So usually only what is within close or short range change, if you sprint to the location of others within medium range you can change what is in medium range. But you can hardly change what’s in long distance unless you have a car (or is super fast, what is also possible) and it can run freely in a large space (kilometers long and wide)

                      The general idea is you can move 1 range band for free, so you can take a few steps easily with no effort.
                      You can use an action (that can be part of an mixed action) to move another range band, so you can move anywhere within a room as part of a mixed action.
                      To move another range band you need to have an speed advantage, in TC you have Edges for that, in Scion you have knacks, things people can reach on the lowest playable character level. So you can, if you have the proper abilities, move to medium range as a part of a mixed action. A character without that special traits won’t be able to move to medium range in combat.

                      To go further you need powers…

                                              Range bands are functional (if unrealistic) for one-on-one combat, but collapse when you introduce a third person.

                                              My preference would be Fate Core's method. Divide every area into zones. Everything in the same zone is Close range. You can move to an adjacent zone for free, and adjacent zones are in Short range. If you spend your action running you can move two zones. Medium, Long and Extreme ranges are progressively longer paths of zones. You do need a map to use that system, but not a complicated one.

                                                  The focus of my interpretation at least is scale and how the characters can move around those distances.
                                                  Each range band is 10-ish times larger than the one before, so it’s something like: 3 feet, 30 feet, 300 feet, 3000 feet.
                                                  That is like that because not only the character is using better weapons but also because the characters in Storypath can be much faster than other people, crossing kilometers in a round.

                                                  I personally do something like Fate Core, but not exactly. The thing here is more about what you can move and what you can’t, if you have 2 “places to fight”, like 2 groups of adversaries or 2 independent adversaries that don’t want to be close to each other you just set the distance between them as a determined number of range bands and to move between them you need to cross that gap. If tou can all move and you all want to be at close range, than you are probably going to be in 2 rounds max.

                                                  Can you cross a “3 range bands distance” by moving 2 range bands each time? Yes, but it will take 10-ish rounds, so why bother? That can be basically an entire scene, as combats in SP lasts usually less than 10 rounds.
                                                  Same for pushing a person against his will to cross a “2 range bands distance” 1 range band each time, it takes longer than take one out here, go there and take the other out.

                                                  But I need to punch that guy that is being shooting me with a sniper rifle, I want to cross the entire space! Than you have a scene for that, one that the characters are avoiding being shot while moving, with each action representing a part of the distance covered, possibly with some difficulties to cross barriers or difficult terrain and a complication that if not bought off means you got shot. Same for the opposite, you have one guy running away from the others, you change scene to a chasing scene, with its own challenges and narrative aspects.

                                                  That takes us to what is a scene. From TC Core:
                                                  Scene: This is the amount of time it takes for
                                                  an entire set of related actions to occur. This
                                                  could be anywhere from a few minutes to an
                                                  hour. Scenes encompass all the action for a sin-
                                                  gle thing, so a car chase is a scene, an interro-
                                                  gation is a scene, and a firefight is a scene. But if
                                                  a firefight transitions into a foot chase then the
                                                  scene changes when the action changes.
                                                  It means, by the rules, you have a scene for approaching the shooting adversary and a scene for fighting the adversary if you are not able to cross the entire distance in a feasible time. Or you break the scene when you start running away (you break the scene for you and those that are chasing you).
