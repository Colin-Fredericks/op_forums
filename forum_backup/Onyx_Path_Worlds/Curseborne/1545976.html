    Power tiers in Curseborne

    11-15-2024, 09:31 PM
    In the World of Darkness and Chronicles of Darkness, most of the monster types had "lesser cousins" (dhampirs and ghouls for vampires; wolfbloods and kinfolk for werewolves; proximi and sorcerers for mages; fae-touched and kinain for changelings, and so on), and a few had greater forms (elders for vampires; archmasters for mages). The "lesser cousins" tended to be more or less human with just a touch of the supernatural about them; the characters central to the lines tended toward a conceptual space where it was debatable how human they still were; and the upper reaches of each type were populated by beings who were quite inhuman, even when they had had human roots.

    This essentially defined three "tiers" of play, with the main gamelines focused on the center tier.

    Scion has something similar going on, albeit with a very different feel: you have Heroes who are essentially human but with a touch of the godly about them; Demigods who are clearly more than human but less than gods; and then gods themselves.

    I wonder if Curseborne is doing something similar; only, instead of the main gameline focusing on the middle, it starts out focusing on the bottom, the equivalent of Scion's Heroes. The Hungry are more like dhampirs than vampires; the Primal are more like wolfbloods than werewolves; the Sorcerers are more like proximi or, well, sorcerers than mages; and so on. But as they get more and more Entangled in the Web of Curses, they eventually enter a new stage of existence where it becomes much more debatable whether they're still human. If I'm right about this it would be akin to how Scion Heroes become Demigods when they reach Legend 5.

    And just like transitioning from Hero to Demigod isn't merely "more of the same", but comes with fundamental changes to how things work for the Scion, it might be that when an Accursed advances to Entanglement 5, the rules Uber which they operate change. The Hungry become more like Curseborne counterparts to Kindred instead of being kind of like dhampirs; the Primal become more like the Garou or Uratha or Changing Breeds; Sorcerers become more like the Awakened; and so on.

    Still with a distinctly Curseborne approach, mind you; I wouldn't expect Onyx Path even to try to replicate exactly what either World or Chronicles of Darkness did. For instance, I sincerely doubt that Entanglement 5+ Sorcerers will have to start contending with Paradox, or that Primals will ever develop an ability to "Step Sideways"; such things simply aren't part of how Curseborne works.

    But high-Entanglement Sorcerers might end up developing a flair for improv; high-Entanglement Dead might start to disassociate from their bodies, treating them more like Wraith Fetters as opposed to how low-Entanglement Dead operate more like Orpheus projectors; and so on.

    What do you think?

    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                      And I don't think they're "holding back" so much as they're using the page count wisely. We're not getting Entanglement 5+ stuff in the core book because there's not enough space to do it justice.
                      I think what makes me go *hmm* at that is that I feel like the whole "entanglement 5+" thing is itself a bit arbitrary so I don't think it's reasonable to say there's "not enough space to do it justice" because it is, itself, something the devs have totally made up.

                      Like my issue with characters in Curseborne as they currently stand isn't that they aren't *powerful* enough; you can build Dracula as an E4 Hungry perfectly well, it's that some aspects of the core fantasy of some families (and arguably some whole Lineages) are missing.

                      I don't think Wild Form not really doing much is a scale issue or a scope issue, for example.

                      From a design aesthetics point of view I'll be particularly narked if they go the Scion separate gamelines route because then, functionally, Entanglement 5+ characters simply do not exist in Curseborne at all. A core assumption of the game is that PCs and NPCs follow inherently different rules and part of that is that "Entanglement" just isn't something NPCs *have*, any more than they have Attributes or Skills.

                      I'd be much more okay with supplements covering E5+, much like Vampire used to save 6+ Disciplines for supplements, but for me at least there's nothing I actually want to see from Entanglement 5 that I wouldn't also want to see from Entaglement 1-4. I don't think the scope of the game should be gated in that way.

                      Compare (and this is quite a dated comparison these days) with Unknown Armies: you could run the game at Street, Global or Cosmic scale right out of the box, and there was no hard mechanical difference between a street-level character and a cosmic level one, the difference was that you were expected to care about different things.

                              I think it's mostly just trying to avoid some of the occasional clumsiness around Chronicles' "minor" templates, which in a few cases sometimes felt closer to the myths and folktales that were supposed to be inspiring the "major" templates than the major ones themselves (lookin' at you, Wolfbloods and Absent). The impression I got was that at Entanglement 1-4, you start off somewhere between what would have only been a minor template previously and the intentionally hyped-up, powerful starting points of certain Chronicles major templates (and I don't just mean Mummy here), eventually meeting or surpassing what was Chronicles' starting point by about Entanglement 3. As a result, there isn't really much need for "lesser versions" of every playable monster in Curseborne - you just have some very real werewolves who haven't yet grown from an otherwise unremarkable aristocrat with an anger-related body hair problem to become The Wolf Man of legendary infamy. (That's what Entanglement represents, after all!)

                              Not saying the "minor" templates were always a bad thing. Methods of creating "ghouls" stuck around in Curseborne for a reason. But not every monster type needs its own equivalent to ghouls.

                                  I think for me the Curseborne-to-CofD comparison (Mummies aside) is less about power level than it is about little fiddly bits that sell the fantasy in a particular way.

                                  Like it's true that Wild Form is nowhere near as impactful as Crinos/Gauru but it's hard to make a direct power comparison because so many fundamentals are different between the systems. There's certainly a much bigger difference between an Uratha in Gauru and an Uratha in human form than there is between a Primal in and out of Wild Form but I'm not sure you can compare the individual power levels. Like I don't think mundane humans are much of a threat to either, and once you start digging deeper into each game's rogues' gallery both wind up meeting things that can take them.

                                  I actually think starting Accursed stack up pretty well with starting WoD characters (even mages, depending on GM generosity) but I also think that depends a lot on the GM being willing to fiat some things (to, for example, let Vorare eat souls and let werewolves have claws).
