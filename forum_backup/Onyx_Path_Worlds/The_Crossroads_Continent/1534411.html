    Current Campaign Background

    04-18-2024, 04:36 PM
    I mentioned in the previous post that I've recently started DMing again for a group of my old gaming crew and a few of our kids. Nice mix of "classic" D&Ders, and players used to 5e. With the players all coming from different directions, I chose not to force a location and theme, but to have them tell me what sort of things they'd like from a campaign and gave them copies of Peoples of the Crossroads Continent as inspiration.

    One player has been bingeing One Piece anime episodes, and wanted to be a genial pirate. They talked with their sister, who was also playing, and decided, based on Peoples, to be Sea Elf siblings who were low level pirates on an existing pirate ship who fled in a dinghy after the ship was caught and scuttled by the Imperial Navy. Another player wanted to be a Dwarven wizard, which are unusual in the CRC, so he was a beginner sent out to gather more arcane knowledge to bring back to their small wizard school in the Dwarven Kingdoms. While in transit, his ship was captured by the very same pirate ship that the Sea Elf duo were working on, and he was held captive for a possible ransom. During the attack and subsequent rowing away, the Sea Elves released him so he wouldn't drown, and the dwarf jumped into the dinghy along with a couple other fleeing pirates.

    Just that level of backstory suggested that their ship was operating in the inner Sea of Scales (where the Whale Bone Islands are full of pirates and has sea routes a dwarf could be on), which narrowed down the places where they could land their dinghy. Next up, we had a player who started with a Human cleric, wanted a sea god as his deity, and then asked if he could be a noble or knightly social class since he liked the feudal feel of the Kingdom of the Twenty Horses, but didn't want to be a serf in that environment. Basically, he didn't want "shit all over him". I said: "Roll percentile dice, and the higher the better". He rolled a 94, so I said "Congrats, you're a lesser son of a lesser noble" between clenched teeth, as I really didn't think he was going to roll it! But from there, he decided that he was inspired by a wandering priest of Njord, god of sea and wind, who was not one of the gods usually followed in the Kingdom, but who had a good chance of catching on in one of the port cities along the coast. With his family glad that he had found something to do and away from them, he headed to the nearest port well caparisoned and on a fine riding horse.

    So that zeroed us in on The Kingdom of the Twenty Horses, as it was easy to have our dinghy land at that same port city and after avoiding the port authorities, the three PCs found a local dockside tavern. Where a very unusual sight awaited them as our next PC was entertaining the crowd by playing music on his arms and legs. He could did this because the players wanted to be both a bard and a Mantid bug person and decided to create haunting melodies the way a cricket creates their chirping. The Mantid, was earning their keep at the tavern, and looking to continue exploring this strange new world - so very different from the desert hive he left several weeks to the west. And finally, one more player decided they loved the potential of a Winged Elf druid renegade, driven out from his people for his obsession with converting others to his worship of the sky.

    Easy enough to have them fly in at any point the PC group got together to explore the area. So, what were they going to do? The male Sea Elf wanted to be the greatest pirate captain in the world, his sister wanted to drink and fight, the noble wanted to spread the word about Njord, the Winged One wanted to shout the greatness of the sky to all and sundry, the Mantid thought these were all fine and fascinating individuals and was happy to hang out with them and share the mead he was fermenting in his backpack (ancient Mantid recipe), and the dwarf was just glad to get in and dry out next to the fire. Everybody had ideas for their characters, we worked out how they could play as they wanted (including if their were any topics they did not want to have included in the campaign - there were not), and all that suggested where we would start, at least. The Kingdom of the Twenty Horses is not the most developed area in the CRC in terms of how much gaming we've done there, but there has been some, so it seemed a pretty promising place to let them get started doing things in.

    Next time: they start doing a thing.

              After the first session the gang rolled up characters and did a short getting-together session, and we now have:


              - J'imknee. A Mantid Bard. 6’ green shiny chitin. Kicked out of the hive for rockin’ too hard and now wandering from performance to performance. He also makes frothy mead and sells it.

              - Corsika C. M'udd. A Sea Elf Fighter (pirate). 6.5’ tall. Her ship was sunk by the Imperial (Empire of the Scarlet Gate) Navy and rowed a small dinghy with a few other survivors to the port at the Hill of the First Horse. Two weapon fighter.

              - Dampe D. M'udd. Her Brother, a Sea Elf Ranger. 6'6" tall. Intends to be a pirate admiral and possibly overthrow the current admiral, Austin Mearic, aka The Red Grin, an infamous villain and member of the Order of the Black Cross. Archer and known for his acrobatic flourishes.

              - Hugh Vandele. A Human Cleric of the Norse sea god. Bald with nice clothes. Nature and Tempest domains. A lesser noble booted out by his dad as he’s the seventh son and kind of useless. But now he has found religion and headed south to the Hill of the First Horse.

              - SmillBjorg. A Dwarven Wizard who had been captured by the same sunken pirate vessel that the Sea Elves (who he escaped with) had been crew for. Looking for more magical knowledge and artifacts to bring back to the very small wizard enclave he had been a part of.

              The three are sharing a table in one of the bars (Pappy’s) at the docks at the Port of the City of the Hill of the First Horse. The dock guards have already popped in and scoped out the trio, but backed off without questions after seeing the noble’s traveling papers. They are joined outside as they begin to explore the port town by:

              - Alabaster. A Winged One (Elf) Druid who worships his own idea of a god of the air and sky - whom he loudly and incessantly proselytizes for. Is wary and nervous when not flying.

              Various errands are run. The lesser noble pays for much of what they buy in preparation for heading north, out of the Kingdom of Twenty Horses, to a land the lesser noble has heard of where worship of his god is more known. The ground slopes up from the docks, and in the flatter areas more to the southeast of the hill itself is where the actual town lies, with the higher ground more of a citadel. In that town is where the tradesmen are, stables and furthest out, before the ground begins dropping again, are tanners, blacksmiths, and stables. While in town, in a tavern, J'imknee begins plying his musical trade, earning the party some free drinks and tips. Which are unfortunately snatched, the coins that is, and the group pursues the thief through several back alleys before losing him. Even the Ranger can find no clue, until Alabaster, who was flying above unwilling to enter the confining tavern, let's the rest know that he saw the thief duck into a hidden door disguised by a pile of crates in an alley.

              Moving the crates aside is a surprisingly easy thing to - they must be rigged with wheels and pulleys suggests the wizard - revealing stairs down into a tunnel. What to do, what to do...

              Next up: Into the underground?

                          The adventure, continued:

                          Down into the tunnels the party goes, although Alabaster, the Winged one, only goes a few feet in before freaking out by the closeness of the tunnel, and, wings flapping against the walls, he scrambles out of the hidden opening and, shuddering, takes to the night skies. Unshaken by his sudden panic, the rest of the party continues down the corridor. The walls are dressed stonework, with sconces for torches randomly situated every twenty feet or so. It's dingy, with small bit of debris strewn about and dried mud staining the floors. Clearly, this is a passageway that sees some traffic, although the party can only be sure that the thief they're chasing used it recently. Hugh casts a small spell that provides some light for him, although the others don't need it. But considering that no one in the party has any real training in the thiefly arts, every pair of eyes that can be used to note possible traps and secret doors has real value. Even the wizard SmillBjorg's years of hearing lectures from his stonemason relatives comes in handy as the dwarf notes the high quality of the stonework, as well as it being several centuries old, although not quite up to dwarven standards. This city on the Hill of the First Horse has been here for quite a long time.

                          Although they find neither traps not hidden panels before descending a staircase to a lower level of passages, they do find that there are very thin wires stretched across their current hallway whose ends go into small holes in the walls, and SmillBjorg notes that not only did they descend the staircase to this passage, but the passage itself is subtly slanting down towards an intersection. The tunnel T's at the end, with two massive ten foot wide iron-banded wooden doors with pull rings at the base of the passage they were following, and a two passages leading off in opposite directions. It is while they are slowly moving forward watching those passages and the big doors that the the trapdoor in the floor gives way!

                          Meanwhile, Alabaster is quietly circling the area high above in the darkness and sees several groups of ruffians meet up and travel to another hidden door in the ground down an alley. They have lit the torches they brought with them, and brandishing a variety of weapons, the gang, numbering about twenty, descends into the earth. Having circled lower Alabaster can catch enough of the low muttered conversation of the angry throng to realize that they are headed down into the gang's base in order to deal with "the trespassers". The Winged One may often seem deluded as he rants about his god, The Sky, but he doesn't hesitate to follow them underground in hopes of assisting the party of adventurers he has only just met.

                          Meanwhile meanwhile, as the five foot long and ten foot wide section of flooring pivots beneath their feet, the Ranger dives forward and rolls to his feet on the other side. Good thing, as his sister, who has upgraded her armor since their time at sea, slips in her metal boots as she tries to jump and barely manages to clutch the far edge of the pit and needs his hands up as an assist to get to safety. The bug leaps across easily with his Mantid insect-like lower legs, and then throws a rope to the cleric, who then doesn't need the safety measure as he broad jumps the five foot gap. The cleric, our lesser noble Hugh Vandele, is also the only one who looks down into the ten foot deep pit to note the web of thin wires mid-way down, just below where the trick flooring section swung down. While dirty, there seems to be no spikes or bones or anything suggesting the trap was designed to be more than a delaying nuisance and some sort of alarm. The others split up to listen at the door and the branching tunnels. It's the Sea Elf fighter who let's the party know that she hears what sounds like a crowd approaching as stealthily as they can from the left hand passage.

                          "Yeah, time to fight!" she hisses, drawing a sword for each hand and trotting up the passage, closely followed by her brother and J'imknee, the bug bard, who chitters something about getting their money back. Hugh and the dwarf follow more slowly; one because of being cautious and the other because of his short legs. This passage slants slightly upwards to a flight of ten stairs across the width of the passage, and the party can clearly catch the wavering glow of torchlight coming towards them as they surmount the stairs. They're about twenty feet away from what seems like quite a large group in the shifting light, and a roar goes out as that gang realizes that the "trespassers" are not only in front of them, but are charging to meet them! The gang lurches forward and even manage to fire some ineffective arrows.

                          Just as the Sea Elf, Corsika, cuts down one of the gang with two slashes even before he can react, a terrifying apparition appears behind the street toughs: mighty wings flapping wildly, shrieking incoherently, Alabaster fills the hall and then hugging tight to the ceiling, he soars over their heads and turning casts a spell creating a thunderous wave of force, throwing most of the street gang off their feet, buffeted and rolling down the hall into, and in some cases past, their friends (along with sprays of blood and other body fluids as some of the gang are crushed by the impact). The thunder clap from this spell rolls through the tunnel, causing much wincing and even fear from friend and foe alike! The gang tries to clump together and still attack, and some successfully get through the party's defenses, but the Ranger does a diving roll and impales a tough on his spear, his sister double-hacks another of their tougher toughs down, Hugh smashes another with his mace, and the bug strums his arms together while blurting out a cruel insult at a heavily built gang member, causing the ganger to drop to his knees, his morale broken. At this point, now having crested the stairs, and with ears ringing from the thunder, the dwarf wizard fires off three bolts of mystic energy that drop two more of the gang - this is more than the street toughs bargained for, and even though there are still some tougher thieves in the remaining gang, they break and rush back down the hall. All except for the one on his knees who sadly puts his hands up like he's ready to be shackled.

                          The Ranger and self-styled Captain for the "crew", Dampe, won't have this, though, and encourages the young street tough, who mumbles that his name is Malcolm, to join their crew! Dampe just knows that they'll have a ship soon, or at least a boat, and Malcolm can join them working on the water with the sun and the wind and the bracing salt spray - which has got to be better than a life in the dirty city streets thieving! Malcolm interjects that he was never really a thief in the gang so much as an errand boy and shifter of boxes that the real thieves had stolen. After some aggressive interrogation by Hugh, who just doesn't like poor Malcolm, the young tough lets the team know that this whole underground complex is where the real thieves and smugglers hide out. He doesn't know where those thieves are, who he still speaks of with admiration, but while he never was allowed inside the big wooden doors, he knows that behind the doors is the main warehouse and resting place and a key part of their smuggling operation.

                          And with that, the party has to decide what to do next...
