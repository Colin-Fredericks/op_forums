    My Aeon Marathon

    04-27-2015, 09:49 PM
    As promised on the Max Mercer thread, I've dug up my notes from my Aeon Marathon campaign. For those who are not aware, the Aeon Marathon was my name for the idea of running a long campaign with the same group taking related characters from Adventure!, to Aberrant, and finally, to Trinity. This was a long game, taking the better part of 5 years and ending almost that long ago. Unfortunately, my notes and my character sheets have not survived well in the 3 moves since, so this will be more of a summary than play-by-play.

    The Adventure! game was mostly fairly standard. The crew fought evil pulp villains (including a masked mastermind with a Zeppelin and a small army of goons, as well as a daredevil mob boss), filled in the map (including rescuing an indigenous population on an island from evil), and set the foundations for the Aeon Society as a whole. They worked closely with the founders (even growing to like Dr. Primoris), and eventually took part (there were some very pulpy time skips) in the battle against Primoris in the end. The characters included a wealthy Indian psychomorph, a daredevil private detective loosely based on the Question, and an heiress adventurer stalwart.

    We flashed forward to just after N-Day. The characters had passed on, but their descendants had erupted. The wealthy Indian psychomorph was replaced by a radiant godling who straddled the line between Utopian and Teragen, a Batman-esque frost-powered defender, and an adrenaline junkie conjuring animal companions from pure quantum. This began fairly standard, superheroes working for T2M to make the world better, at the guidance of their Utopia handler. Over time, however, they found themselves ordered to investigate leads that led more and more to the discovery of the problems in the setting, including the Directive and, eventually, Project Proteus. Their handler was Mercer in disguise, trying to guide the descendants of his friends and allies to Proteus so that they could, as being part of the timeline and not outside of it, fix what he could not. I was fully prepared to run a more limited Aberrant War, one with novas actively fighting on both sides with more limited devastation, but they let the word about Proteus slip (in fairness, I ambushed them with an undercover reporter, just to see what would happen). Proteus had been dismantled by this point, and some Aeon-loyal novas with low Taint tried to force back the destructive aberrants, but it was insufficient to do more than protect some landmarks and leave behind some folk legends of "the aberrants that saved us from their evil brethren" in some areas.

    Final flash forward led to the Trinity era, shortly after the return of the Upeo. The novas could not have children, obviously, so we were dealing with nieces and nephews, for the most part. Clearly, they had suffered a bit in their upbringing as a result of their associations. One was a biokinetic freedom fighter, another a loyal Orgotek employee whose father's hard tech business had been purchased early on, and the last was a vitakinetic with the mindset of a dedicated Doctor Without Borders. This time, Max was again their handler, arranged again because of his memories of their Aeon ancestors, but he was honest with them from the beginning about who he was and how he knew them. The various missions were designed to explore the setting (including slight variations on most of the premade adventures, foiling an FSA plot to engineer "terrorist attacks" near the Orgotek New York arcology to stagger the psions there enough to finally bring them to heel, and a brief, paranoid visit to Nippon), but mostly, Max was curious about the Doyen and finding their motivations. He is a humanist and humanitarian, and the seemingly amoral, selfish, and manipulative Doyen fell more and more into the character's crosshairs as time went on. Eden made an appearance during the Chromatic conflict, although I used a council of nameless novas to replace Apollo Milliken. The game unfortunately petered out before it could reach a real conclusion, but the crew had found, in the final session, a lone, half-mad aberrant in a faded Team Tomorrow uniform who claimed to know the whereabouts of their famous ancestors, who could perhaps explain why their families were so blessed/cursed.

    It was an insane amount of fun, and the players all made considerable impacts to the flow of the timeline. Mostly, they served as the conscience for Aeon, trusted allies of Max Mercer throughout the centuries for keeping rogue elements in the society on the righteous path, and trying to find the truth of the real enemies of mankind from each setting. I only wish I still had all my notes and had written where the story was going, but it was quite the ride while it lasted.

          I really enjoyed the campaign. Max gave me the opportunity to play with the timeline in ways I never would have expected before. I'll answer in order.

          1. Truthfully, that event marked the end of the Adventure! segment of the campaign. Two of the three characters suffered fairly serious injuries during the battle (actually one health level apiece from dead), and it seemed like a good place for them to bow out gracefully from active work.
          2. Like I said, I played with Mercer's timeline quite a bit, and it would vary from appearance to appearance. He had a habit of setting up the mission that would start a story off, and disappear. There were times where they needed some intervention from their local Aeon rep, and not only would he not be around, but no one would have even heard of the pseudonym he gave them. Other times, he'd leave them cryptic messages in a centuries-old safety deposit box (on Proteus: "I'm afraid I might never kill the beast of many shapes.") As important as he was as my tool to steer the campaign when it needed direction, he spent a lot of time absent, and even occasionally turning up as a different age and giving seemingly contradictory information, so making sense of it all was part of the fun.
          3. There were actually moments where descendants and spiritual successors of Adventure elements played recurring roles (The great grandson of the thoroughly deposed daredevil mobster, for instance, was their informant in the New York arcology fiasco). Overall, though, I tried to keep the genres distinct to keep things straight in my own head as well as the players'.
          3.5. (Re: The Mercer clan) Michael was mostly during a time skip, although I always envisioned him being much like his father. Perhaps a little less brave, a little less dogged, but still devoted to the cause. His daughter came up a bit in 1998+, of course, but more as a distant and ambiguous pseudo-antagonist who meant well, but let those good intentions pave the road to Hell.
          4. My general rule of thumb when running a game is this: NPCs might struggle and fail to fix problems in the setting because of fate/the capacity for history to protect itself at fixed points/what have you. PCs are the stars BECAUSE they have the skill and determination to shape the future. In this case, Max needed heroes from that time to get to the bottom of what key individuals in Aeon were hiding from him (With a reaction like the one I described in the Max thread), and put a stop to it if need be. As a time hopping adventurer, his ability to directly affect events out of his own time was limited.
          5. That was actually a derailing moment in the story that I allowed. They did not trust the Proteus revival, even though its intentions and methods were very different, and they took the time to make sure. While much of the Proteus history was edited out, their families would have passed on some information, and they understandably (if somewhat OOCly) handled its reincarnation with suspicion.
          6. It could have gone a number of different ways. I try to steer stories in a direction, gauge reactions, and alter from there. Ultimately, I was going for a chicken/egg approach, where THEIR descendants in the distant future traveled back, met Max Mercer during the tail end of his life and, to help him in his goals, seeded their family DNA to react to the Hammersmith incident and related quantum and subquantum events. Thus, the families he came to rely upon so much were specifically put there for him to rely upon, by allies he would only meet at the end of his life. Alternately, because I have a habit of coming up with multiple ideas, jotting them down, and only making my final decision at the table, there were in fact only 3 characters (as opposed to the 9 there had been, by this point), who had scattered themselves across time, a sort of anthropomorphic Aeon Trinity working towards the greater good. Perhaps a bit overblown, I'll freely admit that, but these players and their characters have been there for 200+ years of the Aeon Society. They've helped fill in the map of the Earth, changed it for the better, and then moved on to map the stars. They'd fought gangsters, demigods, and everything in between. They should have been, needed to be, important.
          6a. As for the Coalition... I wanted to use them, as they fit the "impending alien invasion" trope so very well... But their description is inherently programmed to make the majority of gaming groups uncomfortable, so I was trying to find a way to include them without the joys of explaining that, yes, the core book describes them as space rape monsters, but that's frankly just a cheap shot at the shock value angle and I can do better than that. Ultimately, I chose to save them for later, which never properly happened.
          7. I didn't, sadly, which could have been interesting in a number of ways. In light of what the characters now knew about their family lines, what could happen with another incident? They presumably have other relatives; 200+ years after the start of it all, they probably have some they've never even met. Do they run home and check on their relatives? If a family member becomes an aberrant, how do they respond? Do they even know if the fresh aberrant they're hunting is family? It varies.
