    [TC: Aeon Actual Play] Desperate Stars #1

    01-09-2022, 01:12 PM
    Greetings, everyone!

    As I've mentioned before, I've been a fan of Trinity every since the limited edition core book dropped way back in 1997. I never really had a chance to play it, but I still collected, read, and re-read every book in the game line. I fell in love with the multitude of ideas and possibilities the game presented, and always regretted that I couldn't find a group that showed any interest.

    All of that changed a few weeks ago when I convinced by current group to let me run them through a campaign for the new edition. I turned into a bit of a maniac, writing up two different campaigns for them to choose from, as well as various plot hooks, plot points, alien creatures, enemy spies... You get the idea.

    Finally, after interruptions to our regular gaming schedule due to the holidays, family commitments and Covid scares, we got to play our first real session last night. Being the over-excited nerd that I am, I couldn't wait to post a real play write-up of the session.

    This is my first time attempting a real play thread, so apologies to any mistakes, omissions, gaps in continuity, or lack of common sense that I display as a GM and as as a conveyer of these events. I make no such claims on behalf of my players.

    I'll start off with our actual first session, the Session 0 we had a few weeks ago where I walked them through character creation. I laid out the basics of the setting and its history, and gave them a choice of two campaign styles: cyberpunk or space opera. The group quickly settled on space opera, which I pitched as a mix of Stargate: SG1 and Star Trek. Then, the characters were made based on what would be the needs of the campaign and the group's desires.

    So, here are the PCs:

    Andromeda "Andy" Hassan - A Upeo Wa Macho teleporter and pilot/physicist with daddy issues.

    Giselle - A wandering clairvoyant detective. Her family was killed by an Aberrant as they attempted to flee the destruction of Paris. She now seeks justice for them.

    Radian Moss - A psychokinetic ex-Orgotek operative who now works on a freelance basis for Aeon and the other orders. He did some sketchy things for Orgotek, and does not have fond memories of his time there.

    Miguel Ramirez - An electrokinetic biotech developer who works primarily as a researcher for Aeon.

    Dr. Xin Wu - A vitakinetic doctor who has had extensive experience in Doctors Without Borders. Is also secretly a Ministry spy.

    GM Notes: Andy's player actually read the book ahead of time (shock!) and developed a PC that's roughly a mix of Anne McCaffery's The Rowan and Samantha Carter from Stargate SG 1. Giselle and Radian Moss's (who just goes by Moss) players picked fairly straightforward concepts from the ISRA and Orgotek sections of the book. While this edition is supposed to be more optimistic than 1st ed, I couldn't resist the opportunity to use Moss as an ex-Option 8 operative. Xin's player went for a fairly tricky concept, but we worked together heavily and I'm excited to see how his personal plotline develops. Ramirez's player was swamped with work - Denver finally got its first snows of the season and he drives a plow truck - and was too exhausted to think during Session 0, much less make a character. He told me, "You know what I like to play. Just make the group's mechanic." So, here we are.

      Desperate Stars Session 1: Operation Apophis

      The scene opens with the PCs having spent the past three months, fresh out of the Prometheus Chamber, in a special Aeon-sponsored training facility on Luna. They have been put through rigorous physical and noetic drills by Lt. Munroe of the Legions and Dr. McCoy of Aeon. Thus far, the PCs have superficially gotten to know one another, and they are aware that they are being prepared for a special joint mission sponsored by the Psi Orders and Aeon, but they don't know exactly what that is, yet. They do know that they have been given extensive access to reports on both the Coalition and the Chromatics, which they pursued thoroughly as a group.

      GM Notes: This gave me a chance to dump as much of the in-canon backstory on both the Coalition and Chromatics as Aeon is aware of. The PCs are aware that the Coalition is vaguely "up to no good," and the Chromatics are psychotically devoted to killing as many humans as possible.

      Dr. Xin is extremely curious about why they have been allowed access to this exact information, and how it relates to their future mission. He surreptitiously observed Dr. McCoy comparing the PCs' physiological and noetic profiles against what parallel information Aeon possesses of the Chromatics. Xin guesses that their future mission will have something to do with the aliens, and so he takes it upon himself to start researching them as much as he can. He decides to consult with Miguel on methods to counteract the Chromatics' abilities.

      Miguel is aware of psionic restraint collars, and theorizes that there might be a way to miniaturize them so that they can be delivered via conventional weaponry. But, first, they have to get one so that he can reverse engineer it. Xin taps his patron, a man high in the command structure of the Ministry, who secures one and has it discreetly delivered to the team.

      GM Notes: Xin has a level 3 patron. This "favor" was highly unusual and is definitely going to come up again later. But, on the other hand, player-initiated intrigue! I had to do nothing! I love it!

      These actions do not go unnoticed by the rest of the team, despite Xin's best efforts. They have a group conversation about this. Giselle is reticent about the idea, because anything that could be used against another psionic race could easily be used against Earth's psions. But, she knows that they will go ahead with it anyway, so chooses to simply observe their progress for now. Andy, on the other hand, takes a more direct approach, and physically threatens Miguel. She makes it clear that any screw-ups will fall squarely on him. Moss just plays on his minicomp. With that said, Miguel moves ahead with his project, and successfully reverse engineers the psi-restraint collar and beings his plans for miniaturization.

      GM Notes: Be me, scrambling through the super-science rules in the corebook mid-game while the PCs argue amongst themselves. This is the exact sort of stuff I made Miguel's character to do, though, and the son of a bitch never fails a roll, so he successfully completes the first milestone.

      All of this occurs right under the noses of their trainers. Once the inter-party drama concludes, the PCs move into one of their final training sessions. They know that they are nearing the end of their program, and eager to find out what their future holds for them. Suddenly, mid-training, there is a full base alert. Aberrants are attacking. The team wants to help defend the base, but Lt. Munroe tells them that the Legions, Orgotek, and Proteus are already scrambling their squads to handle it. They're not ready yet, and she orders them to join the rest of the civilians in the base at the emergency bunker 3 sub-levels down.

      That's when the doors to the training hall burst open and sub-Aberrant mutants rush in at them. The group's first taste of combat is swift and hectic. Moss opens with a flash of fire, and it ends with Andy cutting down half of the mutants with her sonic blade.

      GM Notes: Heretofore I just shorten it to "mutants". Saves a lot of time. I made these enemies minor characters, one-hit dropped. As a group we're still getting used to Initiative slots and purchasing stunts, so I will slowly and gradually up the difficulty of combat as the game progresses.

      The PCs experience a brief moment of panic--Andy’s first instinct is to find a ship and get the hell away from here. Moss also wants to find a ship and blast some Aberrants. Giselle, Miguel, and Xin want to head to the base’s emergency bunker, as Lt. Munroe ordered. Giselle uses her psychometry on one of the mutants and learns that it was transported here in a pod with several other mutants and at least one “black spot,” a source of significant Corruption.

      They turned to Lt. Munroe for guidance, and then the roof of the training room caved in. Both the team and their trainers survive, and through their minicomps Lt. Munroe tasks them with finding any other survivors they can, and getting down to the bunker.

      Miguel makes creative use of his powers to access the base's OpNet and uses the survivors' minicomps to locate them and find the safest route to the bunker. The team sets off. They find a couple of groups of survivors easily, and begin to debate what to do after they’ve found them all. Their debate is cut short when a Divergent Aberrant and his mutant cronies round the corner of the corridor.

      Another brief skirmish breaks out. After a lackluster performance with his Thermokinesis, Moss elects to rely on his laser pistol. His beam pins down the mutants, until the Aberrant uses its powers to short out the pistol. Giselle and Miguel fire their own weapons and take out the mutants, while Andy opens a portal next to the Aberrant and hits it with her sonic blade. Xin takes advantage of the now open path and strides up to the Aberrant. After successfully activating Assault (GM Notes: 9 friggin successes!), he slices his bio-claws into the Aberrant’s chest, severing several vital organs in one blow that instantly slays it.

      GM Notes: The group displayed a little more coordination here, and after a little bit of fumbling learned some effective tricks. This is also when I discovered that Moss's player had purchased a Spider Combat Harness during character creation without realizing what an effective armor/weapon combo it is.

      Giselle again uses her powers on the downed Aberrant, and learns that the pod it came in as just one of several that the Aberrants warped to Luna from aboard a Coalition ship. They are the first psions to discover that the Coalition and the Aberrants are collaborating.

      There is another debate about what to do--get the survivors to safety, leave them to join the assault, both or neither. The group eventually opts to follow Lt. Munroe’s original orders. They pick up all of the remaining survivors, and the scenario ends.

      After the team turns in their reports, they are commended by Lt. Munroe for their actions, and informed that their training has ended. They are introduced to Cpt. Everett Thomas, who will be leading a deep space exploration mission aboard the Leviathan ship Askani. Aeon and the Psi Orders suspected that the Coalition had some involvement with Aberrants, and Giselle's visions confirm it.

      The team is given their next assignment. They will be crewing a state-of-the-art ship that will join the Askani on its quest to find technologies and allies that will aid them against Earth’s various enemies.

      GM Notes: I intentionally made this scenario short and hectic, to ease my players into this new system and get them used to its concepts. All of the roleplay at the beginning was totally initiated by them and took up the bulk of the session, and reminded me how lucky I am to be a part of this group. I had written down a couple of possible Complications, but forgot to make good use of them. It's something that I'll have to be conscientious of next time.

      Next Week -- Mars! Intrigue! A brand new ship! This week can't go by fast enough.

                      Desperate Stars #2: Operation Osiris

                      Six months have passed since the team graduated from their training on Luna. They have been training with their new ship, the Manta-class Dayspring. The Dayspring has been refitted so that it possesses some minor differences from its sisters, giving it greater maneuverability at the cost of requiring separate formatting for the ship itself and its Ares pod. Sam has been named the pilot, and Giselle the navigator. Mason* controls the weaponry, while Miguel handles engineering. Xin remains the emergency medical officer (and surreptitiously sets up personal security for all medical files he has gathered, and a secure method of communication with his Patron in the Ministry).**

                      *GM Notes: Sam's player had a conflicting commitment, and so she wasn't able to attend this session. It's part of the reason we made her character the pilot--so that if she has to miss anything, we simply say that her character has to stay aboard the Dayspring as pilot. After a repeated goof on my part where I kept calling Moss the wrong name, the players decided that Mason was actually better, so the PC's name was changed retroactively.

                      **GM Notes: He successfully made his Cunning + Tech roll to do this, but wasn't able to pay off the Complication. What was the result? *Insert Evil GM Laughter Here* We'll get to that later.

                      They've been training at a small Aeon/Ministry facility on Mars, where security has been tight and everything is tense as the team gets ready to start official missions with the Askani and its crew. The team has been living on the ship during that time, and Miguel has been taking time to get to know the ship's Tesser, which also self-identifies as Dayspring. He asks a few questions to get an idea of what its like to be a Tesser, including its opinion of the Askani. The Dayspring has only been in contact with her through intermittent radio transmissions, relaying mission information and updates strictly as necessary, so it hasn't had time to form one. But, it did assure Miguel that the Askani loves and has full confidence in her crew.

                      As the group is wrapping up a routine flight-training mission, it pulls into the facility's hangar bay just in time to register an explosion in the facility's small civilian sector. Facility security scrambles--the crew is ordered to stay aboard the Dayspring while the bulk of security rushes to the sight of the blast, leaving only a couple of guards watching the hangar. The crew, being PCs and not the kind of players to sit on the sidelines, use the ship's sensors to try and get more information. The Dayspring is equipped with an aberscope, which detects minute traces of Corruption both at the blast sight and heading straight for the hangar bay!

                      At that moment, an armed mob storms the hangar bay and kills the guards. They fire wildly at the ship (which does little more than annoy it), while their leader rushes at the Dayspring with a strange device in his hands.

                      Xin, overly confident in his abilities, exits the ship to deal with the attackers personally. The first couple of shots convince him to find some cover. Meanwhile, Miguel, Giselle, and Mason come up with a crazy idea--they harmonize their noetic fields to channel Miguel's electromagnetic discharge through the Dayspring's weaponry and knock out as many of the attackers at once as possible*. It works, and the Dayspring's laser carbines start spitting bolts of electric pain at the attackers, taking out the bulk of them with a couple of shots. The attackers' leader avoids the bolts and gets close to the ship. That is when Xin leaps out from cover and easily incapacitates him. The device falls out of his hands and clatters to the ground.

                      *GM Notes: So, I gushed to my players about Prometheus Unbound before the game. But, I was glad I did, because the creative mode rules served as an excellent guideline here. It was a crazy idea, but I let it ride, and the players got enough successes to pull it off.

                      With the security detail occupied, the PCs are left to handle the aftermath. The attackers are sufficiently stunned that they won't be getting up anytime soon. Xin wants to take the attackers' leader, who is registering significant Corruption, to the ship's medical bay for questioning. Giselle doesn't want him anywhere near the ship, and the Dayspring agrees, going so far as to shut its boarding hatch in protest. Xin relents, and he and Mason find an unused maintenance bay to drag the man to.

                      Miguel picks up the device the man had tried to attach to the ship, and takes some time to study it. With a cursory examination he's able to deduce that this thing is both of alien origin, and has been significantly modified, most likely by someone with an Aberrant's level of genius. Giselle uses her psychometry on it to find that it was given to their attackers by a woman named Madison Short, who is hiding somewhere in the colony, and they are all part of an Aberrant cult.

                      Xin and Mason, meanwhile, have secured the ringleader in the seclusion of the maintenance bay. They use their minicomps to identify him as Kole Silver, a Polish national who is registered as a sanitation worker in the colony. Xin doses him with an impromptu drug cocktail that acts as a truth serum. Kole, in his inebriated state, confirms that he's a member of an Aberrant cult. Madison gave him the device to attach to the ship at any cost. He doesn't know exactly how to use the device or what it was supposed to do, but he was taught how to activate it. He tells them how to find Madison, and that she relayed orders to him and the rest of the cult inside the colony from somewhere outside, where their Aberrant master waited.

                      Xin makes note of all of this. He and Mason decide that Kole's knowledge of the device makes him too dangerous a risk. Mason, whose previous experiences leave him with little compunction about it, executes Kole and uses his powers to incinerate the body.*

                      GM Notes: I used this as an opportunity to give Xin and Mason a Level 2 Bond, "Shared Secret." The execution may come as a shock to some, but not me, considering the players involved. They think they've gotten away with it for now, but someone was watching...

                      With this knowledge in hand, it's childishly easy for the team to locate Madison Short, and colony logs track her comings and goings. Madison would regularly take a rover out to the Martian surface, and after several kilometers its tracking data would blip out, only to reappear hours later when it returned to the colony. Miguel informs Aeon, and Xin informs the Ministry. The team is given leave to track Madison and see where she is going. Aeon takes possession of the device to bury it in the deepest hole they can find, and then throw that hole into another hole.

                      Thanks to her previous use of psychometry, Giselle is able to use Dowsing to trail Madison from a safe distance without the team being spotted. She and Mason follow in a rover, while the rest of the team waits aboard the Dayspring, ready to launch and meet them wherever they end up.

                      Giselle senses that they are getting close to finding the Aberrant. Madison's trail ends at an old, abandoned mining facility. A quick search of Martian records reveals that it was one of the earliest ones built on the planet in the early 2060s, just before the Aberrant wars. The perfect place for an Aberrant to hide.

                      Giselle and Mason are able to sneak in (again, thanks to Giselle), and travel through the mine. It's important to note here that both took the extra precaution of wearing environment suits. At the center of the mine, they find two Aberrants, and a sizable cult of nearly thirty individuals. They overhear the conversation taking place between the Aberrants: One is berating the other for his failure at planting the device, his ham-fisted manner of carrying out his orders, and exposing the cult. He's ordered to clean up his mess before the second Aberrant warps away.

                      The Dayspring has now come within broadcast range of the mine. Miguel hacks the mine's computers and takes control of its atmospheric controls while Xin deploys and joins the rest of the team. The team is safe in their environment suits while Miguel lowers the oxygen in the mine enough to render the cultists unconscious. Without his backup, the Aberrant is easy prey for the team, as Xin and Mason descend upon him. Xin nearly takes a blow*, and delivers an even stronger one back at the Aberrant. Mason finishes it off with laser fire.

                      *GM Notes: Seriously, Vitakinetics are tough!

                      Miguel reactivates the mine's atmosphere, and Xin uses his iatrosis to rouse the cult. Their leader, a man who identifies himself as Cameron Hodge, orders the rest of the cult to surrender peacefully. Aeon and the Ministry arrive to take them into custody.

                      The team is allowed to question Hodge (formally), who is more than happy to tell them everything he knows about the cult and its plans. He doesn't fear them for an instant. He tells them that the device was indeed of alien origin, and one of his Aberrant "gods" turned it into a weapon to cleanse the galaxy of psions and "alien filth." It was going to generate a quantum warp field around the Dayspring the second it attempted to teleport, and the resulting conflict would tear the ship and everyone aboard it apart at the atomic level. Miguel realizes that this would have created a black hole where the Dayspring had been, swallowing Mars along with it!

                      Hodge reveals the names of his gods: Psychomachie (whom they just eliminated), Azimuth, Legba, and Lamia. The other three are on another planet called Duat, where they found the abandoned alien tech, and even now are preparing to be used against the psions. Hodge gives them the star charts necessary to find it. When asked what "alien filth" he's referring to, Hodge names all of the races Humanity has met so far, including some as yet undiscovered.

                      Suitably horrified, Aeon gives the PCs their first "official" mission as soon as the Askani arrives. They are to travel to Duat and assess the Aberrant threat there.

                      Final GM Notes: Okay, this was a big one, with a lot happening in a fairly short span of time. We're all still getting used to the system--I even tossed out initiative in the first fight because it was just easier to referee it that way. This was also the first time I made use of the Bond rules... which now I have to refamiliarize myself with to make sure that I'm using them correctly. As for the complication that Xin's player activated at the beginning of the game? Well, that will just have to wait for another story...

                      Next Session: We finally meet the Askani and her crew! The campaign kicks into high-gear as the team undertakes their first off-world mission! Strange worlds, alien tech, and vicious monsters await!

                          Operation Golden Barque

                          GM Notes: Both Sam’s player and Miguel’s player were absent from this session. At their request, Mason’s player took over for Miguel, and I took over for Sam, who mostly stayed aboard the Dayspring.

                          A few days after the PCs root out the Aberrant cult on Mars, the Askani arrived in orbit above the planet. The PCs join the main Leviathan, docking their ship Dayspring to it. They are joined by the Essex, a scarab frigate, and the Spiral, a biotech dropship used by the marines aboard the Askani.
                          They jump into orbit around the planet tentatively known as Duat, using the star charts that they took from the Aberrant cult. Only Mason experiences a sense of distortion--for the rest of the crew, it took only seconds. A clairsentient scan of the planet reveals that there are roughly three hundred humans on the planet’s surface, including mutants. They can only detect a single, active Aberrant.

                          The moment the small fleet’s jump ended, however, a stray radio transmission was emitted from the Dayspring. The Dayspring (which Mason insists on calling ‘Computer’) doesn’t have an explanation for it, and the Askani’s crew only recorded garbled ‘noise’. Captain Thomas of the Askani is prepared to ignore it, but the crew of the Dayspring isn’t going to let this go, especially when they realize that the transmission was sent from Xin’s personal minicomp.

                          The crew gathers aboard the Askani for a mission briefing. There, Mason enlists Miguel to help him set up a monitoring system to watch the ship’s chief engineer, a man named Kevin Sydney. Mr. Sydney is still technically on Orgotek’s payroll, and Mason trusts the company as far as he can throw them.

                          GM Notes: This edition is supposed to be more hopeful, but Mason’s player and I discussed his character’s background and motivations, and he wanted something a little more cyberpunk. I felt that this was the perfect opportunity to work in Option 8, which we made his PC a former member of. My personal head canon incorporates the plot hook revealed about Cassel in Prometheus Unbound, which I’m going to use to justify Option 8’s existence when I get around to introducing the Doyen to the game.

                          Xin, meanwhile, went straight to his direct superior within the Ministry (and his 3 dot patron) who arranged to be included amongst the Askani’s crew. Xin explains that he believes his equipment has been somehow compromised. His patron agrees, and shows Xin security footage of him and Mason executing the Aberrant cultist Kole Silver on Mars. Xin tries to explain himself by insisting that because Kole’s genetic imprint was the trigger for the weaponized alien device, he was too dangerous to risk being allowed to live. His patron reminds him that he is a member of an order full of telepaths who could have easily wiped the information and the device’s existence from Kole’s mind!

                          Xin’s patron reveals that the security footage was sent to him directly from Xin’s minicomp. He takes the minicomp and destroys it immediately. He also informs Xin that he has taken steps to cover up this entire fiasco, including making sure that all records of Kole Silver’s existence were erased. Xin gets the impression that his patron is more annoyed by the fact that Xin didn’t go through official channels than anything. Xin is then informed that unofficially he will have a black mark on his record with the Ministry, and both he and Mason will be watched more closely from now on.

                          Giselle realizes that there was a minute trace of corruption coming from Xin, but it disappeared when he got rid of his minicomp.

                          GM Notes: Xin going to his patron triggered the complication that he rolled last session. Something was obviously influencing his minicomp. When I later told Miguel’s player what happened during the session, he came to the conclusion that Xin may be secretly compromised by Aberrants, even possibly working with them. I said nothing to confirm or deny this. My group is a paranoid bunch. I blame myself. The last game I ran for them was Vampire the Masquerade. I had them terrified of a conspicuously parked, unmarked van.

                          Finally, the team attends the mission briefing, where they discuss both their objective and how to carry it out. Xin asks why they can’t just bomb the planet from orbit (which the Askani’s tactical officer, Col. Nathan Christopher, agrees with). Cpt Thomas reminds them that the last device salvaged from this planet would have created a planet-swallowing black hole. Who knows what would happen if they blew everything up at once?

                          It’s decided that the crew of the Dayspring will scout the surface and the cult’s encampment, a small colony near the middle of the planet’s largest continent. The team assumes that, thanks to that strange radio transmission, they are expected. They fly down in the Dayspring, and land it several kilometers from the cult’s encampment. Giselle uses her Dowsing to lead them straight there.

                          En route to the camp, they come across several guard patrols. They manage to evade them with little difficulty, again thanks to Giselle (GM Notes: Don’t ignore your Survival skill, kids!). They reach the edge of the camp, where they observe the cult’s setup. The camp is built around a dig site, with several tents lining the perimeter. Cultists turned slaves dig into the dirt and rock at the base of a small cliff, under the watch of brutal guards. At the top of the cliff, priests worship constantly at the shrines of their “gods”.

                          While the PCs watch, one of the slaves emerges from a dug-out pit, holding a strange device, and cries that he found something. The rest of the slaves cheer, while two mutants emerge from a nearby cave. They snatch the device from the slave, then kick him back into the hole and growl for everyone to get back to work. They then carry the device back into the cave.

                          The PCs determine that one of the Aberrants they’re looking for must be in the cave. They formulate a plan to infiltrate the camp and get more information. They double back and ambush one of the guard patrols. The guards are normal humans armed with conventional weapons, and stand little chance against a full team of psions.

                          Xin uses his Ruin to paralyze the last guard standing after the others mop the forest floor with the rest. They take the guards’ uniforms, and Xin uses his truth serum on the remaining guard, and they question him about the camp. He reveals that two of the Aberrants, Azimuth and Lamia, are off-world, searching for a new base since they lost contact with the Mars branch. Legba remains behind, studying and modifying anything that the slaves dig up. The guards, the priesthood, and the mutants treat the rest of the cult horribly--the slaves were promised a new paradise, and instead are being worked to death.
