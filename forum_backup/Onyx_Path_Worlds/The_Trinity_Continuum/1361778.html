    [Speculative Game Design] Tier 3 Talents

    01-18-2020, 03:26 PM
    Tier 3 Talents. We know at least one of them exists, in the form of Maxwell Mercer, a time-traveler with an unusually long lifespan. But we have no real mechanics for them.

    So, naturally, I'm going to try and make some up. But first, we need to set a baseline for what it takes to ascend from Tier 2, where Talents normally reside, to Tier 3. And the only real example we have for that are Psions. So... let's look at them a bit more closely, shall we?

    PART 1: From Psion To Proxy

    First, let's establish what it takes for a Psion to become a Tier 3, Proxy-Tier Psion. Fortunately, we have that spelled out quite clearly for us in the Aeon corebook, on Page 209. You first have to meet the following three criterions:
    1. Psi score of 5
    2. 5 dots in one Mode
    3. At least 3 dots in another Mode

    Then, and only then, can you purchase the sixth dot of Psi, for the princely sum of 24 xp.

    So, let's take a look at two psions built in opposite ways, and then calculate the xp xpenditure required to raise them to Tier 3. We'll start with a Psion built in the least optimal(for this purpose) manner, and then the next one will be built in the most optimal(again, for this purpose) manner.

    Alice is a Biokinetic. She could be any kind of psion, save for a Quantakinetic or Teleporter, but I like Biokinetics, and also they come first, alphabetically, so Alice will be a Biokinetic. Since we only really care about her Psi score and Mode dots, let's deal with those first.

    A starting Psion has four Mode dots to distribute, and only three of them have to go into Primary Modes. So, let's say Alice has a single dot in all three of Biokinesis' Modes, and her fourth dot went into... oh, let's say Perikinesis, so she could have forcefields. Those are always useful. As for Alice's Psi score, she has the option of raising it to 3, but that would require giving up the benefit of an extra Attribute point, which she doesn't want to do, so it stays at 2.

    Now, to get Alice to Tier 3, first we need to raise her Psi from 2 to 5, which means purchasing three dots of it; fortunately, the Storypath system uses completely flat xp costs, which makes the math easier. Psi costs 12 xp per dot up to Psi 5, and so Alice would have to spend 36 xp in total to raise her Psi to 5.

    Next, we need to raise Alice's Modes some. Let's say she really likes Transmogrify, thinks Adaptation is pretty useful, and could take or leave Psychomorphing. So, to raise Transmogrify from 1 to 5, she has to buy four dots, at 6 xp per dot, for a subtotal of 24 xp. Now she has to raise Adaptation from 1 to 3, which only requires two dots, coming out to a subtotal of 12 xp, and a total of 36 xp on Mode dots.

    Alice is now finally ready to buy her sixth dot of Psi, for a princely sum of 24 xp. That, on top of the 72 xp she's already spent on Psi and Mode Dots, brings her total xp xpenditure to 96 xp.

    So, let's look at Bella, who's much more optimized- once more, for this purpose.

    Bella is a Quantakinetic, solely because I think they're cooler than Teleporters. She has to trade her extra Attribute dot for another Psi dot, making her Psi 3. Furthermore, she spends all 4 of her starting Mode dots on just two Modes- let's say Fundamentals gets 3 dots, and Transmutation gets 1, because she's a fan of Fullmetal Alchemist.

    Bella only needs to raise her Psi by two points, which only costs 24 xp. She also only really needs to raise Transmutation from 1 to 5, because she's alreadygot 3 dots in Fundamentals; raising a Mode by 4 dots still costs 24 xp, but that's the end of it, because now, she already qualifies for that sixth dot of Psi, at 24 more xp.

    Her total expenditure to get to Tier 3 was 72; by the time Alice starts saving up for Psi 6, Bella has already achieved it, and is doing proxy-level shit, and also making gold out of nothing, which isn't really the most effective use of her powers but does make her happy.

    Now, while Alice and Bella bicker about character builds before going on a life-changing space adventure that ends with a newfound respect and also a coffee date at a lovely little hole in the wall in New Venice, which is on Europa, we are going to discuss some numbers. 96 xp is the maximum xp xpenditure to ascend to Tier 3- sure, characters can spend more than that without getting to Tier 3, but mostly by spending it on stuff we've already acknowledged we aren't going to pay attention to. And 72 xp is the minimum xp xpenditure to reach Tier 3.

    So, if we're going to use that as a benchmark for how Talents reach Tier 3, we're gonna have to massage some numbers such that they come out to be roughly within the same range, which we'll just round off to 70-100xp, for conveniences' sake.

    PART 2: It's A Long Way To The Top If You Wanna Rock And Roll

    Talents are a little bit different from Psions. Their main power stat, Inspiration, ranges from 1 to 10, and isn't purchased directly. Instead, Talents have three power sub-stats called Facets. Every odd-numbered Facet dot- and Facets have dot ratings from 0 to 5- has the added benefit of increasing Inspiration by 1. So, unless you're putting the very first dot in a Facet, Inspiration pretty much costs two Facet dots, which themselves cost 10 xp each.

    Don't worry if you don't understand, we'll go through a pair of examples.

    Caden is a new Talent; like all Talents at character creation, she has three dots to allocate amongst her Facets however she wishes. Wanting a strong Facet right out of the gate, she dumps all three points into her Intuitive Facet, leaving her with an Inspiration of 3.

    Dahlia is also a new Talent; however, she spreads her three Facet dots out evenly among her three Facets, giving her an Inspiration of 4.

    And now, with the application of 20 xp towards Facet dots aimed at maximizing Inspiration, it is entirely possible- in fact, a 1 in 3 chance- for Caden and Dahlia to end up with exactly the same Facet dots allocation of Destructive 1, Intuitive 3, and Reflective 1. Talents cannot really be optimized for quick Inspiration growth, as you can see. Even if we suppose Eartha put two dots in Intuitive and one in Reflective, 20 xp spent to optimize Inspiration growth will still produce D1,I3,R1.

    So, now let's figure out how much it costs to get Inspiration 10. Let's use Dahlia's loadout, simply because it makes the math easier, but the numbers are the same for all of them; upgrading a Facet from 1 to 5 costs 40 xp and grants +2 Inspiration. Do that three times, and Dahlia's spent 120 xp to get Inspiration 10. That's a decent chunk more than what even an anti-optimized Psion has to spend, and while "Talents hit Tier 3 at Inspiration 10" is certainly an easy ruling to make, since it's a nice round number, I don't think I much care for it. So, let's look at some different numbers.

    For Dahlia, it's pretty easy to say that 20 xp will purchase 1 dot of Inspiration; that it also gets two Facet dots is nice, but not immediately relevant. Pegging the xp target at 80, since it's nicely between 70 and 100, we get Inspiration 8 as our target for Tier 3 Talents, which will, incidentally, pretty much require that at least one of the Talent's Facets is maxed out. This also gives a bit of wiggle room in Tier 3 for Talents- see, Tier 3 Psions can be Psi 6 or Psi 7, which allows for mild power gradients within the Tier, which I think is nice to have. And with 3 different levels of Inspiration a Talent can have to attain Tier 3, that produces some power gradients of its own, which I like.

    But, well, there's other options to explore. Options that could be much more interesting, and produce more interesting characters, than just "max out Inspiration."

    Let's go back to Caden, who dumped all 3 of her Facet dots into Intuitive. It would only take her 20xp to max out that Facet; Dahlia, meanwhile, would require 40 xp to max out any Facet in particular. So, let's say that the Inspiration side of the Tier 3 requirements are simply "5 dots in at least one Facet," and look at some other, more interesting things you can xpend xp on.

    Psions required not just Psi 5 to ascend, but also 5 dots in one Mode and at least 3 dots in another. However, Talents don't have Modes. They have Gifts, which aren't arranged in ranked chains of progressively stronger but also closely related powers, but are instead just a collection of miscellaneous perks one can pick and choose from as one pleases. Still, they're the main way a Talent upgrades and expresses the state of being a Talent, so let's run with them.

    Talents start with 4 Gifts, right out of the gate, and can purchase more for 5 xp apiece. So, to come up with a range of xp totals that roughly matches up with 72-96, let's say Talents need, in addition to a maxed-out Facet, a total of... seventy minus twenty, fifty, divided by five, plus four... 14 Gifts in order- no, 15 Gifts, because that's a rounder, nicer number than 14. 15 Gifts to reach Tier 3. That puts the range of xp xpenditure at 75 xp in the best case to 95 xp in the worst case, which matches up very closely with the Psion's range.

    So, there's our two options for "how do Talents reach Tier 3?":
    1) Attain Inspiration 8(80 xp).
    2) Reach 5 dots in any one Facet, along with 15 Gifts(75-95 xp).

    Having thought about it some...

    ...personally, I think I just like the first one better. Gifts are certainly useful, but one of the best sources of raw power Talents have is Dramatic Editing, which is powered by Inspiration points. Having more Inspiration lets you use more Dramatic Editing, which makes you more powerful. Meanwhile, if our only Inspired criteria is having five dots in one Facet, then some punk could pour all their points into just one Facet and hit Tier 3 with only Inspiration 4, which kinda feels wrong. So, from here on, I'm going to ignore that second option, which was a nice intellectual exercise, but probably isn't a very good criteria to use in actual play.

    Anyhow, now that we've established how Talents hit Tier 3, let's maybe noodle around with what that means.

    PART 3: Tier Three Talents

    So, our only canon example of a Tier 3 Talent is, of course, Max Mercer, a long-lived time traveler. "Time travel" is, therefore, something that a Tier 3 Talent should, in theory, be capable of. Maybe it's a high-level power even for Tier 3 Talents... and, incidentally, we should come up with a name for them, just for the sake of convenience. So... let's call them Heroes, after some old terminology; in Adventure, what we'd now call Gifts were instead called "Heroic Knacks." It's not very original, I'll grant, but it's just a working title, and it's better than my original idea of simply calling them Paramorphs, after the old technical term for Talents/Daredevils. Also, let's call the powers available to Heroes "Heroic Knacks," simply because it's a perfectly good term.

    So, let's assume Time Travel is the highest, capstone Heroic Knack, requiring Inspiration 10 to purchase. The mechanics of how it works aren't entirely germane at the moment, although that won't stop me from saying "treat it like the Clairsentience Capstone power, Destiny, except it can also do stuff that could reasonably be done with time travel, provided the player can justify it." What does matter is figuring out what it means to be a Hero, and what they, generally, do.

    So, let's go back and look at the canonical technobabble for how Talents work. In essence, they utilize parallel universes in subtle ways, through the energy that exists between parallel universes called Flux. Talents use it in subtle ways to minorly change circumstances in their favor, so therefore, Heroes should use it in much more blatant and weird ways to change things much more. For example, Max Mercer can use Flux to time travel, which can be explained with parallel universes in any number of ways. So, what are some other ways for Heroes to utilize Flux in more blatant ways?

    Well, what about jumping between universes at will? It enables some of the shenanigans of Time Travel, too, such as "going back to Ancient Rome and stabbing Caesar yourself," but not all of them- since it wasn't your universe you did that to, you can't actually change your 'real' history with it. It can also enable adventures in the mode of, say, Sliders or Rick And Morty, traveling between alternate universes and seeing whatever weird bullshit is going on there.

    Or how about teleporting within the same world, as an extension of earlier world-hopping, and as a prelude to time-travel; you're getting better and better at unsticking yourself from the universe in specific ways.

    Or, to go in a different direction, how about something like Clairsentience? It starts with stuff like intuitive clairvoyance, with a side order of being able to spot Flux-touched people at a glance, and then upgrades to... Iunno, being able to predict the future.

    Maybe Heroes can do Powered Science, just like Psions and Novas can. Doc Brown seems like he could be a Hero, why not?

    There's a lot of potential powers in the fictive arena of "My powers work by manipulating parallel universes and the boundaries between them." Feel free to supply your own ideas for what Heroic Knacks could do, I'm barely scratching the surface, and I bet this is a fertile ground for all sorts of cool, bizarre nonsense that could, potentially, one day be usable.

                                                      Just to be clear, though: although it claims to be about martial arts, what it's really about is tier 3 paramorphs — in the context of Martial Arts. It mentions Mundane Martial Artists, but says that there's not much to say about them because there's no need for any new game systems.

                                                      I'm not disagreeing with the approach, mind you: I like the focus of the book. But if you're looking for a supplement that focuses on Martial Arts in general (e.g., providing more Style Edges and more Advanced Combat Maneuvers, and showing write-ups for actual Martial Arts from around the world — not just from Asia — in terms of which Styles and ACMs would best represent them), you won't find that here; that's not what Fists of Flux is about.

                                                      What Fists of Flux is about is laying the groundwork for paramorphs with abilities that go beyond human limits. It's a toolkit that lets you make your own “paramorph powers”.

                                                      And that toolkit isn't limited to making Martial Arts themed powers. If you want to make Techniques that deal with Procedurals or Intrigue instead of Action/Adventure, you can do so using this toolkit. Mind you, you'll probably need to introduce some new Technique Effects that are oriented toward Procedurals and Intrigue in order to do so; but that's not all that hard to do.

                                                      There's a lot of potential here; and I'd love to see supplements for this that fully flesh out that potential.
