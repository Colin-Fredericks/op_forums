    What weirdness could you have stand in for supernatural phenomena?

    12-15-2021, 08:06 PM
    Easy answer would be "Just put some ghosts in your game" but working with the Trinity Continuum base assumption of "No actual supernatural stuff" what could you have? Ancient aliens could fill in as eldritch gods. Maybe some kind of Psi phenomena could create a "ghost"? Maybe sub-Aberrant's could be worked into vampires or werewolves, with an actual Nova being the progenitor of the species? You could even strip something that at first seems supernatural in nature and give it an entirely normal (or Trinity Continuum normal) explanation, like a curse the ends civilizations that is locked away in a tomb that turns out to be a plague. You can probably mine Resident Evil for a lot of ideas about Inspired pathogens that could have been the inspiration for monsters throughout history.

          Why not raid the classics? If you ever saw an old sci Fi film called "Forbidden Planet" you've got a gold mine of source material. Both Psi and Quantum powers could unconsciously create ghosts and monsters. We've already seen in 1st Ed Scion a man with a split personality. His "normal" persona hides his Nova powers with dormancy. His abnormal persona has the Nova powers. Both individuals with Quantum and Subquatum powers could subconsciously use their powers. Many people in the real world think that something like this causes "poltergeist" phenomena. Someone with powers and a dissociative disorder could generate all kinds of "monsters" for the PCs to chase.

                      Disclaimer: This is all very movie science with only a passing glance at real science.

                      Ghosts: Flux based echoes of a Talent dying. Or alternatively cracks in the dimensional wall that let images of parallel people thru. I remember a book I read years ago about a haunted house where it turns out the ghosts are energy projections from a tree that recorded everything that happened around it. The tree would have to bleed off energy during storms so would project fragmented and random bits of the stuff it recorded. I don't really remember why it recorded but it was a natural result of the tree growing and not any thing sinister.

                      Zombies / Skeletons: Fungus. Take a look at the Last of Us. Just have the Fungus feed off of bone marrow so its hidden inside the bones, moves thru a biological version of hydraulics with he fungus providing the convective tissue for the bones and you have skeleton's that want to kill living things thru a instinct. You could also do something like the Rage virus from 28 Days Later.

                      Vampires (or anything qualified as a crypted): Convergent evolution of a human shaped predator (Origin of the Uncanny Valley?). Basically any website that try's to come up with theories about how crypted came to be would be a gold mine for non magic based monsters and races.

                                  I think this hits the point of distinctions without a meaning. Given the breadth of what Quantum, Noetic, and Flux phenomena can cover, the only real rule is that any seemingly occult phenomenon needs to be studiable under the scientific method and quantifiable in in-universe technobabble. Doctor Strange can’t be doing something other from what Pym or Richards do with super-science, where Magic is this separate unquantifiable thing, but ancient evils that dwell in dimensions of human dreams, consciousnesses of the long dead lurking in Atlantean ruins at the bottom of the sea, villages under a “curse” to appear non-linearally through time, eximorph shapeshifters who take beastial form to hunt under the full moon, etc etc etc are all totally valid in setting.

                                  For whatever it’s worth, if I was going to represent a Doctor Strange type “true sorcerer” in the setting, he’s be a Mesmerist or Psiad (depending on the power level of the game) with artifacts that are actually ancient super-tech that grant Scale to his powers. But “that’s not Magic, it’s Noetics” in this case just means “we discovered the science behind so called magic”.

                                              I'm working on some setting stuff where parapsychology has a higher degree of status for my campaign, and multiverse shenanigans help fuel the 'magic' used by cults and lost civilizations. The big element I am working with is any magic that exists is not for players, because it's messy and mostly ritual based stuff. Anyone that actually commits to learning 'magic' is committing to a complete philosophical overhaul of their way of thinking (I'm an old Mage player, if it's not obvious), and there are no hobby sorcerers running around out there. Most that end up being able to use magic to throw a fireball or curse someone are channeling multiversal energy and will be paying the price for every spell in a narrative way that is not fun for the average RPG player.
