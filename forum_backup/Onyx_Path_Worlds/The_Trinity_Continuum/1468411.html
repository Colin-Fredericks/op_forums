    Flux weirdness and lost worlds

    11-20-2021, 01:43 PM
    I liked the idea of Flux and the idea that alternate worlds bleeding into each other is a big part of Trinity Continuum. It's a good explanation for how Daredevils, or Talents in this edition, are different then Quantum and Psi powered individuals but still are powered in some fashion. Lost Worlds potentially being other worlds bleeding into our world is also a good explanation for why adventurers could visit a lost city in Africa that is populated by sapient gorillas and nobody else can find it later. It's a meta reason for why the world pretty much keeps on track for the most part while adventurers can still go to these amazing places.

    But I was wondering if Lost World's or other Flux oddities are always going to be examples of cross time weirdness? One of the things I liked about the first version of Adventure! was that Z-Ray's propagated both backwards and forwards in time, so the Hammersmith Event could have been interpreted as having retroactively cause, for example, the Chiquibul caves having two civilizations of people living in it like something out of a Jules Verne story. Is Flux weirdness something that can be a permanent part of the world? Is things like that going to be more a part of the new version of Adventure!, with the "modern day" setting of the Trinity Continuum having Flux weirdness being more transitory crossovers?

                                              In our game we use the idea of guides. Say you visit a Dino swamp (Strange Place) in the 30s, then come back and live the rest of your life till the 2000s. You tell people about the dinosaur swamp you visited but they say it doesn't exist cause they've been to where you went and its not there. However if you decide to take them there your self the "bridge" for lack of a better word to the dino swamp is there when your guiding them so they can all visit it with you. It all comes down to belief. Even if someone tells you about a Strange Place (After Adventure! when it's much harder to get to them) there is always that small bit of doubt that it doesn't exist so the Flux probability don't collapse the right way and you can't find it, however by having someone lead you to it you avoid that outcome because they know the Strange Place is there and their certainty causes the Flux to reconnect the Strange Place to the world but only when they lead the expedition. It explains why in all these adventure movies and stories like The Mummy, Congo, Indiana Jones, or even the new Jungle Cruise move the main character always needs some sort of guide to get there.

                                              To be fair we also use the rule that observing something locks (for you at least) it in place on a Flux level. As in once you've seen a Strange Place yourself you can always go back there, but without seeing it yourself its a crap-shoot whether you can reach it. Evidence it exists helps you reach it if you've never seen it but it isn't certain and the more removed the evidence the less likely. Its why the note book with the route to the lost city is so important, sure a photocopy of the map might have the route on it but as it's a step removed from the main source it makes it harder i.e. adds complications to the roll to get to the Strange Place. Belief also plays a role, its why cults and the like are so prevalent in adventure stories. A strong enough belief in something collapses the probability state of the Flux to get the outcome you believe in. Basically a way for groups of people to unconsciously use dramatic editing. And also explains why the skeptic following the map can never find the Strange Place but the Hero can.

                                              This all grew out of explaining why Max Mercer can't change the past and won't talk about the future. Its because he watched the timeline collapsing that probability state for him. He saw it happen so he can't change it, and if he tells people what he saw because he is such a respected figure and they believe in him it prevents them from changing it as well. So he plays the mysterious benefactor and sends people on butterfly effect missions he doesn't explain to change the coming future. An indirect way of doing things but all he can do since he created so many fixed points in time he has to work around.
