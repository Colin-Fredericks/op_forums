    Honestly, I'd like to see there being more circumstances where you can usefully gain Taint as a PC, to make it more of a left-hand-path temptation option. As it is, you tend to only get it if you choose to buy stuff tainted. Getting temporary taint is actually pretty hard to do.

    My houserule was to let people take temporary taint for extra successes on a 1-for-1 basis for power rolls, up to maximum of their level in the power. This also helped out with encouraging people to invest in actual levels in the power rather than just maxing out Mega-Attributes after getting a dot in certain powers. I think we also allowed for more frequent fast quantum regain rolls, but with a steadily increasing temporary taint cost the more times you did it within the base interval.

                    If there's a need to track how inhuman a given nova seems to the human world, couldn't we just track their reputation?

                        Taint isn't some enforced morality system, or a punishment for players. Taint is an important part of the setting and an inherent part of the game. I have to repeat my earlier argument that Aberrant is not just some supers game. A major part of what makes Aberrant a good game is the setting, and Taint is a major part of that setting. Almost every major organization in the setting takes its stance at least in part based upon its view Taint. Utopia sees it as a major PR problem, Teragen wholeheartedly embraces taint, Proteus sees taint as an indicator of the dangers Novas pose, and Aberrant fears what will Proteus is doing because of its fear of Taint. Taint is a major driving force in the setting an relegating it to the category of a cosmetic choice, or an unnecessary punishment for players strips away what makes Aberrant more interesting than other vanilla supers games.

                            I wasn't trying to say Taint should go away. I was just trying to say that taint shouldn't be bigger than it is, or by default harder to manage than it is. Yes, taint is a part of the setting and helps set it apart from other supers games. However, it is also part of the setting that taint CAN be managed and I will point to Eden from Aeon as a prime example of that. So yes, it should still be there, it should have deleterious effects and yet have enough of a temptation for players to consider using it. Yet despite all this, it should be manageable.

                            If it becomes an unmanageable mess, or at least one too complicated for your average player to potentially do it, then it does become a case of punishing the character. So the developers have to consider the importance and relevancy of taint, and make it a challenge for the players, but a manageable one because if they make it too hard to deal with it will turn away potential players who would otherwise love the game with a more manageable version of taint. I've seen it happen with ST's who were anal retentive on issues of Limit/Resonance/Torment/Paradox in Exalted and people abandon games because of it.

                            So as I will continue to say: Yes, taint needs to be in there and probably about as manageable as it was in the existing version of the game otherwise it will cease to be a thing of thematic importance and become the cause of turning Aberrant into just another frustrating game no one but a few die hard fans want to play.

                                    In a game now stretching over 10 or more years with a few long brakes in it and my character has managed to get I think a point or two of temporary Taint so it is extremely rare. The only way to really risk Taint, aside for buying Tainted powers, is if you constantly try to overload your power uses and even then it's not a terribly big chance. I didn't even really pay attention to the whole Taint trait there on my char sheet until I joined with the Teragen and started to wonder how on earth will I ever get Chrysalis points at all never mind getting enough to go into an actual Chrysalis. Well perhaps when my year old kid moves out of home my char might have managed to gather a few Chrysalis points, or if looking this positively perhaps even on the verge of his first Chrysalis. Anyway it's unlikely to happen.

                                    So anyway back to the question at hand. Should Taint be a bigger issue? It depends wholly on what kind of stories you want to tell. Do you want to tell stories of the inevitable slow slide into Taint and the struggle against it by trying to cling to your diminishing humanity? If yes then Taint should be a bigger issue. If you want to tell stories of the Nova Golden Age, then no Taint likely shouldn't be much of an issue. Like said Taint is a setting concept but at the current system it hardly ever comes up unless one deliberately buys Tainted powers.

                                    So do not loose Taint from the setting but gives us a few optional systems for it, one for low-Taint setting, one for mid-Taint setting and one for high-Taint setting. I know there are some such rulings in the players guide but to my recollection they really aren't that radical to make a huge difference, if I recall correctly they mostly affix permanent Taint costs to buying powers etc. instead of largely affecting gaining temp Taint during game.

                                            Yes, an adjustable taint system would probably be best as long as it is not itself too complicated. If the system tries to enforce some kind of Vampire-like inevitable slide or even just causes an excessive amount of bean-counting a lot of groups will simply ignore it altogether or make their own house rules which is its own can of worms. Having an official adjustable system (or even just an official scale for that matter) wold make getting pickup games together or integrating people from different groups smoother since everyone could sync with the setting on the taint dial for that particular game so to speak.

                                            People invariably want to bring in characters that do not fit well with a narrow, fixed taint formula; they are not usually the majority but there is no reason to summarily exclude them either. In a (more or less) round robin game I ran the characters ranged from one who had a very bad eruption and had to push hard to survive at first and entered the campaign with the realization that she had to go easy and let her significant taint load bleed off or be consumed by it, to one who was a squeaky clean starry-eyed "Feisty Disney Princess" type who did not know what this "dark side" of quantum she heard people talking about was and never built up enough to notice, with the other characters somewhere in the middle. While extreme opposites and skirting the limits of the system both concepts are valid enough on a setting and a game system level in the original game and really should be doable in the new one too. Draconian "thou shalt descend into a tainted monster" (and other similar rules on rails) systems only limit the concepts possible which tends to disappoint players who do not like to march in lockstep so they do not get into the spirit of the story as much as they should and even sometimes drop out of the game altogether.

                                                My feeling on Taint is that it should be a major part of characters, but that it should also have some mechanical tweaks to make it more than just getting tentacles coming out of your forehead or whatever. Taint should represent your character evolving more toward whatever type of post-human being your powers and personality would send you down.

                                                Someone posted a scene from All-Star Superman earlier, that shows that the mere fact of Superman's omniscience leads to an enlightened desire to help people. That's an aberration! This is a being who sees the interconnectedness of all things, every moment of his life, to such a degree that he can't help but feel overwhelming empathy for those around him. That's going to remove you from the experience of baseline humanity just as thoroughly as any other aberration.

                                                My hope for the new Aberrant is that Taint has a much greater mechanical impact, but that it also be used to reinforce your character's move away from humanity in a more interesting way. To my mind, the single greatest example of a high-Taint nova in comics is Dr. Manhattan. He doesn't turn into Cthulhu or start rampaging through New York - he just loses the ability to think like a human.
