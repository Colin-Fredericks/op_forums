                                        One thing I like about these previews is that aside from the Triton Foundation, every Allegiance we've seen so far has a dark side, even if they have noble goals.

                                        9 can be SHIELD, but they're ultimately privatized government spooks preserving the status quo. Les Fantomes can play Robin Hood as much as they want, but at the end of the day, they're still just a bunch of crooks and thieves. The Theseus Club employs individuals of questionable background (see the Yakuza board member), and is perfectly willing to throw due process out the window in pursuit of their prey. Then there's the Transcendent Alliance, who refuses to commit atrocities, but is totally okay with profiting from someone else's atrocities.

                                        A lot of moral gray here; plenty of plot fodder and story-role flexibility.

                                            Excellent observation! I didn't want to have a section where these are the protagonist groups and these are the antagonists. Some are better or worse than others, but you should be able to run some really good stories featuring just the groups presented, whether you choose to use them as protags or antags or both. Uprooting the negative elements of your own group is a well-worn trope with a lot of variations ranging from betrayal, to all-out civil war, to a single bad apple. Season to taste.

                                            (Also, the Triton teaser I posted last October wasn't the full writeup on them, so you have that to look forward to.)

                                            I'd like to point out that the built-in good/evil dichotomy isn't something I specifically requested of my writers; they all just nailed it naturally. They've all delivered some stellar material.

                                                I am very interested in seeing what the Aeon society's moral gray area looks like. I believe in Adventure! Mercer explicitly tells his recruits that in order to maintain good will, they don't go anywhere they aren't invited. Which means that Aeon's opponents can get away with a lot of bad things, as long as they convince the local "legitimate" authorities to wave the Society off and silence all other voices.

                                                That also raises an interesting question of what precisely constitutes a legitimate call for help. Will the Society back off from helping the common people if the government tells them to leave? Will they aid private individuals against the public will and opinion if the individual's goals and methods align with Aeon's ideals? Who decides which calls for aid to answer or ignore, and why?
