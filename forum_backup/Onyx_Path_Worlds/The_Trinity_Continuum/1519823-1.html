    I'd actually go for Talents/Superiors/Psiads for the character types. After the defeat of the Doyen the Prometheus Chambers are either jailbroken or discarded in favour of a new way of unlocking noetic powers, and by the time of the setting psions are rare or (practically) nonexistent. Some of the old psi orders are around, but not all of them.

    Superiors meanwhile are a great way to do 'super soldier' type characters, particularly if the technology to make them gets refined enough to have more control over how they develop. You could probably do an entire setting based around Superiors! Plus even in Aeon they've already not exclusive to Japan, it makes sense that in a few hundred years a lot of other factions would have discovered/stolen/bought/leaked the secrets of their creation.

    And Talents are basically a given.


    I also see roughly two time periods to set such a setting: ~2300-3000ce where humanity is exploring the galaxy and no new big enemies have revealed themselves yet, or far into the future a handful of generations before humanity transcends into ???. For the latter I'd probably pull from stories set just before the fall of civilisation and play up themes of decadence and apathy.

        Decadence/apathy isn't really something that Trinity Continuum games tend to do. I'd go with the 2300–3000 “era of exploration” idea, with a preference toward 3000 and perhaps a downplaying of the continuing importance of Earth.

        In that regard, I wouldn't necessarily go the route of Asimov's Foundation series where Earth ends up forgotten; but there are plenty of space opera settings where Earth is basically irrelevant: for instance, the Battletech universe, where the Great Houses of the Inner Sphere fight bloody interstellar wars with each other, and where Earth, while literally being at the center of it all, simply isn't a power player; or Traveller's Third Imperium, where Earth exists off in one of the corners of the Imperium and has largely ended up as a historical footnote.

            If you have the ability to settle large areas quickly, which the technology of Aeon would give you, and a society were young couples can settle down and have descent sized homes young, populations can grow quickly. Thus allow a few centuries of expansion and the Earth won't be the center anymore.

            Look at Anglo-America. Britain was massively important in the histories of both Canada and the United States. But ask yourself, "How relevant is Britain to most of the populations of either nation?" In Calgary, Edmonton, Minneapolis/Saint Paul, Denver, Seattle, San Francisco, or Vancouver, is Britain really relevant? North Carolina wasn't settled out of Britain. It was settled largely by groups moving from other parts of the colonies or by groups from continental Europe moving to the lands of the King of Hanover. The areas settled by Scots highlanders being the major exception. North Carolina was the first American colony to declare independence from Great Britain. They did this well before the American Declaration of Independence. Only the Highlanders stayed loyal to Britain.

            If a planet has been settled for a couple of millennia, the cultural importance of the place where the settlers came from will likely be merely historical. Spread humanity out in areas of the galaxy were you can't even see Sol (the sun) through a high powered telescope and Earth becomes as relevant as the names of the Pharaohs of the seventh dynasty.

                On one hand I imagine a galactic empire in its golden age ruled by noble houses of Novas, supported by psions and talents, who compete among each other for glory and prestige by expanding the borders of the empire.                             I still stand by the idea that we need a premise for the setting, then we apply the space opera theme/mood and finally we work on the mechanics to model the new setting.

                            Yes, a thousand years is long enough that any polity would likely be unrecognizable. Assuming it even still exists.
                            Still it may be useful touchstone for players to recognize the game world. It's why most D&D worlds use the English feudal model, despite it being the rarer of the European medieval societies.

                            Now a Galactic Empire in a Golden Age with various polities vying for power & prestige sounds like an interesting starting point. Plenty of fertile ground for the stories.
                            Since we need polities for politicking (and to use as Allegiances), the first question is how many should we have to start with? We can add more as needed. But keep in mind we need to differentiate between them. If we are working with the idea of the Continuum in play, something that is coming up more and more as the gameline goes on, then we could have some backlash/foreshadowing. With some survivors from a timeline where the Civil War has already caused the collapse are running around trying to change the future...to the end of their side winning, of course. It also lets us to use both of Firanai's ideas as a single setting. Add plenty of "time portals" to cross over between the ruins of the Empire and the height of it's power. Kind of like how Adventure! and Aether have a crossover portal, but we make them a larger part of the setting. Explain it away as the result of damage to the fabric of reality by some superweapon that was used during the war, but no one agrees on who used it.

                            I think the Houses of the (Scarlet) Realm from Exalted as a decent model (with easy access) to see how these political Allegiances should look like. That said, Trinity does have it's own archetypes for it's Alligiances within each era settings. There almost always an explorer Allegiance, for example.

                            If Inspiration (and all the subtypes) is a well understood phenomenon by this point, at least by the people at the top, then each Allegiance probably has it's own preferences and stereotypes on each of its subtypes of Inspired. That opens ideas for how to play these groupings within each Allegiance.
                            I'm not fond of the idea of all of them being Noble Houses, but I can see a few moving around. Corporations, religious groups, "regional" and "industry" special interest groups and well....political parties sound like potential options and all of them would have different ideas on how to use those abilities within their ranks.

                            Thoughts ripple out, birthing others

                                        The thing I'd be more inclined to borrow from Exalted is the idea of humanity developing “sub-races” — or rather, of humanity diversifying beyond the realistic boundaries of what a human is. If you want to lean into the idea of there being a bunch of “aliens” who are basically humans with unusual skin tones or cranial features, why not make them actually be humans with unusual skin tones or cranial features?

                                        For actual aliens, stick to the guidelines provided in Under Alien Skies; but for these human variations, either make them literally no different from baseline humans besides how they look, or let them pick a mix of minor positive and negative traits to represent strengths and weaknesses they have compared to baseline humans. I also wouldn't go with actual sub-races, per se: they'd be more like family lines, and fully capable of interbreeding with baselines and each other, resulting in more of a spectrum of options than a bunch of distinct species.

                                        For their origins, consider the possibility that they descend from Quantum-powered humans. They are not themselves Quantum-powered or Corrupted, nor do they show any particular bias toward or against Erupting; but they nevertheless inherit and pass on some of the differences between their ancestors and baseline humans. If Superiors become widespread, then these human variations would most likely be descended from them.

                                                My basic idea for the setting is to have a core setting in the main book (which would get more development in a separate book) and two smaller settings in an alternative setting chapter (these settings would also become books).

                                                My preferred setting would be in a renaissance/renewal period. The old society was destroyed hundreds of years ago by invaders from an alternate reality. Certain traces of their influence remains. (This allows the introduction of Aether et al later). Society has begun to recover "Hope, Unity, Sacrifice" is the motto of this new Federal League.

                                                This would be a game involving exploring the ruins of the old society and the new cultures born from it.

                                                The two alternative settings would be...
                                                First a very Noir/dystopian dark ages/fallen ecumen society. Dictators and corporate states would be major threats.
                                                Second a thriving Federal Ecumen society. An expanding state. It could fall into being an Empire, or the Federal Ecumen could grow into something new and more just than past societies.
