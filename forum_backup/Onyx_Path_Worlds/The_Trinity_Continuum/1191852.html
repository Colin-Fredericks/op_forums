          Well, besides the fact that the bonus to other skill rolls in extremely useful (not only is the bonus nice, it also avoids having to spend a lot of dots on skills that are secondary to your concept since the bonus can cover that), you gain a bonus if you fail rolls involving your specialty using the skill it's based on.

          Aim (Pistols) doesn't make you better each time you shoot, but if you're having a rough dice day, or it's just a bunch of tough shots, you rack of Momentum faster so you can shoot really well later.

          The goal, as I understand it, is to replicate something like the end of the movie Jaws. Miss, miss, miss, dramatic beat, one-liner, super hit. Rather than just another way to eke a few more dice/enhancement for your favored weapon.

              My favourite example when discussing this is still the Dirty Harry speech
              Did he fire six shots or only five'? Well to tell you the truth, in all this excitement, I kind of lost track myself. But being that this is a .44 Magnum, the most powerful handgun in the world, and would blow your head clean off, you've got to ask yourself one question: 'Do I feel lucky?' Well do ya, punk?
              Because that's clearly an attempt to intimidate (Presence + Empathy, probably), with bonuses for really knowing your gun.

              It's also really, really powerful. A level of Enhancement is roughly equivalent to 3 extra dice, meaning that at 5 dots in a skill, you're adding roughly equivalent of 9 extra dice when you're working adjacent to your wheelhouse. It solves that problem of you being the gun guy, but not being able to disassemble and re-assemble your gun (Or mod it, or fix it) without also being capable of hand carving a marble statue, or not being able to blend in at a gun club without also being a superspy. As long as you have the bare minimum skills in the field (Because if you don't roll a single success, all the Enhancement in the world won't help you), you're still competent where that field crosses over with the one you're amazing at.

                          My biggest issue with the specialties is I wish they had used a different term for the mechanic. If my group ends up playing the game I'm sure we'll get used to it, but it is going to cause confusion and the less confusion, the easier it will be to get them invested in the game.

                                      My read on these would be that it's entirely down to the group. But from top to bottom, my personal read would be yes, maybe no, probably no.

                                      Pistol whip is entirely something that would be reasonable for someone who specialises in pistols to be better at - You're used to using pistols in a combat situation, therefore you're better at using pistols to hit people. And it's probably not the most unbalanced thing in the world, given how lame pistols are probably going to be as melee weapons.

                                      I could go either way on the Medicine one, but I feel that if you're just going for a hit to the "sensitive bits", you're not going to really benefit from anatomy, because everyone knows which bits are sensitive. I don't need any medical knowledge to know that hitting someone in the testicles or poking them in the eyes is going to hurt more. But I think that if you were a bit more specific ("I'm going to try to injure the nerves/muscles/whatever in his arm, so he drops the weapon"), I think that could totally justify the specialty (And you could then use the additional successes to buy the opponent a Complication that would potentially cause him to drop the weapon, for example).

                                      And for the "One Step Ahead", I'd argue probably no, but again it comes down to the narrative. If it's just an outright car chase on a straight road, then no, because "One Step Ahead" isn't literal. But if you were using it to do the thing from episode one of Sherlock, where you work out where they're going and beat them there by a different route, then it would work - Because you're "One Step Ahead" of them and know where they're going, so you're able to work out a more efficient route to the destination. In that case, you're using your superior Enigmas skill to crack a puzzle, which makes it easier for you to catch them.

                                      Weirdly, I felt fairly strongly that the last one would be a no, but the more I think about it, the more I can easily see narratives in which it works. And that's the deciding factor, to me.

                                      tl;dr - If it makes narrative sense.

                                          A good alternate name would be "Area of Expertise" and then when you use your bonus with another skill it would be "Applying your Expertise".

                                          I.E. I have Close Combat 4, with an Area of Expertise in "Fencing". When I use persuasion to try to influence the rich socialite, who happens to be into fencing, I apply my area of expertise to the conversation, gaining the +2 enhancement, because we share a common interest.

                                          It's wordier, but it doesn't have the legacy baggage of the term Specialty from all the previous game lines.
