                                In Aether, you play as Squires, Gogs, or even Magogs. Squires are individuals capable of wielding Aether devices to spin reality. Gogs have learned to use Aether to enact a temporary change, either in themselves or others. Magogs have stepped outside the bounds of humanity, enacting permanent physical changes, and are able to use Aether without the aid of a device or prompt.
                                As I read this, Squires are roughly equivalent to Daredevils; Gogs are kind of like Mesmerists; and Magogs are kind of like Stalwarts — though they're not the same, and the division appears to be less about what they can do and more about how they do it. Squires are (extra)ordinary humans; anything they can do with Aether works through their gear. Gogs are humans with powers: their powers can work through gear, too; but can presumably also work through “prompts”, which I take to mean special actions — kind of like incantations, though usually with a more sciency flavor. Magogs, meanwhile, are no longer human, and can wield their Gifts at will.

                                            It is a background assumption in the setting that Inspired abilities are growing and changing over the generations, so having characters from the earliest period yet chronicled possessing limitations not found in later generations is entirely fitting. We also don't know what happens to end the era and make the world forget aboth Aether science; I suspect a distraught Tesla will perform a global unwinding that sucks up most/all of the Aether and makes the uninspired forget about it.

                                            Give that Edison's Unwinders actively recruit Squires and require contracts granting the organization rights to any devices they invent, we can presume that Squires have advanced science abilities similar to Talents from the core book. The fact that Gogs can, "use Aether to enact a temporary change, either in themselves or others," suggests Biokinetic and Vitakinetic abilities triggered by external means. Magogs apparently often become monstrous in nature, suggesting they develop aberrations. It'll be interesting to see if characters can switch between types, since it makes thematic sense for a budding mad scientist to start by building devices to use, transition into drugging themself (or others) to produce temporary "enhancements", then take the plunge into lasting transhumanism by changing themself permanently.
