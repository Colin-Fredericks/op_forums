    A Trinity Space Opera game set centuries post Aeon

      One thing I think is vital for this game is the ability to play each of the types of inspired PCs.

      Figures like Northwest Smith, Barbarella, Captain Kirk, Barbara Wright, C'Mell, Han Solo, Flash Gordon, and Dune's Mentats, are all Talents (Barbara is listed as a stand in for large numbers of the Doctor's Companions).

      Meanwhile, Spock, Councilor Troi, Luke Skywalker, the Jedi mainly, the Lensmen, Guild Navagators, and the Bene Gessirit, are all either Psions or Psiads (or something more powerful we'll call Psychics). Let's call this category Psis.

      Novas/Stalwarts/Olympians are represented by figures like the Doctor, Odo, Data, the Green Lantern Core, Paul Antredies, and several members of the Guardians of the Galaxy.

      The Space Opera game setting will need to support all types and have a practical mechanism for stating the power levels and managing it.

      Figures like Sarah Jane Smith, Dr. McCoy, Dale Arden, Northwest Smith, and others are on a very low power scale compared to figures like Odo or a Guild Navagator. But they still influenced the course of events. Something needs to balance things out so that figures like Jo Grant and the Red Lensman can exist in the same story and both have serious effects on the outcome.

      It strikes me that the dramatic editing systems would be the right sort of mechanism for achieving this balance.

                  I'd be inclined to make this setting something of a kitchen sink, fitting as much as possible from as many previous eras as possible. And yes, that includes Ambrosia and Aether, though they likely go by different names in this era. It should even include cybernetics, digital intelligence, and virtual reality a la Anima.

                  Which is not to say that all of these things should be available everywhere. Anima technology, for instance, is probably being used by aliens rather than humans. In fact, I'm figuring that one of the new alien types in this era should be a collective of posthumansaliens that have abandoned physical form and moved into a digital existence, interacting with “meatspace” with robotic drone bodies not unlike the Qin Biosuits (but hardtech rather than biotech). And since even Aegis-era Super-Science is capable of making constructs that are thinking, reasoning creatures, robots in the Space Opera setting should be no problem at all; the only difficulty is figuring out where they fit into the setting.

                  Likewise, Aether should be somewhat localized in where it appears, and should be a warning sign of extradimensional incursions, most likely by the Ascendancy. Gogs and Magogs should be possible in these incursion zones (bearing in mind that since we're dealing with an interstellar scale, and probably a galactic one, a “zone” can include an entire star system) and are generally seen as harbingers of the Ascendancy, especially since the Ascendancy now knows about Aether and actively seeks to use it themselves: 31st century Squires, Gogs, and Magogs are very likely to be aliens from the Ascendancy.

                  At the time of this writing, we're still a ways away from knowing the secrets of Ambrosia (the Aegis KS is still underway); but I suspect that Ambrosia could be recast as a “wonder drug” that enhances the Inspired, and can turn baselines into Inspired. It should be rare, though; although I'm not yet sure what the limiting factor will be that defines its rarity: that will come once we know what the Setting Secrets are for Aegis. Note that Champions aren't just Talents; they're Talents who have been affected by Ambrosia, and as a result can do more. Again, as of this writing we're haven't yet seen Aegis's Gifts; so we don't know entirely what that means. But we have seen the “Special Rules” chapter, and it includes added uses for Dramatic Editing for Champions who have access to Ambrosia. The existing rules please this in terms of the Moirai; but in the space opera setting, I could see recasting those in terms of the types of Facets: Clotho benefits would become Intuitive benefits; Lachesis benefits would become Reflective benefits; and Atropos benefits would become Destructive benefits.


                  Æon, of course, contributes its aliens to the mix, among other things. This includes the Biosuits of the Qin and the alternate “Talent Gifts” of the Coalition Phyla. And it also adds Superiors to the mix, as well as providing the rules basis for Psiads and Psions.

                  Speaking of which: advancing the setting to the 31st century would allow Psiads to evolve further than they have even in the Æon era; and I suspect that the confrontation with the Doyen will likely be long over by then, with one side benefit being that Prometheus Chambers will not longer have Doyen-imposed limitations. It may be, in fact, that the distinction between Psiads and Psions is now strictly a matter of whether they came by their psionics naturally or had it induced in them. Either way, remember that Quantakinesis is humanity's birthright: human psis should universally have access to it, though many may not bother to develop it beyond its basic abilities. Thought should also be put into how or if Oracles and Mesmerists get included.

                  Talents, of course, will have Inspiration; but the space opera Talents might have a further ability to “pay for” Dramatic Editing with successes from appropriate skill rolls, similar to the option presented in Assassins (“Rolled Flashbacks”, p.103). Also from Assassins is the possibility of cooperative Dramatic Edits (“Group Flashbacks”, p.102; the text suggests that such Dramatic Editing has the potential to be overpowered and might need limits placed on it; consider requiring every participant to spent Inspiration, and for the group as a whole to spend Momentum on it.) And there may be “Dramatic Editing Edges” available to Space Opera Talents that define uses for Dramatic Editing that are cheaper than usual (see “Free Flashbacks”, Assassins p.102 for what to do if the cost of a Dramatic Edit gets reduced to zero).

                  I was also going to address Quantum-powered space opera heroes; but I need to leave for work. So I'll come out l back to this later. ​​​​​​

                  My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                          I love the discussion so far.

                          Honestly, I would focus my Space Opera largely on Inspired level characters, so basically the same 3 of Adventure! and Aegis for balance sake.

                          On a thematic level, I would make Talents partially aware of the nature of their powers, like the Mentats of dune, they have powers (mental powers) but they work as a technique learned at school and not a super power (like Quantum and Psi Powers), so they are aware they are more than human, but to them its because they trained a lot with the proper method, and not the use of an external force (Flux). But not all talents are aware or use this kind of power.

                          On a more mechanic approach, I would use the system I created in Fists of Flux Revised (and I love this system, absolutelly proud of it, if you bought FoF its already there, just download the new version) of Stances and Techniques. A Mentat, while in the "Mentat Stance" (I cant remember the name of the trance they enter when doing their thing) they can use some mental techniques to remember a lot of things and do super fast calculations.

                          Psis and Quantum should use less dramatic edition, but have access to more powerful gifts than the ones in Adventure! (probably in the level of Aegis, the chapter has not been available by the time I wrote this) with more scale and versatility to compensate the lack of DE.

                          On Aliens, I defintly would work aliens as variations of humans, because Space Operas dont have weird aliens, but green or blue people that the main protagonist can kiss (and have brief romances). But as Aeon already have the alien aliens (that are far better by the humans with makeup version), we may need to deal with it, maybe make the human aliens human colonies that evolved for a few centuries, since the Aberrant War, into slightly different humans.

                          Next, on Androids (because full robots are not space opera enough) we may use the Quantum Gifts to work as equipments and enhancements on the original model (human level androids), or optionally create a new set of Edges that work like the Cybernetics of Anima, but exclusive to Androids, and they dont have Inspiration (or maybe there are normal androids and Inspired Androids, like Andrew the bicentenial man).

                              I'm all for the widest possible variety of types in this Space Opera setting (Aeon is also a Space Opera, but more restricted options work for that game).

                              I agree with MATEUS LUS that something like either the daredevil/mesmerist/stalwart set up of Adventure! or the champion/oracle/olympian set up of Aegis will suit this game. I'd add a section in the appendix explaining how to bring in all other printed types.

                              Certainly Flux based types, or rather those most easily translated into Flux based types, are the most common protagonists in this type of space opera. With notetic a distant second and quantum lagging behind. But games aren't novels and maybe I'd prefer being Brainiac 5 or Monel to being Han Solo or Spock in a given game.

                              The reason I prefer the name Argosy for this setting is the idea of sailing beyond one's cultural ecumen. I want a big mysterious galaxy that a single being can't know or fully comprehend. Billions of inhabited worlds most of which are as complex and complicated as contemporary Earth. Like Sinbad, Ulysses, Gulliver, or Saint Brendan, the PCs should voyage into unknowable mystery.
