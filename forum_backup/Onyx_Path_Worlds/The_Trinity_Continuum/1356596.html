    Nova... Alchemicals?

    12-23-2019, 02:27 AM
    I love the Alchemicals, and I love Trinity continuum, so… Two great tastes, right?



    During the Nova age, one of the biggest goals for many groups was to accumulate more Novas. Many attempts to reproduce the Galatea incident were made, most of them unsuccessful. As tensions between Novas and Baselines grew, the need for “artificial” novas also grew, with both Novas and Baselines seeking to develop what had become the holy grail.



    One Nova managed to put together a small team of mega-intelligent novas with the idea of devising a technology that could enhance a baseline to Nova capabilities by implanting an artificial device into the baseline. A nova with a somewhat mystical Bent, David Connel christened the creations “alchemicals” after the legendary goal of some alchemists to create life. He would kindle the new life of the Nova within the Baseline body.



    In the short term, what David Connel produced was a large number of corpses, and a smaller number of flux-ridden mutants that went wild. His experiments soon saw him on the run, with Utopia, the Directive, and the Teragen pursuing him, a pursuit that ultimately ended in his death—but not before he succeeded. More importantly, not before he’d hidden his knowledge in a number of places.



    The process:



    The soulcores, as Connel named them, were incredible devices. A small sphere, the soulcore contained three components. The first was an AI core that allowed it to analyze anyone who touched the device, determining if they were suitable for enhancement. Those who were not, or who did not have the pass codes, would come to no harm, or good. It should be noted that the AI core did have some ability to override its directives, for example, if enough time had passed that all likely candidates would be dead of old age.



    The second component is a reservoir of nanites that flood into the subject, modifying them on a genetic and gross level, preparing their body to endure the third component—a tiny reactor system that, working through the modifications prepared by the nanites, triggers the individual while sinking into them, while providing the control and focus needed to keep the new “nova” from dying or exploding into a mutated horror.



    But the problem is that the quantum powers of a person who would not normally trigger are always pushing the body—the more the nova uses his or her powers, the more of their body must be replaced by inorganic components, the nanites constructing a framework which allows the individual to survive and prosper. Eventually, even the individual’s brain is replaced, on a cell by cell basis, by an inorganic structure. Ultimately, the fate of the Nova is to become inhuman, though in a more controllable, stable manner than novas who boost their transcendence. Indeed,it is highly difficult for the Alchemical to suffer from traditional transcendence, though this limits their power drastically.



    However, one factor that Connel was never able to solve was the difficulty of creating a soul core. Soulcores were a form of quantum tech and required a Nova to be created. The fact that upon the death of an alchemical, its soulcore could be used in another alchemical resulted in a certain amount of worry. Furthermore, the time and effort needed ot make a new soulcore, even among those who had the information, resulted in the Alchemical’s never becoming truly numerous, especially since many novas who were “in the know” found the idea of these artificial novas rather repugnant, especially those among the Teragen.



    Process:



    Once implanted, the Soulcore will take a week to fully integrate with the human—he or she will be unable to use any Nova abilities during this period, though they can function as normal.

    A soulcore cannot bond with psiads, psions, talents*, or novas. Bonding with a latent nova makes any later eruption impossible.



    The new nova applies the Alchemical Template:
    • The character gains a Quantum Trait rating of 1 (see below).
    • The character becomes Tier 3 (see Trinity Continuum Core Rulebook, p. XX).
    • The character gains 1 dot with one Attribute of their favored Approach.
    • The character gains 75 experience points to purchase traits with XP costs identical to normal novas.



    Transhumanism:



    The Alchemicals do not suffer from traditional flux and transcendence—the support systems in the soul core act to moderate and prevent such actions. What they do suffer from is the growing divergence from humanity as more and more of their body is replaced by nanotech systems. This does not always make the alchemical inhuman—indeed, A transhuman 10 rating Alchemical may still be “Friendly” but it makes them ever more detached from humanity, so that even a friendly alchemical may be very disturbing indeed.



    Transhumanism can be gained in the following ways:
    • The alchemical gains a dot of transhumanism at quantum 3 and beyond.
    • For every ten points of accumulated strain the alchemical gains a dot of transhumanism.
    • The alchemical may purchase a nova trait for 1/2 experience at creation or during play at the cost of a point of transhumanism.



    Transformations: (note: this is just the short hand. I was thinking of doing the whole clarity thing, but Clarity can be simulated by choosing the right transformations. Also, I didn’t want to add yet another track to keep ah, track of).



    Transhuman transformations work the same way as transcendence with a few differences. First of all, the changes are technologically themed, with the alchemical shedding their human nature. Transhuman changes occur as a process of the nanite network extending itself throughout the alchemical’s body.



    In general these transformations are not directly harmful to the alchemical and do not have to be harmful to baselines—directly. But there should always be a theme of moving away from humanity. An alchemical is very unlikely to become sadistic (at least as a result of transhumanism) but a Transhuman 8 alchemical is quite likely to be able to cold bloodedly triage victims and dispose of those who cannot be saved without any regret.



    One certain transformation occurs at Transhumanism 5—the Alchemical is now mostly inorganic. They gain the adaptation edge and are, without taking an effort to disguise themselves or using domancy 4-5, clearly robotic and no longer human.



    Strain: The quantum flux that is dangerous to novas is handled by the internal soulcore systems—but they can find themselves overwhelmed. This is known as strain and more or less functions like Flux, save that when the strain reaches 10, the alchemical gains a point of transhumanism. (Need more here, but not really certain how to play it. For now, it’s mecha flux.).

    Powers:

    Alchemicals have two ways to gain powers, which are mechanically the same. The first are normal nova based powers, which can be purchased as usual.

    The second is physical modifications to the Nova’s body, which are purchased via the body modification edge. However, this edge can be taken further, up to 10 dots. Due to the difference in body issues, an alchemical who purchases more than five dots gains a point of transhumanism.

    Many alchemical powers have a technological theme, but this is not required.



    The Soul Core (* to *****)



    The soul core isn’t simply a device—it includes an advanced program that can assist the alchemical. From 1 to 4 dots the Soul Core acts as an agent, using the same rules for the Agents in the Trinity book, albeit one that is unique to the alchemical in question and evolves along with it.

    At five dots, the soul core is a fully sapient AI, which advises and works with the alchemical in the same way a normal human advisor would. In addition, the Alchemical can purchase mental edges and attributes for the AI. Alchemicals with Quantum Agent can take a tag (core pilot) which allows the soul core to control the agent independently of the alchemical.


    Past Lives:*-*****

    Some soul cores were used previously. Past lives represents the memories of previous wielders. 1-5 dots allow the alchemical to access a number of skills equal to the dots in past lives, which can be changed between scenes, representing the exploration of the memories of the previous wielders. In addition, these memories can often reveal secrets, usually at the GM’s option.

    Note that it would be highly uncommon for an Alchemical who gained a soul core during the Nova age to have this edge. Furthermore, this carries memories, not the personality of previous wielders.


    Not certain what else I’m going to put. The powers are largely flexible enough that they can work with just about any conception, but there are a few issues:



    1. How to simulate colossal or vehicle Alchemicals.



    2. A near mythical attempt by Aeon to duplicate this process for Psiads. Not certain how to work that in.



    Next up, some concepts.

          What happens when they reach Transhumanism 10? Do they become unplayable and if so, why? One thought I had is that Quantum requires a living being through which to fully express itself. Q-Tech still needs a Nova to use/power it. So, the Alchemical manifests Transformations but his Quantum + Transhumanism can never be greater than 10. If an Alchemical has Quantum 5 and Transhumanism 5 and gains another point of Transhumanism, he now has Quantum 4 and Transhumanism 6. Reaching Transhumanism 10 means he's fully machine, at which point the nanities break him down back into a Soulcore again.

          For that matter, I'd say leave them at Tier II and have it be that each point of Permanent Quantum is balanced by a point of Transhumanism. Alchemicals thus have a maximum Quantum of 5 and can never reach the full power of true Novas.

                  For myself, I wouldn't limit quantum-- and hitting Transhumanism 10 is in many respects about the same as Transcendence ten in the sense that you're now completely divorced from humanity. You may not be as likely to turn into an outright hostile monster, but if the analysis indicates that the long term benefits to the city are worth killing a lot of people--well, affection won't stop you.

                  Comparing it to Clarity, I'll probably mandate changes to various origin's roles, and society paths to express the new alien mindset. The risk to the alchemical isn't becoming a monster, it's losing their humanity and becoming something other. Maybe not a monster, but definately no longer able to empathize on a fundamental level.

                  the big issue of course, is that unlike Exalted Clarity, Transhumanism is as hard to bring down as Transcendence, making it a more or less one way path, and that provides a built in restriction for any alchemicals that want to maintain their humanity.
