      I've only just glanced at the PDF, but I already see one errata suggestion I'll be submitting, but hold little hope it'll be implemented, since I submitted the same issue back during the Kickstarter errata pass, and they don't seem to have changed a single word. I even started a thread on the topic back then: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...-era-ism-query

      tl;dr - Pages 248 - 252 cover the seven evils "Isms" of the Adventure! era, providing broad overviews of why they're wrong as well as details regarding specific atrocities committed by adherents...of six out of the seven. The final evil "Ism" of Socialism appears to be the most awesome thing of all time since it is all about taking power from the rich and giving it to the poor. They don't write a single negative thing about the practice beyond stating, "the nation of Mongolia followed the Soviet model, and after a violent uprising, established the Mongolian People’s Republic", making no mention that early 20th century socialist movements resulting in at least 6.5-20 million deaths (detailed in my linked thread). Four words out of over 250.

      tl;dr 2 - If they're not going to say a single bad thing about socialism, why is it even listed alongside anti-Semitism, classism, fascism, heterosexism (homophobia), racism, and sexism? I'd rather they cut the section entirely than whitewash forty years of detention camps, mass starvation, and acts of genocide.

              Now let us consider Socialism as portrayed by this PDF. I had to do a FIND word search to locate the Holodomor reference. It wasn't in the Socialist entry for "Isms", nor did it mention how Holodomor was specifically tied to socialism.

              The words socialism and socialist appear a grand total of 17 times in the book. Let us look at the context of each appearance:

              pg. 8 - Socialism

              [context: An index entry.]


              pg. 83 - The Bolsheviks, the largest and most influential socialist party in Russia, took matters into their own hands.

              [context: In response to food riots, the Russian government collapses. Various factions fight it out for a few years and the Bolsheviks win. The socialists don't seem bad.]


              pg. 83 - The winter of 1922 gave birth to the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics.

              [context: Reads like a wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad. The next few paragraphs mention bad stuff, but pins the blame directly on Lenin and NPC Marko Burakov rather than socialism.]


              pg. 83 - Caught between a fascist West and a communist East, Eastern Europe is a patchwork of competing ideologies with democracies, socialists, and dictators all vying for control.

              [context: Reads like another wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad.]


              pg. 84 - Post-war Austria consists of various political camps, socialist and conservative alike, both of whom control far-ranging power structures and their own parliamentary forces.

              [context: Reads like another wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad.]


              pg. 87 - Many of the leaders in Central America find themselves wrapped up in the coils of United Fruit, while fighting against a rising socialist tide. Drawing aid from their Euro-American corporate allies, they each try to apply their boots to the necks of their people,

              [context: So the people who are "fighting against a rising socialist tide" are the same ones who "try to apply their boots to the necks of their people"? Why exactly are the socialists the bad guys here, since they're opposing the brutal oppressors?]


              On to the section named "THE ISMS":

              pg. 248 - For a setting based in the 1930s, the most prevalent Isms are anti-Semitism, classism, fascism, heterosexism (homophobia), racism, sexism, and socialism.

              [context: Given the nastiness of the first six entries in this list, socialism must be just as bad, right?]


              pg. 250 - Benito Mussolini, a former soldier and socialist, and his fascists took advantage by siding not with workers but with businesses.

              [context: Mussolini used to be a socialist. He became an evil fascist and did evil fascist things. This does nothing to make socialism look bad.]


              pg. 252 - Socialism

              [context: This is just the header for the entry on why socialism is supposedly bad.]


              pg. 252 - The ill treatment of workers during the late nineteenth century led key intellectuals to lay down a set of principles labeled as socialism.

              [context: Reads like another wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad.]


              pg. 252 - Socialism recognizes class struggle — the conflict between a ruling class that controls the means of production and the working class that are the means of production.

              [context: Reads like another wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad.]


              pg. 252 - Socialism takes power away from the ruling class and distributes it evenly among the working class.

              [context: Reads like another wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad.]


              pg. 252 - Fueled by the belief in fairness and revolution, socialism spread among the working poor of Europe,

              [context: Reads like another wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad.]


              pg. 252 - With the overthrow of Tzar Nicholas II and the formation of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics in 1922, socialist parties emerged
              throughout Europe, the United States, Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, China, and Indonesia.

              [context: Reads like another wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad.]


              pg. 252 - The United States even elected two Socialist Party members to the U.S. House of Representatives.

              [context: Reads like another wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad.]


              pg. 252 - These socialist groups unified under the Comintern, also known as the Third International, which promised unequivocally to “struggle by all available means, including armed force, for the overthrow of the international bourgeoisie and the creation of an international Soviet republic as a transition stage to the complete abolition of the state.”

              [context: Reads like another wiki entry. The socialists still don't seem bad.]


              Aaand, we're done. We've read every direct mention of socialists and socialism in the book and have learned that Vladimir Lenin and some NPC created for this game were bad people. Beyond that, socialism seems historically boring but philosophically awesome. This is a strange conclusion to reach regarding an "Ism" movement which we're specifically warned is a sensitive topic to be handled with care, and that some players may be uncomfortable addressing the issue at their gaming table.

              It is especially ironic that the introductory section to the era's prevailing "Isms" issues goes on to state, "So, it can sometimes do a disservice to the people and cultures who suffered to simply gloss over the ugly parts of history because they make people uncomfortable." Yes, it is a disservice to people and cultures to gloss over historical ugliness. You know, the way the writers of this book are glossing over the historical ugliness of socialism? Socialism is specifically called out as one of the evil Isms of the era, so I'd expect it to be treated as such in the socialism entry for the Isms section. Why wasn't it?

                                              Saying that socialism is not bad per se is like saying fascism is not bad per se, as long as you ignore the nazis and other actual fascists. Theoretically correct, but it is hard to divorce the actions of followers of a political philosophy from the political theories they espouse, and doing so does a disservice to their victims.

                                              You also claim that these atrocities were the result of the leadership rather than their political beliefs, but that isn't entirely true either. The literal definition of socialism is:
                                              A political and economic theory of social organization which advocates that the means of production, distribution, and exchange should be owned or regulated by the community as a whole.
                                              The Union of Soviet Socialist Republics was politically a Federal one-party socialist republic under a totalitarian leader. Their policy of agricultural collectivization was literally an example of consolidating "the means of production, distribution, and exchange" under the control of a government acting on behalf of "the community as a whole." Their implementation of socialist farming killed tens of millions, as I've already noted. This agricultural policy was just one part of their system of five-year plans, which were ideologically driven (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideolo...e_Soviet_Union).

                                              The People's Republic of China is a Unitary Marxist-Leninist one-party socialist republic. Their policy of agricultural collectivization was another example of consolidating "the means of production, distribution, and exchange" under the control of a government acting on behalf of "the community as a whole." The Great Chinese Famine of 1959-1961 killed 15-55 million. It was part of the Great Leap Forward, which was a broader attempt to manage "the means of production, distribution, and exchange" in accordance with Marxist-Leninist political ideology (linked above).

                                              The Democratic People's Republic of Korea is a Unitary one-party socialist republic under a hereditary totalitarian dictatorship. They have a command economy in line with socialist political theory (linked above), and it has lead to crumbling infrastructure, chronic shortages, and a famine that killed millions.

                                              If you want to claim that socialism itself holds no blame for this death and misery, could you point to a nation where the government controls the farms and manufacturing and this didn't lead to widespread shortages of food and goods? It seems to me you're conflating such things as "socialized medicine" and "social security" with socialist political doctrine; they're loosely related, but not the same.​ Read the doctrine, then read the history of various ideologically driven socialist nations, and you'll see how the atrocities were supported by socialist political theory.
