                    Originally posted by lustral_star View Post

                    I think we can agree to disagree -- Golden Rule and all that jazz! *jazz hands* Most of my advice comes from the game that I ran/run, which sets Daredevils, Mesmerists, and Stalwarts on one level with Paramorphs, Psiads, and Novas on another level. I draw distinctions between Daredevils/Paramorphs (which is normal), but also between Mesmerists/Psiads and Stalwarts/Novas (which is not what the game developers intended, per se). Having said that, yes, they really did a bang-up job writing some material for Paramorphs on that wiki. Firanai could probably get a lot of mileage out of repurposing some of that material.
                    I also draw a distinction between stalwarts and novas, though it’s a distinction of degree rather than of kind: as I see it, Stalwarts are what Aberrant calls Stealth Novas: their Dynamic Knacks always take the form of attribute boosts of one sort or another; and in terms of sheer power, they’re much more subdued than the typical Mega-Attributes of Aberrant. All other differences stem from Adventure!’s more streamlined game mechanics and are not actual differences.

                    I generally don’t draw a distinction between mesmerists and psiads: any differences that seem to exist between them are strictly the result of Adventure!’s more streamlined game mechanics. I do draw a distinction between psiads and psions, though.

                    One thing I’m hoping to see from the upcoming Trinity Continuum is a consistency in the rules that renders the above difference of opinion moot, whether it be a clearly expressed vision of just what the relationship between Talents and Max Mercer is (if any) or an overall framework for exotic abilities that is consistently applied across all of the individual eras and eliminates the cognitive dissonance of using different rules to model what it conceptually minor variations of the same thing.

                    And as will probably come as no surprise, I draw a strong distinction between daredevils and paramorphs: to me, they’re not even the same kind of thing, with daredevils being baseline humans who demonstrate just how much baselines are capable of, whereas paramorphs (which arguably ought to be singular, as only one is known to exist) are time manipulators and travelers. What I like about the Aberrant 2.0 wiki’s paramorphs is that if you’re going to equate daredevils to paramorphs, that is how I would do it. I especially like how the Time Powers tend to evoke over-the-top action hero tropes such as “bullet time”.

                    I tend to think, though, that Time Powers ought to come with some sort of Paradox mechanic that risks messing up the paramorph if he doesn’t apply some restraint in using them. The simplest mechanic for this would be to say that each time a paramorph uses Temporal Manipulation, the Storyteller gains the right to use one instance of Dramatic Editing against them when he feels it’s appropriate to do so: low-level Temporal Powers trigger Minor Onscreen Effects, mid-level Temporal Powers trigger Major Onscreen Effects, and high-level Temporal Powers trigger Obvious Continuity Violations. A paramorph can downgrade a Paradox by one step by asking for an immediate Complication, and can negate a Paradox at any time by paying the Inspiration for an equivalent Dramatic Edit; conversely, the Storyteller can intensify a Paradox by one step by introducing a Plot Ramification. I would have this Paradox system replace the usual Inspiration cost for Temporal Manipulation.

                                    05-18-2014, 04:59 PM
                                    Psions don’t have much of anything that would translate to Prime or Spirit, unless you consider noetic energy to be analogous to Prime; they do, however, include three broadly-defined types, one of which could be modeled (very roughly) using Mind and Correspondence, one of which would fall primarily under Forces, and the third of which would be covered by Life.

                                    Novas do deal primarily in Forces, Life, and Matter; but they deal with Prime (in the form of Quantum) to the same extent that psions do. As well, there are Quantum Powers suitable for every Sphere that has a presence in the setting, including mental manipulation and time travel. Personally, I think a better way to characterize them in Mage terms is that they show a strong preference for Vulgar effects. With a few exceptions, they don’t do subtle.

                                    For Paramorphs, it depends on which theory you buy into. I always get them mixed up, so apologies in advance if I did so again: Andrew Bates’ theory is that there’s one paramorph (Max Mercer), whose abilities would fall under the purview of Time. Bruce Baugh’s theory is that daredevils are paramorphs, and that their powers are primarily what Mage would call Entropy, followed up by Time and a smattering of everything else; but much like Novas are better off described as specializing in Vulgar effects, Baugh-style paramorphs excel at Coincidental effects — almost exclusively so.

                                    All that said, it probably goes without saying that describing the difference between the three types of Morphs in Mage terms is going to be about as relevant as describing, say, Vampire Disciplines, Werewolf Gifts, Wraith Arcanoi, and Changeling Arts in Mage terms. That is, it’s better to consider them on their own terms rather than looking at them through the lens of Mage.

                                                        Hello there fellow adventures I wanted your advice on something. My players have asked me if they can include these backgrounds and I wanted to know your opinion about them, not only in terms of system but also if you think that they fit within the setting. Thanks for your help

                                                        -Patron.
                                                        Jacob was surrounded by the corrupt police force, their boss was approaching with a mocking expression in his face, ready to beat and rob him "My boss will want to talk to you but first let's have a little fun". Jacob pulled one card from his pocket and offered it to him, the dirty cop was confused by his act but he took it and inspected it. All color disappeared from his face and now it was Jacob the one with the mocking expression. "Well after this I think my boss would like a talk with you too"

                                                        A patron is a powerful individual that sponsors the player character. Wether a great artist, a stage magician, a brave explorer or a genius scientist, through his actions or his creations, the player has attracted the attention of someone with considerable means. This person can be a playboy millionaire, a crime boss, a religious figure or a complete mystery. His motives are equally varied, perhaps he wants to commission one painting, a machine for a very odd task, search for a lost civilization in the African jungle or maybe he just wants his company. The support that the player receives comes in the form of money, contacts and influence. The patron may get the player out of jail, get him invitations to exclusive parties or provide money for his projects. It's important to understand that although having the backing of someone powerful and wealthy has some juicy advantages it also has it's drawbacks. First of all the player is an investment and from time to time his patron will expect some revenue in the form of a new artistic piece, a new invention or a new discovery. Also rich people love to boast about their possessions and like a prized stallion the player can be expected to attend parties where his patron can show him to his friends. Last but not least reputation is as precious as money for these people and pride runs high in them. If the player embarrasses or abuses his patrons trust he can find himself punished in the best of cases or with no more patron in the worst, perhaps even making their previous patron into an enemy! They must understand what their patron wants and what is his reputation if they want to avoid trouble. The number of dots indicates just how powerful his patron is, one dot represents an individual with just a little bit more means that the player while with five dots it's one of the wealthiest and most connected persons in the country. In the enhanced version of this background the player character has someone truly legendary as his patron. It can be a criminal mastermind like the ubiquitous dragon, an exceptional wealthy individual like Maxwell, the mysterious old man from the IDA or the secret leader of branch 9. Having someone like this as a patron opens a box of almost unlimited resources to the player but it will also make him the target of very dangerous individuals and the expectations put on him by his patron can be soul crushing. In system terms the patron of the player character has the backgrounds influence, contacts and resources at the same level as the players background in patron. The patron can give a number of favors per week equal to the rating in the patron background. Finally the player has a lifestyle equal to the resources of his patron minus 1.

                                                        -Telluric fountain.
                                                        Gaspar was exhausted, in his state he didn't had the energy to even light a match. However he was quickly approaching his secret refuge, once he made sure for the twelve time that nobody was following him he entered the cave. He could already feel it as it got closer, the warm of the light was bathing him when he reached the opening. There he gorged himself in the divine energy, he was ready for whatever fate wanted to throw at him.

                                                        A telluric fountain is a place filled with special crystals that store and irradiate telluric energy which the player has access. It's assumed that the player character is the only one who knows where is it and how to access it (for now). Nevertheless this is not an easy background to have despite it's usefulness. Unless the player has resources, followers and other backgrounds to protect and hide the fountain he's going to have a very difficult time keeping it. With the right instruments telluric energy is easily detectable and there are a significant number of very powerful individuals and organizations looking for telluric crystals. If by any chance they learn of the players character stash they would stop at nothing to steal it from him, something that can make his life very miserable in many ways. In system terms a telluric fountain generates a number of inspiration points per day equal to it's rating in the background and has a capacity for storage equal to (5 x rating). In it's enhanced version the player character has hit the motherload! The fountain generates 10 points per day and can storage up to 50 points. BUT if anybody ever finds it..... well...let's just say that the player character is going to need a whole army of veteran soldiers equipped with the best gear and a fortress with walls made of steel just to fend off the first wave of enemies.

                                                        -Retainer.
                                                        Miss Genevieve was tied to a chair inside a bar with no way out and with several goons ready to take her away. "Nobody is coming for you sweetheart, better for you to get comfortable with our company". The goons hadn't noticed the click in Miss Genevieve ring as she pressed it. "I beg to differ sir. If I may I would like to present you to my bodyguard Mustafah", "oh really? and where is he?" As he finished his sentence one of the walls exploded, taking with it several goons, a giant figure with a fez and a white suit entered and started to beat the hell out of anybody who was still standing till everyone was on the floor unconscious or moaning in pain. "Madam I hope I'm not to late?" "No, mustafah you arrived just in time"

                                                        A retainer is a highly skilled person at the service of the Player. The relationship between the two, the way in which he pays for him/her and what her skill set is depends on the player. He can be trusted friend or just a professional relation. He can be a strong bodyguard or a sneaky thief. The player can remunerate them in various ways like money, contacts or with other more..esoteric means. The retainer although competent can never outsmart or steal the light from the player, except if it's relevant to the story or has an ability that the player has not. He is loyal to the player character and has his best interests in mind but he will tolerate no bad treatment or foolish risks that endanger both of their lives. Although respectful he has enough trust with the player to be honest and blunt with him when he needs to be. Despite all of this the retainer is still a human being and like that is susceptible to blackmail, intimidation and torture and no matter how skillful he is, sometimes he commits mistakes like everyone else. If the player character uses him to much or puts him in danger to often he could find himself with a cadaver in his consciousness, the retainer is there to help but his well being is also the players responsibility. The number of dots indicates just how useful or competent he is, a retainer with one dot is the typical child sidekick that helps more thanks to sheer luck than skill while with 5 dots the player has the equivalent of batman's butler Alfred. In system terms the player creates another character just like the one described in the creation process, minus the inspired part. For each dot in the background the player has 10 extra points of experience to spend on him. The 5 dot gives the right to turn the servant into an inspired in which case the creation process follows the original path and the experience bonuses are cut in half.

                                                        -Spies.
                                                        Mister Garrinton was calmly sitting in his nemesis office, while his Mobster lackey was boasting about his plan to ruin him. "Are you finish?" "What?!" "I'm asking if you have finished, this is growing tedious" "why you little piece of.." "Stop there, let's see what I have here, I think you may find it interesting" He pulled one file from his briefcase and opened it. "Hum...interesting that you would choose that side of the port for the contraband and oh! look at this? you have Judge Carver in your pocket. By the way I think the way you murdered that poor girl Clarice was way to messy...." The mobster had a terrified expression in his face " How did you?..." "Silence!" Garrinton raised from his chair and smashed his fists in the table, leaving a mark. "This is only the tip of what I have on you and believe me it can get much much worse. Now sit down and let's talk about how are you gonna help me get to your boss..."

                                                        Spies represent a highly specialized type of follower or contact, in certain ways they are far more useful but there are also more dangers. Spies are agents at the service of the player that can provide several services related to illegal or secret activities. This includes: access to classified information, planting false information, stealing, assassination, kidnapping, shadowing, smuggling and many more. Each agent reports directly to the player and no one else and has in his charge a small squad of men. The spies can never complete a mission in which the player is participating, they can only help by making it easier. An example would be that the spies of one of the players Kidnap the daughter of the ambassador an while he goes to pay the fee, the players have time to search through his documents in his hotel room. Another one would be that the player sends his assassins to assault the building distracting the goons while they enter using the back door. There are several risks with spies, they are professional but again they are also human. Due to the nature of their operations they are susceptible to be captured, bribed or even killed and if one operation goes wrong it can have some serious repercussions for the player. One of his agents could get captured and interrogated giving away the players implication, retaliation would sure follow. In the enhanced version of this background the player character has his own secret agency which makes him the terror of the underworld but it also escalates the danger to himself and his agents significantly. In system terms the player character can order one operation per dot and per week although the time can change depending on the difficulty of the task. The player has to roll his background dots but before he rolls Intelligence + command and adds its successes as extra dice to the main roll. The difficulty and the consequences for failure are set by the game master.

                                                            Both Patron and Retainer would be very appropriate for a pulp setting in general. Spies could be appropriate, depending on how you want to run your game -- however, it seems (to me, at least) to be something appropriate for a game in the 1940's, 50's, 60's, and 70's. Telluric Fountain... while I can't really imagine those don't exist in some form (those crystals do grow in places, after all), it seems like it might violate some of the foundations of the game (Inspiration is difficult for the characters to regain in large amounts). The game tends to focus on rewarding Inspiration for a character's actions, not being in a specific place or having access to certain materials. That one ultimately comes down to how you want to run your game, and how you want your game to play out. If you include that, be prepared for players to be throwing around fireballs and Dramatic Edits all day long. Inspiration and Telluric Energy will become a much more devalued resource.
