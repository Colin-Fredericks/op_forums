            This is an odd situation in that the official rules support glamourweaver's take on it (as pointed out, they have Inspiration, Facets, and access to Dramatic Editing that is otherwise restricted to daredevils), while I prefer the canonical stance that IanWatson just stated but find myself having to house-rule some things to make the mechanics reflect that.

            The simplest change is to make Dramatic Editing available to all player characters and major antagonists (but not extras), whether or not they're Inspired, through Momentum but not Inspiration; this would include stalwarts and mesmerists, and daredevils would remain the only characters who can spend Inspiration on traditional Dramatic Editing while stalwarts and mesmerists would still get their alternatives to it that they can spend their Inspiration on.

            Maybe pair this with a text box explaining that Momentum-based Dramatic Editing is a genre convention thing and not an in-setting phenomenon; though that ought to be obvious just from the fact that Momentum is a metagaming currency and doesn't actually exist in the setting.

            A further change would be to replace the Intuitive/Reflective/Destructive Facets that daredevils have with Biological/Energetic/Cognitive Facets for mesmerists and Forceful/Adaptive/Stoic Facets for stalwarts. Combined with the Momentum-fueled Dramatic Editing change above, this makes it so that “stalwart Inspiration” is effectively a prototype for “Quantum”, and “mesmerist Inspiration” is effectively a prototype for “Psi”; and that “Inspiration” is used to reflect the in-setting notion that the distinction between the three hasn't been figured out yet.

            But I can understand the revised Facets thing being too much of a change to be officially adopted; but I would like to propose that the Momentum-based Dramatic Editing thing be officially adopted.

                                    Originally posted by IanWatson View Post
                                    I think a little too much weight is being placed on mechanical abstraction. The map is not the territory. No one in the Trinity Continuum has Facets, but their character sheets do.

                                    For the sake of mechanical simplicity and genre conventions, the three Inspired types defined in Adventure! are represented using similar mechanics. That's not intended to indicate that they're all the same thing. Our primary goal is to make a fun game.
                                    I know that; which is part of why I'm not submitting the Facets thing as a proposed change. The Momentum thing, on the other hand, I would like to see officially adopted. My take on it is that Momentum is very clearly not an in-setting thing; Inspiration, on the other hand, at least sometimes represents telluric energies that clearly do exist in the setting. So Dramatic Editing by way of Momentum likewise is clearly a genre convention rather than an in-setting thing, and neatly explains anyone who has a dramatically significant role in the game being able to make the narrative changes that Dramatic Editing represents. Daredevils, meanwhile, are better at it because in the setting they're subconscious timeline manipulators and get to use telluric energies (Inspiration) to manipulate the timeline to their benefit.

                                    The overall effect is very similar: everyone gets access to the Dramatic Editing rules, with daredevils getting enhanced access. But the implications of the rules are very different: stalwarts, mesmerists, and others who Dramatically Edit aren't altering the timeline the way daredevils are; they're just taking advantage of the plot contrivances that are so common in the pulps.

                                    And with all due respect: of course the primary goal should be to make a fun game. That should always be the primary goal, no matter what game you're making. But that's a non sequitur. It's not like these ideas would make the game any less fun. But they would make the game more consistent, which for many of us enhances the experience.

                                                    So when it comes to Powers in a game (any game) I look at them as a major representation of what the group that uses said powers can do. I look at them as a mechanical abstraction of in-game abilities.

                                                    So when a game says "character x has flame powers' but in the power chapter I see no flame powers I get confused, there is a disconnect between fluff and mechanics. In such cases I usually go the path of least resistance - since mechanics are stuff harder to change if there are no flame powers in the mechanics then there are no flame powers.

                                                    This leads me to my original point - Mercer is a mega-Daredevil and thus what he can do should be Daredevil stuff turned to 11, not something radically different.                                                         Originally posted by Penelope View Post
                                                        This really confuses me, what’s in game and what’s not.
                                                        That's always been Adventure!'s cross to bear. The first edition's rules for Dramatic Editing specifically and explicitly said that they were a metagaming mechanic designed to replicate the lucky breaks and coincidences that were so frequently seen in the source material. The only text even remotely saying otherwise was an in-character piece written by Dr. Primorius where he mused that daredevils have some sort of subconscious ability to manipulate probability; and that could easily have been taken as highlighting his prejudice against “ordinary folk”: if you're somehow keeping up with the gods (his analogy for stalwarts), then there must be something godlike about you.

                                                        But some readers took it as an indication that daredevils actually are probability manipulators as Primorius surmised, despite the aforementioned rules explicitly saying otherwise. And when the Trinity Universe was revived as the Trinity Continuum, that view was made the official one.

                                                        Which is why I'm not arguing that a daredevil's Dramatic Editing should be treated as a metagaming mechanic; that ship has long since sailed. But from the core book onward, TC has been very clear that reality warping is strictly the purview of Talents; the psychics and superhumans can't do it. So when TC:A! comes along and grants them access to Dramatic Editing, that poses a bit of a problem. But it's an easily resolved problem; all you have to do is to say that stalwarts and mesmerists who use the Dramatic Editing rules aren't manipulating the timeline: for them, go back to the original game's concept that Dramatic Editing is a metagaming system and not something in the setting.

                                                        That's the reasoning I've been using.

                                                            So there's an assumption going on here that the mechanics are not supporting the fiction, and I think people are getting too caught up in the weeds about mesmerists and stalwarts being proto-versions of later iterations of novas and psions. Mesmerists and stalwarts are INSPIRED characters. They are not novas and psions. Not even "not yet." They are Inspired just like Talents are. The reason the TC Core says that only Talents get Dramatic Editing is because they are the only Inspired type to make it out of the Inspired Age.

                                                            Not only that, but it is essential that all Inspired types work similarly because they are all part of the same game and system. Writing up different Facet types for each Inspired type is a lot of extra work when it still doesn't make them look anything like an actual nova or psiad. We put a lot of work into giving nods to the "future" game lines in what mesmerists and stalwarts might hint at, but they are not those things.

                                                            Daredevils are capable of Dramatic Editing far easier than mesmerists and stalwarts because they are probability manipulators. This is why they have a discount to doing it. But all Inspired characters can manipulate Telluric Energy to get some dramatic effect. This is the nature of the pulp style of the game, and again, all characters need to feel like they are all part of the same game, so they all get to do it.

                                                            Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                                            The simplest change is to make Dramatic Editing available to all player characters and major antagonists (but not extras), whether or not they're Inspired, through Momentum but not Inspiration; this would include stalwarts and mesmerists, and daredevils would remain the only characters who can spend Inspiration on traditional Dramatic Editing while stalwarts and mesmerists would still get their alternatives to it that they can spend their Inspiration on.
                                                            So this might sound like a good fix, but it actually makes Dramatic Editing *easier* for mesmerists and stalwarts. Momentum is a rather easy resources to generate and is intended to fill quickly so players are interested in using it often. Inspiration is less easy to come by, and the design behind it as a resource is to make players weigh their options on what to do in any given scene because it won't come back until the scene is over. We have lots of ways to get it back, but if you spend 3 in one scene, you aren't going to get any of it back until the scene ends. Momentum on the other hand might generate in the middle of the scene due to failed rolls. Meaning that it is actually easier to Dramatic Edit using Momentum rather than Inspiration, as the resource procs more often.

                                                            Both of these resources are wholly out of character even if one describes a character's state. Momentum reflects the push and pull of winning and losing. The motivation someone gets when they get knocked down and stand back up to fight. Inspiration reflects the will someone exerts over the world around them. It's the lucky breaks, the force of mind, and the sheer audacity Inspired characters exhibit. Characters do not talk about Inspiration or Momentum. They do not know that either exists. They might talk about being mentally or physically drained, but they aren't going to say something like "I'm out of Inspiration."

                                                            Danielle Lauzon, Requiem Developer, Storypath Guru, freelance writer
