        I'd love to see Adventure! roll with it's self-awareness and have supplements for archvillains, sidekicks, twist endings and other literary tropes. Like Astromancer suggests, i could be entertained by a set on different genres of stories, romance, mystery, horror, sci-fi, etc...
        The original Adventure! was a kind of light hearted and youthful setting, the wonders and terrors of telluric strangeness were brank-spanking new, entirely emergent and fresh. I agree that in many ways WWII just doesn't fit the setting's mood - the meta-plot has this sad meltdown of the friendship between Mercer and Donighal about that time so really signify how much things have changed by then. It feels like leaving the silver age of comics at that point, i think. WWII is industrial war and inspiration may have been affected by that shift in the global zietgiest.

        As for vehicles and technology, i'd really prefer that they it all get rolled into the same system - so that converting an Adventure! era submarine or machine gun to Aberrant or even Trinity rules wouldn't require intense cross-referencing against other weapons from the destination game. But, i've heard this is planned anyway - so, yeah, a single book/title to cover all the wierd and wonderful shapes that vehicles (and machine consciousness) can take would be great, kind of like GURPS did.

        Tytalan also has good suggestions, the Invention system always deserved it's own book, also preferably not tied to any single setting. Daredevils received the least attention of any in the original print run, but from what i recall the first published book OP is doing is a kind of starter book with only Daredevils for now (but, it's been a while, i could be wrong about that).

        Inclusive Gaming mentions a series of releases on various world locations, and while that could be a minefield to write - it would suite the setting at least. Hopefully the readers would understand that it's meant to reflect the period and not the viewpoints of the authors, because to properly capture the feeling of old pulp novels there is might be a fair amount of casual racism/sexism. Max makes it pretty clear he doesn't tolerate that in his society for gentlemen, but it's a prickly subject either way.
        -shrug-
