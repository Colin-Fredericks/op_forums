    [TC: Adventure! - Kickstarter] Impressions

    04-22-2021, 12:18 AM
    Things I liked:
    • The statement that Telluric energy is formed by Quantum and Psi “glued” by Flux is quite elegant.
    • The explanation of the extension of Mercer's powers is very good. The narrative of the disaster and the time travel, is so cool. Possible mechanics of time travelling are promising.
    • Showing Donighal flirting with Nazism, I think it works very well with the dissociation of humanity, inherent in the all Novas.
    • The addition and contextualization of Sara Kaur, for me, was one of the greatest successes of this second edition.

    Things I didn't like:
    • The lack of the terms “Paramorph”, “Eximorph”... I love those terms. It gave a technical and scientific outlook to the Inspired, they fit perfectly with Primoris' scientific vision. (sci-fi lover detected).
    • The explanation of the behavior of telluric energy. Where it says that Earth Energy exists in a state of fluctuation, that when it is appeased it is possible to harness the energy. In this part in particular I prefer the old version, which was related to the zero point energy theory (Z-rays). Which is a form of stasis energy, which, when excited, generates A LOT of energy and strange effects such as the creation of virtual matter, etc. This energy permeates the entire universe is a great basis for telluric anergy. It may have been just the author's semantics ... but I still prefer the old semantics . (sci-fi lover detected).
    • I am confused if the Doyen barrier that was broken in N-Day surrounds only planet Earth or the entire Solar System. I may be mistaken, but all references in the other books portray the barrier only around planet Earth, so, which one is correct?
    • The fact that there were Inspired and Strange Places before the Hammesmith event. In my opinion, this dissociates the game world even more from our own, and also diminishes the importance of the Hammesmith event. I understand that this and the introduction of the Esoteric Order of the Æons in canon is probably an editorial decision to launch new game lines; I understand that… but I don't like it.
    • And finally ... Max's feelings for Michael .... You see, in my opinion, the best character in the whole Trinity Continuum is Michael Donighal / Michael Daemon / Doctor Primoris / Divis Mal. His evolution from a simple human to a god is simply awesome, and better yet, it’s totally credible. Also, the fact that this character is gay, and has romantic feelings unrequited by Max Mercer (his best friend), gives the character even more depth, because despite having all the power in the world, all his transcendence, he doesn't have the love of the one person he loved. (at least not in the way Michael wanted).

      That said, I think the new alternative, where Mercer also loves (romantically) Michael, lessens the impact of the two characters' story. The Divis Mal tragedy becomes a little less... "tragic" – for his romantic love is at least reciprocated (even if the sexual component isn’t). As for Mercer, it seems a little sadistic, boastful or stupid on his part, to not have a relationship with the person he loves because he doesn’t see himself as the ideal partner for Michael. During the Aberrant timeline, Scripture tells us that Divis Mal transcended his sexual desires (or at least doesn’t pursue them anymore), even if they still maintain their love, so why wouldn’t Max have a romantic relationship with Mal in similar terms? Finally, I always thought of Max Mercer as the quintessential Pulp hero, “loved by women, a lover to them, and respected by men”, whilst still maintaining a progressive spirit for his time (despite this concept being dated, maybe even for a Pulp game). This new take on the character, in my view, does not fit very well with the concept of Pulp hero... but I may be wrong. Still, I didn't like this reimagination so much.

    ​I would like to know your impressions of the game. I liked a lot of things, others not so much, I would like to know your opinion.

    There was another topic I intended to use, for this post, but it was closed.

It is obvious and clear that I abhor racism, homophobia, misogyny, and any form of segregation and social repression, and I am extremely happy that these problems are faced openly today.                               That said, as for fictional and entertainment works, I tend to prefer characters that are in some way, a representation of their own time. Although these characters are imperfect by today's standards.

                              Likewise, Mercer, as a character, in my opinion, should better portray the spirit, genre / era of the game, since he is the guiding thread of this specific scenario (just as Divis Mal is the guiding thread of Aberrant and Sara Kaur is Aeon's). Certainly, it is a posture that raises questions regarding the objectification of women and so on. But still, even though it is a character flaw, it should still be a possible feature of Mercer. Which, in my opinion, would enrich the character, placing him as a contemporary of the twentieth century.

                              See, I think Emperor Palpatine (Darth Sidious) is one of the best characters in Star Wars. But I am not in favor of authoritarian, homicidal, fascist attitudes and so on. However, these characteristics make him a very interesting character.
