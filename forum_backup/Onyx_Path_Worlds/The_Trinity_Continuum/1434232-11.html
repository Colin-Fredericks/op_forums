    Danielle, not questioning the design choices, but the way some terms are being used. I know you wrote Core and Adventure and all the others, just want to understand exactly what is what.

    Inspired is used in Core to describe anyone that has powers, it includes Novas, Psions and Psiads. So saying Stalwarts and Mesmerists are inspired is not really different from saying Novas and Psions are inspired.

    Ian (and I believe you too) said Stalwarts are low powered Novas and Mesmerists are low powered Psiads, that, as I understand, means if a Stalwart train a lot it’s possible to become a full blown Nova. The changes in the system are more about putting everybody in the same level and same rule set than saying they are different. For example, if a Superior somehow were build in the adventure era they would be built as Stalwarts, and not following the superiors rules, the same way we use Aeon Aberrant rules to build Aberrants and not Aberrant Nova rules (ok now it was confusing, but I hope you get).

    From your last post I understand the opposite, Stalwarts are proto-Novas, the same way a Psiad is not a Psion and will never be unless something happen. I don’t know if you are aware of a 1e unofficial source book called Awaiting Inspirations, that basically put Mesmerists and Stalwarts as variations of Daredevils instead of low powered Psiads and Novas, in TC terms, basically they would be Flux users that are really good in reaching the psi and quantum, but still handle only flux.

    My understanding reading last week manuscript is that Flux is so intense (the barrier between realities is so week) that even non-probability manipulators can handle some dramatic editing, and daredevils can DE so easily they don’t spend Inspiration (they spend momentum) and recover inspiration by boldness, things that are not possible in later eras (or before Hammersmith) because the lower level of Flux (stronger barrier between realities).

        Originally posted by impernious View Post
        So there's an assumption going on here that the mechanics are not supporting the fiction, and I think people are getting too caught up in the weeds about mesmerists and stalwarts being proto-versions of later iterations of novas and psions. Mesmerists and stalwarts are INSPIRED characters. They are not novas and psions. Not even &quot;not yet.&quot; They are Inspired just like Talents are. The reason the TC Core says that only Talents get Dramatic Editing is because they are the only Inspired type to make it out of the Inspired Age.
        That suggestion has been made by several of us, and has been shot down by Ian Watson: specifically, he has said that stalwarts and mesmerists are not offshoots of daredevils, and are “proto-novas” and “proto-psiads”. He has said that you're free to treat them as offshoots of daredevils in your game is that works for you, but that that's not the intent.

        I'd be fine if the official position were to change to what you're describing, by the way. But as it stands, the rules do support what you're describing while Ian's assertions don't.

        Originally posted by impernious View Post
        Not only that, but it is essential that all Inspired types work similarly because they are all part of the same game and system. Writing up different Facet types for each Inspired type is a lot of extra work when it still doesn't make them look anything like an actual nova or psiad. We put a lot of work into giving nods to the &quot;future&quot; game lines in what mesmerists and stalwarts might hint at, but they are not those things.
        Again, I never proposed the “alternate Facets” thing as an official change. It was a house-rule musing that I gave since realized was a mistake to even mention here, as people have taken it as an official suggestion even though I specifically said that it wasn't. That said, I'll keep your comments in mind when working out the details of this particular house rule.

        Originally posted by impernious View Post
        Daredevils are capable of Dramatic Editing far easier than mesmerists and stalwarts because they are probability manipulators. This is why they have a discount to doing it. But all Inspired characters can manipulate Telluric Energy to get some dramatic effect. This is the nature of the pulp style of the game, and again, all characters need to feel like they are all part of the same game, so they all get to do it.
        I agree. Not once have I suggested otherwise, and I have explicitly argued against that notion when it has come up.

        The only area where I disagree is that if Ian is right, then the telluric energies that the stalwarts and mesmerists wield are of the same sort that the later Novas and psiads wheeled, which explicitly can't be used for the sort of Dramatic Editing that daredevils do. Not “aren't as effective at it”; but “can't do it”. Though even if that's so, it doesn't mean that stalwarts and mesmerists shouldn't be able to do the daredevil style Dramatic Editing; it just means that a different rationale is needed. Which is where I'm coming from when I suggest reviving the first edition's notion that it's just a genre convention, for everyone that you want to engage in that sort of Dramatic Editing who isn't a daredevil.

        Originally posted by impernious View Post
        So this might sound like a good fix, but it actually makes Dramatic Editing *easier* for mesmerists and stalwarts. Momentum is a rather easy resources to generate and is intended to fill quickly so players are interested in using it often. Inspiration is less easy to come by, and the design behind it as a resource is to make players weigh their options on what to do in any given scene because it won't come back until the scene is over. We have lots of ways to get it back, but if you spend 3 in one scene, you aren't going to get any of it back until the scene ends. Momentum on the other hand might generate in the middle of the scene due to failed rolls. Meaning that it is actually easier to Dramatic Edit using Momentum rather than Inspiration, as the resource procs more often.
        That's one reason I suggested going with non-daredevils paying double the normal cost in Momentum instead of the “+1 Inspiration” that the manuscript requires. If you feel that that's not enough, triple or quadruple it.

        Originally posted by impernious View Post
        Both of these resources are wholly out of character even if one describes a character's state. Momentum reflects the push and pull of winning and losing. The motivation someone gets when they get knocked down and stand back up to fight. Inspiration reflects the will someone exerts over the world around them. It's the lucky breaks, the force of mind, and the sheer audacity Inspired characters exhibit. Characters do not talk about Inspiration or Momentum. They do not know that either exists. They might talk about being mentally or physically drained, but they aren't going to say something like &quot;I'm out of Inspiration.&quot;
        But they might talk about it in the same way that Novas talk about quantum energies or psions and psiads talk about noetic energies. They do, after all, have the notion that telleric energies exist. And there are some that can even sense telluric energies, even if they can't make the distinction between what stalwarts and mesmerists wield, and can't sense what daredevils wield.

        Basically, it would help if I wasn't getting mixed messages from the developers.

                                                        Thinking this over some more: I don't mean to sound argumentative, but perhaps the confusion is coming from a conflation that you have done. What I keep on running into is the way Inspiration conflates four things: the three kinds of telluric energies (which I don't have a problem with) and “it's just a genre convention; don't sweat the details!” (which I do).

                                                        First of all: are stalwarts using telluric energy to do the kinds of narrative adjustments that the core book describes, or is that “just a genre convention”? I personally prefer the latter, and have been basing my arguments on that. But if it's the former, then another can of worms has been opened: specifically, stalwarts are doing something with telluric energy that Novas can't do with Quantum energy. And this implies that the telluric energies that stalwarts wield at least partially includes something like whatever it is that daredevils wield. That's where suggestions such as stalwarts being like QKs come from. Again, not my preference. But at least it makes sense.

                                                        My preference is that it's just a genre convention for stalwarts. My fallback position is that it's a QK type of thing. But to say that they use telluric energy to alter the narrative and then imply that they don't? That's crossing wires.

                                                        The Inspiration vs. Momentum thing is of secondary importance to this. I like the Momentum angle because there's no doubt about what Momentum is; and staying that stalwarts use Momentum leaves no confusion about any possibility that they're using telluric energies to shape the narrative (as opposed to using telluric energies to exceed their limits, which I don't have a problem with, but which I don't consider to be shaping the narrative; that's another conflation which I reject). But the important part is leaving no doubt to the reader that stalwart and mesmerist narrative changes are genre convention, not the result of telluric energy. (Again, that's option one. Option two pretty much requires the rules to remain as they are.}
