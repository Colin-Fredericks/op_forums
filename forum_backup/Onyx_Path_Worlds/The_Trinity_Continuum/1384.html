                              Thanks! To be fair, InUn was a half--and-half effort. I was heavily involved in getting it going and herding various art and writing (which took up crazy amounts of time). It ended up coming out just after I'd moved on, and handed the site over to others i think. IIRC its layout was put together partly on the back of stuff I'd already started, but it was 10 years ago or so, so the details are fuzzy. I think the new site team brought it to fruition after we'd got it something like 80% there, ish?. It was a two team effort, one I didn't complete myself - far from it!

                              All in, its a decent book for a fan supplement

                              I'm quite proud of the community that grew over at EON, but considered myself to have been largely a 'hands-off' curator of it all at the time, for example I never really got into the play-by-post stuff, although that was very popular and I initially resisted having a forum (ironically). Not being much of an Aberrant or A! fan I tended to stick to the Trinity content too - that and I was just being the guy who paid the bills and smacked the server up-side the head when it needed it.

                              The EON regulars are who made the site what it was

                                  The India Underground was a fantastic supplement, when I ran across it years ago I could hardly believe it was free. The campaign I was trying to start at the time was not getting traction until I read the book and it gave me some new ideas which I used in a restart which did take hold. I used a lot of equipment and situations from it, and it got to be a running joke that about the only aircraft that would be issued to one of the PCs (who was a wild and somewhat careless pilot) was an old battered Gila because the thing was so tough as well as long ago paid for. Another plot thread was that it took a while but eventually the PCs figured out that one of their team was Chib masquerading as a low powered electrokinetic but by that time they were too close to her to want to turn her in.
