    My impression on the Sardonyx system (based on the teaser)

    About me: I was a big fan of Vampire the Masquerade, though I had the usual gripes with the rules: the chance of botching actually increasing with your skill, three different ways to alter difficulty, four rolls to resolve an attack not counting the constant rerolls for “0”s and 1s deducting from successes. Ah – and beauty (appearance) as an attribute wasn’t a smart move either. The rules were a mess, but could easily be houseruled. The Revised Storyteller system for the Trinity games almost completely repaired all problems I had with the rules. nWoD I found too abstract, though mirrors had good solutions for that. The new nWoD (God Machine Chronicles) lost me completely. Too meta gamey, too reliant on Willpower, too complicated conditions. And again: much too meta gamey. That’s my history with the Storyteller / Storytelling rules.

    Now to Sardonyx

    Part I: the die rolls

    Difficulty is the number of successes you need to overcome. I just love what you did here. For the first time, you really have only one difficulty mechanism (we’ll talk about Momentum later), and it is the right one. It is very similar to the Revised Storyteller rules, but much stricter. By keeping the number of dice and the target number fixed, the players have very little to consider. As a GM you can just say “roll shooting” or “roll dex + firearms”. This is great. Keep it that way!

    “0” count as two successes. Just like in the first Exalted rules version. I just love that! This prevents the time stealing and unnecessary constant rerolling and makes the game more streamlined and much faster, though rerolling sometimes raised the suspense and freak-rolls with small dice pools from time to time were fun. Still: the best possible solution. Two thumbs up (three if you are a mutant).

    Conclusion: I love what you did to the basic mechanisms. I am thrilled. Now I’ll wait for the rules for damage, armor, soaking, dodging, etc. to tell, if combat will live up to this streamlining.

    Part II: Effects

    I am not sure about this. On the one hand, I love being given options. On the other hand, this may slow down the game considerably. Ever played Dragon Age? You ponder on how to spend your stunt points constantly. Sometimes this is fun, sometimes this is a drag, sometimes it is a curse because you have trouble trying to call for a maneuver as you don’t know if you’ll have the stunt points necessary. This reminds me of that. My impression: offer not more than one or two options for the game and maybe one (and only one) special option for each power.

    Part III: Consolation and Momentum

    I hate meta gaming forced by the rules. I just can’t stand it. One of the reasons I am playing the good old Revised Storytelling games is, that the narrative part comes along without rules from the players and the GM just naturally. Being forced to come up with a special narrative, dictated by the rules, is something, that I don’t want to see in this game. If I want that kind of gameplay, I’ll play Dungeon World or Apocalypse World or one of those World-Games. But I don’t. Problem is: they are so successful, that now every game feels compelled to be more narrative driven. My wish: don’t make such a game!

    Consolation just is a meta gamey thing that can be left out with just the advice “don’t let a bad die role get in the way of the adventure”. There: now you don’t need consolation anymore. Just leave it out. Nobody really needs it.

    Momentum: a resource that is fueled by failures is not a bad idea. Momentum might be some genius move. But again: not too much meta game, please. As a player I want to immerse in the character, want to live through the story, not design it. I want failures to feel as failures and not as another meaningless way to get along. So I would not give Momentum for a “Game Master fiat”, as this would make a “Game Master fiat” a) all the more popular (you got Momentum for it, so why do you complain) and b) make it obvious.

    I feel having one pool for the whole party is a bad idea. I know my gaming group and I know what would happen. Everybody would try to pay into that pool and everybody would be reluctant to draw from it. I’d rather give everybody a personal pool.

    What I like about momentum: spending it isn’t meta gamey at all. It is all a mechanic that doesn’t change the story but lets you succeed more often or might fuel powers. This is cool.

    Part IV: summary

    I love that you built up a really stable and fast core mechanic, though I’ll have to wait for the complete combat rules to judge. I just love what I can see here. On the other hand I got the impression, that you want to make a more story driven game and much more meta gamy kind of playstyle. I don’t like that at all. As long as I can ignore these narrative parts easily I don’t care. But I am not sure about that.

    All of my evaluation is on purely personal basis. Other people might long for more meta gaming, more story driven games. Still – I’d like to see the new rules set be a better version of the older rules, not a complete change of the gaming paradigm from gam/sim to nar. But that is just me.

    This was a long post. You all earned a point of Momentum ;-)

                My primary reason to play role-playing games is to experience the duality of a mechanical game combined with imaginative creation. Pure immersion would be better simply free-forming everything.

                Consolations try to fix what is broken, that is, when you fail a roll and the story comes to a dead stop. No, you don't need a explicit mechanical fix for this, but by putting it in the rules directly, at the core, we redefine what failure means in the setting so it's readily obvious what that solution is rather than having it be some supah-secret thing that the GM is only supposed to figure out on their own after 10+ years of play.

                I would also say it's not very limited. Yes, there are only four categories. Those categories are very broad. Extra information and social benefit could be damn near anything.

                You are also not required to give Consolations. If you don't like them, or they don't make sense in the context of what's going on, then you don't give them out and the players just get Momentum instead.

                Instead of thinking of Consolations as their own category of things, think about them as what they are, which is an alternative to Momentum. If a player fails and you want to give them something so everything doesn't come to a screeching halt, it wouldn't be fair for them to gain that AND Momentum. Consolations make results more fair and give players an interesting choice.

                I'm really getting sick of trying to remember how to spell Consolations, though.

                    Hey. Designer here. I hear you about not wanting there to be narrative interference that's too strident. I want the world to have integrity. That means Momentum has specific limitations to make sure it satisfies its function, which is really to prevent long strings of blown rolls derailing play. I designed Momentum because many narrative systems that say they fix this actually make it worse, or makes failure completely meaningless. It's funny that you mention Consolation, because it's really the part of the system that serves as "training wheels" for people used to heavy narrative fail-forward systems, who may be afraid to take a chance with Momentum.

                    I'm not so interested in imposing larger structural narratives, but supporting up and down beats. "Fail forward" mechanics often hand out little crumbs of satisfaction, that make success feel less satisfying, and the general rhythm of a game feel flat. You do have a "crumb" option, but you also have the "no cake, no cake, no cake EAT THE DAMN CAKE" flow, which is more dramatic and also introduces the possibility that you might wait too long and go down in flames. I want you to get cool variations in play *and* stay connected to the world on its own terms, but I submit a two game losing streak is just as game-breaking as arbitrary success because of "story needs." Consolation and Momentum are ultimately about flow, not big structure.

                    Writer, Game Designer, Pro since 99.
