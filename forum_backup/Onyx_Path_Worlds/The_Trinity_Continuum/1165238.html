    Trinity - Aberrant - Homebrew Files

    12-05-2017, 01:40 AM
    Howdy. For me, the Storytelling System as presented in the original Trinity core rule book is the most elegant set of game mechanics I've ever used. I've used them to create several homebrew games, including the one I'm currently working through now.

    In any event, I thought I'd share some of that work to help out other Game Masters. I'll also be sharing some of my homebrew work for anyone interested.

    Combat Tracking and Visual Aid - When you're group is fighting an epic battle with a few dozen bad guys, it's tough to track all the initiative, damage, etc. So, I made it easier with a couple Excel spreadsheets. The first one is where the GM enters all the key data: https://1drv.ms/u/s!AgMRKARlzJL2hqsXAdmpG03tAkRs5Q

    You enter your player's names, their character's names, all your NPCs, and everyone's Bashing/Lethal Soak, total health levels and current health levels. You also enter initiative rolls. If there are ties in initiative, you simply enter a tie breaker value starting at 1 and increment up from there. This is helpful to the GM in terms of tracking what's happening from turn-to-turn, but the real magic happens when you also use the accompanying spreadsheet: https://1drv.ms/u/s!AgMRKARlzJL2hqsWhHp7utxRr-okMg

    To make this work, it's best if you have a laptop with an additional monitor plugged in. Extend your desktop to the monitor and drag Player Combat onto that monitor. You might need to adjust the sizing a bit to fit everything onto one screen, depending on your resolution, etc. But this spreadsheet pulls its data from GM Combat. It automatically ranks all the characters by their initiative scores, so everyone can see who goes first, etc. It also displays a bar graph next to each player/NPC to represent their current health. Now, players can see which of their buddies needs healing, or which bad guy is close to death. It shows everyone who's targeting whom with each attack. This has been a tremendous help to our group, especially in large-scale combat situations.

    Next up, I once ran a group through the original Aberrant storyline from the core rule book. I thought it would be cool to have the players first make up normal humans, have them erupt, and actually roleplay out them learning their way through their new powers. In order to help keep pace with what was happening with the other, more famous Novas in the rest of the world, I created this spreadsheet to synchronize current day dates with the original storyline: https://1drv.ms/u/s!AgMRKARlzJL2hqsUvQw6o5Fqx4Nexg

    You just plug in whatever date you want to be your zero date for the series of events, and it figures out the rest.

    Uhm, this next spreadsheet is handy for weather events. If you've ever seen the original supplements for the World of Greyhawk, they included some intense tables for calculating weather based a wide range of variables. Yeah, it was originally designed for AD&D, but weather happens in the future, too: https://1drv.ms/u/s!AgMRKARlzJL2hqsSLWh1PvLb55PJSA

    You fill in the variables for the month, a date range, latitudes, sea/land, and it will give you 2 weeks worth of weather, including possible extreme weather events. It also gives you dawn/dusk/sunrise/sunset times for those dates. I turned off autocalculate for this spreadsheet, so you have to hit F9 to manually force it to run the numbers each time. This way you don't accidentally change all your weather and lose what you were doing.

    I've got a couple other spreadsheets, but they're for unrelated games. One handles the Celestial Mechanics for Spelljammer. It'll basically track planetary motion for you. The other handles Astrogation for Star Wars d20. I won't post them here, as they'd be very off-topic, but if someone wants a copy of one, just PM me. I'm happy to give them out.

    Now on to homebrews:

    Section 9 - Are you fan of the "Ghost in the Shell" series? Me too. Remember how Section 9 received additional funding to hire new recruits at the end of Season 1? Well, your players are those new recruits. The game takes place in the Ghost in the Shell world, using the Storytelling System as a mechanic. You use the original Shadowrun cybertech for enhancement, and swap out Shadowrun magic for Trinity Psi powers. Yes, I know, Ghost in the Shell didn't have Psi powers, but why shouldn't this world?

    You treat the Psi powers just like you do Magic in Shadowrun, the more cybernetic enhancement, the more hindered the Psion becomes. Basically, the cybertech lowers the maximum number of dots a Psion can have in any given power, and makes it harder for a Vitakinetic to heal him. All the rest of the rules work the same. I never did a full write-up for the game. The only thing I did was to create a new character creation matrix with a distribution of Ability and Attribute points. If I can dig that up, I'll upload it here. It was my very first attempt at a homebrew, and it was a smashing success. I used a bunch of old Shadowrun modules to weave a story together. It was a series of investigations that turned out to have an overarching plot.

    The Directive - Yeah, I know. It's not really like that.

    The Directive was a supplement for Aberrant that would allow players to basically become spy characters who tracked and sometimes fought Novas. I'm just kind of using that as background material. Instead of being an international force, they're a sub-set of Homeland Defense. And instead of dealing with Novas, I'm doing something quite different.

    First, have you seen the movie or the TV show "Limitless"? No? Stop what you're doing and binge it all. Go. Right now.

    Caught up? Great. What if NZT-48 had been further developed by the US Government? What if they had gone through several iterations to discover NZT-60? And what if this drug didn't just make you super smart (say, one dot Mega-Intelligence super smart), but what if it could affect your physical and social attributes, as well? In this game, characters are limited to the first dot of any Mega-Attribute, as well as one dot Quantum powers. The government has developed the enzyme that allows them to take the drug without side-effects, but they must work for the company in order to continue to receive the enzyme, and doses are very tightly controlled.

    Basically, this game lets you have super-spy characters on par with the Impossible Mission Force. And I must say, our first several games playing this have really worked out that way. I might come back and write up the results of those adventures later on. It's been really cool. But here's the kicker. I'm so excited... I haven't told the guys, but this last mission introduced something that's about to blow their minds. My group doesn't watch Anime, so I'm about to unleash something on them they've never seen before and won't possibly expect. I'm going to mix in Ajin. They're going to be put up against something that doesn't have their powers, and it'll be something they have no idea how it works, or why. I just can't wait to see how it turns out.

    Anyway, please enjoy. The MS Word doc is too large for me to upload, so I'm linking it from my OneDrive account: https://1drv.ms/w/s!AgMRKARlzJL2hNZj0tzO5bHI6EONoQ

    Codex Alera - This is the current homebrew I'm working on. It's nowhere near complete, but I've mapped out the furycrafting for all the elements and their powers. I'm basing the structure on the Psi powers from Trinity. Six Psi powers, Six Elements. Each Element will have a set of three paths, each path will have powers listed from one dot to five dots, with most powers increasing range, duration, or number of targets as the specific power grows in dots.

    Additionally, I'm throwing in a chart based on the character's stature in society. Slaves will be limited to a single fury with all powers at half duration, damage, number of targets, etc. Freemen will have access to two fury elements, all at the base power levels. Citizens will have access to four elements at twice listed duration, etc. And royal blood lines will have access to all six furies at four times listed power levels. I'm not sure if I'll have players roll for their starting position in society or not.

    This will be set at the end of the series, so players can choose to play Marat, Canim, or Icemen, as well. No Vord. I think my group will enjoy it a lot. Again, I don't have a complete write up, but I'm also looking for some feedback on some of the powers/abilities. Here's the chart: https://1drv.ms/u/s!AgMRKARlzJL2hqsTJInsQT2gitEcCw

    Here's what I've got written up for Earthcrafting: https://1drv.ms/u/s!AgMRKARlzJL2hqsYNVRXx4GHVUCWrg

    I'm really interested in some criticism on this one. I don't take it personally, so let me know what you think, please. Thanks!
