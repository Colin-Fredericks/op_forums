                    It has been mentioned that stalwarts are the way they are because the Doyen barrier altered the way that the telluric energies interact with each other and with the Inspired. If you want stalwarts in later eras, you could say that when and if the nature of Talents is discovered (remember: in-setting, most people believe that Talents are “merely” extraordinarily fortunate, competent, and determined; not subconscious reality warpers), someone might engage in some investigation into how their “reality flux” powers interact with the quantum and subquantum energies that are much better known in the Æon era. And those investigations might come up with a variation of the process that Nihon uses to produce Superiors, which instead produces quantum-powered individuals capable of indirectly manipulating reality flux to a limited extent — that is, stalwarts. No Quantum Powers, Mega-Attributes, or Mega-Edges for them; not even Superior Powers; “just” Dynamic Gifts.

                    That is: barring another “Doyen barrier” scenario, naturally occurring stalwarts aren't going to happen, as any that do well end up Erupting as Novas; but it might be possible to artificially induce and maintain a stalwart, given the right knowledge of the “telluric energies” involved.

                    Or consider going with an alternate version of the Æon setting, where Nihon's great innovation wasn't “a process to create Superiors”, but a more localized version of the Doyen barrier: within the confines of their islands, you get mesmerists instead of psiads and stalwarts instead of Novas, and Talents get all of the benefits that daredevils had in the Adventure era. In exchange, there's no such thing as a Superior; reports to that effect are actually references to the stalwarts of the island nation.

                    Of course, if you do that, you'll also want to address what happens when a stalwart or mesmerist goes outside the shield; or what an Aberrant or psion goes inside. Maybe there is a Nakamura(?) Process; but rather than creating a Superior (which now becomes this variant setting's term for “Inspired in the style of Adventure”), it merely stabilizes them, preventing them from Erupting into Aberrants or psiads when they depart the island. And conversely, it's “too late” for Aberrants, psions, and psiads: the genie is out of the bottle, as it were. So entering the barrier doesn't turn them into stalwarts and mesmerists; though it might make it harder for them to use their powers.

                    Definitely not canon; but it might make for a fun alternative.

                    My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                Originally posted by FallenEco View Post
                                I thought the Doyen created the barrier as a response to the Hammersmith event. Can you point me to where it says they put it up?
                                It's in the setting secrets part of the TC:A! manuscript. There's also a post by one of the developers right near the end of the TC:A! Kickstarter thread where she spells it out explicitly; when I have more time, I'll dig up the link.

                                Originally posted by FallenEco View Post
                                And if I understand Guest correctly, Stalwarts are novas and mesmerists are psiads; they use Talent like rules in Adventure because it is easier to model them that way. What with being put on the same tier and not having to reprint 300+ pages of rules and all.
                                Close, but not quite. The presence of the Doyen barrier changed the way that the telluric energies interact with empowered humans. Some of this manifests as a limitation on how powerful stalwarts and mesmerists are compared to Novas and psiads; but it also manifests in mesmerist Gifts being all over the place compared even to psiad Aptitudes and Modes. But most significantly, it alters the relationship that those empowered by Quantum and Psi energies have with Dramatic Editing: without the effects of the Doyen barrier, Novas, psions, and psiads cannot engage in the reality-altering phenomenon called Dramatic Editing at all; with the barrier in place, they can; just not as effectively as Talents.

                                Basically, the way it was described in the TC:A! Kickstarter thread was that it's not just a different set of game systems employed to represent exactly the same thing; the subtle differences that exist because of the different rules exist within the setting as well, and Michael and Sara manifest as a (powerful) stalwart and mesmerist, respectively, because the Doyen put up the barrier almost immediately and altered their relationship with Quantum and Psi, an effect that lasted until Michael broke the barrier on N Day.

                                                        So I thought the Doyen barrier basically just stopped the shenanigans' on Earth from going out into the wider universe rather than also limiting the potential of humanity inside said barrier. Now I am fine with this effect causing a limitation in Nova and Psiad (the book says psion which is technically wrong but whatever) but I am not a fan of this being what causes the manifestation of Stalwarts and Mesmerists. Basically I much prefer the idea that Stalwarts and Mesmerists came about due to Hammersmith only effecting miniscule fractions of psi and quantum and thus they came about due to human action and not alien limitations. Even unintentional ones.

                                                        I do not want Stalwarts and Mesmerists to owe their existence due to Doyen plotting.

                                                        Also, for me, Stalwarts and Mesmerists are something that can and does exist throughout human existence - all thanks to the timey whimey nature of the Hammersmith Event. So instead of automatically assuming its Novas or PSIADS!!! in the past or future one can also say it might be the Inspired.

                                                        By the way the whole 'as the Hammersmith event fades away the Inspired loose access to their abilities' is not mentioned anywhere. A Stalwart is a Stalwart (unless they become a Nova) and a Mesmerist is a Mesmerist (unless they become a PSIAD) till they die - be it early through violence or after a normal human lifespan or even centuries later if they have slow aging or semi-immortality. The idea that suddenly one day a Stalwart wakes up and looses their powers just doesn't really work.

                                                        I will say this - I am sooooo glad that this edition is a Continuum. That way its quite easy to make changes and be like "oh yeah, its a different world than canon, though its similar enough in most ways" and then throw some shinnanagians and have someone or something from canon arrive into the campaign world, or vis versa.
