    Sympathy for the Doyen

    09-07-2015, 03:30 AM
    Those who have humoured me in the past may have gathered i have an intense distrust of the Doyen. The Trinity era 'secret boss' aliens sometimes referred to as 'Benefactors' in hushed tones.
    We can point to very little hard evidence of their activities with what little there is subject to extreme secrecy and routine cover-ups, often performed by the Psi-Orders. Among the few things we can attribute to them is their creation/supply/inception of the Prometheus Chambers and to a lesser degree the Biotech 'Templates' (Fauna, Flora, Bacterial, Plasmoid) that are used in all Trinity era biotech (probably borrowed from the Qin or another species, just as Chromatic tech was borrowed from humans).
    They appear most prominently in the Chromatic Invasion arc, but i'd argue that this appearance was atypical of their approach to humanity. None-the-less, this most unusual Doyen encounter is all we really got to characterize their entire species. Was this an acceptable first contact in terms of doing the mysterious Doyen justice? Is there, in fact, a much more sympathetic approach to our psychic, alien overlords?

    We know Taint is anathema to them, the Psi-Orders clearing out the earth's Taint was very likely an indirect Doyen action. The literature suggests that Taint is thought to more or less be a permanent problem going forward, but much is made of the Taint being cleared up faster than was expected/forecast. Beyond this, the Noetics TFR postulates that Taint has a constricting and self-propagating presence in the Sub-Quantum medium, making it safe to assume it's an even more frightening prospect to aliens (presumably) existing in an entirely psionic (read: sub-quantum) state.
    I imagine that a type of rapid-spreading, malignant force/energy corrupting the medium of my universe might be a decent handhold for some sympathetic xenology in the setting. A fellow poster made this suggestion during digressions concerning Babylon 5:

    Originally posted by Drazosh View Post
    A Kosh-like ambassador would probably do a lot for making the Doyen factions feel less monolithic.
    Now, Kosh and the Vorlons are probably a little too close for comfort - plus, they're getting dated by now (all due respect to JMS). But, it has left me wondering (for a while now) about a more sympathetic portrayal of the Doyen and the cosmic role of Taint in the greater sci-fi universe. How would it be done? Are the Doyen the only species who took notice, or just the first to arrive? What role do the Doyen play in this greater scale? Have they done this kind of thing before, or is this a first for them too? Is it even worth trying to figure out?
    Ideally, i'd have a solid idea of what the Doyen are before fitting a role about them, but because of the secrecy, we don't really know anything about them as a life-form. It's easier to ascribe motivations and goals to minerals, since at least minerals have form and substance.
    I repeat for clarity: The Doyen may have nothing to do with life or matter as we know it. Try sympathizing with that...
    The idea that the Doyen once had physical forms and ascended somehow was done by the Vorlons (iirc), and honestly feels like a cop out in 2015+. But this almost inevitably leads to a kind of sub-quantum 'umbra' however abstracted - something that has been hinted at in the teleportation flavor text.

    So, in lieu of getting out AE: Invasion and DR: Ascent into Light for page referenced quotes, i thought i'd throw it out for discussion if anyone is interested.

      I've working my way through those modules and I've got this to say as someone who knew about the Doyen ahead of time from reading fan sites and such over the years. They are an intentional mystery, so ultimately I'd say their use should be up to the GM. Myself I firmly believe in a spectrum rather then simply binary descriptions. I admit when I read about how the Doyen looked I immediately thought about the Sparks from Transformers.

      Sympathy for me isn't hard and I think its something Trinity encourages, the narrative of the Trinity'verse has never changed but my perspective has over the years, back when I only had the quickstart booklet I could only imagine (I was also a teenager at the time) the hostile and alien menace of the Aberrants and the nobility of the Psions fighting against this Grand Menace!(tm)

      Naturally now I've matured considerably and enjoy the moral complexity of Aeon's story. You have people with powers and they all went different ways, the Doyen are reacting to those powers in as possibly as sane a fashion as they could conceive. One additional thing I would like to know is what is the effect of Taint's beyond biology? From what I've seen its only an issue to organics, what exactly does it DO to the information layer of the universe? My own guesswork has been that it "corrupts" it somehow causing information to bleed over between psion particles.

      Whatever the case one can see the Doyen's perspective, if the Aberrants had not fled to the stars the problem would have been contained to Earth but now the whole galaxy, possibly even the whole universe is at risk from the Strange Matter the Aberrants create (which is another idea I had for what Taint is).

      Doyen are not quick thinkers by nature so having them in a situation where they talk to people feels like a bad idea. Also it makes them very alien, these creatures who can't cope well with sudden changes to their environment, it would suggest a very different sort of evolutionary history.

                                  Since part of this started with one of my off-hand comments, I'll have a go.

                                  Truthfully, despite my distaste for any fictional species that seem to present a unified (or two) front, it's easy to have some sympathy for the Doyen. Fact is, they were most likely attacked, in their own homes (or psychological equivalent thereof), and harmed by something that might otherwise be assumed to be incapable of it. Think of how most humans respond to finding a wasp nest anywhere near their homes, then consider the fact that a significant number of the Doyen opted to find solutions that did NOT involve spraying us with poison and stomping us flat (although the Chromatics may have qualified as that). They're manipulative, certainly, but they're helping humanity deal with a threat we share with them, regardless of their intentions. Rather Vorlon, certainly, but it's a very natural response to fear from a thoughtful, if somewhat arrogant, species. In fact, it's likely a bit more kind than it could be.

                                  Humanity will eventually need to stand a little taller on its own, though, particularly given the themes of adventure and evolution that power the Continuum. In the end, the Doyen will need to get out of the way, either at our side or otherwise. Personally, I'd be a lot more inclined to believe the former if they're presented in a less unidirectional way.

                                          Well . . . they didn't kill us in the wake of the Aberrant War . . . and they probably could have. Earth was in ruins - the OpNet was gone, major countries destroyed, shell-shocked survivors trying to rebuild . . . If the Doyen had simply lobbed a giant asteroid at us (a la the dinosaurs), they probably could've killed everything . . .well, except maybe the cockroaches

                                          Heck, such a tactic probably would've worked against the nova colony at Eden. Even with nova abilities, a colony doesn't just spring into existence. Fields have to be planted, buildings raised, industry established . . . there's a lot to get done, before you even think of defenses. And while they're powerful, "asteroid blocking" isn't exactly a normal nova power . . .

                                          One good smack with an asteroid, and the handful of remaining novas off-world instantly become an evolutionary dead end They could be hunted down, or simply left to die of old age.

                                          Or the Doyen could've conquered Earth . . . but didn't do that, either. Instead, the Doyen turned up, and helped rebuild Earth. Heck, they went above and beyond the call of duty. They didn't have to share their biotech, or the secrets of the Prometheus effect - but they did.

                                          Originally posted by Nihilist View Post
                                          . . . We know Taint is anathema to them, the Psi-Orders clearing out the earth's Taint was very likely an indirect Doyen action. The literature suggests that Taint is thought to more or less be a permanent problem going forward, but much is made of the Taint being cleared up faster than was expected/forecast. Beyond this, the Noetics TFR postulates that Taint has a constricting and self-propagating presence in the Sub-Quantum medium, making it safe to assume it's an even more frightening prospect to aliens (presumably) existing in an entirely psionic (read: sub-quantum) state. . .
                                          Its worth pointing out that "Taint" means very different things to a nova/aberrant and a noetic (whether psion or Doyen). From a noetic perspective, any use of nova abilities is a form of "Taint", because it disrupts the subquantum universe. Black tech, nova powers, the M-R node . . . none of these "should" exist (in the eyes of a Psi user), and only do because some "aberrant freak" has "twisted" the fabric of the universe into an "alien" format . . . and are thus "Tainted"

                                          To a nova (or regular human, during the Aberrant-era), "Taint" only refers to involuntary expressions of nova abilities. Anima banners, involuntary mutations, odd side effects . . . those are manifestations of Taint. Regular nova abilities are perfectly natural, a sign of human evolution.

                                          And to a mundane human, there's no practical difference between nova and psion powers. Quantum or Psi, biotech or black tech, nova or psion . . . they're all the same, to a baseline human. Neither are inherently harmful to them, and a latent can be triggered to either.

                                              I have only ever run the Doyen trilogy once in a Trinity game, rarely came up as an interesting story for my players. I always approached the Doyen as a cowardly diplomat with poison and gifts. They fear humanity as a whole, and after the Aberrant Taint issue is resolved I expect them to pull the psi-chambers and to chemically sterilize the entire human population (not spray them all, rather pull the Aberrant trick of putting the components in food and detergent and when the two mix they person slowly becomes sterile).
                                              The Doyen find ending a life traumatic, but will kill a human that is threatening it without hesitation, at the same turn it will give lavish gifts to a human to endear them and gain their loyalty. Especially since they will most likely be the last (or second to last) generation of their species​.

                                              Not cannon, but how I ran them, indifferent to human suffering (curious), but never cruel to the aliens, they approach humanity like mosquitos, they selected a less aggressive branch and modified them to out breed and to attack the other group of bugs. Once the "Maleria carriers" (Tainted) are dead, they will sterilize the modified group and make the universe a healthier place for their kind. Nothing personal, just managing the ecosystem for their advancement.

                                              I found sympathy for them by viewing the Doyen diplomats as 19th century scientists in the middle of an African jungle trying to save their species from malaria, noble scientists, sacrificing much to make the "world" a better place. and since we could not keep our "illness" on earth, we have to be managed. When they are returned to their home world of aristocratic Britain, their modified specimen are observed with curiosity and condescension, but manners must be observed at all times.
