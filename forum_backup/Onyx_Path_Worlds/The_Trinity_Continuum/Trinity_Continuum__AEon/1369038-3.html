            Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
            No it’s a diegetic joke. Alien is a movie in-universe as well as in reality. Orgotek gave the ship a geeky name.
            Close. The entity that owns the ship, not the one that manufactures it, gets to name it. In this case, that's the United Nations - the Legions are an element of UN Military Command. Hence the "UNS" prefix.

            (In most real-world navies today, classes of military vessels usually share names of a theme. For example, the Royal Navy's upcoming Dreadnought-class ballistic missile subs all will bear historically-significant names of previous Royal Navy vessels, and the United States has traditionally named multiple classes of battleships - and, later, ballistic missile subs - after states. Thus it goes in Aeon, too, where I can influence or rationalize the writing.)

            Unfortunately for the UN's collective sense of gravitas, UNMC delegated the naming of Legions vessels to the Legions, rather than routing it through committee. Seventh Legion has a large percentage of combat-rated nerds among its ranks - of course they're well-versed in military science fiction (see also the recurrence of Starship Troopers on the reading list of US Marine Corps commandants, or the popularity of David Drake's Hammer's Slammers series among combat veterans of a certain age). More broadly, Seventh Legion troops are self-aware - they know they're frickin' space marines, and they cannot help but play to their own self-created expectations.

            So when the Vessel Nomenclature Subcommittee of the UNMC Naval Construction Board said, "hey, we're busy with the politics around naming the Leviathans, can you guys handle this for us?" the Legions shrugged and said, "sure, we'll have the Legion that operates each vessel do the naming for it." First Legion had their PAOs handle it because they're so image-conscious that a pocket mirror is part of every Guardsman's basic issue kit. Second Legion allotted naming rights to individual Knights through random drawing (don't ask about the names the General had to veto). And so forth.

            When Seventh Legion got the assignment, the headquarters staff looked around, hunched over the table, lowered their voices, and said, "let's see if we can get away with this." They expected that someone in the Jar would notice, and they had a more serious second list of names prepared. But either no one caught the joke in time, or everyone else in their chain of command thought it was hilarious too (self-awareness of the Legions' fictional antecedents is not confined to the Seventh). Then the same thing happened at the UNMC staff level.

            Note the parenthetical at the beginning. This was for the names of whole vessel classes in Firebird service, not just one ship. So every Scarab flying Seventh Legion colors is named for a fictional female monster hunter. As the author, I considered anything up to the Nova Age's 2018 divergence from real-world history to be fair game. Many pop culture sources familiar to us, the authors and fans, also exist in-universe, and have resurfaced as part of the 2120s' nostalgia for pre-Nova Age things.

            So the Seventh Legion's Scarab inventory includes UNS Ellen Ripley and UNS Nancy Wheeler, both in Distant Worlds. If I'm allowed to continue these shenanigans, or if future authors see this thread, the Firebirds also operate UNS Sarah Connor, UNS Dana Scully, UNS Buffy Summers, UNS Jody Mills, UNS Esther Falkner, and UNS Machiko Noguchi.

            UNS Joyce Byers is on the reserve list in case Wheeler is destroyed - one of Seventh Legion HQ's rules is no more than one vessel in commission can take its name from any given source. There was some vigorous debate as to whether Noguchi broke that rule but it came down to the officer who suggested it being senior to all of the dissenters. RHIP.

            As you may expect, Seventh Legion is pretty relaxed when it comes to approving vessel nose art, too...

            - C.

                                I'm torn as to where Prince is in the queue relative to UNS Carol Danvers. Regardless - both would probably be Scarabs or whatever class of corvette or frigate follows the Scarabs in a decade or two. This naming scheme only holds for Scarabs, and UNMC won't let Seventh Legion get away with it again.

                                Tangent and one writer's opinion only, not an Onyx Path ruling on canon:

                                I don't see the Legions as having the staffing or the operational need in the 2120s to operate something that would be classed as a "battleship." Such a vessel would have two primary roles: space naval supremacy and naval/orbital gunfire in support of ground force operations. The Legions are not currently in the fleet engagement business (though that may change depending on humanity's approach to the Coalition) - and, quite frankly, the major national naval powers of the UN would be strongly opposed to the Legions being any more capable of challenging their own fleets.

                                On the fire support side, of the six combatant Legions, only Fourth and Seventh commonly undertake missions that need it, and they're probably getting the job done with hybrid strikefighters, "rods from God," guided weapons coming out of Scarabs' missile tubes, and, when needed, joint operations with national navies. That doctrine may change based on lessons learned from the Khantze Lu Gei relief operation, but the end result is more likely to be... well, a "space control ship" or pocket Battlestar. Limited anti-ship capability, heavy anti-fighter/anti-Aberrant defenses, good orbital fire support capability, and a flight deck and marine berthing area that can support any mix of fighters and utility craft depending on the mission.

                                For the moment, though, the Legions don't have the budget to operate anything that size - whether battleship, SCS, or heavy carrier. Also, any capital ship is going to be a major drain on personnel; requires escorts and support vessels to protect it from the ridiculously concentrated power of Aberrants; and, most damningly, can only be in one place at a time (teleporters notwithstanding). Today's psi order needs the flexibility of the multiple smaller vessels that they can have for the price of one cap ship.

                                (Somewhere in the back of my head, when I am dealing with neither another freelance deadline nor my day job's COVID-19 workload, is a rough table of organization for the Legions as a whole. We have established numbers of total Legions personnel and the percentage of those who are psions, but that doesn't give the average reader or author a feel for how big and how capable the whole psi order is. Bear in mind that with slightly under 100k total troops, Psi Order Legions is about as large as the Angolan, Sudanese, Jordanian, or Nepali militaries today [source]. Psions are a major force multiplier but they die like anyone else, especially when their spaceships blow up around them. Biotech and vessel automation are likewise force multipliers, but they aren't unique to the Legions and they are expensive, particularly if you want to harden the automation against hostile psi and Aberrant capabilities.)

                                - C.
