    The Nippon Arcology

    01-05-2020, 05:22 PM
    I've been thinking about this place because I'm planning to set my initial adventures there, basing them off of Kuro and then bringing the PCs into the wider world of Aeon.

    Initially in my head I kind of imagined the arcology as "anime Tokyo except bigger." I still think that's generally true, but there are aspects that idea leaves out. For one thing, figure the arcology is huge. The whole essentially encircles the home islands of Japan. But that's not all. The average depth of the Sea of Japan is around 1700 metes (5800 Feet). So on average, it's a mile deep. It's maximum depth is a little over two miles deep. And I figure that below the surface, the arcology goes all the way down and into the ocean floor.

    The Nihonjin saw the Aberrant War coming. The did the calculations. And they probably came to the conclusion that the war would be an extinction level event. So, the arcology wasn't meant to provide temporary shelter for Nippon's population. It was meant to be something like the Svalbard Global Seed Vault, a way to eventually regrow the ecology of Earth after the devastation of a Nova war that extinguished most life everywhere else. Figure that's one reason that Nippon accepted those millions of refugees from around the world. Sure it was compassionate, but it was also in service of genetic diversity. It wouldn't work too well to have only one genetic type (Nihonjins). That was a potential road to extinction or infertility - especially a couple of centuries down the line with cousins breeding with cousins.

    And that gets us back to the arcology is huge. Figure this thing was meant to a terrestrial "generation ship" until the world outside recovered from the Quantum destruction, which meant take several centuries. So it's probably that the arcology was meant to support up to 500 million to a billion people over time. Given Mega-Intelligent Novas and Nippon overall technological expertise it was achievable.

    Assuming that current projections are correct and by 2045, Nippon's population was around 100 million (down from the current population of around 125 million. Figure another 5-10 million refugees. And they're in something designed to allow 5 to 10 times that population to survive for centuries. Except that's not what happened. The Aberrant never got to the "extermination" level. The Aberrants were driven into exile. And in Nippon, life went on for the next 60 years. Nippon's population continued to age and fall. I figure that with the current 24% decrease, Japan's population would now be about 88.7 million. If even 5% of those are "Forgotten" that's around 4.5 million people - that are at loose ends in a place originally designed to support up to 100 times that number if not more.

    Nippon has essentially turned into a larger scale Detroit at this point, with the normal populace mostly concentrated towards the tops and the "Forgotten" living in the vast areas beneath.

Life is unpredictable and dangerous, so Enclosed System might save her life if things go badly wrong. Neural Regulator makes sense even more. She has to interact with some Otaku spaz who smells like Kimchi and his own spunk. Being able to better conceal her disgust is important. But she also gets Kill Switch and, presumably, the knowledge that if she sings about how cool it would be to have Psi powers, some dude can push a button and melt her brain. And that's how the Nippon government, the moves and shakes who got the arcology built, tend to think. They take safeguards to an absolutely ludicrous degree.

                      But we're not done. Think about the Novas again. Nippon is about conformity and fitting in to an absurd degree. So, if you're a Nova from Nippon the idea of Flus/Transcendence turning you into a Hentai monster is not something you want. So, figure some of them go to the Teragen and learn Chrysalis, which they pass on. As time goes on, the Novas of Nippon still look normal, but their Transcendence along with their Quantum, continues to rise. They look fine physically, mentally, though, is another story. So as the arcology gets bigger, the people most responsible for designing and building it, the Nippon Novas, are getting crazier as time goes on.

                          Japan is all about aesthetics and tradition. I work for a Japanese company and my wife lived there for six years. I try to keep up on their news and trends. In the last decade or two there has been a movement there to bury the countries freeways underground (or build over them) and then build parks over the foot print. To me that gives a kind of insight into what a Japanese arcology might look like.

                          So, I kind of disagree with the books description: I think from the outside, a Nippon arcology would be mostly invisible, its infrastructure hidden behind well manicured facade. The arcology would look like a giant island that is this urban planning wet dream. Lots of open areas, beautiful parks, walking spaces. Lots of sunlight and gorgeous high-tech architecture, strategically spaced skyscrapers and lots of beautiful mountain vistas. Everything is pristine. With the exception of seaports and airports, the rest of the arcology would be mostly hidden underneath this. All highways and mass transportation flows underground. Slums and suburbs, underground. Manufacturing and warehouses, underground. The only hint of the depth of the arcology would be looking down into one of the thousand sky access ports, giant holes which allows sunlight and fresh air into the center of underground residential or business blocks. There is usually a park at the bottom of these. These holes in the ground would be mostly concealed or camouflaged to anyone walking on the surface of the arcology.

                          For manufacturing purposes there would be a vast ventilation system that probably vents out into the open sea so not to disturb the skyline of the island. Those vents would probably be out past a massive sea wall that surrounds the entire arcology. That sea wall not only protects the islands and its massive wind farms from tsunami, but also controls access to Japan's sea lanes.

                          Although we are talking about a cyberpunk like arcology, this is not a dystopia. Even in the worst parts of the arcology, with the exception of foreign ghettos, it would be neat, orderly and tidy. Most people would take pride in the appearance of their space, no matter how utilitarian and spartan that space was. They would also have school children and college students do weekly service projects to clean their neighborhoods.

                          So above ground the arcology looks like Star Trek's San Francisco while below ground it looks like Deus Ex's Hengsha but tidier.
