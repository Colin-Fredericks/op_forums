                            Just came across this little hack and I quite like it for a setting hack I'm putting together.

                            Personally, I'm in the camp (for my setting at least) that the "Talents" are like Jason Bourne, John Mcclane, Nick Fury, Bruce Wayne and so on in that "Dramatic Editing" is an out of game mechanic to explain their in-game awesomeness. There is no "Flux", they're not reaching across timelines/probabilities - they're just that tough, smart, prepared, & yes lucky.

                            I think that "Dramatic Editing" is a great game mechanic in general and the use of Momentum actually makes ludonarrative sense - for every failure/drawback the characters encounter, they somehow make up for it in being well prepared, tough, or just generally "bad ass". Their failures are just story elements that drive their inevitable future successes.

                            The way I'd explain (out of game) that Psiads and low-level Novas don't get the same benefits is that they've already been lucky enough to get powers and so they need more failures to give them a non-powered solution to future problems. They've come to rely on their innate strengths so much they don't need to prepare as much (which is both a story & real world trope). Think about how often Superman needs to pull out a conveniently pre-planned contingency compared to Batman. When Supes comes across an immovable obstacle, he just starts pounding on it harder. When Batman comes across the same obstacle, he's already conveniently researched the issue and created a gadget / plan to get around it. Supes might still need to pull out a Deus Ex Machina stye workaround, but he's got to fail more often with his powers before he reaches that point. Batman pretty much relies on his ability to just rewrite the plot.

                                Originally posted by Eternl Knight View Post
                                they're just that tough, smart, prepared, & yes lucky.
                                FWIW, part of the setting conceit of the Trinity Continuum is that this -- the whole probability manipulation thing -- is how luck actually works. Talents are just better at it than most, but it's not something they're consciously aware of. Like, if you can see a slightly wider range of colour than your friends, you may never notice, and it's not something you try to do, it just happens. There are people who live well into adulthood before finding out they're colourblind. After all, without the ability to compare, most of us are going to assume that our own experiences are universal.

                                This is why I've long maintained that it's difficult to pin down who is and isn't a Talent: they're arguably not a Special Character Type in the way that psions and novas are. They're doing what every other human does subconsciously, they're just better at doing it. So the words "flux" and "probability" never have to come up in conversation, either in-character or at your table, since in the real world no one really tries to explain luck other than just calling it luck. So too in the Trinity Continuum, Talents may never question their capabilities. It's only organizations like the Æon Society that are trying to research and explain them.
