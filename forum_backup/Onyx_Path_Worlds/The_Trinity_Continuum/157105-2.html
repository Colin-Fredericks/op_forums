                                For me, going space opera/planetary romance would be a better fit for Adventure! and/or for a time period after Aeon, rather than Aeon itself. (Which is not to argue against having genre-appropriate dramatic editing and super-science in Aeon; I've got no problem with that.)

                                Basically, Aeon has a particular Earth-centred focus, and the sci-fi subgenres it draws on are generally those that can be applied to an Earth-centred setting - cyberpunk, post-apocalypse, military sci-fi, first contact, first colonies, etc. Space opera and planetary romance are generally _not_ Earth-centric in focus - they're more about exciting adventure in outer space or on other worlds, so they'd be a better match for Adventure!, which is all about the exciting adventure, or somewhere further forwards in time, when Earth's concerns aren't the focus.

                                    Ive been running an Adventure! game set in the year 2550, using trinity tech including the "top secret" and prototype stuff as the norm for technology. Space travel is handled by having each alien species having an FTL capability (hyper drive, gravity well manipulation, etc) except for humanity who has become relient on "jump gates", giant rings that respond to human dna and allow for near Instant travel up to 17 A.U. placed all over space by aberrants before the great war of 2070. By using all this tech but with adventures rules, the pc's really feel like they get into and out of situations that would work as video game cutscenes or movies. They laugh and talk about things like they just saw it in the last nolan flick...which makes me happy, but lets me know that rhey are in a sometimes serious, sometimes silly but always action packed space epic ala 5th element or serenity meets mass effect or star wars. Also-give a dynamic a vibro sword, and silk steel, a heroic a high threat combat rifle, and a mesmerist access to bio computers...its good fun all around!

                                            Super science has a role to play in all of the games - although i dare say the order i prefer is counter-intuitive with Adventure! seeing a relaxed approach to hard science (in my mind because of the Hammersmith effect's temporal proximity), in the Nova age mega-Intelligence bends the rules again, but we see some Novas being attuned to certain forces capable of inventions another Nova of similar intelligence and power but a different anima (suite of powers) couldn't attain and vice versa. Then in the future where things should be more advanced, you have Black-Technologies innovated by Novas being supressed and more hard science as befits a science-fiction setting.
                                            I would actually like to see more theoretical science described in the Aeon/Trinity end of the arc; Nanotech, AI, Time-Tubes and even a few uplifted species or transhuman recipes/rigs/emulators/implants might have been innovated by Novas and suffer heavy research restrictions. But the information while tightly regulated should be available through the Trinity gate-keepers in the interest of advancing stable science - some places like the Colony haunted asteroid belt may still be developing these technologies in frightfully isolated and under-patrolled bernal spheres where technology laws matter little.
                                            I certainly feel the future end of the aeonverse has room for more transhumanism as a kind of variant to Psion characters or baselines. I'm not sugesting you openly rip off Transhuman Space, but i would like to see the scope widened for that possibility. After all Whitley Styles is still around in 2121 or 2122 - so the Trinity are aware if not particularly generous with the technology for enhancing human capacities. The only detailed cyberware was found in Asia Ascendant - which was never published.

                                                To be fair, we don’t know that Whitley Styles is still around in the Æon era of the Trinity Continuum (as opposed to the Trinity Universe — that is, the new game as opposed to the old ones). Likewise, science fiction can range from hard science to space opera; and in general, the presence of psionic powers tends to align more with the space opera end rather than the hard science end. Heck, given what has been leaked about the nature of Talents, my suspicion is that the TC as a whole is going to lean more toward the space opera side: that is, the relaxed approach to science is going to be more or less omnipresent. Remember, the default setting in the TC core book is going to feature modern-day Talents; so the loosened strictures of Adventure aren’t unique to the Adventure era.

                                                All that said: I agree about wanting the Æon era to have a more developed scientific baseline than the Aberrant era (with new technologies that do not depend on Noetic or “Quantum” principles to operate), just as the Aberrant era has a more advanced baseline than the Adventure era. And to the extent that Talents have access to superscience, it should take the form of being able to play fast and loose with the precise limitations of any given era’s scientific baseline.

                                                    Okay, first point is valid, but really what we do know is contained on a short point by point document. I thought perhaps using the original setting might have made for a useful guideline - that's what brought me here after all.
                                                    I'm aware of the genres of Sci-Fi and while hard science is my preference (and not necessarily exclusive of space opera), the boat sailed on that a long time ago for the Aeonverse - but i've always preferred the technology to be at least theoretically possible if humanity is going to get a hold on it - or at least well described, like the noetic principles we were given in various old supplements. Even then, the old Nova-tech and older inspired devices are indivisible from the greater setting - Trinity era games have artificially hardened science - which is about all i can hope for given the "What we Know" document (which finally loaded for me after all my posts last night).
                                                    Talents? This is a new term for daredevils/paramorphs, i take it? I am still missing some of the information taken for granted here i suspect. Further investigation will be embarked upon.
                                                    It's not a question of mechanics as much as one of genre and style differences between the eras - i have no problem with inventions modifying numbers or effects to some extent that seems fair and fun, but i don't think Jekyll/Hyde potions, frankenstein monsters, pedal powered flying machines, energy shields or any other unforeseen advances in science should be bandied about too liberally in the sci-fi end of the story. That said, process 418, by my understanding of how this stuff works, is going to change all that anyway. But i still would argue that this is a matter of genre and not a flat out limitation on the mechanics.
                                                    E.G. Light-speed comes up regularly in Sci-Fi, sometimes in Superhero stories and rarely if ever in Pulp - the three genres demand different approaches to science. Sometimes pulp-/super-heroes even veer into mysticism too, although i doubt it would fit in the ultra-slick Aberrant setting as written.
                                                    The Aeonverse is so versatile in it's genre that these things can cross over to contrast with common contraptions of the age - but it should be done sparingly and with reason - if only to preserve mood and intent of the setting material. If the players prefer a looser, pulpy approach to their space-faring - then the GM/ST/whatever this system calls it should see the desire and shift the genre accordingly. Same when they prefer a more codified framework of possibilities. I would urge a baseline equipment list steeped in hard science as often as practical, so that players can be the ones to innovate the FTL drive, energy shield or pseudo gravity generator. Thereby letting the hard science fans such as myself to run the setting without pruning out parts of hard-wired rules and assumptions. At least that's what i personally hope for concerning super-science.
                                                    The lapses in hard science within the 2120's are mostly well reasoned out anyway; with Psionics being an alien gift, along with the chambers, noetic biotech and jump-drive technology with other culprits such as Nova tech (being encased in quantum taint away from the noetic universe) and earlier relics (created in a period of altered physical reality).
                                                    I understand that we are branching into more eras now, but that simply means i can't guess at what the breadth of inspired innovation for those eras might be and this becomes Schrodinger's point, hidden beyond the terminator. So forgive me for clinging to what i know for now.
                                                    Outside of these special circumstances the technology attributed to pure human development has followed a fairly realistic model - with attention paid to orbital mechanics, reaction fuel and bandwidth - elements that rarely trouble full-flight space opera, which operates more like pulp in space at times. It would be a shame to loose that aspect of the setting because a biokinetic psion can turn his head into a fern or a Talent/Paramorph can return from certain death.

                                                    I do hope i'm making sense in between all this ranting. Let me summarize:
                                                    The boat sailed long ago for pure science - but there is no need to throw the baby out with the bathwater. Especially when the genre is Bab-Fi.
                                                    Or something along those lines.
                                                    -smile-
