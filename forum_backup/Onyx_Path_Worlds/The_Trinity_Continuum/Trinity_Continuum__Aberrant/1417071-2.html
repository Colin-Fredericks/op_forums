    Originally posted by Florin View Post
    I also think equating different dimensions with alternate worlds (and therefore Talents) is incorrect from an MCU perspective. The Quantum Realm is considered a different dimension in the MCU (as shown in Dr. Strange's movie when he's knocked through a bunch of them) despite being modeled as the micro-field in Aberrant and not an alternate world. Dormamu's realm and the mirror realm are more like different states of existence as opposed to alternate worlds.
    Parallel dimensions include realities extremely similar to ours but also realities that work on different physics laws. Sliders (the old series) and Event Horizon (Terror Sci-fi) uses this premise.

    Trying to point at one thing he does, travel to two different dimensions, as proof he's a talent when he does a bunch of other things which are not possible for talents is a step too far for me.
    He is not a talent, he is a magician, but magic don't exist in MCU, all we are doing is suggesting ways to simulating his powers based on his fluffy.

                                                Another inspiration series from the MCU are the X-Men, though there are a number of differing powers that don't represent psi powers, Professor X and Jean Grey, even Psylocke, are all Psi.

                                                Minor tidbit: at one time there was a series of books released by Marvel Studios that were a paper "wiki" wherein each character within the MCU was written up with history, status and powers. Most of the X-Men had powers that, though a result of the extra gene in their DNA, had some application of psi. For instance, Colossus was said to possess the "psionic ability" to transform his organic carbon molecules with organic metal, resulting in vastly increasing his strength, resilience, etc. Cyclops had the "psionic ability" to open a portal to a dimension of pure energy through his eyes.

                                                Mind you, I'm not defending the essays at the time, my friends and I eye rolled over plenty of those power descriptions and joked how the writers used the word "psionic" instead of "magic" to sound more educated while being too lazy to think of any other way to describe how the powers worked.

                                                RE: Captain America

                                                Less certain about being just a superior. Though as mentioned before, it depends on the writer and what they give him as abilities. One series I read he was able to go with paratroopers without a chute, if he suffered any injuries from the jump, he would heal up in a matter of minutes. That same writer gave him a much greater physical density, which made him more resistant to harm as if he were wearing a full suit of body armor as well as much stronger, able to flip a car onto it's side with minimal effort or slam into a tank with his shield and rattle the crew inside silly. That same regenerative capability made him highly resistant to toxins, and diseases, including fatigue poisons and what not. Many of those abilities could be represented by a Darkstar Nova.

                                                But again, repeating myself, depends on the writer. I'm not expressly disagreeing with you so much as pointing out that it depends on which stories one is referencing to represent him.
