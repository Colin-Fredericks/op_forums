    Originally posted by Mateus Luz View Post
    One thing that always disturbed me in 1e was that the metaplot of Aberrant was useless in the long run.

    I mean, the sterilisation of novas would have some effects on the plot flow if the Aberrant war was not so close, and all the investigation on the Utopia end up as nothing when Aeon is back and even stronger after the war. It’s not that it sounds implausible, actually it’s one of the most plausible parts of the entire plot, but we as Novas are just... powerless... on the entire metaplot.

    We were not the main characters of the metaplot, obviously, and we can’t change it, but the things we are working on are meaningless in the long run, nobody in Trinity recall the utopian sterilisation process, or the murder of Slider, or the fact that a Nova run for president. Probably the Antaeus plot (from 1e) was the one that would bring the long term results.

    Anyway, the new plot have a much strong appeal as it is about the rise of Aberrants against baseline, and how the acts of a few people created started the domino effect that end in a war declaration less than 20 years after.
    Everyone keeps saying how they were powerless in the old metaplot, but I don’t get that. Our characters in almost all our games became movers and shakers in the world. I’ve had more than one character that could fight Pax to a standstill.

            Originally posted by Jetstream View Post

            Everyone keeps saying how they were powerless in the old metaplot, but I don’t get that. Our characters in almost all our games became movers and shakers in the world. I’ve had more than one character that could fight Pax to a standstill.

            Well, in my opinion, its because WE knew how it would end if the ST didn't do something to stop it. It would end in fire and war and great destruction. The future was already written and considered pretty much inescapable. Which isn't really the stuff that makes one feel powerful.

            I remember, in the many games of Aberrant I played in or ran the ST had to specify that the future was unwritten, that despite what was said in the books the players were in fact powerful enough to change things if they wanted. But this was always a sort of "hey, just so you know how it is going in this game" rather than an automatic assumption. Which it should be. Especially in a game about superpowered people, of people who now have the power and the ability to use it to change the world.

            Which is why the idea of the Continuum is PERFECT. We know the potential of one future, the one where the Aberrant War happens and ushers in an age of psions, but we also know that this world might be a different one. It also allows for in-game reasons for parallel and dimensional travel and vision, and that is brilliant.

            At least this is my opinion.

            On Aberrations, I hope they remain as I always liked them. I remember going through the book and picking the ones I liked the most for whatever character I was playing. I especially liked the ones that let me be different, but also improved in a small physical sense.

            -----

            For me I prefer human looking, though I don't mind (actually I like) supernal beauty for my characters, that is they seem like perfect humans. But honestly none of my characters were inhuman in appearance.

                                                    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post

                                                    The Aberrant timeline deviated from the “action” timeline described in the Trinity core book last year, if not earlier (I don't know when the Galatea space station was launched in the Aberrant timeline, and it may yet to be launched in the Action timeline for all I know; but the Action timeline definitely doesn't have the Fireman Erupting in 2018); so it isn't just empty words that the future isn't written; it's demonstrably true. I'm hoping for a box somewhere in the text that makes this explicit — if not in Aberrant, then in Adventure, spelling out that Action and Aberrant are two possible futures of the Adventure setting, and maybe spelling it exactly when the divergences point occurred.

                                                    I'm also interested in maybe seeing some settings set in the future of the Action timeline showing how it might end up developing without the Nova Age throwing everything for a loop. Maybe some notes in the Anima setting to describe how it might be recast as a future of the Action timeline? My point is that you've got a Continuum; you should use it as more than just a talking point or a bit of color. Actually develop multiple branches in the Continuum instead of concentrating everything into the Adventure-to-Aeon timestream.
                                                    With the caveat that, although Dataweaver likes to use "Action" to refer to the core rulebook setting, that is not anything official, and none of us use it internally:

                                                    The core setting is intentionally much more vague than the other eras. We paint a broad brush and suggest a lot, but there's no strict timeline of events like you find in the setting sections of Aberrant or Aeon. It's much more of a toolbox for playing a certain kind of game, while the other eras are more specific settings.

                                                    The core is set "now," but it's a very general now. You can certainly decide to set your game in 2019 or 2020 with no novas, just as easily as you can set it in the 1980s.

                                                    The closest we get to reporting on any specific dates is the section with the in-world artifacts at the front. Newspaper clippings, transcripts, pamphlets, etc. The furthest date is a single article from August 2018, but not including that, the latest is early March 2018, before the Galatea explosion.

                                                    So while the core doesn't explicitly call out the Galatea or novas, there's nothing there which particularly contradicts it either. Aeon, Aberrant, and Adenture! are specific flashpoints, "nodes" on a timeline. That sort of thing happens to the timestream when there's a massive flux event. The core setting is not one of these nodes: it's more of a cloud of possibility.

                                                    Anyway tl;dr: play the game you wanna play, I'm not the boss of you
