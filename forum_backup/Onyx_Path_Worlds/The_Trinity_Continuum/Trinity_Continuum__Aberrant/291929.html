                  Originally posted by Magnir View Post

I already know that they are creating a new set of rule within that framework. That doesn't bug me. I'm sure that it will be variant on the tried-and-true rules, not D20 or FATE.
                  The new system uses d10s and dice pools, but that's a far cry from saying it's still the ST system. It is not. For several reasons. First and foremost is that the ST System is owned by CCP, while the new system is owned by Onyx Path. If anything should happen to our CCP license, we don't want to lose our mechanics too.

                  Second is that the ST system was inadequate for our needs, so we started off fresh from first principles.

                  I also think that the question is fairly irrelevant to my post. It really doesn't matter one way or the other WHY I want to use Aberrant over other Supers games. I just do.
                  I'm not trying to tell you what to do, I'm just trying to suss out your reasons. You wanted a generic superhero ruleset, so unless you're using the old Aberrant rules, you'll have to learn new system no matter what. I'm not sure why you'd go with something which more than likely will include something you've already said you don't want, when other options exist which definitely don't have that element.

                                                  Actually you can do a Batman type character in Aberrant (one of the players in my group did); technically it would be a Nova but functionally it would be the same as Batman done in most of the four-color superhero games. Aberrant only had one source of power which made the difference more distinct between normal and super, either one had quantum or one did not. In a standard superhero comic or game it is not quite that easy and lines can get quite blurred.

                                                  Batman is not as normal as one may think; in the comics he is shown lifting around 1000lbs on more than one occasion. Even worse, one of the DC games had him able to lift something like 600kg. Back in the early to mid 1980’s there was speculation that he was a low level Meta (or what would be called that in modern DC comics anyway) or part Kryptonian or something similar though that did not seem to go anywhere.

                                                  Similarly, Catwoman has had implied meta-like powers on and off (her penthouse to pavement fall and return to life trick in “Batman Returns” is far from unique for her) since way back to explain how she does (and survives) the things she does in the comics and the movies. Her “9-lives” power has been explained as everything from just luck up to a full suite of powers similar to what Huntress had in the “Birds of Prey” tv series.

                                                  Even the new “Gotham” series pilot showed her doing things that would take a Meta to do (though whether intentional or just especially bad Hollywood physics gaffs is yet to be revealed); namely she hopped off a roof and landed in the typical three point crouch on the sidewalk, when the camera pulled back the buildings were 2+ story apartment-over-shop buildings. Later in the same scene, after grabbing a quart or half-gallon jug of milk among other things, she is chased down the street and jumps from the sidewalk up to a fire escape with a locked, caged ladder that the unencumbered adult chasing her obviously considered too high up to be able to continue the chase. Also she claims to be able to see fine details in the dark.

                                                  There generally is a big difference between ‘normal human’ and Batman level heroes in games; they are usually actually low end supers not too far behind supersoldiers mechanically regardless of whether it makes sense from their backgrounds. Anything less would simply be unplayable with others playing mainline supers.
