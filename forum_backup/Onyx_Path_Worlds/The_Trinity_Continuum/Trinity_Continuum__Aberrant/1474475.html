    Group is finally running it's Aberrant/Scion crossover, and we ran into some issues

    02-12-2022, 12:44 AM
    So we got our game session 0 out of the way, Our group of 5 players and the GM. 3 Novas, 2 Demi God's (for scaling purposes, it looks like they're around a similar power lvl) And we ran into quite a few minor hurdles. Like dealing with Aberrant vs Scion Scale (and how the numbers don't align quite evenly) and a few others.

    So I went on here, and tried to find if someone had already had this issue, but I couldn't find crossover discussion for these two any later than 2017.

    Has anyone come up with house rules dealing with stuff like this? I know the games weren't designed to interact right out of the box, but we weren't sure if someone had already done the work on what needs adjusting in order to make them fit each other as smooth as possible.

          My suggestion is to go all the way to one side or to the other (Aberrant has more solid rules, so its easier to push Scions to Aberrant side than the opposite), and build all of them under the same base rules.

          To bring Scions to Aberrant (and Trinity Continuum as a whole), create a template for each of the levels, (Hero, Demigod and God) to build on top of the basic character.

          They all would add Legend trait, Legendary Titles, Booms Callings and Knacks to the basic character (that would have Edges, skill tricks, etc).

          The knacks, booms and domains are not that hard to adapt, you just may need to fit scale properly (instead of Legend/2 rounding up, make it 1 + L/2 rounding up)
          Legendary titles add "intensity scale" when used, much like a Talent can do spending Inspiration, so basically you gain scale on your action, no matter action you are talking about, and it wont follow the same rules as Mega Attributes (so its easier).

          I agree with thedonnie about the power disparity, and its more than just the scale they can reach at full power.

          Scions are much more Legend points dependent than Novas to Quantum points, and Legend is far more difficulty to recover than Quantum, so you may need to balance the number of times a Scion may use minor sacrifices to something like 1/scene instead of 1/session.
          Try to keep Quantum lower than Legend, as the first is about 1/1 scale and Legend is 1/2. Demigods can stack scale, what gives them advantages, but its very Legend demanding, so a Nova may not get the same peak but have a better average. If you let quantum go free, than Demigods, and probably even Gods, have no chance.

                      That might be one way to go, yes. I'd be more inclined to say that:

                      • Origins operates roughly at the Talents level:Twists of Fate are paid for by extra successes rather than a hard-to-earn currency like Legend or Inspiration; so even a “baseline” person in the world of Scion can Twist Fate more readily than a Talent can DE. Also, where Talents have Inspiration-fueled Gifts, Origin level Scion characters (whether or not they're pre-Visitation Scions) have Momentum-fueled mortal Knacks.
                      • Heroes are roughly on par with “Dark Star Novas”: Feats of Scale can do much more than the “+1 Scale” that Talents can pull off, and aren't unlike Mega-Attributes, save for being thematically gated rather than gated by attribute; and in a mixed Scion/Aberrant game, I'd consider having Feats of Scale give a flat +Legend bonus to the appropriate Scale (in Scion, Scale tops of at 5; in Aberrant, it goes all the way up to 10). As well, they're able to pick up supernatural Knacks that are not unlike Mega-Edges. On the other hand, they start with two Purviews (a PSP and one from their parent); and note that the Purviews include Epic Dexterity, Epic Stamina, and Epic Strength, with Boons not unlike the Mega-Edges associated with Mega-Dexterity, Mega-Stamina, and Mega-Strength. And while there aren't any direct corollaries to the Mental and Social Mega-Attribute-based Mega-Edges, there are a number of Callings and Purviews that provide similar Knacks and Boons. What keeps Hero-level Scions from competing on par with regular Novas is their cap of no more than 4 Legend, and the need to channel any other Purviews through Relics or Guides.
                      • Demigods, by contrast, ought to be roughly on par with regular Novas. A big change from Hero to Demigod is that Demigods have more options to Imbue Legend, which really takes the edge off of the Legend costs that pervade Scion: it's much easier when the Legend that you put into a Feat of Scale or a Marvel lets you perform it as many times as you'd like until you want to reclaim the Legend for some other purpose. On top of that, you have Casual Miracles and Dominion Stunts.
                      • I cannot comment on how Gods compare to Novas, other than to note that they operate in the Legend 9+ range. Beyond that, Scion: God isn't it yet; so I have no idea what sorts of power upgrades they get compared to demigods.

                                      I would argue that Scions don't have a conscious control over Fate, anymore than Talents or Assassins do: their ability to Twist Fate is a subconscious manifestation; and the difference between what a mortal (i.e., Origins) can do when Twisting Fate and what a Hero can do is that a Hero's understanding of what's possible for him to achieve is much greater than a mortal's understanding. (Of course, that added understanding could be phrased as “the Hero knows that Fate is real”; so…)

                                      But yeah; I would say that Assassins are closer to Heroes than Talents are, in that Masteries can do the impossible just like Immortal Knacks, Boons, and Twists of Fate can. I'd still put them below Heroes, in that a Hero's ability to perform Feats of Scale is still far more impressive than the closest counterpart available to an Assassin; but that's still more impressive than what an Origins-level mortal can manage.

                                              But Talents and Assassins already have Inspiration for that. That's part of why I'm thinking that Scions and their kind are similar to, but not the same as, Talents — unless you want to strip Talents out of the game entirely and replace them with Scions and their kind, which I could see doing.

                                              And if you do that, I could see using “Talents” to refer to Origin-level Scion-style characters, who lack a power stat like Legend or Inspiration, but can still access Dramatic Editing through the Stunts mechanic (i.e., Twists of Fate) and who have Callings and Knacks instead of Gifts.

                                              The only thing I might want to add to that would be an ability to spend Momentum for Dramatic Editing that's luck-based rather than skill-based — that is, to enable lucky breaks that don't involve the character taking any action at all. I'm not sure that's needed, though, as there usually are “passive” skill checks that could be employed, like rolling to see if you notice what's around you: roll a basic success, and the Storyteller tells you about something there that you didn't already know about (or just says “there's nothing else here”); roll additional successes, and you can modify that description as per the Dramatic Editing guidelines, with extra successes counting as Inspiration spent.

                                              (For TC:A!, I'd likely keep the Talent rules in place for stalwarts and mesmerists; but ironically not for daredevils. But we're discussing TC:Ab here, so it's something of a moot point.)
