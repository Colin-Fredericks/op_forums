        Oh God! This is gonna be a long one. feel free to skip.


        For people who are on this board I guess the last update was a bit lackluster. But that is because we are mostly aware of that stuff already, so nothing new.

        For someone who IS new to the game though, those bits are required. Write-ups of mooks are perhaps not required by veterans, but if you are starting out, getting a feel of the 'Power level' of ordinary to elite baselines (and beyond) are rather important I think.

        What DID suprise me is how drasticly stuff like the drug mite enhances baselines. Several dots of Mega-attributes? Again, I think I misunderstood the super-science rules, aren't nova-tech only able to give max one dot of any one super-ability?


        As for ALL of the previews?


        THINGS I MISSED
        * Examples of nova-tech in practical society. Has armor tech advanced with eufiber and novastolo? Has weapons tech? Or have they cancelled each other out? What about those anti-power-shackles? Rules for nova-tech drugs and medicines (I especially missed stuff like mox).
        * Several of the Teragen has bio-manipulation-powers of some sort. But they don't seem to exist in the rules. Or do they? Examples of re-skinning powers to create something not straightforward would be nice.
        * And dear God! Super-science examples. From inception to creation. Let us see the process. What are the things you need to buy with XP, and how much? How do the pre-requisites change? (A rank 9 Q-tech item needs science 9?) How was Mite created? How does it work? And how was it 'down-graded' to nova-tech?


        THINGS I MISSED (but might be better in a Players Guide?)
        * How big is a hyperfusion engine? Do cars have them or do they have batteries powered by hyperfusion? Can a baseline carry one around? Is it expensive to buy and own?
        * What is left? If you are super-intelligent, what do you do? The climate is saved. There is super-cheap energy. HIV/cancer is gone. Aeon/Utopia seems to be sponsoring industri and cheap housing everywhere. Even today hunger is more a socio-political problem rather than 'we are running out of capacity to grow food". The world seems saved pre-game. What challenges are left for the super-smarties (and how do they produce story hooks)?
        * What is it like to be a nova? In the original rules you got a 'stream-lined physiology' where you got some bonus dots, required extra food, low body-fat (quite trim) and some every-day quirks like how alcohol was not very effective now. Is this still a thing? Shooting energy bolts is quite noticeable, but are there small differences common to ALL novas?


        ODDITIES
        * Nova numbers seem to be a quarter of what they were in the previous edition? Changed things a bit. Now any individual nova are more important and noticeable. It's harder to get lost in the 'nova crowd'. Organisations are smaller unless bolstered by more baselines. Is the Amp Room still only open to novas? Might be small crowd, more a country club than an actual party place.
        * Longevity/immortality seems rare now, especially compared to psions (for whom it comes easy, or so the boards claim). Clashes a bit with story/fluff since every 'pre-N-day Nova' seems not to age (much, if any). Also, the Mega-edge slow aging at one dot does nothing (not intended I don't think).


        THINGS I REALLY LIKED
        * The underlying storypath system. It really cuts down on huge amounts of dice. Enhancements and dramatic scale makes things simpler and you get a better 'feel' for it.
        * The more positive tone. You can actually work for Utopia now, and not feel like you are stabbing the world and the future in the back because you are 90's grimdark stupid (old Proteus).


        THINGS I DIDN'T LIKE
        * Not enough clarity in the rules sometimes. This is a thing in development though, so rather natural. It seems to be a bit 'fluff and shorthand' in places. I'm looking at you, 'Instant Expert', which I interpreted to be valid for pretty much _any_ action, but was corrected that it only applies the FIRST time you do any TYPE of action (so, only the first time you try to pick a lock, not the second time you try to pick a lock). That's quite a difference in utility. This might entirely be me though, english isn't my first language. Most of it reads cleanly though, especially Quantum Powers. As I've mentioned before, super-science is what I find hardest to wrap my head around.
        Narrative scale is the most notorius culprit in this though. There seems to be underlying assumptions from the "Story-path explained" document that has not been included at all in the actual books? Narrative scale multiplier seems to have been omitted and it seems to have warped into two different systems behind the scenes. I think I've seen it on the boards as "multiply with (difference in scale)+1" yet sometimes (like in the Quantum Agent power) it alludes to the old "2,5,10,100, etc" multiplier. Well, probably a development artefact, or perhaps both scales are in play but used in different instances (like, 'success-multiplier' and 'numerical multiplier')? I'm far from the only one who have mentioned it. I understand you aim for a 'cinematic take' where rules should be a bit 'hand-wavey' and flexible, but without a solid, well-explained frame-work I actually find it more difficult to use narrative scale.





        typo-inspired name for eufiber batteries: Betteries.

                                            Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                                            As for Mercer, there is no way to make a guy who founded a club for rich people to go adventuring, and somehow made further fortunes “adventuring” (read: plundering indigenous cultures) not a dick.

                                            If i’m Misrepresenting that than please someone tell me how the Aeon Society members acquired fortunes adventuring!
                                            That's certainly a take, and I'm not sure where you got any of this.

                                            Going by their 1e Adventure! bios:

                                            Mercer's parents were wealthy. While they were anthropologists, and thus visited many exotic locales with the young Max, it's specifically stated their wealth came from investments. Max attended Harvard and got two PhDs in as many years. As his parents aged, he managed their fortune, and invested in several museums. He apparently managed that money quite well: the fiction on page 3 says "He’d made a sheik's fortune 11 or 12 times over in his young life from radical new mechanical patents and stock market wizardry and, each time, had given it away, wholly or in increments - to charities, trusts and reform societies." Page 23 has Whitley writing about Max "sketching up a new two-way radio device for Professor Dixon to perfect". It's likely those mechanical patents are the source of a lot of the Aeon Society's continuing income.

                                            The only thing we know about Donighal's past is that his father was a teacher. Perhaps the elder Donighal was a teacher at Harvard, and that's how Michael met Mercer. A Harvard professor probably made a decent enough income.

                                            None of the rest of the core Society are presented as particularly wealthy, or having "acquired fortunes." Annabelle Lee Newfield was a tobacco-spitting 16 year old girl when she signed up. Whitley Styles is heir to a big company, but the company's co-owner is blocking him from taking his legacy. Prof Dixon might be making money on his inventions, but he spends it all on the hooch. It's likely that Mercer pays for their living expenses as a salary for being part of the Society. Despite the implications of the name, the "Aeon Society for Gentlemen" was never a club for rich people to go adventuring. It's a society for people with the will and ability to change the world for the better.

                                            I can certainly see the argument for Jack Tallon having plundered indigenous cultures in his pre-Aeon life as an adventurer, but he's notably Aeon's oldest member and is increasingly obsolete, and again is never really portrayed as having acquired a fortune in any case.
