    Just a little observation: we have to remember that powers, in RPGs, are not only about mechanical advantages. Their first benefit is what I call "Narrative Permission" - that is, you couldn't try to do something, but if you have this power/advantage/whatever, now you can.

    So, even if a Quantum Attack is weaker than a gun, it does allow me to enter somewhere unarmed, even naked, and even then, have the Narrative Permission to do something that only an armed person normally could.

    Wouldn't you love to have the "Narrative Permission" to levitate, in the real world? Even if it was in Speed Scale "0.001"?

                    Originally posted by Mateus Luz View Post
                    Well, a Q5 Nova with no Scale 5 powers made really bad choices in their XP use. I mean, if you have Quantum 5 and Quantum attack 1, you gain 2e in the attack, but lose the chance to destroy warships with your attack. There are those cases, sure, but usually when we talk Q5 we are talking about Scale 5 powers, basically optimized XP spending.
                    Usually, yes, but that isn't applicable here since the OP specified they were looking to run a campaign limited to 1 dot powers. In this case, Quantum 5 isn't meant to provide massive cosmic power, but to provide fuel and consistent success with a bunch of weak powers.

                    Beyond that, I've always assumed you should pump Quantum as high as your Storyguide allows at character creation, no matter how many points you put into your actual powers. After all, improving a power requires you to Max Out that power once, but improving your Quantum rating involves Maxing Out your Quantum rating a number of times equal to your current Quantum rating. So taking a power from 1 dot to 5 dots requires Maxing Out the power four times, but taking your Quantum rating from 1 dot to 5 dots requires Maxing Out your Quantum rating ten times; which is harder than it seems since players are more likely to spend Maxing Out successes on actual powers rather than Quantum. Starting at Quantum 4 or 5 bypasses that training time, and gives you a bigger Maxing Out pool to boot, meaning you gain new nova traits faster and with less Flux. Successfully Maxing Out during combat is also fun and dramatic, which is always important during a game.

                        Originally posted by Timecrafter View Post
                        Just a little observation: we have to remember that powers, in RPGs, are not only about mechanical advantages. Their first benefit is what I call "Narrative Permission" - that is, you couldn't try to do something, but if you have this power/advantage/whatever, now you can.
                        Sure, it's actually part of the reason I want to mix Talents with low-powered Novas. The difference between "lucky/skilled action hero" and "low powered super" is precisely what I'm looking for in giving the players different "Narrative Permissions" at the table.

                        My son playing an action hero, through their Inspiration & Dramatic Editing, gets to be Jason Bourne when it comes to car chases & evading security, John McClane when it comes to shooting things to make them explode, and John Wick when it comes to knowing the right people & convincing them to help him. My son playing a low-powered super, through his low-level powers & maxing out, gets to be Luke Cage tanking a knife to the chest, Peter Parker dodging a hailstorm of bullets, and Jessica Jones throwing a car into a bunch of Russian Mafia goons.

                        It's a halfway point between the flexibility of "anyone can do everything" and the stifling restrictions of classes in D&D. The Talents push themselves to stunts within the realm of physics with skill/luck that boggles the mind, the low-level Novas push themselves beyond the bounds of physics but without the class or skill the Talents can pull out of nowhere.

                        Originally posted by Shepherdboi View Post
                        Usually, yes, but that isn't applicable here since the OP specified they were looking to run a campaign limited to 1 dot powers. In this case, Quantum 5 isn't meant to provide massive cosmic power, but to provide fuel and consistent success with a bunch of weak powers.
                        This is indeed the case. Whilst I understand the arguments when it comes to min-maxing Quantum to match the powers you plan to use (and you are both making reasonable points for optimising a build) - I don't want people able to cut through a warship. They're low-power supers. The high-power supers are the antagonists (at least for the initial games, I have hazy plans about running a supers game in the same universe with the old characters playing the part of team nemeses).

                        I think I'll be taking advice from both of you. I'll bump up the powers cap to 2 (with an optional single "stand-out" power at 3 if desired), bump the Quantum cap to 3, and make a few characters within those bounds to see how it works. Quantum 2/3, with the Momentum pool, should give a reasonable chance of at least one success at "maxing out" when needed, with enough fuel & dice to pull off what they need to with the powers. A max of Quantum 3 means they can be "resistant" to Scale 2 firearms (yes, sticking with that) if they want to be without being immune to gunfire, punch through a brick wall but not (easily) through reinforced steel, etc.
