                  You'd split the effort to prevent starting characters (or their nemesis) from picking up and throwing Manhattan Island (or the planet Earth) whenever they want.

                  Starting novas get 150 xp. Buying Quantum up to 4 costs 48 xp. Mega-Might 1 costs 12 xp. Mega-Lifting costs 12 xp. Mega-Might 1 gives you Scale 2 for lifting and moving, and lets you throw anything you lift. Mega-Lifting lets you spend Quantum Points equal to your Quantum rating to boost your lifting capacity by the number of Quantum Points spent, so by spending 4 Quantum Points you temporarily boost your lifting capacity to Scale 6. Using your assumption, he can pick up and throw Manhattan as a character who has used less than 1/3 of his starting xp. That is obviously not how the rules are intended to work.

                  Since you've still got 68 unspent xp, you may as well buy Mega-Might up to 4 for an additional 36 xp. Mega-Might 4 gives you Scale 5 for lifting and moving, and lets you throw anything you lift. Mega-Lifting lets you spend Quantum Points equal to your Quantum rating to boost your lifting capacity by the number of Quantum Points spent, so by spending 4 Quantum Points you temporarily boost your lifting capacity to Scale 9. Using your assumption, he can pick up and throw Earth as a character who has used about 4/5 of his starting xp. That is obviously not how the rules are intended to work.

                  Look at the Scale examples on page 164 of Aberrant. Might 3 is a gorilla. Size 3 is a small building. Can gorillas pick up small buildings? Not unless they're King Kong. Gorillas (and most novas) are size scale 1, so they suffer from a Scale difference when lifting large objects. Now if a nova wanted to pick up a scale 1 object that happened to weigh as much as a building, that'd be a different matter.

                      The problem there is not really splitting effort, but how Mega Lift and Mega Crush work (and it becomes really bizarre really fast). I would say even letting Mega Attributes as one scale above the other powers is something I would avoid (I have a few suggestions in my book, but its not the point here).

                      We are not talking about these two, as logansrun68 specifically asked about Quantum Anima. The scales table are broken as a general rule, but size is the most solid of them, as there is a clear measurable grow of mass, starting somewhere at human size and growing pretty fast.

                      Different from other games that have a small difference between different sizes, to the point a big person is considered a different size, Storypath scale is all about huge differences. The idea of scale is something normal people just cant beat unless specifically trained for that, for example, a very capable power lifter can lift an scale 2 object, and the Olympic sprinters can run at scale 2. Our characters are not normal people, even Talents can do impossible things, that’s why they can consider the scale gap as a significant increase in difficulty instead of impossible.

                      Now for difficulties, things in the same scale have difficulties between 0 (really easy, would not matter to roll if not for complications and possible stunts) and 5 (next to impossible, virtually in the margin of next scale).

                      So lets say you are trying to lift something your size scale, the difficulty will be 0 to something light to 5 for something really heavy, but its not only a matter of weight, it matter how easy or hard it is to grab and how easy or hard it is to balance the thing you are trying to lift. An object one size smaller should be so easy to lift it doesn’t need a roll, and an object one size larger should be just impossible to lift.

                      Now a Talent or Nova with no Mega Might try to do the same, to them lifting a rock the size of a person (difficulty 5) would be as hard as lifting a big cow (difficulty 3 +2 from their size scale 2).

                      And then we go for the next level, a Nova with Might Scale N (for any number you pick from 1 to 10), When they try to lift an object size N they need to roll, the same way as a person need to roll for lifting objects size 1, but if the object is size N-1 it worth no effort, so no need to roll.

                      Back to Quantum Anima, moving a object the size of the character can lift with the power will require some test, so moving the Sun with Quantum Anima 9 will require a lot of effort, because the sun is pretty big, but moving a really big star (many times the size of the sun) will be more difficult (increase difficulty, not scale). If the Nova can increase their scale to 10 using Maxing out, they can move the sun or virtually any star with little to no effort.

                          It may be a bit too hand-wavy, and I may be remembering 1e rules, but I believe that Mega-Might/Mega-Lifting etc. do allow the nova to lift larger size objects as well. The explanation is that their Quantum essentially holds the object together while they lift it (assuming it is not structurally made that way). That way, you do get the thematic nova throwing a tank, or catching a crashing aircraft for example. Essentially, the assumption was that if the nova had the strength, then they had the leverage, could probably get a grip on it, and it would hold together through their quantum (at least until it landed on something they may have thrown it at).

                          This holds true, but even more so for Quantum Anima, where it is even easier to argue that the Quantum is keeping it together, there is no leverage, or grip issue to speak of. So the only remaining issue is Might.
