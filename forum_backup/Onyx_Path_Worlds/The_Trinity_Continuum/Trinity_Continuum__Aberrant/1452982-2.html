    You know the bit I said about quibbling? This is that.

    I think the point here is that it's not actually quibbling in this context. The question asked wasn't "How would you build something close to DC/Marvel heroes in Aberrant?" so telling people they're quibbling for not sticking to some boundary conditions you decided to add is a bit abrupt. This Superman stuff is stemming (mostly) from my reply to the actual question asked. And the answer is, as you've determined & seem to think is "quibbling", that Superman "as fully represented in the comics" cannot be translated to Aberrant without hitting the brick wall of Transcendence/Transformation making him unavoidably other than baseline human in social situations.

    Dormancy has the downside of not actually having your powers when Dormant. A sniper can pick off a Dormant nova whereas Superman is ALWAYS invulnerable. Being "clumsy & wimpy" is not a downside to Clark Kent because that is simply an act that can be shed at will, unlike Mental Aberrations which are permanent conditions affecting the character. And so on.

    The whole point of my answer (& others responding since) is that Aberrant doesn't create super-heroes like Superman. You agree to that above, but seem to think if someone points it out themselves, that's "quibbling". It's not. It's the point. 😉

                    Honestly, I think that if your goal is to create "Supers" that doesn't really have Transcendence and Transformation problems intrinsic to Novas, you should simply create an XP table with these costs (beware "N" isn't necessarily the same in each case; i'ts just a placeholder here).

                    +N XP = "Don't get a Transcendence Point" tax (add to the cost of something that would get you a Transcendence point; probably Quantum Trait)
                    +N XP = "Don't get a Low-Level Transformation" tax (add to cost of something that would get you this transformation)
                    +N XP = "Don't get a Mid-Level Transformation" tax (add to cost of something that would get you this transformation)
                    +N XP = "Don't get a High-Level Transformation" tax (add to cost of something that would get you this transformation)

                        That's essentially what I'm thinking — except that TC:Ab has a requirement in it that buying up your Quantum abilities requires pushing your powers; so it seems appropriate to me to put a similar requirement on spending experience to delay or reverse Transcendence or Transformations. Tying it to Grounding does that, and nicely reinforces the thematic element that the Novas who remain engaged in mundane life tend to be the ones who remain the most human.

                        As for what “N” is, my logic is that:
                        • not gaining a point of X should cost the same as removing a point of X. (Or maybe it should be cheaper?)
                        • removing a point of Transcendence should cost the same as buying a point of Quantum.
                        • removing a minor Transformation should cost the same as buying a point of Body Modification — or, more generally, a single Mega-Edge.
                        • downgrading a moderate Transformation to a minor one, or a major one to a moderate one, should cost the same as removing a minor one.

                        I do think that you should be able to “undo” Transcendence and Transformations after the fact, not just prevent yourself from gaining them in the first place. Though upon reflection, I could see an argument for the former to be harder to do than the latter.

                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                I like these ideas for house-ruled campaigns/settings having a more "four colours" feel to them. It makes Superman(-like characters) far more than a collection of super powers - they're also made a heavy investment in keeping themselves "human". They've decided that Transcendence is something to be avoided at all costs so are spending a lot of time & effort keeping "grounded" alongside the people they're protecting / hiding amongst.

                                It also allows for stories that push the narrative that there are shortcuts to power, but they can cost you everything. Say the Superman analogue decides to save the President instead of some poor guy's wife (say due to a Sadistic Choice trope decision). In his grief, the widower erupts as a Nova and decides to enact vengeance... but it would take years (decades?) to push himself to the level of his chosen nemesis (all the while, Superman analogue is also getting stronger)... but he could forgo his humanity to get there quicker.

                                Rules as written mean that Superman becomes as monsterous / inhuman as his nemesis over time (& stronger as well). With a setting house-rule though, Superman remains a boy scout hiding amongst humanity because he keeps feeding his XP into staying "human"(ish) whilst his Doomsday analogue grows ever stronger & more monstrous to take him on.

                                Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                                I do think that you should be able to “undo” Transcendence and Transformations after the fact, not just prevent yourself from gaining them in the first place. Though upon reflection, I could see an argument for the former to be harder to do than the latter.
                                Keeping with usual story tropes - I agree they should be harder to buy off than to just initially avoid. In cases like this, think of the Transformations like a loan. You've reaped some benefits in the past/present from either buying power/edge/quantum cheaper &/or having pushed yourself beyond your limits to win a challenge you otherwise would have lost. You don't just pay off that loan XP-for-XP, it comes with interest. I'd even stipulate in my games that the longer you've had the Transformation, the more it costs to get rid of it. Interest accumulates and it works with the story tropes we're dealing with.

                                Thing about it. In the comics, supers will occasionally push themselves beyond their limits &/or get a power booost and as a result they start to vibrate too fast, eyes glow, have flame auras, etc until they can pull themselves together, get better control of their powers, etc. That represents the standard "buying off Transcendence/Transformation as soon as possible" scenario. On the other hand, heroes with Transformations they've had since becoming empowered (e.g. bought at character gen) or simply allowed themselves to have for a long time are always harder for them to buy off in story - often needing more than just self control (e.g. gene mods, psychic therapy, etc) which represent a higher XP cost.
