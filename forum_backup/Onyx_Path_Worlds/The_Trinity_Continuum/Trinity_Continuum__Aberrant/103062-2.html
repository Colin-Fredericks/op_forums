    I've always felt this was the cool thing about Aberrant... Aberrant was about what happened when you gave superpowers to random people and not paragons of Good or masters of Villiany as it usually is in the comic books. The way how most Novas just went around capitalising their skills and the world turned them into starlets of the highest order was a very reasonable way of going into it.

    The fact that the one who claims to be more then human is the one who is most human in his foibles was a really nice way of emphasising the human element of Aberrant. In Trinity, this element was clearly no longer necessary, and the Aberrants were removed from their humanity to become more compelling villians.

    What doesn't kill you, makes you... stranger.

                                                I always took Mal and Pax as being reflected allegories. While the Utopia Project has utopian ideals (sort of), much of what it does in the undertones and in the non-Utopia books is restrict and prevent change.                                                         Another way to think of the reflection is that Mal is very much about personal responsibility and freedoms where as Pax, being the proverbial lapdog of Utopia, is very much about following orders from on high, which is arguably the opposite of freedom will (especially given Proteus and Utopia's hidden agendas).
