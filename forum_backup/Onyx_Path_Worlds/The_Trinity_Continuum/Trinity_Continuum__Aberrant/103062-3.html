                                                            Wow, I can't believe I'm posting to an Aeonverse thread after all these years...

                                                            Divis Mal is not Magneto. I preface my commentary this way because I'm about to compare the two while noting lots of similarities, but to be clear: I am fully aware that Divis Mal is his own character, with a unique character arc, that is not the same as Erik Magnus Lensherr's (or if you prefer, Max Eisenhardt's). Now, having said that:

                                                            Michael Donigal is the best friend of the man who founded an organization (in a private mansion at first, no less), dedicated to teaching the nascent Inspired to use their gifts for the betterment of a mankind that would inevitably come to fear them. This man is an idealist, one might even say utopian, with incredible abilities of his own, whose small group of friends would eventually become a sprawling alliance that strives to use its powers for a better world -- sometimes at odds with its idealistic founder. Michael, meanwhile, has a fundamental, somewhat violent break with his dear friend, and co-founds an organization of evolved supermen who stand in philosophical opposition to this organization. Widely derided as evil, this organization nevertheless has members who simply believe in founding their own separate society, while others range from defenders of their own to psychotics every bit as monstrous as the propaganda says. Meanwhile, Michael himself is a brilliant, charismatic winner of the Superpower Lottery who wields a god-like energy suite with unparalleled power and precision. Sound familiar?

                                                            While Michael is not Magnus, the two have a lot of clear overlap, especially in Magneto's sane periods, and the Claremont Magneto (upon whom the 90s X-Men cartoon version is very strongly based) heavily informs my portrayal of him. Donigal once believed -- or at least, desperately wanted to believe -- in Max Mercer's vision. His deep personal traumas and the horrors of World War II eventually made this impossible. Primoris/Mal desperately tries to avoid open conflict with Max's stepchildren during the Nova Age, but Mal sees that time as his, and when the world goes to war with his people, he fights back. If he really murdered that unfortunate human on his way out -- and we should be suspicious of all pre-crash records in Trinity, IMO -- it was an atypical event, as a warning or a last bitter shot, but not indicative of his nature. (Unless you want to go with the Apostle theory, which is awesome, IMO.) The takeaway for me is, the Nova Age Mal is solely concerned with "his" people, and thinks it's best for humans and novas to chart separate paths. I'm not sure how he feels about psiads by then, but extrapolation implies that he would think it best for them to go their own way, separate from baselines and novas alike. For an excellent portrayal of Magneto who charts closely to the Aeon Society Donigal, watch X-Men: First Class, quite possibly the best version of the Xavier-Magnus friendship to date.

                                                            Note: none of this is intended to step on lustral_star's phenomenal analysis of the character, but as a far too old fan of both X-Men and the Aeonverse, I had to note the conceptual ancestry.

                                                            Heroes die. Hope is a phoenix.
