    Re: Dormancy

    I'm more of a tangential comics fan than a true comics nerd, but I've always been a big rpg nerd, so I tend to see Dormancy in terms of the narratives it enables for players/GMs.

    As a player:
    • Live double lives, potentially in conflict (A famous Elite maintains a residence in a small, nova-phobic town, attends church, and home-schools his kids)
    • Play a character who has difficulty working with the baseline world (ala the Thing), but can set that part aside for a time, at the expense of not having easy access to the juice. It's somewhat obvious, but with a good ST the hard choices are delicious.
    • Choose to infiltrate a place like the Church of Michael Archangel as a baseline, and see how far you get without kewl powerz at your side
    • Potentially play a pre N-day Nova who just hid for weeks/months/years, like nu-Pax. This doesn't require Dormancy, but it seems fun to play someone so scared by their powers they subconsciously took them away. This reminds me of the Novel Diamond Mask by Julian May.
    As a ST:
    • Put characters in situations where their powers/public personas really are *not* the answer, and are in fact a liability.
    • Introduce a "baseline" recurring NPC who is a sleeper agent. It's pretty normal in high-powered games for players to disregard normies, and I love exploiting those blind spots.
    • Say "yes" to a character whose powers/aberrations would normally make my inner ST voice say "Problem character decloaking! Red Alert!" Rogue could be a serious PITA as a PC, but in the hands of a thoughtful player, such a character with Dormancy wouldn't necessarily steal all the shine.
    Re: Post-Human

    I wouldn't assume having "physical" powers isn't an existential experience. Imagine being blind for half your life and then gaining sight. Your experiences change, and experiences drive our self. In that case, your new experiences more closely mirror the mainstream human experience. Now, instead consider that you can move in three dimensions with a thought. Eventually, that capability becomes a part of you, much like sight, except hardly anybody shares that experience.

    There's a big difference between being a guy with an advanced flame thrower on his arm, and literally controlling heat and flame with a thought If you can think of a place you've been, and instantly be there, how does that change you? Do you walk from room-to-room regularly? Do you tend to forget that other people have to get in a car to go places, and might not want to meet you at 5:15 across town?

    These are maybe small things, but it's those little experiences/differences that cause people to change. And many Novas, due to their "fingerprint" of capabilities, might have *nobody* who really knows that it's like to be them.

    That's both amazing and lonely. Maybe it drives them to the Null Manifesto. Maybe they become hermits. Maybe it takes them to a dark and dangerous place.

    ***
    I think part of the tendency to disregard the psychological impact of these experiences is the damned short-hand of modern media: the word "powers." It externalizes experiences that ought to be profound, intimate, even defining (in our 21st century parlance, part of one's identity), and sucks the wonder and mystery out of otherwise great narratives.

    I've seen this corrosive short-hand used to describe everything from magic users to demons and angels, and even a couple times in pulpy sci-fi. About the only place it makes sense is in comics, and while Aberrant is a game inspired by comics, at its best it's broader than a simple four-color spread.

    This isn't intended to sound preachy; people should have their own fun with their pretend-time-elf-games. The most fun I've had with Aberrant wasn't playing some dude with powers. It was the guilty Catholic teen who said to himself, in a moment of rage, said "What kind of God would allow this..." and found himself crowned with molten light. It was the average college guy who manipulated space-time, was fighting multiple Elites, and summoned duplicates of himself from *somewhere*. Not all of the dupes made it out alive. He was mentally f*cked for weeks.

    TL;DR: Skew isn't just a guy with magnetokinesis powers; he can have a moment of rage and pull down a skyscraper. That sh*t changes you.

            My point about drawing a distinction between physical abilities and mental abilities wasn't that physical abilities don't have a psychological impact; it's that mental Mega-Attributes objectively bring into question whether the nova still qualifies as human. Asimov's “the Bicentennial Man” featured a robot who wanted to be recognized by the law as a human being; in pursuit of that objective, he replaced every part of his body with prosthetics that humans used to replace missing body parts so that he could argue that there was no part of him that disqualified a human from being human. But the one part of himself that he couldn't replace was his positronic brain; and the courts ruled that as long as his mind was that of a robot, he was still a robot.

            In a similar way, a nova who develops physical superhuman abilities may identify with the Null Manifesto and think of himself as transhuman or even post-human; but as long as his mind is still human, so is he, no matter how drastic the physical changes he undergoes. But when his mind starts transforming, then there might actually be some truth to his claim of no longer being human.

            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                            I LOVE the Mega-Attributes and the Mega-Edges, they are quite brilliant. I especially like how while reading over the abilities I was like "I want that, and that, and that, and that..." so many times. The fact that some of what was powers in the previous edition is edges in this one is also brilliant. It also simplifies some of the complexity of 1e, in areas that didn't really need to be that complex. Going over the abilities I can totally see how combining some of them together in a character could give the player the power spread they might want, so that is nifty.

                                                            I do, of course, have to read through it in more detail to truly get the understanding of it all, but what I see I like.

                                                            On Powers, well, funny enough, I like them less than the Mega-Edges, lol. BUT, like the other, I need to read over it more to see how I could build what I want to build. I figure frame works and themes would allow me to do what I want.

                                                            For example I could see some Mega-Edges and some powers being used to build Information Mastery, the same with Spatial Manipulation. The difference is that in the old game you had a single category already given description for that while in this one you have to pick what fits you, then add the tags and such, to make the character's powers what you want. Its not bad, not at all, but its different enough to require a bit more thought - which is not, very much not, a bad thing.

                                                            Soooo while I totally get, and understand and accept why telepathy and precog are not generlaly nova based powers in relation to the whole Trinity Continuum of Aeon and such, I also think telepathy is a supers power category that deserves to be available for games that don't involve the whole Continuum and the nature of psi and such. Which makes me wonder how one thinks one would write up a telepathy power in Quantum power terms.

                                                            As a negative there is the whole maxing out a power and thus getting flux to upgrade or purchase new abilities. Yeah, not a fan of that and I won't be using that rule. Not unless there are other and better ways that mitigate flux, which I don't see at first glance.

                                                            Additionally, the whole Quantum 10 going somewhere else, becoming something else, is what transcendence is, rather than how the term is being used in reference to negative flux.

                                                            I also notice that there is Horror subgenre where Flux is made all the worse, I wish there was the opposite of that where Flux was given rules mitigating it even more.
