    eventual new Aberrant-super strength etc.

    04-21-2015, 01:24 PM
    With the OPP folks keeping rather hush hush on the sardonyx rulesystem, and the knowledge that it be core-trinity continuum (aeon time first)- then Aberrant, we have a bit of time to sit on our hands or play the old system. Question to you folks, is this: most people have issues with mega strength and the amount of auto successes it gives based on number of dots. I love it, yet still gripe when a player says they hit my npc bad guy for 20 auto and 12 dice and his soak is 9. "Great...new npc needed." The auto dice lets me know "that person is STRONG!" and it makes sense when thinking about how a superman or a colossus could paste a random thug, but to keep headaches down, how have you ran Aberrant differently to adjust for the strongest wins all issue (in regards to the power creep of m. Strength vs soak abilities)? Keep lifting the same but reduced auto for damage? Make the player focus on one or the other? Adjusted soak to be more powerful (adding say, quantum lvl to all soak ratings?) One gm in my group reduces the auto dam/successes from 5 per dot to 2/3/2/3/2 going from 1-5 mega str. Another just hands out better body armor to all npc's.

          I suppose it's worth asking what the balance is meant to be between - ST and PC? Easy, you are both playing from the same rule-book. Purchase Invulnerability twice to sneak around the 'can't possess both Invunerability: Physical and Invulnerability: Energy' - if the players can do the math and question why you can do this and they can't simply point out to them that it's merely a stop-gap and beyond 6-7 Quantum their Mega-Strength successes will be ruling the day again.
          Then; simply never give out enough xp. or stack every other soaking mechanism until parity is reached. This isn't elegant and more or less ends up as a kind of arms war that the PCs can never win - but that's how i've seen Aberrant end up a few times now.
          Balance between Nova PC and baseline NPC? Quit your fussing, Novas are meant to feel unchallenged by baselines. The first ever Action Comics Superman story features Supes owning on a car with two armed gangsters inside. Eventually, Aberrant war needs for human survival in battle spawned VARGS (mechanized armour suits) and orbital weapon deployment standards but i doubt those measures saved many lives when a nova-bastard went in for sheer kills.
          There is another thread on Balance between Novas and Psions where i've posted at length about my own experiences with 2120 'balances'.
          If i'm misunderstanding you, let me know and i'll try to expand/clarify/reinterpret...

                      So in relation to player vs player you mean, or at least Adventure! creations versus Aberrant generated Novas - if i'm understanding.
                      The best approach, fundamentally, is evasion and range. Adventure! works well with Trinity, but my brother had the most experience using Adventure! in an Aberrant game. I seem to remember he used a variant of 'Untouchable' (Adventure! p162) for an NPC martial artist Daredevil to stay alive in a few battles against a 20-50xp nova with combat powers. But i can't recall any details on how precisely that worked. One could imagine another equivilent Knack might make 'splash damage' effects easier to avoid when the damage breaks line of sight - via sundered objects and scenery rent apart by Mega-Strength attacks.
                      Depending on how you run Dramatic Editing, that could also play a role, so could super science.
                      -shrug-
                      I might even be tempted to use the destruction facet as a type of foil to Mega-Strength - somehow able to redirect the destructive force of the given nova into scenery and other unintended targets. But in my imagining, the higher echelons on Destructive inspired should have some way to play a role in mega-strength battles - simply by right of the mastered facet.
                      To really cross them over with PC parity, you may need to introduce some ST-only Knacks to balance out in-game before offering them to players.
                      I have to admit that i've little experience with Adventure! systems, but the idea wouldn't be giving those characters greater power, but in extending some counter-balance to the extremes presented in so many automatic successes.
                      It's an interesting question, as with many problems Mega-Strength presents, i would have to think about it.

                      The way i've tried to limit this in my most recent games was with Noetic/Subquantum jargon - the drive being; the Subquantum medium of 2100+ is soaked in too much Psi for higher Quantum novas to erupt - using the Asia Ascendant rules for those that erupt at lower power. During the 1998-2060 period, the appropriately thick Quantum miasma for true Novas at full strength to erupt exists, the side effect of that extreme being taint. But the naturally occuring Psiads become rarer than normal - although we're given no reason to believe Daredevil/Talent inspired are affected by the Psi/Quantum medium. Then getting back to the days of Adventure!, you see the balanced Hammersmith Event which generated the lower intensity inspired but doesn't have the 'taint' to generate actual Aberrant-book Novas. Only the prime Stalwart; Primoris compares, but it takes him close to a century to get the level we see Mal at.
                      All that just to say; 'play the appropriate inspiration type, please'.

                              This goes to a debate a friend of mine and I had. He believed that Damage = Soak. I believe that Damage > Soak or else combats get so bogged down. Additionally Soak > Skill. In other words, the person who can tank anything should not be on the same par as someone who just dodges or parries everything away. Mr. Soak can just take it all day long and then laugh at the little guy acting like Daffy Duck on speed. Lastly Skill > Damage. Meaning the guy who can hop all around should be able to not worry about Mr. Smashieface. That is how I balance out those three parts of combat.
                              There should always be balance on some level, even if in the all-dreaded white room scenario. I hate those but they do happen.

                                  04-22-2015, 09:30 PM
                                  Right. Generally, if defense outstrips offense, combat gets really...really...sloooow. You have to whittle down all of your opponents bit by bit, and that gets boring, quickly.

                                  On the other hand, damage offense outstrips damage too much, you can't really have an exciting battle, because it's basically over before it starts. That's the current Aberrant problem. Poking the bad guy in the chest and having him instantly explode is funny a few times, but gets old. Likewise a player basically just getting one-shotted is not fun.

                                  Honestly I'm not sure their needs to be a big difference between Skill and Soak based defenses - honestly, that just seems like a matter of preferred style to anything else. Really they should probably be equally effective, or you start seeing people shift to one or the other just for the advantage.

                                      I totally agree with you on the fact that Damage should not go too too far out. Then it does get just as boring.
                                      "Yawn. Roll your to-hit roll. Ok. You hit. He is now a fine red mist. Next guy is over there."
                                      I have been in those fights and the answer is not to just go with the "bigger fish" route but to have to nerf the rules. It has been my experience as a player that when the ST comes up with someone bigger and nastier than you, then I only see two words flash in my head: Challenge Accepted. I then work on trying to beat that guy. Most players I know are of similar bent, however your mileage may vary.
                                      I choose to opt to rework the rules. Perhaps my mention of nerfing was a bit harsh but, when you retool the rules to make things fair overall, most players think you are picking on them specifically and will ask why you are weakening them. Again, I say most and your table and players may think differently. I do know that the rules are made to keep things fair for everyone. I do know that the rules are even playtested to make sure that this happens. However, no committee can ever hope to compete with the multitudes and their ability to come up with new and interesting twists to things. Twists that make one particular character an unstoppable monster. So, I am always a fan of house rules to keep things easy and fair for everyone at the table.
                                      As far as the difference between Skill and Soak goes, I can see how you may think they are just flavors of defense. I also will not insult your intelligence by explaining the differences between them. You are experienced enough to know what I mean. But I see them as not just styles but actual choices to make. Soak is very static. It just is something that you can reach. Skill is something that can be changed and it can be altered over time. It is a bit more dynamic on things, IMO. There is also the fact that, when someone focuses on a skill, then they tend to have less defenses and less offense, Instead, opting to just not being hit and not taking any damage at all if they have any say about it.

Turn the press against them, with shocking exposes and editorials on the monstrous, murderous PCs. Let them watch their toy sales plummet, and have their sponsors drop them. Have their friends shun them, and kick 'em out of the Amp Club. Bring in the cops, and arrest 'em for murder.

                                                          Originally posted by One Vorlon View Post

                                                          It's not really about PCs being too murder happy, or going around popping thugs like pimples. Moral consequences are an unrelated problem.

Then you have a whole revolving door of these guys, and it breaks suspension of disbelief. The only narrative solution (that is, fixing the problem via story rather than house-ruling) is to have your bad guy operate from behind the shadows so the PCs have to do a lot of leg work to find the bastard, and then when they do find him you get to give the poor slob all of 30 seconds of actual characterization before the PCs whomp him. That's no fun as ST.

                                                          Likewise, even if the PCs aren't killing everyone in one turn, but knocking out everyone in one turn, it's the same problem. The fight is over before the bell rings.

                                                          Also, this is a system that has to serve Scion, and Scion will include things you will want to/have to kill because there is no way for the mortal populace to deal with it otherwise.

                                                          I might make the argument that Aberrant really isn't, or at least wasn't, the game for "good guys".
