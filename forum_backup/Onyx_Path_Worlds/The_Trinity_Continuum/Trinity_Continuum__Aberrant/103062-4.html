    Part of Divis Mal's deal is that he's been through the Chrysalis multiple times - which results in cumulative penalties to understand or socialize with mundane humans. Assuming the count in the Teragen book is accurate, and he entered chrysalis at the minimum levels each time, he's got at least a +3 penalty (i.e. base difficulty of 10) to any social roll involving baselines. As others have pointed out, he just doesn't think like mundane humans any more, and that limits his ability to anticipate their reactions, or have normal, healthy relationships with them (and that's before including any possible mental aberrations he may suffer). Heck, the Teragen book implies that he's having problems maintaining normal relationships with other novas!

    Another big aspect of his personality (and subsequent dealings with the world) is that Divis Mal seems to have been deeply influenced by Nietzsche. If you've never read Nietzsche, his work basically postulates that modern standards of good and evil have been corrupted and subverted by the elites/one percenters/powers-that-be into concepts that merely uphold the status quo, and keeping themselves in power. But Nietzsche also postulated that the time was right for a "superhuman" (ubermensch), someone who would defy such labels, overthrow the status quo, and open a new dawn of possibilities to the world. Sound familiar?

    And in the Aeon Society, Mal probably sees those beliefs (about the corruption of "good" and "evil") firmly validated. Sure, Aeon is backing Project Utopia . . . .but they're also firmly supporting Project Proteus. And Proteus . . . wow, they're into some seriously evil things. Assassinations and black sites. Kidnapping and torture. Raping female novas, so that Project Proteus scientists can dissect the kids to see what second generation novas will be like. During the time of Aberrant, Aeon is effectively the Red Cross - just with death camps.

    And no one seems to care. When Corbin finally gets hard proof about Proteus, the UN turns him over . . . .to the Aeon Society. Later, during the Aberrant War, Aeon endorses the genocide of anyone related to novas (even former Project Utopia members), and destroys all nova-based research it can find, plunging civilization back into a dark age. Divis Mal helped found Aeon, back in the 1920s. . . only to see it turn into a society of monsters. Monsters who've convinced the world that they're here to help.

    (Yep, that's right - the bad guys won the Aberrant War. Hate to disillusion anyone)

                            The story of Mercer's time travel which I put in the ST Handbook was something developed by the original crew; I think Elizabeth D. Brooks was specifically the one who came up with it, but Bruce agreed with it. Part of my goal with the STH was to take a lot of these bits which the team had planned and discussed and put them in a public place for the fans to use.

                            While the flashpoint was the Venezuelan Phenomenon, remember that Max can only (or at least "most easily") travel to periods with a substantial amount of subquantum flux: most notably the Hammersmith event, WWII, N-Day, the end of the Aberrant War, and the Venezuelan Phenomenon. So jumping forward from the Aberrant War to get a peek at humanity's future, he bifurcates: one lands in the 2120s as expected, sees humanity prospering, and returns to his own time to found a more peaceful, hopeful Aeon Society, as depicted in Adventure! and Trinity. The other Max bounces back, and comes to the conclusion that the trip failed because the world has been destroyed. He creates a more pessimistic Aeon, more geared toward damage control, as depicted in Aberrant.

                            So ultimately it was just a way to explain why the Aeon Societies of Adventure!/Trinity and Aberrant seem very different, when ostensibly Max is still in charge either directly or indirectly in every era. When your founder can time travel, you expect that if your organization is up to some shifty business, the founder will ultimately know about it. If he doesn't stop you, it must be because he approves -- or even started the shifty business in the first place.

                            Now with the Trinity Continuum, things work sufficiently differently that we won't need to rely on this explanation. Aeon will be more cohesive throughout each era, the flashpoints are different, and time travel doesn't work exactly the same because of reasons.
