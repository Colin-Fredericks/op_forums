          Aberrant's emphasis is on superhumans, and their interaction with the wider human societies beyond them. It's as much 'what would you do with the power of a god' as 'what would humans do if people with the power of gods showed up'.

          In that, it taps into a particular strain of supers fic, which has become more prominent after the original edition's release, that tries to ask "how would people actually react to having superhumans in their midst?".

          Its starting point is ten years after the first superhumans (novas) appear, so the initial shock of their appearance has passed, and we get to see some of the ways the world has adapted to them.

          Novas can include superheroes, but it's not limited to them - it can include celebrities, mercenaries, criminals, people who emphasise the 'super' over the 'human', seeing themselves as something apart from humankind, people like Antaeus in the webcomic, who've come to value other priorities than humanity... anyone can become a nova, with their own view of the world.

          Aberrant is one of the very few RPGs in its genre, most supers RPGs looking to emulate superhero comics - off the top of my head, there's Godlike, and Wild Talents and its spinoffs.

                                  --> "Imagine the X-Men, but then blur the clear answers away."

                                  --> “Your career is likely Mega, but it doesn’t have to be. You are likely asked (harassed) to sponsor all sorts and got offered big pay deals”
                                  --> "Having power is great, you've had it for a while, now what? What world would you build and who would you worry about?"

                                                          Well... no. Thanks to the Aberrant War, the term used for novas in Aeon is Aberrants, and it's not helped by the fact the ones who've appeared in the 22nd century appear much less human, physically or mentally, than the ones who left after the War.
