                            Just a comment on this all.

                            A guy like Kingpin is normal, except he must be 8feet tall and strong like a bull. He is not different, mutant or engineered to be like this, he just is huge.
                            Tony Stark was a genius since he was young, basically he has the mental version of Kingpin size, far above what is considered normal, but yet normal.

                            Yes, to build what Tony builds he need to have Mega Mental Attributes, but he’s inventions are not powered by his inner power (the mini reactor is not his quantum source), and so all his inventions are essentially Nova Tech (Pepper Pots used his armor, it was not designed for her, and it was his most advanced version at the time).

                            I would allow a Talent to invent Inspired Science to build something like a Rocketeer suit or a Hologram Projector (like the ones in Agents of Shield). Iron Man armor is completely out of the reach of a talent, but I think we miss some good stuff if we limit inventors to Novas.

                            Concluding, Tony works much better as a Nova and his armor as Q-Tech, but there are some stuff in Marvel and DC that don’t fit the Trinity paradigm.

                                The problem you're getting at here are one universe's conventions around the source of abilities vs another universe's. I mean Superman's an alien who is powered by yellow spectrum solar energy, not a Nova drawing on Quantum either - but the fact remains that he is best mechanically represented in this different universe as a Nova. The fact that its a contrivance of the Marvel Universe that Tony is a normal human whose mental abilitiess aren't diegetically Superhuman doesn't change the fact that they are FAR beyond anything a baseline human or even Talent is capable of. The contrivances of the ORIGINS of a characters abilities are never going to mesh when you are translating them between different settings that make different assumptions about such powers.

                                We're getting here at the age old Exalted debate where people want Batman to be playable as a Heroic Mortal because that's the contrivance of his setting - but Exalted doesn't assume Heroic Mortals are capable of the superhuman level of abilities the DC Universe allows via "peak human training" - and inevitably Batman is best represented there as a Night Caste Solar.

                                            glamourweaver Good points those. With my circle one of the things that was often bandied about when discussing such things was what a character was capable of at various periods of their history, and how abilities shifted under various authors to fit their story. Comic characters are decidedly not consistent because they're works of fiction and pay zero attention to being formally represented in a RPG. And of course, the two franchises have different premises and themes that were heavily influenced by the Comics Code slamming down on their creative directions.

                                            Usually when things of this sort pop up in game discussions of say, "I want to play Batman in Exalted", the first thing I do is suggest to them not to duplicate a character with a dozen different writers and power levels within a game system, but to sum them up in a short blurb. I.E. a individual capable of incredible feats of stealth and investigation, a skilled combatant, and a master of contingencies and exploiting weaknesses, now select abilities to grow into that. Makes it much easier for a player to assemble and portray a character inspired by a iconic character.

                                            This dovetails into another talk I recently had, what our setting would be. Marvel and DC are not the same as the Aberrant setting, the former two are exceptionally deep with history and character lore but to attempt to faithfully recreate them via Aberrant means you're introducing taint and the other assorted aspects of Aberrant's power sources, of which magic is absent. The DCU has a good deal more mysticism and magic than the MU, but both have it and Aberrant doesn't (at least not by default).

                                            So the question was which setting are we using, and then what modifications have to be made for a Aberrant game. I decided that I was going to borrow liberally from MU/DCU, and include both PSI since I have Aeon, and magic (though not touching anything from WoD/CoD to govern magic) as a power source besides quantum energies. Then selectively choose characters, but still map them to Aberrants system, so the likelihood of Superman wearing a harness to drag a moon or dive to the deepest part of the ocean and push the earth off it's axis by a few inches isn't a thing. But Batman being a Darkstar or Tony Stark having meta-intelligence and making quantum science tech is definitely within reach and in either case, I'm not concerned with faithfully recreating a character's sum history from a dozen different authors, I'm concerned with projecting my take of a iconic character within a setting of my choosing, at a level of my choosing through Aberrant's rules (mostly, until my grumpy old man syndrome kicks in and I start tweaking or performing system surgery).
