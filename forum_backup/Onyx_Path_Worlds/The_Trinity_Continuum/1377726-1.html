        If you're a mercenary, you probably have an applicable Role or Society Path. You get your gun as starting equipment for that path. If you need a new gun during play, you use your Path contact to get one.

Also, anyone can afford a crap knife since they're effectively free. Take the core book's example knife and remove most of the tags, thereby giving you a free shiv.

                Paths specifically guiding starting equipment is on page 36 of TC core. Getting equipment through Paths is on page 40 via the Access Connection.

                The Wealth Edge specifies that Wealth represents extra disposable income, not the money necessary for your basic needs (and that you can take Complications if you don't have enough Wealth to buy something outright).

                While any weapon is theoretically bound by the weapon design rules, a character isn't bound by them for a weapon they didn't buy. If the PCs take down a rich mobster and find a cache of high end weapons, the weapons don't magically become worse because they don't have a mob boss's Wealth (which would be a GM fiat decision anyway since Wealth is a PC mechanic not a NPC one). So if the mob boss is rich enough to qualify as Wealth 5, that weapon cache having some weapons with 6 points worth of tags in them that the PCs without Wealth 5 take? Completely legit.

                I'm really not sure why people want to fight the game on this. Trinity isn't a gritty survival horror game where you need to carefully shepherd equipment as a vital resource. TC is an action adventure romp. Characters have the basic tools of their trade so they can go have cool adventures.

                        I think the trick to starting a game with appropriate equipment from your paths is to assume you have reasonable possessions for those paths without going full munchkin, since that'll annoy the storyguide.

                        Are you an international jewel thief? You've got a high end (but not top tier) B&E kit.

                        Are you a getaway driver? You start with a fast car, but not some sort of super Wealth 5 car. If you want the Wealth 5 car, that is an in-game goal.

                        Are you a fighter pilot? You have a light civilian or antique military aircraft you take out for a spin on the weekends, but it won't have functional weapons unless you add them in-game.

                        Are you a computer hacker? You've got an expensive new PC with some illegal hacking programs installed, but you're not starting with your own private supercomputer (supercomputer access is gained on a per case basis via your contacts).

                        Are you a PC? (I'll assume the answer is yes). You start with at least one appropriate weapon/armor option; hackers/medics should get a taser or holdout pistol and bulletproof vest, while combat oriented party members likely carry several heavier weapons and better armor. These probably won't be top of the line unless they expend points on appropriate edges, since getting more/better equipment should be a campaign subgoal.
