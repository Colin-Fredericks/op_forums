    So we DO NOT know anything concrete about TC Aegis era 'Bronze Age S-F' or it's fundamentals, even. But let's filled the long months waiting for the Aegis to finish with our fan musings how various Mythical beings can work in the Trinity Continuum world of Bronze Age. 😎

    Here is great source for Greek myths wiki in general - https://www.theoi.com/greek-mythology/nymphs.html

    I'm coming with assumption that general templates divide is this:

    Talents / Daredevils / Squires = Heroes
    Novas / Stalwarts = Gods / Demigods
    Psiads (/Psions?) = Wizards and mystics

    Starting topic with various...

    NYMPHS
    Nymphs are various lesser goddesses, 'youngest' in godly family. They are Quantum users, limited always to having maximum Quantum 1 and cannot pass beyond that. However, they are very hard to detect even as Quantum users - they have +3 bonus on Clash of Wills to get to overpower them in detection. 😎

                                          I tend to agree with the people who think gods and things won't be modeled in Aegis as a playable character type. Beyond just balance and things, if the game is set in the time of the Trojan war you're not dealing with Greek gods, but Mycenaean ones and we don't really know a ton about them.

                                          Like a lot of the same names appear, but Poseidon is the leader of the Gods, not Zeus, and he's more (or equally?) famous for causing earthquakes. Aphrodite hasn't been fully integrated into the pantheon yet from her likely Phoenician origins as Astarte. Dionysus is an extremely fringe wine cult figure who threatens orderly society and is more associated with madness than party fun times. Hades seemingly doesn't exist. Demeter and Persephone might be wives of Poseidon. Persephone might be the goddess of death and the underworld. Hermes and Pan might still be the same god (I forget when we think they started to separate).

                                          Greek mythology is wildly fluid over time and we tend to project the later shape of things backwards and assume it was always that way.

                                          It is waaaaay easier to just deal with the semi-historical heroes and people from a pretty amazing period of history than tangle with the rats nest of nearly unrecognizable Mycenaean mythology. Also, while this isn't a huge danger for Mycenaean or Ancient Greek religion, trying to mash real world religions into game systems/settings can easily become wildly offensive. I remember someone commented that the 1st Edition of Scion's take on Zoroastrian and Hindu pantheons managed to be blasphemous to followers of both religions, which was impressive considering neither faith has a concept of blasphemy.

                                          The same holds true for "the gods were aliens!" which would be very genre appropriate, but treads really close to a lot of the problematic issues around most ancient alien conspiracy theories.

                                          Let the gods be myths and stories to explain what is happening in the world. People might worship Poseidon at a cave that earthquakes seem to originate from and a voice demands sacrifices of metals and food, but it's because there is a crashed alien spaceship with amphibious horselike aliens inside, every time they run an engine test they cause an earthquake, and they're trying to get supplies so they can fix their ship. The aliens don't have anything to do with Poseidon, but the locals assume they do because they're trying to make sense of something they don't understand.

                                                  There are 4 main options to bring "gods" to the game not being our characters:
                                                  The first is the gods are aliens that may mate or impersonate or dominate humans pretending to be gods (they are something between Marvel gods and Stargate Goauld).
                                                  The second is we have alien "gods", but they are not human at all, they are Mythos material and our heroes are the humans standing against them (think something Krull).
                                                  The third is the gods are the a few of the stalwarts that become so powerful they become true novas (or psiads that reached Proxy level) (Something close to Xena and Hercules tv series, but based in scientific premices).
                                                  The forth is the gods are not real, they are based in and the stories of Zeus as described in the mythlogy are not real, but just stories told so many times its scale grew out of its original proportions (you can lift a big rock, but as the stories were told over time that rock become a montain), the ones that inspired the stories are long dead and our heros are their descendents (if not by blood, by concept).

                                                  I am in favor of the 4th because we dont need ancient Novas, we need just powerful characters that inpire local stories, wth different heroes inspireing different stories all around the world instead of the Monomyth that you have with novas capable of moving the moon and things like that.
