                    Well, I don't actually play most of the other games but I can respond to the Scion question of why a new edition.

                    First, and likely the most important being that Scion as it is, was made as a limited game, they never intended it to go past the first 3 books but added the 2 additional books (Companion and Ragnarok) due to overwhelming fan interest....and I can only assume the Yazata file was put together for the same reason, though if you look through it the meat of it was the important part to get out as unlike the other books, they spent exactly 0 hours on the art (Look through the other books and you'll find that, unless I missed something, every single picture in that file is from another book). It is a new company that is starting over and are, I am assuming here, looking to expand past the limited line into the ability to continue the game further on down the road. So in all honesty instead of looking at it as a 2nd Edition, you can really look at it as a Remake.

                    Second, as said in other posts, the scaling for the game is kinda broken. They are also missing things like decent crafting rules for someone who actually wants to play a god of crafting, some rather iconic weapons and armor for various pantheons (Seriously....who the hell doesn't put stats for the most widely recognized armor in the whole of the Roman Empire in the book when you have the Dodekatheon as a Pantheon...that one irritates me more than it should but screw it *L*), and a number of other tiny things that could just really benefit from being well rounded out.

                    Third, as said earlier they are also including rules for playing some "Lower Level" type characters, non-scions, pre-visitation scions and whatnot (Who here hasn't been wondering what it'd be like to play a Sorcerer or a Tengu in a Scion setting?).

                    Fourth, and this one is just speculation here but with all the added space in the books, perhaps along with the revamping of the Pantheons, giving them a nice new polish and whatnot they'll be including more gods in the list (Sekhmet, Thanatos, Hathor, Eris ect ect)

                    I'll be your Huckleberry

                                            As you've said Chattnos, all of that had been long answered, but thanks for your 2 bits anyway.

                                            Never quite sure why people seem to take offense at honest inquiries, but sorry if I hurt a feeling there somewhere.

                                            I've been a gamer for a long time and I've seen a lot of new versions of a book come out.

                                            Sometimes it is only the rules that change. Sometimes drastically and sometimes just little tweaks that a FAQ could have handled.

                                            Sometimes it is the entire concept that changes...the flavor, the major parties, the whole setting. Sometimes it is just an expansion of the timeline...the old book was the setting 10 years in the past, the new book is "now".

                                            I was simply trying to gauge where these revisions of books I already own fall into those spectrums.

                                            In a perfect world, I'd buy them all.

                                            It's not a perfect world. Despite a good income and a huge game room lined with bookcases, I've come to the point where I have to make hard choices. What gets replaced? What goes to the basement? What finally prompts me to try to sell something on Ebay instead of just buy?

                                            This is where I'm at.

                                            With the books that are getting ready to go into 2nd Editions that I was asking about, I already have pretty much everything that has been put out for them in the 1st Edition...and I've loved those books and games.

                                            If these 2nd Editions were simply an expansion on the well written books that I already loved, then it would be a no brainer...gotta buy.

                                            If these are starting from the ground up, ignoring everything that had come before in the books that I already own and love, then they are just going to have to really be over and above good to find a spot on my shelves and get me to remove what is already there. I'm not saying these authors can't do that. But in my case they have an uphill battle (not that it needs to mean a lick to them). Because they aren't just trying to wow me with a cool and original concept (like the Demon the Descent as 50's Cold War-esque intrigue). No, they have to sell me on why their concept of a Promethean is better than the concept of a Promethean that I've already invested so much time, love, and money into.

                                            This is where I find myself in life right now, and why I asked the questions that I did.

                                            As I've said from the beginning...in no way was this meant as an edition war debate. I simply needed to know what "2nd Edition" meant in the specific cases of each of these books.

                                            Thanks for all of the answers directed at the intent of the post.

                                            It has been most helpful.

                                            I love everything I have from White-Wolf and Onyx Path. Thanks for decades of fun!

                                                The purpose of a second edition isn't just to update things - though, that's a big part of it. When Aberrant was originally published, reality TV hadn't yet hit the heights it would, and we certainly didn't have the Kardashians.

                                                Nor is it to holistically incorporate what's come up in supplements into the core game experience - though that's a big part too. You mention Promethean, but the Second Edition has the various sourcebook Lineages and Refinements as part of core gameplay.

                                                If those were simple date changes or copy paste jobs, yeah, I can see your issue. But another edition is a full refocus of the line's themes, mechanics, and play experience. In Trinity's case, it's a way to reconcile and enrich all three games by interlinking them, rather than having winks and nods in the books. Demon the Descent isn't a different take on Demon the Fallen - it's drawing on similar source material to create a whole new game. Politics on a space station? Babylon 5 and DS9. Second Edition? Battlestar Galactica and nuBSG.

                                                And no, we won't take your books away. But the second edition is a chance to more deeply and fully explore some themes the first editions didn't get around to (for many reasons) while refocusing the core play experience.
