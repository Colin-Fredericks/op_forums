    So, minor mechanical note - The Psion template re-introduces the old issue where the order in which you buy things matters. It's only a trivial amount, but literally every character should be taking an extra dot in Psi from the Prometheus Chamber and then if you really need it, buying that Attribute later to save 2 experience. Not a big deal, but a bit weird that there's a hard-and-fast mechanically optimum route.

    Also, I like that most characters should be starting with 20 experience. That's a lot of options (And if you followed the advice above means that you can start at Psi 4, with an extra Mode dot, if you're going full bore on the Psi abilities. Or you can start with Psi 3 and a Mode at 5, giving you Psionic Dysfunction, which was very fun in 1st ed).

                                        Originally posted by Lian View Post
                                        I am a little confused how one becomes a psion in relation to the Orders. As a latent do you sign up for the Order and then before being dunked have the option of swapping to a different tank or do you generally sign up with an order and some people leave and join others? Similar sort of question for freelance psychics..
                                        Okay, take 2 - bigger screen, bigger keyboard.

                                        The answer is that there are several pathways. You can sign up with an Order, get swapped around, or leave. None of them have a hold on you forever. I should also point out that all of this is regarding in-character perspective. Out of character, there’s nothing to prevent the player making any Aptitude* Psion work for any Order (or AEon).

                                        * Barring Quantakinetics, which have a few more considerations.

                                        Building on what I said in my previous post. Say you live in a major city with a choice of testing facilities. You head over to one, looking to be tested. They discover that you’re a latent. Congratulations! You might have generally even latency, or you might be strongly disposed to one Aptitude. The testing facility will discuss this with you and will probably offer to send you on for more screening to see if you can join the Order. Every Order has tests and standards that you have to pass before they’ll accept you. If you’re a latent and they decide you don’t pass, they won’t activate you, even if you have strong latency for that Aptitude. Sucks for you, but from their point better to have a disgruntled latent who might cause some minor trouble, than a disgruntled psion who might cause big trouble later on. Or, you might not meet their standards, but you’ll easily pass the requirements of another Order. Then they might refer you along,

                                        If you’ve tested strongly for an Aptitude other than what they do, they may offer to either arrange to have you triggered and receive basic training by the other Order, as part of the reciprocation arrangements, or you might decide that you’re more interested in what they provide. Either way, you tend to have a choice. Circumstances might occasionally dictate otherwise, and you can build character backgrounds around that, but generally in 2nd edition any Aptitude can (and do) work for any Order.

                                        If you get tested for AEon, the process might be a little different. They don’t have a Prometheus Chamber of their own, so they’ll have to send you out for someone else to trigger. If you meet AEon’s standards, they might recruit and train you, then send you for external psionic training. If you don’t, or you’re not interested, they’ll put you in contact with the Order of your choice, and the previous process takes place.

                                        I think between the two posts I’ve covered the same ground in a could of different ways. Hopefully that helps.

                                            02-21-2018, 02:44 AM
                                            Originally posted by Lian View Post
                                            So its not assumed the would be psion has some sort of debt to a group for going through the process?
                                            Depends on what you’re asking.

                                            If you’re asking if a character has a debt to someone simply for being triggered into an Aptitude - then generally yes, to varying degrees. Every Order has to invest time and effort into their psions, even if it’s just the use of the Prometheus Chamber and basic training. If you pass screening, and they offer to trigger you, then you’ll tend to have some kind of obligation. The Legions expect a three year term of service, Orgotek will have contractural expectations and penalty clauses if you leave early etc. ISRA tends to let you wander off if you want, because regardless of where you go and what you do, you’re always part of the totality and therefore (in their minds) part of the broader Order.

                                            From your character’s background point of view, these terms are usually short and can be woven into the story. (I did my tour of duty, then decided to go freelance when the time was up.) Or you can make it a source of conflict for your character. (I went AWOL before my tour was done, and though the Legions couldn’t be bothered chasing me down, if I cross paths with them they’ll probably take me in to either complete my time, or go to prison.)

                                            If you’re asking whether the character has obligation beyond her Order (or AEon) if she’s triggered by another Order as part of the reciprocal arrangmements, then no - all debts in this case are held by the Orders, not the individuals.

                                            Make sense? Have I answered the right question?

                                                    Originally posted by TheSarcasticSage View Post
                                                    I have a question about this, I know the in game reasons for this, but are there out of game reasons as well? (ie. Are they more powerful then other Aptitudes? Are they troublesome/don't work well with others in group play, etc)
                                                    I have no idea what the 1st Ed OOC reasons were. Since we never saw a complete write-up of QK, it’s possible the answer was one or all of what you’ve said.

                                                    In 2nd ed, QK is balanced against the other Aptitudes. It’s more powerful in its area of expertise, but the same is true for every aptitude. It’s arguably slightly more powerful, to justify its 3 Psi minimum requirement.

                                                    The QK powers are such that there are certainly reasons for others to fear them, but considering a powerful PK could break you into bloody chunks, or a powerful telepath could destroy your mind, any powerful, ill-intentioned psion is a threat.

                                                    A few more days and you’ll see what I mean.

                                                    Originally posted by TheSarcasticSage View Post
                                                    Also, can you tell us what media you used as inspiration when designing the different things that an Aptitude can do/should feel like in use? (ie. Did you use the movie Jumper for the look, or feel, of Teleportation, etc.)
                                                    Trinity 1st edition was the primary point of inspiration. But for the feel of various powers... it’s hard to say exactly. I have very clear images of how they work and appear in my head, and I can trace some of these back to media and inspirations, but not everything. Jumper works for teleportation, sure, but so does Dr Strange. (The rapid one-after-the-other tactical jumps with vehicles are probably beyond the power scope, but personal rapid jump isn’t.)

                                                    Vitakinesis drew on the Captain America MCU movies - especially the enhanced human aspects. Telepathy tends to be unflashy, but Babylon 5’s Psi Corps has nailed it pretty well. Clairsentience draws on Leverage and Person of Interest, in a strange kind of way.
