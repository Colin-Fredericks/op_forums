    Man Amplifiers

    10-17-2014, 06:07 AM
    I've been GMing a game of Aeon Trinity (under the Storyteller system) for a few months now, and I have noticed that powered armour (or armor if you don't use UK English) isn't really an element of the setting. Besides the once off freak of nature that is the bioVAS. So that got me to wonder why powered armour took such a backseat in this setting? I can understand for the purposes of game that its about the Psions and not about the Tech (thankyou FAQ website), though I have found my group of psions are squishy compared to just a single abby. For those who know the abberant Anders Nash from the Darkness Revealed Adventures he is basically the reason my group has a pathological fear of fighting aberrants. If he is an example of a run of the mill abberant what chance if hell does a group of diversified Psions have in killing him? I even toned down his healing factor because he almost TPKed the group on his own with just his powers.

    This brings me to the subject of armours. Alot of the reason the team were so squishy is that the standard armours in the core book are incredibly lackluster. Sure the team is good against normal people and other psions but against aberrants they might as well nuke the site from orbit and move on with their lives which is infact who they killed Nash in the end, by blowing up the Freya with him on board. Now it can be argued that Psions are meant to go toe to toe with aberrants but then there is a major conceit of the Trinity setting proven wrong right there. That Psions (not plural) can stand up to an abby.

    Though I am still willing to believe that the groups inexperience may be a major contributing factor. So my real reason for this topic is to hear thoughts on the subject of powered armour and defences in this setting and what older more experienced GMs them myself did.

    Note that this is intended to be a discussion on two fronts, effective aberrant counter measures and power armour, not about VARGS or vehicles brought to bear against abbies. It goes rather against the mayor conceit of the setting if psions can't fight your average aberrant even when that abby is outnumbred four too one.

      I saw 'Man Amplifiers' and really expected to see a very different type of post

      My take. And spoilers for those that haven't played the DR books or read the old metaplot.

      The abbie to Psion ratio of it taking a well co-ordinated party of Psions to put down one low-mid level aberrant has always been a key part of the appeal for me. Aberrants are more powerful, have been around longer and should be bloody dangerous - psions are weaker, their powers are artificially restricted (see the Chambers / meta plot). Psions need BFG's plus psi powers, rapid thinking and teamwork to stay alive.

      Psions are not weak compared to neutrals of course, but they are not as potent in terms of raw power as Aberrants.

      I've run the same encounter you mentioned a few times now (love the DR stuff), and in every case that fight is a huge, huge deal. But, the players made it through by being inventive. If they go for a straight up toe-to-toe combat they're going to be very badly hurt - and they should be. Psions need to be smarter or they get killed. That combat is brutal, its truly desperate, its in close confines as the proverbial is about to hit the fan. The characters are scrambling to stay alive when its all going to hell. Characters need to come up with every trick they could think of and be well coordinated to put Anders Nash down or get out of his way. While challenging, this is also what makes that fight truly memorable.

      I had the approach that the party was by the end of the fight likely to be down to their last health levels, last few rounds of ammo, and barely any psi and / or willpower left. If they survived they would be exhausted - and yes some may even die. IIRC in the plot they then get to stay with the Chinese on Mars as they are 'processed' so a bit of downtime can take care of particularly bad injuries, psi pool and willpower issues.

      Even 10+ years after running that for the first time I still have players talk about what an absolute SOB Anders Nash was - but they do so with a sense of "man we pulled that off - god knows how but we did, but we did!". At the time, of course, the reaction was "what the hell aggggh god gettitoffmeshootit!!!?!??!", heh.

      Answering your other question regarding, ahem, man amplifiers, I'm not especially keen on seeing a single Psion (or even a couple of them) being able to put down an abbie. I've always taken it as Aberrants being the (Ridley Scott) 'Alien' of the Trinity setting (which makes Khantze Lu Ge absolutely terrifying).

      As a garden variety party of low to mid level psions, if you're up against an Aberrant you may loose people. You may barely come out of the fight alive. It should be very hard. Power armo(u)r reduces the threat. Ramp up the power of psions too much and you run the risk of making Aberrants, if not trivial, 'just another monster'. That cheapens them, to me.

      Aberrants are the elemental threat to humanity. They damn near destroyed the human race before, there's every chance they could do so in the future. Players should be terrified of their characters fighting Aberrants. Doesn't mean they get to avoid fighting them though, and it doesn't mean they'll survive when it goes down.

      Now, Aeon has tons of other (somewhat) less terrifying / powerful challenges for psions to deal with so the stakes aren't always so high (neuts, rogue psions, chromies, whack-job security officers, option-8, merc teams, crackpot governments and so on). Because of all that I've not felt the need to bring Aberrants into a game too often - and Aberrants are not, IMO, the real focus of the DR games in any event - they were set special pieces.

      I keep Aberrants as deadly, as something very special, something that should get every player in the group swearing loudly when they realize they're up against one. I wouldn't want to see power armo(u)r showing up except in very rare circumstances.

          CHILL has it right: Any individual full-on Aberrants are most likely going to turn any individual psion into paste. But bear in mind that the prevailing theme of the game is Unity. Psions are designed (both in and out of character) to require them to work together to bring down powerful Aberrants. While not as clearly one-sided, even weaker sub-Aberrant mutants should still be a challenge to the lone psion.

          Securing humanity's future means working together, both at the scale of psions and up at the scale of the psi orders and world governments. The Aeon Trinity works at both levels to help make that happen.

                      Galaxy Master Zhad, Not sure if this is any use to you.

                      One of the things I do as a GM when using pivotal scenes like the fight with Anders Nash is basically gloss over the rules and focus on tone and outcome. I use the stats presented to get a sense of what the writer of the scenario had in mind, then use that as inspiration for making it up as I go along in the scene - not that the players know I'm doing that. For individual major challenges like Anders Nash, it doesn't matter if I use the 'rules' - it matters how the players feel when they come out of it at the end. For that encounter (based on my opinion of the 'Alien movie' nature of how Aberrants should be presented), I wanted player's characters to come out of it dreading another round, and mortally terrified of Aberrants.

                      It does mean I can't rely on games mechanics to 'decide' if a PC ends up dead or alive - that has to be my call as a result of that style of GMing. My approach is to avoid TPK's, but absolutely deal fairly harshly when a player goes all gung-ho or does something really stupid (I have little patience for 'crazy' players at the best of times). The few times I've run that particular encounter there's been no character deaths. It has been very, very close - and unless my players are reading this now they would have no clue that I fudged the whole thing.

                      They just came away with 'Aberrant = brown trousers time'.

                      Of course, you can't fudge it like that with more 'routine' bad guys as inconsistency starts to creep in and can drag a game down and creates a sense of no control for players, but for things like Anders Nash - hell yeah.

                                                      I also took some time out to hypothesis the "ranks" of armours;
                                                      Ratings have been adjusted to reflect a broader range of armour types. Basic armour covers ratings 1 to 2, this reflects the protection provided by simple or crude defensive measure or means. These armours are typical of a civilian type and are the most common. Intermediate armour covers ratings 3 to 4, these are materials or mechanisms designed to take some punishment our to counter light arms. These armous are typically employed by private security or special task forces. Advanced armour covers ratings 5 to 6, these are typically full body suits usually employed by various armed forces or thick ablative layers or active defence countermeasures. Resistances of these levels are meant to deter light weapons fire as such armours of this rank is normally heavily restricted and used almost exclusively by the military. Superior armours cover rating 7 to 8 though theoretically 9 and 10 are available as well (however armour ratings of 9 to 10 should be an exception to an already exceptional situation), these ratings reflect the very, very best in personal defence and as such is not obtainable over regular channels. These are exotic materials or technologies that can only be acquired through the Storyteller's intervention and as such should be used sparingly.
                                                      An armour may be modified or improved in some way to give it a higher rating, however under no condition can such improvement increase the armour's rating by more than 1. Armours of different ranks do not stack atop each other unless stated otherwise in the description.
                                                      An armour's Lethal soak will usually be higher then it's Bashing soak, this is because Lethal damage is a greater threat to the user's life while dealing with relatively minor blows is not the most important feature of armour designs. There are of course exceptions.

                                                      I also came up with some of my own armours in 3-6 range but have made sure that they are restricted and not commercially available. It is my intent to make sure the PCs don't get anything in the 5+ range for longer than small part in any story.
