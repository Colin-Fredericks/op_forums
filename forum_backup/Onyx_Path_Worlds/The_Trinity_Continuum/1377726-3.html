        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

        Please cite a passage that says you use Wealth to determine starting equipment, and then you can call it a contradiction
        In fact, the Weapon Design rules, under Weapons, is the place that states what tags your equipment can have. Notably, these rules are not even bounded by such things as "starting equipment" or "acquiring resources in play through Access." So your requirement is irrelevant. The king rules here are the rules under Weapons. No other rule in the game, as best I can determine, modifies, overrides, comments on, or nullifies those rules. They aren't rules of Wealth. They are the very rules of weapons themselves.

        Further, the idea of Paths granting equipment is based on several spurious assumptions. Sure, a cop gets their gun and badge from an allegiance. But what kind of gun? Going by the rulebook, it is stated plainly that the tags of that gun depend on the cop's Wealth. There is no rule anywhere modifying that. And what if your background is being a criminal? You don't get a semi-automatic pistol by being assigned by it an agency. You own it, even though, like most criminals, you don't have considerable personal wealth.

        Further, further, the Weapons rules specify that each weapon has unique attributes. The weapon table is identified as a table of example weapons. If you assume you just have stuff based on your paths, you are now treating a table of example, custome-designed weapons as though it were a standard equipment table, like those found in other role-playing games, and then handing them out arbitrarily based on a character's background story. The weapons, at that point, are not connected to any dots in anything, and have arbitrary statistics.

        I'm very discouraged at this point. Even after comments by devs, it's not clear what weapons are. Let me lay out three options:

        1) In a game like Cortex, weapons aren't real. You might have a trait like Glock d8, but that doesn't mean that other characters don't have Glocks. That's just the benefit your Glock gives to you. NPC mobs don't have Glocks at all in that sense. Other characters might shoot a Glock, but they don't have a Glock trait. They might just roll their Soldier d8 trait or their Marksmanship or whatever, but a gun doesn't have any traits at all unless you make it a signature piece of equipment or create it during play as a Resource. In Trinity terms, any character could just roll Aim + Dexterity at any time and declare they are firing a gun. AND I DON'T KNOW WHETHER THIS IS TRUE IN TRINITY FROM READING THE RULES.

        2) In a game like Savage Worlds, weapons are real. Every weapon has a cost in dollars, a damage die, a range, et cetera. You acquire a gun out of your starting equipment allowance, and all guns of the same type you find in the game have the same statistics. As written, the rules seem to suggest this is not how Trinity works.

        3) In a game like M&M, or Champions, there are charts of standardized weapons. There is a light pistol, and all light pistols wielded by common criminals have these statistics, unless the GM gives the guns different statistics. But your gun might have any statistics you give it, using example weapons only for inspiration. The GM might ask, "Why does your light pistol have Damage 6?" and this is a fair question, but there's nothing in the game from forbidding it. To me, this is how Trinity appears to be written, or at least what the book implies to me. Weapons are somewhat "real" but your weapon is a customized trait that does whatever you say it does.

        If we assume option 1 is the case for Trinity, that solves a lot of problems. Any given melee weapon needs maybe one tag for how it wielded (Close, Two-Handed), and any given ranged weapon really just needs a Range. But you could just say, "I shoot a gun" (Aim + Dexterity) and gain no Enhancement, while also incurring no Range penalties or otherwise dealing with the description of the gun. This works just like someone using Larceny and stating they are using lockpicks, normal un-enhanced lockpicks. The main problem, as I noted above, is that this creates an arbitrary relationship between Wealth and firepower. A commando and a millionaire might both have a knife, and purely because of how Weapon Design works, the millionaire's knife is much, much better.

        Bunyip seems to suggest Paths can just hand out appropriate equipment. Apart from being, you know, not in the rules (other than saying, ok, yeah, characters have resources), this relies on Option 2, weapons are real. Otherwise, the free weapons you do or don't get, don't have any statistics. Because Option 3 suggests the example weapons are, just that, examples, and don't really traits you can rely on because of your character. To me, Bunyip's suggestion does not seem relevant to the Trinity Continuum core rulebook. Bunyip's suggestion amounts to, essentially, we have no idea what tags someone's weapon has, based on anything, the Story Guide just sort of approves or doesn't approve weapons with an arbitrary number of tags. During play, you can use Access to access other weapons, using a non-predetermined difficulty, to acquire other weapons with an arbitrary and undetermined number of tags. Because there is nowhere in the book that says, "This is what the capability of a Rifle is." A Rifle has whatever capabilities it has, according to the tags you assign it.

        Almost all the advice I'm getting that has any utility suggests that people are handling weapons as they are handled in other games, and now how they are handled in TC. It's not "bad" advice, it's just not advice for a game in which Weapon Design is a heading nestled under Weapons, and which says you custom design weapons by assigning them a number of tags based on your Wealth, and in which page 36 specifies, in a vague sort of way, that Paths provide Resources, but only providing rules for asking for things during play and then without any suggestion of standard difficulties.

        In my view, this topic requires substantial clarification and/or errata for TC to be usable "out of the box" without leaving GMs and players adrift.

        EDIT: Note that I did not create this topic, I found it while searching the forums trying to figure out how this stuff is supposed to work.
