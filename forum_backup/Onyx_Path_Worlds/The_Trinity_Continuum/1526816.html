    Did the weapon rules ever get some clarification?

    12-14-2023, 06:29 PM
    There's some other stuff I'm curious about, too. But this is kind of emblematic for me. Yesterday I got Aberrant and Adventure! in the mail. It's been, well, quite a long time since I've looked at this rules-set. The last time, I had some questions about how a mercenary would get an assault rifle. It was difficult for me to understand how the rules were supposed to work. According to the rules:
    1. A weapon is a thing that has a number of tags equal to your Wealth.
    2. The assault rifle, one of the worked examples, has too many tags for a character with any normal level of Wealth. It's a billionaire weapon.

    That doesn't seem to work. So I explored a few different ideas:
    1. Maybe weapons don't have fixed stats, and they really do just have a number of tags equal to your Wealth, even if they differ from the example.
    2. Maybe there is some update to the rules somewhere.
    3. Weapons do have fixed stats, but you have to acquire them in a different way.
    4. I'm on my own, it just doesn't make sense and it's not going to make sense. The Wealth rules and the weapon rules refer to each other but don't actually work as written. It's a persistent bug.

    The last time I checked, the answer was, "You get whatever weapons your Path would imply." I found that unsatisfactory. First of all, some characters, like a mercenary, have equipment independent of membership in a faction. Second, it doesn't really answer how you get stats for weapons granted by an organization. Really, for any weapon at all, since a weapon is defined as having tags equal to your wealth, but especially with weapons not already statted. And third, virtually every other bit of social power you have is codified and quantified. You have contacts, you have Edges, you have relationships and bonds, pretty much everything you can do has a dot rating assigned to it. So it seems apparent to me that if membership in a Path grants you weapons or other equipment, there should be a chart you could refer to somewhere that tells you what they grant. Or maybe there's an Edge that turns you into a walking arsenal, like a real action hero, even if your personal wealth isn't otherwise that impressive.

    So. In the last couple of years, any movement on this topic?

    I'm a long-time fan of Aberrant and Adventure!. But I already quit, basically, in frustration in understanding this aspect of the rules. Not to mention the accusation that for some reason I would come here just to be willfully obtuse, and not simply to understand how the game works. I feel so out of the loop as to what has happened in the last couple of years, and how people play the game. I poked through the first couple of pages in this subforum, just to see what was being talked about. I also tried some searches. I didn't find the information I was looking for. Perhaps it is because people are still satisfied, or dissatisfied, with the answers they get the first time around. But it could also be that having been away from the forums so long, I just don't know where to dig up the current consensus on the topic, or any rulings by the devs.

          I must agree with you on the Path X Wealth system, and how it makes wealth useless about 90% of the time. So here is my approach on how to mend this from my House Rules book (check signature).

          Wealth take the importance of paths away, by removing the link between the path and available equipment to the character. To avoid this, the Wealth edge and the cost system must change.
          Instead of using wealth for everything, the character uses Resources, grouping in a single value connections, favors and money. For that, not all resources are available to the character at same time, money is money, and can be used to buy anything, but its impossible to use the technological background to find a farm in the countryside for a good price, or the best place to buy illegal heavy weapons.

          BUYING EQUIPMENT
          To buy an item, the character must have enough resources, that may include political power, knowing people and, of course, money. Resource is the sum of Wealth and the highest relevant path, for example, to buy a popular car the character would use the suburbia path, while to buy luxury cars would use the life of privilege path.
          To buy an item, the character compare the item cost to the related resources, with 3 possible results.
          Cost being higher than the resources: the character can’t buy it at all.
          Cost being lower than the resources: the character can buy it with no other effect.
          Cost being equal to the resources: the character can buy the item, but for the rest of the session the resources are reduced.
          Reduced resources means the character lose access to the Loaded Enhanced Edge, if don't have it, the character loses 1 dot of Wealth, to a minimum of 0, if it would drop wealth to negative value, the character uses one of the path connection uses of the session.
          What exactly the used path means is up to the player and Storyguide, it can be some borrowed money from a family or friend, a bank loan using the character job or friends as references, some favors that were owned form some time ago, or even the character knowing the seller.
          Any item with cost L is treated as cost 7, requiring 7 dots combined between Path and Wealth, or the Loaded Enhanced Edge and a sum of 5 dots between Path and Wealth. This way, even a Loaded character may be incapable of buying extremely expensive (L cost items) if they have no relevant contacts (a relevant path at 2+ dots).
          MODIFIED EDGES
          Some Edges are modified to fit better on the new concept.
          Wealth: Wealth becomes a 1 to 3 dot edge (it was 1 to 5 dots), representing the money the character have available to buy any kind of equipment or object, not related to paths.
          Loaded: The requisite changes to Wealth 3 dots from Wealth 3 dots. It will grant +2 into the character buying capacity, instead of allowing the character to buy items with cost L.

              On weapons specifically, I have the following interpretation.
              There are the standard equipment, like a regular .38 or what ever that have the proper use tag and maybe one more, for example for a gun you have Ranged and Deadly. Most guns are like this, even the ones that are particularly well made or with some nice designs, even most rifles in a war are like this.
              There are also the ones that our characters use, that was individually made to fit a purpose, for example, you have have a sniper rifle as you are a sniper in the platoon, it’s not a standard rifle, it’s a special one, with a few special tags, like a telescopic aim. You can also have some special guns like the ones from the Olympic shooters, their guns are made to fit their hands and to be specially precise. If you have enough money you buy those items for you just because you are rich enough and these are your work tools.
              There Are also Super science items that are basically prototypes and you can have a few because you are a Talent, having access to Advanced (like any rich person) and Inspired (because you know a guy or you invented it) super science devices. Those have special tricks and things, like the ones the R&D guy delivers to 007 for the missions.

              So Basically my view you are not a level 1 character, you are a higher level character walking around with some Magical items from the moment your character is created.

                              So I wonder if anything further has ever been said about how to handle weapons, consciously custom or otherwise.

                              To review, p. 123 says
                              WEAPON CREATION
                              A character’s weapon should be as special and unique as the character itself. Many of the greatest characters in fiction are known for their iconic weapons. In some cases, these weapons can even become characters unto themselves. In the Trinity Continuum, every weapon is custom designed in two very simple steps. First players choose the damage type, and then they purchase tags for the weapon. By default, all weapons give an Enhancement bonus of 1 and are close range only.
                              WEAPON TAGS
                              Weapon tags are what truly customize the weapon. Weapon tags are dependent on a character’s Wealth Edge. Weapons start with a single tag point, and each dot of Wealth gives the weapon an additional tag point. Some tags cost more than one point. Weapons of higher Scale add their Scale rating directly to the maximum number of tags they can possess. Storyguides are the final arbiters of what tags can be combined and should think about the tone of their game when deciding what combinations of tags they will allow.
                              p.125 has a page of Example Weapons. One of them is an assault rifle with 7 tags.

                              So to review, if you play an action cop with Wealth 1, you get an "iconic weapon" with 2 tags.

                              According to at least one of the devs, you can simply use your Path to gain access to a weapon. If successful, you gain a gun. According to the rules, if you successfully requisition a gun, it has 2 tags. Because in Trinity Continuum, every weapon is custom designed in two very simple steps. Those steps are choosing a damage type, and assigning a number of Tags equal to 1 + your Wealth. But somehow, you can request a more powerful weapon through your Path. Maybe even an assault rifle with 7 tags. Which somehow, unlike every other weapon in Trinity Continuum, isn't custom designed in two very simple steps.

                              If weapons aren't always designed in two very simple steps, where do their stats come from? Because the rules for Weapons say every weapon is custom designed. If they are provided by GM fiat, what is a weapon? There are no rules provided for how a GM allocates tags to weapons.

                              All I can gather is that, from my experience playing D&D and GURPS, I'm supposed to intuit the Example Weapons is actually an equipment list, which is treasure doled out by the GM, and weapons NOT custom designed in two very simple steps, they have fixed or GM-assigned ratings that exist entirely outside the world of customization. The rules for Weapon Creation are basically non-functional and are never used. Is that it? Am I just really obtuse, that I don't understand how this is supposed to work?

                              I just find this so perplexing. I really wanted to get into the system, and I backed in Kickstarter. But here I am, staring at a section of rules that doesn't seem to work. TC is a baroque, powerful system that has rules for everything from developing relationships to combat. But right here, when it comes to the question, "What is a gun?," as question surely relevant to what is often used to create modern action adventure games, the rules as written don't seem to be able to provide an answer. Why don't the rules do what they say, and say what they mean? I'm just so turned off, not just by the text of the rules, but by the stunning silence, like these questions aren't even worth asking.

                              It isn't a matter of weapons being "limited" by Wealth or even being a matter of affording one. Weapons are defined by Wealth, and nothing else. And yet all the answers I've gotten previously is that the rules don't mean what they appear to say. I can't get past character creation without likely hitting this stumbling block. And all that Path stuff aside, how do you actually design a character, within the rules, that privately owns a pistol with a decent number of tags that represents their own, personal, "iconic" weapon, as promised by the Weapon Creation rules?

                                                      COMMON AND SPECIAL ITEMS
                                                      This system makes a huge change in the entire equipment idea, giving the Paths a huge impor- tance into what the character have and how they might get access to certain items.

                                                      COMMON AND SPECIAL
                                                      Mundane equipment is divided in 2 different tiers, Common and Special equipments.
                                                      Common equipment is any equipment that people usually have available at home or can easily buy. This division is not about its cost, but about how common the items are, a car can be as common as clothes or handguns, it is all a matter of how commonly it is to find those items in people homes and for sale in the market. Common equipment are worth up to 2 tags.
                                                      Special equipment is any equipment that is hardly find in peoples houses, be it because they are made on demand, illegal or have a very niche use. Again, it is not about the price of the equipment, but about how rarely they are found, a tow truck or a mainframe are as special as an automatic rifle or a Kevlar jacket, all of them need a contact or a contract to be acquired. Special Equipment are worth up to 4 tags.

                                                      OWNED EQUIPMENT
                                                      Any character have access to any number (with reasonable explanation) of common items, they may be brand new if the character has some money or look beaten if the character lives on budget, but if the characters don't have the item with them or in their house, it is perfectly possible to buy, rent or borrow them with no big effort.
                                                      Special equipment is not that easy to get. All characters have a number of special items related to the path equal the dots in that path. For example, a character with the Suburbia origin path at 1 dot, may have a sport car or a particularly powerful computer, while a Street Rat may have a customized gun or some lock picking tools.

                                                      MISSION SPECIFIC EQUIPMENT
                                                      Working for a society have some advantages as the society must source the character with the equipment needed, otherwise the work won’t be done properly. That is where the mission specific equipment enters.
                                                      This equipment is the minimum needed to allow the team to do their job, for example in a war story, the characters will have at least the basic infantry equipment, like a Kevlar jacket, helmet, rifle, ammunition and survival gear. If the mission requires a tank or a machine gun, it should be handled by the society, unless it’s part of the mission to get one to start with.
                                                      The mission specific equipment may be common or special items, and have no cost to the characters, as it is handled by the company, but it is defined by the Storyguide, any extra equipment must come from the character or connection uses.

                                                      ACCESS TO TEMPORARY EQUIPMENT
                                                      Access is a way to gather some extra equipment that must be returned latter. While it's not the only thing access may source, it's possible to use the connection to get some special equipment.
                                                      Getting access to a special item counts as the usage of a path.

                                                      CREATING AND REPAIRING
                                                      Creating and repair Common items takes no rolls, only the time to properly do it, that may varies if the one repairing have replacement parts or may need to create new from scratch, usually no more than a single scene.
                                                      Creating and repairing Special items may be a bit more tricky, as it requires knowledge in the area the item is regularly used, represented by at least 2 dots in the skill usually rolled when using the device (Close combat for melee weapon, for example) and a workshop with the proper equipments (like a garage for vehicles). Other than that it also requires no rolls and the time varies the same way as for Common equipment.

                                                      HIGH AND LOW TECH
                                                      Technology level defines what is Common and Special during any period, from the availability of different tags to the number of tags a item may have.
                                                      The available tags of each period varies greatly, but the number of tags is easier to quantify dividing the technology evolution into 4 periods, Primitive, Pre-Industrial, Industrial and Post-Industrial.
                                                      During primitive period, all kinds of equipments
                                                      are Special, as the existence of Common requires a society with specialists. All Improvised items are the closest to common, worth 0 tags, while Special items worth no more than 2 tags.
                                                      During pre-industrial period, while there are specialists creating all sorts of items, mass production is flawed at minimum, based in the work of the less capable specialists or capable specialists working is fast pace with low quality materials. Common items worth 1 tag and Special items worth 3.
                                                      The industrial era is the current period in Trinity Continuum Core Rulebook.
                                                      The post-industrial period is marked by the presence of advanced super science in the market. Common items are worth up to 3 tags and Special items worth up to 5 tags, equivalent to ranks 1 and 2 Advanced Super Science.

                                                      MODIFIED EDGES
                                                      Some Edges are modified to fit better on the new concept.
                                                      Wealth: Wealth represents not only money, but the contacts money may offer. This Edge grants a number of special items the character may have in addition to the ones granted by paths equal to its dots. Those extra items don't need any relation to​
                                                      the character path, they may be only a expensive hobby the character have or a heirloom. The character also gain enhancement equal to the Edge dots when bribing or showing off some over expensive common items (not worth more money) may give them advantage.
                                                      Loaded: This Enhanced Edge gives access to virtually any kind of special equipment with no reason to have them if the character needs it. It will not be as fast as going to a shop, but the character can get any special item within no more than a session, if needed. As described in the Edge, Government and other organizations keep track of the character action and may ask the character why the need of some equipments, particularly if they are restricted or strictly illegal, so, over using this edge to get those items is possible but should be discouraged.​
