    Storypath works more on the enhancements (free successes that can only be used if you have at least 1 success in the roll) than dice pool, as happen on CoD/WoD.

    You can have a Martial Artist Master with a pool of 8 dice or even less, but with edges and maybe gifts he gains 3 or 4 enhancements that would make him pretty lethal in combat.

    So, answering your question, a good fighter would have 8 dice, 3 enhancements with defense 2 (1 +4 dice) probably can hit a critical every other round, while a 4 dice, 1 enhancement and defense 1 (1 +2 dice) can hit every other round. So the fight will last 2 or 3 rounds, and the good one will end up with maybe one injury , while the weak one will be knowcked out.

    This is considering 2 full characters, minor characters are down with a single hit.

        Ran 15-sessions of Æon Trinity, with combat every other session. Our impression was that it had a "John Wick"-feel, made every combat more unique than roll to hit, roll to damage, rinse and repeat.

        None of the characters were combat specialists, but they had access to advanced weapons and armor so they could go toe to toe with dangerous opponents. Without the enhancements they wouldn't have been nearly as effective as their enemies always had higher dice pools and/or Tier advantage (not counting the mooks).

        The "pin down" stunt led to a tactical gun fight feel, with combatants being forced to either do damage and take damage in return or be ineffective. The cover system is great for this as well, giving the side with close combat capabilities an advantage as they could rush enemies hiding in cover and take them out.

        The basic assumption is that most combats doesn't have PC death as an outcome. I would tell them when they got into situations with potential lethal consequences so that they knew what they were signing up for. Also, most fights ended with the other side running away or surrendering, rather than dragging out the fight to the last enemy.

                            What do you have problems wrapping your head around? Here are a few of the things which tripped up my group at first:

                            1. Initiative crated a PC/NPC order, then you fill in the actual names as you see fit. My group decided to keep the same initiative slots each round to keep things moving quicker, but you can also let different PCs and NPCs fill each slot each round if you want.

                            2. Attacks are better stated as, "I'm making a Close Combat attack against the NPC," and then letting the number of successes generated determine if you're going for damage, knocking them back, etc. At first, they wanted to say, "I'm stabbing him with my claws," but then not generating enough successes to do that. So it FELT like they failed, even though they were able to do things like trip them or feint and pass enhancements on to another PC.

                            3. Defense is not really your ability to not be hit, despite using things like the dodge stunt. It is a combination of lots of factors which determine whether or not your opponent was able to be effective against you in the round. Which means, it's possible you were hit, it's just that the punch didn't impair you in anyway.

                                                Okay. The health system. Let's assume a target with Stamina 2 (and all of their other resistance attributes are 2.) So, they have one Bruised, one Injured, and one Maimed injury condition box in addition to Taken Out. Let's also assume they have soft armor 2.

                                                Now, let's make an attack:

                                                1. I declare a melee attack against the target with a dice pool of 6 and 2 enhancements from my weapon.

                                                2. Since this is the first attack the target is receiving this round, they need to roll for a resistance stat to generate successes for defensive stunts. Let's say they roll 1 success on their 2 dice. (That's why I set all their resistance stats to the same value, to make choosing which resistance stat a non-issue.)

                                                3. The target spends the 1 success on the Dodge Stunt, increasing their Defense to 2 (Base 1 + 1 Dodge stunt) for the round.

                                                4. I roll my attack roll. I get 2 successes on the dice, and I add in my 2 enhancement from my weapon for a total of 4 successes.

                                                5. I now assess my options. The target has a Defense of 2, and their soft armor of 2 means the Inflict Injury stunt is going to cost me 2 successes to buy. (Base 0 + 2 soft armor) That means I have just enough successes (2 Defense + 2 Inflict Injury) to buy the Inflict Injury stunt. So, I decide to inflict an injury on the target.

                                                6. The target now gets to decide the severity of the Injury Condition. Do they want to be Bruised, Injured, Maimed, or Taken Out? Let's say they choose to be Bruised. So, they get a condition, let's call it, Blood In Your Eye. So, now they get a +1 increase to difficulty for any action which involves seeing with that eye. If they are a PC, and they fail their roll because of that condition, they'll get an extra point of Momentum.

                                                Does that help at all? What parts do you have questions about?

                                                    Very good example. Let me just make a comment, not related directly to combat, but, on 6, the condition will grant 1 momentum, no matter the result of the dice, if the condition caused any issue to the character (in this case increased the difficulty of the action by 1), it will grant a second momentum if the character fail the roll (as usual) and a third if the character botch.

                                                    In page 73 o Core it says: "If a Condition causes a character to fail or otherwise suffer a significant set back, it provides 1 Momentum in addition to any gained as a Consolation". the increased difficulty is considered a "significant set back". This is just to differentiate a guy that has a broken leg, when trying to make a speech will not suffer from the broken leg condition, so no extra momentum.

                                                    Also, if you end a condition before it would end naturally, for example healing a character from an injury using VK, you gain a momentum.

                                                            On the "toll" issue:

                                                            First, I'd say highlight Florin's comment about Momentum. If you're playing as a normal old human (not even a Talent or an Origin level Scion), Die Hard 1 is a pretty good benchmark for high action stuff. John does get beat up over the course of the movie, but that means a lot of Momentum granting points where his injuries offer setbacks, letting him fuel Skill Tricks and bigger dice-pools (potentially offsetting injury penalties).

                                                            Second, as a team oriented system for an RPG, a good medic/healer on the PC's side can easily keep people going even during a relatively high action grind as long as there's some breaks for them to do First-Aid and such. Even with just base-line human PCs, you can expect any lull in the action to meant the whole party is going to get some bandages, stitches, painkillers and whatever, and have one Injury removed or downgraded.
