                                Pre Script: I finally got the document to load, while much of it agrees with what i'm driving at, it seems most of the setting is already done - so I doubt anything i say will inspire any detail changes, much less changes in overall tone. I suppose we just wait for more information now.
                                ...

                                Most of this will concern the general meta-plot and mood of the Aeonverse, apologies if that isn't up for discussion here, i'm not able to get the referenced document for some reason...

                                Meta-Plot and Mood
                                I'm not entirely convinced white washing the Aeon society is what is called for in a reboot - the complexity of the Aeon society through history made Max Mercer one of the most intriguingly understated characters i've had the pleasure of trying to unravel. On one side we have an idealistic two-fisted detective who fits seamlessly into a pulp adventure setting, on another we have a father of lies not unlike the Watchmen's Adrian "Ozymandias" Veidt, who makes a centralized earth for a costly greater good (or pure hubris, depending on how you land on the character). The two variations being neither confirmed or denied in the setting left a lot of leeway for ST interpretation (e.g. my brother and i run the Aeonverse two different ways, they both work uninterrupted by the none-the-less rich setting info).
                                You would vindicate my brother and invalidate me by cleaning it up too much in that regard, so i have a little skin in the game there.
                                I'm having difficulty finding the mission statement quoted in earlier posts, so i can't comment on other subjects therein. While i won't deny the thriving hopes of the Trinity settings, a "world of brightness" isn't really what i got out of the series. It reads more like Clive Cussler than Sax Rohmer, more like Watchmen than Justice League and more like Revelation Space than Star Trek. The chilling question on the Aeon societies silence during the second world war, the intriguing absenses of Max from the timeline (i suspect he managed to hide from the society sometimes despite his presence, or simply take another identity) and the heavy inference of the society falling apart after WWII, if only briefly, all point to a grey-scale setting with many of the trappings of popular fiction.
                                The setting was built for deconstruction, FTL & Psionics comes with a price, super powers come with a price, and, if you would indulge me, i always considered Adventure to be leading into a ridiculous, silver-age, Venture Brothers meets Top Ten era during the fifties and sixties as the first hammersmith effect weakened. This history was of course covered in great detail by the society, but like their pulp-flavored periodicals of earlier times - nobody really believed them and the tales were swept under the rug by the society in an attempt to regain dwindling credibility as the inspired people places and things slowly began to power down prior to the N-Day effect created by Mal as a kind of love letter to the Hammersmith era. In other words; a price is paid there too in my own head-canon.
                                To clear it up, i'm not arguing for a darkening of the setting - i'm rather arguing for it's vast genre to not be pruned back in the name of contrasting your other books. Trinity can stand alone - it has done for a long time. The Aeon society is a mirror for the society of the times and as such must grow and make mistakes with the people they serve - i feel the single unbroken society as singularly evil or good would feel out of place and overly simplified in the otherwise complex setting material, the real story of the Aeon society is likely more complex than it seems. Project Rewrite hints at this.
                                In conclusion, please don't take the Gothic elements of storytelling out of this setting - the skeletons in the closets, the secrets, horrors and mystery of the loose threads are an important part of what makes the setting so compelling. It isn't the world of darkness, but neither is it the world of brightness - instead it's something in between, with extensions reaching into both extremes - it can be bigger than light and dark.

                                Aeon/Trinity 2120
                                I agree with the other posters lamenting the lack of detail for the UAN, who otherwise fill an important role vacated by the U.S. and (to a lesser extent) my own nation of Australia as a developing new nation upon which the world can hang its progressive dreams. Further, the handwaving nature of collecting africa into a single entity without exception (outside of the middle east at least) suffers from western-centric generalization, a flaw the setting could do with shedding as much as possible.
                                Some fairly extensive works (India Underground, Asia Ascendant, etc..) were never truly canonized in the setting either, which was sad, considering the amount of thought that went into them. The story of Nippon in particular helped to fill the blanks between Aberrant and Trinity, the controlled utopia of China is an important element as well from that book.
                                In other areas, i wondered at the reasoning, game-wise to cut up the Op-Net into much more nationalized subnets? It seems very strange from a 2014 perspective, what was the difference betweeen the internet and opnet anyway? Bandwidth? Compression algorithms? Handshake protocols? It smelled of needless rebranding and despite it working fine in game, it is showing it's age. I actually like the individual national server hubs, leaves a lot of room for ST's to play about - i played with the idea of some old central-asian servers under loose Russian federation control had been left as-is since Kuwasha's death and still carried a patial echo of the IT nova - a maimed digital version of Warfire in western Europe, but otherwise the region had security levels comparable to the internet we know and love today. The idea of needing physical access to important cables at the hub also made for a fun bit of espionage play for those without electrokinesis. Underground, pirate internet protocols being illegal was delectably Orwellian too. I just think a better technical reason for the change would be appreciated.
                                Finally (for now), the shocking alieness of Trinity era Aberrants created an immense amount of inspiration for this Storyteller, i could pull ideas straight out of lovecraftian horror or victorian ghost stories and isolate groups in deep space while fending off these threats. What i'm saying is that Taint was a great storytelling tool in combination with the semi-magical Novas in a relatively hard science setting - the worst monsters were once us.
                                And the biggest threat we've encountered so far is some no-name, the Colony. What are some of the 'prime threats' like so far in the future? The imagination swoons with possibility.
                                Right: A whole heap, mostly decent futurism, unparralleled scope in combination with the rest of the Aeonverse, No Faster than light travel (violated by the coalition eventually, but in an acceptably dramatic manner), Non-US centric (now expand the worldview even more for extra points), completely inhuman aliens, few explicit bad guys and more sympathetic illustrations of them hidden just below the surface, intrigue aplenty, hopeful depictions of space travel with sufficient dystopia to keep it interesting, corporate dominance, manipulative media and PR muddled subtexts to nearly everything, Eden, Khantze Lu Ge, Process 418 (cyclical conclusion), QK purge (India Underground made the subtext clearer, not sure if that was the intent when written)... Just a wonderful, inspiring game. My favorite, in fact.
                                Wrong: PSUEDO GRAVITY (hard science please), Opnet Protocols (okay, but what's the difference?), not enough Transhumanism (Nova-tech AI, neural uploading of personalities to emulators, cyborgs, spacers with enough implants to walk in vacuum naked, uplifted animals, etc...), not enough focus on the solar system worlds (including moons), too much focus on the Psi Orders (telegraphing the meta-plot much?), no good 'gotcha' moment for the Upeo's return (i know the bright continent and star-crossed were building to something, but we never got the payoff), same with the Doyen. Actually a lot of what's wrong with the 2120's setting is that it just wasn't finished... Let's adress that going forward. -smile-

                                Aberrant 2008
                                I never ran Aberrant in its setting, the Novas i ran were in the 2120's. But my brother ran many games in the setting - in his inimitable silver-age style - throwing a deconstructionist bone my way once or twice a game, but never letting that darkness consume his vision of the setting. Proteus was his main tool in this excercise - although they may seem contradictory or even offensive to the super-heroic feel of the game - reconstructing the genre's assumptions to exclude those possibilities would be folly, in my humble opinion. Many decry the darkening of comics, but there are stories to tell there - see Rising Stars, Powers and any number of other modern deconstructions of the genre.
                                I think the main issue may be the reproductive one - as anyone should be able to sympathise with humanity wanting to protect itself from the nova age, but even then i can't imagine the fertility depression would last forever and a few Novas would be immune by nature/anima. The Aeon society may not have seen this N-Day stuff coming - the realisms of Time Travel being left up to each individual ST, and the effect was created by Primoris/Mal, who at the time, given what we know, was likely a terrifying enemy of the society after their battle in 1943 (again, the subtle silence on WWII leaves a lot of room for interpretation here). Over-reaction by Project Proteus is partly where the Teragen draws their grey-scale mojo from. My brother uses the Teragen as unrepentant super-villains - but i see a whole lot more potential there - why should humans have dominion over a population of superior beings? They aren't looking for dominion over humans only themselves (at least not in the excerpts of the Null Manifesto that we are given), it's very Brotherhood of Mutants and potentially sympathetic - see Synapse 'defending' the Opnet to the best of his abilities in the war. Devolving Mal from Primoris into a kind of comic-book villain seems such a waste of the details given in the books, like writing Magneto to rob a bank just for the money and not for some much grander scheme born from his own torment.
                                I ran low-quantum Novas in two different campaigns in the 2120 setting and found enough references in the Trinity books to do so - it would have been lovely to see those kind of tangible threads in Aberrant from Adventure! Where is Sarah Gettel's legacy? What mark did the Ubiquitous Dragon leave on his Orient homeland? Who/Where is Michael Mercer? (I heard the d20 books adressed the last one with Margaret, but still)
                                Otherwise, my main issue with Aberrant would be the inevitable arms war that occurs at higher levels of physical conflict - Mega-Strength 5+ must be accounted for by all characters wanting to join a fray, Quickness keeps stacking every time you buy it, etc... But i'm going to stay away form sytem discussion otherwise, because i feel i'm a little out of my league having experience in only a dozen or so systems under my belt, most of which old/obsolete by now. Just don't base it off MMO mechanics if possible - they are two different types of gaming, thanks.
                                Right: Intrigue, comic-like superheroes (i like the deconstruction, but they need to be there for me to do that), Media infatuation (N! network still feels relevant in 2014), dubious power structures (Proteus, DeVries, Directive), morally grey factions (in some interpretations, but a Storyteller system strength at the time), sillyness (silver-age stuff can actually work despite the shadowy background), heroics (not just comic book stuff either, some of the Novas of 2008 and earlier are earnest heroes with a whole world rallying against them, despite the cameras and fans - see the Fireman), information contrast (the bright colored and shallow setting material against the intriguing depths of less obvious machinations) and the meta-plot war (the text takes on new meaning in combination with the history given in 2120).
                                Wrong: Not enough tie ins with Adventure!, Obvious trolling by Proteus (more subtlety would have been nice), eventual arms war between players and ST (in my experience), lack of balance with the other games in the Aeonverse (sucks to be them, it can work, but anyone who isn't a full quantum Nova is going to feel left out and underpowered, taint be damned), investment in characters who either turn against earth, go insane or both without known exception, even the Nihonjin remnants are falling apart by 2120.

                                Adventure! 1922
                                While both my brother and i have played about with crossing over with the Adventure game, neither of us have run a game in the setting - thus disqualifying me from any experienced input. But i mentioned my own head-canon about the time between and the Hammersmith effect repeats in 2122/2123 which makes me interested in the meta-plot. I bought the book to answer some of my questions about Max, Primoris and other history. Now days, my brother and i take turns coming up with silver-age nonsense to explain why the likes of Danger Ace, Whitley Styles and Sarah Gettel can suddenly appear at any time in any genre with a smart comment loaded and followed by world-tilting Deus Ex Machina in the wake of their astronomical inspiration. Given Taint and the Doyen, it just seems natural that the era of Adventure! was mostly blind to the ripples their telluric manipulations were causing - however subconscious. But that was when the society was at it's most innocent - it's clear that something BAD happened inbetween this society and the one we see so little of in the following era - but the trinity era once more has some of this hope back - perhaps co-inciding with a more stable return of Max Mercer prior and after process 418.
                                No rights and Wrongs here, i haven't enough experience with the game for that, just the setting material. But the inventions system needed to be in all the books, as did a lot of what was detailed, like background mastery, dramatic editing (as a sidebar, i agree) and a final call on whether Daredevils were a third type of inspired or what everyone who wasn't inspired could become would be useful for the Quantum/Noetic framework.

                                I have a lot of topics to read through, but i'll come back and try to find the document referenced earlier for a bit more more input. Apologies for the long-windedness, it's been too long and i'm a little overwhelmed by all i have to say on the subject as a huge fan and a long time participant in this shared universe.

                                                It's hard to say Aeon got much right; I loved the game for the setting more than for the mechanics. Still, it did innovate some of the features that would later become standard in Storyteller/Storytelling, like standardized target numbers, auto-soak, and matching a skill to an attribute.

                                                Aberrant was actually one of my favorite systems because of the tiers of powers made there costs very easy to remember: 1, 3, or 5 nova points. Simple. You had enough points to muck about with, certainly, but I never felt overwhelmed. Taint also worked out okay for the mechanic. I wasn't fond of the power modifiers found in the player's guide, as I've seen those things in superhero games and I think they cause more problems than they solve - you bloat the power entry, you slow down character creation, you don't get any greater guarantee of balance than if you had just made a new power whole-cloth, and you turn getting the power you want into this cruel minigame that you can fail. No thanks.

                                                Adventure was brilliant - it just needed more stuff. More knacks of all types really, as I felt painfully limited in a nearly limitless setting. Invention was great though, as was dramatic editing.
