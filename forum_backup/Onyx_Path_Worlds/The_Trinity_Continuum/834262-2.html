    The name Continuum holds the key for reconciling all of this. When I finished my epic Adventure/Aberrant/Trinity game 10+ years ago, I thought the story was over. There was a small-scale sequel story that used a custom system to play more space opera but that fizzled and the universe went to sleep.

    Then I heard Onyx Path bought the rights and we're updating the games. Once the term Continuum was used, it sparked an idea to fold in the old with the inevitable return of Trinity.

    "There are infinite universes, parallel to one another in an endless multiverse. Every possible event has occurred or will occur...or so we thought. Oddly, The Hammersmith Incident happened in only a handful of universes. The story of one of them is well documented.
    This is the story of the rest."

    Revisit the Continuum in 2016.

Anything can happen, so it’s up to you and your cast to make it happen.                                                         Great list! I love Zenith/post-Zenith (don't get chubbies, Exalted fans) empires a la Dune or this comic called Low (about a post-post-post catastrophe Earth in the far flung future in which we've been forced to move into deep sea habitats. Their technology is giving out and a savior class of nobility called helmsmen has gone extinct. The inciting incident in the first issue is a deep space probe has returned possibly with the location of a habitable planet.) At any rate, the exciting thing about a number of these kinds of decline-and-fall settings is how relatable it can be. It's not necessarily decadence that does in the home colonies, it's apathy or even a cosmopolitan weltschmerz. While you can set stories in the empire's far-off conquests, it's just as interesting to see a fortress world centric stagnant bureaucracy at work. In general, I'm amped about all of these iterations. I'm not quite as schooled on the new system as I might like but I found the old system to be fairly mutable even if Aberrant was wildly over-powered in comparison to their other post-human cousins.
