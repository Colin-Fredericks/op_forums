                    Then shouldn't your question be, "Why not Cunning + Agility?"

                            I've submitted a suggestion that “tell a lie” be replaced by something to the effect of “convey sincerity”; that is remove the implicit assumption that you’re using the skill to deceive. My reasoning is two-fold:

                            First, Sardonyx is aiming for a more positive tone than the Storyteller/ing systems have; it's why you have Grace rather than Manipulation. "Convincing someone that you're on the level" is a more positive way of looking at things than “lying”. And it's a better fit for Integrity.

                            Second, it has a wider application. “Lying” only comes into play when you're trying to deceive someone; “convey trustworthiness” comes into play any time someone has reason to doubt you. That includes situations where you're trying to lie; but it also includes situations where someone else has besmirched your character (or circumstances have brought your honesty into question) and you're trying to defend your good name.

                            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                BTW, I vastly prefer seeing Attributes grouped by Function rather than by Arena, at least in terms of how they're described; because where confusion between Attributes comes up, it's more likely to involve same Function, different Arenas (e.g., Cunning vs. Grace, or Discipline vs. Poise) than same Arena, different Functions.                                                         So far, I would say Reason and Cunning both pair well with every skill. Some are slightly more offbeat uses, but almost everything works with those two; Reason+Agility is a bit hard to justify but everything else seems to work, and Cunning pairs with everything.

                                                        Might is one of the hardest to pair. Right now, I can see it working with Agility (Parkour), Aim (Hucking Stuff), Analyze (Breaking Stuff), Athletics (Lifting/Climbing/Swimming/Everything), Brawl (Hulk Smash), Performance (Flexing and various feats of strength), Persuasion (Intimidation), Pilot (certain muscle-powered vehicles, like bikes, might benefit from Might+Pilot), maybe Survival (Woodcutting? I'm not sure), maybe by a slim chance Technology (for very primitive tech, or or crafts that are strongly based on strength; blacksmithing might work), and Weaponry (Hulk Smash with indirect assistance from Chrysler Corporation). Finesse pairs better, although of course it's still a stretch for some things. The Resistance traits pair poorly across the board, but I kind of expected that.

                                                        Vitality seems like it would be good for tasks that are physically exhausting, or where sleepiness or other such thing might come into play.
                                                        Discipline seems like it would be good for tasks that are somewhat boring.
                                                        Poise seems good for dealing with tasks that are frustrating or possibly frightening.

                                                        Also, Performance is the little black dress of skills: it goes with everything.

                                                            Wonder what's next. Edges? Perhaps some thoughts on combat.

                                                            Right now I kind of favor the idea of having two offensive attributes, mainly Might and Finesse, and two defensive attributes, mainly Vitality and Cunning. Might gives you you strong attacks; Finesse gives you precision attacks. Cunning lets you dodge/block attacks, while Vitality would let you absorb/soak up attacks. Might is easy to dodge, but hard to soak. Finesse is easy to soak, but hard to dodge.

                                                            It's mostly based on personal aesthetics over any sense of balance, and I should know better than to start developing any kind of expectation of how I would do it, but I'm fond of the idea for now.
