        RE: Spark Soldiers

        Both Spark Soldiers and Spark Juice come from my actual games. For few years, Aberrant was the only rpg I was running (and I was doing twice weekly sessions at that). So alot of my personal game notes and home brew ideas ended up being submitted to various fan projects. The Spark Soldiers were a way to introduce super-powered cannon fodder into the game world while staying within the thematic boundaries of how rare and unique each nova truly was. I provided mechanics and general ideas and let the project wordsmiths polish the concepts. Overall some of my better work.

        RE: Combat

        With my Combat contributions, I went for something that could be used across all the game lines, but felt true to the real world sources. Basing it off of skills specialties was something I felt that with allow the system to be plugged into an existing game with little trouble.
        If I had to do over, I would make the combat styles Merit based (similar to NWOD) and I would have tried to streamline things to keep combat moving quickly.

        I am very sensitive to game balance, but I ran into the hazards of creating way more content than a dedicated team of players could working through in a year. Ultimately I went my instincts. Having played for years with players who seemed to go out of their way to break systems into a million pieces, I developed a good sense of where the system held up, where it bent,and where it broke andlet that be my guiding star. Some of my concepts could stand to be refined, but hopefully not too many, and not too much.

        Looking over the rules, leaving out things like the strength limits feels like an oversight, and would be corrected if I had to do it again.

        As common for the fan projects, there are more ideas in play than what any of us could have rigorously play tested. Most of the time my players were still finding out cool tricks from the published sources, while I had devoured the published sources and was filling in the spaces the canon material didn't cover.If you ever decide to crack open the Trinity books and play with any of those ideas, i would love to know what works and what doesn't.

        At least for my part, it felt to me that the three settings could have benefited from more mechanical cohesion, as I say them and different pieces of a muchlarger story, and playing one part as if theothers didn't exist always felt weird. As such I always used all three settings as a reference point and in points of conflict, I went with what I felt was the strongest example (usually Adventure or Trinity - as much as I love Aberrant, i could almost hear the system groaning under the strain of supporting such a high-power genre).
        When i get my hands on Sardonix I am probably going to see if I can update and convert some of the ideas in my old files.

        RE: Sources of Injury

        This material was almost copy/pasted word for word from my notes. I have enjoyed enjoyed running games where the primary antagonist is nature and the environment. And I had immense fun putting Nova characters into environments that would be fatal to baselines. This involved a good of amout of real world research but I think it translated into alot of cool elements to spice up a session.

        RE: Chapter 4

        Another place where the many of the powers and enhancements I contributed were clipped right from my notes. This was very much a "drink from the hose" chapter. I think we went into this went so much enthusiasm because at least for those of us working on things, Aberrant had so much depth to the setting that was begging to be explored, trying to answer the fundamental position of the setting - what would you do with the power of a god. Over all, most the material is good, some of it is bad,and much of it is just weird (you can decide what is what as you read the material).

            Let's see if I can respond to your comments coherently. A lot of this stuff is a fairly fuzzy memory.

            Chapter 1 and 2 Generally
            I just named Project Harmony. Geoff had already written about it, but he hadn't given it a name. I thought naming it made it easier to talk about.

            I'm pretty sure only wrote the side bars in chapters 1 and 2.

            While I was (and to a degree still am) fairly pleased with the modes I wrote, the general setting stuff I wrote is a mess. It's pretty much an uncontrolled dumpster fire. The modes though I think still hold up pretty well. Design wise, we were writing these after Terra Verde was published. So, we sort of used that as a guide and tried to present something that hinted at the kinds of powers you might see in Aberrant without just making mode dots = specific aberrant powers.

            The Other Chapters Generally
            I can't speak much to the rest. For the most part I tried to leave everything Geoff wrote as he wrote it. It was never the plan that I would be the one with final edit on the book, so I didn't want to disrupt the work that was already done. I think the only thing I changed was the part about the Chib that gets tainted (don't really remember where it was in the book or the name of the character). Geoff had originally written it so that the guy actually became a tainted psion that turned into something like an aberrant character with lots of aberrations. He then had the guy charge off into the sunset like a big monstrous plot hook. I changed it because, as it was originally written, it directly contradicted prior statements in earlier official books about how psi and taint would interact. I don't think I still have the original version anywhere. In hind sight that might not have been too cool of me. But, I really didn't like the original version.

            I don't know what came of Geoff. I think he was from New Zealand. Last I heard he was in college somewhere, but that was a decade ago at least.

            Xavier
            I don't really have any stories to share about him, other than to explain his original creation. He was a character I switched to in the middle of a game while my main character was written out of the story for a bit. Xavier was intended to be totally reasonable at all times. My GM at the time (Jake, the artist), used him as a plot device to convince some of the other players that the Big Bad in our story (a Rogue Chib) was actually a bad guy. He was supposed to die making that point (and at the time did).
            But I enjoyed him so much (and the other players liked him too), so we retconned in a last minute medical rescue for him and brought him back for another story some years later.

            Thought Discipline is a special ability that Directive agents could get in Aberrant. The rules are from the Directive Source book. The story was that he had rediscovered the techniques for Though Discipline and written his Doctoral Dissertation on it.

                This is a slight tangent, but as I found it in the EON Archive, it's pertinent to this thread. Mind you, I'm about to go on a rant about one particular fan product I found in the Archive, namely Space Tactics a.k.a. "Tactics and Strategy in Three Dimensions: How to Wage Combat in Space."

                I know this is a fan product so it's quality is not going to be up to the standards of an officially published book, but I swear, this thing reads like it was written by someone who learned English as a second language. One of the headings is titled "Aloneness" which isn't even a word ("Solitude" or "Self-sufficiency" would have been mildly better choices given the subject of not being able to depend on anyone but yourself). And near the beginning of the essay, beam weapons are mentioned as being advantageous as they do not need ammunition (because they're energy weapons, natch); literally two sentences later, the author says "remember that a number of weapons become beam weapons; with no gravity, mortars and artillery shells have no arcs." So what this author is essentially saying is that beam/energy weapons don't require ammunition but ammunition fired in space becomes a beam/energy weapon simply because there's no gravity to give the ammunition a downward arc.

                WTF?

                The best/worst part was when I read that (and this is what convinced me the author needed to have his ass removed with a spoon) radar is essentially useless in space (and I shit you not that the author wrote this) because apparently radio waves can't travel through a vacuum.

                Double WTF??

                If anyone here doubts whether radio waves can travel through a vacuum, I refer you to Project Diana, conducted in 1946, where the US Army bounced radio waves off the surface of the Moon. Now, I'm no astronomer, but I'm willing to wager that there's quite a bit of vacuum between Earth's atmosphere and the Moon. And that's not even mentioning all the radio and television waves radiating out from Earth into space for the last 70+ years.

                So once I finished reading that paragraph, I thought that the author simply confused radar and sonar. But then a couple paragraphs later, under the heading "Vacuum," he writes "Something that you have this much of needs to be noted. Radar and sonar do not work in a vacuum (lidar systems work fine, however)." As for that first sentence, doesn't that read like it was written by someone who learned English as a second language? It took me a minute to realize that the "something" he's referring to is vacuum and I only realized that because "Vacuum" was the heading of the paragraph, rather than actually mentioning the word "vacuum" in the sentence itself. And as for the second sentence, the author merely compounds his previous error by restating that radar doesn't work in vacuum (false) and then mentions sonar not working in vacuum either (which is true but is so obvious it's like saying water is wet). And by mentioning radar and sonar in the same sentence, the author seems to realize that they're two different things (even though he seemed to conflate the two just a couple of paragraphs ago) but doesn't realize that they work very differently in vacuum.

                Yeah.

                Anyway, my rant's finished. If you're in the mood to facepalm at something, by all means, read Space Tactics. But if you're looking for something useful for space combat rules for Trinity, look elsewhere.
