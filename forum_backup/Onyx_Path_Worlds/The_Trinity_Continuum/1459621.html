    Traditional FTL

    09-11-2021, 01:02 AM
    Now obviiously in Canon, we don't have it. but what impact would say a star trek style FTL drive have on the setting?

    On the one hand, all but the highest end of Star Trek style drives are far slower than either Nova warping or Psion teleports, on the other hand, for shorter ranges, they avoid the need for, well, a psion or nova.

    So how would they work, what would the impact be?

      It’s perfectly possible that Novas have built a Novatech Warp Drive, but if there is any one operating it is not under Aeon watch, so, maybe a Aberrant era colony have one…

      As far it works too far away from Earth or any important colony nothing changes, except for local transportation.

      A ship that takes a few minutes to reach Mars instead of days, and a week or so to get to a nearby colony would be useful but only to local transport, imagining you have a limited number of Teleporters, and they would not be around taking a few tons of material from here to there for a absurd amount of money.

      But if you think you may take a few years from Earth to some colonies, it start to make little sense, because in a few years the chance of a Teleporter shows up and take you in seconds is high.

      I don’t think there would be any big change in the setting, except for maybe the accessibility of a few near by (in the galactic scale) colonies during the absence of the teleporters, but it would not be that significant in the macro view.

          I think their biggest impact would be for planetary travel inside a single solar system. Though I think that impact would be huge, especially for Aberrant era. Even for Aeon era it would be pretty big for stellar mining and colonization efforts as resources could constantly be traveling back and forth without the need of a teleporter of which there are only a couple thousand at most between all the Orders.               The usefulness of FTL is also dependent on the speed, reliability, and resource consumption of the ships. According to the old GURPS Prime Directive sourcebook, even civilian freighters in the Captain Kirk era could cruise nearly 5 parsecs/day, and Alpha Centauri is slightly over a parsec from Earth. That puts them way ahead of modern cargo ships.

                              Different forms of interstellar travel create interesting strategic issues and good hooks for stories.

                              If non-inspired FTL was a gate and jump system, then key points in the various star-systems become vital choke points. Certain star-systems would have more jump points than others and would become natural trade hubs. This would make non-inspired FTL analogous to oceanic travel and trade. Inspired FTL would be more like air travel.

                              There would be complex political, economic, and social, consequences to such a system. The stories would show up in flocks.

                              Another tack might be to make inspired FTL travel cheap and fast, but seriously restricted in some way. Clipper ships compared to non-inspired FTL. So if you have to get it there quickly and in volume, use non-inspired FTL. If you want to explore off the beaten path, use inspired FTL.
