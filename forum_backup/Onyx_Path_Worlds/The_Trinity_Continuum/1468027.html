    Homebrew Aliens, Beasts, & Monsters

    11-16-2021, 02:37 PM
    Greetings, all.

    So, quick introduction, I've lurked and read these forums for years, but never felt an impetus to post with any regularity. I fell in love with Trinity years ago, when I spent my Christmas money on that limited edition black spiral-bound plastic cover. I still have it sitting on my shelf. I've rarely had the opportunity to play, but in a few weeks (after the holidays stop messing with everyone's gaming schedule) I get to run a game for my group.

    In preparation, I've been working on some homebrew creatures to add. I know, the game already has plenty of aliens, but I like my Continuum stocked full of weirdness. So, with that in mind, I wanted to showcase the critters I've been working on. Feel free to savage and critique to your hearts' content.

    First up, a little thing I call "the Mimics".

    Mimics

    Description
    An individual Mimic is only visible under a microscope. It is a single-celled organism, a bundle of mitochondria packed with DNA strands and unique protein chains. It links together with others of its kind and shares information in a manner not dissimilar to a series of hard drives linked together. When enough are linked in such a way, what would be recognizable as the full “Mimic” becomes visible as an opaque mass of ooze. This mass is shiny and black, and appears to have a consistency not dissimilar to petroleum.

    Each organism shares a noetic link with others of its kind that it maintains physical contact with. They appear drawn to one another, and will instinctively seek one another out if separated. This instinct can only be overcome when enough of the organisms combine.

    Individually, no single organism displays any measurable intelligence, and behaves in a reactionary manner similar to other microorganisms. As they come together, they use their noetic link to share information via tactile telepathy. Masses of the organisms visible to the naked eye respond to outside stimuli again in a reactionary manner, reassembling themselves through some manner of biokinesis into the form of whatever they come in physical contact with. Their natural telepathy then reads and copies the mental patterns of other organic forms that they encounter. No matter the physical shape they take, the mimics are limited in that they always resemble a slick, oily mass.

    As more knowledge is collected, colonies of the organisms can “split” away to take whatever shapes they desire. It is uncertain as to exactly why this is done, as the individual organisms that make up Mimics subsist almost entirely on other microorganisms, and continue to do so even when they take on more complex forms. Their sole motivation appears to be to multiply, copy new forms, and learn from them.

    Discovery
    Mimics will certainly be a strange sight to the humans that first encounter them. Contact through light or porous clothing is enough for a mimic to establish the noetic link necessary to copy another bioform. While the mimics have attained a high enough level of intellect to understand that they are “different” from other organisms, they have yet to encounter a truly sentient species. The emergence of true sentience will send a shockwave through the entire mass, one that will be a direct result according to how Humanity initially reacts to them. The first thing a Mimic colony learns upon contact with another organism is how to communicate with it, so such a barrier will be quickly overcome.

    Because the Mimics have never encountered a thinking creature, they only have a basic understanding of violence, tolerance and community the way simple creatures do; the urge to eat, mate, prosper, and defend oneself on an instinctual level. They have no true knowledge of love, hate, revulsion, or curiosity.

    History
    Whoever first encounters the Mimics, assuming that they don’t instill the creatures with a desire to destroy their discoverers, may eventually come to find that the Mimics are not a result of natural evolution. They are a unique form of noetic biotechnology intended for planetary exploration in hostile environments. Their original creators, a species long-since extinct, established laboratories on various planets, where the parent colony was grown and then released. The mimics would spread from there, copying and learning as much about the planet’s native organisms as they could. Since they do not compete with the native lifeforms for resources, they do not put any undue stress on their host biomes.

    When and if Humanity encounters the Mimics, they will find that the species is ignorant of its origins. How large the colony was meant to grow, and how their creators intended to retrieve it and the information it gained is unknown. Though, given the Mimics’ natural ability at the Biokinesis Aptitude, it is likely that their creators released some manner of microorganism that recalled the colony, and then they could use Compartmentalize (or some analogue of it) to “read” the collected data. Humanity will only be able to glean even this much assuming they manage to find the lab the local Mimic colony was grown in.

      Next, the Borgs (Borg? Singular? Plural? Maybe I should just pick a new name)
      Borgs

      Description
      Despite the crudeness of the nickname, it is apt. Borgs appear to other species as sentient and highly intelligent cybernetic organisms made entirely of synthetic materials, their minds housed within individual positronic matrices. To the Borgs, one form is as good as another, depending upon one’s needs. They typically assemble and control these bodies using advanced Technokinesis, which they have refined over the course of centuries.

      Though they make frequent use of their synthetic bodies, which can appear as whatever the Borgs desire given available materials and need, most prefer to spend their time within the Great Matrix. The Great Matrix is a bogglingly massive positronic network where the Borgs exist as pure noetic thought and energy.

      Discovery
      Humanity’s first encounter with the Borgs might come about in one of two ways. First, psions might pick up the species’ electromagnetic transmissions that it uses to explore nearby space. The second, and more dangerous possibility, is if an exploratory fleet runs smack into the Great Barrier that the Borgs have constructed around their solar system, an electromagnetic shield that repels both conventional and most Inspired modes of transportation. This barrier is impassable without the Borgs’ expressed permission.

      If Humans attempt to first establish long-range contact with the Borg, they will find the machine-people to be intensely, even ravenously curious about the universe beyond their little system. Still, it will be some time, and dependent upon the establishment of peaceable motives on Humanity’s part before the Borgs allow them access to their home system.

      Accidental discovery will initially be a shock, and any Human ships that linger near the Great Barrier will soon be confronted by several soldier-ships of the Borgs’ Self Defense Fleet. If initial hostilities can be cooled or avoided, the Borg will remain cool and suspicious of Humanity until their good intentions are proven.

      Establishing a shared form of communication will be tricky. The Borgs use electromagnetic signals to communicate with one another in their native form. They have no biology to speak of, and a telepath without a sufficient understanding of physics (Science 2) will find their thoughts to be strange. But, physicists, mathematicians, and electrokinetics will find communication with them to be both simple and fascinating.

      Stranger still, the Borg will consider any Leviathan’s Tesser computer to be a kindred spirit, of a sort. The Tesser will happily serve as an intermediary.

      After that, the Borg will be happy to share whatever knowledge they can with Humanity, particularly in exchange for access to and help discovering raw materials for their machine-bodies. If an alliance can be brokered, Humanity will find the Borg most willing.

      Because the Borg are a people under siege, and slowly growing desperate for any aid in combating a vicious and implacable enemy; the Doyen.

      History
      The Borg were once sentient, organic lifeforms not dissimilar from Humans, though it’s a period of their past that they are reluctant to talk about. The Borg were, and still are, a forward-looking people. They became psionically active a millennia ago, broadly accessing the lower levels of Electrokinesis. They eventually focused on the Technokinesis mode, refining its parameters until the line between their organic minds and synthetic constructs became nonexistent.

      It was at this point in their history that the Borgs achieved world peace amongst their species, but at the cost of exhausting their home planet’s natural resources. Having achieved near-immortality by transferring their consciousness to hardtech brains, it was simply a matter of patience for the Borgs to begin colonizing their own solar system. Soon after, they finished constructing the Great Matrix, a noetically powered, shared electromagnetic positronic system unlike anything else another species had accomplished. Unfortunately, this increased their noetic presence in the universe, and attracted the attention of the Doyen.

      The Doyen realized that the Borgs were within a few generations of achieving noetic ascension. But, because the Borgs had somehow successfully integrated hard tech into their noetic paradigm without the need for noetic biotechnology, the Doyen determined that allowing such beings to achieve anything close to their own level of existence would be an implicit threat.

      The Doyen then began their assault as they often do, by employing a primitive, but sentient nearby species to act as their army. This species, which possessed almost no psionic ability, was swiftly wiped out. Meanwhile, the Doyen undertook a program of possession, infiltration, and sabotage. The Borgs’ electromagnetic sensitivity suggested that something was awry, and after careful analysis they discovered a pattern to the attacks. They also realized that they were ill-equipped to fight back against an enemy that could be anyone, and strike from anywhere.

      The plan for the Great Barrier was conceived and followed through within the deepest layers of the Great Matrix. The vast majority of the Borg did not realize what was happening until after the Barrier had been activated. The Doyen were violently cast out of the Borg system. There, at the Barrier’s edge, the Doyen seethed at the insult, while the Borg continued to search for and devise some means by which they could truly strike back at their enemy.

      This state of siege has persisted for the last two centuries. Most of the Doyen involved in the early phase of the war have moved on to more pressing matters (like those pernicious little primates called Humans), content that the Borg are contained within their own fear and paranoia for now. Only a handful remain at the Barrier’s edge, testing it for weaknesses. They believe it is only a matter of time until the Borg run out of the resources necessary to power the Barrier. They are correct in that belief.

      The Borg know that their time is running out. Although they can fabricate most anything they desire, they still require energy to power their machine shells and the Great Barrier. The Matrix has calculated that they will have exhausted the available resources in their own solar system within a generation, if not sooner. To make matters worse, various factions within Borg society have grown restless. Each one proposes a different solution to their current state, but so far none have achieved the political power necessary to enact it.

      Some want to drop the Barrier and take the fight straight to the Doyen, believing that the weapons they have developed during their siege will win out. Some wish to abandon their home system, and attempt to flee to the outer reaches of the universe where there are no Doyen. One faction looks inward, attempting to reach noetic totality as a means to escape--none of the other factions see this as practical. For now, the Borg send desperate signals into the night, hoping that someone, or some force, will answer.

          Now, for something that can be dropped on whatever strange planet your PCs happen to find themselves on.

          Gargoyles

          Description
          The “Gargoyles” are a non-sentient genus and the apex predator of their planet, with several different species and subspecies scattered throughout. They all share several common traits, which consist of a bulky, saurian-like body with four primary limbs, the forearms complete with a set of large wings and large hind legs, and one set of secondary limbs located just beneath their torso that are capable of basic manipulation. These secondary limbs are usually--but not in every species--long enough to reach their large, box-like heads. Their winged forelimbs also possess large fingers with broad, flat knuckles that they use to lope around on the ground in a manner similar to Earth primates.

          Smaller species possess a long tail for balance in flight, while the larger specimens have only short, stubby tails, and spend most of their time on the ground. How they are capable of flight at all is a mystery, but one that they are not cognizant enough to ponder.

          Every species is carnivorous, and have adapted to hunt and devour nearly every other variety of fauna on their planet, including each other if it becomes necessary. They vary greatly in size from species to species; the smallest of them is the same general size as an Earth brown rat, and can be found in almost every environment on the planet. The largest, by contrast, are the size of large trucks, and dwell primarily on coastal cliffs where they can prey on sea life that comes close enough to the surface. The most common, however, are the wolf-sized pack hunters that dwell in the planet’s temperate forests, ambushing their prey from thick upper canopies.

          Discovery
          Explorers to the Gargoyles’ home planet shouldn’t have much trouble encountering the beasts. Typically, this will be accompanied by panicked screaming and weapons fire, as the gargoyles determine whether or not these strange new creatures are edible.

          A skilled psion will be able to easily detect a Gargoyle's presence, sensing a weak but innate psi ability. Any researcher who spends adequate time studying the creatures will discover that they supplement their clumsy flight with Telekinesis.

          With time and effort, psions will be able to establish a rapport with the more intelligent subspecies. The Qin will also be intensely curious about them, to see if their template is suitable as beasts of burden. The unscrupulous will also be interested in smuggling the animals off-plant to sell on the black market. They will find out the hard way that Gargoyles are not easily subdued.

          History
          Anyone who digs through the planet's fossil record will find that the Gargoyles evolved from small, flightless creatures roughly 200 million years ago. They had fierce competition from other (relatively) large predators until a mass extinction event occurred roughly 75 to 50 million years ago.

          Their basal, cliff dwelling ancestors were able to survive in rocky burrows, and soon after evolved crude wings and gliding. They have been the dominant predators, if not the dominant genus, on the planet ever since.

          How they developed psionic aptitude, normally found in higher life forms, remains a mystery. If it was the result of alien experiments, the shockwave of a distant noetic disaster, or even plain mutation, there is no record of it. Xenobiologists and paleontologists will surmise that it had something to do with the planet's last extinction event, but beyond that the origins of the Gargoyles' abilities are lost to time.

              Here's something a little closer to home, inspired by the Tasty Bits: Pets pdf.

              The Huang-Marr “Neo-Xenos” Series

              In an effort to distinguish itself in the already crowded designer pet market, Huang-Marr looked for a cheap, but lucrative licensing deal. Unfortunately, the only one they could afford was a half-forgotten media property from the late 20th century.

              After several grotesquely failed attempts to superficially alter existing bio-pet templates, Huang-Marr’s R&D department finally had luck combining desired traits from an invertebrate template with a simian one. The results were grown in special bioware wombs, and the “Neo-Xenos” were brought to the market. The horrific little creatures are marketed mostly to eccentric collectors and horror-vid aficionados.

              Neo-Xenos (so named because “Yutanis” didn’t test well) are the size of a house cat, and the base model looks like a monkey wrapped in a glossy black exoskeleton, with a bizarrely elongated skull and an extra set of mandibles that extend from the throat. It lacks the ooze and slime of its movie version, and isn’t capable of inflicting any more damage than a particularly vicious alley cat would be capable of.

              OpNet rumors abound with tales of other small pets going missing from the neighborhoods that one of these things calls home. Some say that a breeding pair escaped their owner, and is slowly multiplying in the local sewer system. Another rumor insists that these creatures, like their horror movie counterparts, lay eggs in the stomachs of other creatures. They gestate and emerge in the same famously gory fashion, too.

              Huang-Marr insists that these are nothing more than vicious lies and slander spread by their competitors. None of the creatures are capable of reproduction by design, and since they are bioware they require the same nutrient injections or formatting that any other bio-app would. Seriously, it would require some sort of random mutation in the growth matrix or purposeful sabotage for any of those rumors to be even remotely true. Right?

                          The Nagah

                          Description
                          The Nagah, as they in fact refer to themselves, are one of the rare humanoid species in the galaxy. They look like the “snake men” out of pulp fiction and fantasy tales; human-shaped legs, arms, and torso with a serpent’s head, and maybe a tail. They dress mostly in heavy jewelry and thin cloth, and prefer a warm, humid climate.

                          Despite their outward physiology, they are in fact warm-blooded. They reproduce sexually, and lay egg clutches of anywhere from three to five at a time, with more or less eggs being rare outliers. The eggs are kept in a communal hatchery, and the young are raised by specially trained priests and priestesses.

                          The Nagah are deeply religious and ceremonial, conducting even the most basic day to day events with ritualistic care. Their society is highly stratified into distinct castes, each one serving a purpose. These castes can be differentiated by ritual scars and dyed scale patterns--but don’t expect outsiders to have an easy time of deciphering them.

                          The highest castes among the Nagah are priest-kings and nobles, and they are not only markedly different through their attire, but also their ability to manipulate quantum forces in displays that the rest of their society regards as miracles.

                          Discovery
                          The most obvious likelihood of Humanity discovering the Nagah is when one of their priest-kings unleashes a great burst of quantum energies through the use of its powers. They’ll quickly discover that Nagah society is not homogenous--there are several clans and tribes scattered across the planet, and many of them engage in blood feuds and tribal warfare like it’s a national sport.

                          It is during these minor wars that the Nagah’s priest-kings push their powers to their limits. Though no Nagah has yet displayed what would be considered Aberrant Syndrome, these clashes often leave Quantum Flux zones that can devastate large swathes of land and leave them uninhabitable. The Nagah are not yet capable of large-scale devastation--they are still deep in the analogue of what would be their Bronze Age, and rely on relatively simple technologies for their cities, farms, and trade.

                          Psions entering the system will detect one of these bursts of quantum power unleashed without difficulty. The real question is will they choose to investigate?

                          What PC would pass up the chance?

                          Any Earth human who attempts to make contact via landing ship or psionic power will be regarded as either an equal and honored quest by the priest-kings, or a demon sent by their enemies. Either encounter is sure to be memorable.

                          The Nagah will be surprised that visitors do not share their ritualistic nature, but any display of power will be enough to convince them that these visitors should be dealt with patiently. They will invite visitors on hunts, raids against their enemies, and to join them as they pay reverence to their god, the great Quetzacoatl, who slumbers beneath the planet’s largest volcano.

                          History
                          Despite the fact that the Nagah appear to be an ancient and unique culture, their native language is a derivative of Proto-IndoEuropean, and much of their art and architecture appears to be a mix of Babylonian and early Southeast Asian. Any clairsentient skilled in Psychometry who uses the mode to dig into the Nagah’s past will discover that their existence, despite the Nagah’s fervent beliefs to the contrary, doesn’t go back farther than fifty years.

                          The reason is that they did not exist before then. Fifty years ago, a young man named David Nesbitt erupted as a nova. David was a lifelong science fiction and fantasy fan, and when he discovered that he had the ability to manipulate life on the molecular level, he did what any nerd would do: he created his own race.

                          David didn’t create the Nagah right away. He started out creating designer lifeforms for the wealthy and elite, including other novas. Many of these creatures became weapons during the Aberrant War. David didn’t care about the war--he sank further into his private world, “birthing” his creations on his private island. When it became clear that Earth was no longer safe, he hired another Aberrant to warp himself and his creations away.

                          David immediately killed his “ally” after they arrived on the planet. He had no desire to share his world, and he was afraid that the warper would lead others of their kind to the planet. He knew that they would despoil and destroy his paradise just as they had Earth.

                          He spent the next few decades refining and perfecting his world. The first few attempts were unsatisfactory, so he got rid of them. There is no evidence that they ever existed, except as ripples in the noetic past.

                          David was finally satisfied when he created the Nagah. He created them and their culture, using bits from his favorite stories and Earth history. When he finished, he felt massively drained. He’d pushed his powers to their limit more than once in a relatively short span, and the Flux coursing through him needed to be channeled. He now rests in Chrysalis, hidden away inside one of the planet’s volcanic mountain ranges.

                          The Nagah call him Quetzacoatl, and speak of him in hushed and reverent tones when they speak of him at all. To them he is a great and terrible god that created them millennia ago. They war with one another in his name, trying to prove which is the strongest and most worthy to stand at his side.

                              I often come up with ideas for alien species by surfing Pintrest until I find something that makes me go, "Ooh! That might be neat." This is an example of what happens. This entry is a little bit drier that the previous ones, sorry. I kind of got caught up in it.

                              Titanodactyl

                              These creatures roughly resemble Earth giraffes, hence their name. They are large, two-toed mammal-like creatures with a tough keratin shell covering their backs, and a long neck ending in an oblong head. The resemblance ends there.

                              They are easily half-again as tall as any giraffe, and their legs are notably thicker. Their hooves are broader, and end in a sharp edge that is useful for self-defense and digging.

                              They have a pair of long, feather-like protuberances extending from their shoulders. These appear to be for display amongst themselves, using them to attract mates or ward off rivals, with males’ being notably longer and brighter than females’. These “feathers” are made of keratin, but are not nearly as sturdy as their armor.

                              Their heads do not possess a giraffe’s ossicones, but their ears are quite similar in both structure and auditory acuity. Their eyes, by contrast, are so small as to be nearly invisible when the creature is viewed at a distance, and their eyesight is extremely poor. Their senses of smell and taste, however, are extremely acute by any metric, and they possess a pair of glands just below their nostrils that are used to detect pheromones.

                              Their most notable feature is their tongues, which are nearly identical in both structure and use to that of an Earth anteater. They are covered in an incredibly sticky, numbing mucus and micro-barbules which will latch on to anything they contact.

                              This tongue and their sharp hooves are extremely useful when feeding, as the Titanodactyl is not a vegetarian, but an insectivore. Their main prey are a native arthropod that, much like an Earth termite or ant, build truly enormous hives out of mud mixed with wood detritus. That these “bugs” are nearly the size of a housecat may explain why the Titanodactyl is itself so large.

                              The Titanodactyl will first use its sharp hooves to open up a hive by rising up on its hind legs and kicking at the mud and dirt. This opens up easy access for its young, as well as the myriad of smaller creatures that symbiotically follow the Titanodactyl in its shadow. It will then use its tongue to reach into whatever opening it can find. The tongue latches onto the insectoids, and pulls them into the Titanodactyl’s jaw for mastication and consumption.

                              The Titanodactyl lives in sparsely forested grasslands, where a mature specimen has no natural predators. Only the very young, old, or sickly have anything to fear from predatory species. Any that attempt to prey on a mature one, or a youngling when their parents are nearby, swiftly find out that the Titanodactyl’s sharp hooves are an excellent defensive weapon. Pods with newborns are often followed by scavengers who wait not to feed on the Titanodactly, but any predator foolish enough to get too close.

                              They are the largest terrestrial creatures in their home environment, and possibly the largest on their planet. They live in pods of anywhere from five to a dozen, with only one to three breeding pairs depending on size. They give live birth to a single offspring every other mating season, allowing for a great deal of variance in the size of individuals within the pod.

                              Titanodactyls typically communicate through pheromones released from glands near their hind quarters, but are also capable of vocalizing in high-pitched oscillations that can reach far beyond the pheromones’ range, up to several kilometers. They typically only use these sounds to signal the discovery of food, to scare off competitors, or if one member of the pod is in distress.

                              Curiously, members of a pod will gather together at each sunset, and repeat the same series of sounds in what one researcher naively described as “singing.” The true purpose of this behavior has yet to be determined, as each “song” is different from pod to pod.

                              Once each planetary year, all of the pods within a given geographic area (no more than a few hundred hectares) will gather together in a large herd. It’s during this time that freshly mature bulls will compete for the right to mate, often joining another pod or creating new pods entirely. This event will typically last for a few days, depending on the number of participants.

                              The last evening of the event is marked by each pod reciting their “song”. The next morning, the pods go their separate ways, their “songs” noticeably changed by the event.
