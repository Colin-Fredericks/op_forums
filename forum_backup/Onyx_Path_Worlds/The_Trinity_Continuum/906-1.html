        We're tweaking the backstory a bit - it will be different, but also still perfectly recognizable. However, there will be a few surprises, and also a few obvious changes (for example, wireless voice and data are no long strictly limited and exceedingly expensive, PCs can have smartphones & AR glasses). However, the big change is that we aren't keeping secrets from the GM. The secrets section of the Aeon core book won't have details on everything, but will give a broad overview of all the actual truths of the setting. Also, future supplements will provide more detail, but won't radically change the setting. The change in setting is more like the difference between cWoD 2nd eds and cWoD Revised, but we aren't going to have an evolving metaplot that will radically alter the setting. As an example of what I'm saying, Aeon will be set shortly after Upeo Wa Macho has returned.

                                                Originally posted by The Revenge of TV Head View Post
                                                It is an awesome power. But it's a bitch and a half to balance while still keeping fun.
                                                The balance depends on the players and environment just like it does for the other types, besides the chronic problem of the game system itself threatening to fall apart at higher levels the mechanics of the balance in regard to teleport powers is not much if any worse than it is for the others. Of course how the group interprets the rules makes a difference so the relative effective power of the various psionic aptitudes can be different from group to group.

                                                That said, there is a complication with the teleporters and quantakinetics in that there are two entirely different balance points for each of the two depending on exactly when the game is set. Pre purge / pre exodus games where the character can openly use their powers they both tend to be slightly on the high side but not overpoweringly so whereas post purge / exodus games mean that they have to masquerade as members of other orders and do things that could be explained as effect overlap with other aptitudes, the use of secondary modes, or else do things with such subtlety that it does not appear that they used psionics to do it at all which limits their power considerably in “public”.

                                                I ran a campaign that had both a Upeo and an “India Underground” style Chib in it along with a mix of the usual types that worked out rather well.

                                                ** Sorry about the length of the post, I would have folded it here with a spoiler tag if I knew how to make one. The gist of it is above so you can skip the rest if you are not interested in the details of how I came to the conclusion above. **

                                                The teleporter was a protégé of Khaldun al-Masna who came from the same kind of Legion background though she grew up in the war-torn American southwest. When, like al-Masna, she was tagged as a latent psi it was unclear whether she was teleporter or seer material so she was sent to the then convalescent semi-retired Legion teleporter for evaluation. In his relatively new (and still unknown outside the Upeo) role as Monitor chief he recognized her potential as a deep cover agent and after recruiting her for it sent word back that no, she was not jumper material, she was primarily a clairsentient candidate with the probability of teleport secondary modes (when she was really the opposite).

                                                Taking advantage of the rare “black market” in dunkings and ISRA’s sloppy organizational security, Upeo agents arraigned a carefully orchestrated shell game to have their newly Upeo-dunked mole slipped into the ISRA recovery room and smuggle out the new “unregistered” clear that was actually dunked in the ISRA tank in place of the PC. After ISRA training in the basics of clairvoyance she was sent back to the Legion before anyone noticed that despite a strong psi potential she could not do anything beyond the most basic levels of clairvoyance.

                                                In the Legion she is an exceptional scout and artillery spotter, her uncanny ability to get into and out of hazardous locations undetected is generally chalked up to her supposed clairvoyant abilities coupled to stealth and climbing/wriggling skills learned in her life in an FSA guerilla warzone. She keeps close ties with her friends in ISRA too and when she is detached to go on “merc rotation” often works with the Qadiyya, especially on dangerous cases were someone with experience in deadly situations is a plus (and of course it is an excellent place to pick up information on goings on in the Order and elsewhere).


                                                She did such a good job of both infiltrating the Legion and keeping an ear to goings on in ISRA that when the ball dropped and the Upeo bugged out it was decided to leave her in place to continue her intelligence gathering and also to look into the looming malevolent presence Atwan so strongly suspected was manipulating the Orders but could not quite put her finger on.


                                                The Chib was one of a pair of “twin cousins” like Patty and Cathy Lane in the old “Patty Duke” show who like the girls in the show looked alike but were quite different in personality and potentials (though also like them they could easily impersonate each other so well that they could even fool their parents). One turned out to be a quantakinetic and the other electrokinetic, one more studious the other more artistic, etc.

                                                On the fateful day of the purge the two were impersonating each other, with the spark in India spending some time with her boyfriend (one of her cousin’s peers) and the Chib in New York with her American cousin’s family and the arts scene there. Her cousin and parents were killed and in the days following she had several close calls with the intense search for Chitra Bhanu survivors, each one getting closer to detecting her. Worn down by grief, exhausted from nightmare wracked sleep, and constantly near panic she eventually confessed the switch to her uncle and he turned to his employer, Aeon, for help.

                                                They kept her on ice (literally, in suspended animation), tucked away under Lake Michigan for a few years until the furor of the hunt was long gone. Afterward, since she was more than willing to completely throw her lot in with Aeon they did not keep her locked up though they kept her moving from safehouse to safehouse while she went through training and indoctrination and their study of her while her while they made sure no one picked her trail up. She emerged as a promising young field scientist with supposedly mostly neglected low end electrokinetic powers and an excellent shot at being a science officer on one of the new starships.


                                                The campaign started out with the PCs involved in a project that was meant as a mix of potentially valuable exploration and research into the most inaccessible areas of the European badlands with an eye towards improving overall conditions, training for hostile environment exploration and combat, and field testing the technology for that before it is cleared for general deployment, with of course a generous dash of PR for the organizations involved as well.

                                                In that context there was little problem with balance with the teleport or the quantakinetic powers in either “stealth” mode or full out in the latter part of the campaign. Playing an underground character like that is not for everyone though, it only works well if the player is willing to accept that their power use is limited compared to the others and that they have to concentrate more on skills and equipment use. Later on the campaign moved into space and there was a bit more freedom for the “underground” characters to use their powers and as I said earlier the balance was still not significantly more difficult than the others.

                                                    Originally posted by John Snead View Post
                                                    However, the big change is that we aren't keeping secrets from the GM. Also, future supplements will provide more detail, but won't radically change the setting.                                                     This is the best news of all. In retrospect, keeping secrets from the GM (or, in our case, ST) may have been what killed a lot of metaplot-heavy RPGs of the millennium's turn (Trinity, Aberrant, Brave New World​ and others I'm probably forgetting); at the very least, it can't have helped sales as much as the publishers who supported the decision thought it would. Quite the reverse, in fact. To paraphrase those DirecTV adverts that keep showing up on my household's Dish Network: When you only reveal the game's metaplot a piece at a time, GMs don't understand the metaplot. When GMs don't understand the metaplot, they can't run the metaplot. When they can't run the metaplot, they don't run the game. When they don't run the game, they don't buy the supplements for it. When they don't buy the supplements, you stop publishing the game. When you stop publishing the game, the rest of the metaplot never gets properly revealed.
