                    A combination of a few things.

                    1. At the conclusion of the Aberrant War, there were two novas who spent a great deal of their time within the OpNet, existing as energy signals "riding the rails", so to speak. One of them was a Terat named Synapse, who was very anti-baseline but was also relatively sane due to Chrysalis. The other was Mungu Kuwasha, now known as "Backlash", who had started out pro-baseline but gradually went insane over the decades due to Taint. Backlash ambushed Synapse, and the two of them fought for hours across the OpNet. Eventually, Synapse won -- but Backlash's "death scream" was essentially a quantum-powered EMP that blasted throughout the OpNet and shorted out most, if not all, technological devices connected to it. All electronic information that wasn't in air-gapped servers or printed in hard copy file cabinets was lost. This includes a significant amount of technological know-how.

                    2. Many inventions created by novas are what are termed "Advancements" in the game system. Advancements are a form of technology based on mundane, legitimate scientific knowledge -- they're very advanced, though, so they're much better than anything anyone else is doing. An advancement is anything that a baseline could theoretically make allowed by the Laws of Nature. The OpNet, synthetic eufiber, the hyper-combustion engine, and flying cars are all examples of advancements. HOWEVER, there is another type of invention that novas can create. These are termed "Innovations". Innovations are devices which are not based on scientific principles -- they are created as an extension of a nova's world-changing Quantum powers. Essentially, these Innovations are just devices that somehow recreate a Quantum power. Because only novas can channel and properly control Quantum, an Innovation requires a nova operator (or at least, a nova technician to make sure it stays in working order). When the novas and aberrants left after the Ultimatum, there was no one on Earth who could maintain any of the incredibly advanced devices. Example Innovations would include climate-control devices, global CO2 scrubbers, terraforming equipment, interstellar spacecraft, RNA sequences that let infected individuals adapt to any environment, a Cerebro-like device, etc.

                    Combined, this means that when the novas left, they took their understanding of their Innovations with them. And when the OpNet failed, Earth lost most (but not all) of its understanding of the Advancements made during the Nova Age, as well.

                        A few ways -

                        Many of the environmental fixes were the result of nova powers or nova technology. Many of the more radical nova Tech inventions operated using the same "breaking the laws of physics" principles the novas themselves employed. Without a Nova to maintain it, much of the Nova Tech simply ceased to function.

                        The Aberrant War was devastating. Some of the more powerful Nova's were natural disasters on legs. The result of these forces in conflict crippled large portions of the existing infrastructure. Most notably, a clash between two cyber-kinetics destroyed the op -net (like the internet, but better), permanently erasing a vast amount of information stored on servers with online access.

                        As a result, many of the post-war factions were very leery of anything that seemed Aberrant-like, probably including a fair amount on non-Nova advancements.
                        All in all, for a few decades the world went into a mini - dark age.

                        Curios, Relics, and Tomes - A collection of Relics (Cursed and Otherwise)
                        The Horror Lab - A collection of Beasts, Monsters and less definable things.
                        Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
                        Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities

                                    Chrysalis is a means by which members of the Teragen could manage their Taint -- essentially letting them guide and direct their own "evolution" instead of just letting it happen. The most prominent and favorable feature of Chrysalis was the ability to take mental aberrations and turn them into physical aberrations -- it could not forestall your distancing from baseline humanity, but it could keep you completely sane and of sound mental state. The best explanation I've heard is that Taint turns you into an insane human monster, while Chrysalis turns you into a sane inhuman monster.

                                    Project Rewrite (sorry, I got the name wrong the first time) was an effort by the Aeon Society to "fix" the damage wrought by the destruction of the OpNet. With the loss of so much information, Aeon tried to collate all of the remaining knowledge it could -- be it from computers that were thankfully disconnected from the OpNet at the time, or hard copies left in boxes in someone's basement. They did not, however, just re-upload all of this information: they went through and selectively chose what to allow back into the world. Information that they deemed "dangerous" or in opposition to their world-view was suppressed and removed. They pretty much used the whole thing as a way to completely and totally revise history to their preferences. In this way, successive generations gradually forgot about the mistakes Project Utopia (and, by extension, the Aeon Society) made with novas. Big things like that weren't exactly forgotten in their entirety, but they were put in a different light. For example, the loss of the OpNet: while it was actually a result of two novas fighting each other, that idea didn't fit the concept of turning novas and aberrants into a monolithic opposition force (why would two guys on the same "side" try so hard to kill each other?). Instead, they simply removed Synapse from most records. In the version of history most people in the Trinity Era know, Backlash destroyed the OpNet "just to see if he could".

                                                            Hello all, thank you for keeping this inspired setting alive. (pun intended) I legitimately got goosebumps and butterflies in my stomach when i saw there were actual publishers interested in continuing the setting.

                                                            I did find myself often wondering how i could disable the Zushima Macrobe created by Dr "Antaeus" Balmer, as i agree with Zhad above, that an ecologically bankrupted earth yields a few new possibilities the utopian 'magic macrobe' perspective doesn't lend itself to. The ideas sometimes dropped by futurists for limited terraforming of the earth to allow further exploitation are just too heart-breaking to pass up on (e.g. stained white magnesium skies), just the kind of arrogance that fits in with the setting in my opinion.
                                                            Some of the climate change would even open up certain areas of the world for story development - the thawing arctic ocean and terraformed reserviors in the UAN (and other newly developed states) to bleed off increased sea levels in vast new maritime environs could make for interesting boom towns and even nations considering the scale. A thriving economy and ecology in what was once unused desert or plains could help mitigate some of the lost hope from the fall of the mother earth. The setting i love walked that fine line between utopia and dystopia, but as time passed this topic started to stick out like a sore thumb - although easily fixed.

                                                            As mentioned above; I had always assumed that certain bits of super-science found in the original Trinity setting were dubious Nova inventions (Innovations) that became too useful too mothball or simply impossible to stop, like the macrobe. Psuedo gravity stuck out the most here - Zhad's logic directly above my post (To paraphrase; Where are the derivative weapons and other gravitational tech?) is the reason i assumed the post-utopia society locked away technological advances that were poorly understood. That some of these technologies slipped through the loophole was necessitated by the Rewrite project or other goals championed by the Trinity organization (generation arks, terraforming, etc...)
                                                            I assumed hyper-fusion birlesme chambers were also in this category and Banji monopolized the industry from the (admittedly unpublished) Nihonjin remnants described in the web supplement "Asia Ascendant" or whatever it was called...
                                                            In any case, the ecological strain on earth isn't the only planetary concern in the setting (especially after 2121 and the return of the Upeo and renewal of extrasolar exploration/settlement) and while certain pockets of ecological balance clearly exist in the original source material (Sudamerica stands out) - the ruined, depleted earth is a powerful motivator for expansion into the solar system and beyond. We already have the world in tatters after 2061 and earth's resources and sustainability should be easy enough to slip in. It would go a long way to adressing the (impressively few) scientific discrepancies between the original setting and a more modern understanding. The pockets of balance could be realistically maintained with industrious effort where needed, using biotech and theoretically sound hard-tech solutions in concert.
                                                            I personally consider the setting's (mostly) hard science a strength - anything outside of our current framework is given a decent reason for being there. (e.g. Nova Innovations, Inspired Objects/Locations, Godlike Doyen/Aberrants) Why, i'd even see sense in a kind of suspended-reality for a period after (and even before) the Hammersmith effect and Process 418, not unlike the different galactic zones in Vernor Vinge's "A Fire in the Sky".
                                                            So i hope the new books take a few lines to deal with this - after-all it really doesn't require much work.
                                                            Thanks for reading.
