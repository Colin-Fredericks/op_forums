    [TC 2E] Magic in Aether and Adventure! 😲

    08-02-2022, 11:27 AM
    WE HAVE MAGIC IN TRINITY! 😲

    P. S. Only I think that Magic is precursor to later Psi energies in later eras? 🤔

    Quoting newest Aether Preview :
    'Scientific Practices
    In Aether, there are three major scientific practices: Advanced Science, Aetheric Science, and Mystical Practices.
    Advanced Science is the rule of the day. It’s responsible for the many steam-powered contraptions that are now commonplace modern conveyances: trains, steamships, and balloons. Its advances aren’t limited to hardware. Selective breeding of livestock also falls under the purview of Advanced Science.
    Aetheric Science is only in its infancy, but already it produces fanciful contraptions once relegated to fiction — submersibles that explore the mysteries undiscovered beneath the waves and grand dirigibles that ply the skies the same way sailing ships travel the seas. Dread whispers also tell tales of Aether’s ability to even bestow life on dead flesh. The ranks of the scientific elite keep Aetheric Science from the general population. Finally, Mystical Practices are not new to the world but are just beginning to gain acceptance in the halls of academia. They’re responsible such phenomena as tarot decks that foretell the future, channeling the voices of the dead, and even a painting that ages instead of its owner. Practitioners of Advanced and Aetheric Sciences scoff at such things as mere superstition, but none who witness such workings can deny their potency. Anyone’s capable of Advanced Science, but Aetheric Science and Mystical Practices may only be used by Inspired individuals. One must be able to touch the Aether to manipulate it. Advanced Science works as described in the Trinity Continuum Core (p. 90). Aetheric Science and Mystical Practices are both variations on Inspired Science and function the same as the Trinity Continuum Core instructs with their exceptions outlined below.'

    And
    ' Mystic Artifacts Mystic Artifacts are those whose principles can be explained by neither steam nor Aether. The only path to understanding them is through Mystical Practices. They create effects beyond the ken of humanity. A Mystic Artifact might be a pair of spectacles that allow the wearer to see beyond the veil and interact with lingering spirits, a tarot deck that shows the subjects whatever source of harm is mostly likely to befall them next, or an amulet that burns red hot in the presence of a lie. Mystic Practices can come from anywhere — the wisdom passed down in a family from one generation to the next, the traditions of a druid grove or a coven of witches, or the long forgotten magickal lore fiercely guarded by Samuel Liddell MacGregor Mather and the Golden Dawn. They may even be a unique phenomenon manifested by a single individual, unable to be replicated by anyone else. Wherever they come from, the Mystic Artifacts they produce are equal in potency to any Aetheric Contraption. Their workings are only more intangible than those of the material sciences.
    Creating Mystic Artifacts Creating Mystic Artifacts largely follows the path laid out in the Trinity Continuum Core for Inspired Science (p. 90), except for a few notable changes. Mystic Artifacts usually produce mental, social, or spiritual effects, the type of things largely impossible for mundane technology to achieve. The flaws a creator unwittingly introduces to them are of a similar vein. They affect the mind, perceptions, and emotions of the bearer. A gentle soul is consumed by murderous rage, or a normally level-headed individual finds his hold on sanity growing increasingly tenuous. Mystic Artifacts don’t require research, experimentation, or the manual labor of assembly. Instead, their creation revolves around meditation, the proper alignment of the heavens, prayers to otherworldly entities, and ritual actions. These Artifacts all require rare, occasionally otherworldly, materials and arcane preparations. These peculiar requirements may result in additional Complications to an Artifact’s creation process. A character’s Paths are vital to any Mystic Artifacts she creates. They’ll inform the approach she takes creating them and their resulting form and function. A traditional witch and an expert in numerology are all capable of creating an Artifact that warns of impending danger, but the way they construct it and thefinal form it takes varies. The witch hails from the Occultist Origin Path. Her early warning Artifact is a set of runes carved from the bones of an animal she sacrificed to the Earth Goddess. Casting the runes tells if a course of action is safe or if it’s likely to result in her physical harm. The numerologist possesses the Academic Role from his career of studying numbers and their hidden meanings. By careful thought and introspection, he creates a mathematical formula through which the numbers surrounding him reveal if a space is safe or not.
    The Skills that apply to Aetheric Science rarely apply when creating a Mystic Artifact. Enigmas or Culture are often more appropriate than Science when planning a new Artifact, and Enigmas or Science take the place of Technology when performing the actions. A Mystical Practitioner relies on her mastery over mind and spirit or her investigations into the supernal mysteries to forge Artifacts with power beyond the mere physical. Any attempts to repair or reverse engineer Mystic Artifacts likewise require knowledge from within or expertise in that which lies beyond the mundane. No amount of mastery over biology, chemistry, or physics will allow an inventor to work on an Artifact that operates on a spiritual plane. While a Mystical Practitioner and an Aetheric Scientist find each other’s works impossible, they’re still able to collaborate and create an Artifact or Contraption that synthesizes their expertise. Working together, disciples of both practices can enhance an item from one field to incorporate elements of the other or even invent entirely new devices containing Mystical and Aetheric features. They allow the two creators to operate and maintain their own creation, which makes the contraption nearly impossible to reverse engineer if it’s ever stolen. The thief would need an accomplice with knowledge of the practice he’s ignorant in.'

              Basically, yes. That said, Adventure! doesn't restrict Artifacts of Enigmatic Power to mesmerists; it just says that the effects tend not to be physical.                       Psi has this "magic feeling", but also does Quantum and Aether (and Flux to certain extend).
                      Magic is a complex thing to define, you have Force that operates as magic in StarWars, and in the other hand you have all the powers of the BG from Dune that are not magical at all, while can do almost everything force can (except moving things with the mind).
                      Psi is not different, in Aeon it is completely studied and analized, they have labs and can create technology with it to be used by the general populaton, that is very non-magical. In Adventure! many of the characters see their own powers as magical, and there are a lot of objects that are magical in nature (dont have an scientific explanation to work as they do), yet it is psi (mostly).
                      Quantum has the same aspect, not understood in Adventure! but explored as something scientific in Aberrant and Aeon.
                      Honestly, TC, if not for the sci-fi bases, is basically a fantasy setting in the modern/pulp/future/steampunk era.

                          Trinity might be soft and pulpy sci-fi, but I still prefer it as science fiction where there's no such thing as magic. Personal opinions will vary, but to me anything 'magical' is just stuff the user/witness doesn't understand. Anybody who could tell the future via a tarot deck either has a deck made via the principles of Quantum, Psi, or Flux, or is telling the future themselves and would do it just as well if they were throwing darts at a corkboard or staring into the flames.

                          People's opinions will differ from mine, but to me this is what separates Trinity 2e from Chronicles of Darkness. The latter is a grim world of unknown magical where monsters lurk in the shadows to devour us, whereas Trinity is a bright world of Science! where something might be unexplained now, but it eventually will be.

                          This is, of course, a separate issue to characters believing their powers to be magic. I suspect that magic in Aether will primarily cover the abilities of those psi and quantum users running around at this point, whereas aetheric science will primarily be based around Flux. But I'm also pretty certain that the categories won't be that neat, and you'll find a bit of everything everywhere.
