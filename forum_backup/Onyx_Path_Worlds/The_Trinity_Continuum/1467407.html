    The Argument for Scale 2 Firearms

    11-10-2021, 11:05 PM
    Having established that direct dev feedback on the topic is unobtainable (either deleted or just lost), I thought I'd discuss this as general community sentiment seems to contradict the books and I think there are good arguments on either side that could do with a little testing.

    OK, to start with, let's get the obvious out of the way. Regardless of what may or may not have been said in these forums by devs or others, the books reference firearms as being Scale 2. Pistols specifically in Core and both Pistols & Rifles in Aberrant. Æon doesn't give Scale for their weapons ( they do show 'slug throwers' have more tags & therefore a higher Enhancement). Annoyingly, they don't reference Scale at all in their later tables listing weapons, their Enhancement, and tags. Which is what actually made us talk about it at our gaming table - knives, guns, and unarmed attacks in those tables look to have the same effectiveness and, as Aussies, that really struck us as being odd as gunshot wounds are 5-8x deadlier than stab wounds let alone injuries from punches/kicks.

    So, "rules as written", guns are Scale 2. But I am a big proponent of "rules as intended". Storypath is not really the kind of crunchy system anyone should "game" using poor grammar or editing. It's one of the more cooperative narrative-focused games with "just enough" crunch to let the yarn spinners completely run away with the story. Perhaps, being based in Pennsylvania, Onyx Path devs actually don't consciously differentiate that much between being stabbed or being shot. I will not rule that out having had way too many a... "spirited debate" with friends & colleagues in the US on the subject of firearms.

    Which brings me to the difference between fists, knives, and guns in action movies (i.e. the oft-stated genre Trinity is trying to emulate). In the movies, at least the ones I watch, you can pound on action heroes with your fists A LOT, you can slash or stab them a couple of times, but shoot them and they are (at the very least) injured by it. John McClane (& the more important villains he fights) take an absolute POUNDING in fist-fight after fist-fight. But when push comes to shove, point a gun at their chest and they either give up or die. One shot, one kill most of the time. John Wick stabs & gets stabbed repeatedly, but gun shots either kill or severely injure. I could go on, but I think I've made the point. Whilst the movies have bullets flying everywhere, when they hit someone - they hurt. A lot. The action heroes are cool, not because they can take multiple bullets to the chest, but because they're capable of not getting hit (whilst taking out each bad guy with one bullet).

    Mechanically, Scale 2 makes more sense (for me) too because Scale with weapons isn't just about Enhancement, it's also about the Inflict Damage stunt & how often it can be applied. A Scale 1 weapon vs a Scale 1 durability opponent means (at most) a Bruised and an Injury. That's at best, with no extra Defence from the Resilience stat roll and requiring 5 successes (1 to overcome Defence, free Inflict Damage stunt to Bruise, and 4 to Critical for the extra Inflict Damage stunt to Injure). For fists or knives, this makes some sense. But the idea that punching an action hero in the jaw and shooting them in the chest works out the same is (again, to me & my table) against the tropes of the genre. Adding one level of Scale to firearms over knives/fists, makes it possible to actually Maim someone by shooting them in the chest (due to the extra Inflict Damage stunt allowed per Scale).

    Thoughts / opinions? (Am responding to previous comments below)

Sniper rifles and elephant guns are specialized weapons used in specialized circumstances, and the users are presumed to be experts trained to take the issue into account.

                                  The default assumption of the system is that the Scale of guns and armor is appropriate to the Size Scale of the weapon. (Not taking into account any tags.) Humans are Size 1 therefore human-Scale guns and armor are Scale 1. A gun suitable for a car is Scale 2, one for a Jet Fighter Scale 4 etc.

                                  However, all Scale is relative as defined by the needs of the narrative, and genre emulation comes into this. For action hero genre where the only people who die with one shot are mooks, guns and armor stay at Scale 1 - at most they’ll wound a hero with two Injury Conditions but they won’t kill them. For slightly grittier games, guns and armor can be Scale 2 - getting shot may kill you, and you need armor to defend against it. (If you increase the base Scale of a human-Sized gun, you probably need to increase the Scale of vehicle-mounted guns to match.)

                                  The key message as applies to TC isn’t ‘what’s the absolute Scale value does X have’, it’s ‘what’s the relative difference between X and Y for my game’.
