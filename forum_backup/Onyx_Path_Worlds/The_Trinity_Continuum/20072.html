    My Evolution of the Trinityverse Continuum Rules

    11-29-2013, 12:12 AM
    OK. So. I briefly looked over the Scion rules and I looked over my notes on the cWoD20 series. This is what I've come up with by way of House Rules for my new Adventure! series that starts early 2014.

    Nature/Virtue/Vice: Expanded this list with archetypes from Mage:tA-revised and PGttTechnocracy which includes Strengths and Weaknesses standing in for Virtues/Vices.

    Origin: Expanded this with a comprehensive list from concepts scattered throughout the cWoD lines and additions.

    Concept: Added this in. For example, "The greatest surgeon in the world!", Unparalleled Stunt Flyer, Raised by Wolves, etc.

    Attributes: 3 categories, standard 9

    Abilities: I took V20, WW20, extrapolated M20 and came up with a list of Talents/Skills/Knowledges that a normal mortal would have in the cWoD. They are: The Mortal 33

    |Talents|Skills|Knowledges|
    |Alertness|Animal Ken|Academics|
    |Athletics|Crafts|Computer|
    |Brawl|Drive|Culture|
    |Empathy|Etiquette|Enigmas|
    |Expression|Firearms|Investigation|
    |Intuition|Larceny|Law|
    |Intimidation|Melee|Medicine|
    |Leadership|Performance|Politics|
    |Streetwise|Stealth|Science|
    |Subterfuge|Survival|Technology|
    |(Hobby Talent)|(Professional Skill)|(Expert Knowledge)|

    In anticipation of the Scion evolution of the rules, I took these, broke them out of categories and listed them alphabetically.

    Advantages: Backgrounds, Knacks, Health Levels, Willpower, Inspiration, Facets, Initiative, Walk/Run/Sprint. Taken directly from Adventure! and included as is. Soak uses optional Lethal soak rule.

    Character Creation: 1 Allegiance background, 1 Concept background, Attributes 6/4/3, Abilities 23, 6 Backgrounds, 3 Willpower, 15 Bonus 5/2/1(specialty)/1/2.

    Transformation: 1 Inspiration, 13 Transformation Points (1 gets you Attr: 2 Abil: 5 Backg: 4 Willp: 2 Insp: 1 Mastery: 1 Enh: .5 Knack: 2 or 1/2/3) No more than 6 Transformation points on Knacks, No more than 3 Knacks.

    Merits and Flaws: Once again, list edited from cWoD, Aberrant, and Trinity. Divided into Physical, Mental, Social, Inspirational. No more than 7 points of Flaws. 1:1 conversion to bonus points. Substituted additional dice or additional successes for difficulty modification.


    Advancement:
    Episode rewards:
    One Point — Automatic: Each player that participated gets one point at the end of each chapter.
    One Point — Discovery: Ask the player: "What did your character learn in the course of the episode’s events?" If you agree with the answer, give the player one experience point.
    One Point — Role-playing/Concept: The player carried out the role of her character well, not only entertainingly but appropriately. The player performed as the character should in the circumstances.
    One Point — Heroism: On occasion characters can truly behave as heroes, risking all to let friends or even strangers escape from disaster or death. If a character acts heroically and manages to survive, he should be rewarded. Some players might try to take advantage of this idea. Stupidity and suicidal behavior should not be mistaken for heroism.
    One Point — Wisdom: The player, and thus the character, came up with a brilliant plan or even a spontaneous strategy that enabled the team to survive when it would likely have failed otherwise. The right thing to say at the right moment. If the character (or player) said or did something that was resourceful, cunning, or just plain brilliant, give them a point.
    One Point — Acting: A special award for the player who was the most exceptional role-player that session. Award a point to the player that was the most entertaining or was the most true to her character concept (perhaps in a way that put them or the rest of the group in more trouble).
    One Point — Success: The characters achieved all or part of the goals they set out to accomplish. The goal doesn’t have to be a complete success. Even minor victories can be rewarded if they pushed the game forward. Even a marginal success should be celebrated.
    One Point — Danger: The characters survived against harsh odds and grave dangers. If the character was in serious danger, they should get a point.

    Costs:
    |Trait Increase|Cost|
    |Attribute|[new rating x 5]|
    |Ability|[new rating x 3]|
    |Willpower |[new rating x 2]|
    |Inspiration |[new rating x 7]|
    |Background |[new rating x 2]|
    |New Trait| Cost|
    |New Ability |[3]|
    |Specialty (max. 3 per ability) |[2]|
    |Ability Mastery (max. 3 total) |[12]|
    |Background |[2]|
    |Background Enhancement|[12]|
    |Knack, Heroic |[12]|
    |Knack, Psychic or Dynamic (level one)| [10]|
    |Knack, Psychic or Dynamic (level two) |[14]|
    |Knack, Psychic or Dynamic (level three)|[18]|
    A character may only buy one trait dot improvement per stat between episodes.
    No character may improve more than one dot in any particular trait between stories or during downtimes.

    Rules: Directly from Adventure! so far. May add more wrinkles as play begins.

    Not sure if I'm looking for feedback on my choices or comments on how close you think I might have come to a new rules set.

    --Jason

      After some looking over some probability charts for different size dice pools and botching in revised cWoD (1s and no successes), and talking with another gamer, I decided to change something in the basic rules.

      "If you have a 5 or 6 in an Ability, you may cancel one or two 1s respectively, when and ONLY when you would Botch." For example, an extremely competent driver is trying a maneuver (Dex 4 + Drive 5, difficulty 7). The player rolls 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5. Ordinarily this would be a botch (1 and no successes). Since this character has Drive 5, he negates that one 1. He still fails, but his skillful driving saved him from the catastrophic failure of a Botch.

      This would represent a character's momentous skill saving them in a situation where they would otherwise botch. I have no idea the effect on probability this has, but I suspect it helps the situation where rolling large pools of dice actually increases your chance of botching disproportionately to succeeding.

      I'm going to try this out in my game and keep track of what anecdotal evidence I find. Any other math people out there are welcome to try and figure out if my hypothesis is correct, please.

      Thank you.

                  New Hack: Changed distribution of points in phase 1 (-1 background, -1 willpower, +6 bonus points) and bonus point ratios (+1 bp per background dot, +1 bp per willpower dot).
                  Changed exchange ratio between transformation points and dots gained per trait to make 1 transformation point worth 10 bonus points (in 3 of 4 cases anyways)

                  Phase 1 Origin: 1 Allegiance: 1 Attributes: 6/4/3 Abilities: 23 Backgrounds: 5 Willpower: 2 Bonus Points: 21 Attr 5/Abil 2/Spec 1/Backg 2/Willp 3
                  Phase 2 Inspiration: 1 Transformation Points: 13 Attributes: 2 Knack: .5 or 1/.5/.67 Backgrounds: 5 Willpower: 3
                  1 Transformation Point Equals Abilities: 5 Ability Mastery: 1 Background Enhancement: .5 Inspiration: 1

                  Previously when experimenting with character building, I'd dump the majority of bonus points into willpower and backgrounds because attributes and abilities had such a better ratio of transformation points to bonus points.

                  Per my previous post, I'm now considering requiring a 4, 5, or 6 in ability in order to purchase an ability specialty. This change and the increase in bonus points brings it closer to the cWoD systems I'm most familiar with.

                                  I started my Adventure game this past weekend. The core ST systems are solid. A few things we ran into:

                                  Extras: We did an example combat where 1 PC took on 5 extras. He was buff and he had the Knack Body of Bronze giving him a bashing soak of 9. He almost died. The extras were able to connect enough times coupled with the minimum 1 dice of bashing damage to reflect a glancing blow that he went through all his bashing levels and started taking lethal.

                                  We quickly revised that minimum damage rule to "character only takes a health level damage if the damage die rolls a 10". After the game session we still didn't find quite what we wanted with that rule and are looking to revise it again for next week.

                                  Initiative: The group found the "declare up-act down" system too cumbersome. We're going to try either a) straight high to low or b) low to high where higher can interrupt lower actions and anyone can abort to a defensive move (willpower roll) or change their action (-1 die from pool).

                                  Extra rules: Stealing a page from V20 and WW20 and using a hack of those Extra rules and Adventure's Extra rules. Hurt (-1), Maimed (-3), Incap, Dead. Also the three additional successes on the attack roll automatically takes out an extra. Extras are still using 1-3 dice to attack (Dex2,Ability1), rather than the single die.

                                      We tweaked things just a little further this week. The new Extra rules worked well. We adopted the armor and weapon ratings from V20 which made things more like I wanted them to be. The really big bear of a Russian (with Body of Bronze) was able to take on 10 super goons shooting at him at once and only take one level of bashing damage. PCs switched between firearms, melee weapons and brawling without being able to find a significant exploit that made one overwhelmingly better than the other.

                                      For initiative, we went from the bottom up, with higher numbers able to interrupt lower and slower characters.

                                      From a GMing pulp standpoint, there was one thing I could have done better. I put in some strange shaking and vibration going through the player's zepplin. It took about 45 minutes of game time for them to figure out it was coming from the front and there was a large tow cable clamped to the nose. I should have just said at the beginning, "There's another violent shaking and shudder that runs through the airship and this you can tell it's clearly coming from the nose."

                                      Adventure! games shouldn't be about inconsequential investigation and problem solving. Clear clues and cues should lead characters to the next stage of where the action is taking place.

                                      The 45 minutes the PCs spent on that investigation was sorely missed at the end of the game during the climactic confrontation. We had to cut the climax off in the middle because we had to end the game by a certain time.

                                      If you've run, thought about running, or played in an Adventure! game before, how prevalent were the presence of Z-Waves and Telluric Energy? Did the game have to conform to the metaplot where Aberrant and AEon were upcoming, or did you let the Series find it's own way?
