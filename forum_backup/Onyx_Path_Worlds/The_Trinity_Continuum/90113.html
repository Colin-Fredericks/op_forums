                                              Originally posted by Jae View Post
                                              One thing I loved about the original Trinity was how it covered various ideologies of the future eg The Joyceans and provided various backgrounds, environments and organisations that reflected those ideologies.

                                              I hope that this will be expanded upon and updated to include Peak Oil, Global weirding (warming), sustainability, pollution, currency wars, class wars, bank coups, the globally linked economy etc and how the setting and people are affected by these issues. Or the consequences of ignoring them and continuing to follow an economic model that demands constant growth in a finite world of diminishing resources and greater population and greater energy demands.
                                              They already deal with a lot of those things in the first edition, some up front and others implied by things like the tech and society.

                                              Peak Oil is not a big issue when most vehicles are powered by batteries charged from the mains, fuel cells, bio processes of some kind, biofuels, solar, or even nuclear power. The Esperenza disaster documentation mentions that the ubiquitous and normally clean and safe microfusion generators that power much of the world mysteriously exploded near the impact points, so things like coal and oil fired power plants were probably gone long before. The microfusion process was becoming commercially viable in the “now” of the game so it is probably safe to assume that fossil fuel use was mostly gone by about 2020 with the mania with which Nova-tech was embraced by society at the time.

                                              Global warming might still be an issue in Trinity, I do not remember anything specific about it in the fluff offhand, but whatever greenhouse gasses hardtech would still produce would probably be offset by the biotech absorbing them for its own use. The loss of huge tracts of vegetation in North America and possibly in Europe to taint effects, and loss of rain forest to clearing for farms elsewhere could pose some problems but it is probably offset by things like the greening of Ethiopia and growing use of biotech (there is even mention of some self-sustaining biotech arcologies in one of the books).

                                              Sustainability is probably still something of an issue but not a particularly pressing one since energy is not a big problem by then and there are a lot more options for recycling than there are today, including an extensive array of biotech ones like digestive materials separation/sorting and whatnot. In addition, there are asteroid and extraterrestrial planet based mines bringing in new resources as well. Terra is in much the same position as the industrialized early USA east coast, with a vast wilderness to explore and exploit available.

                                              Pollution and Politics (including economics) seem to be the biggest issues on that list that would still be a problem. Besides chemical toxins there is the issue of radiation (particularly in Shattered Europe) and Taint, and the Orders killed off their best experts in the radiation and taint departments so they are stuck with the problems until they can develop some other way to handle them. Mostly they seem to cover up the problem in the fluff.

                                              On the political and economic fronts it seems to be business as usual, the main difference being the players are all shuffled around and todays third world are the leaders in the future and the big players of today have sunk considerably (which is chillingly realistic if the current trend continues).

                                              Some of the colony writeups mention population pressure, political differences and/or power games, profit, and even just general wanderlust and scientific curiosity as reasons for various colonies being started so those factors obviously still exist too.
