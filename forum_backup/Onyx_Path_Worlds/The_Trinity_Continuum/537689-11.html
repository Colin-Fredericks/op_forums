    Originally posted by Neall View Post
    To me, Aeon has always had a Mass Effect vibe, partly because I played the first Mass Effect at the same time I read the original Aeon books bought in a collection of RPG.net. So...yeah, I wanted Aeon to feel a lot like Mass Effects 1 and 2. I'm confused as to what you think of are flaws in the initiative concept, but very well and good - to me, Aeon's always been a game of slick cinematics and MIDI-style music.
    I can see the Mass Effect vibe to some degree, sure. Aeon was always fairly dark in my interpretations though, and while details may change generally that's what drew me to the setting concept in the first place. I never read felt that Aeon was a game of slick cinematics (in terms of game-play) - we take completely different things away from Aeon, clearly.

    On initiative, I will never run with the idea of a player picking which player goes after them. There are two reasons. First, it doesn't really reflect on the capabilities of each individual character and feels somewhat arbitrary. Second, I know players who are always a lot quieter or less experienced who are going to end up being the last one picked. Sure, there's a mechanism in there to compensate for but I'd prefer not to have that problem in the first place to need compensation (hence the clunky comment earlier). That is easy to fix though, I'll just throw it out and go with something that doesn't require players to decide when other players act. There's a handful of items that I can easily change, initiative is one of them.

        Originally posted by Malcolm View Post

        I'm a fan of Aberrant and had its bona fides in mind while working on this. But here's the thing: Aberrant often didn't do what its fiction said it did. So the situation you'd often get is yeah, sometimes Novas would paste each other, but sometimes the dice would be pretty underwhelming. Scaling is calibrated to that there's still fidelity to that fiction *and* there's a range of playable results. Testing the numbers and using plug-ins designed for specific games will refine things.
        As someone who has issues with some of WW/OPP's other properties, I know how frustrating it is to say "this isn't the game I wanted" while other folks are going "but this is awesome!", so I sympathize. That said...THIS. Lots of powers were not calibrated to each other, especially combat powers (though social powers could be pretty weird too). I can't wait to see what TC: Aberrant looks like. (It might not even be called Aberrant! It's like calling an X-Men game "Muties." That has power, but it's also weirdly off-putting to me as someone who's also a fan of Trinity.)

        On a more general note, it's clear that Continuum's going to be calibrated to the existence of all three major splats from the get-go, which was a huge problem for the old setting from day one. The Aeon Society photo in Trinity clearly demonstrated the setting's pulp roots, and we got teased Novas and Daredevils from the beginning. Problem was, none of the eras gelled with each other, and they were obviously intended to at the beginning. I love all three games, but outside of Adventure! (where Stalwarts and Mesmerists were, necessarily, flickering shadows of their descendents' might), each was -- and had to be -- its own thing. That was a problem, and right now, Sardonyx looks like the solution.

        Heroes die. Hope is a phoenix.

                        Okay, before I dig into any really criticisms, I have to say I enjoy the Information Gathering system presented. I think it's robust without being too complex, and offers fairly solid advice. It seems to work for good utility. I don't think much needs to change about it in the final form.

                        Secondly there is this:
                        Furthermore, social actions as a form of “combat,” is not the core element of the system, but only one category. You don’t overpower someone to make friends with them or fall in love. That’s why social systems include cooperative and collective action. Systems should avoid modeling all social interactions as combat or imposing power over another. The manipulation system (see “Manipulation”) covers that aspect of social play, but does not define it.
                        Thank you. It's really refreshing to see someone both trying to make an in-depth social system without feeling the need to make it a 1-1 analog to the combat system, which almost never works in my opinion.

                            Initiative order is going to produce some weird results. It's also a little difficult to parse. Basically, tick order really doesn't matter for PCs, except to see if they beat any NPCs. After that, it doesn't matter. Tick order only matter for Director characters. Given the way the system functions, I wouldn't call them "ticks". Ticks seem to imply a counting down and are more associated with some of the older Exalted systems. Here I might call it "Priority" of something instead.

                            We will have some weird situations, like if we have a really slow. powerful NPC opponent - a glacier as they are sometimes called - they will always get to go before the rest of the party. They may not get to go before one guy...but everyone else is just out of luck, even if they are normally a lot faster. We also have situations where PCs aren't handing off to people who should be faster until later, which may seem weird. Then again, it may not be so bad with the base system, plus we still have kewl powerz to expect.

                            I'm not really sure what Fields bring to the table, or what exactly they are meant to do for the game. Right now, they look like a mostly unneeded complication, but I will accept some explanation from the crew on it.

These players were not the last picked, because the more active/aggressive players were the ones doing the picking, and they'd chose the character that made the most sense to go next. If the coolest thing to happen right now would be for the brick to throw a barrel to distract the goon squad, setting up the others in the party to pick them off with blasters, then you hand the initiative to the brick, regardless of the activity level of the player.
