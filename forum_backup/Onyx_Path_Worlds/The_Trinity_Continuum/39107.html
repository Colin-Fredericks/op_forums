    Should taint be a bigger issue?

    12-30-2013, 12:26 AM
    In the storyline of aberrant, a great many novas eventually go insane/become deformed by taint. This is in part due to the existence and nature of taint being suppressed by Utopia.

    Players, of course, won't suffer from this lack of knowledge when making and playing a character. Going by what I remember of the old system, a character without any starting taint who is careful with the use of his powers might not get a single dot of permanent taint until Quantum 6 or 7? At that point, you automatically get taint when raising that stat, but even then, it's possible to reach Quantum 10 and have only glowing eyes or a fancy special effects display when you use your powers.

    Will/should the new edition address this? Make taint a more serious problem for players?

                          Eh. Taint isn’t a “punishment”, per se; it’s a thematic element of the setting with a massive implication for the overall timeline. At its core, Taint is this: superhuman powers have a dehumanizing influence.

                          Looking at the source material for Aberrant, a surprising number of superheroes have decidedly inhuman characteristics — usually physical, like rocky skin or a pair of bird-like wings sprouting out of one’s shoulder blades; but sometimes psychological. And yes, there’s also the tendency of writers to saddle characters that have great powers with unusual weaknesses as well, such as Superman’s infamous vulnerability to Kryptonite. Taint serves to provide these elements of the superhuman comics; but it does so in a way designed to move the Aberrant era toward the Æon era, when superhumans have become monsters.

                              Taint really should be fairly easy to avoid if care is taken to do it; it is a symptom of power abuse as much as anything else. Characters who deliberately run their powers too hot too often should get burned with mutations and defects that others avoid, it fits the traditional part of the superhero genre where the heroes look heroic and the villains very often look like weird exaggerations or worse. Evil, greed, and unbridled hubris shows in the comics, and the taint mechanic is how Aberrant goes about simulating it.

                              Often characters in comics start out with some unusual features from having extra powerful powers awaken in their origin but then do not change further, the taint option in character generation fits that type well as long as the person does not build up too much taint during play which would be impossible if rising taint levels were inevitable. On the other hand the heroes who are so driven, unscrupulous, or otherwise so obsessed with power or revenge that they are little different from the villains they fight eventually get as twisted as them which also fits the genre.

                                  My thoughts are threefold:

                                  1) Taint should not be harder to avoid.
                                  2) Taint should provide larger benefits in many cases, so that multiple sources of it are equally valid.
                                  3) Taint's negative effects should scale up so that you can't go as long without it affecting you at all. A minor drawback of some kind at Taint 2 would be nice.

                                  The key is that Taint is what creates Supervillains. The Bad Guy is often stronger and tougher than the heroes, because he's one guy and there's four of them, but he's also kind of unstable in ways that don't really map to real-world mental illnesses, and he can't be talked down. That's what Taint does, mechanically.

                                                  When I play Aberrant, I'm not looking for a general comic book simulator. There are dozens of supers games that can simulate the comics. I play Aberrant because of its unique world, and its place within the greater Trinity Continuum. Both the Aberrant setting, and the overarching Continuum setting tells me that the supers of Aberrant are beings of Earth shattering power, but those same beings must struggle against being consumed by the very power that makes them so impressive. That's drama, that's interesting character choices, and that's what makes Aberrant special and set apart form other supers games. It isn't just a comic book simulator. So, while I understand why a less Taint intensive system would make it easier to emulate this comic character or that comic character, that argument does little to move me. I don't want to dilute what makes the Trinity Continuum my favorite game line in the name of generalizeability. I wouldn't be opposed to alternate low taint rules, but I want the base system to be the world described to me in the setting materials, and in that world taint is an ever present threat.

                                                      I am not saying taint should not be an ever present threat, what I was getting at was that there is no reason to take it beyond what was in the first edition and turn it into some inevitable 'all Novas must eventually become monsters' thing either. For one thing that kind of inexorable degradation does not fit what is seen in the Aeon Trinity era; despite the popular belief of the neutrals not all Novas became monsters, there are still plenty of sane, relatively normal looking Novas out there in space and some of them are even from as far back as the original crop of Novas. In fact most of the Aberrant monsters seen are the spawn of The Colony and a few other taint-ridden nutcases that keep making trouble and therefore stay in the public eye.

                                                      Also, an ironic effect of the exile of the Novas is that new Nova potentials do not have much in the way of "clean" quantum to trigger eruption, chances are far higher that they will be exposed to taint left from Aberrant incursions and their eruption twisted as a result of being triggered by the taint instead.
