            Originally posted by ManBearPig View Post
            ...the question I have is how have other gm's adjusted so that their bad guys dont have to all be soak machines AND super willpower strong AND smart enough to screw with the pcs to survive a single punch/single domination/mental blast and be not game breaking every dot filled sheets.
            First of all, thinking of enemies as 'bad guys' doesn't help you out - the idea is to make everyone sympathetic. The world of cardboard bit is only as effective due to Supes' universally understood restraint against 'bad guys' - showing a level of care and regard for life in the character's approach to his power even against most of his worst enemies who have threatened mass murder countless times prior. It would sound stupid coming from a Lobo or any other anti-hero who didn't give a toss.

            I'll throw out a few ideas for narrative aversion of mega-combat and you can take what you please, if any pleases you at all:
            I saw some success with a combination of a couple of bodiless characters (one via tainted permanent density decrease, another via neural upload), the social combat system in Forceful Personalities (plus some mental attempts), subquantum jargon, public pressure and endless hordes of squishy, human guilt-trips (usually behind the controls of a main battle tank, fighter jet or attack helicopter).
            It seems like a nice anti-PC could help you, a character with whom the player is inexorably destined to clash with, but is measurably more noble than they and won't lower themself to the PCs level. Mix up the perception of good and evil, redeem 'evil' npcs the PCs have vanquished and sully pure npcs after following a PCs poor advice/lead. With all due respect, if the PCs treat a turned friend no differently than the average foe, then there may be a problem with characterization on one side of the screen or the other.
            A good recurring boss for Aberrants is one the PCs a strong stake in not killing - their actual boss; who pays their checks, gives them equipment and lends them some influence when required might escape summary execution after a few schemes to embarrass or otherwise one-up the players, same with any established characters who the players have invested in - by whatever means.
            While we're on the subject, fans, admirers and saved baselines tend to massage the PC ego enough to slip them under their skin with a little targeted characterization. Funny characters can also win points with player perceptions - the idea is to make them want to be better via emotional manipulation by proxy of actors. Make the PCs social standing matter, make it a real thing that touches them in game so that they don't want to go without it.
            Make a character arc out of the Nova's capacity for destruction - i've enabled more than one murderous PC into remorseful acts and more with a personally crafted subplot just for them. Do it right and they won't even know you did it, but be cautious to not drag players in directions they don't want any part of - that's no fun. I've found a few players who were frightfully immune to NPC or setting manipulations (often also equally prone to murder) - and you may need a nice tasty carrot and a stick to keep them civilized for long.
            Do stories that make enemies weilding ungodly levels of anti-nova tech commonplace - handwave and force every last drop of quantum you can from characters prior to a named villain encounter and distract them heavily both during and afterwards. Always plan a couple of escapes (at least) for climactic encounters you could be interested in extending.
            Never leave a character hanging without a deadline - the PCs will find a way given the opportunity, so keep them off balance and divide their attention. This can be frustrating when you want to characterize the NPC you are protecting - but given enough lucky/daring escapes, the players will start going out of their way to draw you out on the details they missed earlier, in preparation for their next chance to dick-punch them.

            But in the end, i'm perhaps not the best advisor - in my experience, Aberrant eventually always breaks down into an arms race between players and ST. Every pool is ten or higher and certain powers are required for any characters wishing to be competitive in X or Y. While i don't at all disagree with Solar, i've found Aberrant power begins getting in the way of more local/personal storytelling scales. Which just seems a strange tangent for such a character driven setting to be derailed onto.

                        Metaphors provide scant context, but if by 'friends eating' you mean Aberrant PCs engaging in challenging high-level physical conflict and if 'broken car' means cheap systemic superiority of Mega-Strength to most (if not all) soak powers; then there are three paths immediately obvious to me:
                        De-power Mega-Strength
                        Empower soak (but not enough to outstrip damage in the endgame)
                        Narrate your way out of the conundrum

                        Since balance can remain iffy with the first two options, i decided to suggest the third.
                        -shrug-


                        Edit:
                        At risk of straying further tangent-wise and wasting more time, i'll try defining the issues by mechanism of extremes. In the process, i've found that Aberrant soak eventually outstrips physical damage but at much greater expense, which Wolfgar has already pointed out as a poor endgame experience. So, if anything - Mega-Strength is the one that needs more by the end - as there are many more sources of soak out there than of extra physical damage.

                        Strength 5 + Mega 5 + Node 5 + Crush = 81-133xp or 18-20 nova points for +25B-45L auto damage and 5+ dice (+1 auto damage and all damage made lethal for 1q per turn; +20 auto damage and all damage made lethal for 20q per turn)

                        Stamina 5 + Mega 5 + Resilience x 2 + Durability + Armor 5 = 127-167xp or 39-41 nova points for 35/25 soak (soak all as bashing for 1q per turn), 10 health levels

                        Strength 5 + Mega 10 + Node 5 + Crush = 256-308xp or 33-35 nova points for +50B-70L auto damage and 5+ dice (+1 auto damage and all damage made lethal for 1q per turn; +20 auto damage and all damage made lethal for 20q per turn)

                        Stamina 5 + Mega 10 + Resilience x 2 + Durability + Armor 5 + Invulnerability (All Physical Extra) 5 = 381-421xp or 79-81 nova points for 75/65 soak (soak all as bashing for 1q per turn), 20 health levels

                        P.S. Didn't include Quantum costs, but that's not too hard to figure in. Pure oversight.

                                                            i dont think strength is the problem people think it is. the reason mega-strength is so potent, is that it has to be in order to keep up with an equal investment in quantum bolt+quantum, which does the damage of an artillery strike at the high end. balance is about comparing alternatives for same cost.

                                                            and on the defense side, for an equal investment you could get enough soak to withstand the average attack- and still die in one hit to a very high roll. but then a high-rolling force field could survive even that with the right dice pool.

                                                            i think we need a new mega-stamina enhancement- spend 1 quantum point, and that lethal hit simply incapacitates you, just short of death. then some mechanism of how long your out based on how excessive the damage was. it would be very theme-appropriate, useful for keeping important characters alive, and also leave the character helpless for some time. this would fit well with some of the other things novas can do anyway, and not make anyone better at winning a fight. does make murdering someone more difficult, but not impossible.
