    OK.... I've skimmed through enough of this, but haven't read every word.

    I think I have a feel for where this is going, and some components of the other previews make a bit more sense tonally now. This isn't just giving options for cinematic play - cinematic play appears to be the point. Momentum / fail-sideways, players getting to choose who goes next in initiative, Focus, how scaling appears to work as just some examples - I can see what OPP is going for here the more I read.

    I keep thinking of the Avengers movies action sequences as I read through the various mechanics. And that's not a good thing.

    Unfortunately, what Im seeing is too clunky for my tastes. There are some specifics I really welcome (target numbers is done well, as just one example), but Sardonyx is appealing to me less with each preview. For example, I would completely change how initiative works to ditch dice pools (more dice = slower combat), and I'm not interested in the players choosing which player goes after them - there are so many issues with that concept.

    Also, Marvel style action-hero tones are uninteresting to me and not remotely a good fit for how I interpret the Aeon setting, so lots of this doesn't do it for me at all (read: Momentum).

    Its a shame, I was hoping for something streamlined that allows each setting to emphasize their own unique tones. I'm not feeling that with the mechanics, and I suspect setting material (unless some core mechanics variants are provided) won't change this.

        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
        Aberrant 2e is whatever the authors want it to be.

        I'm sure they're aware that there are some 1e fans that will not be happy if 2e does not follow all of 1e's setting and style assumptions. But Aberrant 1e doesn't magically stop existing (and frankly it doesn't sound like the Sardonyx system is incapable of being dialed in to recapture whatever people want in to make it more 1e-like), and what really matters is Aberrant 2e being a really good game; not a game that clutches to every sacred cow of the last edition.

        Thank you.

        A lot of the objection in the vein of "this isn't what Aberrant is about" is based on individual interpretations of the original edition of Aberrant, which is not necessarily how we are interpreting the new edition of Aberrant. It seems to be fairly easy to tweak the rules how you like them in any case, but do not assume that because you played Aberrant 1e one way that TC:Aberrant necessarily must be designed the same way.

        Part of my mandate for the Continuum was to tweak all three lines to bring them into better harmony with each other, to create a true continuum. This is part of that. It's entirely possible that what we're doing may not be to everyone's tastes -- several people, like CHILL, have already said they don't want novas or Talents in their Aeon setting, and that's fine. Really! Once we release the games into the wild, everyone's free to splice it up however they like, and I'll be delighted to hear what people do with both setting and system when they're done. But we still have a specific direction we're going in to make the games we want to make. While I'm sure everyone would prefer that we're creating exactly the game they want, that's not possible, so we're creating the game we want, and hoping everyone else digs it (or at least finds enough to enjoy that they can ignore the bits they don't).

                                                    Okay, thoughts on this teaser, having reread this so I'm going off of more than my sleep addled thoughts from right before bed.

                                                    I don't like the Initiative system. Now, there's obviously intended to be powers and what not that affect it, but going off the base system from the teaser the only thing that matters is who wins. After that, it doesn't matter who came in second or third or whatever. After the winner goes, then they hand it off to the Director(or vice versa), then they pick who goes next, etc. How well or how poorly you rolled doesn't really seem to have any effect if you're not the one who won.

                                                    Focus......I just don't like Focus. Not my cup of tea.

                                                    Then we've got Scale. I get what is intended with this and I appreciate that you're coming out and saying outright that it isn't supposed to be realistic, in so far as such a term applies when quantum powered super heroes and mystical deities are involved. I also understand and appreciate the stated reasoning, that encounters must be survivable instead of "Your characters run into the wrong Giant and get stomped flat. GG"

                                                    That said, I dislike the sort of divide proposed, where a monster doesn't hit as hard when fighting a PC or when an attack that could slag a tank hits the Mystery Machine and it's still in one piece. Now, if there were in universe mitigating factors, like say, you're the child of a death god and are leveraging the power of your blood to sap a monster's vitality or the Mystery Machine is equipped with a forcefield, I'm fine with it.

                                                    I just dislike it when the mitigating factor is "Because you're a PC."


                                                    Last note, uh,...the math for the Scale Table seems to be off. It says to add the previous two Factors together to get the Intensity for the next level. Scale 5 for Proxies is +5 and the previous levels were +3 and +2. Scale 6 for Demigods should be 8(5+3), but it's listed as +7, which affects all of the numbers from that point on.

                                                            Obviously, you're gonna do what you have to do, and I respect your right to decide, but that just makes sense to me.
                                                            All I can do is really comment on what is put in front of me at any given time.

                                                            The thing is, what I am being told about what the devs aim to do and what the mandate is for the overall system just doesn't sound like it wants me to run and play games in my preferred style, in fact it seems that it is actively against that style. What I am saying in terms of the scale not feeling right for Aberrant, and not feeling like the narrativist ideals of the system are suitable for the setting, that's not stuff that seems to be part of the optional elements of the system. It's baked in as an ideal from day one. I raised this issue, and was told to wait for the scaling rules to come out, as those rules would alleviate my concerns. They have not alleviated my concerns.

                                                            Sardonyx, no matter what it is intended for now, is intended for Aberrant at some point. I don't care about Adventure, and I'm not really bothered about Aeon, but I do care about Aberrant. So if you put it in front of me, with that in mind, I'm going assess it in terms of what I actually want it to do. After all, Aberrant is one of my favourite WW games ever.

                                                            Which was, in fact, mostly due to that Aberrant was, in a good and proper sense of the word, edgy. It said "you know what, super strength guy? If you punch this baseline, no matter who they are, you will kill them" That was... hugely important to everything Aberrant said about what it meant to be a Nova. You had the powers of a God. You could change the world. But the world is a fragile and delicate thing, made of people who tore like paper when you unleashed your power within their vicinity, never mind at them. If you were in any way a moral person, you had to be incredibly disciplined in what you did and how you did it..

                                                            A baseline, no matter how heroic, could never hope to a fight a Nova whose powers were related to combat, no matter how villainous. And that was kind of the key, in the sense that the world of Aberrant was not one where heroes won because they were heroes. Being a hero means nothing when you get splattered by a few hundred tons of force behind a fist that goes through your skull a a few hundred miles an hour. It didn't matter that things like that were unfair, or harsh, because like in the real world, power can be unfair and it can be harsh and it can destroy people, good people, if not used responsibly and ethically. And that was why Aberrant was cool, and different, because it actually took a look a superpowers that made them seem powerful, terrifyingly so.

                                                            The system we have here says that if a plot designated hero has the camera on them then they get all these narrative bonuses that let them ignore those harsh realities, with incoherencies where a Nova can annihilate a fleet of armoured cars when they are crewed by randoms but struggle to trash one just because it contains a designated plot relevant individual. And that's great! For Adventure and Aeon and stuff, that works fine. But not for Aberrant. And I ask, if you're not going to do Aberrant with those harsh realities, what the hell is the point of even calling it Aberrant? Why even bother using the name and the setting, if you're going to just make it like every other modern superhero comic based setting that originated from four colour silver and golden age comics (which I love, I own over three hundred modern Marvel graphic novels, I just think that Aberrant doesn't and shouldn't try to be those things).

                                                            I dunno. I love Aberrant. I never thought like I ran or played it in any kind of unusual or fringe way, I always thought that this was the true, genuine core of the game, the ethics of being a superhuman in a world which takes a more realistic assessment of how your powers would affect it. If you add narrativist protection for a heroic SWAT team guy who goes in to take down a Terat bad guy, then you're taking away from the core themes of the game, because really that guy should be SWAT-ted (couldn't resist) regardless of how brave or whatever he is. And as a Nova, you then have to shoulder the responsibility of being the guy who actually has to take action here or watch that SWAT team die. That happened, in a game I played, and it was really cool and really integral to how I see the setting. But what the scene (the death of a brave and heroic individual to a piece of shit asshole with some superpowers because life is not fair) showcases is what the system seems to be actively trying to work against, here.

                                                            But from what I can tell, none of the developers want that. Which confuses and disappoints me. Because I really like that game, and I feel like I'm being alienated in favour of a new way of doing things just to make it fit with other games that I neither care about or play.
