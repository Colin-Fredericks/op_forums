    Thoughts on Quantum Attack

    11-17-2021, 05:05 AM
    As part of a campaign setting I'm putting together, I've been looking at the kinds of nova that can be put together with low Quantum ratings. In doing this, I noted that one-dot Quantum Attack is actually rather under-powered for the investment.

    At one dot, at Quantum 1, the Quantum Attack power is barely better than a basic unarmed strike. It provides (rounding Quantum/2 up) a Scale 1, Enhancement 1, Close Range attack with a single dot for an extra Tag whilst costing 2 Quantum to power. Unarmed strike is the same without extra Tag. Body Modification Claws (mentioned as being weaker that Quantum Attack) actually adds up to the same (Unarmed strike but with Edged & Brutal tag) without a Quantum cost. Admittedly, it is as good as a pistol if you choose the Ranged tag (and accept guns are Scale 1*)... but only as good as that, costs the Quantum to power, and pistols are ubiquitous in the Action Hero & Super Hero genres.

    Perhaps it's due to my reading that guns are Scale 2, but as a house rule (not arguing for official rules change here), I am considering adding 1 Enhancement (i.e. it gets 1 + Quantum/2 Enhancement instead of just Quantum/2). This makes it not quite as good as a gun, but better than a Body Mod that doesn't need Quantum to power it. What do others think?

      Personally, Quantum Attack is not bad the way it is, basically because there is nothing really equal to compare. Sure guns are everywhere and there is Body Modification, but in practice, Quantum Attack will hardly be a single dot and will have a better enhancement, what the other options won’t. Also you can pick any form of damage, what can be very useful.

      Second point is, all defensive powers have durability scale equal to dot rating, that is limited to quantum. It would put Quantum attack one scale above all defensive attacks.

      I, as a houserule, consider that Mega Might does not apply it’s scale to punches, only when crunching things with pressure (smashing the object between your hands, or bending an iron bar), and a quantum attack must be used to create a “high scale punch” if fits. If you don’t want this rule, consider MM apply one scale below the might scale to the punch, to keep it leveled to other powers.

      If you are planning to use guns as scale 2 weapons, I suggest you to pick Quantum Attack at least at 3 dots, where you have scale 3 and 3 free tags and enhancement 2 (considering Q3). In fact, to make a Nova powerful enough to be street level without worrying much about guns, your nova must be all scale 3+, otherwise armed robbers will be real risk.

      Not all super powers must be more powerful than guns, they can be just as good as punches at distance, but, if you think it is not a big deal, increase the scale of Quantum Attack.

                                          If you're limiting the game to Quantum 1, I can see five potential problems you may not have considered:

                                          1). If my quick flip through the book was right, 18 of the 32 powers (not Mega-Edges or Mega-Attributes) need Q2-Q4 to take them. That's over half.
                                          2). Many dice pools are Quantum + Power, which means they'd be rolling 2 dice and failing a lot.
                                          3). Maxing Out is unlikely to work when you're only rolling 1 die. Frequent failures are frustrating.
                                          4). Q1 = 15 quantum points. If they have powers that require quantum points, they could run out fast, which would also be frustrating.
                                          5). You can't take more points in a single Tag than you have dots in the power. This is very limiting if you only have one dot in the power.

                                          My off-the-cuff solution would be:
                                          1). Let them buy up to Quantum 5. This does a lot to solve issues 1-4 while giving them something to spend starting xp on instead of 12 different powers.
                                          2). Let them take 2+ levels in specific Tags with Storyguide approval.

                                                      OK, to give context, the idea for my campaign setting is based on 'The Boys' (comic as I've not seen the TV series). In that universe, the source of powers is a chemical agent that costs unfathomable amounts to make in a pure form and the younger a person is given a dose, the more powerful they are. The world's Superman analog needed to be given a one or more large, pure doses of the chemical agent in his infancy. Large doses (of varying purity) in childhood is pretty much a requirement to get powers recognisable as "super" (and come with the complication the world's Evil Corp pretty much owns you afterward). Get dosed as an adult, even with the high quality / pure stuff, results in being tougher, stronger, and perhaps a bit faster than baselines... but to (at best) Captain America levels.

                                                      For my purposes, the players will be taking on the role of plucky heroes taking it on themselves to deal with supers that get protected by the evil "Super Heroes Inc" corporation. They'd either be Talents picked for their outstanding (but still "human" capabilities) or they'd be 'baselines' affected in some life-altering bad way by supers who get given a dose of the super-power catalyst as an adult, getting low level super powers.

                                                      With that in mind, I had been thinking about keeping Quantum capped at 1 to reflect their "low level" powers, but as I'm working it through, I'm finding that low-level novas in the current rules are very underpowered compared to low-level novas in the Aberrant of my childhood. A Quantum 1 "old school" nova with Quantum Bolt 1 in the old rules was working with the equivalent of an SMG or Heavy Pistol. A Quantum 1 with Quantum Attack 1 "new school" nova is just throwing the equivalent of a ranged punch.

                                                          Originally posted by Shepherdboi View Post
                                                          Not really. The difference between a Q1 nova with 1 dot powers and a Q5 nova with 1 dot powers is that the Q5 nova tends to succeed more consistently and last longer before running out of energy. There is no huge power difference.
                                                          Well, a Q5 Nova with no Scale 5 powers made really bad choices in their XP use. I mean, if you have Quantum 5 and Quantum attack 1, you gain 2e in the attack, but lose the chance to destroy warships with your attack. There are those cases, sure, but usually when we talk Q5 we are talking about Scale 5 powers, basically optimized XP spending.

                                                          Now on Q3 feeling in practice, I have had the chance to run a game where most players were Q2-3, with a single Q4 and the the Q4 was afraid of his powers (he thinks he killed people when erupted). The general feeling was interesting, we didn’t use that much of scale, the game was a lot of investigation with a few combat scenes, but was fun, with no cases of Collateral damages (and I tracked it per session). The climax was a fight against a Nova inventor that had Durability scale enough to be completely immune to all their attacks (6 if I am not wrong) and a serious mental unbalance that compelled him to invent stuff, basically supplying gangs all over the city with Nova tech just to get more money to invent more stuff, but no intention to harm anyone.

                                                          My idea for Q3 cap is to keep it on reasonable scale, like you can take a tank cannon shot in your chest, but it will hurt as hell, and you can attack it back, but it’s not going to be with a single attack that you will destroy it. If you max out you could do some collateral damage, but not level a city by mistake.

                                                          On the idea of a The Boys like game, instead of Q1, use Talents. You have no idea what they can do until you use talents in your game. They are more flexible than Q1 Novas and may have the feeling of low level super powers without the flash part. Talents can for example spend inspiration for scale, and with a gift ignore scale differences, what means their attacks gain scale against high scale targets, but it will happen just once in a while (2 times per scene, possibly), not all the time like novas. And they could gain a lot of advantages with some Novatech, if available.
