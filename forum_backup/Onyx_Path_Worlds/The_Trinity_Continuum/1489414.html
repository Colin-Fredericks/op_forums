    Exalted Talents

    08-07-2022, 11:48 AM
    Inspired by a question that glamourweaver recently raised (how to treat Talents as a kind of magic user?), I found myself revisiting an idea near and dear to my heart: the concept of the “upgraded Talent” as TC's counterpart to the Exalted.

    Where a psion or psiad can be thought of kind of like a wizard and a Nova can be thought of as a kind of demigod, the “upgraded Talent” can be thought of as a master of his art. He can take the supposedly mundane skills that he has and can achieve miraculous results.

    It turns out that there's a remarkably easy way to model this in-game: in settings that don't feature mesmerists or stalwarts, allow “upgraded Talents” to design “Aptitude Masteries” that mimic some Dynamic or Psychic Gifts found in TC: Adventure!. This can be as simple as changing the keyword, but sometimes requires some changes to the prerequisites. Some examples:
    Ascetic Trance: as a Psychic Gift, this has the Biological and Momentary keywords, and requires two dots of Integrity. As an Aptitude Mastery, replace the Biological keyword with the Aptitude (Integrity) keyword. It's no longer something she can do because she has a psychic ability to control her body; it's something she can do because the self-discipline that her Integrity represents now extends to control over her autonomous bodily functions.

    Cloud the Mind: as a Psychic Gift, this has the Cognitive keyword and requires two dots of Larceny. As an Aptitude Mastery, replace the Cognitive keyword with the Aptitude (Larceny) keyword. It's not a psychic ability to conceal yourself; is just that you're that good at blending in.

    Sleight of Will: as a Psychic Gift, this has the Energetic keyword and requires a dot of Larceny and the Telekinetic Summoning Gift. As an Aptitude Mastery, it replaces the Energetic keyword with Aptitude (Larceny) and removes the Telekinetic Summoning requirement. But in exchange, an additional condition is needed: the object to be conjured must be hidden where it is, and can only be conjured to somewhere where it's hidden.

    Signal and Noise: as a Dynamic Gift, this has the Attribute (Composure) keyword and requires two dots of Composure. As an Aptitude Mastery, replace the Attribute (Composure) keyword with the Aptitude (Enigmas) keyword and change the prerequisites from Composure •• to Composure •, Enigmas ••. You're really good at spotting obscure connections and patterns in information. Think of the chalkboard scenes in A Beautiful Mind.

    Masteries should generally lean toward Aptitude (Skill) keywords, although there are a few that might be Aptitude (Attribute) instead. Psychic Gifts which already have Skill prerequisites are the easiest to turn into Masteries; Dynamic Gifts are designed to operate through Attributes, and adapting them as Masteries generally require swapping out a dot or two of the Attribute requirement with twice as many dots in a suitable Skill. There are some “best fit” defaults for this: Dexterity Gifts generally work with skills like Aim, Athletics, or Larceny; Stamina, Composure, and Resolve can usually translate to Integrity; Intellect Gifts tend to work well with Enigmas, Humanities, or Science; Manipulation Gifts translate nicely to Persuasion; Presence Gifts to Command; Cunning Gifts to Empathy or Enigmas; and so on. But there's are merely suggestions.

    Dynamic Power Gifts and Psychic Energetic Gifts generally don't make sense as Aptitude Masteries; but a few do, such as Blocking Bullets or Inspired Genius. As always, the Storyguide is the final arbiter of what is and isn't allowed. Take the setting into account: when up against Novas, the Storyguide is encouraged to be more permissive than in the Æon era, for example. In general, if the Gift can be viewed as a side effect of the Talent doing things (e.g., blocking attacks or talking to people), it's probably permissible; if the Gift is what the character would be doing (e.g., firing off energy bolts or entering dreams), it probably isn't.

    And don't forget that Talents can be master craftsmen: while they can't create Powered Super Science, they can create both Advanced and Inspired Super Science. If you want a more mystical bent to them, let them turn their Super Science to Artifacts of Enigmatic Power or Mystical Practices (found in Adventure! and Æther, respectively).

    Thoughts?

              I didn't come up with it with the idea of stealing. I originally was going to say, “hey, why don't we start coming up with new Masteries for Talents that reflect taking their Skills to impossible lengths?” I even started putting together a list of examples of how each Skill might be supercharged. I had things like “supercharged Integrity might allow you to take your self-discipline to the extreme where you can control even the normally automatic bodily functions”, or “supercharged Survival might let you understand animals so well that you can actually converse with them”. I had an example worked up for every skill except Humanities and Technology.

              And then I looked at the Psychic Gifts. Just a brief look, at first, and then more in-depth. And you know what? Every single one of my “supercharged skills” ideas was already implemented, either as a Psychic Gift or as a Dynamic Gift.

              The issue isn't that the notion of supercharged skills is bad; it's that Adventure! assigned everything potentially like that to mesmerists and stalwarts.

              So I figured: why reinvent the wheel? Since we already have the Gifts I was going to suggest for this, why not just use them?

              I do get where you're coming from, though, which is why I suggested doing this only in settings that don't have stalwarts or mesmerists. In those settings, recreating Psychic and Dynamic Gifts as Aptitude Masteries doesn't trample on anyone; it just expands the repertoire of the “Exalted” Talent, making Gifts available that would otherwise go unused.

              Again, these are Gifts that are conceptually an excellent fit for the “Exalted Talent” concept; and I'm not going to say that the Talent shouldn't be offered those Masteries just because mesmerists and stalwarts got there first.
