    (Storypath) Concerns about Momentum?

    03-05-2022, 03:50 PM
    I've been skimming through the Trinity Continuum core rulebook, and I see some problems with the Momentum mechanic that I'm unsure how to fix. This is probably a Storypath system-wide thing, but I'm posting this here because TC is the only Storypath book I own so far and there's no "general Storypath" forum. I do plan to get more if these concerns are resolved, as Aberrant, Aeon, the They Came From books, and Dystopia Rising: Evolution all look very interesting.

    First of all, the rules as written don't allow Momentum to be spent without the permission of the entire group, and make it one pool for the entire group. If I run this, it's pretty much guaranteed that my players will get into arguments over when to spend it that wouldn't really take place if it was a per-character resource. In addition, it effectively makes it impossible to use Momentum in instances of conflict between PCs, especially during the vastly-more-common forms of non-combat conflict where a very relevant mechanic (skill tricks) would be completely shut down by PCs vetoing each other's momentum usage.

    Second, what prevents players from simply spamming low-risk rolls they know they're going to fail at to quickly replenish their Momentum pool? Right now, I don't see anything aside from GM fiat to prevent this, and quite frankly I'm confused as to why this mechanic incentivizes players to fail rolls because it results in absurd outcomes such as this.

    Third, how does Momentum work for major antagonist NPCs that have full character sheets, as suggested on page 138? How do they use Skill Tricks? Do they have their own Momentum pools or can they just use Skill Tricks without using Momentum?

    Are there any solutions to these problems, either official rules options in other books, or homebrew rules you would suggest?

      I have a house rule where Momentum is an individual shareable resource instead of a collective resource. That is, whoever generated the Momentum “owns” it, and has full say over how it can be used; but with his or her approval, anyone in the group can use it. I'm thinking of maybe pairing this with an option to the effect that you get a refund on your Momentum when you spend it on someone else and it doesn't work out: that is, if the roll fails and the guy who made the roll earns a point of Momentum in the process, you get your point of Momentum back; but only if you're not the guy who made the roll. The point is to minimize the risk of spending Momentum on someone else in the group and disincentivize hoarding.

      Second, I'd adapt a rule that exists in Hero Games: “a Disadvantage that doesn't disadvantage you isn't worth any points”. In Hero Games, it gives the GM permission to “zero out” Disadvantages that are taken for the purpose of gaming the system. Here, the adaptation is to provide a guideline as to when “Storyguide fiat” is reasonable: if the failure of a particular skill roll doesn't actually cause any problems for anyone, then the Storyguide is well within his rights to veto the Momentum reward. That said, the real solution is to talk to the player who thinks that “spamming for Momentum” is a good idea and to try to get him or her into the mindset that Momentum is there to help promote the story, not the other way around.

      Officially, the Storyguide is free to activate Skill Tricks whenever he sees fit, just as he's free to add dice to rolls as he sees fit: in effect, he has an “unlimited Momentum pool” to “spend” however he likes. This is because his responsibility isn't playing a character; it's guiding the story. If you don't trust your Storyguide to do that, you've got bigger problems than whether or not a “primary antagonist” has access to Momentum. That said, there's nothing preventing a Storyguide from imposing a limited Momentum Pool on himself, and only “spending Momentum” that he has “earned”. That said, such a pool ought to belong to the Storyguide, not to the characters — though if he wants to further subdivide his pool among various factions of antagonists (so that the failures of one such character doesn't empower another who's mutually antagonistic), he's free to do that, too. Just remember that any such restrictions like this are entirely voluntary and unenforceable: the Storyguide can make exceptions to this whenever he wishes.

      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                          I find it's not a problem at my table, but that said, my players are not looking to fight one another. For the most part, Storypath rules work best when the intra-group conflict is kept minimal. You're working together to build a story, not get one over (or be better than) other players at the table.

                          I find that the game also works far better with a shared pool over individual pools. A table of three players can build up to nine dice in the pool allowing for a Momentum use of 4-5 dice. Personal pools (without other changes) would limit the player to only 1-2 dice extra max which lacks the punch. The game also works well when everyone's failures contribute to everyone's successes, especially when you (like my table) approach it as a way of telling a story together.

                              Originally posted by Eternl Knight View Post
                              I find it's not a problem at my table, but that said, my players are not looking to fight one another. For the most part, Storypath rules work best when the intra-group conflict is kept minimal. You're working together to build a story, not get one over (or be better than) other players at the table.
                              I've been running Scion for over a year, which uses mostly the same Momentum system, and have to echo this sentiment. The players are generally happy to help each other succeed and being able to spend a ton of momentum for a big roll one player makes is something they're all happy about.

                              Also with Momentum resetting every session they're keen to see someone spend it so they all get the experience for spending half the momentum pool in a single scene. Also to help with group cohesion we've started having everyone going over their short term aspirations at the start of the session, so the players can discuss how they'll help each other achieve at least one short term aspiration, and get another experience for it.

                              Interparty conflict can lead to some neat story moments, but it helps if everyone is on board with that story and not trying to undercut each other. You might find the experience awards and how they reward everyone for doing group things can tempt your players to be aligned and not trying to stop the others from succeeding. Someone spending a ton of Momentum on one roll means everyone gets an xp, discussing their goals for the session can lead to mutually aligned aspirations so everyone gets an extra xp. The players can discuss how they want two characters to have a fight that session to get some things out in the open, and get rewarded for it.

                              Basically, I'd suggest trying it as a shared pool and using the carrot of bonus xp to get your players into it before going with individual pools.

                              Scion presents individual momentum pools as an alternate rule on page 133 of Scion: Origin. In Scion the momentum pool isn't reset every session, so keep that in mind. In this alternate rule the book suggests a cap of 12 momentum for each player. Also if your players aren't failing rolls or engaging with Conditions very often they suggest an alternate rule where you hand out one momentum per scene or round of combat. If you did that, I'd def keep a lower cap than 12.

                                          I would also like to add that
                                          player vs player should only come into play as long as both parties agree to things ahead of time. By that, I mean that the only time players should bump heads is due to a long term growing rift. Even then, they should talk to each other and the GM ahead of time to discuss how it should end. In other words, should they resolve it or come to loggerheads at it or else to let it build up until it comes down to a battle (verbal or physical).
                                          Also, if it comes down to a battle between players, then I say do not allow a veto possible by the other party. For example, if you have Players A-E and Players A & B have a dust up, then the only players that can veto something are Players C-E.
                                          Or else do not allow either side to use Momentum but you could do something like to allow them to spend an Inspiration to activate any Skill Tricks for the duration of the battle.
