                                            I think part of the problem is that the value of +3e isn't a simple calculation. I think the +3e is oriented towards supplementing low rated Skills, where you have a small dice pool (and thus significant chance of failure) and aren't going to have a lot of other sources of Enhancements around. The complaints about it tend towards when two high level Skills (with Tricks, Edges, etc.) compliment each other.

                                            A character with Str 2, Close Combat 1, and Medicine 5 that has a Specialty that could apply to attacks isn't going to turn into a combat monster just because of a +3e. You're still failing a good number of rolls outright, and you're going to struggle against a decent Defense/Soft Armor when you do roll any successes. But it's also a good enough boost that with some teamwork/setup/etc. you could get a cool moment in where you inject someone with a drug, slice a tendon, or whatever.

                                            The problem is when it starts t get closer to Str 5, Close Combat 5, a few Close Combat Skill Tricks, some combat Edges, and then Medicine 5's +3e stacked on top of all that where it starts to put you over the top and finding a Specialty like that gives you a major boost over what should otherwise be equal foes. It becomes a must have to be competitive in upper level combat (or other conflicts).

                                            I think another possible house rule could be:

                                            The bonus from a Specialty can't increase your non-Scale based Enhancements above itself. So if you'd get +2e from a Specialty, and a +1e from equipment, you'd get +2e total instead of +3e. If you already have +4e from various sources, a +3e from a Specialty stops doing you any good.

                                            This would allow the +3e for shoring up lower dice-pool actions to play that role, without it becoming an optimization issue.

                                                            OK having started this thread after some discussion with my players, I wanted to double check my understanding of the mechanic as written, I wanted to get a feel for the intention of the mechanic. This is usually my approach before I begin pounding on (house ruling) a mechanic. The following is the text I intend on presenting to the players. I am throwing it out here for two reasons:
                                                            1. Any feedback regarding clarity, readability or flow.
                                                            2. As a resource for any one else who wants to use it or is sparked by it for their own spin.

                                                            Please note this is not a I'm right your wrong, it is just the use for my campaign. Keep in mind I really love the Storypath system, have tried to order every storypath books as released (funds permitting) and started a journey to convert Chronicles of Darkness an Exalted 3E (eyeing the new Essense project) to the Storypath system. Any house rule change I make is not a knock to the system but another compliment that I am using this system. It just needs slight tweaaking to fit my campaign or gaming style., which is true of almost every system I've ever touched over the years. Also I think when one or more players raise a question or concern with a mechanic (out of game) that as the Storyteller, Storyguide, GM you need to be able to answer or have dialog and be willing to make changes, if it helps.


                                                            Specialties

                                                            A Specialty is narrowly focused expertise within a Skill. For example, Aim grants skill with all ranged weapons, but a character may have a Specialty in Pistols. If a Specialty applies to your situation add a 1-point Enhancement.The specialty could apply on the direct use of the skill or be applied to another situation that it could benefit, within reason. If you find yourself going to great lengths to justify a Specialty, it probably shouldn’t apply.

                                                            For example, a character might apply her Pistols Specialty from Aim to an action using her Culture Skill to suggest a deeper knowledge and understanding of gun shows than another character who is equally adept in Culture might have. The Storyguide is the ultimate arbiter of what constitutes a Specialty and what doesn’t; Specialties that are too broad or too narrow can hurt the story or never come into play.

                                                            Sometimes specific actions might require you to have a Specialty to simply perform that action. When something requires you to have a Specialty in a Skill to do the roll, it just unlocks the ability to take that action, it does not apply Enhancement to the roll. some examples could be ...
                                                            • Learning a new language unrelated to your origin, "I come in pieces ..."
                                                            • Performing medicine on a new species or xeno, "Hmmm we don't have one of these ..."
                                                            • A combat fighter space pilot using an FTL ship, "Now I'm pointed away from the sun, I think ..."
                                                            • Learning sorceries or mystic rites, "Hmmm a void like black portal, cool, run ..."


                                                            House Rule & Reasoning Note

                                                            Specialties were house ruled because you do not gain an enchancement with the primary skill that you spend time practising, perfecting and gaining expertise and you only gain the enhancement in the other skill. In a case of two equal skill levels, Aim 3 (to shoot with a pistol) compared to Command 3 (to intimidate with a pistol) your results gained an enhancement bonus of 100-300% for the other skill on a successful roll. This mechanic is counter-intuitive to the narrative description and use of the word "expertise" The final reason to house rule out the scaling plus 1 to 3 is to leave the unique and more powerful bonus to the realm of Skill Tricks.
