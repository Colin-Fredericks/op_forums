        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
        I think part of the problem is that the value of +3e isn't a simple calculation. I think the +3e is oriented towards supplementing low rated Skills, where you have a small dice pool (and thus significant chance of failure) and aren't going to have a lot of other sources of Enhancements around. The complaints about it tend towards when two high level Skills (with Tricks, Edges, etc.) compliment each other.

        A character with Str 2, Close Combat 1, and Medicine 5 that has a Specialty that could apply to attacks isn't going to turn into a combat monster just because of a +3e. You're still failing a good number of rolls outright, and you're going to struggle against a decent Defense/Soft Armor when you do roll any successes. But it's also a good enough boost that with some teamwork/setup/etc. you could get a cool moment in where you inject someone with a drug, slice a tendon, or whatever.

        The problem is when it starts t get closer to Str 5, Close Combat 5, a few Close Combat Skill Tricks, some combat Edges, and then Medicine 5's +3e stacked on top of all that where it starts to put you over the top and finding a Specialty like that gives you a major boost over what should otherwise be equal foes. It becomes a must have to be competitive in upper level combat (or other conflicts).

        I think another possible house rule could be:

        The bonus from a Specialty can't increase your non-Scale based Enhancements above itself. So if you'd get +2e from a Specialty, and a +1e from equipment, you'd get +2e total instead of +3e. If you already have +4e from various sources, a +3e from a Specialty stops doing you any good.

        This would allow the +3e for shoring up lower dice-pool actions to play that role, without it becoming an optimization issue.
        This is exactly how our discussion started.

        Several skills running parallel to each other seems to be when players would make heavy use/break the spirit of specialties.

        Skill Aim | Speacialty Pistols
        * Gain a better shot by considering the angles

        Skill Command
        * Used the pistol specialty to gain a an intimidation factor with a weapon

        Skill Humanities | Specialty Angles

        Again I'm not saying I would approve any use in this manner, it is all case by case, but I like to prevent to easy or low hanging fruit problem early on.

        And this discussion started first from my player misunderstanding of the mechanic.

        I had intially thought that it was some type of scale like 5 - other skill (max 3), so that it would benefit low level skills and not high level skills,

                                            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                            Skill Tricks already have that design space fairly well handled.
                                            Not really. Skill Tricks come off more as minor Knacks more than anything else. The idea that Billy Ray can hack into a computer by bashing it with a rifle butt using the Gun Tool skill trick has nothing to do with his actual skill in using rifles. I think I've decided that Skill Tricks will be reserved for Talents (including Superiors) alone to use. They fit much better into the whole "that really shouldn't have worked but it did" thing that Talents have.

                                            I think I'll allow Specialties to be limited to up to three (one for each dot above two) but with the proviso that can spend Specialty "slots" to double or triple up on the difficulty reduction. So, if Billy Ray has 5 dots in Aim he could have Rifles3 as a Specialty. When Ray uses Rifles he reduces his Difficulty by 3 down to a minimum of 1, but that's the only Specialty he gets. Or he could have Rifles2, Pistols1 or Rifles1, Pistols1, Direct Fire Vehicles1 (Coilguns, lasers, etc instead of bombs or missiles). As usual, any Specialty that could reduce a Difficulty lower than 1 instead applies as an Enhancement. Even a world-class marksman still has a chance to miss or hit the wrong target (botch).
