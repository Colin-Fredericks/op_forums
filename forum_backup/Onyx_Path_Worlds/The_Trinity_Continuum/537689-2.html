                            IMHO, there have been two major schools of superpower systems over the years; and ironically, they tend line up with the two major comic book companies.

                            On the one hand, you have the DC class of RPGs, including but not limited to Mayfair Games' DC Heroes, Champions, GURPS Supers, and Mutants & Masterminds. These games tend to favor a deconstructionist approach to powers, where they've dispassionately analyzed the assortment of powers out there and reduced them to a set of game effects and modifiers, complete with some sort of point-based power construction system. This leads to a robust game system capable of simulating a dizzying range of powers, but tends to encourage a gearhead mentality.

                            On the other hand, you have the Marvel class of games, featuring the likes of Marvel Superheroes, Truth&Justice, Marvel Heroic Adventure, and Icons. This tends toward a much more freeform approach to powers that doesn't try to spell out every little detail about what a given power can or can't do. Instead, it focuses on hitting the highlights and leaving the rest to the imagination of the player. Its main weakness lies in how much it depends on the GM and player being on the same page.

                            Ironically, M&M and Icons were created by the same guy (Steve Kenson).

                            Aberrant 1e leaned toward the DC model. I'd like to see Aberrant 2e lean toward the Marvel model.

                            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                I really appreciate the team making this preview available. Peeks behind the curtain are great for fans, but stressful for developers trying to balance getting feedback with saving some surprises.

                                                It's impossible to judge the entire system on just what's here, so here's my gut reactions to the parts we can see.

                                                Enhancements. This looks like a way to make actions more powerful, without altering their probability of success, like a poison that inflicts a bunch of damage but you’ve got to scratch the target first. I like this and suspect I’ll like it more when a list of examples is released.

                                                Complication. As an alternative to modifying the TN and/or dice pool to set difficulties, I think this mechanic will be faster and allow the players to inject more flavor into the actions. Thumbs up.

                                                Consolation. I really like this as a tool to make failures interesting, which has become a major goal in my personal GM’ing skill set. I like the examples given, and I hope the full version of the text gives players the freedom to make suggestions.

                                                Effect. I’m generally positive on this one. The examples that go along with this mechanic will be critical in establishing the power level of a ‘point’ of Effect.

                                                Mixed Actions. I’m neutral on this. I’d prefer a hard line of “one action is one action,” but if you’re going to allow this sort of over-the-top-ness, this is a decent solution.

                                                When to Roll The Dice. Excellent, in terms of rules, in terms of setting a tone of collaboration, and in terms of explicitly setting the precedent that players can negotiate.

                                                Momentum and the Black Pool. I think I see what the goal is -- not letting failures derail the game -- but it adds unnecessary complications. So I can’t Activate a Power until someone fails? I’m rolling a bunch of dice poorly in a Complex Attempt, and the solution to keep the action moving is to let me roll more dice? (Caveat: I don’t find ‘rolling lots of dice’ to be particularly dramatic, so I’m not a fan of the overall complex action.) With the Complications and Consolation mechanics already in play, I don't see the point of Momentum.

                                                I do like the idea of Immediate Aid, so your failure helps your fellows, but I see that as a Consolation, not Momentum.

                                                The Red Pool. Very much against. As part of Momentum, I don't see the utility. Additionally, as the GM, I have more than enough going on -- describing the world, responding to my 3 players destroying my prep work, balancing screen time, anticipating what comes next. Tracking an additional resource, one that may leave me unable to activate an NPC’s dramatically appropriate ability? Nope.

                                                Is the goal of the Red Pool to limit the GM’s power? For my money, Sardonyx is going to be the base for a game that requires extraordinary GM trust from the players -- as a PC, your opponents could literally be gods. Attempting to put mechanical barriers in the GM’s path seems counter-intuitive.

I like a game like Champions that says "You have the ability to cause damage at a distance." and then I choose if it is electricity or fire or gamma radiation blasts or shooting my fingernails. I then get to take the base of "I do x amount of damage out to a range of y meters." and then I customize it. It allows me to make my character's powers and make them my own.

                                                    It is always something that I like to do: make my character my own. I do it from the concept and I will do it with the attributes and abilities and merits and backgrounds. Since the powers are going to be a part of the character, I want to customize those as well.
