        Originally posted by Shepherdboi View Post
        The thing that puts him clearly beyond human is his aggregate abilities. I remember reading an official DC explanation of Batman's capabilities from about 10-20 years ago. He was basically olympic or near-olympic level as a sprinter, marathon runner, swimmer, gymnast, weight lifter, and martial artist. He's also a brilliant polymath and businessman. He manages to fit everything into his daily schedule via short power naps and a specialized high protein diet.

        Being any one of these things is fine, since real people do that stuff. Being a few of those things is also fine, since exceptional people have been known to do that as well. Doing all of it isn't fine, since real humans can't train for all of that at once. A man with a weight lifter's build is flat out the wrong body type to properly excel at several of those other athletic tasks., so at least some "normal" humans in the DC universe can apparently train themselves to an impossible degree of competence.
        This is just PCs of any sort with enough xp, though. In real life, nobody actually has the equivalent of Mit 5, Dex 5, Stm 5, but Batman (as a baseline, if you made him that way) would have exactly that...as can your PC if that's how you spend your xp. It's terribly unrealistic, but fits the genre convention of highly competent protagonists that Trinity Continuum is following.

        It's certainly doable even at less stats as a Talent, simply by using Extraordinary Effort as necessary (Mit 3, Athletics 3, and Extraordinary Effort are mechanically better than Mit 5, Athletics 5). Really, a Talent can thus duplicate every Mega-Attribute at 1 in terms of their maximum potential, if only briefly. That's enough for Batman, at least in theory (and in combination with The Bigger They Are for fighting superhumans).

        Personally, I still think he generally works better as a Nova (Mega-Intellect, Mega-Cunning, and Mega-Composure in excess of 1 are all very on-theme, as is a bit of Q-Tech), but it depends on what version of Batman you're doing, and he's also viable as a Talent.

                Originally posted by Mateus Luz View Post
                Batman can also have some skill tricks to increase scale on specific situations, and with enough skill tricks it’s possible to cover a large spectrum of actions
                Quick note: in the rules as written, there's a hard limit as to how many Skill Tricks you can have: no more than three per Skill, and then only if you have the Skill at five dots. IMHO, though, Talents should be an exception to that, either by loosening up the cap a bit (e.g., they can have twice as many Skill Tricks then a baseline for their skill level) or by removing it entirely. Or maybe a mix of the two: for instance, let them have two Skill Tricks at the third dot, four at the fourth, and as many as they want at the fifth.

                Also: if Batman has The Bigger They Are, then there are a number of cases where someone might mistakenly think that he's somehow got Scale (because “you'd have to have Scale to deal with that”) when in fact he's merely ignoring the opposition's Scale.

                And finally, I wouldn't give Batman maxed out Might, or Manipulation, for that matter. In the first case, he's regularly shown to be at a disadvantage strength-wise against muscle-bound goons. And he's not really the type who manipulates people, save for a handful of cases that can readily be handled with Skill Tricks and/or Gifts; he tends to rely more on Presence in his social interactions. He's still better than average in these “weak spots” of his attribute spread; but I could easily see him “only” having three dots in each.

                    Skill Tricks have another problem, besides what Dataweaver pointed: they are fueled by Momentum. This makes them a) more rarely used than we normally think they would and b) bound to other player's permission to be used.

                    There are three rules about Skill Tricks that I think the Core should have addressed, but as far as I know, didn't:
                    1) Create some mechanism for Talents to use Skill Tricks by spending a personal resource.
                    2) Create some mechanism for Talents to have more Skill Tricks than currently allowed (as Dataweaver pointed), and/or emulate Skill Tricks (similar to Aberrants Prodigy Mega-Edge).

                    3) Clarify the issue about NPCs and Skill Tricks: do they have them? If so, how they use them, since they spend Momentum? I house-ruled this one like this: whenever an NPC is in the verge of using a Skill Trick, the Storyguide decides if doing so is something "pro-troupe" or "against-the-troupe". If it's pro-troupe, the players pay with their Momentum (exactly as if it was a PC); if it's against-the-troupe, then any Momentum the NPC "spends" is actually added to the Players Momentum Pool.

                        Yes; Momentum being an inherently shared resource has proven problematic for our group. Instead, we treated as a shareable resource: each character has his or her own Momentum Pool, but any player can freely donate his or her character's Momentum points to anyone's, for any reason. Rather than there being a “group veto” on spending Momentum, everyone can spend the Momentum that he or she has earned freely; and that includes spending it on what someone else is trying to do. It solves a lot of problems for us.

                        That's why I didn't really address the first and third points Timecrafter mentioned: at my table, they're non-issues.

                                                            Nobody is saying that Manipulation doesn't let you read people, but many actions fall under multiple stats in this system. Sure Manipulation covers reading people, but Cunning also says that 'notice details quickly' and includes 'understand a political situation', 'act' and 'con' in its list of actions you can do with it (and conning absolutely involves getting a read on people). Similarly, Composure specifically notes that it covers 'recognizing when one is being lied to or otherwise deceived'. Under 'Empathy', the actual skill for reading people, telling when someone is lying to you is listed as Composure while 'reading what a business rival wants' is Cunning + Empathy. Only playing good cop in an interrogation is listed as Manipulation.

                                                            None of which is to say you can't do some of those things with Manipulation as well, but Cunning and Composure really do cover most of it.

                                                            Now, if talking about Batman as a Nova specifically, this is a lot less true, because Mega-Cunning and Mega-Composure are pretty divergent from Mega-Manipulation in what they do, and Batman probably would need Mega-Manipulation's Scale increase...but as a Talent, he can raise Scale on Cunning or Composure just as easily as he can Manipulation, and thus can get by just fine with Manipulation 3 or so.
