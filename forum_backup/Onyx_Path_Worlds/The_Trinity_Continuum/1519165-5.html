        With a quick victory in WWI, you don't get the crushing reparations that Germany was made to endure in our timeline; which in turn means no fall of the Weimar Republic, no rise of Hitler and the Nazis, and no WWII. For that matter, a quick WWI victory would have ended the war before the US got into it; and you wouldn't even get as much of the horrors of trench warfare. It wouldn't be World War I, and probably wouldn't even be The Great War; it might just be “the War in Europe”.

        The latter half of the 20th century was shaped by the World Wars; without them, the world would end up looking very different.

                My initial thinking is that, For Reasons™, there never is a naval arms race between Britain and Germany, lowering the tension between the two powers. Germany instead uses that money on the army, and instead of going through Belgium, manages to fight through the French forces at the border and eventually take Paris. The French capitulate, and only end up losing a few of their African colonies and a couple of Pacific islands in the treaty. But it's enough to eventually make the Republic fall. And Paris may be the new hotness for international Marxism.

                Meanwhile, the Eastern front is more of a slog, but the Russians eventually lose. Finland, Poland-Lithuania, and Ukraine are established as buffer states/puppets, and the Revolution happens, but Lenin is still sitting in a German jail, so the Bolsheviks never come to power. Russia is probably a weak and corrupt Constitutional Monarchy for the next few decades.

                Austria-Hungary could perhaps try to evolve into a Tripple Monarchy (adding Serbia), but the early 20th century European obsession with ethnic nationalism will likely tear it apart regardless.

                In the US, Wilson doesn't get reelected. I'm still working on specifics for it, as well as what happens in the Middle East, Japan, China, Mexico, Britain, and Ireland.

                            Looking at the Kaiserreich timeline, I am reminded about Irish home rule (and certain matters with the Welsh church) being delayed because of the War. So, in this hypothetical setting, that delay doesn't happen, and Ireland gets its own Parliament. This means no Easter Uprising, and the Irish independence movement probably still being mainly focused on legal maneuvering rather than violent rebellion. (Although, things being what they are, there's probably going to be outbreaks of violence in the north that may eventually require Ulster being split off on its own anyway.)

                            I want the Ottoman Empire to still collapse, possibly because of civil war (likely between the Committee of Union and Progress and more traditionalist factions that grows to include the various ethnic independence movements) and other factors. The UK and Germany probably broker and enforce a peace treaty of some sort. I'm thinking that Constantinople and the surrounding Bosporus Straight become and international zone, full of various intrigues, spies, smugglers, and other fun adventure/espionage tropes. There's a Turkish state in much of Anatolia, but also a larger independent Armenia in the east along with an independent Kurdistan. Much of Mesopotamia and the Arabian Peninsula are ruled by the Hashemite Kingdom (that may or may not declare itself the new Caliphate). Palestine is a German protectorate. Kuwait and Oman are under British influence.

                                The reason why I like the Kaiserreich timeline so much is because their political ideologies and national landscape have a lot more shades of grey than our world. Without the extremism of Stalinism in the soviet union and the pure evil of the nazis, democracy is not necessarily considered the standard "good" system and the only viable option, even though some ideologies are clearly more oppressive than others (https://kaiserreich.fandom.com/wiki/...cal_Ideologies). What this would mean for the adventure setting is that the political inclinations of the characters would play a much bigger part. For example, I could see another internal division within the AEON Society that is purely about politics in which the most idealistic members constantly argue about which political system should set the path to the future. Maxwell then would have to decide if he should pick a side and risk losing members who could create more radical organizations, or try to appease everyone at the cost of stability.

                                                        Assassin group concepts in Aquarius (none of these are named, and there are probably preexisting groups which can be slotted into these roles; I just don't have any books handy to check at the moment).

                                                        The Western Star Chamber - nominally American, but possibly also including other NATO members and allies. The super secretive agency that in theory answer only to the President, but in all likelihood are a law unto themselves. How "honorable" they are would really depend on just who is in charge. A Wilford Brimley lookalike working an anonymous job in a bank basement who truly believes in American ideals is going to run a different shop than a psychotic albino kept alive via blood transfusions from orphans. Their targets are those threats to Freedom/Queen & Country, but whose existence can't be exposed to the public or even most normal government offices.

                                                        The Third Party - The middle faction in the Cold War, who seek to play both sides against each other in order to weaken them and eventually knock off the winner. Exactly what their motivations and post-victory plans might vary, from world domination to some sort of idealistic "greater good" utopia (that like most utopias ends up being build on the corpses of anyone in the way.

                                                        The Ladies Finishing School for Assassins - The femme fatals, motivated by a mix of thrill seeking, profit, liberation, and group loyalty.

                                                        The Hunt Club - the thrill seekers who do this for fun (and a lot of money).

                                                        The Octopus - The Man, aka the People in Power and their agents working to advance their way up the ladder. Essentially killing to maintain the status quo and their place in it.

                                                        The Ancient Order of Assassins - The "exotic Orientals", drawing on Ra's al Ghul's sect from the 1970s Batman comics and Fu Manchu in Marvel's Master of Kung Fu, as well as all of the other period material that influenced them.
