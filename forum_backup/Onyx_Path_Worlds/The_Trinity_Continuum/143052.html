      Weeeell...
      Not the most original people for me to start with but here's some classic fictional characters:

      Indiana Jones - Resourceful Daredevil that constantly outsmart, outfight and outrun enemies of superior power and resources.

      Doc Savage - Can't think of anyone that's a more stereotypical Stalwart. A supergenius scientist and superpowered adventurer with an implacable moral code. Most likely one of the inspirations for the games Primoris

      Fu Manchu - A diabolical genius whose mesmerist powers can twist the mind of even the most resilient and turn the greatest of friends into the deadliest of foes.

      The Shadow - Unrelenting vigilante of justice. His mesmerist powers allow him to both "know what evil lurks in the hearts of men" as well as to "cloud men's mind" to move and strike unseen.

      Rick Blaine - Smooth, calm and collected. His current career as a simple nightclub owner in Casablanca hides a colorful past. Though a mere mortal, this Daredevil shows the full capabilities of humans: No woman can resist him and no man can stand against him. That is, whenever he can get his head out of the bottle.

      And a real life daredevil as well:

      Amelia Earhart - Fearless and talented, this woman would not let social mores or silly gender expectations choke out her adventurous spirit . Lost to the world, we must wonder: Did tragedy befall her? Or did her journey lead her to discover wonders and fortunes beyond the imagination of us lesser mortals?
      And now with her gone what will become of her legacy, The Ninety-Nines? What adventures await this organization of all-female pilots in the times to come?

                              Interestingly, in the pulps, the Shadow was a mere vigilante. A couple of stories have the villains seeing the Shadow's "true face" and remark that he is a man of many faces with no true face of his own. Sounds very much like a stalwart shape-changing ability. Interestingly enough, the Shadow is Kent Allard, with Lamont Cranston being a separate character whose identity Kent assumed from time to time.

                              In the radio drama, they couldn't easily describe to viewers how and where the Shadow was hiding, so they introduced the idea of the Shadow having spent some time in the east and learning the ability to hypnotically cloud men's minds, making him almost certainly a mesmerist. For simplicity's sake, the Kent Allard identity was dropped and the Shadow was simply Cranston. This version, the millionaire vigilante detective cloaked in darkness, is almost certainly the origin of heroes like Batman.

                              I believe one of the radio stories off-handedly mentions that the ability to become nearly invisible is "a technology soon to be available to police officers everywhere," implying the super-science of a daredevil.

                              So really, depending on which interpretation you prefer, the Shadow -- and many pulp characters -- could fit into any of the three Inspired types.
