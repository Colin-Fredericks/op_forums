    Trinity Continuum: Essence (An attempt to add the Exalted to the Trinity Continuum)

    11-23-2021, 11:09 PM
    This is a rather simple attempt by me to create the Exalted within the world and the system of the Trinity Continuum. Link HERE.

    I was inspired to make this homebrew thanks to the efforts of a number of different individuals as well as various Onyx Path properties. These include (of course) the 2nd Edition of the Trinity Continuum, the Essence version of Exalted, Dragonmystic 's "Exalted Reincarnated", Mateus Luz 's "Fists of Flux," as well as Exalted vs. the World of Darkness.

    In the current state (v. 0.1), I do not currently have any Charms, Caste/Aspect Powers or lore established. Almost all of the basic rules (outside of Caste/Aspect Powers and the Great Curse) are filled out, and I'd like to receive some feedback on what could be done better. My design intentions are as follows:
    1. Exalts are powerful forces of nature, able to change the world for the better or the worse.
    2. Like Psions and Psiads, Exalts can access the 3rd Tier of Power (some naturally, others with effort) but have not yet evolved (or perhaps "returned") to the level of strength seen in Quantum 6+ Novas.
    3. Charms are simple, singular and powerful. There are charms which every Exalt can use, and some which only a specific type can use (a la Exalted Essence).
    4. Terrestrial Exalts start better, Celestial Exalts finish better. However, both are still powerful and have their niches even at the very beginning or ending of campaigns.
    5. Essence is an easy-to-use, simple-to-retain, but difficult-to-regain resource a la Scion's Legend. While a portion is returned every so often, Exalts can also draw upon the strength of their bonds in order to regain points of Essence, at the cost of weakening those connections.
    6. Martial Arts will make their reappearance, but Sorcery will not, as I feel it intrudes in the niches carved out by Novas.
    7. Artifacts will also make their return alongside Evocations as Exalted "Super-Science." Like some of the items discovered in the time of TC:Adventure!, Exalted Super-Science will have a much more mystical nature to them, such as flaming swords or magic carpets compared to the jetpacks and laser rifles of Novas and the Biotech of Psions.
    Again, I'd love to hear any feedback you might have, and answer any questions that might come up. I'm looking towards filling out all of the Castes and Aspects next before moving on to the Great Curse and finally Charms. Thank you for your time and interest!

                  For the Nova/Talent comparison, I don't think comparing any Exalt to a standard Talent in terms of power level is a great idea. In Essence there's a lot of general "prowess" even among the Terrestrial Exalted, allowing them to still accomplish impressive feats, which leads me to believe that a Dragon-Blooded is at worst about on par with a Superior that then "evolves" into a Nova at a high enough Essence level. That's why I made the difference that I did between Celestial and Terrestrial: Celestial Exalts begin at Tier 3, meaning they have an easier time succeeding on rolls, but start at Essence 1, which means they're fairly strapped for MP. Terrestrials on the other hand start at Tier 2, so they don't do as well on rolls in general, but not only do they start at Essence 2 thanks to being an Exalt most/all of their lives (giving them more resource points and making their Excellencies even better at the start) but reaching Essence 3 and thus ascending to Tier 3 is only a mere 15 XP away. Maybe I should instead increase it to Essence 4 to give more of an edge to the Celestials, but that's something that can be dealt with later.

                  My personal vision of Charms is that they work like Stalwart Gifts, providing a lot of power and having two different categories (General and Typical). I drew inspiration from DragonMystic's Exalted Reincarnated for the different "tiers" of charms, albeit 3 instead of 2, and combined that with Essence's Charm Modes for the idea of Leeways, exemptions that make certain charms easier to access depending on Exalt type. Being able to fly for example might be a 2nd Rank/Immortal Athletics Charm, for example, but Lunars can just shapeshift wings already, and so they can learn it at the start as a 1st Rank/Mortal charm. Solars unlike the other types do not naturally have any Leeways, being the most bread and butter Exalt type there is, but can opt into it with their Supernal Skill trait.

                  And yeah, I will be using the Storypath combat system, I'm already working on how certain Anima Banner powers (Chosen of Battles and Endings, Dawn Caste, etc.) for example would function within it. Charms will be the same once I get started on those.

                  ...Actually, now that I'm thinking about it, I'm kind of worried about Getimians, same with Exigents. The whole schtick of the former is "Alternate Timelines," but that steps on the toes of Talents and other Flux users, while the latter's customization clashes with that of the Nova. In comparison, one could make the argument that a Lunar intrudes on Novas and their Mega-Attributes, but I think Lunars are sufficiently specific enough in their powers and intent that they'll be easy to set aside from the Novas, who are far more Build-a-Bear.

                                      Off the top of my head: Exalts come in two power levels, Celestial and Terrestrial. Solars (as well as their variants, the Abyssals and Internals), Lunars, Sidereals, and Alchemicals are comparable to Novas in power level, while Dragon-Blooded are comparable to Psions in power level. Exigents are, by definition, all over the board; they're literally intended to be a catch-all to let you make anything that doesn't fit the other types. I don't know enough about the Getimian or Liminal Exalted to say anything about their power levels.

                                      In terms of the types of Exalted, a lot of them are Talent-like: Solars/Abyssals/Infernals, Sidereals, and Dragon-Blooded have skill-based Charms that would work like what Talents and Assassins have, save only for the power level. Lunars and Alchemicals have Attribute-based powers that would be reasonable matches for Nova Mega-Edges. I'm pretty sure Liminals also fit the Attribute-based Mega-Edges approach (they're basically Frankenstein's monster); again, I'm not familiar enough with the Getimians to say; and again, the Exigents are catch-alls that could be either.

                                      In short, there are two power levels (Nova-like Celestials and Psion-like Terrestrials) and two power types (Gift-like skill-based Charms, and Mega-Edge-like attribute-based Charms).

                                      Solars are the most directly skill-based, though a number of their powers tend to have a sort of “radiant energy” theme: conjuring swords or arrows composed entirely of energy, for example.

                                      Lunars are shapeshifters.

                                      Sidereals are astrologers and martial artists.

                                      Dragon-Bloods are “element benders”.

                                      Exigents, as stated above, are a catch-all.

                                      Abyssals are kind of like Solars, but with a death-aspects flavor to their powers.

                                      Infernals are also kind of like Solars, but with a demonic bent.

                                      Getimians are a sort of counterpoint to the Sidereals, people who were erased from the web of fate.

                                      Liminals are, as I said earlier, Frankenstein's monster.

                                      Alchemicals are mystical robots.

                                      That hits the main points of the Exalted setting. There are also a trio of inhuman races: Dragon Kings (supernaturally empowered dinosaurs), Mountain Folk (vaguely like dwarves), and Rakshasa (the Exalted version of the faerie folk).

                                      There are also some near-human variants, which don't have built-in powers; rather, they can Exalt the same way that regular humans can.

                                                      So, an update on the project's progress: Currently it's at V0.2.0 after I added the Caste/Aspect Powers. I decided on giving each Caste/Aspect two powers, one passive and the other either dependent on having the Anima Banner active, or by spending 1 Essence. Solar, Abyssal and Infernal Castes share these categories (So all Night, Day and Nadir Castes have a power that activates when the Anima Banner is active, for example.) These abilities haven't been playtested yet of course, but I'm hoping that it will be refined to a better state in a quick fashion.

                                                      While I don't have my notes with me at this moment, I do have everything regarding the Great Curse finished and ready to be transcribed onto the official document. One thing I've noticed in various editions about what to do regarding the Limit track is that they advise the player to roleplay as their Limit gets closer to the end, such as the character being more irritated or stressed, but nothing beyond that. Instead, I decided to create mechanics for this phenomenon.

                                                      Every Exalt has a Limit Track with 10 spaces. Certain conditions can activate once per scene to give the Exalt 1 Limit (such as a Dragon-Blooded being in an environment devoid of their element, or a Lunar being restrained against their will.) Other conditions can once per session offer a reprieve, lowering limit by 1 (such as a Dragon-Blooded spending a scene surrounded by their element, or a Lunar breaking others free from confinement.) For the first 5 spaces, there are no additional effects. Past that, however...

                                                      At Limit 6-7, the Exalt suffers from a condition called "Limit Tension" that impacts roleplaying (like a Sidereal experiencing bouts of paranoia, or an Abyssal generating deathly phenomena.) Terrestrial Exalted don't suffer from Limit Tension, but have another permanent condition that complicates their life (such as Liminals exposing their hideous appearance when their Anima Banner is active.)

                                                      At Limit 8-9, the Exalt gains an additional condition called "Limit Strain" which has a drastic effect on the game (such as Sidereals infecting others nearby so that 2's also count for botches, or Abyssals becoming either too hideous or too beautiful and causing complications.)

                                                      Finally, at Limit 10, the Exalt undergoes Limit Break and completely empties their track. For the rest of the scene, they go absolutely nuts (or cause others to go bonkers), causing major issues with the current situation (Sidereals for example cannot gain or spend momentum for the scene and neither can their allies. Abyssals become compelled to annihilate all life and it's very difficult to stop them from doing so.)

                                                      Altogether, this system will hopefully make it so that the climb from 0 to 10 Limit will be more interesting and serve as an opportunity for more roleplaying.
