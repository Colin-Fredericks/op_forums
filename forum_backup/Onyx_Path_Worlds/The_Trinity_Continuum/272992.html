    Why a Second Edition?

    10-05-2014, 12:51 PM
    This is not a negative post or the start of an edition wars.

    I'm seriously asking...what has changed with the game so much that a second edition seems necessary?

    I have the same questions regarding Promethean and Scion.

    I understand why some other games have been reimagined. For example, Demon the Descent is a totally new take on the oWoD Demon the Fallen. So I see where you need a new book and line for that product.

    But are Promethean, Aberrant/Adventure/Trinity, and Scion changing scope to the degree that Demon did (love the new Demon by the way) or is this just a fiddling with the rules tweak and the fluff is still the same?

    I'm just trying to decide how far down the rabbit hole of new editions I can go. If the various Second Editions are totally new directions for the property, then I'll have to look at picking them up. If they are just rules tweaks, then I'm probably good with what I've got...and I've got just about everything from those three (five) properties.

    Thanks for any insight into "why a second edition".

      It's been ten years since the New World of Darkness started.

      Demon: the Descent is not a second edition of Demon: the Fallen - it's a separate game in the same fashion as Vampire: the Requiem, Werewolf: the Forsaken, and Mage: the Awakening are separate games from Vampire: the Masquerade, Werewolf: the Apocalypse, and Mage: the Ascension, respectively.

      Promethean's second edition is more akin to Blood and Smoke: the Strix Chronicle, which is functionally and soon textually going to be Vampire: the Requiem Second Edition.

      In short, a second edition because the games have evolved over time and ten years is quite long enough to justify refining the material's presentation and incorporating mechanical adjustments.

          Well, we're getting mortal-level play, ten core pantheons, and the opportunity to play non-Scion supernaturals.

          Scion's mechanics were infamously broken, and really couldn't handle the power scaling the game called for, so they've been spending considerable time on a new Storyteller-inspired system that can handle scaling better.

          Then there's the opportunity to include more accurate research (most notable with the Loa/Yoruba, but most of the pantheons need it to a greater or lesser extent), to provide rules for PCs to create their own pantheons, for PCs to adopt other divine identities, and thereby alternate powersets...

              Hi. The various developers can tell you more about the specific changes in the second editions, but I can give a broad overview.

              In Promethean, there's a lot changing, although it's not a Fallen to Descent jump. The setting is being renovated to make it more accessible and more exciting, including expanding the roster of splats. Powers have been redesigned from scratch, going even further than in Vampire's second edition, Blood and Smoke. It's being built on the second edition nWoD rules, first seen in The God-Machine Chronicle.

              In Scion, the game is being expanded to include a "street-level" power tier and multiple variations on the core setting. The treatment of several pantheons is being overhauled: for example, the Mesoamerican pantheons are getting a more authentic portrayal. The rules are new from the ground up, since there were a lot of issues with the first edition mechanics and powers. We're taking an improved (and entirely different) approach to the scaling from mortal to god.

              In Trinity, the setting is being evolved in a number of ways, including introducing a modern day (pre-Aberrant) branch of the universe with its own organizations and history that tie into the larger chronology. I'll defer to Ian, the lead developer, on a more detailed explanation. It's also using the same new rules as Scion.

              All of these second editions include significant setting additions, and the rules changes go beyond tweaks and bug fixes.

                  Even if we changed virtually nothing in the setting and mechanics, it wouldn't be fair if we started releasing new material for a game, but required people to buy/own a rulebook released 15 or more years ago in order to play it. Those original rulebooks are often hard to find or prohibitively expensive or both.

                  It makes a lot more sense to release new rulebooks, especially considering that we are changing both the setting and system.
