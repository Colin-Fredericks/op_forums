        Well, there are 2 big options on his family tree

        1. Margaret Mercer is Max granddaughter and Michael is his son (biological or not, probably biological because he is described as very similar)

        2. Margaret is his daughter and Michael is Max himself. He pretends to be his own son, pretend to die and keep his old self identity but keeps his actual daughter in the shadow pretending to be his grandfather (that would not make much sense to me).

        In the 30s and later, I believe there was a significant number of people that pretended to be straight to fit in the society and avoid persecution (not all LGBT+ did it, but maybe he did). It was a matter of survival, he loved Michael, he probably did love him a lot, even named his son as Michael (even if he is Michael himself), but he probably got married and his wife eventually died of old age (he is immortal, the others not).

        Anti-Max is possibly himself, after many many years, trying to correct his own mistakes, with a point of view different as he has seen much more things than his younger self. This would be a plot similar to the one of Nozdromu becoming Murozond and starting the infinite dragon flight in WoW, after centuries of corruption (or in Max case centuries changing the timeline with qusestionable results)

            We discussed both of those actually. The implication in 1e was that Michael was Max himself from a earlier point in time. However for our game we decided that we like the story possibility's of Aeon better if there was a shadow war between Max and Anti-Max going on in the background of the setting with each running butterfly effect missions using Aeon personnel to change the future at cross purposes. It has nothing to do with Max being gay or ace or whatever, more of tailoring it to our group as we tend to run more spy or covert ops style games with intrigue and clouded motivations for even the best of intentioned missions.

            Spoilers ahead for Adventure! setting secrets.

            I may be mistaken as I don't have the book to check (I mainly haunt the forums while I have down time at work) but if I remember correctly doesn't it say in the setting secrets that Anti-Max is a creature that was feeding off the Flux energy released by Max on his first trip thru time that was influenced by all of the negative things it saw following him. At some point it had watched so much of Max that it decided to copy him becoming Anti-Max.Or something like that, I'm sure I'm getting some of the details wrong. I know in 1e Evil Max was a version of Max himself from an alternate timeline connected to 1e Aberrant in some way, but they dropped most if not all of that for 2e.

            The rational we had for Anti-Max being Michael was he wanted to experience what being human actually was like and not be defined by being just a copy of something else, hence the leaving of descendants. He is trying to steer the future to something that matches his outlook on life which is a bitter and cynical version of the bright and shinning future Max wants to lead the world to.

            Ultimately it comes down to what works for your group. We've had to change our "cannon" for our game every time a book comes out already, the way we play until it comes up in game nothing is set in stone and can change at the drop of the hat if it makes for a better story. Of course once someone encounters it in game it's set and the rest of the "cannon" has to take it into account when it changes.While it dose mean that we write ourselves into a corner every so often when newly released information contradicts something that has been established in-game as true so the other game master and I have to scramble for a reason both can be true at once (once we had to default to "you were being lied to the entire time" which changed the course of the entire campaign at that point) its fun and adds a layer of flexibility which comes in handy when the other game master and I trade off who's running the game.
            Hell that just happened with the latest release from the current Anima kick-starter. We had discussed how we were going to fit Anima into our overall metaplot and were comfortable with it until the chapter on allegiances dropped the other day and the info in that is making us reevaluate quite a few assumptions we had made about Aeon and refactor a large chunk of the campaign metaplot..
