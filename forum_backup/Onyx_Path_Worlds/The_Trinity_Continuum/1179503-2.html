                    Originally posted by CHILL View Post
                    How 'baked in' is utilizing momentum and dramatic editing into how the psi powers work?                     Hard one to answer, as it’s both ‘not at all’ and ‘completely’. Let me expand on that a little.

                    Dramatic Editing - Psi powers don’t take this into account at all. Psions don’t tend to use dramatic editing, that’s a Talent thing. Individual groups can have psions use DE if they want, since the Storypath system is flexible and designed to allow cross over of the different rules across the Continuum. But the default assumption is that psions don’t.

                    Momentum - You might need to give me more on what difficulties you see in using or removing Momentum, because I’m not sure if my next answer hits your concerns. In the sense that you add to the momentum pool when you fail, and take dice from it when you want to use them, the psi system works like every other part of Storypath. Fail a roll, add to the pool. Need a bit of a boost for a dice pool, take from it. I don’t recall that I referenced Momentum at all in the chapter, as it’s just a thing that’s assumed to exist, like Target Numbers. I didn’t reference them either, I just assume people know to roll agains the one that applies to their character.

                    So, with that said, if you want to remove momentum from the system, it shouldn’t impact the psi powers as presented at all.

                    Does that answer what you’re asking?

                    Writer. Developer. World of Darkness | Chronicles of Darkness | The Trinity Continuum

                            Originally posted by CHILL View Post
                            But it isn't especially woven into Psi powers specifically from what you're saying, outside the core die roll.
                            Correct. None of the Psi powers as written have anything that calls for ‘spend X Momentum to gain Y ability’. (With the caveat that the developer is fully within their rights to change things as they see fit. But to the best of my knowledge, John has seen no reason that the Psi powers need such a mechanism.)

                            All Psi powers are run off a dice pool of Psi + Mode. There will be occasions that you’ll want to try and increase your dice pool (or successes) - the psi system is a little more flexible than it was in 1st edition, and you’ll see what I mean in a week or two. For these occasions you’ll improve your chances if you can take advantage of things available under Storypath, such as items or situations that grant Enhancement, for example, or taking a few dice from momentum. But none of these are required to use psi powers.

                            I hear what you’re saying about meta-currency, but remember that the Trinity Continuum is a rather narratively-driven universe, where the PCs are heroes who do big things. Talents tap into this most directly with Dramatic Editing to have things go their way, but Psions (and presumably eventually Novas) get some benefit from this with momentum. You can run the game without such things - you might need to do a little tweaking - but the universal assumption (meaning - the universe assumes this to be true) that heroes are heroes and should strive to overcome obstacles, get back up when they’re knocked down, and eventually succeed and reach their goals.

                            Originally posted by CHILL View Post
                            I'm very keen to read your take on the Psi powers when the Aeon manuscript drops. I'm especially interested in seeing how the relative power scales all function between the Aptitudes. Not to mentioned how you've incorporated Teleportation (which was arguably not as as low key as the powers in the 1st edition tended to be).
                            The Aptitudes will be recognisable, but some things are different. Some powers have shifted around, some are gone, some new ones take their place. Some Modes are different, some are pretty consistent with what they were before.

                            There’s some cool new stuff in Teleportation, but a lot that will feel familiar to people.

                            In terms of power scales, I think I’ve balanced the different Aptitudes pretty well. My overarching design goal was to make players curse that they can only take one Aptitude, and want to play them all. From playtest feedback, I think I’ve hit that goal.

                            (I can’t wait to talk more openly about these with all of you!)

                            Thanks for the reply, I really appreciate it!
