      From the Con:

      - Trinity Continuum core is modern day; "the world outside your window." Despite that, there are numerous differences - the Continuum is a more optimistic place to live, and little things like more money in the space program, more environmentalism. Heroes can make a difference, and more importantly, they have.
      - There's a "secret history of the world" and the Continuum is plagued by scientific anomalies and causality issues.
      - One writer's biggest inspirations were the TV shows Continuum and Fringe.
      - Aeon's developer is John Snead. He's looking at making the game more "metaplot agnostic" - setting the game around the latter third of the metaplot, but making it clear that the future of the game may not hold the previous one's in store.
      - Important distinction - "Trinity Continuum", not Trinity Universe. The Continuum has a unique relationship with quantum mechanics and time travel, inherent in the way dramatic editing works within the setting. It adds an extra weight to Aberrant and Trinity's assertion that, "no, the future needn't go this way."

      - The game uses the same base system as Scion, a far more action-oriented version of the Attribute + Ability classic system. You'll see a familiar-looking character sheet.

      Neall Raemonn Price
      Beleaguered Scion Developer

                  There was a Trinity Continuum sample document at the Onyx Path booth after the GenCon panel. I went and read through it the next day. Unfortunately it didn't have any system information, but it did have some great setting content.

                  The document was broken into two parts: IC fluff documents from all throughout time that gave little interesting pieces of the setting (some of which seemed like adventure setups), and a partial list of important modern groups that the players can interact with/be part of. The IC setting content was pretty entertaining. I especially liked a really well characterized missive from Houdini to the Aeon Society, which had the most perfect line in the entire document.

                  IIRC, the groups chapter had four groups in it:
                  - The Aeon Foundation (?) - A branch of the Aeon Society that does their operations. The coolest thing I remember from this group is their organization: they have set groups of normal people, and then people with powers act as floating consultants for the organization.
                  - The second group I don't remember the name of, but they were a group that fought against people that hunted people. The central theme of this group seemed to be that 1) the events of the most dangerous game actually happened, and 2) that they weren't an isolated occurrence but were the result of a secret society that hunted people for fun.
                  - Again, I don't remember the name of the third group, but they seemed to be based on a certain kind of transhumanist thinker. They are fairly libertarian, sometimes a little morally grey, but strongly against existential risk. Because of this, they fight strongly against nuclear proliferation, self-replicating nanobots, etc.
                  - A Caribbean/Central American group (leaving them unnamed because their name is changing before release). This is a group of freedom fighters whose goal is to take down dictatorships across the world. I really liked this group a lot. They were depicted in a very positive light, and it was pretty clear that although their methods were morally ambiguous, their goals were good.

                  There was a feeling with all of these groups that any could be posed as the heroes or villains of a story. There were also a number of references to a mysterious group called the Lightkeepers either helping out or fighting against various groups. We asked Ian afterward if he could tell us any more about them, and the answer was, "No. They're really awesome, though."

                  We also got a bit of clarification about rules. As mentioned, characters should be transferable across all systems and still be effective thanks to the environmental dice, so you should be able to, say, play a Scion in your Aberrant game (the example given was Thor). There are also not going to be any more mega stats in Aberrant, and I believe no Legend points in Scion.

                  My memory is not great, so if anyone read the document and wants to make an addition or correction, I'd be grateful.
