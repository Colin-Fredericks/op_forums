                                          My only concern is whether high-level Talents will still feel like, well, talents. High-level Gifts can certainly be beyond regular human capacity, even overtly so; but for the most part, I would expect them to be rooted in competence: things like ninja invisibility (your ability to sneak around gets upgraded to impossible levels), wuxia wire-working (applying athletics to the point where you're running up walls or drifting through the air in defiance of the laws of gravity), or more modern gun-fu offerings (most famously involving bullets that don't follow straight-line trajectories). The sort of things that one might expect of contemporary equivalents of Exalted's protagonists.

                                          The line between that and Novas is a fuzzy one; for instance, the earliest concept of Wonder Woman would have fit firmly in the “high-level Talent” concept as I imagine it, despite her capabilities being more or less the same then as they are now. But I feel that it's important that there at least be a significant portion of the high-level Gifts that are Skill-based.

                                              I just listened to the Podcast. Some clarifications and thoughts:

                                              First, nobody involved in the creation of the book said that high-level Talents would bear any resemblance to Æther's Magogs; that comment was made by the host. And personally, I think he got it wrong: Magogs are based on Æther, which is
                                              a blend of Flux and Quantum, and owes as much to Aberrant as it does to Talents.
                                              That was the main point of my last post.

                                              Next, I misread something in wyrdhamster's last post; so in case anyone else did the same, let me point out that TPG will not, repeat not, be including high-level Talents. Which is fine by me; because what I'm looking for is actually a tier of “Talents” that's higher than regular Talents (in that they can do blatantly impossible things), but still below the level of Max Mercer; and it's the “Max Mercer” level that the podcast's guests described when they said that they're never going to write rules for high-level Talents.

                                              Psychics have mesmerists and psiads at a base level, psions at an intermediate level, and proxies as the top-tier Psi-empowered; Quantum-powered heroes range from stalwarts, Superiors, and Dark Stars as the “hard to distinguish from baselines” bottom tier, Novas as the middle tier, and Transcended Novas as the top tier. I view Talents as the bottom tier of the Flux-wielders, and Max Mercer as the top tier; and I have no interest in games that operate at that upper level; so the reveal that they intend to never touch that level is perfectly okay with me.

                                              But conceptually, there's a “middle tier” here as well, where the characters are able to do blatantly impossible things, but aren't so OP that they need to mostly remain in Storyguide hands else they'll break the game. The “middle tier” is more like Assassins, but without its near-monomaniacal focus on one type of story (i.e., everything's geared around the way assassin movies play): my “high-tier Talents” would wield Masteries, but with none of this business about everything necessarily being divided between Preparation, Evaluation, and Execution.

                                              Which brings me to Heists. What I'm hearing about them makes me think that they cover much of the same ground as Assassins does with its Hunt mechanics, but with less of a straitjacket feel to it — largely because it's fully optional. The Masteries in Assassins assume that you'll be playing within the story structure of a Hunt, to the extent that which phase of the Hunt that you're in is built into their write-ups. As far as I can tell, there will be a few Gifts and Edges designed with Heists in mind; but for the most part, Gifts and Edges will be more broadly applicable than that.

                                              As well, not every Mastery from Assassins will be ported into Trinity Player's Guide as a Gift; only the ones that it would make sense for regular Talents to have as Gifts will be converted, along with daredevil and Squire Gifts from Adventure! and Æther that likewise would make sense for a modern-day Talent to have.

                                              My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
