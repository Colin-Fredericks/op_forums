                                                            What is the numerical measurement of a scene, then?

                                                            Hint: There is none because it is a dramatic unit of time, and therefore covers the time needed for the drama to occur and achieve payout. A scene could be a couple of seconds or the payout of several years. This is film language, think of how films handle it, and how you have definitely seen scenes where years pass. What keeps this from being overpowered is that the nature of the genre provides a clear sense of what's going on-this is "until the clock strikes twelve" sort of stuff, Till the Moment Is Gone sort of stuff.

                                                            As for the logistics and realities, dude, this is fairy magic going on. A few hours is a lifetime to the fae, and people who have only just met could have a household full of children before the sun is born again. The Contract is a momentary suspension of reality, a slice of time so infused with desire that, in this hour that goes on forever, anything could happen. The dream, of course, ends, and time and tide take what is owed-but what is left is promised and therefore kept.
