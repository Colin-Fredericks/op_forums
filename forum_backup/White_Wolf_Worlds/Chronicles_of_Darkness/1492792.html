              Mummy: The Cursed 2E has a few scenarios that are tantamount to time travel, if you really play with the disconnected nature of their memories. Such scenarios involve the Arisen somehow awakening in the time of Irem before the Rite of Return, or awakening in the far future where humans have attempted to colonize Mars; Arisen have the benefit that you do not need to run them as having memories aligned to linear time.               While Mummy is definitely the forebearer for the subject now, there's a lot of games that either feature time travel as part of the powersets of monsters or a prominent feature of the setting. Mage inherently has time travel through the Time Arcanum(I think you only need to 3 dots to get engage with it), the newly released Kith & Kin for Changeling introduces wide temporal displacement as a more regular feature, and while it's not a highly controllable factor, travelling forward and backwards in time is discussed as a reason to return to Arcadia, Hunter's implementation of Interstitial Spaces has always had connotations of time travel potential, Demon's signature city is built on time travel as a feature (and has had it crop up in some of the other settings, such as Istanbul), and Deviant's weird science keeps a foot in a door(a cut sidebar from the upcoming Clade Companion was all about how one form in particular was a great excuse for time travel). There's also implied spaces where it's engaged with, such as nods towards the Werewolf Lodges of the Clocktower and Tindalos respectively.

              Particularly in a post Dark Eras/Mummy 2E, a lot of games have either added (or had the benefit of hindsight retconning connotations to the effect) some way to engage with time travel as a story mechanic without utterly ripping apart a currently standing chronicle that might want to explore it. The bigger catch is whether or not a playgroup wants to engage with it-even with Mummy, that is pretty effectively the time-traveller splat, just as much care is put into establishing that you don't have to engage with the Timelessness of the Arisen as much as you absolutely can.

              I don't think, in terms of a splat splat, there's enough meat on the bones of time travel to warrant it, at least within the context of Chronicles interest-but if someone wanted to make a big grab bag of time-traveller facilitation for al lthe gamelines and wonky weird powers alongwith that, they might be able to get somewhere. The largest I would go with trying to make a Time Traveller Explicitly kind of template would be akin the the Minor/Merit Templates you find in Hurt Locker.

                                  The ones that come to mind are Mummies, Mages and some God-Machine shenanigans.

                                  Some additional ones would be to go to a Lower Depth where time is absent, have the players procure some kind of temporal buffer or time bubble, then figure out how to exit it at the point of their choosing.

                                  A far more complex option is to use black holes. Should someone figure out how to survive them, then time travel is possible. If the would be traveler could survive its forces, then move through the distorted space, survive the bent laws of physics and navigate the wibbly wobbly timey wimey ball, then they could exit the "labyrinth" in a date of their choosing. Setting up the systems to make all of that work would probably require an entire chronicle. But unlocking true time travel is too much of a legendary feat not to warrant that.

                                  Then a followup chronicle could take some cues from Chrono Cross to make them realize that they made a mess on the cosmic tier and now they need to fix the fallout.

                                  New experiences are the font of creativity, when seeking inspiration, break your routine.

                                                  Originally posted by Cauthon View Post
                                                  Let's just assassinate the Prophet. If we eliminate the embodiment of Tyranical Time, then Time should be free to flow as it pleases, right? :P
                                                  Suppose you miraculously figure out how to unify all its possibilities and defeat the symbol of tyranny infamous for its incredible ability to predict the future, would the cabal be ok with the existential dread that follows? The Prophet defines the Truth of "Great people shape history, everyone else follows or they are crushed", that works for positive leaders too, both mortal and supernatural. Take it one step further, the player cabal lead the story because they have such greatness, what happens if the cosmic contract enforcing that is missing ?

                                                  Furthermore, depending on the Ministry, agents of the Prophet may seem neutral or even helpful at first glance. They don't sweat the small details, they can give up something valuable now to gain something even more valuable 5, 10 or 100 years down the line. They think long term, they look at the big picture.

                                                  They can give you what you want, setting you up to self destruct by blind hubris. They could simply be engineering the scenario to make a future star cabal defect to the Seers or unknowingly take care of a problem for them. Your time travel device might be a convenient Iron Seal taking the cabal exactly where they want, because their actions were already accounted for, the trap laid out as a Xanatos Gambit serving his own interest.

                                                  The Prophet's machinations can be a lot more subtle and meticulous than the norm, similar to the Nemesis, the information war and layered intrigue are their greatest weapons. His Ministers are the ones that would arguably be running the Adjustment Bureau as a front, with a real objective of inducing false or ultimately detrimental choices.
