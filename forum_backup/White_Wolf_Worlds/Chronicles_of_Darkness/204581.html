    Lightening Up on Cover

    07-21-2014, 11:13 PM
    All but one of my players have refused to use any of their demonic powers that might cause a compromise. The one that did complained bitterly about the compromise roll. In general, the table complaint was that you are aggressively pummeled by the system not to be a cool, bad-ass Demon. Yes, you can make gadgets, but that's not anywhere near as cool as actually having a power you can use. This isn't something any of us realized when we invested in the game, since most of the book is about cool stuff and not about Compromise.

    Is there any way I can house rule this to make the system fun without breaking it. Or have I somehow misunderstood the rules (that's more likely).

    I've already house ruled Legend. They LOVED the Legend rule, and even tolerated the Compromise roll, but were going to stab me over the Impostor Condition, so I left that out.

    I was thinking of giving a flat massive bonus if no one saw you and the result was something that could have been conceivably achieved some other way. Logically, how was your cover strained if there's nothing it had to to accomidate what you did?

          0)Talk to your players and find a balance to what you're doing. Figure out just how hard you want to hit the God-Machine Surveillance. Also figure out hte math and point out how likely you really are to get screwed. Just talk it out.
          1)Remember you have some leeway. Breaking Cover is primarily a drama point, and thus should be invoked when the dramatic tension would cause a nice snap.
          2)Reward Risk. Your players might be overly paranoid about breaking cover, which is a thing ot talk to them about, but as a rule, find out what level they're worried and produce rewards that makes it worthwhile. Using Incendiary is a risk, but if you can use it to really screw with the G-M's day, you're more likely to do it.
          3)Feel free to create ways to subvert the threat. Create zones or times where there's just too much interference for cool power usage to be noted-you can explain it later. Creating windows where it's okay, if nothing else, helps foster the feeling it's okay to use these powers, which you can then slowly yank back to normal modus operandi with.
          4)Gadgets. Even before Flowers of Hell, Exploited Gadgets created a safe way to use powers, and Flowers just expanded the genius.

              Here's something I want you and your players to think about: you generally risk compromising your Cover during periods of intense conflict, whether it's triggering an Exploit to violently remove an obstacle, using fatebending Embeds over and over to bend probability in your environment to a whim, or turning into a monster of steel and fury to rend through your enemies. On the flip side, you recover it during periods of relatively stress-free play through living your Cover or shoring it up with pacts, mostly during the planning before a big set of actions or in the downtime afterwards.

              The way I think of it now is that risking compromise isn't actually a penalty at all. Morality meters aren't there to penalize you and to never be violated, it's to emphasize the themes and gameplay habits that the game wants you to do. Vampire is all about the slippery slope to becoming a monster, and Humanity reflects that.

              For Demon, the Cover system gives you bonuses when you're living subtly, is inherently tied to your most blatantly obvious powers and actions, and is naturally cyclical. Risking cover isn't a penalty, it's using a resource. You trade the potential for long term drawbacks for extreme short term benefits; you strain the Cover that keeps you safe by saying "I'm just a regular human, nothing to see here" when you reassert the fact that you're a terrifying inhuman monster to the rest of reality. The most extreme example of this is Going Loud, of course, but even the normal up-and-down movement of Cover reflects this.

              ​Let's look at three Unchained and their habits to see if we can't get a glimpse of this:

              The first is a Psychopomp whose entire goal is to avoid the gaze of the God-Machine. He figures the best way to do this is to get married, have 2.5 kids and a white picket fence in Wisconsin so through time, effort, and a little bit of luck he starts his existence as a family man. He has no contact with demonic society, he works at home, he uses Embeds now and again to smooth things out (especially when he feels the God-Machine approaching and has to relocate his family under false pretenses) but otherwise just...lives his life. He works his way up slowly to Cover 10 and, for the purposes of our story, lives happily ever after. Not the type of character you'd see as a PC very often, but it's representative of those who stick to one Cover and who won't jeopardize it at all unless forced to.

              The second is a Messenger who's a little more of regular PC material. She primarily moves between two Covers: her "day job" is being the owner of a hot little club by the waterside, but a lot of her attention goes into carving out a little criminal niche in town under the guise of an up-and-coming drug and weapons trafficker. She regards her club owner Cover as her safety net and is less likely to endanger it, but she still uses her wits, her Embeds and her mortal resources (along with those of her other Cover, in a pinch) to keep her club hot but not too noticeable and to scout out potential allies and enemies for her other gig. She treats her other Cover as more of her proactive identity, so while she isn't reckless she's a lot more willing to risk compromise to build up a reputation and client base while cementing her status as a major player. What little bumps she encounters here and there are smoothed over with Pacts and living out the life of a rising criminal star, and when things get a little too hot in one Cover she lies low in the other until it blows over. With the resources from both of these Covers she can live a life of comfort while establishing connections and gathering materiel for her comrades' fight against the God-Machine. Cover goes up a little, Cover goes down a little, Cover goes up again - this is what I'd consider the "normal" way that things go.

              The third is an almost cliche Destroyer Saboteur: it gets called in when the plans of the God-Machine in the area absolutely, positively have to be derailed. Ideally, if the danger was identified early enough then the demon's standard operating procedure is dig in and through its demonic contacts and hard legwork make a Cover that's tailor-made for this situation. If not, well, the demon spends his off-time creating and trading for Covers that are good enough to get you in but are entirely expendable. That's the key with this demon's treatment of Cover; whether it's creating a scientist, a middle manager or a street thug, the watchwords are Inconspicuous, Expendable, and Untraceable. The demon builds up a Cover as much as it can so that when everything falls apart and you have to hit hard, fast, and loud there's plenty of fuel for the fire. Once the job's done the Cover's either ditched quickly or traded away, the demon walks away and lays low until he can clear the Conditions or glitches that he picked up before getting right back to it. This demon's use of Cover is atypical but it's all about resource management: you build up a Cover that you don't intend to keep to a decent degree, then when it's all on the line you go hog wild with the pyrotechnics.

              I hope that's clear enough, though if you have any questions, feel free to ask and I'll try to help out again!

                                      Originally posted by PencilBoy99 View Post
                                      I've already house ruled Legend. They LOVED the Legend rule, and even tolerated the Compromise roll, but were going to stab me over the Impostor Condition, so I left that out.
                                      There is no compromise roll for Legend. It's a roll of Cover as a dicepool modified by the number of dots you're instantiating.

                                      The Impostor Condition is perfectly sensible in light of Legend being a stopgap measure and there being at least three Embeds and Exploits that can be used to more reliably come up with Skill and Merit dots.

                                      Besides which, if they're complaining about the prospect of losing Cover then they oughtn't be getting mad over getting Cover Beats for permanently improving their own Traits.

                                      Originally posted by PencilBoy99 View Post
                                      I must have missed that. How do you get enhanced attributes from Embeds? Most of them seem to be about doing things (like pulling objects out of your pocket).
                                      Getting better at Embeds is achieved one of two ways: enhancing your Skills, which only covers some Embeds; and enhancing your Finesse Attributes, which covers most Embeds, particularly in the case of Wits and Manipulation.

                                      Compromise rolls are made with precisely those two Finesse Attributes.

                                          Embeds don't risk Cover unless they muck directly with the G-M, are overused in a scene (Knockout Punch on everybody in a building) or there is a dramatic failure on an Embed. Remind your players they should not be afraid to use Embeds. If they are afraid to use Embeds, I can only assume you've been asking for compromise rolls after their use. Don't do that. Have your players read the text box on page 124.

                                          If you are going to ask for a compromise roll, again, under those specific circumstance, let them know, ahead of time.

                                          Exploits are a conscious decision to risk cover for immediate and dramatic benefits, but even then, that compromise roll can be nullified by spending of willpower (pg 158). Even if they don't spend the willpower, as long as they have a normal wits + manipulation (4 or more) and a decentish cover (6 or more) they have 5 dice and a greater than 80% chance of success. Details.

                                          Demonic Form: partial transformation Compromise rolls have a +1 for each form ability not accessed, so if you have at least 7 abilities and only access, eh 3 of them, they still have a +4 bonus. Under the circumstances above they're now rocking a 96% chance of success. Full Demonic form is much riskier, but it's supposed to be, they're making a desperation move.

                                          As Satchel said, they get beats for resolving conditions, and certain conditions can yield multiple beats. Point this out to your players.

                                          Finally, remind them, "you're demons, deal with it. Find a human who has a serious problem (7.046 billion at last count) and say 'I think we can help each other' "

                                          Give them and point out opportunities to improve their cover, at least once per session:
                                          • They over hear a man drinking at a bar, "she's going to leave me, she's going to take the kid's"
                                          • They see a stack of bills from a children's hospital on their boss' desk
                                          • They overhear a child bitterly complaining about not being able to buy a new toy (the Unchained give candy to babies, for a low, low price in four easy installments)
