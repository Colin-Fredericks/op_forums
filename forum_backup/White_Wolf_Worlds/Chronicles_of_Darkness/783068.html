    Long Term Consequences of Feeding

    12-09-2015, 06:01 PM
    So we've all used those rolling to feed scenes in our games, more like asides really, where a character might jump a hobo or seduce some guy at the bar and feed on them in the bathroom, all that jazz.

    We know the personal consequences for taking more than one should: That NPC suffers the Drained Condition, and is at risk of getting hurt/generally not doing well as a result of it. Focusing on this with a single character can be a nice and dramatic way to get across how even non-violent feeding and overindulgence can ruin lives, but are there any other ways?

    Has anyone tried to make a system or mechanic to cover the long-term consequences of stuff like taking enough Vitae to leave a number of people drained? Any thoughts on how you might do that, to make the consequences of feeding rolls possibly more dramatic?

    I'm thinking of the Torches & Pitchforks kind of system from the Hurt Locker preview (located here):

    Originally posted by Hurt Locker
    System option: Torches and Pitchforks

    With this system, human communities have an instinctive awareness of the predators moving among them. They don't know who, or what the predators are, but they feel the damage they inflict as a slowly building tension in the community. As the supernatural predator takes human victims, or manipulates them through strange powers, or seeks to dominate, this pressure rises. Eventually it reaches boiling point and the mob unites, calling for the head of the predator. The only way the monster can avoid this fate is to go quiet long enough for the anger to die down, or move onto another community.

    To show the increasing tensions among the human population, each time a supernatural predator leaves evidence of her existence where humans could find it — such as bodies from her kills — add a die to a Tension pool for the community. Whenever Tension increases, the Storyteller rolls the pool, applying the predator’s Stealth as a penalty. (The Storyteller may choose to increase this penalty for predators with persistent, or easily accessible, supernatural stealth powers.) The Tension pool starts with a number of dice equal to the predator’s highest Social Merit pertaining to that community; a predator deeply entrenched suffers greater scrutiny.

    Such predators can make coverup and public relations efforts to diffuse Tension. Once per chapter, a successful coverup effort can remove one Tension die. The predator can temporarily expend Social Merit dots including Allies, Contacts, Mentor, Retainer, and Status to remove a die of Tension. This requires dots equal to the total Tension pool, and can only happen once per chapter.

    Dramatic Failure: The community finds (and likely murders) a scapegoat for their simmering anger. Reduce Tension by the predator’s Stealth (minimum 0).

    Failure: The community’s anger continues to rise, but the humans find no outlet to target their rage.

    Success: The humans catch sight of the predator, or find some other clue that confirms their suspicions. The predator gains the Hunted Condition.

    Exceptional Success: The humans catch the predator in the act, or find proof of her crimes and location. The predator gains the Hunted Condition, and the size of the force hunting her is doubled.
    Looking at it again, I think Torches and Pitchforks provides a good system, but how do we personalize it for Vampire? Does anyone have alternate suggestions for ways to enhance it, maybe personalize it to the character's method? I want to refine the idea if not the system into something that's perfect for Vampire.

    Some weird thoughts that occur to me:
    • Another place to look for determining dicepools and how to apply multiple effects/issues might actually be 2e Mage's Paradox System? I.e. the idea that each thing you do and the magnitude might add dice, but then certain advantages you have might subtract, with most cautious vampires operating at a chance die or 1-2 at most for the community to detect their wrongdoings.
    • What if instead of jumping right to Hunted, there are like 2 tiers of Conditions related to it. Like you first get a condition representing that you're being scrutinized or that your actions/haunts have come under scrutiny, which then in turn can pretty easily become Hunted if you're not cautious.



    I'd love your thoughts. Vampire does a great job of making it clear that you can't just go around feeding on people willy nilly, and a system to help determine response/give the ST ideas on how to handle things would be really cool.

      I might have more ideas tomorrow, but my first idea right now is adopting the Ambience system from Damnation City for repeatedly feeding in one's territory. The fiction supports this, but there are no strict mechanics given. The point is, the people bleed into the vampire and the vampire bleeds into the people. To underline the parasitic nature, the change of Ambience should be negative (at least for the mortals living there), but a character should be allowed to counteract that.

                      I think you're misunderstanding what I'm asking for. Torches and Pitchforks here isn't needed for the game to function, but it's a tool to assist it in functioning and to help map out the consequences of player actions in a fashion which is tangible and tense. The mechanics don't make something possible that was impossible before, but it adds a system that both the player and the storyteller can latch onto for the enjoyment of both.

                      Think of it in terms of Conditions. Yes, I can arbitrarily decide that at THIS time THIS happens as a consequence of THAT, but what I'd enjoy is a system that engages an element of chance in it. Sometimes leaving a string of people that are Drained wherever you go doesn't lead to anything. Sometimes it does. Systems like this facilitate that "sometimes" and also helps take the conceptual load off of Storytellers by offering suggestions as to the kinds of Conditions that apply, as one example of the consequence that arises.

                      Charlaquin summed up my feelings on it nicely, but to keep to the topic: I'm not asking whether such a thing should exist, I'm asking for suggestions on how to possibly enhance the Torches and Pitchforks system or another way of doing what it covers.

                      As Vent0 observed, Surveilled is another good Condition, if not for its mechanics than for the concept. Maybe another one might be a Condition which imposes penalties on those Hunting rolls, to increase the possibility of Dramatic Failure and to showcase that if you start leaving a lot of people pale and sickly, there might be that low level, instinctual reaction to the Kindred predator among them.

                      Damnation City's systems of Ambience and some of the fiction do suggest and model how Kindred can effect the mood of an area through long-term predation, and a Condition like this might help get that feeling across and communicate to players that risky feeding can have dramatically cool consequences AND that Kindred do generally try to not take so much Vitae from people that they're like that all the time. It's dangerous.

                      EDIT: So thinking about it some more, I think what I'm imagining is a version of the Torches and Pitchforks system with more options for Conditions that are specifically suited to Vampires, so it's actually a really easy one.

                      So that means the first object of the game is to come up with those Conditions. The only other thing I'm thinking about is when discovering circumstances where Tension dice are added, should there be an opportunity for player characters to try to mitigate it? How do you think that should be handled?
