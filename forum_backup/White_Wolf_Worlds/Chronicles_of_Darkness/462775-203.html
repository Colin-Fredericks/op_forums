        In 1e, both non-success results for Stepping Sideways meant you stayed on the same side of the Gauntlet as you started; failure locked you out from trying again yourself for an hour, while dramatic failure prevented you from trying again until the following night, although in both cases a third party or an alternative method could bring you across.

        Translating that to Reaching may not be completely one-to-one — there's no Pathfinding mechanic described in 2e, for example — but you can probably transplant the dramatic failure result from Stepping Sideways and waive the cooldown on failure, since Reaching has a lower potential dicepool.

                As with questions about interpreting the tribal bans, assume some degree of flexibility tempered by the need for the character to genuinely believe whatever interpretation is being used to determine the mechanical effect.

                Killing a human or wolf is a breaking point toward Spirit, but violating the Oath of the Moon is a breaking point toward Flesh, which, like eating human flesh for Essence, means werewolves who've sworn the Oath risk destabilizing in either direction due to the need to roll twice and Harmony breaking points being penalized by how far from the middle you are if they think of killing another werewolf as as bad or worse than killing a human; if a werewolf doesn't draw that equivalence, then the spiritual consequence is "just" a higher than usual risk of falling out of alignment with your spiritual half due to breaking an oath with supernatural backing.

                Consistency and attention to the character's worldview is the primary point of focus, essentially.

                        Originally posted by Bwhite24 View Post
                        Does anyone have tips on what makes a good elodoth? Mine is a stormlord in San Francisco who has his own charter fishing boat for a living. I think I’ve got his personality and stuff down but I just wanna make sure I’m playing this auspice to the best of my ability.
                        Awareness of proportionality and boundaries, to be brief. The auspice Renowned for its Honor is called upon to serve as judges because they're expected to be fair.

                        "Fair" doesn't mean dumb or soft, of course — taking a handicap in a duel is different from throwing the match, for instance, and using appropriate force to take down prey of a given magnitude doesn't mean letting bad actors act out until they're big enough problems to warrant a full-scale chase — but just as an Ithaeur should reasonably be expected to know what sort of fetish vessel is appropriate for a spirit to inhabit, any Elodoth worth their salt should at least have a functional understanding of the appropriate response to a situation within a complex system.

                        Rahu lean on the core principles of Oath of the Moon above all others, Irraka do what it takes to get results even if it's "cheating," but the Elodoth's focus is on staying impartial enough for things to work out. Sometimes that means giving up territory your pack is ill-suited to take care of. Sometimes it means testifying against your packmate in a formal inquiry. Werewolves with an affinity for Honor are coolheaded and in control enough that they have a proven (and testable) track record of doing The Right Thing for the contexts they find themselves in when it matters. The Ithaeur deal with spirits through mystical insight, but the Elodoth simply understand political and social dynamics; the Cahalith are often called the heart of the pack, but the Elodoth are its level heads, whether that translates to a leadership role or not.

                        Half Moons are the folks you expect to have considered all the angles and gotten the big picture view of their circumstances (and those of their pack). Part and parcel with their ability to manipulate Rage is a certain amount of expectation that they be unlikely to fall into Kuruth themselves, and statistically that trends that archetype especially toward maintaining balanced Harmony.
