        Originally posted by KaiserAfini View Post
        I would like to propose a system based on 8 ratings. First lets start with the simple ones, based on the 3 standard attribute types:

        Physical
        Mental
        Social

        These cover most basic challenges. Next we will look at ones that are more subjective, but still play an important role:

        Volatility: How likely it is to experience a fluctuation in that splat's power level. A vampire chooses to lower Blood Potency, so they are low volatility. A mage chooses to over Reach for all its worth, which is more common than a vampire. But its still sporadic, so mid volatility. A mummy doesn't choose to Descend, so high volatility.

        Variancy: How big the difference is between the splat wielding its full power is from their standard. A mage with time to prepare has high variancy, since they can have imbued and enhanced items, tass, bonuses, etc. A demon going loud or a recently risen mummy embody high variancy, so does a deviant at full power. A werewolf giving into Death Rage has medium variancy, since they can wield the garou form's power for a bit and with more precision.

        Combat: How quickly the splat can neutralize their opponents (immobilize, kill, knock out, turn in their favor, etc). The value is simply how well they could be expected to emerge when caught in single combat while fully healed, with their personal resource pool full (Aether, mana, Essence, etc) and in neutral ground.

        Information Gathering: How easily can the splat figure out what is going on. All the raw might and careful use of their power won't help much if they don't understand what is going on.

        Requisitioning: How easily the splat can be expected to secure or procure the resources and contacts they require. A vampire has the blood bond, Disciplines to support this and any favors they can procure, so very high requisitioning power. Mages have their Order archives, which leads to a medium. Werewolves need to barter or hunt for each fetish, which leads to a low.

        Lastly, each of those is rated on a 5 dot basis, with the general meaning outlined as follows:

        • (Frail): The splat has notable difficulty with this aspect, probably having penalties associated with it. Promethean's social rating are a great example, since their Disquiet makes interactions more difficult.

        •• (Average): No great focus, but no great penalties. A splat at this level is about as competent as a mortal with equivalent training. They can be more competent than a mortal, but its not their specialty. Mages fall in this category for physical, since their bodies are still quite mortal, even when they have a few healing tricks.

        ••• (Gifted): The nature of their supernatural condition supports this pillar in a minor way. Geists are at this level due to their natural ability to perceive and understand ghosts, which is specialized, but gives them more options than most mortals.

        •••• (Skilled): This splat's default supernatural powers support this pillar in a major way. Demons with their perfect ability to lie are amazing at social, vampires due to blood bond. Vampires, werewolves and prometheans are amazing at physical. Mages are masters of investigation and changelings are great at mental due to oneiromancy and pledges.

        ••••• (Superb): The splat not only has default supernatural powers that greatly aid in this pillar, but the available suite of powers lets them specialize in them. With an optimized build, they can face with the highest echleons in the setting (short of transcendently powerful beings like Kerberoi, archmages, Rank 6+ spirits, etc) and expect to either win or put up a strong challenge.

        While there are a lot more factors at play, hopefully this gives GMs a baseline that is good enough for planning challenges for players.
        I like this system quite a bit!

        One factor I think is worth considering if we're rating the splats "in general" rather than in a hyper-optimised situation is to consider how easy it is for them to fill/refill their resource tank. Geists might be top tier in this regard since they can refill their Plasm as needed simply by unlocking keys. Vampires are maybe at the 4-dot level since acquiring blood is always risky but it's also very plentiful, and it's not hard for a skilled vampire to feed from multiple mortals in the space of a few hours. Demons although they're one of the stronger splats, have trouble safely keeping their Aether topped up since the only way they have of collecting Aether that doesn't involve plugging into the God-Machine is to assume their demonic form, and that risks blowing their cover, so I would probably rate them at one dot.

                Thank you, glad you liked it. Regarding resupply, how about something like this:

                Scavenging: This represents the average ease that the splat has to procure the resource that fuels their supernatural abilities quickly and without relying on support infrastructure. Its rating is inversely proportional to the rarity, time commitment and average risk of refilling their supplies. Vampires have high levels, since blood is easily available, yet obtaining it at high Blood Potency requires some extra care. Mages have a medium level, since mana is neither terribly rare or common. Werewolves have a low rating, since hunting spirits is neither quick or safe. Changelings have very high levels, since Glamour is plentiful.

                The last factor is growth ratio. But this can be simulated by simply remaking the evaluation for new, initiate, experienced and master levels of the splat. Then sum up all the ratios to get a threat level.

                Based on the aggregated value, you can speculate the difficulty of an encounter. The total indicates the danger of the group, the individual values indicate how difficult it will be to neutralize that target and probably priorities (who is the best orator, most prepared spy, most dangerous ghost, etc).

                There will be outliers like AoE capabilities, puzzle encounters and unusual items/abilities. But I believe this system can help provide a decent foundation.

                                    Originally posted by Lashet View Post
                                    OK, people
                                    A personal request.
                                    In the context of this thread- say something nice about Uratha potential?
                                    Werewolves are very, very strong at character creation. The ability to heal all bashing and lethal every turn, combined with at least 10 health (and more often 11-14 health) in Gauru makes them very, very difficult to kill. Some other splats can do aggravated damage at character creation, but you can quite easily create a Werewolf who absorbs up to 4 agg per turn, and has a fairly high defence, which makes it difficult for even these splats to take them down.

                                    The weakness Werewolves have is that they don't have very many abilities to incapacitate opponents, or to nullify their abilities, while other top tier splats like Mages and Demons have access to many of these kinds of abilities. Werewolves generally just become better hunters and fighters as they gain exp - which in fairness is what they are supposed to be. Having said all that, Werewolf gifts are often very cost effective (compared to say Vampiric disciplines) in terms of exp. They also have great combat focused merits - things like Embodiment Of The Firstborn, Favoured Form, Fortified Form, Living Weapon, Instinctive Defence, and Relentless Assault all enhance a Werewolf's combat abilities in ways which will be useful in nearly every fight, whereas the combat merits available to other splats tend to be much more situational.

                                    There are also a few ways in which Werewolves can become extraordinarily powerful at high exp levels, but they require much more storyteller cooperation. For example, a large pack (say with more than 5 Werewolves and as many wolf-blooded and humans) could potentially have a totem spirit which is rank 4 or even rank 5 - which would pose a serious threat to even high Gnosis Mages. Investing a lot in the totem also enables the pack to go one dot above their normal limits for select skills and attributes (as described on page 92) which you could stack with merits and the form bonuses to get Werewolves with some base stats close to 20 in Gauru, Urshul, or Urhan. A cooperative Storyteller, or a pack with access to the Fetish rite, could create some incredibly powerful Fetishes to supplement their power. The book only has a short list of example fetishes, but the guidelines for creating 5-dot fetishes include suggestions like "[grants] 8-again and the rote quality on particular dice pools" and "allows the Uratha to outright break some rules such as using their Defense against mental attacks or to count themselves as the bane of something that doesn’t usually suffer a bane." This last point in particular would get around the difficulty Werewolves usually have dealing agg to other splats. Fetishes are not something you can buy with exp, but it's not unreasonable to assume that a Pack with high Primal Urge is likely to have access to such items, which could help rebalance the scales against more powerful splats - imagine if Mages had to subtract the Werewolf's defence (which can easily be 10 or more at high exp) from all their spellcasting pools that target the Werewolf.

                                    Of course, the real in-world reason why Werewolves are always threatening is that they are a pack. Werewolves one-on-one can often be beaten, but they have lots of Gifts, merits, and Rites that affect and strengthen the whole pack. What's more, their hunting abilities are unparalleled. When they come for you there's a very good chance that they will get the drop on you, and have set up the environment and conditions to their advantage. By contrast, it's tough to get the drop of them, especially in their territory. Someone like a Mage might be able to just portal in and surprise them, but there's every chance that the pack's Cahalith foresaw the attack, and they've taken steps to prepare.

                                            Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
                                            In terms of practical play, basically no one is more genuinely dangerous than the Uratha. Outside of white room scenarios where every opponent has a theoretical means to overcome them, Werewolves are horrifically dangerous, the splat is so loaded for hunting that even without Gifts or Merits they the barometer of combat for every whiteroom scenario.

                                            In other words, if CofD WAS a dungeon crawl, Uratha would be the splat people would complain is OP.
                                            Also, to draw a different comparison, because I've had Blades in the Dark on the brain for a good long bit now: Werewolves rule at the turf game.

                                            In addition to pack structure giving the average player werewolf more people to call on for assistance, the default assumptions of having territory (to say nothing of the rites and Facets that play off of it) and the fact that their near-native otherworld hews so closely to Earthly geography most of the time gives the Uratha a focus on cultivating the home-field advantage in a wide area that only Beast gets near to. (Vampire and Mummy have major mechanics built around it, as do most other folks with Safe Place modifiers/analogues, but there's a world of difference in scope between "don't fight the monster in its own house" and "this monster owns the next six blocks in two separate dimensions.")

                                            Also, it's funny (because nomenclature) how the main other splats to angle toward teamwork and role-distribution to accomplish a very focused goal are Changeling and Hunter, both of whom fall far behind in terms of general institutional reach by dint of more disjointed routing — there's an assumption of being the underdogs with the other two that werewolves often need to be fighting gods to fall into, because their playing field spans wider and has a more consistent web of contacts to lean on.

                                            Demons and Deviants can both theoretically give the wolves a run for their money in the general "doing a heist" department, but the average werewolf is not a fugitive riven with justifiable paranoia about the actions of a nigh-omnipresent web of conspiracy to which they are of interest, which (as above) means their social safety net is a fair bit stronger even outside the confines of the pack.

                                            I guess what I'm getting at here is that werewolves also fill the dungeon-crawl-as-heist mold pretty damn well by virtue of being a speciated gang of criminal-adjacent folks with Connections thanks to natural role-division and involvement with the setting's most Earth-adjacent otherworld, and that's pretty neat.

                                                Werewolves fight with guerrilla tactics. There is very little you can do if they want to find you, they have tons of methods. So long as you are on land, their forms let them adapt well to the chase.

                                                They are relentless, healing quickly. But good luck being so yourself. Some of their Gifts can rob you of a peaceful sleep. Some even send spirits to constantly harry the prey.

                                                They are an elite strike team, with abilities to support each other and synergize their attack. They are efficient killers, being able to have an efficient use of Essence and recharging it with their bite. They are stalking predators, waiting for the right moment to pounce.

                                                Above all, they are pack hunters. When they descend like a bolt of lightning, amids a storm of blood and claws, even the most veteran supernaturals can feel the pack breathing inches from their neck.

                                                    Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                                    ...In addition to pack structure giving the average player werewolf more people to call on for assistance, the default assumptions of having territory (to say nothing of the rites and Facets that play off of it) and the fact that their near-native otherworld hews so closely to Earthly geography most of the time gives the Uratha a focus on cultivating the home-field advantage in a wide area that only Beast gets near to. (Vampire and Mummy have major mechanics built around it, as do most other folks with Safe Place modifiers/analogues, but there's a world of difference in scope between "don't fight the monster in its own house" and "this monster owns the next six blocks in two separate dimensions.")...
                                                    Indeed, in terms of group dynamics few splats have as much support or as many options as Werewolves. Sin-Eaters are probably the closest in terms of scope, with access to the Underworld, and broad network of ghosts and mediums, and the Crew system (which almost certain took some inspiration from Pack mechanics). Changelings too, though their group dynamics are more about communal security and preparedness. A differences that can be tied back to themes, as Changelings are the prey of their otherworldly enemies, while Werewolves are the hunters (in broad strokes).

                                                    Hunters have group dynamics as well, but their in-setting position as underdog limits them from having the deep territorial enforcement mechanics key to the Uratha. Same can be said for Prometheans, Demons and Deviants, for whom being ‘on the run’ is an essential theme. Meanwhile Beasts are intended to glom onto other splats coteries, but don’t have much in the way of interaction with their societal mechanics (though Lair mechanics give them the most potent ‘home world’ mechanic short of Archmages).

                                                    Vampires, Mages & Mummies are, weirdly, too big. With world spanning societies and mechanics that allow them to flex their influence at a regional level. However, in terms of actual play this scope is generally a backdrop to their individual stories which tend towards the local. Though all three can have fantastic ‘home bases’ if they put their minds to it, but it’s not the assumption that all Mages have an semi-clairvoyant extra-dimensional sanctum guarded by custom built spirits.
