                            Originally posted by Vent0 View Post

                            Okay... I think examples would help illustrate. Previously, Catalysts represented different takes on a Mad Scientist or their motivations - Ambition, Curiosity, Revenge, Ego, Regret. What spread do you see?
                            • Grimms: Grimms want things, and feel that their ability to do something is the same as a reason to do it. They break their opponents down, they demand things of others, and they justify it with their superior intellect, because in their minds, intellectual might makes right. At best, they are pushy, at worst, they can become violent and abusive.
                            • Hoffnung: Hoffnungs are the classic "egotistical intellectual" type. They consider their those less intellectually-inclined to be inferior, and judge people by their degrees, rather than their character. They feel their intellect makes them better, and act in that framework. At best, they're patronizing. At worst, they're tyrannical.
                            • Klagen: Klagens see their gifts as a curse, and tend to construct situations in which their intellect makes them a victim, while they're usually doing things to bring suffering on themselves, and then use that victimization as justification to disengage from others. At best, they are depressive and lathargic. At worst, they use the their suffering as justification to make others suffer.
                            • Neid: Neids feel a sense of inadequacy that they project onto others. They feel like they aren't good enough, and then turn that out against others, coalescing into a paranoia that everyone else thinks that they're as inadequate as they feel themselves to be. At best, they are sensitive and volatile. At worst, they are paranoid and willing to crush others to protect their egos.
                            • Staunen: Staunens want to know, damn the consequences. They aren't malicious, but they are more concerned with advancing their knowledge, and less with getting it safely and morally. At best, they are irresponsible, at worst, they are utterly amoral.

                                                        Bardos

                                                        So, one difference/clarification between 1E and 2E being proposed is that many1 of the Bardos that were "setting based upon previously held, but then disproven ideas" were Astral(Temenos) Realms. Occupying to collective consciousness, they were suddenly (and not so suddenly) undone by their own natures being found impossible. But some didn't let their end, well, end them. They started scrambling for resources and traction to sustain themselves, and found ready help among the Inspired. With imported material and Mania/Impetus to support them, they became something more. Now, similar to the Pangaeans of old, they2 (both the realm and its natives) are part material, part ephemeral, sustained (but often perilously so) by their new power sources.

                                                        But what does this mean?

                                                        Bardos are still elsewheres defined by what sets them apart. They have specific manners and places that they can be accessed (much like any other otherworld in the CofD). Their denizens could range from I-can't-believe-they're-natural! flora and fauna, to material supernatural beings (use Horrors and Brief Nightmares, maybe Claimed, with the odd Integral Wonder in place of Dread Powers), to still living-thought-shapes like3 Goetia. They are fine in their native environs, but Bad Thingstm happen when they leave for any length of time, subject to their degree of supernatural nature.

                                                        1: Some Bardos weren't (and aren't) clearly created from ideas humanity previously held (like wherever the Phantom Slaver Yetis came from). Those obviously aren't former Temenos Realms. At least, Human ones. From this timelines. Add additional qualifiers, etc., etc. They still require specific materials, actions, times, or places to get to, so many Genii just group them together and call them all Bardos.
                                                        2: Some Bardos and their residents still haven't integrated with this whole "material existence" thing, either because they are new, or it just doesn't mesh with their nature. Those tend to be treated much like other Astral Realms, albeit cut off from the greater portion (can't reach other parts of the Temenos, the Anima Mundi, etc.) and their residents much like Goetia, though some exceptions3 apply.
                                                        3: Ephemeral-like residents of Bardos (sometimes called Manes) possess many of the traits of Goetia, but break some of the rules - they can have Manifestations naturally, some need Mania/Impetus as opposed to Essence, and some experience Havoc-like events instead of Bans or Banes. They are mostly similar, but assumptions are dangerous.
