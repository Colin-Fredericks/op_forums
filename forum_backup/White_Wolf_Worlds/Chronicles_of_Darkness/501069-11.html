            Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
            And this was mention on 1st page of topic - those scenarios almost ALWAYS result in the Clarity loss, as we are playing RPG game here.
            And this remains a poor assumption. The 2e breaking points systems so far have made if likely that PCs will pass breaking point checks more often than not. We don't know the specifics of Clarity yet, but if it works like Integrity, have 5-6 dice means that most of the time you don't fail the roll, so you don't lose Clarity.

            The idea that these scenarios will almost always put Clarity at risk is a very different point that these scenarios will always result in Clarity loss.

            This distinction is especially important with the Seemings also providing ways to guaranteed Clarity gains. If you are getting your automatic gain as frequently as the dice are failing on you and resulting in loses, you're netting even; not a net loss.

            Every time something goes wrong, or some bystander dies.
            There is a fairly massive gap between "something goes wrong," and "someone is unintentionally caused significant harm;" the second being the actual Curse and such. Plans simply not going 100% as intended isn't an issue. Plans not going as intended and bystanders needing to be dealt with isn't inherently an issue either. If you have a way to incapacitate an unexpected bystander without causing them serious harm, then no risk of Clarity loss.

            Even just because ST need a twist in the plot when the game session is in still.
            Poor STing doesn't mean the rule in question is bad. If things like drug trips can still risk Clarity, and the ST constantly smacks the changelings with drugs via plot until they're all Clarity 0, that's not an issue with Clarity. That's just bad STing. It's no different from sending wave after wave of combat enemies until the PCs are all dead. Sure you can do it in the rules, but that's not the problem. If the ST is constantly pulling "Gotcha! You didn't plan for X so roll for a Clarity break!" then the problem is the ST. Because they can do this without Seeming specific ones.

                        Odds are I won't be getting into a mass combat game of changeling but I can see the problems for those who might.

                        As a player in a motley, what constitutes leadership? We don't have a 'group leader,' but if one member asks, 'What should we do?' does the Fairest make themselves eligible by offering their opinion the way every other player might? And if the fairest's idea is followed, would players be more reluctant to take a Dramatic Failure, knowing it might cripple a member of the motley? Or would players simply not ask the fairest's opinion, an option I could see taken in more gamist groups?
