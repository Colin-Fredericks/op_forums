                            So far, we've been talking about who and to a lesser extent how. But why is a question, too. We know the Shan'iatu wanted an empire, and going by Menat they wanted to become gods, and sacrificed the empire to that end. (Her account is slightly ambiguous, though, on whether they founded their empire for the purpose of sacrificing it, or decided it was expendable later on). So how would these motives line up with different games?

                            Sineaters: Several of the archetypes are associated with judgment (especially reapers), preparing people for death (especially pilgrims), and maintaining order between life and death (especially gatekeepers). If a krewe's philosophy were formed around a fusion of these three, then could Irem have been an especially grandiose exercise in carrying them out? Put the people into rigid niches that lead to predictable (and thus usable) ghostly echoes? What would they sacrifice it for? Unless they felt their ends coming, and weren't confident about any possible successors - sineaters are durable, but not infinitely so - and wanted their creation to outlast them. So they made it a death realm, and created mummies to carry on their work in the living world. That would suggest that it's the exercise of judgement on behalf of the judges and glorifying Irem that's the more important purpose than sending relics to Duat.

                            With the idea of ghosts and revenants from a former civilisation, their motive would presumably be about recreating as much of what they'd lost as possible - creating Irem and the empire as a giant, mutual anchor for themselves, maybe. Until they conceived of becoming deathlords and/or kerberoi and turned it into a dead dominion instead - perhaps creating the mummies as replacement anchors. Also, either sineaters or geists could be from the aforementioned civilisation, combining this with the previous paragraph.

                            Mages: I can't think of a reason beyond what I've already suggested.

                            Changelings: If the Fae were behind the fall of the empire, it would imply a massive kidnapping - perhaps by a Keeper who wanted to play at being a god-king. But nothing like that's happened before or since that we know of, implying that it would normally be beyond the Fae to accomplish it. So either the empire was owed to them in some way - did they provide power to the Shan'iatu, or did that thing the Shan'iatu found belong to the Fae in some way? Or the Fae provided something to pay for it - and the mummies might be that. Given that time goes weirdly in Arcadia, it opens up the possibility of an Iremite changeling.

                            Mortals: Lines up by itself. Long, grand projects are frequently the province of mortals in the WoD after all, and they're the ones with the most motive to elevate themselves. I don't really see what would motivate hunters as Hunter depicts them, though.

                            LostLight came up with a good motives for vampires for the gamelines he covered.

                            Though regarding Prometheans, the empire could have been a vast Pilgrimmage: despairing of achieving humanity through "normal" routes, they tried to remake humanity in a mould they could then fill.

                            Finally, what about Claimed? Perhaps there had been thousands of people who'd had small dreams of the things Irem came to embody - giving rise to small, weak spirits of those things. Who then banded together to create a concrete example of their concepts to increase their own power and meaning.

                                                    Well, we'll see eventually. I'm not expecting too much, however, going forwards. I feel like we've reached a saturation point in the World of Darkness, and much of what we get from here on out is going to be covering a lot of material other games do. Like I said, Mummy shares a lot in common with Mage. Promethean with Demon. The Inferno and Vampire. More and more, I feel like there's so much overlap between games, and less and less new space being explored; just new twists on the old stuff. Sure, you can create a new realm of animistic spirits doing things, and it'll be different from the Hisil so as to have its own realm, but does it need to be?


                                                    I look at it like this - changelings and mages both access the same dream world. However, they do it in such mind boggling, vastly different ways that they might as well be different. I think that all these realms could be interconnected rather than individual playgrounds; however, each supernatural gets a different view of the pie. Qashmallim and angels could be the same beings, just seen from different angles. An infernal whisper could be a variant strix (or the other way around) that infects spirits instead of physical bodies. And so on.

                                                    That's why I like thinking of the Judges as uber-necromancers of fantasy with their undead armies. They see and access the astral / supernal / abyssal space differently than the Awakened, and learned to steal power from living beings from contact with it.

                                                        I don't think there is any problem with Duat being classified as a "Lower Depth" by mages. The "Lower Depths" is simply a term to define any realm which is "metaphysically distant" from the Supernal Realms, and as such it is harder for mages to effect it. Dave once said that the reason mages give such shiny and big names to things it is in order to claim that they understand them- even if they don't. In short, the Lower Depths is simply a term for realms that mages don't know much about them, and don't really have power upon them- and Duat seems to answer that definition.

                                                            You know, there is actually another game I would do the whole origins story thing with, again. The God-Machine / Demon /Promethean. Mummies are Created beings, just like Prometheans and Angels. All the games have a strong vibe that deals with godly beings, evolution of the self, and breaking free of the chains of slavery of your master.


                                                            I guess, what it comes down to for me, is that there are three things going for mummy:
                                                            1) Name magic, magical empires, magic cults.
                                                            2) Created servants
                                                            3) Undead army and emphasis on the past
