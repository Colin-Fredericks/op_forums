    Hunter: Cults, development revival thread

    07-04-2014, 02:08 PM
    So for a while I have found myself without a project, and quite a few people have expressed interest in Hunter: Cults. So lets give a try,


    For those of you who don't already know, the goals of Cults are as follows:
    • To present a dark mirror of Compacts and Conspiracies, just as Slasher presetned a dark mirror of individual Hunters.
    • To explore the way heroic Hunter Compacts and Conspiracies slide down the slope towards cultdom - with a theme of isolation.
    • To present Compacts and Conspiracies as actual organisations.

    Along with the following lesser goals:
    • To explore the vigil's isolating effects. A Compact where everyone maintains strong ties to mundane life is pretty safe. A Compact where members only really talk to each-other is in the danger zone.
    • To embrace the mostly overlooked theme of faith and religion in Hunter. There's a lot of Compacts and Conspiracies that are explicitly religious and a few more that are spiritual. But after all this time, there's not much for storytellers who want to have a Christian angel visit their Long Night players or the Biblical Lucifer contact his child in the Lcuifuge.



    So if you're interested catch up on the wiki and start posting ideas. Just to get started, I feel that the third goal - To present Compacts and Conspiracies as actual organisations - has been neglected and have just started thinking about creating Endowments that play to the ideas of large organisation. Either an Endowment that's explicitly used to organisation or an Endowment that basically requires 15 or more Hunters in the current scene. Though due to a coincidence, the Ascending Ones already have one (they need an Endowment that's slightly cult like instead)

          From what I read, an interesting issue to consider is whether the supernatural, ironically, would cause people to question their faith.

          To explain, Vampires, for example, are clearly unaffected by traditional holy objects, and aren't consistently either. Additionally, for all the monsters running around... where are the Angels? From the Hunter's POV, they are nowhere to be seen. This, and other examples of how the monsters essentially refuse to respond to what is supposed to hurt them under religious gospel, traditionally from what I know anyway, I'd think would raise questions.

          Now, the Long Night does have an interesting response to this question, but how could this impact others view of their faith? After all, shouldn't say, the Abrahamic God, from a religious perspective, intervene most when there are supernatural entities running around? And for those who do find out about the God Machine... well, again, I doubt in most cases it'd match their perception of what they think the Abrahamic God should be, for example.


          I did actually see Cults have an interesting response to this, with the whole cult that summons demons to protect its followers, however I'd think it could use more exploration, if religion is a major theme.

A religious hunter is certainly going to be shaken when they first see evidence that challanges their beleif, but they're hunters. When challanged they'll fight back. Some will discover doctrines similar to the Long Night, some will turn their backs on faith, and some will look for more protective gods. Sometimes they'll find someone like the Athenic Church, other times it's more like the Sheltering Spire.

                                                  Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post

                                                  I am always interested in nihilistic Buddhist mastermind psychics.
                                                  Okay, here's the into writeup. Keep in mind I also intended them to be the co-conspirators in chronicles where the slarecian race (psychic projections of godlike alien intellects that were trapped in the world as it formed, and absolutely hate it) are the main antagonists, so that's what I'm referring to when I say slarecian, uthriarch (the godlike alien intellects in question), or god-king, and they're based on Second Sight rules. The language virus is a nasty trick taught by their masters, it overwrites the normal lingual centers of the brain with the incomprehensible-to-humans slarecian language and makes the vectors only able to understand slarecian likewise. It only lasts for a month or so, but by that point the infected are institutionalized.

                                                  Cult: The Order of Divine Shade

                                                  Religions that deny the flesh and irrational to exalt the spirit and rational are nothing new. Buddhism, one of the world's largest religions, is based on that idea at its core, and all the other major world religions, Islam, Christianity, Judaism, and Hinduism have elements of it. All agree that the flesh is a creature of weakness and vice, and an impediment to the virtuous spirit and soul.

                                                  Most devout adherents of said religions would find the idea of destroying that flesh to protect the spirit both horrifying and ridiculous-besides the whole "survival instinct" thing, you can't improve the spirit if you're dead, and suicide does nothing but hurt the world around you. Nonetheless, most is not all, and there are those who somehow think removing themselves from the world is helping it.

                                                  Somewhere beyond that would be the slarecian cult priest, who thinks removing everything is somehow helping it. Cults are not known for their abilities in the "logic" department.

                                                  True it's only the truly high-ranking members, the direct lieutenants of any reborn slarecians and the hands of the uthriachs that truly buy into the whole "destroy the universe to save it" line, and that's partially the influence of the God-Kings speaking. But still, they wouldn't have been as receptive to the Formless Ones' nihilistic view of things if they hadn't already hated the world-the one commonality between all slarecian cults.

                                                  Of course, one doesn't come to despise the world without the world doing something to you first, and most servants of the mind tyrants have had bitter experience with life. Most slarecian cultists have suffered greatly in the past, and most of those for one particular reason that draws them to the attention of the uthriach's vast intellects-psionics. By accident of birth, two-edged supernatural gift, or exposure to a mutant strain of the slarecian language virus, there is no shortage of humans in the World of Darkness with some degree of psychic ability. Most of this is small enough to be unnoticed, and deliberately so in order to make the world seem far, far saner than it is, but there is always going to be the outlier (especially in the last case, where temporary aphasia is the cause). Humans being humans, their first reaction is to shun what scares them and force the psychic in question to "tell the truth" about their abilities being clever hoaxes, or just force them into exile among the crowds, unable to connect to the people who run away.

                                                  Enter the cultists.

                                                  The first step is usually to befriend the alienated psychic in question. This is usually the easiest step, as it is a rare one who has been burned that badly by a false friend in any world, even one of Darkness, and the psychic is usually desperate for a sympathetic ear. From there, it's a little more work to convince the psychic that the problem lies with the rest of the world and the nature of flesh itself, and not them, but that's a pretty low bar to clear. Nor is convincing them to take a special concoction, brewed from the blood of slarecian shadowmen that puts them in brief but direct communion with the minds of the Formless Ones.

                                                  The hard part is convincing them that, once they have had the dream of the uthriachs, that the instinctual fear they have of such callous beings is simply that their minds are too attached to their bodies, and that was the mere uncanny valley effect applied to individuals who obviously no longer need their own bodies-something alien to the naive human experience. Often, it doesn't work, at which point the failed recruit is either murdered or hit with the Betrayal of Memory rite, whatever causes less of a mess. But sometimes, especially with those who have become disgusted with the human condition altogether, it does, and they see the raw power that could be theirs-the raw power to take what the world owes them.

                                                  The truly nihilistic teachings come later, but by that point, the cultist is usually so immersed in mystery cult brainwashing and community that they simply nod their head along to the idea that the world must be returned to a place of pure thought and chaos, devoid of the pain of flesh.

                                                  And thanks to the ability of the God-Kings to telepathically network cultists through linking dreams, they are usually quite competently organized in carrying out that idea. And by far the largest and most organized is the hidden pseudo-Gnostic fraternity known as the Cult of Divine Shade.

                                                  The Shades are an old organization. How old is obscured by a thick layer of obfuscation and false leads, not all of them intentional. From what little can be gleaned, the Order came into being around the same time that the Gnostic Christians were deemed heretics and hunted down to the last, in the 4th century AD. Said gleaning also implies they were largely responsible for the declaration of heresy by the early Catholics-and deliberately so, the Gnostics having seen their formation for the ill omen it was, although they did not realize how truly insane these "abusers of enlightenment" were.

                                                  This implied manipulation speaks to the modus operandi of the Shades-while the Catholics were already seeking to consolidate their power, and the Order quietly pressured them into consolidating said power at the expense of an enemy. Thus, their basic tactic-manipulate, pressure, and puppet, all to trick their enemies into destroying themselves, and giving ample chaos as a cover to work under.

                                                  And with the power to manipulate minds, the ability of the Shades to do that is great indeed.

                                                  While not all members of the Order are psychics, enough are to the point where the Shades usually have more than enough mental trickery to accomplish whatever false flag scheme they plan and infiltration. And they are quite good at both, given how their desires for whatever institution they quietly commandeer-either they want a good vantage point to seek out recruits or they want the best place to turn others against their enemies from. Thus, they tend to take over things like psychology departments, insane asylums, or neighborhood watch organizations, usually by finding someone with a loud voice or high-up and then figuring out how to best mentally influence them into courses of actions suitable to their agenda. If they have their way, even their agents won't know that they're working for the Shades.

                                                  Beyond those two motivations, the basic goals of the cult are twofold-refine and hone their psychic strength, and resurrect the "Sophic Children"-the slarecians. The first is simple enough to understand, but is actually a little more altruistic than it seems-most of the Shades suffered greatly for their powers, and indeed that's why they came to the attention of recruiters. Their quest to understand the source and nature of their powers is at least partially so they can teach others to control theirs and avoid the pain they went through (as members of the Order, but still..). The second is both more pragmatic and more involved: The Shades take it as an item of reality that the assistance of a Prince of Mind, with their divine psionics and instinctive mastery over social matters make them ideal leaders, as well as galvanizing overall to have the patronage of a demigod behind a particular chapter. It's also hard as hell-besides requiring a specific, level-five version of the Compel the God rite (and there's only a few people even capable of that level of power, let alone have that one rite), and several powerful psychics (one focusing on a different discipline each), it also requires slarecian relics from deep under the earth, the correct calculations for the ritual drawing, and for it to be cast in during "void of reason"-which is to say, complete anarchy where there is no form of hierarchy at all in the immediate area, except for the Order, which is harder than it sounds; The rite takes a long while to prepare and cast, and anarchy is quickly undone as legitimate authorities reestablish their power or criminals fill the void. But that's breaking a hole, however small, in a prison for alien gods long enough for them to pod off a smaller version of themselves for you, and the benefits far outweigh any losses.

                                                      I'm going to be honest here; I think there's some good ideas - evil Buddhist psychics, bodyless god-psychic minds, but you're putting all the focus in the wrong place and you're diluting the concept with too much generic-evil-cult.


                                                      First of all, you need some way to tie the core thematic elements together. You need some aspect of Buddhist philosophy that can tie into the nihilism. You should base their methods of indoctrination on how Buddhist monks are actually trained - read this (start about half way down) for example. Again, the recruits should have a Buddhist themed reason for joining up, being shunned for having powers is a very western troupe, I don't know if it's shared with eastern media but it still feels western. The Slarecians also should be made more Buddhist; ideally you should discover if there's any equivalent in Buddhist mythology to selling your soul to the evil. Try and find what motivated those mythological Buddhists to do evil and work that into the Shade-Slarecians relationship. Also the prefix -ian is pretty western.

                                                      Also, there's no need to mention Catholicism or have them be active west of Tibet; at least not before modern transport. They should stand on their own.


                                                      In general though your priorities should be in order:

                                                      1) Reinforcing a strong central theme: Nihilistic Buddhist mastermind psychics.
                                                      2) Stuff that's helpful for a storyteller or player trying to portray an individual charachter.
                                                      3) Stuff that's useful for a storyteller trying to portray the organisation as a whole.


                                                      I would suggest trying to make the write up short but sweet. At least for myself, I find it's easier to write a good short - corebook sized - Hunter organisation than a longer one like the Canites or Knights of St George. It's harder to keep a longer piece of text focused.
