                    Basically, if you use a garrote as a garrote, it should be a strangulation-type move in a grapple, causing strangulation. In that case the material doesn't change the damage type because you aren't cutting with it. Using a cord to strangle someone would be the same type of move but with a penalty for being improvised.

                    If you do use a garrote as a cutting tool, it would be a different type of damage move with different effects. Thus, it needs to have some way of determining how it deals damage in two different ways. Given that the strength required to cut human skin and other tissues is quite high, the other way of dealing damage should somehow be gated behind high Strength.
                    One way to do it is to have two different statlines for the same weapon (with the cutting action having a much higher Strength requirement), or (which I personally prefer) have the second move be granted by a Merit (with a high Strength prerequisite) like many other alternate/modified grappling moves.

                    Edit: A whole different way of dealing with all of this is to not base strangulation damage on the tool used for it. Instead, have a generic system for strangulation (with appropriate targeting penalty, like -3, to establish the grip), and add equipment modifiers from using tools. That way garrotes wouldn't deal different types of damage regardless of materials used, unless you use them as cutting weapons and not as tools of strangulation, in which case use a damage modifier instead of the rules for strangulation.
