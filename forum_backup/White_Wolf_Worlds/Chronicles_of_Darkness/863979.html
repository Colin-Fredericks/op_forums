    Your Character Concepts

    04-11-2016, 08:20 PM
    Since we've got one for silly ideas, why not have one for serious ones? We could discuss them and maybe workshop them for people looking for critique/to try and improve it.

    I'll start with one I'm currently noodling on, a Werewolf concept that draws on some real world stuff. His name is Santino Duku and he's a Rahu Blood Talon that's in a pack with his "adoptive family."

    He was one of the Lost Boys in Sudan, trekking across the country with friends after losing his family, seeing many of them die along the way as well. I imagine he had his First Change while on this trek, likely when he and his friends were attacked by SPLA soldiers who wanted to recruit them. It ended in a bloodbath, but with his new abilities, he escaped the country. It took him awhile to get there, but eventually he made his way to Toronto as an immigrant, working humble jobs. He didn't really have any friends, his family was gone, and he was in a new country he wasn't familiar with. It was a rough time for him.

    Then the Family enters into the picture. They're a family of wolf-blooded and uratha with a few humans that are both an actual family and a pack. They take him into the pack, teach him about Uratha society and 'adopt him' in a manner of speaking. Finding comfort in the strong cultural identity of the Forsaken, he really takes to Purity as his favored renown, and he tries his best to prove himself worthy of being part of this new family.

    Already, he's setting down roots. He has a relationship with one of the wolf-blooded in the family, Celia, and he is close with the newest members of the Family to undergo the First Change. He thinks a lot about the future, something he's had a hard time doing before, and he finds himself worrying. Would he be a good enough father? Would he be a good enough Wolf? Does the Family truly accept him? It's a constant source of doubt in his mind.

    ----

    The theme of the game I'm intending to play him in is about the interplay of family, and I thought it might be interesting to explore it from an outside perspective. Santino is a guy who hasn't had a family in a really long time, has endured an unimaginable amount of loss in his life, so he is desperate to protect his newfound family, whatever flaws it might have. He really wants to be a part of it, but he recognizes he's also very alien to them, and it's a source of pain and frustration for him.

    Another theme we've discussed is a critical look at Uratha as a warrior-culture and society. The Family brought Santino in and taught him the party line as it were, encouraging him to Hunt, not just because he needs to, but because it is good, the right thing to do. But Santino is one of the Lost Boys, a man who has undergone considerable trauma being made the pawn of others in war, experienced great suffering via violence and these other things. How does that complicate the Uratha existence and modus operandi? What does the Family do by taking him in, and are they doing the right thing by putting him in this world of violence? Does he have a choice in the matter?

    These are questions I'm definitely interested in exploring with him. Do you guys have any thoughts or comments? I'd love to hear them! I am also very keen on discussing your concepts as well. What kinds of characters do you have on the brain?

      My Werewolf character, already played.
      ​Faith Grissholm, privileged daughter of an upper class family (extended family) with a proud history of Naval service. She had planned out her whole future, good grades in high school, join the Navy and attend the academy with possibly one day the command of her own ship (dream big). After spending her youth sacrificing and working towards this future she undergoes first change (1ed). Now she learns that her family is part of a wolf blooded clan and with the change came a whole new plan, one designed by the clan patriarch, hers is now scrap. They even told her who she would marry (or at least have children with). She rebels, symbolically runs away by joining an unrelated pack (the players') and after a life of following the straight and narrow becomes a little bit of the "I'll try anything once" sort of wild child. The patriarch hasn't given up on her though, he figures one way or another she will still serve the clan's purposes, or her children will.
      I wanted to explore the wolf blooded/Uratha connection and play with the total life disruption of the change.

          Played and loved him to bits, but:

          Lionheart (real name Richard Brandt) was a police officer and happily married man when the Fae stole him away to fight endlessly in a petty war for their amusement. After seeing too much horror and bloodshed, he organized a suicidal attack with all the other changelings of the domain... only to run from battle and escape while his companions were slaughtered. Returned to reality, he scorns his wife, children, and fetch, in favor of rising to prominence in the local Summer Court; his eventual goal is to forge the freehold into an army that can march back into Arcadia and kill his former Keeper, but he understands and anticipates that it's likely a doomed effort. Pride and survivor's guilt conspire against him, constantly working towards his self-destruction.

          It was really fun to play a charismatic, popular figure in the local political structure who rose to hated tyranny and was eventually killed by the other player characters on the eve of battle with one of the Gentry; they voted to dissolve the freehold rather than likely be wiped out in the fighting.

                      Leetsepeak, I like Santino Duku. The fact you didn't take the Sacred Hunt for granted is admirable, and a totally valid way to play the game.

                      I've been trying to round out a Namtaru Collector. His Horror would be like this. I'm having a hard time imagining how exactly he would Feed, but I know he would be mainly Low Satiety.

                      I considered making him a sort of Franken Fran figure. That is, he almost always has a remedy or surgery of his own design that can fix problems big and small...but do you really need the help of such a shady character? Can't you figure this out on your own? He does his best to minimize side-effects, but he can't guarantee you'll walk out of his Lair just fine if you don't give him all the information about your condition that he needs, which can be a lot. And if he thinks you can give him more than just cash, he might offer...freebies. He tells himself he always puts all the cards on the table, but his Hunger sometimes growls louder than his remaining conventional morality.

                      So, he could be Namtaru as Fear Of The Cure, the uncertainty of easy solutions. What sort of Aspirations would go well with all of that? I imagine that when it comes to Prometheans and Changelings, he can be anything from an open-minded and highly useful mentor, to a Qashmal/True Fae coming to cull the weak and gullible with plagues disguised as panacea.

                      EDIT: By the way, he didn't really build the house/laboratory in his Lair. He grew it. Sometimes, when you're lying down on a surprisingly comfortable operating table, surrounded by the impeccable, fractal blades, you can hear the Lair. You just can't tell if that's a vast pulse, or humming.

                                      Right, sorry for the second post.

                                      Another idea I've had is a character for the Sundered World Dark Era. I'm not a backer, so I'm working from what I've picked up; forgive me if I get anything wrong. Gonna put this in spoilers, since I've put more thought into this than someone who doesn't actually have the document in question has any right to.

                                      He's a Mage (or Wise, as the case may be), to be specific, a Obrimos (or Storm-Wise). Considering that the People apparently go with title names, I'm think of calling him... well, Something of Names; currently I'm considering Carver of Names, Assessor of Names, or Seeker of Names, but I'm not really sure. I'm not sure what the Vinca people looked like, but I imagine him as being tall and somewhat thin, the sort of person who's always moving, pointing, gesturing, and fidgeting whenever, constantly filled with energy.

                                      As for his personality, he's an energetic soul, filled to the brim with an obsessive curiosity. He talks quickly and almost breathlessly, barraging the poor souls who interacts with him with questions, suggestions, and queries. For him, everything is fascinating, everything is worthy of total understanding. Even a simple bull can inspire a involved study as to its nature. But the biggest question, in the end, is "How can this help the People?" For he believes in the ascendancy of humanity, the belief it is the right of the people in particular and mankind in general have the natural right to tame the chaos of the world into a newer, better order. To this end he encourages cooperation and alliance between villages, people, and even potentially peoples. When he allows himself to dream really big, he dreams of all of humanity (possibly even the wolf-shifters and their ilk, if they can learn to be civilized) united into one grand village, a towering wonder that rivals any ruin of the time before, surrounded by farmland as far as the eye can see, a world of peace and plenty where humanity has tamed all of existence.

                                      Mechanically, I'm thinking he favors Mental and Social Attributes and Skills. I'm thinking going for the Virtuous Merit, if I can. In regards to the Arcana, well, the most practical would probably be Forces (storms and flame), Life (Agriculture and wild animals), and Spirit (because those are a big threat in these days), while the Arcana most useful to his ambitions seem to be Prime (to know and use the Forms and Names of a thing), Mind (better understand and navigate the Astral), and Space (travel and communication at a distance). To be honest, I imagine he would like to get a dot in all the Arcana (foe access to the Knowing, Unveiling, and Mage Sight), but that sounds impractical.
