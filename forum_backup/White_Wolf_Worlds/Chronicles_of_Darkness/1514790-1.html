        First Impression score for each of the orgs in Tending the Fire:

        Ashwood Abbey- 7 (Art gets 9, Logo 9, however, but I feel the writeup doesn't give anything new compared to 1e. AA are still awesome anyway so I can't score it too low)

        Crimson Halo- 9 (Art 8, Logo 10, blown out of the park imho)

        Crossroads Convoy- 7 (Art 7, Logo 8, like they fill a good niche and could maker good stories, but don't fully click as a PC I want to play. Useful as NPCs however)

        Eden Covenant- 6 (Art 5, Logo 7, a bit all over the place and definitely more of antagonists than protagonists).

        Keepers of the Source- 8 (Art 8, Logo 8, again don't give much beyond 1e but they do polish a few rough edges imo)

        Las Guadanas- 10 (Art 9, Logo 8, honestly amazing. I didn't thought I would like them from the snip but boy I was wrong! Also, remind me a lot of my Ebony Fangs and I can really see how I can make them into a chapter of the later, which makes them even more of use to me)

        Night Watch- 6 (Art 5, Logo 5, I honestly never clicked with the original 1e writeup and I am not sure they add much more. The fact that the original writeup very much emphasized them being Afro-American and the art misses that part also penalize the score)

        Recalibrators- 7 (Art 7, Logo 5, they are not bad and they fill a good niche, and also not what I was expecting, but again something more that I would use as NPC than as a PC)

        Aegis Kai Doru- 9 (Art 5, Logo 8, Relics 8 which is, honestly, disappointing, but the writeup compensates for it by a lot. They clearly draw upon the extra lore from C&C, and add new twists which wraps it ionto a very cool package. Relics are still cool as they always were)

        Asclepeion- 8 (Art 9, Logo 7, Endowments 9, they are cool and fill the "street doctor" niche that was needed, but something between them being strangely young, a yet another explicitly Greek conspiracy right after the AKD and actually have the AKD in their writeups kinda takes away some of their points. Their Endowments are rad, however, even though I would have prefered them to not be so balantly supernatural)

        Campion Wildlife Service- 7 (Art 7, Logo 7, Endowments 7, the concept is cool, but something feels a bit lacking. Them marking the Deviant conspiracies as enemies is a cool bit, but feels like they would have worked much better as a compact than as a conspiracy. Also, having the founder's name in the title only reinforces this feeling unfortunately. The Endowments also feel a bit.. weird? somewhere between Advanced Armory and Thaumatech. Just too generic imo).

        Enigmatics- 7 (Art 8, Logo 5, Endowments 7 again a cool concept but they are feeling that they are still at that level- a concept. Feels like they need a lot more development. The name is also too shallow imo, another symptom I think. Also, a question mark for a logo? for a group of artists? The Endowments are cool, but too... insidious? They all sound pretty dark. I mean, rewarding cannibalism? Causing insanity? Amnesia? I can see those guys more as slashers than as hunters, honestly)

        Operation Nebula- 8 (Art 9, Logo 9, Endowments 9, my main issue with those guys is that it is not very clear how did they became a conspiracy. Being a group of ancient astronaut theorists and conspiracy theories doesn't really make a material for a conspiracy, and sounds more fitting for a compact. The use of the internet alone is not enough imo. The Endowment itself is pretty cool, with the emphasis on harvesting "alien cores" and all of that)

        Survivor's Club- 6 (Art 10, Logo 10, Endowments 9, feels a lot like a compact, and if they really wanted to present them as a conspiracy they should have just "promoted the Sisterhood imo. Again, not really sure what made them into a conspiracy- a running theme here for most writeups. Still, a way to play Buffy I guess? The Endowments are pretty rad, however)

        Watchers- 8 (Art 8 with an extra bonus for presenting a person with dwarfism, Logo 8, Endowments 8 like I personally made a conspiracy with a very similar concept, but this one suffers a lot from being, again, very shallow in terms of development. The name is, again, too generic. I liked the nod to the Dog Star, but it feels too focused around the Deathless and I would have loved to get a bit more about how they operate when Sothis does not mess shit up, especially as mummies are somewhat more active in 2e. At the very least they are useful as material for my own Avicii Sutra. Endowments are cool though)

        White Hare Society- 9 (Art 10, Logo 8, Endowments 8... Look I can live with the similarities between the Survivors Guide and Aspel's Final Girl Club and the Watchers using the same concept as the Avicii Sutra as it is pretty generic, but the Society being "Portuguese Age of Discovery Conspiracy that roams to Otherworlds sounds a little too similar to the Navio del Tolos... it seems that I am going to need to revise them as I planned to make them a STV release, and I am not going to let my sister's art go to waste. Thankfully the idea of a explorer conspiracy is wide enough to be adapted. Still, can't say anything bad about this writeup considering that I made a very similar conspiracy myself. Anyway, the Endowments are cool, but a bit weird? The use of potions as a medium feels strange imo).

                    Ok, I finished reading the supplement, overall I give it a positive grade because it's full of exciting ideas to create games focused on organizations and social intrigue.
                    That said, on the negative, I will say one word that came to my mind constantly while reading: rushed. There are just not enough words dedicated to some of the compacts and conspiracies to flesh them out and as a result, sometimes I get confused about what exactly is the role of the organization and what I'm supposed to do with them. I get there are space constrictions, but given the number of pages, I think it wouldn't have been unreasonable to add one or two pages more to each compact and conspiracy.
                    Then there's what some have mentioned before, some conspiracies feel more like compacts and vice-versa, and also some read as if the only reason they really exist is to support other hunter organizations or that their objective is unrelated to the vigil and only hunt monsters as a side activity, great as npc characters and factions, but not so much as player characters. Again, another page or two would have gone a long way in helping to know them better and clarifying how to better use them.
                    I want to end on a positive note and emphasize that I liked the supplement, there's plenty to use. I just wish it could have been longer.

                        Funnily enough, I can't see a quick reference to which Conspiracy relates to which Endowments. It's not hard, they did spell out in the Endowments section but...I'd find it helpful.

                        So here it is:
                        • Aegis Kai Doru - Relics (as before)
                        • Asclepeion - Enkoimesis (mystical healing)
                        • Campion Wildlife Service - Animal Control Kit (tools of the trade & extracted mutations)
                        • Enigmatics - Inspiration (magical masterwork art pieces)
                        • Operation Nebula - Xenotechnology (Gizmos made from salvaged "alien" technology)
                        • Survivor's Club - The Horror Within (embracing the "scourge" ie the slasher "virus" within)
                        • Watchers - Lives Remembered (knowledge leveraged from past & future lives)
                        • White Hare Society - Infusion (alchemically infused earthly components with otherworldly energies)
                        Given the limited write ups, I do feel the Endowments help with the feel of the new Conspiracies.
                        As an example, The Survivor's Club comes off a lot darker and more dangerous when you know it isn't just a bunch of survivors playing Buffy, and they are doing the whole "using the power of their own archnemesis" things.

The Red Wax Mask animates statues until dawn and can be paid for with the user's blood to the same degree as its normal activation cost (and I here note that the vampire blood used to make the Mask didn't come from nowhere). The conspiracy doesn't focus on killing monsters, but that doesn't mean they don't do so at all

                                                        I am not saying "those guys are slashers", but saying "are you sure they are not?" because either intentionally or not, it is not clear to me how their Endowments actually help their own Vigil of getting inspired by monsters or the Vigil as a whole. Even if they are not outright slashers- well, they are foot and a half in the way, imo.
                                                        Lost, I'll be frank, this smacks of using a term with a specific meaning as a general "it's bad" word — a slasher is a human driven to murder people. I should not have to tell you that this is more than a full step in the opposite direction from "monster-hunters who don't focus on killing monsters."

                                                        Again, I personally like the idea of artists being non violent in terms of their Vigil, but in that case the Endowments can't be so violent- because someone is meant to get hurt, and ion most instances the texts explicitly says that bystanders are going to be the ones getting screwed.
                                                        The very extremely obvious workaround for this, which the creator of a masterwork will know because any successful roll to create a masterwork tells you how the thing works — is to not use the thing around bystanders in uncontrolled circumstances. The Recalibrators demonstrate that enough hunters understand this concept to build a compact around it.

                                                        Masterworks created through Inspiration are supernaturally-empowered artworks with their own malevolent agenda. Our presented examples run as follows:
                                                        1. A painting inspired by struggles against demonic temptation, which can make observers looking upon it for (typically multiple) minutes at a time recover a Willpower and become Addicting to it, allowing them to gain more Willpower from fulfilling their Vice within view of it at the cost of all other sources of Willpower recovery; addicted monsters also become Obsessed with having and showing the painting and gain a Vice for the purposes of the painting's effects; the Enigmatic can render addicted characters in the painting's immediate presence Insensate and then Traumatized/Insane, Tiltwise, ending the Addiction and Obsession for anyone outside the painting's immediate presence. Relatively harmless for a Depraved Art storyhook and you can use it as a lure for monsters if you're playing the long game in a social context.
                                                        2. A song inspired by an encounter with a werewolf eating a family, which makes monsters' Dread Powers more costly to use and tempts humans to eat human flesh for Willpower and a first-time breaking point or be Spooked enough to resist; the song lives in one head at a time and wants to have its power used, so its host may find themselves singing it unconsciously unless they catch themselves, a Dramatic Failure on the activation roll lets it jump to someone else's head for the chapter, and anyone who's engaged in cannibalism retains a full copy of the masterwork afterward and can spread it to others. Solid way to deprive a monster of their cost-bearing abilities, good rationale to strengthen your Resolve and get better at Inspiration and not use it to confront monsters around people you think might be proactive about consuming human flesh if given the opportunity.
                                                        3. Scrimshaw disks depicting aquatic monsters, which dramatically enhance rolls to go unnoticed except to a specific category of people that varies by specific masterwork, with whom the Enigmatic becomes mutually Swooned; the cost is becoming Deprived until the user fulfills their Vice or spends a scene with their Touchstone, and until then they'll sleepwalk toward a body of water or the nearest other such masterwork. Mild touch of the Depraved Artist, mild touch of occult romance, not exactly popping with deadly intent on its own unless you have some particular ideas about why cursed objects might be trying to get together.
                                                        4. A bust of Pallas Athena inspired by witnessing things mortals shouldn't see, which offers knowledge in exchange for memories of secrets based on the success of the activation roll; regardless of the outcome, the player always has the option of losing the memories temporarily, only a Dramatic Failure results in permanent loss of an important memory, and the character can choose what memories they're sacrificing by making the memory loss more severe. Obviously the product of a traumatic experience and doesn't have any direct effect on bystanders at all because it only affects the user by trading memories for knowledge.
                                                        5. A mask cast from a monster's face using wax mixed with vampire blood. Animates all statues within range below a certain Size to act as basic Retainers for the rest of the night, returning to their original poses at dawn; the mask must be fed with the user's blood to activate, and in the last hour before dawn, the statues seek out people (including, potentially, the user) to sacrifice to bathe the mask in their blood, and if the mask does not receive such a tithe by dawn, it inflicts another point of lethal damage on the user. A fairly dire power to use for a fairly difficult masterwork to create, but this one notably doesn't have any side-effects based around compelling its use and, even if you rule that monsters don't count as people for the purposes of the mask's criteria, it's tailor-made for dealing with monsters with a retinue.

                                                        I frankly wouldn't consider these meaningfully more morally iffy for a hunter conspiracy to be using than the full span of effects demonstrated by tokens in 1e Changeling.
