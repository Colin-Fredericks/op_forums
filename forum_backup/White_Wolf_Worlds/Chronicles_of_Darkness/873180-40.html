    CofD is generally pretty lenient with clothing when it comes to shapeshifting. Vampires with Protean always subsume the clothes, and werewolves only rip clothes as a penalty for bad Harmony. For most werewolves the clothes can actually change to fit them when shifting to their near-human form. Skinthieves, on the other hand, requires an additional Merit in order to keep their clothes when transforming. But that's also a minor template that puts much more focus on the details around shapeshifting than even werewolves.

    When a power doesn't specify you should look to two things: What is the purpose of the power, and how does the power work? The purpose is to look like someone, and clothes can definitely be considered part of that. The power is an Atavism, so it works by channelling the nightmarish symbols of the Primordial Dream into the Beast. That means shifting the clothes isn't really an issue, unlike The Thing which shapeshifts through a biological process. Therefor I'd definitely allow the clothes to change too. It fits the purpose of the Atavism, and it definitely fits the theme of Inguma who has it as an In-Family Atavism. Just look to their Birthright.

                                        The thing about Hedge interactions -- in Changeling 1e, the Hedge reacted to the presence of a Changeling based on their Wyrd, and only at very high Wyrd could a changeling actively manipulate the Hedge. 2e has thrown that out in favor of the Hedgespinning subsystem, where any Changeling can attempt to actively manipulate the Hedge, but there's no hard-and-fast passive effects from just being there. (At least, I couldn't find any mention of the Hedge reactivty subsystem in the Kickstarter previews for CtL2, and I looked pretty carefully.)

                                        But the Beast corebook stilll points to the 1e subsystem, using Lair instead of Wyrd. The intended effect of which, I guess, was that Beasts and Changelings would be indistinguishable from each other in their effect on the Hedge...but, unless this is errata'd, they now look pretty distinct from one another. (Which, for the record, I like better for fluff reasons--Beasts are the dreams, changelings are the dreamers).

                                            Is this valid? Does the game talk about this kind of thing anywhere? What if I don't even want to bother with the speech myself, does my Horror have my Skills normally? Can I tell it to do the copywriting while I hit the pool? How about my Merits? Do I have access to those things while merged, can I make use of kung-fu as a monster that isn't humanoid?
                                            There's a bit in Book of Spirits that points out the common-sense meaning of this, but the short version is: No, the PFR Attribute Spread does not translate to all of the things in its subset being equally effective at the tasks they correspond to — a Horror with Power 5 is not in practice the equivalent of a mortal with Strength 5, Presence 5, and Intelligence 5. The concept of the entity in question takes precedence over any computer-dumb literalistic reading of the mechanics when it comes to framing what it can and can't do within the story.

                                            A Beast with the Strength of an Olympic medalist is more effective at imposing their raw will in the Primordial Dream when merged with their Horror, but they don't actually get the equivalent of Intelligence 5 just because they're rolling for Intelligence-based rolls as though that part of their dicepool was 5.

                                            High Attributes are only going to compensate so much for low Skills and lack of usable tools, is the other thing — the effect is more comparable to you just spending Willpower on a bunch of rolls to do the thing you're not good at than it is to suddenly becoming a virtuoso in the dreamscape.
