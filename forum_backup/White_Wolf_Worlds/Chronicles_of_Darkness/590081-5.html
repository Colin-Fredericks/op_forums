                                        My interpretation was that what was described was the Truth of the phenomenal world: Ammut is oblivion, not abyssal (though would be classified as Lower Depths - only because that means "I don't know, but its hungry") and a little further past Entropy; she's where ghosts go when destroyed, the bottom of the underworld, and the eventual nothingness that will consume everything, but born of this realm.

                                        Likewise it seemed to me that the Scroll of Ages was the Truth of the world; the supernal is merely the symbols that compose everything. If the supernal is the math that describes the universe, the Scroll of Ages would be the laws of physics . They do directly create one another, but one is potential, one is fact.

                                        In that sense, I could see Khepera as the supernal: the substance from which all is made, "the state of existing... and am everything created, but I am not the act of creation". Atum, then, is the soul and experience ; perhaps the very force of Change which allows creation to become (or, one might say, the Principle which composes existence from its constituent parts).
                                        Edit: Then again, the Heretic's testimony explains that the Law of Suffering causes change, so that might have a more direct link to the Principle (at least from my understanding of it from forum conversations). However, that clashes a little with the transhumanist element the Principle inspires toward, which is why I'm interpreting it as associated with Atum. Or perhaps it could be a fragment of the time before (the law of change prior to the Exachs' influence making that suffering) which was able to hold onto its will and existence and carve out a continuing role for itself...

                                        But I could definitely see the link between the Judges and the Exarchs. As you said in my thread, the Judges and their Law of Suffering could be the causality mechanism of Fate; after the Exarchs became the symbols of oppression for all existence, the Judges would have certainly incorporated them- a blending of "survival of the fittest" with tyrnnical oppression (and since supernal changes are retroactive, the Judges would have always had that nature).

                                        That's just where my musings have taken me to, though
