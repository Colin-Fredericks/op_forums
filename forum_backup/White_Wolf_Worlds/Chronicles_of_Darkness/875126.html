    Going Against the Steroetypes...

    04-26-2016, 10:19 PM
    So... we all know that every splat basically has inherent incentives to play a specific way, with their powers, weaknesses, and so on encouraging that, basically attempting to guide you to a stereotype.

    My question is... how effective would they be at not being what they were meant to be according to the stereotypes?


    For instance, Werewolves are heavily melee monsters... so how good are they at NOT being that?

    DarkFrame: Crossover setting that puts Chronicles of Darkness in the far future that is Warframe.

                                      It's perfectly valid (and fun!) to go against the stereotypes, but your character should have something in common with the X-splat and Y-splay you chose.

                                      Let's suppose you're playing Vampire. You have Social Traits as your Primary for Attributes and Skills, but the Disciplines of the Nosferatu really appeal to you. This Clan's Bane gets in the way of social interaction, so you decide to keep your Humanity as high as you can. Your profession is counselor for humans and vampires alike, and you're a decent individual overall. Where's the hideousness that is part of the Clan? You can say that it manifests in the form of occasional possessiveness of the people your character helps. Sometimes, people say they feel ready to face life on their own, but your character is uncertain, and that turns into paranoia. We all fear being the target of an obsessive individual. It's intrusive and just plain creepy. There's your hideousness. You may not invest any dots into Vigor, but you will get a lot of mileage out of Nightmare and Auspex.

                                                      Generally speaking, a supernatural template's base abilities make them good at the things they're supposed to be good at as a baseline.

                                                      Even a werewolf that starts with 2 Strength, 2 Dexterity, 2 Stamina, 0 Athletics, and 0 Brawl still forces Down and Dirty combat on many opponents, with a 4 dice pool and +2 weapon modifier. In regular combat they've got 3 turns of full lethal and bashing heals at 9 health, during which their opponents can't add Athletics to Defense. At the end of those 3 turns, they can either take 1 more turn and then risk Death Rage, or play it safe and "just" have 1 bashing heal per turn, 8 health, and a a 3 dice Brawl pool with a +2 damage modifier that automatically inflicts a Tilt on the first hit. Not great, but pretty damn good for a character with the minimum possible combat investment.

                                                      A vampire with 2/2/2 in Social Attributes and 4 dots in Animal Ken with nothing in any other social skills can still manage a 4-dice pool at a Perfect Impression on any Social action against any human if they can manage to slip a drop of blood into their drink on three separate occasions.
