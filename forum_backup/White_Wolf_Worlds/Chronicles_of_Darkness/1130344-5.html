        All things aside, I gotta admit that as someone who fell in love with Forsaken because of Predators and read that book so many times I lost count, it is quite clear the book shows its age nowadays.

        I was talking about this with another fellow Werewolf fan the other day. It's not "just" the mechanics (though having new ones would be great for sure), it's also a matter of tone, lore and focus. Predators is an antagonist book written in early 1st Edition, where all the big three lines had a little problem of identity issues. Again, awesome book, but after 2nd Edition came in years later and sharpened the themes while rebuilding the setting, it also brought up the need to have detailed material about the antagonists which is up to par.

        I'm not saying the gameline is abandoned or that other lines are to blame and all, I disagree with that line of thought and trust we'll get more stuff, but, if anything, we have to be fair and say that there's both need and demand for a book like that. Especially for a game like Werewolf were conflicts external to the characters are more frequent than in others. Hunters and preys, after all.

                                        Yeah, the dictionary definition of the word "fluff" is something light and unsubstantive or something trivial and superficial. The later definition specifically refers to "fluff articles" in newspapers, which is a term for an article that doesn't have any real meaning and is only there to bump up a paper's word count. Hence why a number of RPG authors are not fans of the term.
