              Since the PCs are super fresh, I would do something slightly different: The characters get a Mask to replace their Virtue as normal. This Mask is directly based on their lost human lives. However, they still have their human Vice until the end of the first Story. At which point you may as well let the players decide. You could allow them to delay this switch for a bit longer too.

              This could also be a nice bit of bringing in the psychological change via the character sheet. They are not quite proper vampires yet, which feeds well into 2e's theme of slowly growing into being a proper vampire, instead of it being a instant binary switch.

                              Gravitas Man,

                              Well, my group is using the original characters. However, there is a couple of reasons I don't feel like using the original Virtue/Vice combos:
                              1) The Virtue/Vice mechanics changed between editions. The characters in the 2nd Ed. are supposed to be able to regain WP through the Vice more frequently and easier than in the 1st Ed. The original Vices don't really support that well, at least in my mind;
                              2) The Virtue/Vice feel changed between the editions. I believe they are supposed to be much more "mundane" and less "pretentious," if you will. It is not the Vice of Greed, but your character is cheap - does that make sense? It looks like they should be much more down-to-earth; and
                              3) In the 2nd Ed. Virtues and Vices have to be adjectives, if I remember correctly. Adjectives based on some of the original Virtues/Vices sound terrible, lol.

                                      Most of the 1e virtues and vices have direct equivalents in the example virtues and vices in GMC, and those that don't could be substituted for indirect equivalents or custom ones made for them.

                                      Charity = Generous
                                      Hope = Hopeful
                                      Justice = Just or Righteous
                                      Prudence = Patient

                                      Greed = Greedy
                                      Pride = Arrogant or Ambitious
                                      Sloth = Pessimistic
                                      Wrath = Violent or Hateful

                                      That leaves Faith, Fortitude, Temperance, Envy, Gluttony, and Lust.

                                      Faith could easily become Faithful, which could restore Willpower when the character puts herself at risk in defense of her beliefs. Fortitude could become Resolute, which could restore Willpower when the character sticks to a chosen course of action despite great adversity. Temperance could become Moderate, which could restore Willpower when the character turns down an opportunity to gain an advantage because it would be too indulgent. Envy could become Jealous, which could restore Willpower when the character takes an opportunity to point out someone else's value as greater than their own. Gluttony could become Indulgent, which could restore Willpower when the character indulges to the point of excess for excess' sake. And Lust could become Lascivious, which could restore Willpower when the character objectifies another character.
