        Originally posted by WHW View Post
        Huh. I thought that each type of bonus (circumstancial, equipment, etc) maxed out at 5, not 5 per source (so 2 sources of equipment bonus to +6), to avoid equipment arms race.
        I think you're right. It's not terribly clear in the core rules. Here are a few relevant quotes:

        Originally posted by page 69
        Many rolls have modifiers, either from equipment, circumstance, or someone working against your character. Most modifiers are within the range of +3 to -3, though they can range as low as -5 or as high as +5. Apply the modifiers to your dice pool before you roll. Add all the bonuses first, then apply penalties.
        Later on the same page there's a similar quote about circumstantial modifiers:

        Originally posted by page 69
        Most of the time, the modifier from circumstances will be between +3 and –3, though in very rare cases — picking a lock while the room is on fire — it can range from +5 to –5.
        Between these two quotes, I got the impression that each individual source of bonuses and caps at +/-5, but nothing to suggest stacking bonuses and penalties from different sources couldn't exceed that cap. Later, however, there are some potentially contradictory quotes.

        Originally posted by page 78
        Clues can go above the normal +5 limit on dice bonuses; they’re limited only by the character’s Investigation Skill.
        This quort mentions "the normal +5 limit on dice bonuses" as if that's a thing that's been established, but I couldn't find anything earlier in the rules that actually says such a limit exists. However, in the equipment section (later in the book) it says:

        Originally posted by page 100
        Most equipment offers a bonus to dice rolls pertaining to its use. Multiple items can influence a given roll, but a roll should not receive more than a +5 bonus.
        From context, it seems to me like this is specifically talking about equipment bonuses.

        Originally posted by WHW View Post
        Is Investigative Prodigy doing literally nothing if you bought only 1 dot? It seems silly; I think it should allow you to gain Clues equal to 1+merit dots instead of merit dots, or, even simplier, up to one extra Clue per merit dot.

            I personally follow a guideline from Building the equipment that suggests to assign non-dice bonuses when you go beyond +5. You know, like small perks; you might have a perfect dress, incredible make up and fantastic dossier on your target, but while first two combine to +5 together and dossier has a value of +3 (so adding it would go beyond 5), I will translate it into stepping up impression level.
            Keeps going beyond 5 interesting, and doesn't escalate the arms race into DND levels of "but thou must have level appropiate sword to reliably hit this!". I like idea of bound accuracy.

            How Dark Eras and other pre-modern settings utilize ballistic armor value? Is there anything interesting written about it, for example a suggestion to use it for something else (like, downgrading weapons attacks to bashing)? I like split of General / Downgrading armor, and I would like to keep it even in games where firearms are not a thing. I feel it adds some granularity without going too deep into armor charts and tables.
