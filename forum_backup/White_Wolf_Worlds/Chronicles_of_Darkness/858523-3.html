    And now, werewolves.

    *****

    Werewolves: A pack of Uratha isn’t a terribly large organization, but it is undoubtedly a Cult. The rite and ritual demanded by Totem, Lodge, Protectorate, and Tribe is distinctly cultic. The psychology of the Uratha is equally divergent, and the importance of the pack looms so large in their minds and social lives that seeing pack members together almost screams ‘Cult’ to the casual observer. A Forsaken pack is, almost always, a Gang or Tribal Cult. A pack that leans more towards Tribal affiliations is a Tribal Cult, and is generally a good deal larger than a pack that is less strongly affiliated with the Tribes. Packs generally don’t invest much into spreading the influence of the pack directly- temporal power and running a Cult isn’t exactly their strong suit. When they do need wider influence, they generally invest in Wolf-Blooded Agents and Operatives, or invest greater power into their various packmates.

    Packs normally have a certain amount of Lore, generally enough to possess the Patron Merit, which acts as an extra three dots of the Totem Merit. The Second Sight Merit generally insures that every member of the pack possesses at least a dot in Totem. Some packs, in particular those with strong Fire-Touched and Bone Shadow connections, cultivate more Lore to build Cults tied to the core pack made up mostly of Wolf-Blooded, or mortals with Supernatural Merits intended to facilitate easier interactions with Spirits. On rare occasions, packs dabble in other Cult structures. Ivory Talons and Storm Lords have inclinations towards Elite Clubs and Cults of Personality, while Iron Masters occasionally branch out into Enterprises to compete with similarly adventurous Ivory Talon elite. The Predator Kings, if they bother to build a Cult at all, are generally very small, and very Isolated, with significant Home Turf. The scions of Dire Wolf would prefer to run in Pangaea reborn, thank-you very much.

    But far more, and far better, writers than myself have gone over the intricacies of Uratha packs- what are of more interest for the purpose of this work are Spirit Cults and packs solely comprised of Wolf-Blooded and humans. In such cases, the Wolf-Blooded become masters of Rites, learning them from Spirits and the careful observation of the Hisil and learning the flow of Essence and its laws. A Spirit Cult can be of any Foundation, but the nature of the Shadow generally pushes them to things that suit their nature. Tribal Cults and Unorganized Cults remain the most common, but every other Foundation sees some sort of representation amongst the worshippers of the Hisil. These Cults, for obvious reasons, often end up in conflict with the Uratha, though typically under very different motivations based on the circumstances. Some see themselves as ‘monster hunters’, others believe that they have as much a right to the Shadow as any werewolf, and some simply seek the occult secrets of the Spirits. In rare circumstances, the Uratha find some Spirit Cults comprised of nothing but humans, without even the Wolf-Blooded to support the Totem bond. Worryingly, in such Cults the Claimed seem to occupy a place of honor equivalent to the Uratha, and may practice Rites that call upon the Claiming Spirit’s nature- Rites no werewolf has ever seen before.

    Hosts of Foes: Spirit Cults are amongst the most dangerous of all the organizations the Uratha can come up against- just as capable, if not more so, of providing a hospitable environment for Spirits to operate, driven by intense personal motivations as well as the manipulations of the Spirits involved, they are capable of coming close to matching the mystical might of the Uratha. Just as frightening, if not more so, are Cults that spring up around the shartha. A parade of fresh host bodies for the Shards, allowing them to return time and again to plague local packs, and mortal resources that allow a potent Shard to expand their area of influence and find others of their kind. Persistent, terrifying, rumors indicate massive cults of potent Azlu Crones and Beshilu Ministers that destroy all who come before them and ceaselessly seek out the hearts of their Pangaean progenitors.

    Skinthieves are always a concern, finding their way into Spirit Cults and Uratha packs alike. They are generally harmless, to a certain quality of harmless, making good packmates- most are often Wolf-Blooded, or at the very least experienced occultists. In the unusual cases of a Skinthief capable of transforming into a Spirit or other Ephemeral Being, they make especially important pack members, capable of infiltrating the local Spirit Courts and assisting the Totem directly. In the case of an antagonistic Skinthief, there are few as dangerous. No one is quite sure what would happen if a Skinthief managed to kill and skin a werewolf, but no one really wants to find out.

                Some additional content I threw together from Guildhalls of the Deathless and the Secrets of the Covenants spoilers. Enjoy.

                Temple (o - ooooo)
                Prerequisite: Safe Place (equal or greater value)
                Effect: The Cult, or your character specifically, maintains a place of worship exemplifying one aspect of their faith. Actions in support of the Cult’s Purpose or Doctrine gain a bonus equal to the dots of the Merit within the Temple’s walls. For example, a Lancea et Sanctum Temple that espouses resisting the Beast would grant a bonus to resisting Frenzy. Additionally, those blessed by the Temple (IE. Those who participate in a service at the Temple) carry its benefits beyond its walls for a number of days equal to dots in the Merit. During this time, the Cult may use any Rites, Rituals, or other powers against the blessed character as though making physical contact.
                Note: Like Safe Place, more than one character may invest dots in this Merit.

                This Merit now serves as a prerequisite to Altar, and, much like Cult itself, will probably have a few Merits branching off of it. This little project is just full of inspiration, and I would like to open it up to the public. I know a lot of people have called for a Cults Bluebook, and some people have even made significant progress along those ends, and I would be extremely grateful if people interested in contributing to this humble bit of homebrew could come forward, turning it into a community project.

                            They support them quite well, honestly. A Cult doesn't go away just because the character who invested the Merit dots dies- the institution still stands, though in the case of a Cult of Personality it takes a pretty serious hit. In the case of Institutional Covers, I'd probably rule that Reach = Power and Grasp = Finesse, with Resistance= Lower of the two, so the Demon could wield the Cult like an extension of themselves. It would be an Eldritch, crawling thing- an organization that builds and controls itself; a miniature God-Machine. In such cases of Cults As Characters, I'd probably still invest in an Agent or Operative, just so that there's this sketch of a character who can be the 'face' of the Cult.

                                    So, decided to come back and post some new stuff here. I've been working on a Sin-Eater 2E conversion, and the Krewe Building material was too good to pass up for making interesting Merits to tie to a Cult. Also, Mage 2E came out and that had some juicy material to work with as well. Here are some new, and reworked, Merits.

                                    *****

                                    Hidden Master (ooo, oooo, or ooooo)
                                    Prerequisite: Reach and Grasp 3
                                    Effect: Regardless of whether your character founded the Cult or simply joined it later, they managed to weasel their way into a position of great power, and then quietly receded into the background. Your character benefits from Status or Mystery Cult Initiation equal to their dots in this Merit. Additionally, you no longer suffer the Drawbacks of the Cult Merit; they have no formal ties to the Cult, and as such don’t suffer any Conditions that apply to members of the Cult.
                                    Drawback: Without formal ties to the Cult, or standing in a position of visible authority, their means of manipulating the Cult directly are greatly reduced. They may only benefit from a number of dots of Reach and/or Grasp equal to their dots in Hidden Master. They can still gain the benefit of the Cult’s full authority by employing their own Conspirators, or engaging in Social Maneuvering with the Cult’s ‘leader’.

                                    Ritual Savvy (oo)
                                    Prerequisite: Lore 2+
                                    Effect: Even if the priests don’t totally understand why they’re gathering the tools and symbols you requested, they at least know what to look for. Roll an appropriate dice pool to gather ingredients or prepare the ritual space. Each success on the roll is considered a bonus die to using Rites, Creative Thaumaturgy, Blood Sorcery, etc. that the supernatural patron of the Cult may ‘buy’ with a point of Willpower. For maximum effect, gather as many ritualists as you can in advance and have each of them take advantage of this Merit as an Extended Action.

                                    (Author's Note: These two are reskinned and modified Merits from Mage 2E, Mystery Cult Influence and Ritual Savvy respectively.)

                                    Potent Rites (o - ooooo)
                                    Prerequisite: Altar, Temple (equal or greater value)
                                    Effect: When the Cult gathers together, their place of worship truly becomes hallowed ground. What form this ceremony takes is dependent entirely upon the Purpose or Doctrine, but the true key to this effect is sincerity. The participants must be there of their own free will and be open to the idea something meaningful is going on, but they don’t need to be aware that an occult rite is happening. Such a rite takes about three hours to perform, and at the end it produces a temporary sacred place with a dot rating equal to the dots in this Merit. The form this sacred place takes is dependent upon the supernatural being in charge. For an Uratha pack it could be a Locus, for a Mage cabal it could be a Hallow, for an Unchained ring it would count as Subverted Infrastructure, for a Sin-Eater krewe it is a Haunt, and for one of the Deathless it could be treated as a Vestige. The sacred place weakens by a single dot once every (Lore) days.
                                    Drawback: You need a certain number of worshippers in order to accrue the metaphysical weight necessary to produce such a sacred space. For a powerful and far-reaching Cult, this isn’t really a problem, but gathering large numbers of worshippers to perform the rite could impair the Cult’s actions for days.
                                    Sacred Place Rating Minimum Number of Worshippers
                                    1 10
                                    2 50
                                    3 100
                                    4 500
                                    5 1000
                                    (Author's Note: A little something to replace the Dedicated Altar and Consecrated Altar Merits. Upon review, those didn't make a lot of sense, and this one is more flavourful and comes with a bit more mechanical bite anyways)

                                    Mythic Aspect (oooo)
                                    Prerequisite: Cult Leader
                                    Effect: The Cult Leader taps into the mythic ideal behind their Purpose, Relic, or Doctrine- taking up some of the power of the divinity they use the symbolism of. When taking this Merit, choose an Attribute or a Skill- this cannot be changed after purchase. By spending a point of Willpower (or Supernatural Fuel, depending on Template) they may increase that Skill or Attribute by +3 for the scene, even above their normal Skill/Attribute limitations. This Merit may only be taken once.
                                    Drawback: By tapping into the symbolic power behind the Cult, the leader starts to accept the ethical or even spiritual authority of the Cult. In any scene where they take advantage of this Merit, they suffer a Breaking Point with a -2 penalty should they break one of the laws outlined in the Cult’s doctrine.

                                    Mythic Avatar (ooooo)
                                    Prerequisite: Mythic Aspect, Supernatural Resistance 3+
                                    Effect: The Cult’s leader acquires the power to become the physical embodiment of the Cult’s god, becoming a half-divine being from one perspective, or a twisted monstrosity from another. By spending a point of Willpower and a point of Supernatrual Fuel (or two Willpower if they do not possess a Supernatural Fuel trait) spiritual power explodes from the character, surrounding and merging with them, transforming them into a being with the following characteristics. The transformation lasts for a number of turns equal to Supernatural Resistance + Composure.
                                    • +2 to Stamina and Presence, and +4 to one Attribute that is suitably related with the Aspect’s nature. This can take them above their normal limitations.
                                    • The character’s unarmed attacks may inflict Lethal damage- the means by which they accomplish this vary depending on the nature and symbolism of the Aspect. If they already possess a means by which to inflict Lethal damage unarmed, then this adds a +1 weapon modifier.
                                    • The character gains a number of points of Armor equal to their Supernatural Resistance which they may divide as they see fit.
                                    • The character may interact with anything in Twilight.
                                    Drawback: Accepting the transformation means giving in to a kind of divine power, enabling the character to act without compunction. The exact means by which this occurs varies Cult to Cult, it could be a temporary Claiming by a Spirit, a manifestation of a Beast’s Horror, an expression of a Vampire’s Beast, or something else. Regardless, the power held strains the soul. At the end of the scene the character suffers a Breaking Point.

                                    Glorious Divinity (ooo)
                                    Prerequisite: Mythic Avatar
                                    Effect: When the character makes use of Mythic Avatar, they gain access to a Dread Power they wouldn’t be able to otherwise use.
                                    Drawback: For every purchase of this Merit, the Breaking Point following the use of Mythic Avatar is made with a -1 penalty.

                                    Sending Forth the Aspect (oo)
                                    Prerequisite: Mythic Aspect
                                    Effect: The bonds of initiation and belief unite Cultist and Leader, enabling the Cult Leader to be present in spirit even when they cannot be present in the flesh. The target must be a member of the Cult and within the Leader’s line of sight, or under the effect of worshipping at a Temple. The leader spends a Willpower point and rolls Resolve + Composure (+ Supernatural Resistance), the target can choose to resist with a roll of their own, but should the Leader succeed they send forth their Aspect which treats the target as a Fetter, except that it can use its powers on its surroundings The Aspect functions like a Dream Form, resembles an image of the deity, or something formed from Mythic Avatar, and may use any abilities the Leader can. It gains the Twilight Form and Materialize Manifestations for free. The Aspect is always visible to the target, and may be seen with suitable Unseen Senses. The target and Leader may communicate through the Aspect, verbally or telepathically, but this does not include subconscious or hidden information. The Aspect remains for a number of hours equal to successes on the activation roll, and the Leader may reset the time limit by spending another Willpower point.
                                    Drawback: The Leader must concentrate in order to use this ability. If their physical body is injured, engages in combat, uses a supernatural power on their end, or otherwise suffers a serious distraction, the use of this Merit ends.

                                    Ascension (ooooo)
                                    Prerequisite: Mythic Avatar, Supernatural Resistance 6+, Advanced Reach or Grasp 2
                                    Effect: The faith of such a massive Cult is sustaining to a Cult Leader who has invested in taking up the mantle of a god can truly become one; the character gains all the benefits of the Immortal Dread Power and stops aging. This functions differently for Deathless- they may grant themselves a Descent Reset by suppressing a Willpower dot, but suffer a Breaking Point with a penalty equal to the number of resets they granted themselves once they do Descend. Suppressed Willpower dots return when they lose a dot of Sekhem.
                                    Drawback: Though Immortal, they gain a Bane. Additionally, should the primary Cult (and not any Splinter Organizations, Operatives, etc.) lose its Advanced Reach or Grasp rating, the benefits of this Merit are suppressed and they begin to age normally again.

                                    (Author's Note: The following are all edited from Geist, except for Glorious Divinity, which is something of my own creation. These Merits explicitly stack with supernatural powers and natural capabilities, so enjoy making a seriously hulked-out character with Mythic Aspect and Mythic Avatar. The Mummy addition to the Ascension Merit was inspired by the Affinity "Speaking with the Master's Voice".)

                                    Symbolic Potency (ooo ; ooooo)
                                    Prerequisite: Mystery Cult Initiation (Equal or Greater value)
                                    Effect: The symbolism and teachings of the Cult have truly ingrained themselves on this particular character, making their use of supernatural abilities significantly more potent. At three dots, when incorporating suitable symbolism they gain the 8-again quality to the activation roll and regain a point of Supernatural Fuel on a success. At five dots, they gain the rote action quality to the activation roll and regain a point of Willpower and a point of Supernatural Fuel.

                                    (Author's Note: A little something to represent a dedicated mystical Cultist. A Mage with this Merit can really make the most of Order Yantras.)

                                        The Honorable Order of Soon To Be Renamed Hedge Sorcerers
                                        Concept: Some time ago, the founder of the organization made a Pact for power, both for themselves and for those who would follow them on their mission. All members of the organization swear into in and become signatories into this initial Pact, which grants them certain powers (Contracts) and responsibilities. Additional, specialized, initiations are encouraged, as is bargaining for additional power from approved sources (subject to internal review). Together they use Hedge Sorcery (Contracts) to accomplish the organization's goals.

                                        Needs: More established founder, a concrete Mission or Creed, the Pact spelled out, Cult structure, an actual name.

                                        [Hedge Sorcery (and Hedge Sorcerers): Mortals who beg, bargain, or steal access to Contracts (as seen in Changeling the Lost 2E). Lacking Glamour (usually) they activate them through their Catches. When Wyrd is required in an effect (but not the roll), treat them as if they had Wyrd 1, unless otherwise noted.]
