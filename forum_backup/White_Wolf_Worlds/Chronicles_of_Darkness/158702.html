    Advises on running Shadow Realm

    06-04-2014, 01:04 AM
    So my Wolf-Blooded players will hunt for Totem in our next session. That means they are going in to Shadow. I got cool idea of keyed Verge on the frozen waterfall, I readied all about ecology of Realm and interaction with spirits, and I get whole "Princes Mononoke meets Sailent Hill" vibe of Hisil. But still I can't run smoothly the Shadow in my mind, even if I am normally Mage ST and can come up with very interesting solutions is my mark.

    I think the problem I have is, compared to the Hedge from Changling, Shadow Realm can't be "edited" - that mean's the setting is putting it "it's mostly like Material World, only filled with monstrous spirits in place of humans". I just can't take out portions of game world I don't have cool ideas to run for, like I would in Changling, and on the other hand I don't have rough game map to make something on fly, like I was doing with Silent Hill game.

    Best indicates a problem my current situation - I have cool "portal" scene with frozen waterfall. I got disturbing and mysterious scene of first confrontation with would-be Totem. But I don't have ideas on 2-3 scenes of interaction and travel in between, so players would get they first taste of Shadow Realm, like in books. Any advises how to improve this?

              Here's my best advice on running things in the Shadow.

              1) Don't worry about the actual terrain as much as the impressionist terrain. Does the area look rocky and dangerous in the material realm? Then it probably is. Does it look like it might be filled with tranquil lakes and easy trails in the material realm? Then it probably is. The Shadow is a reflection of the impressions and feelings of the area. If no one is sure what's up there, then it might look more foreboding with dark areas due to the extreme tree cover, which also makes it a little claustrophobic.

              2) I usually treat the interactions with the spirits in the Shadow like an amusement park horror house, but instead of people in masks wandering around, they let loose a bunch of wild animals. The characters will wander thru stretches of general shadowy areas, then suddenly something will jump out at them, and it'll either disappear just as suddenly or go straight for the characters. The weaker (prey) spirits will jump thru and scamper back off into the darkness. The stronger (predator) spirits will see potential prey in the characters and go after them. A quick, ferocious, and bloody battle takes place and then the whole place gets quiet again. Too quiet. Like everybody's watching now.

              3) Not every spirit is awake. The majority of the spirits are just filling in the exact space that their material counterpart has. The tree spirits are there for the trees. The rock spirits are there as rocks. The wild sheep and rams spirits are just grazing on the grass spirits and the grass isn't trying to move away from them. Most of them are barely beyond motes. The dangerous ones are the ones who have started to stray from their material anchors. These are the ones who've been snacking on other spirits that may or may not be an acceptable part of it's diet. The owl spirit who's been dining on mice and rabbits is fine. The owl spirit feeding on darkness and flight spirits is just getting more specialized, but still not far off from where it started. The owl spirit who's been scooping up wolf spirits and tree spirits.......that thing ain't right!!

              4) While spirits are generally hostile to the Uratha, not all of them will outright attack them. Some of them are small enough and weak enough to know they don't stand a chance. So they run and they hide and they WATCH! Oh my goodness, the number of eyes staring out from the darkness in the Shadow is enough to make even the most level-headed turn paranoid. And what are they watching for? An opportunity. Maybe the characters know a way out to the material realm. Maybe something bigger will come along and rough them up enough to make them easy picking. Maybe something will just tear them apart and the others can take the scraps. While not every spirit will attack, they are still willing to become a predator (or scavenger) if the prey is weak enough.

                  The Shadow is a place of horror. It's the haunted house you have to enter, the recurring nightmare you're worried about dreaming every night, and occasionally it's the battlefield you're conscripted to fight on, against your will. The Shadow part of your territory might be different, but only insofar as it's a place of horror you're intimately familiar with.

                  Remember that the Hisil is a place of meaning, spirituality and, quite literally, a Shadow of the Flesh. Spirit encounters should never be random. You don't get to face some anger spirit, you'll face the embodied wrath of a teenager about to go on a killing spree in his school. The murder spirit trying to rip your guts out is an idealized reflection of that serial killer who slaughtered a two digit number of people in the city decades ago. The lust spirit trying to seduce you - it's the PC's own repressed feelings of sexuality, trying desperately to find a release.

                  Remember also that, most times, you can't meaningfully, permanently change the Shadow without also changing the Flesh. Spirits you tear apart will quite simply reform and come back to haunt you once they have gathered sufficient essence. Spirits are fought by changing the Flesh so that they lack appropriate sources of essence.



                  Don't rely on logic. Spirits aren't exactly a model of constancy. The interesting thing about the Shadow is that as soon as you draw a box around it and say "this is the Shadow, and this is what its inhabitants are like", it pokes a tentacle through the box and reminds you that you missed a spot. And if you follow that tentacle, you'll only discover that it goes on forever.

                  That's how the spirit realm works. It defies common sense. It doesn't have to operate according to rules that can be understood.

                              Try this - start with figuring out where the Bear's Graveyard is in relation to the Waterfall.

                              Is it up the mountain? Then they'll encounter more spirits related to higher elevations and what exists up there. More snow and cold and rocks, less animals and trees, the avalanche spirits love to eat wandering people in waves of snow and ice.

                              Is is across the glade in the hidden valley? Then they'll encounter more plant and animal spirits, negotiating with the mountain goats to cross the glen without a fight. The goats are very territorial and with ram the shit out of anyone who tries to just stroll across their glade.

                              Is it under the lake? Then they have to figure out how to convince the salmon spirits to take them down there. The salmon offer to have them ride in their mouths......yeah, that's not too obvious, is it?
