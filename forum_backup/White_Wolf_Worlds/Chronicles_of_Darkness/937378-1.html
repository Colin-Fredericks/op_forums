        I'm not trying to start an argument or anything: I agree that there are some downright silly elements in all of the gamelines, I've just always thought that the goofier elements juxtaposed with the dark stuff created a jarring, repulsive quality that I find amazing. Compare High School of the Dead and its buxom schoolgirl protagonists in a legitimately horrific, visceral situation: yeah, there's frankly stupid amounts of fanservice, but when scenes transition from fanservice to zombies tearing teenagers apart it feels so lurid that I feel guilty watching it.

        Granted, I'm a big fan of mood whiplash and fridge horror in my CofD.

        Speaking of which, that describes how I like to do things in my CofD. There aren't a lot of in-between situations in my games: I've had PCs go straight from tense survival-stealth sections to relaxed character moments to lethal combats without any sort of transition scenes in between. The characters should feel like their lives jump back and forth between pleasant and horrifying, and more often than not they should be the reason things are horrifying.

                To me, one of the central themes, probably the closest thing there is to a Grand Unifying Principle of the Supernatural is "there's always a cost". All power damages the user in some way, be it literally or through subtler means such as isolation. Growth is loss. To become an adult, you must lose the bliss and innocence of childhood. All change follows the same rules. Power, in all its forms, makes you less human. That's not to say that this is an objectively a bad thing. Human =\= good and inhuman =\= bad. But to truly, wisely use power, you have to be willing to make that trade. There's no such thing as a "higher" being, only parallel beings. Mages are not "higher" than normal humans, they are parallel: they paid a cost in humanity for their power. This isn't a personal philosophy of mine in general, it's just something I think is necessary for a horror setting.

                About splats, I think anyone, Onyx Path or fan, who wants to make a new splat should always ask two questions before they do:

                1. Is this a recognizable type of monster or horror archetype, and 2. Does it deserve more representation in ChroD than it already has?

                About the setting itself, I think others have described it better and more eloquently, but I generally see it as a place with more crime and corruption and disaster than our world, but in a subtler way than the apocalyptic oWoD. Lots of crimes go unreported or ignored, lots of people disappear. People recognize on some unconscious level how tenuous everything is, and this is what keeps most people from investigating strange events or inconsistencies. Everyone has had some sort of brush, however brief, with the paranormal, but most forget about it and most of the others tell themselves that it had to have a rational explanation, even if they don't know what it is. A few believe this, but a lot of them tell themselves that to cover up the real reason why they never tried to pry into it.

                    In my CofD games, the multiverse is very Cthulhu-Mythos-like in that the Exarchs didn't oust benevolent Old Gods: the Old Gods were always monsters. Maybe not evil, per se, but still monstrous and grossly indifferent to human welfare. And the Fallen World is little better: the Shadow Realm is full of monomaniacal spirits that are slaves to their natures (even Luna and Helios) and so cannot demonstrate qualities they do not already embody.

                    Goodness is something human. It's a treasure our species has, that the gods don't even know they're lacking.

                                                    Sorry if this counts as thread necromancy, but I had some thoughts I wanted to share.

                                                    Fundamentally, I play the World of Darkness as a World Half-Full.
                                                    There is fear and suffering in abundance, with secrets and shadows so deep that -nobody- will ever learn all of it. It is a large and terrifying world, where people suffer even more than in our own. But there is hope.
                                                    There is hope that if people work together and work hard, things may get better. That through determination and struggle, the world will become a better place. It could get better, or fall further still. The hope exists to make the horror even worse and the horror exists to make the hope brighter. There is a catharsis in that conflict. There is worth and value in this fallen world, but it's a struggle to keep it alive.

                                                    I tend to view it more as exceedingly dark fantasy, then allow the horror to naturally emerge. The world is too large to be confined to the genre of horror alone.
