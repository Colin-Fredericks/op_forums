                Thematically Beasts and the God Machine are two different types of horror.

                Beasts are anthropocentric. They exist because of humanity's collective fears and live off causing humanity to feel fear. Humanity may be their prey but in a way it shows how immensely important humans are. They are something "special" that causes monsters to come knocking at their doors.

                The God Machine on the other hand is the Lovecraftian cosmic horror that inspires fear precisely because it shows humanity how unimportant and insignificant they are. The God Machine doesn't care about humanity other than for its usefulness, but the same could be said about G-M's views towards other resources like metal.

                                    Actually, I thought the oil and water part worked perfectly. Beast and Demons are very much like oil and water. What doesn't work, which was my point, was the idea that Beasts are something "natural" and Demons are something "artificial." Oils occur naturally too. But more to the point the machine imagery in Demon isn't really serving the purpose of drawing a distinction between what is natural and what is artificial. Demon is a allegory for the system that the universe seems to operate by. As such I interpret the machine imagery as serving as a metaphor for the uncaringness of that system (and certainly you're free to YMMV as you like). Wild animals and other natural things also have a certain uncaringness, especially with regard to human beings. Seen in this light, the natural/artificial portion of Nyx's mixed metaphor doesn't work because whether they're natural or artificial, neither Demons nor Beasts care overly much about human beings. This is a property they share in common, regardless of their supernatural origin or the imagery used.

                                                            For me, the answer to the original question is spelled out on page 170 of Chronicles of Darkness corebook (dunno which page it is in the previous GMC book):

                                                            "No human mythology ever conceived of a god so alien to the mortal mind."

                                                            Monsters have fired up human imagination pretty much ever since the dawn of our species. For as long as we've existed, we've been making offerings to placate the hungry dead, praying to gods, spirits and demons for protection or mercy, avoiding the magical groves where the Fair Folk might dwell, looking up to the priests, magi and assorted mystics wielding supernatural powers.

                                                            Chronicles of Darkness is a game where all these ancient fears have actual basis in reality, even if the stories passed may get a ton of details wrong. Gods (spirits) exist, so do the hungry dead of both material and immaterial variety. Mad Fae can indeed abduct you, you can become a target of a werewolf hunt, get caught up in the intrigues of mages or chance upon pretty much any other creature from assorted mythologies, folktales and legends.

                                                            Then, there's the God-Machine, a being with no semblance to any known horrors of yore. Maybe it's because it didn't exist until recently. Maybe it's simply been more careful, better hidden than the other monsters lurking in the dark. Maybe it's because just a couple decades ago, our knowledge of the universe was too unrefined to comprehend the terror of the God-Machine, even if we could glimpse it. Whatever the reason, God-Machine simply isn't something our ancestors feared, had nightmares and told cautionary tales about, and as such, isn't a part of the Primordial Dream either.

                                                            Maybe it'll change over time. Computers dictating the course of our lives are a modern fear, but one that gradually gains basis in reality (just ask anyone who got screwed over by a credit history he didn't even know he had). Conspiracy theories and stories about weird things found in the deep web are quickly becoming the modern folktales. Maybe over time enough knowledge of the God-Machine will trickle down into our species' memory that he, too, will become part of the Primordial Dream, and the Begotten of the future will welcome his angels and demons as kin thanks to that.

                                                            Then again, maybe it's just the God-Machine's nature is so fundamentally different, it'll never be able to tickle our lizard brain the same way a vampire or werewolf does.
