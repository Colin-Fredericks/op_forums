            Originally posted by The Kings Raven
            Not all mutations are cool powers. Hybrids must take Genetic Conditions to represent harmful side effects of their Mutations. A Hybrid takes one per depth, a Lahmasu takes one at Depth 1, 3 and 5.
            This was pretty much just the house-rule I made regarding defective mutations.

            Regarding mutations, there should be at least a few that are more oddball or circumstantial, for story/scene purposes. Maybe one that allows the Hybrid to secrete addictive chemicals (like the "heroin pissing dinosaurs" I've heard about in Exalted). Gives the referenced corporations an obvious non-combat motive for using/hunting Hybrids.

            Other ideas:
            • Hypnosis-based mental manipulation. Possibly using a combination of psychic, audio, and scent inputs. For implanting commands. Possibly illusions. "No, I don't look at all like a fish-man, sir."
            • Pheromone-based emotional manipulation. More subtle, but less controlled. Mostly social bonuses (but including Intimidation).
            • Would something that essentially grants a scaling Influence Water be appropriate for Elements? Using Ichor instead of Essence? (The spending cap on Ichor limits it's combat applicability, but allows for the reclusive frog-shaman to do freaky things with water)
            • Some kind of minion spawning? Based on Womb of Terrors (but obviously greatly reduced in power), or corrupting other wildlife? Allows for a minion master, or brood queen hybrid.
            • Some psychic powers requiring the Hybrid to be dreaming, to mirror those that Leviathans possess. (Though that could just be flavor or a drawback applied to other psychic abilities)
            • More like Leech's Hunger that allow for alternate Ichor gain. Destruction based (inferring that this Hybrid possesses more of the Bestial nature of their forebearers than their peers), or "purpose" based (same, but for the Divine part of their heritage)?


            Thinking more on the proposed temporary mutation ability, it could easily be used as a combined buffing/debuffing/healing ability if it grants (or modifies) the Hybrid template and selected Mutations. Buff? Give the target combat powers. Debuff? Give them bad Genetic Conditions. Healing? Give the target a healing/regen mutation and have them heal their own self.

            Mechanics: Successes - add/change/subtract 1 dot of Mutations (positive or negative), extend duration 1 interval/step? Aggravated damage per Depth when it wears off, with Lethal damage dependent on a resistance roll (or the number of rolls made for the initial transformation)?

            (need to work on mechanical balance...)

                    I felt like doing Womb of Horrors - it's now in the updated Channels document and it's a doozy. Aside from being extreemly long. With the obvious shout out Adaptation "Heart of the Swarm" a Sheol 10 Leviathan can create 1000 minions per 4 Ichor, and if those minions eat 4 people it regains 4 Ichor.

                    Powerful, but surprisingly counterable. A swarm of minions can't stop mortals from bombing the Leviathan to smithereens and they're not very powerful - to the point that a regular military grade chaingun will probably kill dozens of them every turn.

                    And that's against regular humans. A Genius with Katastrofi 5 can build a device that blows up every Leviathan minion in 10 miles without hurting anyone else or a 10 mile wide force field that requires minions to roll at a maximum of -2 dice (-7 to -10 dice would be easy) if they want to break through. A Dramatic Failure just fries them and I'd rule that you get auto dramatic failures at -5 dice.


                    So basically it's the usual. A Leviathan against an unprepared city could wipe it from the map without trouble. A Leviathan against a prepared foe with the power of Science! is doomed and the only question is how much damage it inflicts before it's inevitable end. A prepared military base might even withstand the assault. A Marduk base will just ward off the entire swarm with the Sigil. Well, unless mortal Cultists can clear a way in.

                    Incubation of the Second Self is there now. It's just a few Adaptations though.

                                            I'm not sure how this turned out but the first sample character. Well half charachter, half hints for how to play those Gugal underwater cities that have been mentioned a couple of times.

                                            One thing I'm determined is that 2E will actually have reasons to go underwater - and putting cities full of people there means that what happens matters rather than underwater just being a place you go to look for that one specific thing then go back to the "real world".

                                            So:


                                            Maxwell Clarke, Agent of Atlantis

                                            The oceans cover 70% of the planet, in this boundless hidden space dwell countless hidden communities of Hybrid. Most are villages or nomadic tribes with bronze age technology and their Mutations. Others are thriving city states or nations inhabited by the Gugal, the only Lahmasu Strain able to create genetically stable populations.

                                            Maxwell Clarke hails from one of these thriving city states. One of many underwater nations named Atlantis after the mythical sunken city. Maxwell's Atlantis was formed by the children of Geoffrey Clarke; a Leviathan born in the hight of the British Empire who's plans to travel to the colonies and live like the worst sort of imperial oppressor were cut short; but not so short that he didn't have time to sire dozens of children.

                                            After Geoffrey died and his children climbed free of his Wake they quickly concluded that Geoffrey was: Firstly, an evil tyrant who had made enemies of both his fellow British and the local population. Secondly, the product of a deeply racist society which incidentally he had frequently introduced his to obviously inhuman children. Thirdly, too dead to protect his children. Fourthly, in possession of a well stocked library detailing how to found a colony. With these facts in mind Geoffrey's children wasted no time in vanishing beneath the waves and founding their own colony. After a few centuries of all the usual drama of national politics and avoiding Bahamut's hereditary curse of stagnation, Atlantis still stands, still embroiled in all the usual drama of national politics.

                                            And this is where Maxwell comes in.

                                            A nation state needs it's spies, and Maxwell is one of Atlantis' best. A drop of Hemitheos blood, not enough to make him a Hybrid, set a lust for exploration and adventure within the young Gugal. After being caught trying to leave city limits without a permit Maxwell's parents were encouraged to find an outlet and after trying various options Maxwell chose the intelligence corps for the opportunity to see the surface first hand.

                                            He did not distinguish himself in a single bold heroic action, nor did he save his mentor's life or dramatically upstage him. Maxwell simply displayed a natural comfort with both surface culture and two dimensional life before steadily rising through the ranks on the back of consistent solid performances. Today he is a highly respected secret agent of Atlantis.

                                            Appearance

                                            Maxwell doesn't look even remotely human. He is a greyish green colour, save for a white belly, with slippery and slimy fish like skin except for a scaly back. His head is that of a fish with bulging, unblinking eyes, and palpating gills at the side of his neck. His hands and feet are webbed. A discription that applies to hust about everyone in his home city.

                                            When travelling on the surface Maxwell wears a glamour to conceal his identity and may take any appearance with roughly his size or shape – from a regular human to curling up and disguising himself as a rock. This disguise would not stand up to a Leviathan's Channels.

                                            Storytelling Hints

                                            Atlantis has definitely avoided all contact with Leviathans and if Maxwell identifies one he is unlikely to seek confrontation – however if he believes this Leviathan is a threat to Atlantis he will go to great lengths to destroy or distract it by provoking a conflict between the Leviathan and human society.

                                            Maxwell is also a proactive character who is always at work promoting Atlantis' interests and these may conflict with the players. Players might seeking to keep the Tribe hidden might discover that Maxwell has been trading Tribal sorcery for favours or players seeking to recruit Lahmasu into their cult might find they've disappeared beneath the waves to make a new life among “their own kind”. Like many Gugal cities Atlantis' population become closer to outright genetic clones each generation and the city has implemented a breeding program to introduce new abilities and remove defects into the population before the genetic template is finalised. Thus one of Maxwell's primary duties is to attract immigrants or acquire sperm and egg samples. Maxwell is particularly known for recruiting from "care homes" for Hybrids and isn't above a little revenge if he thinks the residents have been mistreated; especially if they're fellow Gugal.

                                            In terms of Resources Maxwell is limited. Atlantis is a magic society first and foremost. Technologically they resemble the western Roman Empire at it's peak but with drastic exceptions, for example their cloning and genetic technology is world class because they brought it from another underwater city who in turn adapted surface technology for underwater (and came up with some nifty discoveries when a few of their scientists went mad at the right moment).

                                            Magically Atlantis can do most things that WW1 era technology can do, often in a smaller and more convenient package. This is just a generalisation in some areas such as Maxwell's glamour they exceed modern technology. In other areas they lag far behind, most notable in the ability to mass produce anything.
