    A Mage in the Hedge, What Happens?

    07-28-2014, 02:49 AM
    Okay, so I've been reading up a bit on CtL. Before, I read about MtAw, and because of that my brain decided to mash the lore of both games for a bit because of one thing: Arcadia.

    Correct me if I'm wrong, but I think the Arcadia in CtL is the same Arcadia in MtAw. I mean, it's all part of the World of Darkness, afterall. Well, Arcadia in MtAw is one of the Supernal Realms, meaning that if a mage managed to get into Arcadia, they would ascend, right? Obviously, this be very troublesome for Fae/Exarchs/anyone on the mage's blacklist. Basically, pretty game-breaking stuff.

    But then there's the Hedge, the maze between Arcadia and the Fallen World. So, if it's between the Supernal and Fallen Worlds, could the Hedge be a manifestation of the Abyss? Like, a part of the Abyss that the Fae decided to create so they may take humans away as servants? It does seem to have a natural ability to turn any human treading there insane(though to be fair, that could just be the simple alien nature of the Hedge, not that it's a property of the Abyss if it is even that).

    So here's what I'm really getting at: The Hedge is part of the Abyss that the Fae constructed to kidnap humans. However, being the Abyss, it is the natural bane of humanity and any human, even a Sleeper, can't last very long before being consumed by the Abyss. The Fae could only simply slow the consumption, giving them enough time to nab a human and make them a slave. Thing is, while the Abyss gobbles up humans in general, it's especially keen to chew on the Awakened. So while a Sleeper has enough time to run through the Hedge from Arcadia back to their home(how else could Changelings come back?), the same cannot be said for the Awakened. In essence, the Hedge is like a meat-grinder for mages, they're gonna get gobbled up in ten seconds flat.

    Would any of this be or could be true? I dunno, there's probably something I'm missing here, or maybe I'm allowed to interpret it that way, it's just not official canon. If so, maybe there could be/are already mechanics made for this. Like, the greater a mage's Gnosis, the quicker the Abyss consumes them(in keeping with the whole greater power, greater risk irony of Gnosis).

    At the end of the day, this is simply my mind wandering as it does. What are your thoughts about all this?

                      Originally posted by crapcarp
                      A Mage in the Hedge, What Happens?
                      Adventure. Adventure happens.

                      However, I am going to chime in on things with one caveat: Nothing I say dictates your stories, this is a game and you can interpret any part of it how you want. Ok? Let's begin.

                      So let's assume Fae Arcadia is Supernal Arcadia (I don't think so, but let's go with it for the sake of argument and story-telling) and the travel between the Fallen World and the Supernal World is best represented by movement in cartesian space rather than a metaphysical shift of existence (i.e., you get there by putting one foot in front of the other ad infinitum rather than phase-shifting). The point about the Fae constructing the Hedge is also in my head-canon.

                      My issue with saying that part of the Abyss was rewoven to specifically provide a conduit for kidnapping humans is that it seems slightly hubristic to look at the part of what's going on that directly affects humanity as the one and only reason for it happening. It's rather limiting to have absolutely alien and powerful entities focusing so much on an entire world to simply spirit away some here and there. I've always thought collecting servants was an after-thought, myself. There are two reasons to build a hedge/fence/barrier; either to keep something in, or to keep something out. In my head-canon the Fae built the Hedge to keep the God-Machine out of their Arcadia, but that's harkening back to C:tD's metaplot of banality vs. creativity.

                      But back to the Mage in the Hedge.

                      Firstly, the Hedge isn't Arcadia. It's the passageway there. Secondly, Gnosis is knowledge of the Supernal Truth and to some extent the shaping of one's soul to better channel it (though really it's Legacies that are soul-sculpting), so if anything having a higher Gnosis would making being in/on the way to the Supernal easier. Why do they not get altered into Changelings by being in the Hedge? They were already changed by their first trip to Arcadia. The difference is that they attuned themselves to the Watchtower, which provides the connection through which they cast Awakened magic. (This is also after a couple assumptions of Mage metaphysics that I don't hold in my own games, but they're the easiest explanations that don't change the outcome, since this is the Changeling board.)

                      Do the Fae "slow down the Abyss' corruption"? I would think they have far better things to do. However, would they want higher Gnosis Mages, since they don't need to follow any of those pesky Contracts, with their Clauses and Catches.

                      Now let's say the Mage actually makes it to true Arcadia through the Hedge. Their first Awakening happened when they attuned themselves to a Watchtower, so it might make sense to visit there again. Good luck finding it. Other mages might try to claim territory, use the Supernal Denizens (Fae/Hobs/etc.) to their advantage, and politick their way into power. Good luck with that.

                      Well, I've rambled enough. Any of that help?

                      Once is happenstance. Twice is coincidence. Three times is enemy action. Four times is Infrastructure.

                      Rule #2: Never let the game-mechanics get in the way of a good story.

                          Paddon, I personally am a fan of the banality vs.creativity theory for why the Hedge exists since it would also explain why the Gentry must abide by a strict set of rules, lower power and restricted form, and cannot exist indefinitely within the mortal world unless shielded by the Wyrd. However I digress.

                          As for the Hedge is a part/reflection of the Abyss theory crapcarp I'm more inclined to believe that it's not. The Abyss warps and corrupts those that come into contact with it and while it may appear that the Hedge does the same another key part of what makes the Hedge unique is that it can be manipulated by those with strong ties to the Wyrd, which in tern is strongly associated with Arcadia and the Skein (Changeling iteration of Astral Realms). The Abyss hungers for all equally and if the Hedge was an aspect of it I feel that it wouldn't have any favoritism for those who has high Wyrd score. Another key part of the Hedge is that it's a source of Glamour for a savvy Changeling, in contrast the Abyss never give away anything without the gift being a bane in disguise. I feel the fact that Goblin Fruits, let alone Hobgoblins themselves, thrive within the Hedge is damning evidence that the Abyss is related to the Hedge in any way.

                          That said though I am of the camp that believes Changeling Arcadia and Mage Arcadia are the same realm. I must admit this is mostly because I like how the nWoD universe directly contradicts itself in certain parts so that it gives room for Storytellers to get creative and adds the realism that the universe is so big that nobody really knows how everything fits together, if everything did fit together that is, but I have some evidence that may lend credence to this belief. First off the True Fae don't really use Contracts as we know them in Arcadia. They do use the deals they made the many elements and concepts that make up Faerie but they aren't mechanically similar to any powers Changelings will ever know. The power the Fae have over their own realm is so absolute that it more similarly resembles the Awakened's Arcana than the Lost's Contracts, while outside of Arcadia they're far more restricted by what their Contracts entail. That's why it's even possible to combat the Gentry outside of Arcadia, however unlikely success may be. And secondly Awakened magic works by utilizing the sympathetic connection a Mage has to their Path's realm. Rather than just willing something into being a Mage literally changes the laws of reality to reflect the realm of their Watchtower. This means that when an Acanthus uses a spell they're basically making the Fallen World reflect the laws of Arcadia so that they can physically accomplish their desired goal. This, if true, would mean that the Mage would technically be using the ancient Contracts that the Gentry enjoy within their own domains within Arcadia. This could be plausible because the Gentry, and to a lesser extent every other fae, must concede to the natural laws of the Fallen World by utilizing only certain Clauses of their mighty Contracts while outside of Arcadia but are completely exempt from Paradox since they're essentially using "loopholes" within the already existing natural laws that are sanctioned by the Lie, while a Mage on the other hand directly changes those same natural laws and briefly replaces them with the Supernal laws of Acradia and can therefore utilize the full extent of the Gentries Contracts, so long as they have the proper knowledge (Arcana) to utilize such Contracts, but by doing so violate the natural laws sanctioned by the Lie and invoke Disbelief in all Sleepers who witness Vulgar displays of such breaches of the physical laws. In a delightfully twisted way each time an Acanthus invokes a Paradox they're being punished by the Lie in a similar way to how a Changeling might be punished by the Wyrd for breaking a Pledge.

                                  Well Nyrufa,

                                  You have exactly the right idea when you say that the WoD series can either be played as it's own separate universe. My favorite part about this dynamic is the rare moment when characters from different game lines get to talk together about the unique differences in their own cosmology and try to piece together what's really going on. There are a few ideas that I really like, and since this is the Changeling Forum I'll keep these more to the Changeling perspective. Please enjoy, .

                                  The True Fae are the Exarchs: Or at least the Exarchs of Arcadia. The Seers of the Throne already structure their hierarchy around privilege and personal struggle as opposed to cooperation and self-exploration. It wouldn't be so hard for a Changeling looking in to see the equivalent of Mage Loyalists when they see the gross cruelty and subtle metamorphosis of Acanthus Seers. Especially when it comes to their treatment of normal humans. Changelings may not be above manipulating mortals with Contracts but using magic to change the very nature of a person into something that they can use and exploit like a common house tool is just...way to much like the Others for comfort.

                                  The True Fae are at war with the Exarchs: Many Changeling were taken to be soldiers in the wars of the Gentry. Since the nature of a Changeling's durance is purposefully disorienting and hard to recall it could be seen by a Mage as the last lingering effects of the Quiescence upon a Changeling. Perhaps clouding their memory of facing off against the servants of the Exarchs in the sections of Arcadia that are under their control. This could also be another reason why the Gentry capture humans, to rob the Exarchs of future soldier and resources and then turn them against the intruders. Whether this revelation would shed positive light on the Gentry or the Exarchs is questionable, but it could lead to an interesting alliance between a Freehold and a Seer Pylon.

                                  The Exarchs are not as influential as Mages believe: Who gives a flying crap about some Ascension War! After everything a changeling's experienced at the hands of their Keeper the idea of going back to being a normal, ordinary, oblivious human would be a dream come true. It would mean that they wouldn't be Lost any more. Perhaps the notion that the Oracles and Exarchs are having a Highlander style war in the heavens to decide the fate of the universe is another sign of a Mage's hubris. For the Lost they might see the conflict for what it really is: A bunch of greedy humans with near god-like powers fighting over the idea of a perfect world rather than fighting for one. After all, if the Oracles want people to Awaken and the Exarchs want the world to stay ignorantly asleep then...why don't they lift even a finger to stop the Gentry from stealing their lives?!?

                                  There are no Exarchs or Oracles: Changeling are intimately familiar with sanity and the effects otherworldly power have on a human's Clarity. It wouldn't take long for a Changeling who's listened to a Mage's account about how the universe is set up and about the secret history of the world to realize that they might be farther down the rabbit hole than most Changelings are. It's no lie that a Mage draws their power from somewhere, but come on. Noble Atlantean Kings? Sinister Exarchs? 5 Supernal Realms that just so happen to encompass and influence all existence, in which Arcadia, god forsaken Faerie itself, is one such place? No, that's crazy? That's pure madness. These Mage's are just trying to assign iconography and meaning to self justify their own desire to become gods. Stay away from then, nothing good can come from being around someone who actively want to go to Arcadia.

                                  The True Fae are in league with the Exarchs: It make perfect sense if you really think about it. If Arcadia is supposed to be a place of unbridled imagination and glamour then why is it so decidedly sinister and tyrannical? Simple, all of the benevolent (though still completely insane) Fae were either slain, enslaved, or forced into hiding by the nightmare Fae. And how could they do that? With help, that's how. The one's who would become the True Fae of today, perhaps long ago called the Fomorians, long ago made pledges with the would be Tyrants of reality and secured dominance over their own realm. In exchange the True Fae would supply the Exarchs armies with creatures of glamour and slaves taken from other realms, and in turn the Exarchs would aid in the construction of a passage to bypass the Abyss so their allies could steal away those destined by Fate to Awaken or oppose the designed of the Exarchs. If this is to be believed then the Lost and the Pentacle Mages are really brothers in arms against the tyrants of the supernal on two separate fronts, perhaps a Freehold/Consili alliance could turn the tide of war in their mutual favor.

                                                      Okay, well I've been reading up a few books that were mentioned in this forum, especially Equinox Road, and the conclusion I've come too about all this is merely my own head-canon. This is because there really is no official canon at all, just a few laws of the world and several ideas to work with. With that outta the way, on to my head-canon:

                                                      Supernal Arcadia and Fae Arcadia are one and the same. I personally like this because to me it gives the world a sense of cohesion, like everything's all a part of the World of Darkness. However, mages who manage to enter Arcadia through the Hedge aren't gonna ascend. This is because Arcadia is simply one realm of the Supernal, not all of them, and you need to ascend yourself to the entire Supernal world. And that's assuming a mage can enter Arcadia in the first place.

                                                      Thing is, mages can't actually enter Arcadia on their own because it doesn't agree to grant them entrance, there's no Contract for them. And yet, the Hedge can let them in, and Arcadia lets ordinary humans in, too. How is this so?

                                                      Because the Oracle who erected Ancanthus made a Contract with Arcadia and the True Fae long ago. At first, the True Fae could do nothing to humanity, for there was no Contract. Humans didn't even know about the True Fae, so how could a Contract be formed? The Oracle made the Contract that allows the True Fae to take humans and do as they please with them and the Oracle would even deliver them to the True Fae as well(through the Path). In exchange, the Oracle could create a space to erect the Watchtower. But the Oracle was very clever and managed to trick the True Fae.

                                                      Part of the exact wording of the Contract was that the True Fae were promised "the souls unworthy of Awakening". However, those unworthy souls who went back to Sleep immediately left Arcadia, something the Oracle didn't tell the True Fae about the nature of Awakening. Angered, the True Fae are now hostile towards all of humanity, and utilize the Contract to make slaves of those unworthy souls(humans are very useful for feeding the True Fae anyways). But this doesn't mean the True Fae are gonna tolerate mages lying down(that just ain't the True Fae's style, y'know?).

                                                      No, the True Fae are clever and deceptive. They also know that mages are a nosy lot, humans generally are, but mages much more so. The True Fae take advantage of this by creating the Hedge. The Hedge grants humans entrance in exchange for the Thorns allowing to cut their souls away should they brush up against them. This makes a lot of sense because mages need their souls to use magic. Without it, they're in the same boat as ordinary humans. The mage is cold, alone, and going insane by being utterly demoralized because not only is their soul gone, but so is their magic. At that point, the mage will be desperate and stupid enough to reach out to anyone who promises to bring their magic back. Thus, the True Fae can create a Contract that gives them magic, but it's not quite the same, and the price was servitude.

                                                      Another way is what results in the Unmade. A smart mage who has studied the dangers of the Hedge, knows of the wide and narrow paths to get to Arcadia, but didn't find out the most essential secret for getting in: a Contract to get into Arcadia in the first place. So a mage prepares their spells for the trip, goes down the path, and then doesn't see an entrance. They try every spell they know and might even improvise a few spells in a desperate attempt to enter, but nothing will work because Arcadia simply doesn't agree to let them in. Frustrated to no end, the mage is now vulnerable, but in a different way. A True Fae then promises entrance to Arcadia, and the mage agrees to a fair price because this smart mage knows how the True Fae really are, so they're cautious about that Contract as a result. Problem is, the reason Arcadia doesn't let mages in is because there are no Contracts for them to exist there in the first place, the new Contract merely grants them entrance. So the mage has to self-cannibalize their mind, body, and soul to cling to life in a reality that doesn't agree to give them substance(remember, the original Contract grants substance to those who are unworthy/haven't Awakened yet, but once a human does Awaken, all bets are off), resulting in an Unmade.

                                                      But those are just two of the infinite amount of ways of dealing with mages, and the True Fae take advantage of all of them because that's what the True Fae do best. I just listed these two because I figure these are simply the most popular ways as they're real nasty ways for a mage to go, and the True Fae want to cause as much pain and suffering to these nosy bastards out of spite and revenge. So it's like Paddon said:

                                                      It's just a particularly perilous one for mages, and that just makes things all the more fun. Thanks for replying everyone, I really appreciate it!
