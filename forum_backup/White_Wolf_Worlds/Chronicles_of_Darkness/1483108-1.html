    Dwarven Sub-Race: Subterranean

    It's common for most Dwarves to live within the Earth in some form or fashion, but the Subterraneans take it to an extreme. They view the Earth as being the only point of safety in an ever-changing world, and so they spend most of their lives underground, safe from the rain, snow or scorching rays of the sun. The Earth is a trusted friend, a protector against outsiders and chaos. At the same time, one cannot truly survive on what lichen grows inside the cavern walls, and so at the entrance to their caves the Subterraneans set up trading posts and inns. Generally, any person is welcome to stay at a Subterranean entrance Inn for free the first night, and this is because of the Cavern-dweller's view on Perseverance. In order to secure the deals needed for you to survive, you need to have some level of trust with the person you are dealing with, otherwise negotiations will just fall through. Subterraneans are willing to endure even deprivation to feed and clothe someone if that will earn them their trust, because without that trust, there is no trade, and without trade they are left with whatever mushrooms they can scrounge up beneath stone. As such, the Cavern-dwellers view bartering as the greatest form of Craftsmanship, above any individual art. Despite this need for trade, the Cavern-dwellers rarely exchange goods amongst themselves, as they generally don't have the needed outside goods that their kind looks for. Ironically, a Subterranean is likely to have an easier time trusting other races than their own kind because of competition for these resources. Thus, the Cavern-dwellers have no form of government outside of their own Families, acting much like a wild pack of wolves, with the adult children often leaving to find their own fortune.

    Relation to the Paths

    Mastigos (Main): With a simple glance at another person's mind, it doesn't take much to find out if the person at the other end of the bargaining table views you in a favorable light or not. While most Cavern-dwelling Mastigos prefer to earn their trust the old-fashioned way, in desperate times they may sometimes force a connection with others or to make a slight change in their mind to acquire the resources they need. Pragmatism must ultimately fill in the gaps left behind by idealistic practices.

    Acanthus: The Acanthus often find themselves as arbiters over deals, making sure that the deals being made are honored by the spirit, not the word. They also are the only Path of Cavern-Dwellers to often leave the underground behind, making a trade as salesmen, lawyers, or both. This is because they are able to trust in their own luck and ability to see the future to get them through what would normally be devastating circumstances for anyone else.

    Moros: The outside world has landlords, and so too does the underground. These Cavern-dwellers use their mystical senses to detect precious gems and metals within their holdings, and then rent out these spaces as mines to merchants, kings and other nobility. With their own ability to change matter to suit their needs, they rarely find much use for these goods except as currency for outsiders. Among all the Dwarves, the Subterraneans deal with ghosts the least and give the same amount of respect, often just banishing or killing any ghosts in their lands. After all, the dead cannot produce things for the living, in their eyes.

    Obrimos: The Aether's chosen mostly work as hired muscle for other Subterraneans, though they generally prefer the term "Private Detective." The Obrimos will enter someone's territory, interrogate them about their activities, and if found lying may make an example out of them through violence. It's difficult to fight back against them, as they protect themselves with anti-magic shields and runes that slow down kinetic force. Amongst the Paths, the Obrimos are the least-liked and, ironically considering their abilities, the least trusted.

    Thyrsus (Unfamiliar): In comparison, the Shamans amongst the Cavern-dwellers tend to be the most trusted, albeit the most rare. They are able to provide a vital service as wandering doctors, but can also double as entertainers by working together with spirits to put on fantastical displays. A Thyrsus, when they can be found, will often stay at another Subterranean's inn, working as entertainment to bring in customers in exchange for a place to sleep and food for themselves. In a pinch, Masters of the art can also change or conjure up plants to be eaten, though the taste can sometimes leave things to be desired.

    %\%\%\%\%
    AN: So, I know I kind of overlapped some things from previous entries with this one, but I felt that it worked here. I didn't want to do a more stereotypical criminal thing with the counterpart to the Grey Dwarves, so I instead went with a more pioneer-esque life style that required a lot of bartering to work properly. The idea of Thyrsus as entertainers I drew from the Gleemen from Wheel of Time, since I wanted something interesting besides just "healer."

    In any case, next time I'll be working on the Orcs! My plan for the races is Dwarves (which I just finished), Orcs (since I have the clearest vision of them next), Elementals, Elves (Philosophies can be difficult to come up with) and Humans (Who I'm still trying to work into the world in a satisfactory way.)

        Wouldn't Thyrsus dwarves focus on combining craftsmanship and biology ? If you modify the right animals with inheritable changes with Transform Life, you can accumulate changes over generations. Animals adapted to survive longer underground, digging better, adapting to the dangers of mining and more. Plants made more nutritious, able to reproduce faster or give out bioluminescence, amongst many useful features. Houses and estates grown over years, each generation adding their own crafting genius to the design, an adaptable and self healing living home, like the towers of the Telvanni.

        Spirits can do anything, so a Thyrsus who knows how to manipulate or bargain with them can create ample stocks of talens, providing a supply of more easily available supernatural consumables.

        The most skilled and veteran ones create fetishes, some workshops containing wonders that can enhance any profession. Because their power supply is more easily available than mana, these can become just as valuable as imbued items. Indeed, one of the ancestral duties of this Path could be to cultivate the right Resonances, balancing out what is best to dwarven society, what is currently needed to recharge fetishes and what will be needed to make the technospiritual innovations of the future.

        So they would theoretically have a good way to synergize with subterranean dwarven culture.
