                        Ok, here are some things you need to know about what I've been using and what I haven't (obviously I have no feedback for the stuff I haven't used yet) before we begin.
                        I have been using the classes, and skill tricks as you suggested.
                        I've used your merits except for any item creation ones. I do something different for that.

                        I have not touched or even looked at race, since I use a different variant for that in my chronicle.
                        I have not used alignment at all, nor even read that section.

                        All-in-all when comparing them to a typical splat (vampire, changeling, etc) the big thing I've noticed is spellcaster-specific. When you are a vampire and you want to use a power, you spend blood. Then you go eat, so you get some blood back. Then you have full blood. (Simplified, I know, but it's the point I'm trying to make here.) When you are, say, a wizard, you cast your spells. Then you wait until tomorrow. I think the spellcasters could do with some way of getting their "mana/spellslots/what-have-you" back that wasn't just "go to sleep." World of Darkness as a whole tends to avoid the "per day" thing and I think it's a point in their favor for it. Each class could even be different if you wanted (I.E. Bards have to socialize, similar to a changeling gleaning glamour, Wizards study, Clerics pray, Druids tromp around in the woods, etc). I know my players often take breaks in the middle of whatever they are doing to feed/whatever and come back at an issue at full strength. These classes would just have to wait until tomorrow.

                        I've also noticed that the "template" as a whole is a little underwhelming when compared to the other splats. (Maybe the racial stuff would fix this, I will take a look at that.) One of the things I've always noticed about the Dark Hero from Mirrors is that it's missing a lot of "cool things" that simply being one of the other things gives you. (for example: a vampire doesn't breath, so gas effects won't work on them. Most poisons don't affect vampires or mummies. Prometheans can eat almost anything and go for days without sleep, etc) It's a lot of little things I guess, but if you are intending to be "dungeon delving" and more "heroic fantasy" in your chronicle, it's certainly stuff that has great advantage.

                        Now, onto the classes.
                        As a whole, most of the spellcasters are pretty useful, cool. They are things I would try out (and have, as NPCs for some of them.) So I don't have a problem with your spellcasting system overall aside from the energy recharging I mentioned above. I will list out the individual class in order and my thoughts/suggestions on them.
                        Bard: This one has strong flavor and overall works well. A couple of notes/questions though. If a bard has to maintain concentration (which implies she needs to keep singing) to fascinate how does she talk to people to benefit from the +2 to presence rolls when talking to people? Inspire competence is a little underwhelming. I have to use one of my uses of music for the day to give an ally a +1 to something? Why don't I just teamwork instead and give her my successes? Chances are, even untrained, I'm going to give her a +1 without resorting to magic.

                        Sorcerer: I've only had one so far and only at the second level. But so far worked great.

                        Wizard: This is probably more of an issue with your item creation merits than scribe scroll but we're talking about the wizard here so I'm bringing it up. Perhaps this was your intention but Willpower is easy to get back. "Spell slots" come back every day. The extended action to make a scroll doesn't take that much time/effort/successes. Do you realize that an average wizard can churn out whatever spellslots they have in scrolls each day fairly easily? Give a wizard a week in a city and they will literally run out of carrying capacity before they run out of spells or even need to take a break to replenish.

                        Barbarian: I only just introduced a barb antagonist but so far this class is looking fine.

                        Fighter: Here is where the criticism comes in. This class is just worthless. At the higher levels it has some interesting things which I think should stay but it definitely needs more. For example, at the first level, literally the only thing you get from this class is the ability to change your weapon specialization (which you do at least, get for free) So I'm getting something that would cost me 1 experience and the ability to change that into a different one? And I would prefer this to spells? or raging? or sneak attack? The answer is no, no one would ever take this at first level. It needs more. And 2nd level isn't any better. You get bonus initiative (a merit) but only when using a specified weapon? That's boring and underpowered. Third level improves a bit, but it's confusing. I spend an action to lower an opponents defense (for everyone? or just me?) for the scene. I assume by only one since the next line tells me I can spend multiple turns to lower it more, up to my fighter level. That ability is useful, and also flavorful. You read their body language to counter their fighting style. Yes. Then we get back to ho-hum and nigh-useless. A dice bonus to things Brawl covers? Seriously? All this weapons stuff up to now and then we make them brawl better? What about letting me use my weaponry (with my specialized weapon, of course) instead of brawl, to grapple, trip, or whatever. And the culmination being to lower my target number is pretty neat, but this should not be the pivotal ability, that thing I've striven for my entire fighting career. This needs to be like the second level ability. It's not powerful enough as the ultimate ability when the wizard up there is throwing around chain lightnings. And why doesn't the fighter (as one of the few classes that uses it extensively) get any armor abilities? Like lesser speed penalties, or better armor bonus because they wear it better. Something like that? Overall the fighter needs a lot of help.

                        Monk
                        Paladin
                        Priest
                        Druid
                        So far haven't fit these in just yet.

                        Ranger So far so good. Don't have one above the third level yet but everything seems to be working well so far. Though their woodland stride ability would be better if there were more things in WoD that used this. I've only seen about two tilts. So perhaps add more "terrain tilts"? I know I'd love to see some terrain stuff.

                        Rogue: I like the rogue so far. Though I allow the danger sense merit to apply to traps automatically (and give the bonus when she's actively searching for them as well). I think you should really just rewrite it to a +2 bonus to notice hidden enemies and find traps/secret doors (and of course giving her 9-again on the traps from her trapfinding). Sneak attack is a little underwhelming so far. I might rework it to be like powerful success from the Dark Heroes and let you know how that goes. I don't know yet. (Also, for the sneak attack, if a person is hidden and attacks from surprise, they already get rote quality on the attack. So technically all your sneak attack does is give bonus dice on attacks when "flanking".) And you might want to include things that sneak attacks works/doesn't work on. Depending on the edition in D&D it didn't work on undead, constructs, plants, etc. In your system it works on everything. An fyi.

                        Merits: So far, from the ones I've looked at/used, I'm pleased. I do think, however, you could use some more class specific ones for some classes. For instance the Barbarian, Fighter, and Rogue, don't have any. For the Fighter and Barbarian, fighting style merits could really step up their game. And a rogue should shine with merits. They are, after all, the skill-heavy class in D&D. They should have so many tricks.

                        That's what I have so far. Hope it helps, and I'll keep you posted as more becomes available.

                            What do you think of this change on the fighter and the Merits? Any suggestion or substition?
                            Fighter Experts in the techniques of fighting, they can be soldiers, men and womens at arms, noble warriors or trained rebellious. Class Skills: Archery, Athletics, Brawl, Crafts, Weaponry
                            • Weapon aptitude, Weapon specialization, Fighter’s measure
                            •• Uncanny initiative, Armed Expertise
                            ••• Study enemy
                            •••• Maneuver expertize
                            ••••• Visage of threat
                            Weapon aptitude: If a fighter can take some time with an adequate weapon (or his body) for at least ten minutes of a quiet place, he can switch a Specialization (and any related Area of Specialization) in Archery, Athletics, Brawl or Weaponry related to a weapon (or his unarmed attack) to the weapon he wants to master at the moment. This change lasts for the rest of the story or when the fighter decides to use this class feature again.
                            Weapon specialization: A fighter gains a free Specialization in any weapon of his choice, or takes the Area of Specialization in said Specialization if present.
                            Fighter’s measure: Once per session, a fighter can make a guess on an enemy, trying to determine who is the strongest.
                            Dice pool: Wits + Level - Composure
                            Dramatic failure: The fighter jumps to wrong conclusions.
                            Failure: The fighter can’t read the opponent.
                            Success: The fighter understands whether the enemy is less, more or equally powerful, and to which lenght. Note that this class feature only determines powerfulness in “combat”. An expert assassin might not sound as stronger than him, but he might be much more powerful when he can choose the moment of the attack.
                            Exceptional success: The fighter gains the Steadfast Condition.
                            Uncanny initiative: At 2nd level, the fighter gains an Initiative bonus equal to his fighter level when he uses a weapon which he as a Specialization for. This includes Specializations acquired through Weapon aptitude.
                            Armed expertize: At 2nd level,by spending 1 Willpower point, the fighter can reduce the target number of his attack rolls to 7 instead of 8. That means he can get successes with a result of 7.
                            Study enemy. At 3rd level, the fighter can study an opponent for a turn to lower its Defense by one against the fighter for the rest of the scene. The fighter can take more turns to lower this value up to his fighter level in Defense. The fighter must be able to see the opponent. This class feature can occur prior the start of a combat, if the fighter can take time to study the opponent.
                            Maneuver expertize: At 4th level, the fighter can spend 1 Willpower to add half his Level (round up) to all contested rolls to grapple, trip, disarm and knock him back for the entire scene.
                            Visage of threat: At 5th level, when the fighter is in combat, he looks immediately more threatening to those who oppose him. All enemies facing the character suffer -4 to their Initiative modifier as they flinch back from his presence.


                            CLASS EXCLUSIVE MERITS
                            Warrior’s Insight (•••)
                            Prerequisite: Fighter 3, Wits 3, War 3
                            Benefit: The character can use Fighter’s measure once per scene instead of once per story. If the character attains success in this roll while studying that enemy with the fighter’s class feature, the character he will subtract -2 instead of -1 to his opponent’s Defense (up to his Level as normal). The fighter can use Fighter’s measure on enemies he already studied to gain the benefit of studying, exactly as written above. For example, if the character takes a turn to study the enemy and uses Fighter’s measure successfully in the same turn, the Defense penalty would be -2 instead of -1.

                            Quick study (••••)
                            Prerequisite: Fighter 4, War 5
                            Benefit: The character using Study enemy can roll Wits + War - the highest Composure to apply Study enemy to all opponents he can see. A failed roll will apply Study enemy only to a single opponent.

                            Pre-emptive counter (••••)
                            Prerequisite: Fighter 3, War 5, Weaponry or Brawl 5
                            Benefit: If the character has used Study enemy at least one turn on the enemy and be ready to attack with a Brawl or Weaponry attack, the character can attempt a “counter attack”. The enemy must be within reach of the weapon. The fighter uses the attack to subtract successes on the attack of the opponent.

                                                        I'm using different skills depending on the spell. One of the thing's I've done is gone back and given all the classes a Fuel Stat, Arcane classes (mages, warlocks, necromancers) use Mana, Spiritual classes (Priests, paladins, shamans, druids, monks and rangers) use Essence, and Martial classes use Conviction (like a small extra pool of Willpower). I feel that this helps balance casters and non-casters, while also keeping the 5-minute workday problem be non-existent. As for the skills, I use all of them. It's not... mm, based on Spell School (since those are kind of guidelines) but based on it's effect, and which class it's on a list. It makes it a pain to do all the dicepools, and so far, I've just told each player what it is when asked, based on their class.
