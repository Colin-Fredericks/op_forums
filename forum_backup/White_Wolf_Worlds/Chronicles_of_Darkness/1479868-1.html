                In that case, I think the Aegis spell (core book page 156) would be the simplest option. I believe the effect could also be accomplished with a Perfecting of Fate. Using Perfecting spells to increase Wits, Dexterity and Athletics automatically increases defense as well, which is not specifically armor but can make a tangible difference. When focused on dodging, use of Acceleration with rote quality (core book page 188) creates another very powerful way to avoid deadly attacks, creating a chance to reposition for a counterattack, distract a foe while cabalmates prepare something powerful or even escape the battle altogether.

                Following in the spirit of creating a powerful magical armor, in theory you could possibly use a combination of Fate and Space to build upon the Ban spell (core book page 176), which would only exclude things that done with hostile intent. It wouldn't cover automatons, natural occurrences, sensory spells or ones cast at sympathetic range. However, most threats would just pass through the mage, unless they use a supernatural power that can enter and win a Clash of Wills, making it a very potent defense nonetheless. Alternatively, there is a way to create a version of this with Time. That could create invulnerability against anything that doesn't win a Clash of Wills.

                Lastly, there is always the option of using magic to design and build top quality armor (Shaping and Enhance Skill are some examples of these spells), obtaining a high modifier (+5 in general and/or ballistic armor) which spells can build upon to higher levels that stack with mage armor. This would probably require expensive components, which can be mitigated by perfected materials (such as perfected leather blocking all bashing damage). However, chances are you will also need to use Veiling spells to disguise the equipment.

                But as far as stacking goes, I would say Mage Armor should only stack against things the armor defends against. Prime armor will not work against bullets, Spirit armor will not help against aimed spells, Mind armor offers nothing against automatons and so on.

                        Actually, I am assuming a max stat, Gnosis 10, all Arcana you need at 5 mage. Such a mage can defeat the vampire in melee, but it will be suboptimal.

                        So lets say the vampire started combat suddenly, the mage can use the reflexive version of Exceptional Luck to gain Charmed, in addition to a mage armor of your choice. This is because we are facing a max vampire, so there is no way to get enough Potency to overcome his Withstand and apply Knocked Down via a hex, at least not without previous preparations. The exception would be if the mage had personally made Exceptional Luck into a rote, granting them a higher chance at getting an exceptional success to bypass Withstand. A vampire playing things safe would start with a grapple, relying on his Disciplines to get an exceptional success and only then attack for massive damage, which Charmed still covers.

                        The next move is Temporal Pocket boosted by Time in a Bottle. Now you have a year's worth of time to prepare your next move (you might also want Veil of Moments to survive without food). If the vampire grappled you, then you Teleport out.

                        This is the turning point, from here you secure victory. The optimal move is to use Miracle to give an exceptional success on an Unmaking spell, completely bypassing Withstand and one shotting the vampire (like using one of Forces for a classic disintegration beam) or a sleeping spell if you are going the high Wisdom route. Using this path, you will burn through some mana, but not much.

                        The suboptimal way is to use cast buffs to all your Physical Attributes using Life, spending mana on boosting them beyond trait limits, surpassing Vigor's bonus. Then cast Acceleration to overcome Celerity. Now add a high Potency healing time boosting spell to make you more resilient than a werewolf. Now cast Endurance of the Ages to protect you from any vampires attacks, unless they win a Clash of Wills. Next is boosting your equipment as much as you can using Matter, giving max damage per hit. Lastly, boost your Willpower dice bonus via Mind for a safety measure in Clashes of Wills, alongside mental Attributes (Wits is especially important for defense) and any relevant skills (your main weapon skill and Athletics for even more defense). With all that, you stand a chance of defeating them in melee combat, but they can still kill you in one good hit.

                        You can do all this using normal preparation time instead of Temporal Pocket, but I also wanted to illustrate the worst case scenario. The first option would use some mana and secure a win with minimal risk, with preparation time you can do it at sympathetic range for no risk at all. The second option uses a lot more mana, commits more spell control and is still placing you within one shot range. High defense makes you difficult to hit, high general armor absorbs a lot of damage, plus high HP and high speed regeneration makes you very durable. But the vampire can still hit for a scary amount, then again so can you and Exceptional Luck gives Charmed as a safety net.

                        There are more things that can be done, but I think the general idea comes across, a pure melee win is doable, but resource intensive.

                            Originally posted by shadryx View Post
                            Is there any way to improve on Mage Armor? Our ST is ruling That nothing stacks with Mage Armor, limiting us to 5 points of armor total. The adversaries he's throwing at us can dish out way more damage than that. Is he right, or is there a legal way to get a higher total armor rating?
                            Use Shielding spells and/or give yourself general armor rating with Creative Thaumaturgy spells. With Advanced Duration/Scale you can target whole cabal and make it stick for some time, while putting all your dice into Potency would result in having 6+ armor ratings. Use magic to buff your magic for this spell; Exceptional Luck, As Above So Below, Choose the Thread are all good for those purposes. Don't forget that you can create Steadfast (Your Magic), reduce casting pool to 1 with penalties and still be guaranteed success on the spell. Ritual spellcasting, spending willpower and doing teamwork helps as well. With all this, Potency 10 spells are hard, but not impossible.

                            Also, don't forget that since armor rating can have different stacking sources (for example, you can wear body armor while having magic force shield on yourself), different magical sources of armor may stack as well; for example, personally I would've allowed to stack armor given by Aegis, and, say, Forces-analogue of Aegis. Your ST may disagree, though.
