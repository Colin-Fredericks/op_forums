    Some thoughts, though I'm not totally sure how many of these fit what you're seeking MachineVI.


    I'd like to see rules for characters developing, teaching, and adapting these fighting techniques. The mechanics are pretty good: some hiccups, sure, but those have essentially been addressed. Still would like to have a mechanical benefit to having a teacher who mastered a technique over one who kinda gets the point. I mean, a poor teacher hinders development, a mediocre teacher can generally help over none at all, but an excellent teacher can help you advance in leaps and bounds.

    Why focus on the therapist with a new merit when the key point is the coping mechanisms? Seems overly specific to me. A therapist is a councilor who aids a person in learning what they need to cope, so I would think treating them as a teacher who makes learning the merit (or merits) that permit coping would make more sense. Is the concern the sheer volume of merits that could arguably require? (an approach to containing could be to make a merit that applies to a specific trauma as selected on development.)

    "Zanshin" is often used specifically to refer to the state of mind after an attack, though I double-checked, and it does literally fit use as a more general awareness term as in Iaijutsu. Is that how the term is applied in iajutsu schools? I'm more familiar with it from in kendo, jodo, and aikido, and, for me, that term did not properly introduce the idea of the merit.

    Calling your generic striking art "martial arts" is even more generally confusing, I suspect.

    "Boot Party" called to mind exactly what you were intending.

    Transfer Maneuver is a good merit, and one that applies very well to ninjutsu and aikido, among others. Maybe mention that?

    The karmic burdened who become "demons" through combat would make a great template for such a book. Kinda hoping for those and berserks.

    Grump, grouse, and/or gripe.

        I've finally done a more comprehensive examination of the normal, non-fighting-style merits and figured I would toss my thoughts up.

        - Air of Menace: My initial impression was that this costs the same, but is better and more wide-ranging than an intimidation specialty + area of expertise. However, the drawback is significant and forces a shift in the way any character with this merit is played. I really like that, it means that taking this merit is a meaningful choice that not everyone who picks up Intimidate would want to take. Honestly, this merit is excellently done in my opinion.

        - Cohesive Unit: This synergizes really dangerously with Inspiring and Small Unit Tactics... which, not exactly my cup of tea, but I can certainly appreciate the opening of this as an effective character build. It should be noted that this merit really needs to specify how often its bonuses can be utilized because that will significantly affect balance - and while you're at it, a penalty for a few rounds when most countermanding orders will be issued outside of combat is insignificant to the point of triviality, it's not a drawback in anything but name (especially because the bonus appears to apply to out-of-combat situations as well). I would personally suggest a drawback duration of "one scene." to make it more meaningful and encourage those who take this merit to actually be tuned into the needs of their superiors.

        - Support Network MK I: I hate hate hate hate hate True Friend, as can be seen in my post against Sanctity of Merits, so can't provide meaningful feedback here.

        - Support Network MK II: Far too cheap for the benefit, and Mr. Feral has expressed the rest of my feelings regarding this merit.

        - Professional Therapist: See Mr. Feral's post, but I also think that this combined with Meditative mind makes for a character who will virtually never fail a breaking point.

        - Support Group, A.K.A Support Network MK III: See Mr. Feral's post again for issues with tone, not to mention once more how easily this merit will pay for itself now that experience costs are flat progression.

        - Torturer: How is this different than Vice-Ridden (GMC p. 153)? Does it stack? Can I have a character with three effective vices? If I fulfill my vice while torturing someone do I get 2 willpower for that action?

        - At Any Cost: I like it. It stands at the same tier as Hardy/Iron stamina for similar effect and cost. I'd only suggest, like them, making it a 1-3 dot merit, rather than a flat 3 dot.

        - Defender: The bonus willpower is a strange notion. How is it supposed to be denoted on a character sheet, or should you just make a mental note of it? That's a pretty trivial concern all things considered, but it's mostly by way of saying that I think there are more elegant ways to achieve this base effect without introducing "temporary willpower" as a mechanic. The writing is another weak point of this merit; it's just terribly dramatic and reading it leads me in mind of those marty-stue Lawful Good paladins of D&D yesteryear. It feels written to suit an opera character.

        - Dominating Center Line: I like the idea, but once more it feels like a merit better covered by a specialty+area of expertise... and would probably occur more often. If they stack, then that gets a bit absurd and reinforces the notion of GMC as the game where two people wiff at each other until one miraculously succeeds on a chance die.

        - Know the Territory: Really nice, not gratuitously powerful, decent cost. I like it a great deal. My only concern is this line: "This prevents the enemy from escaping" when setting a trap. That needs a great deal of more detail, if not removed entirely.

        - Living With Death: I've always felt that AOAs were unimpressive for the cost of dropping your defense. It's nice to see a merit that makes them more worthwhile.

        - Mental Scarring: Nothing really to say mechanically, but I really like the emphasis on trauma being not the sort of thing you suck up and move on entirely from, that old scars can be reopened.

        - Power Object: This needs a new name, but I love its effects both in terms of benefits and drawbacks.

        - Master of the Mask: Another merit that is perfectly replicated at the same cost and no drawback by a specialty + area of expertise + interdisciplinary specialty. I like the idea, but again, unless it stacks with aforementioned combination, it's never going to be worth it for the drawback alone.

        - Aggressive Driving: The second dot should be resisted by the opponent's Drive, or wits+drive, not Defense. Especially because a fair number of characters take the relatively cheap option of having Defense operate off of Brawl or Weaponry. J-Turn I feel like should be contested, unless the wall of police cars behind you all magically vanish. Other than those concerns, I like it. It makes chases more engaging and is good without being unbeatable by those without it.

        - Body As A Weapon: I like it, but then, given my propensity for liking things with hefty drawbacks, is that any surprise?

        - Dirty Fighter: The description in the drawback should state "know and care about his reputation." Just because people know about a reputation for dirty fighting doesn't automatically make them less trusted in other areas of their lives.

        - Drone Control: Where to begin? It's reaaaaally hard not to mock this merit mercilessly. It's awful. Where in NWoD is there the need to waste space and word-count on something better covered by obscure applications of the drive and/or computer skills? Where is the need for characters that sit passively removed from the threat? Play NWoD with all the fun and excitement of an actual drone operator! Days and days of boredom before melting unsuspecting enemies from distances too far to be detected! All the tension and horror of staring at a screen, unmoving for days!
        I'm sorry I can't give constructive feedback on this one because my only advice is to remove it entirely. If you are really interested in including rules for remote drones and robots, then make that an actual section of the rules, not a fighting style merit. That's like having a fighting style for carrier groups. I am being serious though. Could someone explain what this merit provides that would be worth the wordcount?

        - Fair Fighter: This merit is overpowered, undermines the tone of NWoD as well as the supposed idea behind Hurt Locker that combat is unpleasant and brutal, and has a drawback that barely deserves the name. For mortal characters, this has a surprising "paladin aura" vibe coming off of it in waves.

        - One-Handed Driving; This should have a mention of how it interfaces with Ambidexterity, but otherwise I like it.

        - Punch Drunk: My feelings on this are well documented and don't bear repeating.

        - Roadkill: Wow. Vicious. Not bad, selectively useful, and quite dangerous. All to the good I say. I mean evil. All to the evil.

        - Survivalist: Nice. Though I see a herald of the return of Page XX, its coming is nigh! Nigh I say! Woe to the unbelievers and those who have forgotten its glorious reign!

            Okay, I noticed with the Deconstructing Fighting Styles rules that some folks are focusing more on the math-hammer of the WiP rules, than how the rules themselves actually work. I think actually looking at the rules intent over how expensive it does or doesn't make fighting styles will go a long way in actually understanding the potential in these rules. What follows is an example of my own personal interpretation of how these rules are supposed to work, which is based mostly off of how things are described and how certain merits have actually been implemented.

            First, though, I would like to explain that my interpretation of how this changes Fighting Styles, is each level is now treated as it's own individual 1 dot merit, but what the levels mean now is the same as before, i.e., how capable in the tagged style one actually is. However, in order to actually be that capable, you have to have a broader range of knowledge in lesser abilities, which is where the prerequisite dots element comes in. So, with that said, let's look at a step by step process of how I think this works with a grappling monkey.

            Okay, so going with Grappling as the example here; I am going to cater my grappler towards a series of throws and locks, mostly aimed at subduing, incapacitating, or damaging an opponent in debilitating ways. To start with, I need a level 1 maneuver, as I do not yet have any to draw off of to take any higher maneuvers. So I take Standing Throw. I now meet the prerequisites for a level 2 maneuver, so I take Takedown, as it's befitting the theme. I now have 2 dots within the grappling tag. Now, let's say that taking a level 3 maneuver to unlock the abilitiy to take further level 3's is actually the prerequisite. I buy the character Ippon, as the character meets the prerequisites for that particular manuever already. I now have access to further level 3 merits.

            So far I have 3 dots in fighting merits which are tagged grappling, and while I am now able to purchase future level 3 manuevers, that is as high as I can progress in grappling tagged merits until such a time as I get 6 dots spread across grappling tagged maneuvers and merits rated up to 3. So I buy the character Ground and Pound. Which instantly boosts his total dots to 6. I can now purchase maneuvers in the level 4 category.

            Given the access to level 4 maneuvers my character now has, I take the Dynamic Guard maneuver, but I also find the Joint Lock maneuver very fitting for the methodology I am creating. So I spend 1 exp on Dynamic Guard, another on Joint Lock, and since I feel that it is also fitting, I spend 1 exp on Clinch Strike, and another on Sprawl, so that people have a harder time using his own methods against him. This has thus raised my total dots in grappling tagged merits to 10. This thus allows me access to 5th level maneuvers.

            Given as there isn't much choice to draw from in the grappling tag, I simply take the Positional Dominance maneuver.

            And for anyone who wants to math hammer it, we can break it down like this. 1 (Standing Throw)+1 (Takedown) +1 (Ippon) +3 (Ground And Pound) +1 (Dynamic Guard) +1 (Joint Lock) +1 (Clinch Strike) +1 (Sprawl) +1 (Positional Dominance) = 11

            If this is indeed the intent of the writers, then I hope it hopes folks actually envision the new rules in execution. However, if I have in fact misinterpreted the rules, I apologize in advance for added confusion!

                    I really like this draft (the style tag option in particular is going to be incredibly helpful in reflecting different characters relationships to violence by customizing the way they fight to the kinds of violence they expect to deal with). That said, there are a couple ideas you might want to consider.

                    1. Air of Menace- I am not sure if Intimidate dots should be the only resistance option as it seems to conflate courage with the ability to be scary. Maybe there should be an option for characters with high resolve and/or composure to resist it as well in at least certain situations?

                    2. Cohesive Unit-- The wording makes it unclear how this would apply to groups with a rotating leadership system. Say you have a team with a rotating system of leadership based on task (e.g. one team member is in charge of matters relating to combat, another social/political maneuvering, a third research/investigation). Does this merit's benefits and drawback always apply or can they be dialed back when the member with this merit is not in charge of the given subject area?

                    3. Dominating Center Line- This is problematic because it means that the failure of someone higher in the initiative order can cause problems for people farther down that are not necessarily logical. I have played in games where rules like this were used and it made a lot of players (who were using similar fighting techniques) incredibly frustrated as they felt they were punished for the failures of the other characters.

                    4. More options to help run away could be nice. In games that I have run, the hardest thing about making violence real has been countering my players notion that the only viable outcomes of a fight are you kill your opponent or you lose. It would be nice to have a few more options to facilitate characters fighting to create a viable opening to escape (if say a PC is jumped by goons while investigating something odd a combat maneuver or two that imposed a tilt that aided escaping and penalized the targets chasing them would make a nice addition to the light melee, street, and striking styles). This would also have the advantage of creating more options to use the vehicle and chase rules (not to mention the safe place merit would come into its own as that hidey-hole could be more readily brought to bear) While Avoidance helps, it could use a 5th dot option to help with disengaging from combat altogether and does not provide options for the rest of the group to create their own break off the engagement space. If this could be done, it would make players deal with the consequences of choosing more lethal combat builds as they would now know they could have tried to avoid fighting in at least some cases.
