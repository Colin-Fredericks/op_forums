                              So, Vampire Combat. I'm assuming that you have a vision of a rough and tumble Gangrel character on the front lines, dishing out pain with his bare hands. Working through minions is almost always the better option, but it's not for every concept.

                              Considerations for Offense:
                              - Raw Damage. I expect anyone presenting themselves as a combat character to, out of the gate, be able to take down a 7HL, 5 Defense, 1 Armor target inside two turns and have decent initiative (or a way to just go first, like Celerity). This, generally speaking, means you need to be able to reliably deal 5 damage per turn. This means you need an effective 20 dice on offense, with negating defense being worth 5 dice and each point of weapon damage being worth about 3 dice. Someone with a 2L weapon and an attack pool of 8 can qualify using All-Out Attack. Vampires can kill living targets with blood more easily because they can Grapple, Damage Move and Feed all in one action. 20XP in it's very possible, though not required to be taking down this kind of target in one turn.

                              The easiest and by far most overpowered way for vampires to accomplish this is Professional Training. With a mortal's game, combat dicepools are going to hover around 4-8 after defense. In a Vampire game, dicepools can get considerably higher, making Rote extremely powerful. Only take Professional Training if the ST is okay with you being able to one shot most non-combat threats. I personally don't see it as an issue, just something to plan around, but I don't think your ST will appreciate being blindsided by it.

                              - Deal Lethal and Non-Lethal Damage. You need the ability to kill targets and the power to take them in alive. This is why brawl is one of the best, if not the best combat skills in my opinion. Chokehold, although not required, is of particular note when trying to take out mortals and other creatures with less than 6 Stamina that breath. When facing undead, particularly vampires, you have the opposite problem in that you need a reliable way to deal Lethal or Aggravated damage to them or you need to be able to stake them through the heart reliably.

                              You're using claws, so you have brawl, so you have bashing damage vs mortals covered. You also have fangs, so grappling and biting vampires does give you a way to drink blood but you'll have issues with addiction and blood bonds. You can either muscle your way through this with Blood Potency or find an alternative.

                              As an alternative, you can purchase Protean 4 and take the Claws option to deal +2L damage with 2 Armor Piercing to Kindred. I recommend against claws of the unholy.

                              Staking someone through the heart basically requires that you buy the Light Weapons fighting style to 3 dots (which lets you Feint and convert your defense into offense) and/or gaining 5 dots in a relevant Professional Training. Vigor is also absolute gold when it comes to this.

                              Attacking with Fire is going to require some way to not frenzy and one of weaponry (a torch), firearms (flamethrower), or athletics (molotov cocktails).

                              - The most important thing is... Surprise! Whenever possible, you should be negating someone's defense through surprise, which also negates their option to use Celerity defensively. There are plenty of options for this. The best ones are...
                              a) Celerity
                              b) Obfuscate 3
                              c) The Cheap Shot merit
                              d) Mundane ambushes.

                              You'll be picking up Celerity anyways, but blood is precious. I recommend the Cheap Shot merit. Obfuscate is a good alternative build, but costs too much EXP for this one. Also note that mid-combat, using Celerity's Reflexive Action option can be used offensively to prevent someone else from activating Celerity in response to it.

                              - The second most important thing is initiative. Always go first. I'm not sure it's worth investing overmuch into this, but whenever possible, always go first. If you go first you can negate the opponent's action using knockdown or something else. Vampire has an interesting dynamic where your Celerity Clash of Wills dicepool is situationally more important than your actual initiative.

                              - The third most important thing is Action Control. Thankfully, a lot of this is going to come from the core rules rather than options you buy. Brawl is by far the best at this, because of Grappling. Your other options are Stunning the target by inflicting their size or more in damage in one attack, knocking the target down before they get their turn in the initiative order (sacrifice damage if you need to), taking them out entirely, or disarming an armed opponent.

                              - Fighting the Swarm. Try and have something to do against other vampires in Swarm and/or Mist form. Not going to lie, the hypothetical Gangrel with Protean 5, Resilience 5, and Coil of the Ascendant 5 is essentially unkillable. I believe, though its not spelled out, that Swarm Form can fight Swarm Form without running into the 1 damage per attack cap. Check with your ST.

                              Considerations for Defense:

                              - Remember the Most Important Things: Surprise and Initiative are the best offense and the best defense. If you get the drop on your enemies and go first, that's two turns and potentially two combatants removed from initiative. The best defense in Vampire is truly the best offense.

                              - Get an Oh Shit button: This is going to be Celerity 1 + something you can use to nope out of Combat (Swarm Form, Mist Form, Obfuscate 3, Nightmare 2, Parkour 5). If things even remotely look like they might not be in your favor, then you need to make combat a non-viable strategy against you.

                              - Negating Surprise: Unfortunately, you won't always be getting the jump on the opponent. Sometimes, they'll get the jump on you. You need to have a good Wits + Composure (+ Blood Potency with Acute Senses) pool to negate someone's Ambush roll. After you have 6+ dice in this pool, you want to grab Trained Observer. If your relevant traits are low, Danger Sense can compensate.

                              - Negating Action Control: The Stun and Insensate tilts, as of Beast, can be negated by spending willpower. If you're relying on Professional Training, this is a considerable hit to offense but still ultimately worth it. Ride the Wave with a clever and opaque phrasing of your goal (never "kill Bob" make it "Completely, physically overwhelm Bob and bring him under your heel dead or alive." i.e. lean Competitive, not Monstrous) should negate most forms of mind control. This is why Ride the Wave and the required Resolve + Composure rolls and Willpower maximums are all very important.

                              - Handling Damage and Staking: Well, it happened, you got hit and are taking damage. You'll probably be taking a lot of damage, and you may or may not be able to act next turn. See if you can't wear a chain-mail mesh under your shirt. It's not labeled discreet but being discreet is literally the reason people still wear chain-mail. The 3/1 Armor will save your ass more times than you can count. The two-dot version of Iron Skin can help negate the minimal bashing damage or the one point of bashing damage you'll take from firearms. Other than that, the only other option you have is Resilience. Resilience is a fantastic buy, especially for a Gangrel. If you ever hit BP5 and Resilience 5 and get Blood Armor? Watch out. It changes the whole dynamic. In theory High Defense should also help you. It really depends on how often the ST goes around negating your defense. If they haven't negated your defense, then why is the opponent not Stunned or dead?

                              - Handling Numbers: Really, you shouldn't worry too much about a single combatant. Either you're going to surprise / win initiative and wreck them, or you aren't and you're in trouble and should probably be trying to leave. Multiple opponents make things tricky. This is where having a high defense and high armor will help out.

                              Dicepools to Watch For:
                              Perception: Wits + Composure + Blood Potency
                              Frenzy Check (and Willpower Maximum): Resolve + Composure
                              Clash of Wills (Celerity): Celerity + Blood Potency
                              Initiative: 1d10+Dexterity+Composure
                              Attack Roll: Strength + Brawl, Strength + Weaponry, and/or Dexterity + Firearms
                              Defending against Dread Powers: Resistance Trait + Blood Potency

                              What Else are You Going to Do?
                              A "physical" character doesn't necessarily mean a "combat" character, and the game is probably not going to be a combat simulator 24/7. In the physical skillset alone is Athletics, Stealth, Larceny, "Breaking Things" (Strength + Stamina) and Survival, all of which are important skills. If you follow my build you'll also have another 4 or 7 dots to put into mental skills and another 3 or 0 dots to put into social skills. Make them count.

                              Attributes:
                              Intelligence 2, Wits 3, Resolve 3
                              Strength 2, Dexterity 3, Stamina 3
                              Presence 2, Manipulation 1, Composure 3
                              Note: Clan Bonus in Stamina

                              It is irrelevant to combat whether you have Manipulation 1 or Presence 1. This block has a minimal number of weaknesses while still allowing you to qualify for every merit you could potentially want. Taking Intelligence and/or Presence down to 1 in order to boost Composure, Resolve, or Strength won't affect combat much, but it may get you some odd looks from your ST or other players.

                              Skills:
                              Athletics 2
                              Brawl [Grappling] 4
                              Firearms 2
                              Stealth 1
                              Weaponry 2
                              Streetwise 2
                              Subterfuge [Cheap Shot] 2

                              This set of Skills qualifies you for any combat merit you could want to take. We are going to wind up bumping our Brawl up to 5 through Professional Training. You can lower Firearms and/or Weaponry if you want to, however keep in mind that Firearms contains a strong room-clearing element with fully automatic weapons and shotguns. If can help a lot against numbers.

                              As an aside, ask if you can stake someone in a Grapple using the "Damage" move and taking the requisit called shot penalty. If yes, Weaponry may be completely useless.

                              Disciplines:
                              Celerity 1
                              Protean 3

                              This serve a nice core. If you were a starting character, I'd say get Protean 3 so you can begin with Swarm Form. 20 EXP in you can save an EXP by going Celerity 1 / Protean 2 and picking up Protean 3 and Swarm Form with EXP. If you want to purchase Disciplines going forward, I'd recommend more Protean or Resilience. If you find your damage to be low or if you want to turn Swarm Form into a room clearer, then buying Vigor may be worth it.

                              Merits:
                              2 Cheap Shot
                              1 Defensive Combat [Brawl]
                              3 Street Fighting 1-3
                              1 Unarmed Defense 1
                              5 Professional Training [Kindred Sheriff; Brawl, Firearms, Streetwise] 1-5
                              - Contacts 2 [Your Pick]
                              - 9-Again
                              - Specialties in Streetwise [Tracking] and Firearms [Called Shots]
                              - +1 Brawl
                              - Rote Actions.
                              2 Swarm Form
                              1 Acute Senses
                              2 Meditative Mind 1-2
                              3 Resources 1-3
                              10 Blood Potency 2-3

                              Fighting Styles are amazing, to answer a question in your OP, and they stack with a vampire's powers beautifully. The exception are Style Merits that are easily replicated elsewhere. For instance, the first two dots of Martial Arts are amazing. The third is meh (usually they will have at least reinforced clothing). The fourth and fifth are kinda useless (you'll be grappling, biting, or clawing most of the time). '

                              Equipment:
                              Fashion (+2)
                              Makeup Kit (+1)
                              Disguise (+3)
                              Protective Amulet (+2)
                              Reinforced Clothing (1/0)
                              Chainmail (3/1)
                              Stakes (0L)
                              Throwing Knives (1L)
                              SMG, Small (1L)
                              SMG, Large (2L)
                              Shotgun (3L)
                              Crossbow (2L)
                              Smartphone (+2)
                              Supporting Equipment for your other skills.

                              Services:
                              Black Market Access (Streetwise; +2)
                              Crash Space (Streetwise; +1)
                              Paid Informants (Streetwise; +1 or +2)

                              Derived Traits / Relevant Dicepools:
                              Health Levels: 8
                              Initiative: 1d10+6
                              Speed: 10
                              Defense: 8 (10 vs Brawl Attacks)
                              Armor: 3/1 (Torso only)
                              Willpower: 6
                              Perception: 9
                              Frenzy Check: 9 (includes Meditate bonuses, which you should always have)
                              Clash of Wills (Celerity): 4
                              Ambush Roll: 4
                              Cheap Shot Roll: 6
                              Attack Roll (Brawl): 7 (+1 in Grapple)
                              Attack Roll (Weaponry): 4
                              Attack Roll (Firearms): 5
                              Note: Frenzy / Ride the Wave adds +3 to all Ambush, Attack and Cheap Shot rolls.
                              Note: Physical Intensity adds +2 to all Ambush, Attack, and Cheap Shot rolls per Blood Point spent, to a maximum of +6. This bonus extends the full turn, so you can get the full bonus on Ambush/Cheap Shot roll and an Attack roll on the same turn.

                              Bloodline:
                              Just a shout out that the Kerberos Bloodline is fantastic for physical builds, especially ones with some EXP and Blood Potency under their belt. In particular, you want the Monstrous or Competitive version of their merit to the point where you can reflexively lash out at people for the dice bonuses. It's by no means necessary.

                              Going Forward:
                              Blood Potency is probably the best over-all purchase and bang for your buck, until you hit Blood Potency 5. Other than that, it's about shoring up your weaknesses. I'd probably purchase some combination of Blood Potency / Protean / Resilience / Style Merits / Trained Observer, assuming that I wanted to get better at combat.

                                                      Excellent post SoulGambit. You basically wrote down my own thoughts about how combat in vampire works and what you have to look out for (as well as some cool things I did not even consider until now). The combat is really quite different in its dynamic compared to many other rpgs and I find that refreshing.

                                                      First off all I want to emphazise this point (I could not agree more):

                                                      - The most important thing is... Surprise!

                                                      Vampire combat in a nutshell: Either get the jump on them or get the fuck out.

                                                      A couple more thoughts:

                                                      The thing about having the option to deal lethal or none-lethal damage for me is also about your humanity rating. Killing all your oponents can get very costly. I also think that banes und touchstones are worth discussing for a physical char. Getting into fights can get ugly for your humanity very quickly.

                                                      One reason why I find action control so crucial (and brawl so damn powerful) is the need to stop enemy vampires from getting away.

                                                      Also I would like to add something about fire. Protean 1 is one of the best way to stop continous burning. If you are fully sunken into the ground there is no oxygen anymore ^^. A really good save if playing around with your flamethrower went wrong.

                                                      I feel like obfuscate 3 is probably the single best (combat)disciplin in general. There is just so much utility in this one. Makes hiding armor and weapons so easy (and use of firearms as well in some cases). You can get the jump on people and run away from them, You can seperate single targets from the herd and you can extend the effects on your allies. Not to mention all the non combat options it provides. At level 3-4 its an absolut gem that stands out in an already well balanced and stacked field of powers. Its like masquerade obfuscate and requiem 1.0 obfuscate had a love child with (most of) the best attributes of both and some additional powers on top.

                                                      For the character template SoulGambit provided I would try to get my hands on animalism 1 and maybe even stay on BP 2 for the beginning.CSSchulz bascially made this point already. Having your own little pet army with a bit of prep is extremly powerful espacially for a 1-2 dot disciplin. Also keep in mind that with the new vampire senses finding animals is not that hard, dead animal followers are much better for your humanity compared to dead human followers, you get additional swarm control against other animalism users (clash of will), access to emergency blood (especially interesting since the char does not have a really good alternative blood source) and, maybe most important, you win a crazy amount of utility outside of combat.

                                                      I would also like to echo that I think that weaponry is not that important. If you are really short on points you can skip firearms as well (although full auto fire+beaten down is gold vs. normal mortals/grunts). Athletics + grenades/molotov coctails/tear gas canisters can go a long way for crowd control as well and athletics is a top skill for any physical char outside of combat as well.
