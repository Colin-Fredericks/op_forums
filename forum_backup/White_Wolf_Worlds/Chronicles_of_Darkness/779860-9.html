They join with Echidna because they want her monstrous powers for themselves, because they want her monstrous troops to use as they desire, or because they truly believe in her cause, that the world is already doomed and that it's best to make a new race to survive.. Regardless, while Abyssal Sirens can work with Current Sirens on a good day, the Echidnal Sirens are, though understandable, uncompromisingly evil in their efforts.             To be entirely honest, I feel that naming things entirely in Greek or Latin is kinda cheesy. Not that I'm putting down the suggestions. It's just that we have the term "Akasha", which is a Hindi word that refers to, "a supposed universal etheric field in which a record of past events is imprinted." Granted, Sirens themselves are Greek, but we have extended the term "Siren" to refer to mermaids, nixies, river spirits, rusalkas, selkies, and such. We could also incorporate Nagas from Hindu/Buddhist mythology. Despite the fact that they are serpent-people, technically, they are sometimes depicted as water-based, like the nagas in Warcraft who run the gamut of mutations, which could inspire how Alterations are seen.

            For my push of Hindi/Sanskrit terms, Pneuma, which refers to "breath" or "soul, could be changed to "Prana", which literally means "breath of life" or "essence of life-force". Serenity, the trait that gives Sirens calmness, could be worked into "Dharma", which refers to righteousness in actions, such as duty, law, behavior that is "morally" correct. Like Serenity, it could also be the source of a Siren's calmness in her own actions. As for Dissonance, I myself am torn between calling it "Klesha", a mental state that poisons the mind with negative emotions like anxiety, envy, and depression, or basing it on a musical term like "Cacophony", though I feel that Cacophony is being used for the Verses.

            As for why else I feel like pushing for a Hindi/Sanskrit-based terminology, the working lore has Sirens from a different existence surviving The Deluge of their world and coming to existence in a parallel world where an event like The Deluge is coming. The Siren then feels that her duty to this world is to prevent this event from occurring by pushing things in the world towards coexistence or preemptively stopping things before they happen. The lore has a "rebirth" bend to it and a "karma"-like way of approaching the world by attempting to do good things to ensure a good future.It even has a "Samsara" aspect as Sirens are in a, as far as they know, second cycle where things will reoccur unless something changes.

            Still, these are my suggestions. It may give the game a slight religious bent that might clash or make people feel awkward or it could be taking from a culture too much or whatever. Hopefully, we can have a discussion about this.

                    For me, the issue is less of "let's use this language gratuitously rather than this language gratuitously" and more of a "people rely on a specific language to make things "fancy" too often, so let's try a language that is less-often used to shake up the formula". For me, I see a high level of usage from Latin words or Greek words because they are the root of Romance languages which also contributes to English, a language that many people use. Whether it's Latin chanting or quotes from one of the great philosophers, it's used to add an extra "oomph" to terms that are meant to be seen as important. Vampire uses it quite a bit, which would make sense as Vampires have existed for quite a long time and some of the oldest Vampires hail from ancient Rome/Greece. However, Dragon: the Inheritance uses it with their Juice stat, Kauchaomai, as with their forms, Drakonos (though it could be argued that it's supposed to be Latvian) and Anthropos. Princess: the Hopeful uses it to refer to their Monster-of-the-Week creatures, the Cataphractoi, Genius: the Transgression has their abilities known as Axioms based on Greek terms. For me, it's an issue of oversaturation.

                    The other reason is because, if we group things together, there is a language that goes with certain concepts. For the Siren's powers, the Verses, the different styles of performing Verses are related to Italian musical terms. For the antagonists of Sirens, we have the Vodianoi, which is a Slavic creature. However, they are kept as separate entities that are partially influenced by two different languages. The Siren's Power stat is Akasha, which is a Hindi term that relates to a core aspect of the Sirens' lore. I would push for Hindi terms to refer to the Siren's attributes, like their Power stat, Juice stat, and Integrity stat. Of course, I wouldn't object for Turbulence or Tarachi as the Frenzy-like state that Sirens might go into. I would say that if we go for either Hindi-based or Greek/Latin-based, it should be pretty streamlined so that each aspect fits. I feel that if we stay with Pneuma and Iremia, we would need to change Akasha to something that would relate to Greek.

                                                        No, as the name for the Frenzy-effect. The one that was tentatively named "Dissonance".

                                                        Insofar, There is Akasha for the Power Stat, Pneuma for the Juice Stat, Serenity for the Integrity Stat, and Dissonance, which I feel should be switched to Maelstrom as a Frenzy-effect. Both Akasha and Pneuma conflict because they are two terms that are unheard of and would make it difficult for the players to grasp the concept of what they mean. For example, Vampires have Blood Potency as their Power Stat, which gives a good vision of what it is supposed to relate to when it comes to Vampires. They also have Vitae, which is an odd word to have but, because it is the Juice to Blood Potency, would relate back to the vision of what Blood Potency means. With Mages, they have Gnosis as their Power Stat, which is something that, arguably, not many people have heard of. However, their Juice Stat, Mana, is close to ubiquitous in everyday life with the rise of video gaming, so even if someone doesn't know what Gnosis refers to, they can infer that it involves magic because they know what Mana is or the connotations of Mana. Basically, if there are terms used that are kinda odd or unheard of, context clues help out. However, it's all meant to be understood with only superficial knowledge. Vampires are associated with blood, which is further emphasized by Blood Potency and allows people to understand what Vitae refers to. Mages do magic stuff using their Mana, so Gnosis would be understood as some sort of magicky thing.

                                                        We do have this overarching backstory for Sirens, which is fine. Every splat needs that. However, the splat is still "Mermaids that sing", like how Werewolf, despite being half-spirit enforcers, are supposed to be about "werewolves and the beast within". It's reinforced by the fact that Werewolves have Primal Urge as a Power Stat, referring to their bestial nature. Akasha, while it ties into the backstory, means nothing when it's placed next to the word "Siren". The same goes for Pneuma, although it could work for anyone who has a working knowledge of Greek. '

                                                        Akasha works into the Siren's ability to use multiple Opuses in her Verses and allows her a set number of Alterations. It also causes her to suffer greatly from Maelstrom, due to it allowing Echidna to whisper to the Siren. Serenity allows a Siren to stay calm in her decisions and actions. For Akasha, maybe Immersion would work, as it would reinforce the idea of water, would allow players to infer that Pneuma relates to water, and it allows for some wordplay, as Sirens of this reality are only partially aware of their selves in The Deluge at Immersion 1; they are only partially immersed in their role. When Sirens reach Immersion 10, they are fully aware of their other self and have merged their selves from reality and The Deluge together. This also means that their exposure to Echidna's whispers are stronger, as they are deeply immersed in their role. As for Serenity, perhaps Lucidity to refer to a Siren's awareness and ability to detach herself from a situation, Placidity to further establish a Siren's need to remain calm and unmoving, or, as a fancy word, Ataraxy, which is defined as "a state of serene tranquility".

                                                            Now for the counterarguments. For one, Akasha refers to a supposed etheric field that records past events. This fitting for Sirens, as the Deluge is connected to the concept of Time and as such it connects to past events and has anything within it's waters. Also, literally almost every culture has some stories on water spirits or gods, or things that connect to water in general. Though it's not so much, doesn't it sort of fulfill the same definition that Akasha does? "Etheric Field" in the form that the tales are worldwide, and "recorded past events" is the definition of history itself. Using this reasoning, Akasha can still be fitting. At the least though I can imagine Akasha being renamed to "Akashic Record", to ascribe to what the Siren has learned during her time as a Siren.

                                                            As for Pneuma, the original ideas behind it were for it to fit the Succubi trope as well as Siren trope, basing on the idea that Sirens feed off of human souls to keep themselves alive. Connecting to this is the idea of the water-air connection that we have for Sirens who reach the "Archmaster" state so to speak. Not to mention with the powers being based on music and song, a fuel stat that literally translates to "Breath" is fitting to power it. If it helps even more, one of the most common diseases people hear of is pneumonia, a disease where the lungs are swollen and air cannot move. If nothing else, it's an allusion to what it means (breath) and if the concern for a fitting enough name for the fuel stat is great enough, we might have to change Pneuma back into it's original form. Breath.
