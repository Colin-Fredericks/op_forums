                                I have mixed feelings. Good swords are like candy, with a brittle edge and a softer, chewy (okay, not chewy, but candy!) interior. I mean I can see a katana being tougher by virtue of its shape, but this is more a function of shape and not the magical qualities of a katana. But a longsword is . . . uh, longer, and longer weapons really do help determine who gets the first shot in. So maybe this is more a function of weapon length regardless of the sword's uh, nationality. So an arming sword (what people call a "longsword" in RPGs but isn't) or katana or cutlass get the Durability bonus, while a two handed actual-longsword gets the Initiative bonus.

                                EDIT: Yeah, I know a katana is used with two hands but at least by the time of Edo-period standardization it's shorter than a longsword. So it's not the handedness, just blade length. Funny thing is right now I'm researching English longsword and Silver recommends the same blade length for a one or two handed weapon, which makes a later English longsword kinda katana-like in its dimensions.

                                                So, a couple of things that don't feel like strictly errata but I still want to mention before the final version goes to print:

                                                Psychokinetic Combat 3, Trapped Potential, doesn't specify a size limit on the objects you can detonate. The text pretty clearly implies that it's meant for size 0-3, given that the examples are a lighter or a welding torch, the damage is limited to three bashing, and the resulting Force is Size 3, but I could see someone trying to use it on a truck, which seems beyond the scope of intent but within the rule as currently written. (Or more humorously: "I blow up... THE EARTH")

                                                Sojourner is a neat upgrade to apportation, but while I assume it's intended to remove the drawback it doesn't say so. Thus, teleporting a person one mile would deal 1760 bashing, making it an extremely potent killing power but useless for its (presumably) intended function.
