    Originally posted by Almarck View Post


    We'd need more non Western writers for that. The main reason we don't have as much Eastern/non-European centric rooted splats is due to limited exposure for many of these creatures in popculture combined with the fact that by default most of us are in/very close to the Anglo-world by default... which tends to be a big bias on monster sources. We see minor splats and creatures from other cultures, but we'd need more things first.

    I will support Djinn over Naga though. I am really against snakes. And Robin Williams's Genie would make for a intersting shoutout character.
    I could see djinn being a splat in a system that deals with things like nymphs, nature spirits, and animist creatures: beings that were once humans but have become something far more and are now bound to a place, thing, person, or element. You could pull from contemporary works like Swamp Thing and maybe some of the weirder Japanese and Korean ghost stories too. You've been touched by something greater than yourself, and you have the power to help the people and ideals that you love with your powers more than you could as a mere mortal, but every act of altruism makes you more alien and less able to connect with the things you once cared for. Maybe you were cursed or blessed by a god or touched by a being beyond the veil. But now you're something more. You could find antagonists in those of your kin who have become so alien that they've forgotten their base selves entirely, or poor unfortunate creatures who have become bound to would-be sorcerers who have subverted these creatures to their will. As you move closer to your pure form, your virtues and vices become more rigid obligations and compulsions, making it harder for you to connect to the world around you and making it easier for clever and dubious mortals to manipulate you to serve their ends. I suppose obsession would be the core theme?

    I think that would create a unified setting with a broad sense of globalism that can pull in Native American, Middle Eastern, Greco-Roman, and SE Asian sensibilities.

            See, I was going to go a different direction. They start off as humans with "patrons" that represent the sort of mercurial and fickle obsessions of the traditional Greek gods or utterly alien beings representing incomprehensible concepts.

            The young woman born and raised in Bakersville, a quiet and sleepy town that saw drastic change as she started to grow up. She was the first to stand up when the factories moved in and started polluting the nearby river. She was one of the first to start protesting, and eventually the obsession cost her her comfortable job at the bank. She led local political action against the transgressions of the big businesses nearby. In her dreams, the River would speak to her, offering her the power she needed to push those negative influences out of the town. What started as peaceful protests and lobbying expanded to acts of industrial espionage and murder. And like all rivers, the one that wound through the city was just a tributary in a greater form. As her obsession spins out of control, she's becoming more than just the crusader of this river's health. She's becoming the water itself.

            A middle aged man who's once happy marriage fell into stasis. His midlife crisis found him increasingly disconnected from his wife and kids. The final straw was his purchase of a cherry red vintage Mustang. It cost him everything. His wife left him, his kids became distant, and he lost everything in the settlement, including his prized car. The Mustang has hopped from owner to owner, every one speaking of how it seemed haunted. He would dream of his car, not of owning it but of being it, free and liberated on the open road, but eventually the dreams weren't enough. He stalks the car conventions throughout the Eastern seaboard. They talk to him, and he can commune through them. He's found himself as a nomad, traveling from convention to convention, shacked up in cheap roach motels, injecting motor fluid into his veins.

            The earnest and driven Tokyo detective who's husband and children were murdered in a horrific gangland attack. She tracked down the killers and delivered swift justice, but she quickly discovered it wasn't enough. Locals started whispering about the spirit that haunts the underworld. She demands blood, and the spirit of vengeance that purrs in her ear insists more and more that blood is the only valid currency for any ethical transgression.

            The graffiti artist who found his muse in a modern Aphrodite. Her patronage transformed his pedestrian scrawls into works of transgressive beauty. She enlivened him. In her presence, he felt human, felt greater than human. But then her fickle interests waned and he was left nothing more than a shell. Now the only time he can feel alive is when he is in the presence of mosaics and tags. He can crawl through the inner workings of the city, feel the pulse of life that permeates everything. Unfortunate then that the new mayor has implemented an aggressive stance towards beautifying the city and whitewashing street art.

            Virtue/Vice would become a core element of the characters and eventually a trap that makes them vulnerable to the agendas of others. Obsessions would replace ambitions and would be the crux of power levels, becoming increasingly grandiose and dangerous as the characters grow.

                Originally posted by dxanders View Post
                In all seriousness though, I'm curious to see where you'd go with this. Vampire and Mummy really do seem to have it pretty well covered in my mind ...
                What do you see the core mechanics looking like, and what breed of creature would you use as the template?

                The first things you learn is everything is cyclical. It was your first mistake.

                You were no one. You had no contacts, no resources, and you had nothing... or so you thought. Day in-day out your life repeats the same social interactions, the same menial tasks, and the same complaints. Everything is Cyclical. But then you are in the right place at the right time, you decide to skip work today to hunt for that ridiculous legend of a hermit in the swamp, or you're simply found by some fancy suit driving a fancy car. You're given a wish, a demand, or a chance to change things for you. You take it, this is what you've wanted but you didn't know the price.

                Now you are someone. You have contacts, more resources than you can count, and you have everything... or so you think. By making the choice you've bound yourself. Not to any one thing or one person in particular. In fact it's to everything all at once. You find that you have all that you need and much much more but this has a cost on your soul, if what you have can be called such. The Universe gives you what ever is your desire but the more you use it and the more your desire increases the more the more detached you become. The less real you become. And there are things hunting you, things that desire what you have: the infinity essence that threatens to swallow your being into oblivion. It is coveted greatly and only the powerful succeed in combating these (would-be) thieves. But the stronger you become the more your threads of reality start to unravel themselves. You are losing yourself to irrelevance and reality loses you amongst infinity.

                The only way to gain equilibrium is to drain infinity. And draining that which is infinite is deceptively simple. It just takes a simple wish, a single demand, or a thorned chance to change things, for them... Everything is Cyclical.


                ____________________________________________

                My idea is what if the entire game is about how you've already gained relevance in one way or form. But the more you grow the more your hold on reality thins. Your entire essence is infinity and the natural end of that to cease to exist and yet exist in infinity. I think it terrifying to see yourself build up but then watch actively as your everything, resources, social merits, and relevance in common human society just drain right before your eyes during play. It's all a choice.

                Like changelings they can drain their powerstat to re-anchor themselves and to do that you need to give that to someone else. It is always safe to give it to those of your kind but it increases their powerstat (or does something with it). Giving it to random humans is less common due to the risk: it's possible that infinity will take hold in their wish and they will find themselves in your place. There are tales that given certain circumstances you may exchange places with a human. But attempts in recent knowledge have all failed (most of the time in disaster). Most of these wishes are temporary and help you to stave off critical increases in your powerstat. It's a battle between having what you want and the threat of irrelevance.

                The "Monster" template? I'm still stuck Djinn. My brain wants to play a genie really bad and the above sounds fun.
