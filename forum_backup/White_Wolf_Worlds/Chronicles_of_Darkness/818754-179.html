        I don't see why this shouldn't be possible. There are no hard rules for when an enemy is decapitated, and there are no rules stating that decapitation kills a vampire.

        Since Resilience doesn't actually heal wounds (visually, it does of course reduce/remove wounds in your Health track), it just ignores the damage being done, it's perfectly possible that if a vampire receives a blow that would have been lethal but Resilience is what's keeping him alive, he could still be alive even with his head severed. He'd likely be in torpor though. Or not. If it's a strix-possessed vampire or it's an Ordo Dracul member with Coil of the Wyrm currently in a frenzy.

        Honestly, with this sort of stuff, I'd actually be alright with going for the cinematic and dramatic approach and just kinda handwave the actual rules or mechanics for it. If it's dramatically appropriate for the vampire to be beheaded, have her be beheaded. If it's dramatically appropriate for her to still be conscious at that point and have the head and body move separately from each other, make it so. As long as it's a one-time thing or an anomaly, I think it will be cool and the players wouldn't feel cheated.

                I'm boring, and tend to say that since decapitation is what happens when you hit full agg, anything less than full agg doesn't decapitate you. If you say "full agg for an average human (=14 lethal) decapitates" you end up with the question of "well, this guy has Stamina 4 and/or the Giant merit or something, so he can survive 14 lethal if he gets to a hospital…how did he survive the guillotine??"

                In other words, decapitation potentially happens at full agg, but never earlier. If an effect like Resilience gives you extra health boxes, or vampires downgrade lethal to bashing, or anything like that, so you don't reach full agg, then you don't get beheaded.

                                    Originally posted by Enokh View Post
                                    How to challenge the group of them in a fight is already a thing I have to think about, and the ability to turn into blood-drinking mist is, at first blush, the pinnacle of that. Yeah, there's fire, but there sure aren't flamethrowers anytime soon (other than wizards). Any particular advice on this front -- either Protean 5 specifically, or fights that challenge them?
                                    A few notes:

                                    * Fire has always been a huge, huge thing in civilization. Buildings were mostly out of wood (even the "stone" castles were supported by heavy wood buttressing), and the main source of light and heat was burning stuff. Oil lamps from this period on are vital, and oil can get sprayed, thrown or otherwise ignited to hilarious effects. Fire is also really, really hard to contain during the period--the modern measures that have greatly reduced fire risk are not present.

                                    * Gangrel survive. It's what they do. Protean 5 and to a lesser extent Resilience 5 underline this. Animalism serves as a tripwire, giving the Gangrel time to prepare so he can... survive. Don't take that away from the player. Instead, show that just because you're physically intact doesn't mean you aren't hurt. Everyone around you that you care about is less durable, even vulnerable, and while your body lives your mind and soul may not. Or not without injury. What steps will you take as you age to safeguard what is really important to you, now that you can in short order, live through danger?

                                    (BTW, the movie The Last Witchhunter (2015) hooks onto aspects of this.)

                                    * Surprise. Use it sparingly, and when necessary for the story apply it viciously and without remorse in overwhelming force. Go for staking, which paralyzes and prevents misting. Prep the encounter scene in advance and ensure the antagonists have had enough time to prepare and work out the PC's movements.

                                    But really, the role of the Gangrel should be "the one that comes back to get the rest out of trouble." The party should know their unstoppable bud has their back. Or not--in which case they should be honestly afraid. Ultimately the greatest vulnerability of a Gangrel is betrayal; and that's a fact that drives a wedge between them and others, reinforcing their loner-hood.

                                    --Khanwulf
