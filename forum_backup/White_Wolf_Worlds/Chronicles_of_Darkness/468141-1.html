                Artificers
                The Devils in the Machine

                Any society that wishes to function must have those who make things, and Hell is no exception. Though damned souls toil in countless numbers to provide the brute labor - or raw materials - upon which the Infernum is supported, it is the artificers who truly create the Infernum. They are the engineers and the architects, toiling ceaselessly to shape metal, stone, flesh, bone, soulstuff and other, more esoteric materials upon which hellish civilisation depends for buildings, tools, weapons, everything.

                And yet, hell has little gratitude to spare. Artificers have long been commodities at best, dismissed as little different to the tools they wield at worst. Even though the black economy of torment and destruction rests upon their squared shoulders, other, "nobler" hellspawn have looked past them. Small wonder, then, that the artificers have been involved with and supportive of the Free City Heresy since the beginning. Some wonder in private if maybe the artificers somehow created the Heresy.

                Appearance: Artificers are as variable as all hellspawn, but there are certain traits that define them. All artificers are smaller than humans, averaging a childish stature. They are rarely as scrawny as imps, though they vary from squat, muscular dwarfs to eerie fey child-things whose delicate limbs, so perfectly suited for worming into machines to work upon them from inside, conceal a hellish strength. Supple, many-fingered hands are definitive to these hellspawn; they frequently have more fingers than a human should, and they are common longer and/or more heavily jointed than a human's. Some may have tentacles, mechanical plugs or similar accouterments in lieu of fingers. Tied to the machines by their nature, metal and machinery is a frequent component of their physical frames, often increasingly overtaking their flesh as they grow in power. This may vary from plating of metal upon their skin to visibly mechanical limbs and organs intermeshed with their bodies to machinery of metal and/or bone tearing its way through their bodies.

                Visiting Mundus: Artificers have less interest in the mortal world compared to some hellspawn, but they still find reasons to visit. Some are summoned to work for ambitious sorcerers, or by small cults hoping for infernal revelations into the sciences or possibilities of occult engineering; this latter group is likely to find an appreciative audience, for it is rare that artificers have willing students. Other artificers sneak through into the mortal world to find inspiration, or retrieve rare ingredients for their projects, or simply to hide from the cruel masters in the Infernum.

                                Okay, thanks, I can't guarantee I can fill all of them details, especially the more crunchy parts, but I should be able to give you a skeleton to frame up.


                                Artificer
                                Keepers of the Infernal Engines

                                In The Infernum:
                                Any society that wishes to function must have those who make things, and Hell is no exception. Though damned souls toil in countless numbers to provide the brute labor - or raw materials - upon which the Infernum is supported, it is the artificers who truly create the Infernum. They are the engineers and the architects, toiling ceaselessly to shape metal, stone, flesh, bone, soulstuff and other, more esoteric materials upon which hellish civilisation depends for buildings, tools, weapons, everything.

                                And yet, hell has little gratitude to spare. Artificers have long been commodities at best, dismissed as little different to the tools they wield at worst. Even though the black economy of torment and destruction rests upon their squared shoulders, other, "nobler" hellspawn have looked past them. Small wonder, then, that the artificers have been involved with and supportive of the Free City Heresy since the beginning. Some wonder in private if maybe the artificers somehow created the Heresy.

                                Artificers are as variable as all hellspawn when it comes to appearance, but there are certain traits that define them. All artificers are smaller than humans, averaging a childish stature. They are rarely as scrawny as imps, though they vary from squat, muscular dwarfs to eerie fey child-things whose delicate limbs, so perfectly suited for worming into machines to work upon them from inside, conceal a hellish strength. Supple, many-fingered hands are definitive to these hellspawn; they frequently have more fingers than a human should, and they are common longer and/or more heavily jointed than a human's. Some may have tentacles, mechanical plugs or similar accouterments in lieu of fingers. Tied to the machines by their nature, metal and machinery is a frequent component of their physical frames, often increasingly overtaking their flesh as they grow in power. This may vary from plating of metal upon their skin to visibly mechanical limbs and organs intermeshed with their bodies to machinery of metal and/or bone tearing its way through their bodies.


                                Upon the Mundus:
                                Within the Mundus, artificers serve much the same role as they do in the Infernum. They are engineers, architects, medics, beast-handlers, and other roles where their keen grasp of the sciences of machinery and flesh may be useful. Although artificers have been known to slip through into the Mundus of their own free will, generally for the sake of hiding, gathering rarer materials or particular prime souls, or to seek inspiration for new infernal engines, it is much more common that they are summoned by exterior forces.

                                Hellspawn prefer to summon artificers for matters relating to mortal sciences and technology - the typical artificer in the Mundus serves as a brood's "techno-wizard" or "smart guy", using its hellish insight into all forms of machinery and sciences to aid them in whatever schemes have brought the brood to the Mundus in the first place.

                                Mortal summoners are less common in the modern era than they once were, but still possible. Scientists driven to the brink of madness by their obsession with some technological marvel - or horror - may summon one or more artificers to their side to create it, or to teach them how to make it themselves. Maybe even doing so as a result of whispers sent to them across the void by the artificers in the first place. Other hell-cults with delusions of grandeur may summon artificers to create elaborate manors to dwell in, or diabolical traps to protect what is theirs.


                                Sample Characters:
                                #1: Your "bog standard" artificer, this hellspawn focuses on Mental skills, predominantly those related to engineering and electronics. He hacks, he cobbles bombs, he builds things, he maintains ally gear, and generally is intended to stay off of the front-lines and back behind doing tech support.

                                #2: This warrior artificer uses his small stature and intelligence to be an expert trapsmith, assassin, spy, saboteur and marksman. Much as he prefers to crawl in through vents and rig the building to detonate, or snipe from afar, sneaking up on someone and murdering them isn't beyond his skill range either.

                                #3: This artificer defies the stereotype and approaches social interactions as just another complicated mechanism to pick apart and rebuild. Placing Social first and Mental second, he excels at dissecting the minds of others (metaphorically speaking) and then using that knowledge to his advantage.


                                Building Artificer Characters:
                                The typical Artificer puts Mental skills first, Physical skills second and Social skills last. The mind is most important, but the strength to carry heavy loads of raw parts, devices or big guns is also valuable. Social skills suffer, at least if you're playing to stereotypes. The most common Virtues relate to endurance or technical skill - the artificer who takes pride in his diligence, his control, his intellect. Fortitude and Prudence are the most iconic Virtues. The most common Vices relate to their egos and need for respect, though their repressed resentment also finds common expression - Pride, Envy and Wrath, in other words.

                                                    Alright then, I'll see what I can do. Here's a shot at the Beast for you; I just wish I could be more helpful.

                                                    Beast
                                                    Magi of Blood

                                                    In The Infernum:
                                                    Laying claim as one of the oldest and most important of the breeds, the feral forms of these hellspawn belies their keen intelligence and perverse ingenuity. They are the scholars and sages, scientist-wizards from whom most of the innovations and technologies of the Pit have flown. Artificer hands may labor to give life to their vision, but it was this breed who designed the first torture palaces, who pioneered the arts of sorcerery, who mastered the art of soul-binding and luring mortals into Hell's embrace.

                                                    Beasts can be found throughout the Pit, in every conceivable role. Hermit scholars of sin and sorcerery who dedicate eternity to abstruse occultic projects, mercenary warrior-wizards, advisors in the fields of leadership, generals, sorcerers, and petty kings amongst the damned. Beasts gravitate towards positions of authority, propelled by their intellect and focus, and driven by their feral hungers.

                                                    Hellspawn show great variety in their forms, but even they find beasts to be twisted and hideous creatures. Growing ever more feral as they age, the least terrifying of beasts resemble a perverse melding of man and beast. Many others resemble nothing human except maybe in vaguest outlines - nightmarish chimeras born from the imaginings of the most demented souls.


                                                    Upon The Mundus:
                                                    Beasts are second in entering the Mundus of their own free will only to deceivers, and second in being summoned only to malcubi.

                                                    Beasts need souls perhaps more than any other breed, to sate their hunger for the raw fuel to conduct their blasphemous experimentation. Their perverse interests in living humans - as test subjects for some, as food for others, and as playmates for the most perverse - only enhances this desire. So it is that many beasts turn their sorcerous abilities towards breaching the veil and finding their way to the world of man. There, they found cults and seduce minions, offering their skills as teachers in the black arts - or even as untraceable killers - to lure the unwitting into embracing corruption, and damning themselves to be fodder in Hell.

                                                    Yet other beasts are summoned by hell-cultists and would-be sorcerers, and often the beast is not unwilling in its participation. Most commonly they are summoned to be teachers - their effectiveness at tutelage in the black arts or in an advisory role having leaked to the mortal world in certain corrupt texts. Others, more foolish, seek to employ the beasts as living weapons, agents of their own will. These latter cults are most likely to be subverted, either destroyed for their affrontery or twisted to the beast's will, as they foolishly underestimate the true malign cunning that lies behind the inhuman facade. In truth, it is rare indeed where a would-be master does not ultimately find itself conquered and made the slave of the beast.


                                                    Sample Characters:
                                                    #1: This beast is a hermit, with little need for even the fripperies of infernal civilisation. Dedicated to the mastery of sorcerery, it focuses on enhancing its mind and, secondarily, its body. Social niceties are a weakness that it refuses to allow to poison its skill. Although abrasive and unpleasant in the extreme, with little tolerance for those who cannot keep up with its mind, its sheer power as a sorcerer allows it to command a high price.

                                                    #2: This beast has pursued a particularly unlikely course for one of its breed; the path of the healer. Fascinated by the way that flesh, bone and ichor move and can be made to move, it has delved deeper and deeper into the arts of manipulating - and, ultimately, preserving - life. Its ministrations may rarely be pleasant, and are certain to leave scars of some kind, but in its own way, it is a devout medic, mending injuries with a macabre mirth as it hones its skill.

                                                    #3: Whereas most beasts are predators of flesh and blood, this beast is one for minds and souls. Social, charismatic and affable, it interacts with others with surprising adroitness. Beneath its affable exterior lies a hungry for more; those who think it a friend are likely to find themselves its prey once it has satisfied its need to bind them to its will.


                                                    Building Beast Characters:
                                                    Mental Skills are almost always Primary for beasts, though whether Physical or Social is secondary is more individualistic. Iconically, they focus on Mutations and Sorcery over other forms of merit, save those relating to their twisted physiques or their ownership of cultists, slaves or indentured allies. They tend to specialise their skills in branches relating to either the occult or to more abstract areas - a beast is more likely to know how to hack computers than how to forge metal, for example. Diligence, Temperance and similar demonstrations of control, dedication or superiority are the most common Virtues, whilst Wrath and Pride are the most common Vices.
