                                        How big a spoilsport am I? This big.

                                        "Hmmm...so hauntingly beautiful yet utterly empty mansions (and the city Amenti?) hidden beyond the reach of the living and by and large the dead, beyond even the ken of nothingness incarnate, filled with stars that are not stars and secret words that can shape the face of reality or drive men mad. What does this remind me of?....or yeah. Carcosa. This sounds like the vague and brief descriptions from the King in Yellow. Hey, doesn't Mummy have an Ochre Masked King? One that gets banished to an unfathomable 'hell' when his name is erased by powerful Nomenclature every 1000 years? Why yes, I believe it does."

                                        In addition, A'aru is said to be accessible solely by the Judges and seems to be the key to some of their plans. The Judges are merciless, enigmatic entities who cannot be looked upon directly for their true forms cannot be fathomed by the minds of mortals or Mummies. Are we so sure that journeying to this place they covet for themselves is a good thing? These are uncaring god-things who answered the question 'What is the meaning of life?' with 'To Suffer'. They covet A'aru...and that's a good thing? Are we prepared to say that what the Judges want for themselves is something humanity should claim? This is where the Scroll of Ages shines in the night sky, right? The Scroll of all things, with words that not only describe but ARE the underpinnings of all reality, that contains the secrets of existence and the truths underpinning reality itself. Is humanity, or the Arisen, prepared to read those words and know those truths?

                                        Or put it yet another way. A'aru is, arguably, the place where all of Irem's magic began. The stars in its sky are the Words from which all Hekau are drawn- every art of the Guild has its basis in the Scroll. So when has anything Iremite ever, EVER, allowed its magic to be worked without a curse? A'aru sits overhead, waiting for the doors to open, waiting for the purest, ultimate, first curse to issue forth...waiting for THE CURSE to fall upon the world.

                                        ​Have fun climbing the ladder.

                                                        The impression I get, honestly, is that A'aru is actually beyond the reach of the Judges. The Temakh who narrates the introduction to the Book of the Deceived certainly believed that the Judges ruled from A'aru, but as we've seen, the Shan'iatu were mistaken about a great many things. All the Judges seem to be able to do is bar entry to others, but those with sufficient Will can press past them. Indeed, Sutekh explicitly describes the gates of A'aru as "beyond" the Judges, and says that those who look upon the sky beyond the sky will see "why [they] are alone." Along with the reference to A'aru's fields being barren, this seems to suggest that the place is empty save for those who climb to it by Sutekh's ladder.

                                                        But A'aru clearly isn't just empty. Sutekh doesn't describe the fields as deserted, or fallow, but rather barren, as in infertile, incapable of bearing life. I get the sense that something is terribly wrongwith the order of the cosmos if A'aru's fields are barren. Maybe the Heretic isn't just a sort of Deathless Bodhisattva, forsaking paradise in order to help others reach Apotheosis. Maybe Apotheosis is only the first step; maybe once the Deathless have seen "why the fields are barren," they'll have some inkling of what might be done to restore them.
