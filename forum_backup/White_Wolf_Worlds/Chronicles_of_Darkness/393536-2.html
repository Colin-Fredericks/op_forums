The idea of demonic entities based on the 'Seven Deadly Sins" feels restrictive in a broader, global context, given that the mythological precision required by Inferno was so memetically tiny that it did not cover the vast capacity of the human imagination to invent terrors of its own folly and instead narrowed itself down to such a tiny fragment of even Christian ideology that the supplement was nearly useless on its own, unless you used it to describe, perhaps, a specific realm in Shadow or any of the broad Otherworlds already present, in which case you're better off seeing one of the supplements for that Otherworld in general. Talking about how the World of Darkness world doesn't fit well at the universal levels? That's great - Inferno doesn't fit at all, even at its own level.

                                I mean - who's to say "demons" aren't conflated with fae, spirits, or ghosts, or all at the same time? Faerie legends were certainly integrated into Christian mythology as demons, that's where the whole thing about iron came from - Fae can't enter graveyards/holy places, most of those places used iron gates, so iron became the foe of the devil (so is salt - you're supposed to throw that shit over your left shoulder into the devil's face) - which, the idea of a fallen Lucifer is conflated from the story of an unnamed human Babylonian king associated with the astrological sign of Venus who was being poetically dissed by Isaiah. Baptists believe that any contact with ghosts is actually with demons in the shape of ghosts, and that the gods of Hinduism are creations by the devil to lead people who have never heard of their god astray, never mind that Hinduism is the oldest extant, still practiced religion in the world. Vampires can also very easily be conflated with demons - beings that are here by some cosmological fluke and represent the maddened desires of mortal flesh? Buddhism is rife with them, but they eat your shit, not your blood, which they take out of you through your stomach using their hands - but they are certainly capable of enslaving and corrupting mortals with their forbidden temptations.

                                I guess my question is "why?" What would Inferno 2 do that, say, Dread Powers or Numina (or - dare I speak it? - a Tempter run out of Descent) don't do already?

                                                            The inherent problem with the Inferno is its universal nature wherein it positions itself as the source of all these entities. Oni, for example, are mentioned as languishing on piles of bones in the Inferno when, quite frankly, the attitudes of the Oni (and indeed many if not all Eastern wicked spirits) are more in line with the attitudes of the True Fae. That is to say, the mentality of these entities is supposed to be alien and destructive in that the entity in itself encourages destructive attitudes out of an inability to do anything else.

                                                            The (new) World of Darkness is full of alien forces encountering the familiar and twisting them in strange and bizarre ways. The God-Machine does this, the True Fae do this, the Spirits do this. Inferno attempts to bring a familiar element - human evil - into the scenario and then make that evil supernatural (something already achieved by malevolent ghosts) but also inhuman and alien.

                                                            Mechanically, the creatures that reside in Inferno are just mean ghosts. They are a creature of murder that encourages people to murder so that it can drag them into its own torment. Just like the ghost of a slasher might linger after his passing and encourage the birth of a copy-cat killer. Functionally then we see, both within the narrative and the mechanics of the game, there is little that differentiates the Infernal from a malignant ghost or spirit.

                                                            Indeed the book even suggests the already present element of the malignant spirit - the Maeljin from Werewolf - are likely these selfsame entities. If Immundi are like Maeljin of another name, both functionally and fictionally, and the Larvals are just malignant ghosts in the same respect we're only left with the supposed distinctive qualities of the Dominion. However, what separates a Dominion from an Immundi? Both are ephemeral entities, both have rank, both rely heavily on Vice. Within the context of their own rules both even use the "Malapraxis" and "Demonic Universal Traits".

                                                            Within the context of the updated second-edition ephemeral creatures rules this becomes even more obfuscated. Spirits can already embody any and all vices. A spirit is fueled entirely, as its sole motivating existence, by its nature. Anything relating to this nature, in any distinct way is Resonant with the spirit. Therefore if its nature is vice then is it not just one of these above mentioned "demons"? What makes a Dominion different from a malevolent spirit other that the fact that it comes from a place called "Inferno" and possessed a certain set of rules in the previous edition? If a spirit is a "Spirit of Lust" then anywhere - from nightclubs to brothels - will resonate with it. People behaving in reckless pursuit of their desires, especially bodily, will resonate with it. It will regain essence and willpower as easily as any demon of the Inferno, its more or less mechanically functioning like one, isn't it?

                                                            While contrary to my above argument I took the time to convert Dominions (and to a lesser extent Larvals and Immundi) into the new rules - that conversion was mostly just creating a dependent condition (similar to Resonant), setting up the demonic traits - which really are just numina - as things that all demons possessed (like the Liars Tongue) and lastly a manifestation power to cause and improve their dependent condition. In doing this I noticed very little actual difference between them and a Spirit mechanically. Really it all came down to the trappings and that isn't really worth a new book, is it?

                                                            In the end I noticed there was only one thing I truly liked and felt supported Infernals as something different: the whisperer. This I felt translated best as a condition. I also felt it lent better to the idea presented in the whisperer section's sidebar; the demons aren't "real". They're born of human thought. As the Buddhists imagine, we make our own demons, and therein lies why they might be deserving of some separation from spirits. Spirits aren't born of our interaction with the world around us. They spontaneously come into being through presence and absorbing energies. But a whisperer pops up when your psyche shatters, something slips up through the cracks of your growing insanity and whispers into your ear for you to break the status quo.

                                                            I have recently found myself finding the Inferno and its inhabitants rather redundant. Between Spirits, the God-Machine's ever deceptive angels and their fallen counterparts, and malevolent ghosts any group of mortals, hunters or even magi have everything they could want and more to bind up with the Lesser Key of Solomon and flick holy water at and hell, it might work on some of them too, who knows? The only thing I really find myself clinging to and still thoroughly enjoying from the book is the idea that a Whisperer might latch onto somebody when they take an integrity hit and become something new. No ancient evil from the birth of creation, but something young and fresh, a day old evil that crawls about gorging itself desperately in a dim hope of survival before fading away or infecting something else.

                                                            Bringing my post back to the beginning. Why does the book present the Inferno as human evil when it is another dimension full of alien monsters that don't really understand our world and just want to destroy it? Why are they so much like us? They sound like the True Fae to me, just ones that like to throw up the horns and wreak a little havoc before they drag you to their weird, conceptual world of nightmare.
