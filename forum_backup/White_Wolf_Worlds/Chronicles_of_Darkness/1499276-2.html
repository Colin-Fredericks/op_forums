                                        Originally posted by GibberingEloquence View Post
                                        If the consensus is "Priest" but also "it'd be nice to keep the symmetry with the adjectival names of the other Forsworn", why not "The Sacrosanct"?
                                        Funnily enough, that did cross my mind last night, particularly since the Sin-Eaters aren't really using it anymore. I'm chewing on it.

                                        Originally posted by Aeveth View Post
                                        I got a bit confused with "rejecting Fear of the Other" should I switch track to some kind of character that inclusive to the point of foolishness? if they reject the fear of the other then aren't they accepting the other despite clear warning signs present on them?
                                        A thing of use for the consideration is to remember that the lie Heroes in the Forsworn model tell themselves is that they have no fear of [insert Family Nightmare here], and that any instance where it seems like they do is because of outside forces instead of anything that reflects on that them. The justification need not be logically based or sound, because that's not how people work, but it does need to be rooted in something that feels justifiable. Not every Hero is strictly going to adhere to that script, but we're trying to build a splat here, and commonalities exist between excuses. It's bullshit of course, but with Astral/Dreaming metaphysics backing that perception, it is a lie with enough heavy reinforcement in place as to operate like a truth.

                                        In the particular case of the Forsworn of the Other, the notion is that you could present the Hero with the most disturbing, alien, out of your comfort zone sort of person to them, and they wouldn't flinch, and if they do, it can generally only be because the person in question has malevolent intentions and it's showing. Because the fear of the other manifests in a lot of ways, even in the most casual of ways, the easiest way to justify that is to assume malice is just present in a lot of people who do not form the mainstay of the people the Hero is regularly around-the Other is not othered because we're all just a little bit scared of difference and need time to understand and get used to it, the other is Othered because there is something immutably violent or violating in the sort of person they are, because there is just something that will always be wrong about them. Everyone knows black people want to enslave whites, every conservative wants to throw trans people off a cliff, and that anyone who puts broccoli on pizza just wants to ruin everything good and wholesome in the world, that sort of thing.

                                        This sort of things spirals inward for Heroes as well-they have to know themselves, who they are, where they're going, and why they feel and do things. A Hero can't("can't") be self-alienated, can't be self-othered. If a foreign thought or feeling comes in, it's not because maybe they're gay or, hell, just likes flowers or whatever, it's because they're dumping chemicals in the river that's turning the frogs gay or that they keep hammering this idea that everyone has to like flowers and the brainwashing's getting to them. The Hero, of course, knows who and what they are and why that is-until they don't, if they ever do. The alternative is admitting to being afraid of the other.

                                        The three big things I keep in mind for the Inguma and their Forsworn are the formative effect of self-alienation put forward in Lacan's theory about mirror stage initiation, taking Abed from Community's statement of "When you really know who you are and what you like about yourself, changing for other people isn't so hard." as the thesis statement of the Inguma, and that while I am always thinking Sutek's statement and doubly so for Beast, it hits particularly hard for the Inguma:

                                        Originally posted by Sutek, Dreams of Avarice pg. 58
                                        I am Will. I defy the Law of Suffering. The Judges of Duat deprived me of my body, but gave me all Sekhem in which to dwell. I am the secret father of the gods, for the Judges and all their servants erase my legacy from Remet memory. They make me the Stranger, Lord of the Sandstorm. I am these things and more, because winds of disquiet roar in your five Pillars, and until you know me, you are a foreigner in the lands of your spirit. I am the tempter of the Shan’iatu, for I do not dwell in Sekhem alone, but in all acts of desire.

                                                        THe only one of those I know off hand is Javert, but Javert is actually an interesting case study for the Inguma's Forsworn. Having been "born inside a jail" and "from the gutter too", Javert dedicated himself to the law as a desperate scramble to separate himself from his origin, and Javert's entire problem in Les Mis is that "Men like you can never change-men like me can never change." He has to see criminals as consistently evil, unable to escape that "every man is born in sin", as a justification for his own abuses, abuses he sees as baked into himself but rendered to the service of orderly powers. People are what they are born into, and Javert's fatalism has trouble reconciling with Valjean because his actions don't make sense in that world model, but accepting the alternative means that not only is Javert wrong about every criminal he's ever worked with, but himself as well. Small wonder he commits suicide when forced to face it.

                                                        As a general rule, the Forsworn Heroes are tyrannical and inflexible. Because they're afraid of fear and deal with it the way most of us instinctively do (by avoiding the things that scare us), they can't (well, really really really don't want to, more like) look in when it comes up, so instead they look outward, order and justify the world, and violently react when things challenge the worldview.

                                                        THe Priest/Sacrosanct(giving it a try) can try and just be cool and calm around everyone no matter what as their initial expression of it, but eventually, whether it happens in a benign or malevolent way, they're going to run into a situation where they seem skittish around someone. It can't be they're afraid of the other when that happens, that unfamiliar forms of people are just inherently unnerving (because our brains are wired to then pay attention and closely scrutinize a person to see if they might be secretly harmful or are harmless but odd, because survival). No, it's got to be something wrong about them to begin with. But coming up to individual determinations of why each and every single person you come across is individually wrong is a lot of work, so they rely on personal experiences, stereotypes and biases they've heard all their life, expectations dervied from jokes and other casual language, and build a model where everyone has a score, where groups are uniform and in lockstep with each other as far as forms of malice are concerned. Because they're denied reaction because of their avoidance, they have to pre-empt and assume in advance, and to do that, you have to "Know" your enemy. So, that's what they do. They "know" what those people are like, they "know" what's always on their mind, they "know" what evil lurks in the hearts of men. They "know" that men can never change, that the cirucmstances of one's birth define one's life, and any attempt to persuade against that is just deception, setup for what they were always going to do. Eventually, this escalates into just "knowing" human nature, which often includes having an "absolute knowledge" about humanity's role in eternity and infinity. Whether this endgame is understanding humanity as a collective machine meant to discover the Ultimate Question to the Answer of Life, The Universe, and Everything or that God has a Plan of Salvation that spells out who will and will not attain the highest kingdom of glory in the universe isn't all that important, so much as the Hero "knows" what betrays a person's malice as openly and plainly as possible, and that there's a explanation for why that's the case.

                                                        It's a negative arc that builds on itself. Nothing is never enough, and all they can do is build up, build out, and try to control as much as they can, until one has a kingdom of spears that can never be aimed at the actual problem, given the demon is their own heart, but allow them to lie to themselves and others again and again. That it cages and strangles everyone else isn't their problem.

                                                            ArcaneArts I noticed a concerning and disappointing pattern with your framing of each Forsworn so far, especially toward the end segments. They all describe some terrible fate, some failure-state that is a mostly logical conclusion of the corresponding beliefs, a sort of Greek tragedy, a self-fulfilling prophecy... something that is embedded into the bedrock of reality, so to say. I might be reading it way too cynically and overall being reductive, but my current takeaway is that Forsworn-Heroes have only 2 endings: accidental or deliberate self-destruction as the price of their demented crusades to prove they are not afraid, or "lmao just stop being a Hero, dumbass".

                                                            And that's... kinda too bleak for me. It doesn't feel mythic, or like horror. It feels like a reason to give up on my desire to GM or play Beast one day. Because I "know" how it's gonna end, and it's going to be the hollow sort of tragedy that doesn't get the sorrow out, it just... leaves me questioning why I ever bothered at all. I don't want it to turn out that way, and I'm quite confident others wouldn't want that either, including you. So, to make this into a more concrete criticism:

                                                            1) Are the Forsworn the only plot-relevant type of Hero? Beast Core had that downer of a sidebar, you know the one. "Good Heroes exist but don't really matter because they are not proper antagonist material", was my takeaway from it. I am well aware we did get more nuanced Heroes in supplements, I appreciate that (especially Soccer Mom Gigachad and Tinnitus Black Manta, I might actually use them as NPCs!).

                                                            2) If there are other types which are plot-relevant, are they actually Heroes as such? Or are they distinct archetypes, with their own metaphysical niches and traits? I vaguely remember a thread that talked about such alternate archetypes, such as The Fool.

                                                            3) Can Heroes change into other mythic archetypes? Does a Hero's Journey have to end in either doom/death or giving up the sword to plant flowers, or can they find fulfilment and purpose by adopting other roles, perhaps by mixing and matching ideas and symbols from the Bright Dream, Primordial Dream, and other Temenotic Realms? I wholeheartedly believe there is genuine material to explore in the idea of people who are sick of pseudo-Heroism, and want to live up to the ideal version of that role.

                                                            And perhaps for a chosen few, the Astral listens and becomes psycho-spiritually linked to these individuals. Not quite Comic Book Superheroes as we see in Marvel, DC or various animes/mangas, though. Something that is a mid-point between "glorious shining paragon of humanity fighting back the forces of darkness and inspiring others towards greatness" and "broken empty shell of an idealist who somehow has not locked themselves up and cried until they drowned in their own tears".
