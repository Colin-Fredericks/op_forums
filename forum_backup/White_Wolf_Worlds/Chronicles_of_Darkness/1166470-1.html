                Originally posted by Eremite View Post
                I’m thinking they have three Covers from different social circles/strata; the Mayor (seldom seen, too busy/important and obviously a part of the conspiracy), a prominent citizen players regularly interact with (and hopefully come to trust) and someone quite innocuous (but a well-placed spy) like a homeless person or child.
                The covers:
                The mayor. Recommend the mayor's secretary. A good secretary can control access to who gets to see the mayor, and what the mayor in turn gets to see, while being nearly invisible, the person at the front desk is just so much furniture, right? Also, politicians come and go, but career civil service is in the ground floor for life.

                The prominent citizen. Recommend the town grocery store owner. (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JgJt4sArUHI). If the community is small enough, they might be the only game in town. In addition to be a major nerve center for town gossip, the owner is also in a position to get what people need ("Yes, Ms. Thompson, I understand that times are tough, tell you what, I think we can fudge today's bill in exchange for a small favor")

                The innocuous. Possible choices include a local veteran, or a disowned LGBT youth forced out onto the streets. Both can disappear from and reappear in town without arousing suspicion.

                    Originally posted by Eremite View Post

                    I quite like this... as it can easily be combined with suborned Infrastructure ( once operated/protected/attended/etc by human cultists). This town was built around some Infrastructure. Perhaps this fell into disuse at some point and the demon moved to reactivate it (or just took it over), creating/subverting a cult and using the Infrastructure as an Aether supply.
                    I like this theme. The remnants of abandoned factories and stores dotting the landscape, leaving desolated communities in their wake, reflects the G-M's habit of starting projects, abandoning them, and then repurposing them without regard to anyone caught in It’s way.

                    Sources to mine:

                    Jan Harold Brunvald has written a number of works about urban legends and American folklore, it sounds like you’re using a UK location, but there’s still good material

                    UFO Crash at Roswell: Genesis of a Modern Myth by Benson Saler

                    Methland: The Death and Life of an American Small Town by Nick Reding

                    RPGs: Heaven and Earth is an old series by Abstract Nova Press, it has a small-town Weirdness setting with theistic horror theme.

                    The Esoterrorists by Pelgrane, is more developed around an eeeevil cult, but it does have a nice, modular town that you can pull apart for details

                    Podcasts: Welcome to Nightvale

                    The Black Tapes

                    Someone Knows Something - Nonfiction, very good.

                    King Falls AM

                    The Ozarks Explained


                    Suggested locals:

                    The Abandoned Airfield. I guarantee you, this will get your players attention. Doesn't matter what kind of clues you put there, the words Abandoned Airfield will draw them like like flies to honey. Could be a pilot’s logbook with UFO sightings, could be the remains of an old smuggling operation cargo, could be an active smuggling operation with modern tech (drones, ultra-light cesna aircraft)

                    The Local military installation. Is your small town dependent on a military base for economic viability? How do the locals feel about it?

                    Third generation legal practice. Lawyers/solicitors can effectively become local aristocracy, potentially making their mark in local politics. If a particular law firm has operated across multiple generations, it likely has rooms full of old files that aren’t digitally stored, possibly going back before the town's founding. Good place to investigate land/property transfers, odd deaths, etc.

                    The town recreation centers. Look, even cultist like ice skating, or bowling, or eating frozen yogurt.

                    The remains of other supernatural societies. Despite the whole idea of supernatural societies dating back to Atlantis/father wolf/the dark mother/ whatever, my view of supernatural groups is that they’re more like street gangs or role-playing groups: the come together, they last a while, then they dissipate. Finding out that your town once had a group of supernatural protectors (or overlords) that just moved on with their lives can be a poignant statement on the eventual fate small town they once protected… and then there’s any supernatural threats they might have left behind, unattended.
