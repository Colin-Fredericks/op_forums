    Must Be This Tall To Be a World Power-Reconciling Organizational Power Stats

    03-27-2023, 08:59 PM
    Immediately clarifying, I don't mean the actual Power (Reach, in the case of Curse) attribute, I mean Power Stat in the way that it would be applied to templates, specifically talking about Esotery, Dominance, and Standing.

    Okay, so this could actually be a bit of a conversation because of farther reaching implications, which is why this isn't in Ask a Simple Question.

    So ever since Geist: the Sin-Eaters Second Edition, we've had a steady roll out of organizational rulesets, the sort that's intended to tackle larger group where everyone is unlikely to know about everyone else, with it's culmination coming in Deviant: the Renegades (for which this ruleset was first imagined for), and with the Devoted Companion out to backers, it's proven to be a very power and useful toolset for what it's exactly meant to do-and more asides. As many people have pointed out, the Conspiracy rules are solid for creating any sort of organized society, in different levels of granularities. As a handful of examples, you could use it to model occult matrices in Descent, a freehold in harmony with each Court as their own branch wtih their own Hierarchical Node or a freehold more at a distance or odds with each other by giving each court their own full mockup, the various levels of Mage's sectarian organizations from the global perspective down into individual Caucuses/Assemblies and the Consiliums between that form from them, and even for mapping the global spread and quarantine of the Contagion (ala a near-Pandemic like implementation). It's versatile, is what I'm saying.

    But of course, each of these models aren't simply copy pasted-The system for Krewes has notable differences from the Cult system in Curse, and both are subtly different from Conspiracies in Renegades. On the whole, this is good, because these games are saying and doing something different with the rulesets in accordance to their own themes and mechanical structuring. But when we start cross-applying the tools, a thing does come up that becomes worth questioning, and that's the matter of scale.

    To best illustrate this, I'm going to hone in on Esotery against Standing*. See, Standing's pretty good for systems that are large for their tier, and it particularly stands out well for laying out globally wide organizations-you could very easily display the collective power, authority, and resources of the Mage Sects or the Arisen Guilds, as sideways examples. But despite being in a format that assumes a ready one to one, that's not the same assumption you could make with Esotery in Sin-Eaters. Esotery already represents something contextually different-there's a lot more emphasis on religious practice and spiritual belief as both "glue" and power as much as it represents institutional reach, presence, and influence-but going back to a bone dry comparison, a Krewe of Esotery 10 is just not the same as a Conspiracy of Standing 10-comparatively, you could make an argument, depending on the exact nature of the Krewe, it would have a Standing likely somewhere in the range of 5 to 8.

    Since there's a lot of utilization in implementing the Conspiracy Rules for these other communities, I figured it's probably worth having a chat on how to handle the disparity, or if that disparity is actually as much their as I assert it is (arguably, Esotery and Dominance can still be on scale with Standing, just more readily split between worlds). So, let's have that discussion.

    *Dominance from Curse, and Arisen Cults in general, are both pretty different that it becomes hard to cut lines across, and Cults sit in this weird range where it can both make a lot of sense to draw one-to-one with Conspiracies and not make a lot of sense to make those same drawings. The Arisen are complicated, who knew.

                          So as far as the existing systems go, the Devoted Companion's expansion of the conspiracy mechanics actually has me thinking on Standing versus Dominance; stating outright that Resistance is never rolled for conspiracy actions makes it a little easier to align Reach and Grasp with Power and Finesse in comparable mechanics, for instance.

                          I've remarked before on how conspiracies adding a lower fixed value to an Attribute for their Association makes them more fragile than an equivalent krewe or cult, but it's interesting to note that Fidelity being calculated off of the lower of a cult's two Attributes, combined with the cheaper dots from the Cult Merit capping at 5, incentivizes against skewing its legal and illegal influence too far in either direction.

                          It's also interesting to note, bringing it back to krewes, that Congregation is the only Organizational Health Stat that actually models a state that can be directly harmed by killing its membership — Fidelity and Association represent faith and loyalty to the organization, whereas Congregation represents the membership's overall well-being alongside its unity and cohesiveness (Association also corresponds to the size of a conspiracy's following and can be damaged by massive loss of human life, but conspiracies occupy a narrative niche where goon combat is a feasible arrow in their quiver and krewes give an approximation for your group's NPC headcount by Resistance).

                          Taking cues from these (and, to a lesser extent, Social Maneuvering's grounding in the assumption that the other party's continued trust in you is a legitimate stake), expanding the organizational rules to other gamelines might have to account for different functions — allowing Vampire to play the Institutional Corruption game means something different than modeling spirit-ecological give-and-take through the Web of Pain's mapping structure, and the way the God-Machine treats its tools means a set of Infrastructure treats crises of faith as either trivially unimportant or catastrophic depending on what's having them.

                          On the topic of mapping Esotery to Standing, there's also the general scaling issue of the vast majority of krewes starting out with a foot in the door re: the Underworld where a conspiracy would need to be particularly high-powered or have an Icon lowering the effective scale of a Narrative Project to make headway in another world — the fact that most people who don't have major-template-level association with a given otherworld have to fumble around with Keys makes comparing a handful of death-divers and their ghost-and-medium support crew to a global conglomerate looking to frack The Oldest Oil gets a bit squidgy.

                          Some of this is admittedly just rehashing the points addressed in the starting post, but it probably bears acknowledgement that the games have generally done fine with e.g. Shadow Potency and Rank not being wholly equivalent stats despite both functioning as Supernatural Tolerance ratings. I vaguely remember there being a Charm in Exalted that allowed you to act as an organization of a scale determined by your Essence, which might be an interesting concept for a power in any given other game or at least a basis for bridging the gap between "here are two to five PCs" and "here is a Standing 1 organization."
