    I mentioned earlier how the animals a group hunts informs their culture in an earlier post. I'm gonna build on that in two ways.

    1)While there are huge differences between killing a bear and killing a snake(namely I shouldn't even call hunting a snake hunting really), consider that learning how to hunt an elk informs how to hunt many other animals, even if it's not a usual translation. The skills of one may not perfectly apply all the time, but it's easier for someone with expertise with one type of prey to adapt and apply that to others.

    Let's apply this idea to the Tribes....Because it's even simpler to see. For example, let's go with the Storm Lords and their targets of the Spirit-Possessed. Quite frankly, while the specialization helps, consider that a Spirit Ridden often has similar(if sometimes diminished) advantages as a werewolf, as a creature of flesh and spirit with the two warping each other, so a Iminir isn't going to be freaking lost filling in for the Blood Talon. The Shartha also apply to the moniker, albeit with a tough escape mechanism that unless you spend a lot of time thinking on isn't going to be easy to deal with, but by all means a Lord is going to be able to handle the stuffed flesh bags in their first incarnation thanks to what they know about the Spirit Ridden just as easily. To deal with them either as Urged or as Ridden, You have to understand the way a spirit and a human thinks and how they respond under different circumstances in order to harry,trap and deal with them, so while their knowledge isn't as extensive as the Bone Shadows or the Iron Masters, they have a fair amount of knowledge under their belt because they needed some of this knowledge when hunting their chosen prey.

    Other tribes might not have so lucky a spread(In fact, I suspect now that the reason the Lords specialized in the Spirit Possessed is because it does give them so much of broad advantage), but the simple fact is that it's kind of stupid to assume that just because your Tribe claims that this is the biggest prey means you'll be left on the sidelines when this isn't the main focus. It's not hard to jury rig what you know into application for whatever the circumstance is.

    2)The chosen prey thing is foundational. It is the seed, not the tree. It's a cornerstone, not the house. The Tribe is not the Prey, the Prey is the Tribe.

    Again, I already brought up the idea of how the things we hunt informs culture-but it is very important to understand that it informs it, rather than completely define it. Will it matter more with the Uratha, with their focus on Hunting, tradition, etc., etc.? Sure, yeah. But it's not going to be all consuming.

    So with that said, a few other things.

    @MCN: If I was your player, and you told me you were running a a game with the Pure as the main antagonist, I'd probably play an Iron Master, maybe a Storm Lord.

    Why? Because I like playing Iron Masters and Storm Lords.

    I understand people's concerns about twinkery, but quite frankly WoD has never struck me as a game that's rewarding when you twink-it lives more off of the characters being characters that optimally primed to take things out like a nuke. If a person twinks their character to be a supreme Werewolf Killer, that's fine, that's a concept in and of itself, and so long as it works as a character that's cool, but I don't think WoD plays out like that most of the time.

    I think there's a problem in the way we conceive of our stories. I build my stories off of the characters, so that might just be me, but even when I come up with generic ideas, it's never really a "this is my big antagonist, this game is defined by that". For generic scenarios, I build off of a theme and flesh out the city and characters, including the antagonists, from there. I have trouble coming up with a scenario that is explicitly "This is a Pure Driven game, ergo a Blood Talon game". I tend to think more in terms of "This is a game about familial expectations" and build from there, and if the final antagonist is a Pure who happens to magically be everyone's grandfather, that's circumstantial. Probably the one to strike him down is not the Blood Talon, but whoever is hurt by him the most.

    I'm just saying, maybe we want to step back from the strokes and see the painting.

    Annnd I actually don't have as much to say as I thought I did.

    EDIT: The more is revealed about the Storm Lords, the more they make sense.

        Originally posted by Rose Bailey View Post
        I don't want to get too far into spoilers that are rightfully Stew's, but I wanted to clarify this.
        Yes, any pack can hunt any prey. Tribal prey represent a cultural focus with some mechanical teeth, but you don't need a specialist from a certain tribe to take down certain prey.
        Thanks Rose!

        The way I am choosing to look at this is each Tribe has a specific/cultural long term focus and as such, have a certain mechanical bonus for hunting that type of prey. This in no way means other tribes can't hunt them or even develop a bias versus a type of prey they are not typically known for. It is much the way that Auspices work in that Ithaeur have an advantage with rituals due to the auspice blessing, but that doesn't mean you can't find a Rahu ritualist who is just as good.

        Now admittedly, the mechanical benefits are still unknown, but I doubt it would be something earthshatteringly overpowered compared to the rest such as Blood Talons deal aggravated damage to werewolves while everyone else does lethal. It may be something as simple as +1 extra die to resist werewolf powers or +1 to use powers against them.
