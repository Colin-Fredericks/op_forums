                            For a start, I think that the world featured in the Chronicles of Darkness contains more than enough things possessed of magical powers that it's not really necessary for any given one of them to become made out of Perfected Material in the process, nor is anything about the process likely to track to the established premise of how material becomes perfected. On the face of it, I find it no more necessary than any random imbued item qualifying for such. Hell, actual Artifacts are often supposed to have a premise of quite literally being Supernal objects, and even those aren't intrinsically made of Perfected Material.

                            Perfected Material has the quality of an idealised form of what might be regarded as that material's purpose and function, which mages regard as a thing being brought as close as possible to becoming its symbolic form. That's not my understanding of what the likes of Relics are. I gather that they're more like objects that have been deeply imbued with meaning, often particularly placed in their cultural context and perception. A Relic object that is made of glass is not necessarily the idealised form of what glass can be used for, but possesses properties evocative of what the object represents or how it was valued. If Sekhem can be described as the vividness of life, then my impression is that Relics are instances of that being literally captured in objects that express or represent that vividness. I find that distinct from some Supernal metaphysics in general, and Perfected Materials in particular.

                            Besides, the description of what Perfected glass actually is probably precludes any written example of a Relic that is made of glass.

                            With regards to how Mummy metaphysics might interact with Mage ones (a subject I think is prompted when some crossover references go out of their way to describe mage ideas of what the magic of the Shan'iatu was like), I speculate that it's something like... when you look at a place with Mage Sight, the Supernal symbols you can perceive are the lens through which you recognise its meaning, the underlying emotional quality native to it. Sekhem is the actual aspect of the place that provides the basis for those symbols appearing. Any given Awakened may or may not believe that the Supernal and its symbols are the actual origin point of all that, but Sekhem as Mummies recognise and interact with it could probably be seen as its presence in the world as actually experienced.

                            Think of it like how Mage Sight through the Matter Arcanum might tell you many and true things about a crystal of some salt, and you can even cast spells to affect it, but at the end of the day (barring certain philosophical abstractions) the crystal is still a thing that exists for itself rather than just in terms of the symbols it's seen through, and a chemist can have the expertise of that existence to do things with it that are distinct from Supernal magic.

                            I'd say that if a Mummy well versed in their own metaphysics gained a good grounding in mage philosophy, they'd be of the opinion that Sekhem is a quality of the world that precedes any Supernal nature to it, and there can even be mages who would agree with them. Not all sects necessarily regard the relationship of Phenomenal and Supernal in the neo-Platonic terms of "the idealised Forms of which the Supernal is composed are the original template for all phenomena", and the core book describes some of the ambiguities of their cosmology from which comes a speculation that things might exist first in the Fallen World and then become rarefied to the point of becoming the pure symbols they perceive as transmitting reality (even before getting into the question of influences brought in by strange and alien dimensions).

                            I have approximate knowledge of many things.

                                    Originally posted by KaiserAfini View Post
                                    With knowledge of Prime or Life, a Node can bring any plant put there to any point it its life cycle, as long as that point would take a year or less. Lets say a mage has a peach seed, which takes up to 4 years to bear fruit, could a they use the effect of the ley line during 4 continuous days to hasten the process ?
                                    My read would be no, because the intention seems to be to specifically to prevent characters from crossing two leylines and thereafter being able to get drastically accelerated production on e.g. the Sierra Redwood's usual decades-to-centuries-long timeline for viable seeds.

                                    The idea is pretty clearly for things that would hit the flowering/fruiting/etc stage within the year to be able to have that stage brought about with a day's work rather than that a non-Time-based Node effect allows you to accelerate growth at an arbitrary rate of up to one year per day without additional magic. It's for gardening and for getting more out of an orchard that you've got ready moreso than for making oaks from acorns.

A Dedicated Tool is a specific instance of a yantra which the mage habitually incorporates into his spells. Its the form and symbol of the tool that are important, rather than the material.
