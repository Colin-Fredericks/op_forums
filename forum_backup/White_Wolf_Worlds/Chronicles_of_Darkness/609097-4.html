                                    It's weird, I never considered a True Name to be "True" and therefore unchangeable because someone gave it to you or by some law of a Narcissistic universe; I figured it was because you responded to it an extremely formative state and capable of identifying with it seemlessly because you had no predisposition as to what "Your Name" was meant to be beyond the entity that is "Self" which in its on way made it Sublime. In my games I house rule the "Truth" of "True Names" and make it more of what you identified with more so than what was on a birth record. Only after being introduced to the prejudices of the big ugly world does a person lose value in their "Name/Identity" which isn't to say it wasn't true, just tainted by alien ideologies.

                                            As indicated earlier, the act of naming is a powerful and controlling force, often part of creation itself, it often has nothing to do with what the subject actually wants or believes, and is consistent with much of the body of established literature and myth concerning names. The shadow name is what reflects a character's identity, and the real name simply is a mage's first identification in the Fallen World, no more, no less. The issues you describe are also a potentially plentiful source of role-playing opportunities. For instance, read the opening fiction to Imperial Mysteries, aptly entitled "Names."

                                            Nevertheless, I understand how the concept, no matter how well established, may be uncomfortable for some people. If that's the case, either permit a change of the character's real/true name as part of the chronicle or character backstory (i.e., some involvement by an archmaster or significant power) or simply modify the rule to suit your preferences. I don't, however, believe the game needs to change a default, well-established, and fairly fundamental, mechanic that is consistent with real world mysticism.

                                                    I ways kinda figured that the True Name thing worked alittle like a computer. When a sympathetic spell is cast, the spell is given a name that that spell-action is to target. A file it searches for, so to speak. Lets say that that file is called "Krobeles". To aid it in its search, it looks up other reference to "Krobeles" and finds relatively few and/or incorrect referances, because most of the people I interact with on a daily basis dont know that I use "Krobeles" as my internet handle. If you'de used my real name on the other hand, multiple references would pop up because I am associated with more of those referances.
