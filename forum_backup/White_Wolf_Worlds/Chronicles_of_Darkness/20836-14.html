            Ok, before I start that other thread, it sounds like this is the new direction we're taking. I have a code-name for this project that's been floating around in my head, so I'ma use it now.

            Dragon Rekindled: The Orobori Chronicles
            A New Direction
            Basically, we're going to be taking 2nd-edition as a base, things we liked from 3rd, and adding in new stuff that we're coming up with. The new edition will be built for compatibility with the new God Machine and Chronicles setting and rules.

            2nd Edition - It sounds like we'll be going back to the original Dreamtide storyline, because that works, using more of the old nomenclature, and building onto what was there.

            3rd Edition - The re-differentiation of schools and expansion of edicts seem to work, and there were new mechanics and ablutions that would be nice to bring in. The fact that losing a Heart isn't insta-death looks like a huge help for party-paranoia. I think the problem with 3rd edition was that there was a lot of response to unqualified criticism, but there were still some nice additions.

            New Additions - It sounds like we're going to expand on the Dreamtide origins of Dragon's powers, including things like Reimagining, "Divine" Power, and Dream Sorcery. Also seeing an overhaul will be the Integrity system, which will have less emphasis on "being in charge" and more emphasis on Guilt, Hubris, and balancing Divine powers with lingering Humanity. On top of that, we'll probably have to rewrite the Ominous Drums story eventually...

            Research - It will be very important to read up on older stories about dragons of this nature, especially since there is a strong tendency among this generation's writers to pull things from things like anime and video-games without really understanding them. For more recent fiction, I can recommend The Dragon Knight, The Hobbit, and Dragonheart as good sources of inspiration for talking dragons living with humans. (especially Dragon Knight, because it's directly about a human who gets magical powers and a dragon form) For older fiction, we should look at older accounts of Dragons (obviously), especially old chri            stian lore and grimoires. Stuff like Walking with Dragons MAY be useful, but I WILL note that the Orobori are imagined creatures and don't have to follow real-life physics. There's also the matter of philosophy in play here: I already have ideas for pulling things in from Rene Descartes' meditations on divinity and Plato's concept of forms and purity, as well as the works of Machivelli and Anton Lavey.

            Contemplation - Standing rule: everything we get into here should be thought about in dual terms of "how does this fit into the established meta?" and "what would happen if this was in the real world?" Because that's where it will be. Even the Dreamtide is merely an extension of a real thing, because humans dream and imagine things. Don't have dragons doing insane things unless you've thought through why they make sense and how the world would react

            God Machine - The Meta will be partially re-written, both to fine tune the old stuff and to incorporate the setting with the God Machine, as well as the World Of Darkness setting in general, the interconnections of which is often under-anticipated by people who haven't read a few of the Mage and expanded-lore / mechanics books along with the core books. (trust me, once you've seen a bunch of them, it all fits together: thanks Exarchs.) As per the other books, affected mortals will see their own appendix with powerful stats.

            Other Books - I've always seen Dragons as a cross between Vampires, Werewolves, and Shape Changers, with a bit of Mage and Changeling lore in there. Like Vampires, they must feed on something relatively simple to regain their pool, but they also need to spend one a day to simply survive, and pretty much have to use it to heal themselves. Like Werewolves they have these powerful forms which are holdovers from a realm which they can go to but is now hostile to them. Like Shape Changers, their alternate forms are highly varied and the "War Form" doesn't make them go nuts. Mage and Changeling deal heavily in the concept of things made of concepts. Unlike the other books, however, Dragons don't have much going on in the way of boons and banes for having non-aware humans see their powers, and they're very rare, so meeting each other isn't going to be common, nor is meeting someone who knows what they are. Unlike the other stuff, Dragons were Outsiders, so Orobori don't have a pre-built niche in the Supernatural Ecology, which is something that (among the most major books) only Promethian deals with.

            Formatting Thoughts - The new format is a bit confusing, and as much as I like the new content, I feel like we might want to have fewer chapters which seem to ramble on: it's been increasingly hard to navigate the books when trying to find rules or build characters. I'm gonna suggest we try and be a bit less complicated than that, at least for now. Also, I REALLY like the thing that books like Scion have done, where you see an actual party being backstoried, formed, and going on an adventure in the game-world, which is then explained through the chapters. That might be really effective with Dragon, which means that we would have to write in a new story, possibly from playtesting sessions, at the expense of the old one. (as much as I like it)

            A General TODO list.
            1. Build a Transitional Edition of Embers for use as a starting point.
            2. Start work on the Playtesting Draft, building a complete meta, iceburg, and rewriting old mechanics.
            3. Get feedback to see what people would build with these rules, and make sure they are either able to be made or that a clear reason is given for why they can't.
            4. Finish the initial Playtesting Draft of Orobori Chronicles.
            5. Run a Playtesting game with some good writers who know World of Darkness well (NOW SEEKING!)
            6. Build the full book while running those sessions

            Remaining Questions
            • Did anyone write down notes on things like the old playtests, meta-discussions, and unwritten lore?
            • How do we feel about the new morality system I've put forward? Is there a better idea?
            • How should the "magic" the Dragons brought from the Dream-Tide work? How do the Orobori enter the Dreamtide? (I'm thinking Jade is required here)
              • How hard should it be to cross into the Dreamtide?
            • How should we respond to the new format? How important is trying to replicate it?
            • IS there a "hope", as Shock seemed to imply, or is this really a game about people running around trying to steal Hearts from a depleting supply?
            • Who's actually joining me here? I can't do this by myself.

            Before I finish here...
            Is there anything else that we need to discuss before I go ahead and move forward?

                Wow. You are the lead this project deserves; that was an excellent mission statement.

                With regards to your remaining questions...
                • Old playtests, meta-discussions, unwritten lore
                  • I don't have many lingering notes from old playtests, though I can say the Ablution system worked better with a static experience cost rather than a scaling one. Since Chronicles made that baseline, it's a big win for Dragon. I'd say price them like merits; players shouldn't hesitate to spend experience on more Ablutions.
                  • The Asset system was built to redirect fuel recovery to the themes of the game. Since those themes were--at the time--divine right, Breath recovery became affiliated with tending to those Assets. I know you intend to remove that system (and as project lead you should not let me influence you on that matter, see... I'm haunting you), but it dove-tails well with eating to regain Breath.
                    • The fast approach to replenish Breath is to eat something (we shifted towards living things, like dragons munching on livestock, but drinking gasoline would still work), whereas the 'healthy' approach is tending to your possessions.
                    • You could discard Province as a type of Asset and just keep it as Hoard and Maiden, since so many European dragon stories involve greed and kidnapping.
                    • Even if you completely discard the Asset system for a more traditional energy pool, you should consider keeping Hoards and Maidens in some fashion, preferably as Breath production mechanics or merits.
                  • Unwritten Lore
                    • Antagonists
                      • Chimerae
                        • Chimerae are animals which consume Orobori hearts. This transforms them into beings which are essentially identical to an Oroboros (apply the Embers template to their animal stats). However, being that they are animals, their shape is defined by their own perception of what a 'dragon' should be (an apex predator).
                        • A chimera's idea of what makes the optimum apex predator warps them into increasingly complex monstrosities (as they see a biological advantage they perceive as useful, they incorporate it into their body).
                        • It also drives them to violence; Chimerae are incredibly territorial and kill humans, supernaturals, and anything else to establish their hunting grounds. Chimerae are generally made when a foolish Oroboros thinks it might be neat to have an immortal pet.
                      • Phoenixi
                        • Orobori who learn the story of the five dragons and the creation of the universe (or rather, the creation of the Dreamtide depending on who you ask) and are driven by sorrow or guilt to madness.
                        • Phoenixi believe the only course of a brighter future is to return all of the pieces of the Center Long to her and compress the universe back into her body. To this end, they gather up people, animals, and other Oroboros and 'feed' them to the Ocean Oroboros (where they are erased in the blood of the Long).
                        • Phoenixi generally incorporate flaming feathers into their divine appearance as it symbolizes the rebirth of the universe they seek. In the long game they are pursuing the Big Crunch, smashing all of reality back into a singularity to re-birth the Long.
                        • Phoenixi are only viable as an antagonist if the story of the five Longs persists in your version.
                      • Shayatin (Deep Ones)
                        • Forgotten Gods who lost their reality as they lost their worshippers. They are reduced to empty husks, hungry to feel again.
                        • There was a pantheon of gods who predated the original Nidhoggr as rulers of the world. They make up the bulk of the Shayatin.
                        • Shayatin attempt to piece together pieces of other people in order to regain a sense of identity. However, a collage of the traits of others is never enough and they perpetually seek to become MORE real, more present, MORE a part of the physical world.
                        • It is implied, though not explicitly stated, that those who bargain too much of themselves to the Shayatin join their ranks, pulled out of reality into the Dreamtide and losing their existence.
                      • Heart Devourers
                        • Remember, eating a Heart anchors ALL antecedents to the new heart. Hostile antecedents can begin to manifest 'tumors' on the devouring Oroboros, such as eyes or mouths. These can be ripped away but will eventually grow back (This was in the third edition, but I thought I'd reiterate it here).
                    • Purity of Jade
                      • Jade is a symbol of purity to dragons, but the stain of the first usurpations lingers on the hearts of the Orobori. This is why they are susceptible to Jade; it burns at the corruption inside of them. Nidhoggr do not share this vulnerability.
                • Morality System
                  • This is excellent, I have no notes.
                • Dreamtide (Magic and access)
                  • Edicts are the ultimate form of solipsistic reality and an extension of the power a lucid dreamer exerts over her own dreams. In essence, an Oroboros (or any Dreaming god, such as one of the Shayatin) turns the physical world into her waking dream and then forces her will upon it. At least, that's what I was going for.
                  • When I wrote up the Dreamtide, most of the player access was based on the Playstation game Alundra by Matrix Software; it's a typical dreamwalking scenario where making contact with the sleeping party allows the dreamwalker to step into their subconcious, body and all. The stuff about the Miasma of active thought being aligned into clarity by a sleeping party was inspired by old analog televisions and rabbit-ear antennae, along with a healthy dose of psychology (since dreams are the brain's means of sorting and pruning observational stimulus for storage in long-term memory). Hopefully knowing where I was coming from with that mechanic will help you in some regard.
                • Book Format
                  • Use what works with your goals, rather than a standardized form. If it helps you to cleave to the format presented--as it did with me--follow it. But do not feel constrained by it; if it interferes with your creativity you should abandon it without a second thought.
                • Hope for Hearts
                  • There were two hopes for the creation of new Hearts for insertion into the Oroboros spiral of destruction:
                    • Butchering Pilot Lights
                      • Draconic offspring (or Spartoi, if you keep them) sometimes have additional chambers in their hearts. By collecting nine of these superfluous 'dragon' chambers (through serial murder), an unscrupulous Oroboros could perform a blood ritual to fashion these severed pieces into a new Dragon Heart. It was never confirmed whether this method actually worked or not; the intent was to allow the Storyteller to decide.
                    • The Primordial Dream
                      • Before the Dreamtide was removed in the third edition, it was intended that dragons could enter a group-guided dream and attempt to fashion a new Heart out of their own thoughts. This Heart could then be collected and brought back to the physical world.
                      • Living Dragons do not have sufficient attachment to make a functioning Heart, however. If players attempted this act, the Heart would corrode upon crossing the threshold into the physical world.
                      • There was another option, though. An antecedent, who is forever trapped in dreams, could dream of his own heart and make it real in the process.
