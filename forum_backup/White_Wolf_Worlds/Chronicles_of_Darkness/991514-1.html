    I'd like to recommend an alternative: Run each species and class as a major splat, instead of a merit-based template addition. This will involve packing away the supernatural creatures from ChroD but might result in a more interesting game (and could give you some interesting implications about each class and species) [I say species because there's no evidence in Dragon Age that the different "races" are genetically compatible; you never see a half-dwarf, half-elf, or whatever].

    We'll start with Dwarves. Due to their penchant for alchemy and their restriction from magic, I'd recommend running them with the Promethean book. Make disquiet specifically and exclusively affect mages, and perhaps attach a potency reducer to it based on the stage experienced.

    Elves, particularly the Dalish, would benefit from being run by the Werewolf book. Their dedication to the spirits (many of which are revered as gods in their society) and their connection to the Fade (which contains the Shadow if you're using the "break upon the fall" model) feels like a good fit. Snip around the shapeshifting unless the elf also happens to be a mage.

    The Vashoth I'd create using the Changeling book, with the Qunari serving explicitly as the True Fae. The Qun is a harsh philosophy not above stealing people away from their lives for service, which makes this an appropriate fit.


    Humans are trickier. I'd be inclined to split humans across different books based on what class they choose.

    I'd run Templars with the Vampire book. Lyrium is actually a living mineral, and...
    Red lyrium is lyrium infected with the blight

    so simulating the magic halting power of a templar with the blood suckers feels par for the course. Keep in mind Templars stop magic by widening the veil with their training; Lyrium just facilitates that mental control. It IS magic in that it is anti-magic. I'd also say lower the potency of magic against templars as you would against a dwarf. Double for a dwarf templar (only the player ever attained this combination in canon, but it is hypothetically possible).
    Other Warriors would be built with the Heroic Mortal template from Mirrors. Emphasize the extra health levels in particular.

    I'd run Rogues with the Geist book. Something about manifestations screams ambush preparation to me; you can strip away some of the magical components of these ghost powers and make them into exploitation of terrain instead. The Boneyard manifestation is particularly appropriate in this regard. Also, delaying injuries but then succumbing to them later is such a rogue thing to do. Instead of Plasm for this, make them use herbalism to gather poisons for the purpose of attaining the same effects.

    Mages are tricky. As has been stated upthread, the Awakened in the Mage book are seriously powerful entities and it may be best to reserve the splat for big figures in the world.
    Since Dragon Age Mages have the potential to 'pupate' into horrible abominations, I'd say make them with the Demon book (and make "Going Loud" the mutate part).

    As for other major players...
    I'd make Flemeth with the Mummy book. Primarily because:
    She starts off incredibly powerful, but gradually degrades and must reincarnate in a stolen daughter to regain her might


    And as for the Archdemon, I'd make it with the Beast book. While you might be inclined to make it as a spirit or not stat it at all, the Archdemon has been killed at least five times in the Dragon Age universe and so much exist as a slayable entity.


    Of course, if you do this you might end up blurring some template lines, particularly with dwarven and elven rogues (who would need to be able to at least purchase Manifestations). Still, it might be worth consideration... at least to shrink the power disparity your current model presents with magic.
