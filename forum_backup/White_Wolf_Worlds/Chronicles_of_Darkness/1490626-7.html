    Originally posted by Chris_John View Post
    I was looking forward to the Outer Hedge, but in terms of systems it's awful. "You have to do Hedge Navigation Chases five times to reach the end of the trod" is horrible when Hedge Navigation Chases are just... the actual worst and most anti-fun game design I've ever seen, and especially when you have to pay such a hefty price to get to the Outer Hedge it makes me wonder why anyone would want to include it as part of your Chronicle.
    My guy, they are not that bad, especially if you are actually raising the stat that makes Hedge Navigation explicitly easier. By the time you've hit Wyrd 4 — which you should be trying to do if you're engaging with the game with intent to mess around in dreams and the Hedge at any degree of success — navigating a five-dot trod is of negligible difficulty; accumulating five successes before an opponent that starts with a chance die can accumulate at least eight goes by fairly quickly.

    Entering the Outer Hedge is a commitment and leaving it is likewise an involved process. This is to be expected, because it's space. The fact that there's a freehold on the moon doesn't make the moon intrinsically easy to access for changelings any more than every dreaming person having a Bastion on the Dreaming Roads makes it easy for any changeling to mess with anybody's head through the Gate of Horn. It is easier the more magical you are, which comes with its own commitments as your Wyrd goes up, but Changeling has literally always been a game where the magic comes with a cost.

    Hexes and Curses don't do it for me. Too open ended, too little of a cost. Apparently now Changelings aren't tricksters waiting for someone to trap themselves in their own words, they're just able to brute force trap someone into a magically binding contract without even accidental consent, and for the same cost as a pledge.
    The logic you are using here also supposes that changelings shouldn't have Contracts and the book elsewhere makes it plain that precise wording matters less than the spirit behind a pledge. Misdirection at the same level as "demonic pacts cannot actually lie in their text" is the standard here; pledgecraft has you looking for people to make rash statements in the heat of the moment, not going over their every word with a thesaurus and a legal dictionary.

    What's a Pledge even for anymore?
    Making agreements.

    Sealings can be done subtly. Hexes and curses require you to state the terms in a way the victim can perceive and understand. This isn't a leonine contract, it's an ultimatum that can come back on the hexing or cursing character by virtue of it being a type of pledge and not a casual outside-context problem for whoever it's directed at.

            …so, I have a few thoughts. I admit I’ve been distracted of late, so I had to reread these. So kiths are supposed to be half of the answer of the “what archetype are you”, and are pretty major themes into a character, that one can subvert or reinforce, depending on the character. To help with that, kiths get a power, and a blessing. There is a balance of theme, power, and blessing, that I don’t think all the kiths completely nail. Some appear to be the same Kith, but with a different focus, like Ghostheart and Gravewight. One focuses on hedge ghosts, the other on normal ghosts. Stuff like that’s pretty common, and probably are good examples on customizing kiths. The Witchtooth has a great theme, but I’m not sure what illusory terrain has to to do with the archetype of witches. It’s not bad, just a little strange. The only ones I’m really questioning are Bricoleur, Wisewitch, and Shadowsoul. Everything else is fine, and a lot is really cool.
