                                            Originally posted by Michael View Post
                                            Not exactly. You've presented a highly abstract issue with nothing even vaguely like a solution. Frankly on the back of all the other threads, I'm not sure what you were expecting.
                                            The solution is that these things be portrayed as less "invisible law of the universe" and more something that you can see and interact with.

                                            Take the fact that Time is stable, but Arcadia says it's chaotic. My approach would be to say that Time IS chaotic, even in the Fallen World. Mages can see and document natural Time eddies and storms. Time sight can see constant minor variations in the flow of Time. These Time storms cause deja vu, make people unexpectedly late, and sometimes cause unexplained disappearances. Now let's say there's some mundane object or action that interacts with Time, quelling disruptions in time. This deserves more thought, but for the sake of expediency, I'll say wood. The grains in wood somehow are able to stabilize Time's natural fluctuations.

                                            With these changes, the Lie is now something that mages can interact with and study. It becomes a plot point and a story hook. Giving Time something physical to interact with makes it less abstract, more tangible, easier to grok, and pushes it away from being a deus ex machina. The players can investigate the time-affecting properties of wood, and perhaps find that these properties are somehow artificial, giving them evidence that stable time is part of the Lie.

                                            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                            No, Mage Sight is seeing the Supernal symbols that underly Fallen reality.
                                            And those Supernal symbols are called the "Truth" because they are in direct opposition to the "Lie". Hence, why I say that a Mage sees through the Lie using Mage Sight.
