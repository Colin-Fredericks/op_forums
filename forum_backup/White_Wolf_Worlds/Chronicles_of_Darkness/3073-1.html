                                Dataweaver:
                                Who or what should be the “plot motivator” (e.g., God-Machine, Strix, Idigam, Fallen World)?
                                The most obvious answer is something intruding from the Underworld, being it Deathlords or Chthonians, but the Strix and the Idigam are already about intrusions. So maybe Geist should be about something else.

                                The Geist mythos is materialistic. Everything is solid in the underworld, and the setting abounds with enchanted objects; the Bound themselves are materialist in the other sense, often using their knowledge and position for profit.
                                Sin-Eater society is trashy and iconoclastic, the Bound putting together their mythoi from whatever they fancy, with little regard for history or sanctity. The tier 3 krewes are a thing of the past.

                                Or are they?

                                ABenniton cites the suggestion that the Deathlords are epic level geists - but what if instead they're epic level sineaters? The old krewes are still around, and they are the Deathlords, engaged in a long game to wrest control of the Underworld from the Kerberoi? (And maybe rule the living world for good measure). The classical channels are taken - sineaters who follow them get suborned or eliminated - that's why the ones who survive are the ones who DIY their spirituality.

                                So the game becomes learning this and deciding what to do about it. If anything - after all, maybe sineaters ruling the Underworld is better - but maybe different sineaters (like us) doing so is even better still. You find out there is a Man, and now you need to decide - quickly - whether to fight him, serve him, avoid him, or become him.

                                Alternatively, maybe it's less a conspiracy as such, and more a kind of autopilot. Geists ought to be changed by their time connected to a sineater - and they're certainly changed by their inclusion in a powerful krewe mythology. So instead of an active malign conspiracy, you have the what amounts to the ghost of a conspiracy, pursuing power and influence that's wielded blindly in the service of goals and ideologies that no longer apply to the world. The sineaters' usual job writ large.

                                Either way, the second chance becomes something they don't just have, but something they can offer to individuals - living and dead - and to the world at large. (Even if nothing else I suggested suits, this last paragraph is what I feel most strongly should drive the book).

                                    Whatever the plot motivator of Geist is; it has to do two things:

                                    1) Be relevant on both sides, Life and Death. Earth and Underworld. The whole theme is about having one foot in each world (even more than Werewolves, Geists have far more ties to mortal society).
                                    2) Work for all the Archetypes. It's got to work for a Necromancer delving underworld lore and a Celebrant who just wants to party. Professional ghostbusters and professional accountants.



                                    That's why I think the plot motivator is the Geist. Life or Death, party or accountant. The Geist is the one thing that's always with you, and it can be customised to fit any plot. Your Geist could be pushing you to reclaim the Underworld from day one or point you to the best rums for the party.

                                                Originally posted by SunlessNick View Post
                                                ABenniton cites the suggestion that the Deathlords are epic level geists - but what if instead they're epic level sineaters? The old krewes are still around, and they are the Deathlords, engaged in a long game to wrest control of the Underworld from the Kerberoi?
                                                I had to double check, and I did say that, but what I meant was more along the line of what you just said here: Deathlords as Cosmic Tier Geist line (which makes them Cosmic Sin-Eaters). I like the motivator being a war that plays out in different ways (in homage to the old Guild system of Oblivion and trying to wrest control of Stygia), but I don't think it should be Sin-Eaters-as-Deathlords v. Kerberoi because that's too easy to play off as the "other". I feel that the Kereboi should be tools, employed by the Deathlords, as are other Geists, and ghosts, in a strange and Byzantine hierarchy building throughout history.
                                                So what then is the Motivation? Because what The King's Raven said is right: it has to be relevant to both sides, Living and Dead. So, why are all these Deathlords going to war with one another? The only answer I could easily propose is tacky at best: "Souls".
                                                Each Deathlord, even each Dominion, is fueled and gains power from the souls within it. If each Horseman represents a Threshold, the Deathlords represent a specific type of death (electrocution in a puddle for instance), and each Deathlord uses this Byzantine power structure in order to complete their goals in the physical world, which in truth are elegantly simple: "Get more deaths". (It also makes for a callous and terrible Quintessence alternative for a cosmic level Geist game.)
                                                So then the motivator is immediately relevant: what do you do when the Other-you tries to murder the people you love? Or the people that take care of you? Or your clients? And then the Sin-Eaters become these... thorns in the proverbial side of the powers that be, gaining power in order to combat the reality of the situation, all the while pied-pipering themselves into becoming the very thing they're fighting. Rebels in an endless war...

                                                            I disagree. If GMC taught us anything its that the plot motivator doesn't have to be engaged with. Sure, if you're a mortal, maybe the God-Machine's plans are messing with your life. But maybe you don't figure out the Infrastructure in time, and maybe you don't stop it. The messing around in your life though? It stops as the Infrastructure completes is goals and then is disassembled or left "off". (A suitable scenario for a game that is supposed to be about occult mysteries and then the players end up more interested in interpersonal affairs.)

                                                            In the same way: You're offered a second chance at life by the Bargain, and just like in the current version: you can totally ignore what your Geist tells you to do, or look at, or be a part of. You can take your mystical powers and do exactly whatever it is you want. In this hypothetical Chronicle book, you could even forge a Krewe mythos that doesn't deal at all with this war raging around you. But the fact is that things are trying to kill people in a very specific way. And you are these hapless people's only opportunity for a "second chance".
