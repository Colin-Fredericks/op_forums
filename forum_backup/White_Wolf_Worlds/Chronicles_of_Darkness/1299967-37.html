    Originally posted by Hilary View Post
    For me, it depends what you think a Shadow name should =do=. The transliteration into the Latin alphabet was deliberately 'off' - because in the writer's opinion a Shadow name is intended to deceive or, at the very least, to conceal. Going from letters on a page to speaking or hearing is a process that depends a great deal on where the speaker, hearer or reader comes from, which accent is the most familiar to them.
    They're about concealment (at least originally; in modern days I think most mages rather considers them as boundaries between their dual lifes) but the concealment comes from having a Shadow Name at all, and reinforcing it. Further obfuscating your Shadow Name might actually have the opposite effect in diluting your Shadow Name's symbolism (i.e. dot rating in the Shadow Name Merit) and thus how effective it is at concealing your mundane life.

    At least, that's my take on it. And even if I'm right about it, a mage might see it differently and still opt to use several layers of obfuscation. It certainly adds a layer of concealment in practical terms rather than mystical since it's more difficult to look up a name if it's spelt incorrectly or is spelt differently by different people.
