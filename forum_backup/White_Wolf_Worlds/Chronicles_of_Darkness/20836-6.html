        Okay... I've been thinking about this for a while, and it seems like the Oroboroi's mechanics are okay: they're GMC compatible now, the Edict laws play off of each other relatively well, tells have been reduced in complication to make them more fun and interesting to use, Breath has been adapted to be both unique and a driving force at emphasizing core concepts of the game, and Mandate has had its sins simplified and made GMC compatible (Granted the fluff around mandate is bad, but at the moment all of the fluff is bad, I'm just talking mechanics here).

        This means we can (and should) focus our energies on the thematic components of the game. As it stands, we need:
        • 2.5 more Philosophies, depending on how we settle the Caitiff dilemma
        • An enrichment of Antagonists, including a superstitious threat antagonist
        • A firm decision on which B-Splats to keep, which to discard, and how to make them work mechanically
        • A decision on whether or not to bother with the Treasury/realm invisible for dragons, and a precise definition of what said realm should include and more importantly what it should not include
        • More myths about dragons, including conflicting mythology
        The problem is, that's a tough list, and when we try to do the whole list at once things get lost, people get frustrated, and the project grinds to a halt. So we're going to set most of the list aside and focus on one thing at a time (writing philosophies excluded because we've been having good fortune with that thus far).



        So the first thing I want to focus on is the Antagonists, since they will have an impact on the way the entire manual is written.

        Here are the antagonists we've developed for Dragon so far, in no particular order, followed with a brief description of each. Some of them have already been shot down, but I thought I'd mention them again in case they serve as inspiration for something else.

        Chimerae/Chimeras: Animals that eat an Oroboros Heart and turn into monsters. To be honest, I completely forgot about these guys, which is why they didn't get mentioned in the CIJ half-book. There's a lot of confusion about how these things would work; would they be mindless man-eating maws? Or sinister manipulators like their Oroboroi cousins. Animal instinct coupled with human intelligence is a very scary prospect, and the game practically encourages the creation of Chimerae with the Husbandry Edict. Still, I don't think they're the terrifying threat that would drive enemies to join forces.

        Knights of the Bloody Chalice: The knights were really nothing more than blood-bathing immortals, with the exception that they actively bled through their skin in the presence of supernatural beings. Their purpose was to discourage overuse of Ablutions by presenting the hunter threat, but they became increasingly unnecessary as the project progressed. At the moment, they are not in the document and not slated for reintroduction.

        Volcano Philosophies: Frogs proposed using the original five philosophies as an antagonistic force, and it isn't a bad idea. As written most are single-minded and more than a bit malicious; they'd work well as a Pure-style antagonist if developed properly, but I'd need help to do so.

        Einherjar: The Einherjar are sort of like the knights, only less organised and more persistent. They are freebound souls that possess injured parties and imbue them with superhuman strength, but also initiate their natural rigor and decay. In short, they're revenants. What makes them scary is that they are persistent; kill an Einherjar in a host body and it'll just possess somebody else to attack again. I think these guys have the potential to be the terrifying threat, but they'd need work to do so. They do fit the "hero determined unto death" niche, which would be naturally frightening to dragons as heroes are generally the only ones that slay dragons in mythology.

        Deep Ones: The Deep Ones, or dark bargainers, are essentially a grim prophecy of what will become of the Oroboroi once all of their reverence has turned to rot; they are the fate of fallen gods, clinging to existence by clinging to the warmth of other beings (by trading favors for organs). Deep Ones might have the ability to be an absolutely terrifying antagonist, the type of which Frogs was seeking, but at the moment they share much of the mechanics an Oroboros does and so they aren't particularly mysterious... at least in that sense.

        Shayatin/Chthonic Ghuuls: The Shayatin were really written as a complement to the Deep Ones; they're beasts that go around eating the corpses of memory (in the Dreamtide version of the game) or the wandering souls of dangerous Einherjar (in the Treasury version of the game). As such, they aren't villains so much as they are uncomfortable allies, much like spirits are to Werewolves. They are NOT a good fit for the terrifying antagonist and their inclusion is dependent on both the inclusion of the Deep Ones and that of the Treasury itself. In short, they're new to the pile and thus first off the pile if we've got thematic problems with the intangible/mystical half of the game.

        Dracoliches: These are what I came up with today, and the reason I am posting. I've been thinking about what else might threaten a dragon to the point of terror, and I came upon nature itself. An illness, especially a contagious one with limited or painful treatment (or none at all) can bring down even the greatest of beasts. So I thought about White Nose Syndrome and how it's killing bats. And I thought about the mold monsters in Fallout New Vegas. Mold killing a body and controlling it like a puppet is pretty scary; possibly superstitious terror scary. Essentially, an Oroboros succumbs to the mold and then it uses their body to take a joy ride, flaunting Ablutions and Edicts as it looks for more bodies to pollinate. What makes it a dracolich? Well the mold isn't actually a dragon, so as it attains degrees of divinity the flesh fetters and then falls off until it's little more than a mossy mass of skeleton and tendons.
        If the mold angle doesn't work for this sort of rotting danger, perhaps this notion could be incorporated into the Einherjar to make them less hunter and more terrifying revenant.

        Nightmares: To speak shortly, Nightmares are essentially Freddy Krueger: they transform, do scary things like shatter bones, and leaving lasting injuries on their victims even when they wake (at least in the Dreamtide version). They were really included as a means of fleshing out the Dreamtide, and are mechanically similar to ghosts and spirits (though their energy and their health are the same stat, so using energy actually harms them). They'd be easy to keep or remove, depending on the verdict on the Treasury.


        So... that's what we've got so far. Frogs is correct, we need something that will fill an Oroboros with blind terror... are there any ideas?

                                    The Edict explaination was a bit long, so I shortened it a bit and make it more synthetic. Note that I changed the name of the "combination" for law into Law corollaries.
                                    Edicts and Laws
                                    Part of their divine right of the dragons is the ability to impose things on matter by force-of-will, and Edicts are the way a dragon can enforce them. Their words are charged by their majesty, and are given the possibility to re-weave the fabric of reality at their command. It’s good to be a king.
                                    Each Edict is accompanied by three laws, with each Law bearing its own unique purpose. Laws enable the Oroboroi to perform one particular action in the vein of the Edict. Every dot in an Edict unlocks one Law in any order. Everytime an Oroboroi learns his first Law of an Edict, he learns the Law’s Veto (see below). Activating a Law or Veto is an Instant action, and it’s called “proclamation”. When the dragon knows at least two Laws of an Edict, he can purchase a Law corollary. A Law corollary take two Laws and roll them into a single proclamation; the laws may be proclaimed in a single Instant action and at the same Effect Rating but the mechanics are otherwise unchanged. This way, it’s possible for the dragon to reach up to three Laws and three Law corollaries.
                                    Edicts decrease in power every time they are used within the same scene, and this is reflected by the Effective Rating (similar to how manifest Ablutions are). The Effective Rating begins each scene equal to the dots known in the Edict. Every time a proclamation is resolved, the Edict‘s Effective Rating is reduced by 1. Ordinances work as Skill Specialities for Edicts, but the Philosophy of the Oroboroi grants him a peculiar Ordinance that applies to all Edicts. It might describe a type of target (such as “Squirrels” for the Husbandry Edict) or a specific situation (such as “Raining” for the Water Edict). The Effective Rating of an Edict can be increased in four ways:
                                    • +1 to the Effective Rating per Breath spent.
                                    • +2 to the Effective Rating if the proclamation meets the condition of an Ordinance the Oroboros owns.
                                    • +2 if the Oroboros uses the Draconic 3 Merit to proclaim it
                                    • Always adds the current Divinity of the dragon.

                                    These are only temporary boosts, and the Effective Rating still decreases when the Edict is used, regardless of any empowerment the dragon applied. A Law the reaches a natural Effective Rating of 0 ceases to function for the rest of the scene. When calculating the natural Effective Rating, don’t count Divinity. You can track the current Effect Rating of an Edict by checking off the boxes under the dots as proclamations are made.
                                    When making a Dragon character, that character receives a fleeting two dots to place in Edicts, and one such dot must always be placed in the Edict that matches the dragon‘s Philosophy. These first two dots reflect the dragon‘s introduction to her new worldviews, and the influences that come with such worldviews.
                                    • Imperators:One dot in Fire
                                    • Paragons: One dot in Wood
                                    • Savants: One dot in Water
                                    • Viziers: One dot in Metal
                                    • Caitiff: One dot in Earth

                                                        Attribute: The lower of Stamina or Intelligence
                                                        "Let the pollen cloud their mind"
                                                        The proclaiming Oroboros triggers a spray of pollen from all nearby plant sources. Anyone within range suffers an immediate sneezing and coughing fit, even if they are not particularly allergic to outdoor irritants. This law requires plants to be nearby in order to have an effect. The plants affected gain the highly allergenic pollen permanently; new beings entering the area suffer the same fit but are permanently acclimated to the irritant once they finish sneezing and coughing.
                                                        Dice Pool: Stamina/Intelligence + Wood + Furnace
                                                        Roll Results
                                                        Dramatic Failure: The Oroboros triggers the pollen spray, but subjects herself to the allergic fit.
                                                        Failure: Nothing happens.
                                                        Success: Within a number of yards equal to the Effect Rating, hyper-allergenic pollen clouds the air. Anyone within this cloud suffers a sneezing and coughing fit, suffering 1 point of bashing damage per turn of combat, and must roll Stamina + Resolve -3 or lose the action. A creature that successfully acts for three consecutive turns from contesting the allergen becomes immune to the allergen for the entire scene. This fit lasts for a number of turns equal to the Effect Rating of the proclamation.
                                                        Exceptional Success: The duration is improved by 3 turns.

                                                        Dice Pool: Resolve/Manipulation + Wood + Furnace

The Oroboros can grow the plant so greatly that they can be used for narrative purpose to create means to reach impervious places expending 1 Breath. A plant that bears fruit can be enchanted using 1 Breath to sprout a magical fruit capable of healing 1 bashing or lethal damage per Furnace dot. A single plant can only bear one fruit of this kind.
