    How specific can you be with "Shifting the Odds" and to recognise what you ask?

    06-25-2019, 05:12 AM
    Greetings,



    As the title says, the questions:
    • What level of details can you use on Shifting the Odds? I know that it can not be used to find a particular person, so no casting to find "Matthew Dawkins", but could I be very detailed: "high schooler kidnapped since last week", "female supermodel that finds me alluring", "Colonel Mustard's assassin"? At some point the specificity should fail since there might be no kidnapped high schoolers, no female supermodels that find my appearance attractive, and Colonel Mustard just had a stroke.
    • How do you know you have found what you are looking for? If I ask for a "Corrupt Cop", how do I recognize them? I might meet the cop outside work hours, or among many other cops.

      I put the results down as anyone who could theoretically fit the bill while falling into a category of people rather than being marked as an individual. So the first two would be fine to ask, and the last one wouldn't because the question itself implies there could be only one person after whom you're inquiring. I'd also be inclined to make either of the first two questions succeed however improbable, because Fate does the probability thing unless I had a good reason for not letting it happen. In which case I'd inform my player.

      As for how you know when you've found what you're looking for, I just tell the player, "You found it."

                  Even more outlandish was the request for: &quot;A Seer Minister that will switch sides just by meeting us.&quot; (Seers harassed us quite a lot, we lacked magical muscle to curb stomp them, barely escaping many times). The Seers losing a resource and the Pentacle gaining it at the same time seemed awesome (and broken AF).
                  The rational approach to take is to disallow specific conditional qualities like these -- setting aside how this is functionally equivalent to setting a Key for a Space spell that's literally "the exception is whoever I want," the spell is letting you happen to run into someone that fits the bill for whatever purpose you invoked Fate to help with, which tend to be categories that are more archetypal or indirect.

                  You can probably find a generous or tractable millionaire easily enough, but one that will alter their last will and testament on a whim is so narrowly defined as to be a specific person or nonexistent. You might, barring magical interference, be able to luck into a meeting with a particularly mercenary or inscrutable Minister whose sudden defection wouldn't cause more problems than it solved, but you run into the same so-specific-you-couldn't-mean-anyone-else problem as with the millionaire and involved someone who's very likely a Prelate with your probably-not-Masters cabal.

                  It's a storytelling tool that lets you benefit from convenient strokes of luck along the way to a goal that isn't whatever person you happen to meet through the spell. If you wouldn't accept the degree of plot convenience a category would obviously produce by its very wording then it's probably not fair game for Shifting the Odds, at least without incorporating an extra Reach or two.

                          Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                          But without conditionals how can you specify what you need?
                          You will notice, if you pay attention, that none of the examples the book provides have anything to do with the character casting the spell or how they would react to a given circumstance. A corrupt cop is a corrupt cop regardless of who they happen to run into. A supermodel who will find you attractive is banking on qualities that have nothing to do with being a supermodel and may change based on how your encounter goes, at which point you might as well just broaden your context-specific quality to "is susceptible to the advances of a person who knows Fate magic."

                          When I say "conditional quality" I don't mean qualifiers on a larger category, I mean qualities that are themselves contingent upon things that haven't happened yet. You could reasonably use Shifting the Odds try to pre-trace your steps to one of the witnesses of a particular robbery, but you couldn't find "someone who's going to be robbed after I talk to them" without getting involved with some weirdly specific people.

                          So if “corrupt cop” is valid, then “kidnapped high-schooler” is valid too. As it would be the female (Trait), supermodel (occupation), that finds me attractive (context-specific quality), perhaps pushing the 3 descriptors but it fits too.
                          No, it really doesn't. Fate doesn't do this kind of hyper-targeted outcome through the spells that give you a serendipitous route to a goal. You get whatever's handy at the time, and if you don't like the result, you try again in different circumstances.

                          This is a spell whose hard-mechanical outcome is that you get temporary access to a few dots of Social Merits for the Duration of the effect through incidental contact and lucky circumstances, which you may further capitalize on long-term by spending Experiences to make the Merit permanent. Its immediate framing is "this lets you get stuff you need in relatively short order." The plain-narrative outcome is obviously not supposed to be dramatically more potent than that effect.

                          (Also, "female" isn't a Trait. "Supermodel" comes closer to being a Trait, because Striking Looks is a Merit and so is Professional Training. Given that "context-specific quality" provides "corrupt cop" as an example, the "context" is very probably "reasonable subset of people within a larger category," not "way this person will react to a given stimulus.")

                                          On a first look, there are 3 possible interpretations:

                                          1- Nothing happens: This isn't a Making of Fate, there is a limit to how much it can do. If its too specific or improbable, you can simply say there is no one that qualifies. Especially since that inheritance part goes beyond an Allies merit and a bit into Strings of Fate

                                          2- Works as expected: You find the effect is not particularly disruptive to your story and don't mind characters having affluence. You will need social consequences of some kind to explain why every mage doesn't do the same, such as Guardian, Arrow or Lex Magicka dissatisfaction, TELM group sanctions, government scrutiny, etc. In essence, they have to earn it.

                                          3- Be careful what you wish for: Works as expected, but the source of the wealth is dangerous. Maybe the millionaire was a crime boss, perhaps he was a favored pawn of a Seer, maybe he made a deal with a wealth spirit and the character inherited the debt, maybe he worked for a Cthonic patron of greed from the Underworld, etc. Make them have to deal with the ensuing consequences.

                                          In short, if the effect goes against the themes of your campaign without breaking them, add a little push to balance their pull. That way both of can be satisfied by the results. Maybe having an NPC explain how such a specific search makes fate over complicate and tangle itself to fulfill it.

                                          The idea of including Serendipity is also really good. Break it into steps since Fate can't do all that work at this level of mastery. But its messier this way, which makes for interesting plot hook opportunities.

                                              To the main issue with Shifting the Odds, I would cut off "traits" that I interpret as verbs and gerundives. It's a basically the same as a the "script" issue, but putting it in a more direct sense of how these things get phrased,

                                              What's difference between a corrupt cop and a kidnapped teenager? Corrupt is a simple adjective. Nothing the cop did, or had done to them, is specified. Kidnapped is a gerundive adjective as it springs from a specific action. "A teenager in trouble," would then be in line with "corrupt cop," and lack the specificity that Shifting the Odds clearly is trying to avoid.

                                              Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
                                              And then use Serendipity to know what to do about your intention. So:
                                              • Use Shifting the Odds to find a "Female supermodel" and Serendipity to know what to do so she will "find me attractive / sleep with me"
                                              • Use Shifting the Odds to locate a "Multimillionaire" and Serendipity to do what is need so he/she "will make me an heir".
                                              • Use Shifting the Odds to request a "Seer Minister" and Serendipity to learn what it take so he/she "will join the Pentacle".



                                              Does it make sense this way? And what about when I want to find the kidnapped high-schooler? Is that the province of Serendipity alone or does Shifting the Odds work there?
                                              While using both is certainly a valid option, it by no means guarantees any form of success; or that success is even possible. Serendipity is, after all, only going to tell you the next action that's most likely to get you to your goal. Using Shifting the Odds to find a female supermodel, and then using Serendipity towards your goal of seducing her does not tell you the whole picture of what it takes to get her to that state, just the next step towards that goal. Serendipity could easily tell you to go study Life or Mind because radically altering your body or overt mind-control is the next step it ever getting her to want to do that.

                                              Even if there are less drastic options, Serendipity is going to give you, "Buy the Seer a gin based drink because they'll appreciate the gesture," not, "here are the next ten steps to get them to join the Pentacle." You can, of course, keep casting Serendipity lots of times, but then the ST should probably be making those steps interesting/challenging.

                                              This applies to the kidnapping victim. Just knowing what the next step is doesn't mean you can actually do it, or want to actually do it, etc.
