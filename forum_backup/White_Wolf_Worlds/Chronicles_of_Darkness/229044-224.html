            Originally posted by The Revised Introduction
            Hunger

            All Beasts have a primal drive called Hunger, which they must indulge to sate the appetite of the Horror. Hunger can be a very simple, direct thing, such as a drive to stalk and kill prey, or it can be more abstract, such as a need to punish others for their transgressions. Though the primal drives are the same, how Beasts interpret them can vary — instead of literally hunting prey to consume them, for example, a Beast might more metaphorically stalk a target and “consume” their trust.

            However it is interpreted, Hunger must always feed the Horror’s appetite. Put another way, as older Beasts sometimes tell younger ones, “if someone eats, something gets eaten.” A Beast may console herself by thinking that she only hurts “bad people” to sate her Hunger (either to scare the subject straight or make an example of him), for example, but deep down she knows that as far as her Horror is concerned, really anyone would do — and one day they just might. It is rare for a Beast’s Hunger to change, though it can happen in certain extreme circumstances and always signals a profound struggle to reconcile with the Horror.

            Beasts sate their Hungers to feed the Horror, but also to teach a lesson from the Primordial Dream. Every Beast has a lesson that it imparts in various ways; the lesson comes from the intersection of the Beast’s Family and Hunger. In order to teach the lesson, though, the subject of the lesson has to survive it and internalize it. A nightmare is only useful if the dreamer awakens, and wisdom is only useful if someone survives to use it. The Horror doesn’t understand this (or doesn’t care); it is just as happy if the Beast kills to feed it. Beasts that simply indulge their Horror’s whims, though, tend to come to a bad end. Some face the judgment of their kin. Others attract Heroes.
            Lessons are not a harm mitigation tactic; they're a modus operandi. What specific fear your Beast represents informs how they hunt, and the default assumption is that most of the time, no one dies.

            Crunch isn't a hobby; it's a calling.

                    Okay. I'm going to try and sum up what about this revision Ruffles my Jammies, so I can get all of my thoughts down so I don't get confused and end up contradicting myself

                    1: Beasts are now Mandated to harm people, when it was entirely possible to make a Beast who could spread his necessary emotions to feed without causing harm

                    2: Beasts are mandated now to be the monsters from Cautionary Tales. There may be a great degree of flexibility. but a lot of those tales contain morals that are utterly abhorrent, most of them are the so called "Children's Boogy Men" the majority of those tales involves disproportionate retribution(Seriously, In the Original Story Little Red Riding Hood was forced to strip naked and crawl into bed with a stranger impersonating her grandmother who proceeded to "devour her" when her only crime was choosing to listen to her parents instead of her gut. That is the opposite of a good cautionary tale)

                    3: The Fact that Beasts, to a man, chose to be what they are, in addition to complete reversing the assumptions we'd been going in with, boils each one of them down to "asshole who decided that he has the right to arbitrarily enforce his morality on others, while before he was someone who was born that way and may have been trying to make the best of it. They might have a point, but the mere fact that they chose it is a huge tonal shift that adversely impacts morally gray theme of the game.

                    There's a major moral difference between "Somebody who is born a monster and has to hurt someone choosing to hurt people who might deserve it in order to mitigate the harm he causes and direct tit to good ends and a person who chose to be a monster specifically to do so because another monster convinced them to

                    The first comes across as someone trying to make the best of a bad situation. The second comes across as an asshole trying to force his arbitrary view of morality on people.

                    It's not even necessarily true, but it's the tone, the way it comes across.

                    4: Mandating Beasts to be monsters from Cautionary Tales pretty much kills dead a lot of concepts.

                    I can't think of one concept that is both the monster from a Cautionary Tale and fits the hungers.

                    Not even a Nemesis, since Nemesi don't care for justice, they just care for the punishment

                    Meaning the Hungers all have to be rewritten, because there's not a single Tyrant, Collector, Predator, Ravager concept I can think off that could be motivated by serving as or providing examples of what you bad behavior can get you and still qualify as their hungers, and with Nemesi you have to squint to get it to fit.

                    5. Making it something you choose, as opposed to something you're born with, is far inferior to being the Reincarnated Soul of a Primordial Monster. As is the "The Soul has utterly no connection to you until you agree to merge with it."

                    6. The "Horror" moving into your body after another Beast has talked you into letting them eat your soul, at best, Makes Beasts a Pod Person. At worst, it makes it a rip off of Geist.

                    7. The "Seve as a cautionary Tale" runs complete counter to the Original Documents theme of subverting and deconstructing the Heroic Narrative, since you're just trading one story for an other. It is also a complete 180 from the stated theme of exploring the world of Darkness o learn more about yourself and your family(teaching others VS Teaching yourself.)

                    8. The term "Horror" is incredibly bland and Generic. Soul was better.

                    9: The Devouring, by it's nature, requires a significant change in how a Beast is played and what kind of Backstories a character can have, and the involvement of a preexisting Beast seems like just an excuse to shoehorn in a new social structure, even though that social structure would have fit very easily into the original Beast with minimal adjustment.

                    10: This line "It should be the prerogative of the Begotten who possess the souls of these Horrors to consider the matter, and /that/ should be what drives their moderation. The fact that they understand that’s what they are and what their souls represent, but their Horrors themselves /don’t care/." which I like because this apparently needed to be said out right, since a bunch of people missed it last time around, feels quite hypocritical with the knowledge that every Beast chose to be someone who is mandated to hurt people.

                    Relatedly, I personally feel that New Beasts are morally worse than Old Heroes, because the Beast chose to be a monster and chose to hurt people for the "greater good"

                    11. In both drafts, both paradigms, you're going to have to have Beasts who say "fuck it, I'm a monster" and go around hurting people with no remorse. In the First Draft, these people are clearly in the wrong. In the revision, coupled with the people who chose to be Beasts, the mandate to sate your hunger by hurting people, and the hypocrisy mentioned above, really makes it sound like the asshole Beasts are just being honest with themselves.

                    I think that's everything.
