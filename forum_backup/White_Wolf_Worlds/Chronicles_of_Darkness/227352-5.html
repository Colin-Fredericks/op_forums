    I think on a personal scale, being a Sin Eater should be about unfinished business an the repetition of actions from life after death. There are some Sin-Eaters who slip back into their old lives and continue to do the same shit that got them dead in the first place, some use their death as a cleansing and abandon their old ways, giving it the figner as they drive into the sunset.

    But just like he ghosts that they deal with, the life they left should call back to them and influence their actions in perhaps odd and unusual ways, in the same way a phantom is drawn to wail and re-enact the night of their death every year.

    It's a game of second chances: Do you grasp it or let it slip away, and what are the consequences of each choice?

            Originally posted by Satchel View Post
            "How did this guy die?" is already immediately half-answered by the nature of Thresholds and deathmarks and the point of dealing with ghosts is more "how do we get them to leave?/what do we do with them?" than the more peripheral question of "how did they get here?"
            This question is a lot easier to answer when you can say "how do we get you to leave?"
            Think of Ghostwhisperer or Supernatural, or any show like that. Almost all of them would be solved instantly if they could ask the ghost questions and communicate freely.

            "Make a ghost able to be interacted with as a character instead of a setpiece at the cost of blood and plasm and also you have to be able to touch them" is something that is rather more and less than what you're making it out to be.
            Ghosts aren't setpieces to begin with. There's not really even any purpose to dealing with the ghosts that are only set pieces. They just loop around and do their thing, playing out how they died. It's the ones like Poltergeists and Yellowbones that you have to worry about.

            "Spend ten minutes running and screaming and trying to bind the thing" is not what I would call "plot."
            Look at any ghost story. Most of the plot is centered on knowing who the ghost is and what they want. Once they figure that out, the only problem is the ghost stopping them from burning it's bones or whatever when the ghosts are the enemies (Supernatural) or solving their death if the ghosts are victims (Ghost Whisperer, Tru Calling). Sin-eaters skip that step. They go right to "hey, what's the problem? Cool, let's solve it." In fact, their only real issue is often going to be binding the thing. They just have to get it to hold still and they can probably stop it murdering people with a bit of Plasm and Blood.

            Except the text points out that ghosts are biased and often incorrect and do not gain knowledge they did not have in the process of dying, lethal damage is not a minor cost to pay, and plasm scarcity is observably a thing.
            No it doesn't. In fact in several places it says that ghosts can gain knowledge. Ghosts get experience points even after they're dead, that's why Yellowbones are so deadly, and why the Underworld is a great place to learn. They also can actually be smarter than they were in life because Power=Intelligence once you restore their cognizance. Sure, some might be dicks and hold back information if you're just interrogating them, but I can't think of a reason a ghost wouldn't give you straight answers if you were literally asking for help solving the ghost's own murder.

            More than that, Plasm isn't actually that scarce. Regaining it is just unobtrusive and undefined. You can get Plasm back by getting into a big fight, or doing drugs with a rusty needle. You also get all your Plasm back for helping a ghost pass on, meaning that it's always worth it to spend a point of it to understand a ghost. Or, since 1L is a heavy price to pay, 2 plasm and 1B.

                                            If your PCs are entirely apathetic dickbags, isn't Any story not really going to go anywhere? Sure, other gamelines may have more invasive and threatening things to throw at them, but no one's 'forcing' them either; the baseline assumptions of all the games is that the PCs care about the splat's "main job" at least enough - or they'd be playing a different game. Vampires can ignore blood and politics by grabbing a few dominated humans and going on vacation; werewolves don't /have/ to deal with spirits; changeling can entirely focus on trying to be human if they're willing to ignore people getting taken. Sin-Eaters might not have a "strong motivation" (if, say, a man perpetually on fire begging for his daughter to be safe isn't strong enough for you), but character apathy can ruin every game.

                                            [addendum:] I will concede that Sin Eaters would be well served by a stronger motivation, but I think that is more of an issue that stems from poor presentation (which I'd say is geist's primary weakness), not from total apathy. [and, lest we forget, many initial corebooks suffered from poor presentation; I tell my werewolf players to ignore the core and read Lore and Rage first.] I'd also love to see an "outside context problem" introduced - but that's a concept that was launched with the chronicle books, and it's not necessarily a fault of 1e to lack them. But they aren't without a reason to care, certainly no more so than other splats.

                                                        Originally posted by Krobeles View Post

If your PC's are entirely apathetic dickbags, then your story isn't really going anywhere in the first place, no matter what you throw at them. Unless you throw it directly in their faces of course, which you still totally do with the Abmortals.
                                                        Originally posted by Gabby View Post
                                                        Yes, thank you! The "they have no reason to care" argumentation was starting to bug me and I hadn't put my finger on why.

                                                        Your reason to care is the same reason any medium in fiction has to care: you can see and hear the ghosts. They can tell that you can see and hear them. So they're going to keep asking and you're going to keep seeing problems. You could obstinately ignore them, but that would make you a tragic (and probably unbalanced) NPC.
                                                        The issue is that without some overarching reason--the Sensitivity in Princess, the Orders in Mage, the Oath in Forsaken--you don't really have any splat-wide reason for doing anything. Yes, individual Sin-eaters with a conscience--likely player characters--will care. But there's no reason that the average Sin-eater would. The setting only works if the players decided they want to be good guys who benevolently take up the mantle of ghost busters.

                                                        The other half of this is that the fluff acts as if they care but gives them no reason to do so. Why would krewes fight over territory for the right to... solve crimes and lay ghosts to rest? Why would they bother with Abmortals that aren't serial killers? I mean, the Hungry Exorcist eats ghosts and that's supposed to be super bad, but... why? Why should Sin-eaters care about that?

                                                        The average medium in a fictional setting is going to be motivated by a sense of purpose, but that's because we're only focusing on that one medium who makes for the best story. We don't see any others in the world who ignore their gifts or who use their ability to talk to ghosts to figure out the password and security codes of bank vaults, or just have ephemeral beings shake the tables and make spectral manifestations pretending to be dead loved ones to con people. Yes, the PCs will likely want to do good things because they're good guys. But many won't care because it doesn't personally effect them. Order and Covenant and Tribe gives someone a reason to care.

                                                        Geists don't have that. So their entire society seems to work on "It's the right thing!" but there's still talk of infighting and all that even when there's no logical reason for it. The setting is very flat. And even then, this is the World of Darkness. Not every PC is going to want to help lay ghosts to rest because "it's the right thing to do".
