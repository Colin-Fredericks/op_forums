    Ideas on how supernaturals respond to the Rite of Investment

    09-08-2024, 02:16 AM
    Hello everyone!

    Today I'm asking for ideas on how various kinds of supernaturals might respond to the Rite of Investment compared to Immortals and Ephemeral Entities (whose response to the Rite is already detailed in Book of Lasting Death), particularly if I decide to run a crossover adventure where all PCs are invested cultists or Arisen, but they can be from other lines, like vampires, werewolves, etc. I'm using the 2nd edition rules.

    Thank you for your help!

                                  Vampire Inheritors
                                  Vampires can be invested with up to 4 Pillar points.

                                  When invested, they gain additional dots in a Discipline (which also counts towards the dice pools to activate the Discipline's powers) determined by the Arisen master's defining Pillar. The number of dots gained is equal to the Arisen's defining Pillar rating.

                                  A vampire does not gain extra powers for dots beyond 5 (between his own dots in it and those provided by the investment), but excess dots still apply as bonus to the dice pools to activate the Discipline's powers.

                                  Ab - Majesty
                                  Ba - Dominate
                                  Ka - Protean
                                  Ren - Auspex
                                  Sheut - Nightmare

                                  Example: Seti is an Ashem with Sheut 5. He invests 4 points from any Pillar in Phobos, a Nosferatu elder with Blood Potency 7. Phobos has Nightmare 3. Thanks to the investment he has effectively Nightmare 8. This means he can access the powers of Nightmare 4 and 5 which he did not know before, and adds 8 dice for Nightmare to his dice pools to activate the discipline's powers rather than 3 only.

                                  Using Discipline powers beyond those already known to the vampire (like Nightmare 4 and 5 in the example) costs 1 Pillar point per scene, in addition to other normal costs.

                                  If the Arisen reincarnates in the invested vampire's body (let's say it's possible), what happens? I want to avoid a weird vampire-mummy hybrid. Is the Beast cast out? What does that look like? Or does the Arisen need to fight the Beast psychically before taking over?

                                  Thoughts?

                                      Thoughts?
                                      It is for sure an interesting option but as a game master i would not recommend it, for two reasons:

                                      1- Discipline levels are an expression of fine control on the Beast and Vitae power, Pillar points don t seem to heavily resonate with this.

                                      2-"Gifting" free dots of something very costly in terms of experience seems too powerful considering the small investment by both the mummy and the vampire.

                                      From my experience, when designing those weird powers i would recommend those fundamentals:

                                      -The Decree Affinity is a main expression of the Mummy essence imposed on the cultist,it should always appear.

                                      -Human Sorcerer can use Pillars as substitute "ingredients" for Rituals, but Dark eras implies that as Pillars are the stuff of creation every kind of sorcery could benefit from them.
                                      Mages can use Pillars as Yantras.
                                      Vampires should absolutely use Pillars as Sacraments for appropriate blood sorcery rituals (if you use Theme rules you could associate each of them with a specific Pillar) and even devise special over the rules rituals that must use them.

                                      -Immortal and Sadiks are very compatible with Mummies powers, so it s natural that they benefit more from their Master power.
                                      Other creatures are less compatible, their benefits should be less powerful, also as a matter of balance

                                      -Benefits examples seems unrelated to the kind and quantity of Pillars invested, probably because they tend to change frequently and that limit eases book keeping.



                                      For taking over Vampires cultist i would personally rule that while merging the character can use both Vampiric and Mummy powers (with the limits stated on the memory bleed conditions) but suffers both drawbacks.
                                      For added detail every time you spend willpower to unlock Utterances you could also lose access to a Discipline dot.
                                      After the fusion ends, the character is simply a Mummy with some memories and physical features of the ex vampire

                                                  Here's another attempt. I have revised Blood Sorcery, Mysteries of the Dragon, Carthian Law and Invictus Oaths, but I don't think the Pillars relate well enough to them. In a way, yes, as they all seem to stretch the vampire condition in one way or another, but not in a way relatable to the Arisen.

                                                  Vampire Inheritors, second suggestion
                                                  When looking at the effects of Investment on mortals, sorcerers, Sadikhs and Immortals, it seems clear that in all cases the Arisen's Defining Pillar is what grants the core boon (the Decree Affinity), while greater attunement to Sekhem leads to increasing ability to draw from the mummy's power. Which Pillars are invested makes no difference to a Mortal, Sadikh or Immortal except for the different feeling they give to the heart, while it's significant for Sorcerers because they spend specific Pillar points to lesser the requirements of rituals.

                                                  With vampires, I don't want to use Mortal Decree because I want to show how different a vampire is, rather than just framing vampires as augmented mortals. I therefore present you with:

                                                  Vampiric Decree: an Invested vampire feels the Arisen's Pillars tugging at the Vitae and the Beast from behind his heart. His soul now different from that of mortals, he instead filters the Mummy's Defining Pillar through the Beast, which sees new or latent potentialities in herself being brought out by the Investment.

                                                  Depending on the Arisen's Defining Pillar, the vampire becomes able to learn a new Discipline without needing to imbibe first Vitae from someone who knows it. Should he have already imbibed such and gained access to the Discipline, he may now purchase new dots at in-clan cost rather than out-of-clan. Should it be already a Clan dicipline for him, he can spend a Pillar point to gain bonus dice equal to the Arisen's master dots in his Defining Pillar on a single activation roll.

                                                  The Pillar-Discipline combinations are:

                                                  Ab-Majesty
                                                  Ba - Dominate
                                                  Ka - Protean
                                                  Ren - Auspex
                                                  Sheut - Nightmare.

                                                  A vampire can store a number of Pillar points equal to his Blood Potency divided by 3 and rounded up, hence:

                                                  BP 1-3 = 1 point
                                                  4-6=2
                                                  7-9=3
                                                  10=4
