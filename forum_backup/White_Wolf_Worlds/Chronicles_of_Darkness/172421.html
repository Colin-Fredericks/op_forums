              Having read and played Requiem and read Awakening, my experience is that, in general, powers that affect wide swaths of people or areas of land, or that deal with spontaneous travel over anything bigger than a room, are the powers that get changed the most when it comes to the translation into MET. And both Requiem and Awakening have some nice sidebars on what changed and why.
              The primary problem I see with a Demon LARP is the simple fact that a lot of the Embeds/Exploits fall into the category of altering wides swaths of people, as they alter the observers perception's of the scene. Coupled with the fact that most of them also don't incur a Resistance... I can almost hear the whining now.
              I do like the idea of the plastic name-tag for Covers though.

                  I was playing around with a Demon LARP (played Masquerade and Requiem LARP for several years). The Rules are rather easy imho. But the problem is the espionage and pact part. If you have a lot of people, some who play human, some who play angels and GM-Cultists, it would work fine. But as long as you have just 10 or 20 People to play, and everyone like to be a demon, I see no chance for a satisfying game.

                  Vampire is lot easier because Vampires have an own society. You can always celebrate religions festivals, perform rituals, fight against the Covenant you do not like, save the Masquerade and judge People who violate the traditions.
                  But Demons? What did you like to do on a session?
                  “My name is Mr...”
                  “Psst! No Names!”
                  “Oh, OK! I am a Guard...”
                  “Psst! No personal information, the GM is everywhere!”
                  “Oh, OK! I... I... well, forget it!”

                  You just need people (mostly normal people) around the Demon for the full fun.

                  I tried to solve that problem for weeks but I do not have a answer for now.

                              Originally posted by Sethos View Post
                              Phew! Excuse me, Satchel, English is not my first language. I understand the most but I am not sure what you want to say. Could you pleas repeat it with other words?
                              Any sane game of Demon is not going to result in "No names! No personal information!" because 1) these are both aspects of demonic Covers - i.e. not aspects of the demon that the Machine is after, as well as capable of being instantly discarded by all but the weakest Unchained - and thus the bigger threat is having the wrong Cover be found somewhere it shouldn't be, 2) demons favor oblique, matter-of-fact terminology for precisely the reason that it's easy to play off compared to the poetic/occult terms other splats use in their respective patois, and 3) I can practically guarantee something with as big-picture a perspective as God's is not going to be monitoring conversations for words like "guardian" or "wheel." (And even if it did, a) that's what codes are for and b) there are Embeds for the specific purpose of covertly sharing information or obscuring conversation.)

                              In short, "everybody shushes everybody for three hours" is a rather poor game of espionage, techgnostic or otherwise.

                                                  Originally posted by Sethos View Post
                                                  How would you handle pacts? Because the fun about it is to outsmart the human. How do you do that with no one playing the human?
                                                  I would make them in between sections. This is not a theme for the night of the live action, this should be handled with an appropriate storyteller, before or after the big night.

                                                  Step one would be defining which contacts the demon has. Does his cover has a profession? A family? Old friends?

                                                  Step two, the storyteller would look at the demon's social and mental traits and make a roll based on how will this demon is trying to locate the appropriate "victim". Will him go around spying on their lives to find their flaws and take note on hiw he could make it better? Will him seduce his victim with the lust of power, women, money or a new job promotion? Will him create a situation that the mortal would be forced to make a desperate pact?


                                                  After analyzing this roll, the storyteller should provide the player a short list of mortals (no more than 3) with a few nice details based on the sucess of the test. Excepcional sucess will reveal the perfect victim or that specific person could even contact the demon in search for help.

                                                  Step three, after finding the right victim, the storyteller would roleplay that person and set up if he will accept, deny or negociate the pact, based on how good the player can convince, intimidate or seduce.

                                                  Step four, the storyteller records on a special sheet the pact, the victim's details and the terms of it.

                                                  Done. I think imho that is very silly to have mortal players on this Live Action. They not just would have more fun role playing demons, but also as soon as they discover that their role on this scenario is just to make a pact and they are among players with a lot of powers that they don't have, I can picture them very pissed off. I would have a lot to explain to them off character! In a live action is crucial to have fair rules and make the most out of every character, everybody must be protagonist and antagonist to someone else. Remember the storyteller should not interfere that much and it would be boring as hell to have only the ST as an enemy.

                                                  But these are my players, and I don't know if such an unfair environment would be fine with yours.
