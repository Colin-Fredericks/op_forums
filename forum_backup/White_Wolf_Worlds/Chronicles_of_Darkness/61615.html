    Citybuilding for changeling

    01-30-2014, 01:45 PM
    I'm planning on running a changeling game next fall and I've gotten to thinking that I may want to write up my own city to set it in. Previously I'd mostly run games out of published cities (mostly Chicago) but I want to keep the focus on Changeling and my last experience with miami wasn't great (the players got fed up with the major NPCs all being evil, crazy, incompetent or some combination thereof and I don't exactly blame them). I already have a couple basic ideas of what I'm doing with the setting, but it's still very barebones. What do you think I should keep in mind while I'm writing this? how do you generally approach the setting for your games?

          The only prerequisite for any setting, I find, is conflict. Not necessarily violent or supernatural conflict, but every character should be facing opposition in their plans either from the environment or other characters. And if they aren't, then their plans should be having ramifications on other characters that pose significant obstacles. All stories are predicated on conflict of some kind, after all.

          On to city design. Personally, I loved building my setting around my local city. There were just so many perfect little facts and details to pull on. The Freehold, for example, was meant to feel like it was under siege. In real life, our state government has been instituting all these new laws giving police more powers while drumming up bikie gangs as this terror threatening the state. So I threw copious amounts of that into the game, putting the Freehold at risk from all kinds of human threats as well as the supernatural.

          The home town was fantastic for me because it was so easy to find inspiration just by walking around. There are some locations that are just too perfect. For example, there's a suburb in the inner city called Spring Hill, and there are a few Gentleman's Clubs, registered brothels, even a few BDSM establishments up there. How could I not make that the home base for the Spring Court? From there, the Spring Court evolved into a Court involved in prostitution, the occasional spot of blackmail, and with members who possibly have access to some powerful local businessmen. For a fun contrast, I then made their Queen a child about eight years of age (and also a phenomenally powerful Oracle). I like finding out some fun facts about my home city and figuring out how those little details could intertwine with a Changeling chronicle. Makes for great inspiration. Once I have a place, person, or fact that fits so well into a particular Court, I like to build the Court's members around that little tidbit and then start to tweak the individual members to reflect and contrast their Court's source of inspiration.

          I hope that made sense. It did in my head anyway. I really enjoyed creating my game's setting. The setting I made for Changeling was more fun to build than any other I've created in my time as a GM, and I'm really happy I decided to set it in my home city like I did.

              I think a lot of GMs trip way too hard on their setting city when it comes to World of Darkness games. The amount of detail they put in them often goes unappreciated and thus wasted. I feel it is a remnant of habits picked up playing more tactical roleplaying games with sandbox settings, where GM duties almost always included a large investment of time and energy designing engaging set pieces, which are later used as cool backdrops for fights. While I am sure that one can run a Changeling game like this (and I bet it would be fun if done well), I don't think it's strengths really dovetail with that playstyle.

              My advice is to devote your energy into developing the Dramatis Personae; They are your setting and they are the elements that will provide the most conflict, and as Azahul as already mentioned, conflict is important when it comes to running an engaging game. If you have a strong cast of characters, it almost doesn't matter where you set your game. In the majority of your scenes, details such as city and geographic locations are just colorful descriptions. The perspective is often much closer in.

                      I picked a place to set my game that would be inspirational, but practical:
                      • The city's name is Athens, the setting for a Midsummer Night's Dream (for me, this alone is nearly enough)
                      • Athens OH has a university in it, which means its a small town, but it has people from all over the world in it (so the players wouldn't feel limited)
                      • The city has a lot of neat odd things in it-- a famous old asylum that has been refurbished to be part of the university, they have a pagan festival each year and a heavy metal festival, there are cool native American earthworks in the area, an old growth forest, etc.
                      • It's small enough that I don't have to imagine a huge population of mortals or changelings
                      • It's big enough that a lot of goods and services are available and it's possible to hang out but also avoid being seen (at least for a while)
                      • I wanted a place that would mirror where the players actually live, but isn't our town (so it would be realistic, but fictional to them)
                      • It's near a major city, so if the characters hate it, they could just shift the game there-- they also can easily access anything you would need a city to access
                      • Some of the real places there are neatly quirky and easy for me to think about in the World of Darkness setting, like the Smiling Skull Saloon (a cool dive that welcomes bikers and college students)

                      So find somewhere that you think is cool and just dig down and do some research on it-- there are often local legends and myths that help a lot. Maybe start with somewhere that appears on the "reportedly haunted" list? http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of..._United_States
                      Right now I'm doing a side adventure for my Las Vegas game in Twin Falls Idaho-- it's been a great location to build a story off of.

                          Narratively speaking, the setting of a story can be as much of a character as the PCs and NPCs alike. I would definitely recommend doing research into the history of the place you're setting the game; It'll give you tons of material with which to design your freehold. Try to reflect the personality of your city in the freehold's culture and customs. I designed my own setting based on the area I grew up in, and my local historical society's records were an invaluable resource to me. I've gotten a lot of mileage out of abandoned iron mine/ghost town from the turn of the century that's just north of my hometown, too.

                                  I've got some thoughts written up here that might help, and a couple to add: Check out Damnation City. Really. And once you think you've got a city in mind, if it's a real one, Google-haunt it. Zoom in and over the streets. Look at the things that seem weird, don't make sense, or stand out. Look up its public art. And get ready for those Wyrd coincidences, like streets with fairy tale names.
