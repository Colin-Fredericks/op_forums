    Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
    Sure, you'd have no chance of doing the super high technical aspects of the mental skills but that applies to the highest form of any of the skills. You'd have about as much luck performing a complex acrobatic routine as you would open heart surgery given no experience in Athletic activites and only a 5 minute explanation on proper form.
    I don't actually consider the two equivalent within their respective skills, but... we should not neglect equipment bonuses. Mental skills have plentiful equipment bonuses. If I perform surgery in a hospital with staff and have relevant manuals then I'm up to a +4 or 5 bonus. I get +1 for appropriate attire during the acrobatics, not much else will help. Standard rules makes the two actions roughly as likely to be successful which is about right for the given situations. It would be much easier to perform the surgery if the untrained penalties were the same for all skills.

    Realism isn't the only consideration. Physical and social skills are good choices for every character. Some character types have little use for mental skills. Decreasing the incentive to buy at least a dot in a mental skill that a player might want to use increases the likelihood of some players feeling like they are wasting 4 points at character creation dumping them into mental skills when they probably don't need to do it. Then anyone that wants to play a character focusing on mental skills has less motivation because their character is actually balanced weaker than the physical and social types.

    It should be easy to see the benefit to buying a +4 bonus equivalent for that first dot of a mental skill, twice that of physical and social skills. All skills being equal makes mental skills less appealing. Their purview too often falls into the mundane, the parts of a story that are skipped. The system allows mental skills to be impressive on the occasions they are useful. I'm just not feeling the "make them the same" perspective.

                            I think one of the ways were the higher Mental untrained penalty can be removed and compensated for that hasn't been brought up is that Mental Skills lend themselves more readily to extended actions in a lot of basic applications (not that there aren't plenty of Physical/Social examples too, but in application I think there's a solid case for them to be less common). Being untrained is a massive penalty to extended actions because your maximum theoretical rolls are based on your unmodified Attribute + Skill + Specialty; even if you take out the dice penalties (not that I'm advocating this, I think the idea of standardizing them at -2 is better than most other proposed changes so far).

                            Consider an Attribute 3/Skill 0 character, vs an Attribute 3/Skill 1/one applicable Specialty character (again, ignoring dice penalties).

                            In a single roll it's 3 dice vs. 5 dice, which is a decent advantage, but not a ground shaking one.

                            For an extended roll it's a maximum of 9 dice vs a maximum of 25 dice... which is a huge advantage.

The rules abstractly create a simulation only to provide a gaming environment. It is invalid to take the position that the rules should simulate reality in order to simulate reality. The rules are always about the game, solely and entirely. Anyone that disagrees and wants to take another position... we are at an en passe.

                                                            We aren't talking about the same level of use. The rules never talked about the same level of use. There are a few levels; automatic failure, automatic success, simple untrained and difficult untrained. The default for mental actions is difficult while social and physical is simple. Look at the sample actions provided. It isn't about hacking the pentagon. We are talking about hacking computers, programming or database searches and contrasting that with hitting something or picking a pocket. The first group requires knowledge to succeed without blind luck.

                                                            Long story short, it is an invalid comparison, super specialized to super specialized, as you put it. The only proper comparison is typical roll versus typical roll (because it is a game rather than a pure simulation). We must neglect all the possible rolls that we would consider unnecessary to roll.
