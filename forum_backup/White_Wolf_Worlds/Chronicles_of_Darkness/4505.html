    Human Threats

    11-06-2013, 03:05 PM
    So, beyond using Hunters, how would you guys make humans more of a threat in Werewolf? I'm one of those people who like to strike a balance between spirit/human antagonists - the pack needs to patrol both sides of their territory to keep things in check. However, most of the games I've played centered solely around spirit politics and Pure smashing, with the effect of these things on the herd left as little more than an afterthought.

    I saw someone mention cutting out Lunacy, and I think that's a step in the right direction, making it more risky to shift in public.

    Ideas, everyone? Maybe we could turn this into one of those 101 Story Hooks threads.

                  Like Papa Bear briefly mentioned, Sorcerers. The idea I've been working on for my game is of humans which abuse the shadow for their own gain - something that came out of a discussion on the old forums (about halfway down). The concept that I've been developing an internet fueled, structure-less... movement which regularly generates small local cults by simply sharing information about spirits - summoning them, common ban and banes, how to generate types of essence to bribe them. And they use this knowledge for personal gain in the physical world; the particular cult I'm working on are eco-terrorists. It's a nice turn on the usual situation (spirits abusing humans) and a possibility that really deserves to be expanded more.

                  I would imagine that any humans which pose a threat to werewolves in direct conflict (ie combat) are going to end up looking a lot like Hunters. That's not a bad thing, but its the most obvious and common route to go.

                  In a general sense, I think that normal humans have the most utility in very indirect or modern threats. As complications to a situation. Most of the "threats" and "enemies" that we talk about today are things like police investigations, the press, ruining someone's reputation, finances... Werewolves are generally not good at handling these sort of problems. If, for example, their photos were to be uploaded to a pedophile watch site, they would have a hard time ever doing anything in the mortal world again, and that opens the door to more potent problems.

                  The interplay between physical and shadow is a big theme in my game. Because of the location, I haven't gotten to use some of the more blatant ones, but all human activity causes reflections in the shadow: gang violence, a sudden upswing in chemical abuse, community projects, corruption and greed... These all shape what the Shadow looks like, and werewolves tend to want to make it look they way they want. A good pack has to care about human things, which they aren't very good at, because of this. It makes for excellent crossover with Changeling the Lost, by the way.



                  I will also agree with King's Raven on the point that regular mortals should be considered the most dangerous around. Dresden Files does a really good job emphasizing that. Sure, they're weak, squishy, and ignorant, but there are so damn many of them - that's the reason almost every splat has a masquerade equivalent: if the secret gets out, you suddenly have 7 billion hunters. And like Papa Bear said, it is the information age. . .
                  My personal twist to this is to emphasize that humans have the most terrifying and potent gift the world has ever produced, one that can work miracles and devise unspeakable tortures: creativity.
                  In my experience, the most terrifying antagonists are much, much weaker than the players, and know it, and plan for that very carefully.

                                  Generally, I find humans (especially humans armed with guns) to stand up against werewolves just fine in the games I've played in/run. Don't get me wrong, that healing rate means werewolves can hit-and-run like nobodies business, but person with 2 Dex, 2 Firearms (not impossible, especially if your games are set in a country where firearms are around, like the US. Unusual, maybe, but not out of the question) and a shotgun rolls 8 dice with 9 again, against which werewolves don't get Defense, and only Armour if they're in Garou (even then, 1/1?). So, unlikely to kill/incapacitate one straightaway, but it only takes one good roll on the part of the human to cause serious damage to a werewolf in question. And as any GM knows, the antagonists always roll well against your players. And even if they only hurt the werewolf, say deals 4 damage. Wolf turns to run, starts bolting, human takes another shot. So, on a physical basis, humans with guns tend to be plenty threatening. Lunacy negates some of this, but I believe it even says in the book that this should not be relied on. And if you don't like guns, say they're using a machete or an axe?

                                  But yeah, putting that aside, the main threat humans pose to werewolves/vampires/whatever are simple numbers, tendency to notice patterns and ability to organise. If even a small group (say 5?) gets it in their head to investigate the odd goings-on that happens on the nearby hill every full moon that occasionally co-incides with bodies turning up mauled by animals, the local pack can't really do a whole bundle about it, at least in a way that won't cause more problems. Just have humans notice the patterns or evidence the players leave behind: if your group is any like mine, they tend to be kind of arrogant about that kind of stuff and forget details. So keep track of things that might leave traces as a scene goes, and unless the players make a point of addressing those issues (like, say, going back to pick up their pants - and wallet - after they change into Urshul and run away), make a note. Once they start to pile up, have an NPC notice. Either you'll have an antagonist, or the players will make a point of covering their (and pardon the pun) tails to avoid that situation. Which is what you want.

                                      Normally I do this over on the Exalted Forums, but I love me some Mortals (if you see me refer to 'em as Mortals, that's just habit), and I love me some werewolves. Therefore!
                                      Baseline humans as Legitimate Threats to Werewolves Plothooks, Go!
                                      1. Local factory is dumping chemicals/polluting in ways they shouldn't be, but the local government is looking the other way because it's a small town and the place employs a significant percentage of the populace, and it being shut down means lots of people out of work. Unfortunately, this pollution is slowly tainting and corrupting the Shadow near the factory, empowering spirits of illness and poison in the area. Dealing with the spirits won't solve the root problem.

                                      2. One of the PCs gets pulled over for speeding or driving with a tail light out or something. Best to pull this one after a bad night when said PC is all beat up or covered in blood or has a car full of ritual ingredients or something. If you just pull a PC over and give 'em a fine that's a good way to remind the PCs that mortals have the backing of scary, dangerous society behind them...and if they get taken in to the station for questioning (this is mostly for the "covered in blood" option), things get complicated fast. And if they just tear the cop in half and throw his car in the weeds and high tail it out of there after destroying the camera in his police car...well, the resulting manhunt gets interesting too, especially once forensics investigators start looking at the PC's DNA when they find the cop's body.

                                      3. Similarly, you can serve the PC with a lawsuit or have a landlord evict him or have an NPC try to start a romantic relationship. Entanglements with the mortal world always lead to complications, and lots of werewolves are the loner/nomad types, and don't exactly have much in the way of legal documents on them...and if you can actually get a PC to care about a human lover, who then gets suspicious...things get fun quickly.

                                      4. Spirits possess people, when the can. You can't always exorcise or banish 'em: usually, you end up killing people who get ridden. And that leads to investigations of the strange murders, which can get you in hot water months after you've moved on to worrying about other things, as these sorts of investigations can go slowly. Good as a background-level threat while something more pressing and immediate is going on, so that the PCs have to debate whether to go all-out against the present threat if that increases risk of exposure.

                                      5. Sheep's clothing: have a human who likes one of the PCs try to RECRUIT that PC as a hunter. Bonus points if the human is hunting vampires or a Promethean or something. This gives you a chance to show that there are humans in the know working together to fight the supernatural...and to show how scary they are in action. This is best done when the NPC has been introduced before as a likeable person: a beat cop who knew the PC's dad, or a doctor who patched them up one time no questions asked, or something. If you can actually get a PC werewolf to care about an NPC hunter (or at least see them as a weapon to aim at things threatening the PCs' territory), then trying to maintain the relationship with this dangerous band of humans while hiding your nature is pretty fun. Especially when the vampire hunters start packing silver crosses and bullets, because everyone knows vampires don't like silver, right?

                                      So those're a few from me. If you've got any fun ones you've used or been meaning to use, I'd love to hear 'em.

                                          Wise Old Guru’s post has a number of interesting ideas; my only quarrel is that all of them are examples of hunters in action. That’s what nWoD’s hunters are: mortals who have gotten proactive about dealing with supernatural menaces.

                                          I would ditch Lunacy, but mainly because the new Integrity mechanics already cover the “mortals freak out when seeing a man turn into a beast and start tearing up the landscape” thing. Lunacy is now mostly redundant; where it isn’t, it’s either a useful shorthand mechanic for dealing with extras (i.e., “how to describe the effects of supernatural-violence-induced Breaking Points without having to track an extra’s Integrity”) or it’s a pain-in-the-rear obstacle that gets in the way of an otherwise-valid antagonist being a threat to werewolves.

                                          My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                          Wise Old Guru has the right idea so a few more of those.


                                                          Your pack's territory has been locked up in a court case for longer than you've been around. Well now the Judge's final ruling is in and the land must be returned to the First Peoples, Aboriginals, Native American's or their local equivalent. Immigrants and news crews are converging on the territory en mass, there's even one influential politician who thinks he can get some votes out of this. To make matters worse, the pack's primary locus is of enormous cultural or religious importance.

                                                          Five years after a bit of fun, a lawyer hands a member of the pack a summons to a paternity suit. The mother has a bit of local fame so don't expect this to end with finance. Possible twists include the child having clear behavioural problems that show it's a Werewolf, and the mother insists the child needs your family medical history to get the best possible care. A PC below the age of majority is tracked down by his family and the police say they've got to go home.

                                                          A young girl is killed by a wild wolf, a perfectly regular wolf. All the local men are getting ready to go on a wolf cull. Do Werewolves have any loyalty or duty to wolves?

                                                          Some talented and collage kids have managed to discover and cultivate a new smokable plant. It's effect on human are basically weed, but at the moment it's still legal. The problem is that on Werewolves this stuff works like class A hardcore drugs; just getting miles downwind of a lit spliff and sniffing the air is dangerously addictive. Don't expect help from law enforcement, it's legal and in this small collage town a bit of weed was the biggest crime. The Sheriff is quite happy to see the crime rating vanish overnight.
