    Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
    It is entirely possible to believe something exists, even observe signs of its existence, without ever witnessing the thing itself.
    I agree, and don't believe you even need to cite black holes for the proposition. Far simpler, you just observed the phenomena of "religion," observed by billions of people across the our world in a myriad of forms.

    Heck, some people's religion explicitly includes what many would consider "magic."

    Believing in the existence of supernal magic and Awakened mages is hardly a leap of faith for people in the WOD.


    Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
    Take the hunter scenario I put forward several pages back: You've got a group of hunters who wake up with foggy memories. They recall overhearing a report of suspicious activity at the local cemetery, where they went and met a man (whom they remember fairly well) doing...something. The details of the scene are foggy. However, what they do know is that the following day two of them were injured with what looked like human claw and bite marks, seventeen of the most recent graves are now empty, and the bodies that were in those graves cropped up three blocks away looking to all eyes as though they'd walked there on their own.

    Now I don't think it's too big a leap for those hunters to think, "That man we met yesterday is some scary necromancer." Though they might also tag on, "And he fucked with our memories!" They might also leap to conclusions like, "He must be using some form of blood magic, like those vampires we stamped out last year" or "He's clearly possessed!" Because off the mark judgement about the supernatural are a common theme of Hunter. But it's fair to say that they can absolutely reason out that he's a risk, even if they cannot recall the exact act of animating the dead.
    I think it's less of a situation of "foggy" memories, than either total amnesia or having clear and "rationale," albeit totally wrong, memories. Suffering the accompanying Breaking Points and potentially losing Integrity, i.e., a damaged soul, also has serious repercussions.

    I would additionally note that any rationalizations for those who have knowledge of the supernatural could include scenarios where the mage is seen as an unfortunate victim of supernatural forces or entities, and thus needs to be saved by the Hunters, whether the mage wants "saving" or not. Such an encounter would not go well...

    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    Dataweaver, the Merit is called Actively Oblivious, and it is official, though I think it was from first drafts, so it may or may not end up looking the same in the final product. It wasn't from a leak, it was from one of Dave's open development blog posts. I think the one about Proximi.

    EDIT: Yep, here it is.
    "Actively Oblivious" is hardly a panacea for Sleeper Hunters. It requires the character to ignore the supernal magic, a bad situation for a Hunter, and it largely only delays the damage, rather than prevent it.

Their memory warps, and they rationalize the event away or substitute a non-magical equivalent. Convincingly reminding them (including using magic to do so) causes them to suffer another Breaking Point, and then forget again.
            (emphasis added)
