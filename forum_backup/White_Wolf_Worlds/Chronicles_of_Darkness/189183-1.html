            Minor Update, added two Alterations that I felt were interesting to the Vicissitude write-up. Reposting them here for ease of access:

            Rank 3 Alterations
            Butchery of the Able Flesh
            Target Number of Successes: 10
            The flesh-sculptor is able to refine the violent properties of Vicissitude that allow him to mutilate the bodies of others, refining them to focus on specific body parts and damaging them. This Alteration allows the flesh-sculptor to inflict longer term Conditions and Tilts, representing refined strikes against the functional anatomy of the target. The flesh-sculptor may inflict 1 Condition/Tilt initially, and others for every 3 dots of Potency.

            Tilts such as Arm Wrack, Leg Wrack, Blindness, and Deafen are often inflicted, and Conditions such as Disabled or Mute may also be applied. This Alteration may be used as a Hanged Spell or can be cast as normal upon a subject of Vicissitude. The ritualist must touch his opponent by making a Dexterity + Brawl roll minus the victim’s Defense in order to activate the Hanged Spell.


            Rank 5 Alterations
            ​

            Ascendancy of Alacritous Change
            Target Number of Successes: 10
            By using this Alteration immediately before another Alteration, the subsequent use of Vicissitude is rendered into a Hanging Spell, prepaid for seven days, with a trigger of the flesh-sculptor's touch. Unlike the Gift of the Slumbering Flesh, the Alteration isn’t held dormant in its target, the flesh-sculptor holds it and can inflict it upon a target of their choosing when touching them.

            This power allows the flesh-sculptor to seemingly inflict instantaneous Alterations that have been prepared, touching them using a Dexterity + Brawl minus the victim’s Defense. Any Alterations which are subject to Ascendancy of Alacritous Change are treated as Hanging Spells and must be extended to keep them after the initial time period is up.


            What do you guys think? I feel the ideas are solid, but I feel like the mechanics might need tightening. As always, all feedback is appreciated.

                    Well, there's two mechanical things to understand:

                    Vicissitude isn't permanent. When trying to naturally recover from Vicissitude's Alterations, you treat them as lethal wounds equal to the minimum number of successes required for the Alteration. So for instance with Supplication of the Skin, a human can heal it over time as if it were a 7L wound. It is not an actual 7L wound, but the duration for an unmodified human to heal away Supplication of the Skin would be 14 days, as they heal 1 Lethal damage every 2 days at their natural rate. Ghouls and Kindred can (in the case of Kindred, they have to) spend Vitae to accelerate this process. If I were Kindred and I were afflicted with Supplication of the Skin, I could spend 7 Vitae and heal it immediately.

                    Hanging Spells are a mechanic from NeoTiamat's Blood Sorcery rules which makes certain rituals such as the Touch of Morrigan or Mutilate the Earthly Form into Hanging Spells. Once you cast them, they lie dormant until their trigger is activated. They last for 24 hours after you finish casting the ritual. After that, you have to spend 1 Vitae to extend it another 24 hours if you want to hold onto it longer.

                    I've reproduced the explanation for Hanging Spells into the Vicissitude section on them, so hopefully that will help clear up that confusion.

                                Given that I was the original designer of the system you are now junking, I am probably exempt from having to provide commentary. But I've a few minutes till I finish work, so you get random philosophizing. I will say that there is something fascinating about the way that Tzimisce and Vicissitude have caught the fanbase's imagination and will not let go. A quick glance at the Homebrew Hub shows nearly half a dozen variants of these guys.

                                From a mechanical perspective, Vicissitude is a bloody nightmare (and not in a good way). Basically, my perspective is that there tends to be a kind of three-cornered tug-of-war between Versatility, Simplicity, and Balance -- you can have any two. Versatile powers can do a lot of things, so you either need to spend a lot of word count setting limits, or risk having that power become a kind of "and this is all you need" ability. If you have a strict limit to your word count, then you can either spend it describing all the stuff the power can do (versatility), or all the stuff it can't do (balance), but probably not both. If you want to keep your powerset balanced, then you need to devote extra word count to clearly delineate what it can and cannot do, which means that you either sacrifice what the power can do in order to keep your explanations concise, or you end up with long lists of what can or cannot be done.

                                This isn't quite a zero sum game -- good writing can mitigate things to some extent -- but in my experience one of the three tends to suffer. My blood sorcery system emphasized versatility and balance, but yes, it was a bloody long thing.

                                As for what's interesting or not... I personally feel like slaughtering sacred cows is the wrong approach, because the sacred cows are what make the Tzimisce interesting. In other words, like a lot of Masquerade clans, the Tzimisce were a ludicrously cobbled together mixture of a dozen different ideas, but more than most of the others for them that approach worked. They got Dracula+Flesh Crafting+EEEEEEVIL, and it seems to have hooked into the fandom's psyche in a really strong way. If you strip out much of that, what's left is... eh? Not bad, but nothing to grab the imagination in quite the same way.

                                That said, I also think that in NWoD, the Ordo Dracul has inherited a lot of the Dracula/Mad Scientist vibe from the Tzimisce (and the Dragons are regularly called the most popular covenant, coincidence?). So what might be interesting would be to, instead of making the Tzimisce a Bloodline, work them up as a variation on the Ordo Dracul. A set of traditions and powers, perhaps with Vicissitude being a Mystery and attendant Scales.

                                        I confess Mysteries are a weak area for me -- B&S came around after I'd already invested a LOT of game time into the Coils, so we just updated those and didn't convert the system. That said, the thought which occurs to me is the fact that Kindred have a unique relationship of unliving flesh and living blood. In other words, a large part of a vampire's physical body is inert. It's why they're bullet-proof, because if a bullet punctures a vampire's lung... eh? So what? On the other hand, vampiric Vitae is more active than blood. Yet given this weird setup, there's nothing that says a vampire's body has to be in a standard shape. So long as it's got Vitae flowing through it, it'll run.

                                        I think that a Mystery of Vicissitude would take this idea and use it to counter that aspect of the Curse which requires vampiric bodies to stay in stasis. I can see some 'positive' side-effects in the idea that Vicissitude can allow a vampire to simulate aging, or fix certain pre-mortem disabilities, and so forth. But from a Vitae-Science approach, it's a short jump from "adding wrinkles to your skin" to "adding knives to your fingers." Using Scales to allow you to inject Vitae into others to work its transformative magic seems eminently reasonable.

                                        In other words... the Bane of Stasis locks vampires into the moment of their death. The Mystery of Vicissitude reclaims for the Kindred the ability to grow, change, and mutilate their bodies however they want.
