                          Happened all the time in X-files. Didn't seem to stop anybody there, either.

                          Really, the "mainstream" UFO community believes in astral projection and can't even agree on whether the greys are from Zeta Reticuli (a phsyical impossibility, I might add), are faeries, are demons, or are humans from the post-singularity future projecting themselves back in time inside a computer simulation of reality. Half the community believes that bigfoots are dimensional interlopers with psychic invisibility fields and telekinesis. There are people who believe the Dulce base with all its aliens and mutants is also a clearing house for the demonic magic of the reptilians.

                          Mekhet backstory involves a giant bug monster - could it be a mutant sea louse whose progeny are corpses controlled by the bugs in question? Could Nosferatu be animated by psychic parasites from the astral plane?

                          What bout DMT and its links to spiritual beliefs in regards to dimensional entities in control of the flow of life and death? Could that be linked to Sin-Eaters and Geists?

                          Could John C. Lilly's Solid State Intelligence and ECCO be linked to the God Machine?

                          Are the greys a cryptid, a God-Machine plot, a spirit of fear of hospitals, fey abductors, manifestations of the divine fire shooting itself off without knowledge of what it's doing, searching endlessly for psychic context, or are they really humans from the post-singularity future?

                          Are werewolves a segment of humanity that has learned to self-observe from several universes at once, and now at least one of those universes has noted - one from which they must feed to prevent themselves from experiencing quantum annihilation?

                          If you're doping UFO culture and aliens into your WoD, you've already gone full occult.

                          Speaking of, though, one of my STs ran a badass adventure series linking Resident Evil, the Thing, Starcraft, and the Mi-Go into one solid believable chunk.

                              I have been mulling this over. While (at least in my approach) the line between sci fi horror and supernatural horror is fuzzy at best, I do feel your choice of theme and flavor has a tangible impact of player expectations and how they approach the problem solving.

                              I would be interested in seeing the results of running a session with different groups but with a similar premise - A mysterious organization is abducting people and extracting their brain matter for a otherworldly patron. In one scenario, the patron is a Extraterrestrial, and the organization uses strange techno-organic devices. In the other, the patron is an eldritch entity and they use ancient ritual tools. In all other respects (locations, encounters, npcs) the scenarios are the same.

                              Does the shift in genre trappings create a fundamentally different experience, or do the trappings not matter much overall?

                              The Artificer's Workshop - A collection of Exalted Artifacts
                              Curios, Relics, and Tomes - A collection of Relics (Cursed and Otherwise)
                              The Horror Lab - A collection of Beasts, Monsters and less definable things.
                              Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
                              Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities

                                                  Well, what elements are you planning on using?

                                                  Maybe it would help if you were more specific - because "weird fiction" does encompass vampires and werewolves, just in a different context than "magical dead guy." The process of weird fiction is very different from modern definitions of "science fiction" and "fantasy" in that it is from a period in which there was no division between the two. "Occult" merely means "knowledge which is occluded," and pulps and weird fiction are rife with vampires and werewolves. What I was trying to explain is that the terminology isn't specific, and "weird fiction" encompasses far more of spec-fic than what you're talking about, so maybe provide some tropes here and there? Something to work with - because, otherwise, "supernature" has to encompass what is, in our world, not a component of nature - which includes nearly everything in section 000 and 130 in the public library.

                                                  What I'm getting at is that these words - "vampire" and "werewolf" - much like "demon" - could be broadly applied to a wide spectrum of creatures, mythologies, technologies, and belief structures. These things aren't even "real," physically, but they're real to communities and subcultures, so, when you're constructing a fictional context in which to place your potential stories, you have to decide how these physically incorrect assumptions interact with the reality of your fictional context (one of my professors called it "mythic interfacing," which is such a cool term I had to bring it up). I am merely saying that the same groups of people who believe in aliens also tend to believe in vampires.

                                                  So, how about instead of saying "yeah but no supernatural or occult stuff," which is a statement that has very little meaning, considering that UFO lore is technically "occult stuff," throw out some tropes. I see your example media list, and none of those things preclude the existence of werewolves.
