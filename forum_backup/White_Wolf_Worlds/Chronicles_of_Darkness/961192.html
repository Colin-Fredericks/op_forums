    Problem – Nameless mage in Pentacle cabal

    08-31-2016, 04:14 PM
    Lately, I have a greatest Mage ST headache – Nameless PC with Pentacle cabal of others PC.

    It's constant pain. Example:
    Storyteller: What do you do?
    Rest of cabal: We go do things for mentors/Consilium/Orders.
    Nameless: I go to library to read about occult and learn about magic on my own terms.

    It’s like all possible answers are ‘I’m brooding in corner until someone need me to call ghosts/spirits’. PC has rejected offers of mentoring from Silver Ladder, Seers of Throne, Guardians of Veil and even Free Council representatives. ( In this sequence, Adamantine Arrow also do not sounds as fitting for her. )

    As 2E setting, Nameless are not having access to Pentacles Orders supplies and help - I’m wondering on to how to show the PC that what she is doing is stupid to not join any Order, from in-game perspective.

    And how Nameless in general are convinced to join Orders at large?

              Or don't bother to fill the Order space at all. Just leave it empty, if that's the way the player wants to play then let them.                               Yeah, but public library doesn't require active membership in a dangerous religious cult. Which is, basically, what Orders are. The difference here is "are you going to enter this creepy occult organization, do their creepy rituals, and participate in their weird and potentially dangerous faith"? I know that joining an Order seems like a no brainer for players, but I can see thousands and hundreds of reasons why a character wouldn't want to do that.

                              What are character's aspirations? What is her Obsession? If the character is capable of ghost/spirit calling, then you probably can make exciting plots about exploring magic on your own by exploring ghost and spirit mysteries; if only option you present to them is brooding in library, then they will brood in library. If they have their Path Arcanum at 3, they can Summon things to ask questions and learn from.
                              I mean, it's a game about exploring Mysteries, not playing Consilium Politics. They don't want to engage with political plots? Don't force them to. I can sympathize, because I find political plots incredibly boring myself, and also like playing self-taught characters that explore and learn on their own. Some people just find that more fun, and not having access to Order's resources might be a boon for them, because hey, what's so fun about relying on others?

                              Problem with this is that while the setting sells you that the life of the nameless is harsh the mechanics dont support that. You can raise your main arcanum up to 5 and then learn the rest by your cabal or none at all. From what you said this PC started with the library merit so actually she joining a order is stupid in this case.
                              Nameless Mage doesn't get 6 Rotes, they don't get 3 Rote Specialties, and they don't get 2 dots of merits. Being Nameless is extremely harsh - you lose out about 11 XP.

                                                  It may be that all you require is a small change in set dressing or how you present things. Instead of setting your players off on a task by having a mentor assign it to them, you throw a bone directly into their path and let them pick it up on their own initiative. For example: instead of having the Mysterium assign the cabal to find the lost tomb of Martek, the characters find a possible map to the lost tomb and rush off with visions of treasure as their motivation. Instead of having the Arrow assign them to investigate a series of mysterious disappearances among the sleepers someone connected to them disappears, or maybe the heroes stumble upon an abduction in progress. Most players will pursue an interesting story on their own, that is why they are playing the game, you just need a hook that doesn't require membership in the army or police force. Also, just because a character isn't a member of an order doesn't mean they can't have a place in Mage society. You can go to a community dinner without having to drink the punch.
                                                  ​I would also point out that if your player wants to play the outsider, that they are not just being difficult to ruin it for everyone else, then as ST you should try to find a way to let them play the character they want, especially if you can exploit it for everyone's enjoyment. The other players may enjoy 'protecting' their friend from the orders.
