      Originally posted by Amravanti View Post
      How does one calculate social doors for spirits? I thought I saw it somewhere once, but I've been unable to find it ever since.
      I can't see a specific rule for it anywhere - the Gathra section is relevant here, but doesn't mention numbers of Doors. Since usually it's lower of Resolve or Composure, I'd go with spirits having Resistance in Doors.

      Another question re: Social Maneuvering, are rolls to open doors always contested?
      Well if someone *wants* to do something that you want them to do, there's not really any need for Social Manouevering in the first place, I believe.

      And can spirits still learn/have gifts/facets. I assume they can have them, if they can teach them. Power and finesse like any numina? That makes the most sense, but then what about the ones that scale on renown?
      No, spirits cannot learn or use Gifts or Facets. They do not teach Gifts - they tear the symbols of those Gifts into the Essence of a werewolf.

      - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                                                          Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                          Social Manuvering should be used in cases where the target is strongly opposed ( like most spirits in interactions with werewolves ). If we used this every time, it will go in to things like "I want to buy a gun from him - Shopkeeper need to be Social Manuvered in to this" - and a like nonsense.
                                                          Why would you even call for a roll when trying to buy a gun from a shopkeeper? I mean, unless you're playing in a setting with strict gun control laws and you're essentially trying to convince this shopkeeper to break the law, but in that case I would consider that significant enough dramatic consequences that Social Maneuvering would be appropriate. The Social Maneuvering system is simply not designed to only come into play when the mark is strongly opposed to the person maneuvering. If it was, why would there be good and better impressions?

                                                          Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                          Until the target is not resisting coarsion, there is not need to Force the Doors - as the Target do not have putted those Door in first place. Or like the nerd is pick up by hot chick - it's his dreams come true, why would he resist her advnaces to get with him to bed?
                                                          Again, if there's no resistance, why would you even need to roll dice at all?

                                                          Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                          In mine game I used Social Manuvering when mine mage PCs wanted to coarsed the witch to letting them in to her inner sanctum. It is a big deal, if PCs are touched by Abyss. If she would not oposed the idea of them casually see them, I would not use Social Manuvering.
                                                          Right, sure, but once again, if she's not opposed to the idea of them casually seeing her inner sanctum, why would you even use dice rolls to figure out if she'll let them see it? They want it, she's cool with it, you shouldn't even need to make a single roll to determine the outcome of that situation.

                                                          Personally, I only call for rolls when two conditions are met:
                                                          1. There is a reasonable chance of the roll failing. If you don't have to roll dice to see if you tie your shoes correctly, why would you need to roll to buy something from a shopkeeper?
                                                          2. There are dramatic consequences for failure. Otherwise, why even bother rolling? Just adjudicate the results so we can move on to something more interesting.

                                                          Now, I have seen quite a few people who don't use Social Maneuvering when the players need to convince a mark to do something (something worth rolling dice for) right away, because they feel the long intervals between rolls makes Social Maneuvering ill-suited to such a situation. To that I say, that's why the Impression and Forcing Doors rules exist. If you're in that much of a hurry to convince this character, you'd better make a damn good impression with him, or Force Doors to coerce him into it. 99% of the problems people have with the Social Maneuvering system can, in my opinion, be resolved by the ST being a bit more generous with Impressions, and the players being a lot more willing to Force Doors.
