                                Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                I did. "When Kindred notice werewolves" and "may[,] with Storyteller discretion" do not translate to "Vampires can detect any other kind of supernatural being with an aura 'similar to' their predator aura as a matter of course."
                                You are taking things vastly out of context. First of all, the bit about werewolves and their aura isn't optional or "with Storyteller discretion." It flat out says that Kindred detect them. And the developers have stated that the intent of the game is that, when a vampire and werewolf meet up, they instantly recognize each other. There is no room for debate here. Vampires detect werewolves as a matter of course. I'm sorry, but you are mistaken on this account.

                                The bit about ST discretion actually says "Other creatures in the World of Darkness may exhibit predatory auras with Storyteller discretion." Not that the ST decides if a vampire detects someone with an aura, but that the aura exists at all. There is no ST discretion with mages - we know they have an aura. Its in the rules; unless you're making some changes in game, detection is on. I suppose one could argue that its not a "predatory" aura, and therefore doesn't count, but that feels like a stretch to me.

                                Now, Changelings and Beasts? These don't necessarily have auras by default. These are the kinds of guys that ST discretion bit apply to, deciding there's an aura for detection. However, as per playtest rules, all you need to see past the Mask are supernatural senses (which vampires have), so an aura is a moot point. As for Beast, even with the superimposed image when using an Atavism, I found that its pretty common for Atavisms to be on all the time. I made no assumptions about which way an ST would rule when it came to these latter two, because its simply unnecessary.

                                This is the trend that 2e, as a whole, seems to be moving towards and embracing as a whole. You can houserule things differently, but its pretty clear that vampires can detect more than other vampires by RAW. That ALL supernaturals detect others with fairly high ease.

                                As for the "notice" bit? You're exaggerating with the difficulty of it. Its the same difficulty vampires have recongizing each other, which, from how the game plays out, is not difficult at all. Unless someone is actively hiding (which is a stretch to assume) or using magic to hide/strengthen Mask, all it takes is a casual brush, an errant sniff, a glance around a room. Given the context of our discussion (if supernaturals will be in the dark about each other), that's all that's necessary to break open the entire secret. And that's all that matters. "Notice" isn't a big restriction for the matter at hand. We're not worried about detecting every last supernatural, we just need a few to break everything open, and the Law of Averages is on the vampire's side.

                                            The question of how many supernatural residents are too many really comes down to what kind of chronicle you're running. If it's one based around a society with role play interaction, friendly and hostile, than you need enough to have such a society (note they don't all have to be from the same splat, as long as they interact with each other). Also if you intend to use other supernatural characters as antagonists and significant npcs. If on the other hand you are playing a 'lone wolf' style of story, with a solitary supernatural working with normal people and dealing with either mortal or extremely rare supernatural plots you only want a few or none.

Or a design decision to help people think of this new ability as the replacement of Aura Sight and to highlight why. This, in no way, negates how auras work.
