    Kindred and the God Machine

    09-22-2019, 04:34 PM
    I've been spending a little time toying with the concept of the God Machine and how it would interact with Kindred existence and culture. As yet, I've only heard of a single minor covenant with any particular attachment to this... deity? Unknowable Cosmic Horror? Whatever the case may be.

    The Kindred, as I understand them, possess a certain earnestness of ego stemming from a need for control. This is born from the Beast and the constant, perpetual knowledge of their own fragility. Ever present is the worry that their will and being will be subsumed by the whiles of the ravenous monster that is inside them and the inevitable degradation of who they fundamentally are. As a result they cling to whatever it is that grants them a sense of stability, an immalleable "truth" by which they can conduct themselves in an uncertain world of darkness. This psychological need is deeply human, but the Kindred feel it in a way that far surpasses our understanding because we are neither immortal nor possessed of a semi-sapient monstrous Other. This also makes them prime candidates for stories involving mind-shattering, Lovecraftian-esque horrors that are capable of subverting their "truths" beyond the capacity for rationalization.

    As for the God Machine itself, it's a touch more complicated. The problem with unknowable cosmic powers who have active control over the fabric of reality is that they are... well... unknowable.

    How have you utilized the God Machine in your chronicles? What interest does your iteration of the God Machine have in Kindred, if any? Has it, in your vision of the World of Darkness, had any appreciable effect on the Kindred throughout history?

      I'm throwing this out there because it always ends up banging around in my head whenever I think of this topic: VII.

      I've always loved the idea of VII. Bizarre motivations, menacing iconography, blind conviction to a cause they don't seem to fully understand. Thus far, my favorite vision of VII was from the 1E Covenant Book, specifically The Sleepers. I liked the concept, but the presentation of what they were and what motivated them (not to mention how young they were in comparison to the Kindred as a whole) wasn't really for me. I'd thought of VII as something older, something that has harried the Kindred for as long as they've known, something that exists outside of any moralistic belief. I have my own issues with the Golconda angle, but that's another story. I liked the purity of the Sleeper personality, an aspect without pity or remorse, a deadly functionary in undead flesh bound to a program that it doesn't need to understand to execute on.

      My potential homebrew iteration ties them to the God Machine and its Angels. Their origins are ancient, but unknown. They keep no records, honor no heroes. There is no higher calling than their directive. Technically they are no longer Kindred as is properly known. The process of creating a sleeper effectively amputates the Beast, grafting in its stead a "mechanical" facsimile. This prosthetic mimics the Beast's behavior most of the time, enough that it's sufficiently convincing to others. For the sleeper, it feels fundamentally wrong in ways they can't quite put their finger on. This prosthetic waits, watches, measures and transmits. When it becomes active, the Kindred is technically in Frenzy with all of the accompanied dissociation. Functionally speaking, VII sleepers behave like white blood cells, dutifully culling irritants and "correcting" imbalances in accordance with whatever holistic sense defines their actions. I'm still working on the origins of their more ritualized behavior, their connection to certain icons and some of the underlying lore (fallen king, rising city, mark of the betrayer, etc.). The idea needs some work, certainly, and I'd welcome any suggestions.

                  Hey. Thanks for the replies!

                  The Kindred-as-failed-experiment is an interesting idea. How I've been approaching the GM's relationship to the Kindred is one of curious disdain. Whatever facet of that higher intellect that takes an interest in our tiny lives views the Kindred as an impossibility, an unnatural variable in whatever hellish algorithm defines its husbandry of the cosmos. They're parasitic perpetual motion machines, primal and (theoretically) eternal, but still so flawed and human. Naturally, that would need to be addressed.

                  VII could easily serve in that respect. Those whose actions and projected futures who do not serve to benefit its influence are targeted while others are left alone. Then again, so could the Ordo Dracul; a sire-less Kindred, supposedly cursed by divinity, founds a Covenant based on the principle of perpetual self-modification. It's potential GM intervention, an attempt to render Kindred en masse into something more appropriate to its sense of what should and should not be. They're all options.

                  The Strix... I feel like the Strix are something else, removed from the God Machine. A facet of whatever "natural order" served as Kindred origins. I personally saw the Strix as the dark truth behind the Golconda myth. The idea is just so pie-in-the-sky, it translates to me like the Disney adaptation of a Grimm fairy tale; it glosses over the blood, murder, dismemberment, and every other affront to decency in favor of a more friendly, palatable tale where everyone lives happily ever after. "Of course, you'll defeat your Beast and spend eternity prancing through the woods like an undead nymph! You certainly won't fuse together with it into a horrible, owl-shaped chimera made of bestial cunning, human bitterness and smoke. Certainly not..." But that's a topic for another time.

                  Tessie:
                  Thanks for the suggestion. It may take a tick, but I'll definitely take a look. It's been a slow slog to pick up 2E stuff, so I know I'm behind on established lore.

                                          The GM is, by design, intended to cut across products and touch--albeit sometimes lightly--the goings-on of the supernatural in CofD. Here's my meta-understanding of it, drawn from various sources:

                                          The GM was created to stabilize reality such that parallel dimensions did not intrude, destabilize and ultimately destroy the integrity of the one both the GM and Mankind exists in (Prime). That reality, however, grinds against a variety of spiritual and mystical realms that can loosely be considered both part of and separate from Prime. Imagine a series of Venn circles, overlapping, of which one the GM is intent on keeping in good order--it thus touches all the intruding realms and has an interest, to such an extent that they impinge on Prime.

                                          Realms that are NOT naturally connected with Prime include diverse timelines, places with very different physical and spiritual rules and regions utterly corrupted by paragon concepts (thought left hanging on purpose).

                                          Examples could be: the Prince of a Thousand Leaves, alternate timelines and the like. The GM has its own mystic substrate realm it can set up and configure for its purposes, though the rules for its use and access are almost completely unknown. Somehow mechanisms are required for its use, which implies rules that may be exceedingly complex but probably not shifting. The mystical realms that impinge on Prime, however, DO shift, and over time the GM itself has broken and very imperfectly performs its created job.

                                          You could imagine a perfect, ticking world in which Mankind goes on about its business without the supernatural touching anything, anywhere--that *might* be what the GM desires. What it gets is a pragmatically managed reality in which it has to constantly fight fires that threaten to consume both it and Mankind in soul-shattering chaos. There are probably constant forks going on, and GM devices emplaced to KEEP such forks apart, lest natural sympathy crash dimensions together....

                                          Whew, ok.

                                          So vampires are a supernatural element; a parasitical intrusion on Prime that began at some point probably due to corruption from another, spiritual realm. Despite any early efforts, they have persisted but are not, in general, hostile to Mankind or the integrity of reality. They break some ideal rules, notably persisting much longer than the minds of Man do, and as such may accumulate glitches that represent vectors for hostile dimensional intrusion or threats to the secrecy directive the GM follows (and that vampires generally agree with).

                                          They, like Man, occasionally require "pruning". They, like Man, occasionally are useful in their unmodified state. They, like Man, occasionally are MORE useful, modified.

                                          The GM has protocols for each condition.
