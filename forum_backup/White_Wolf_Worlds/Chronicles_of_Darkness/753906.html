    WoD Setting - Metro Manila

    11-04-2015, 09:59 AM
    To anyone on the forums who is Filipino and/or happens to live in the Metro Manila area, this is an open invitation for brainstorming ideas. I am looking for help in putting together stuff for a World of Darkness setting in Metro Manila. I have some stuff written up for a few of the supernatural lines, but I'll probably need help working out details and story hooks.

    So far, the references I have include the Trese series of comics, as well as what I remember from certain teleseryes like Encantadia (and its sequels), Sugo, and Aso ni San Roque, among other titles.

                      Thanks, and it's great seeing a fellow Manileño on the forum too. So far, the Yosei Kingdom, is one of the most creative locales I've read. Plus points for basing it on a Japanese folktale. Can't see why a Manila Freehold would be any less interesting in its own way. Kudos on the Diwata Courts. Were you reading American Gods?

                      As for Mage, our 'Fallen Manila Chronicles' involves Mulat who live, breathe, and practice sorcery in The Pearl, Manila's underground society for magic. The mtropolis' administrative body, the Camara is hopelessly mired in red tape and prone to the whims of large cabals. Rather than deal directly with threats, the Camara is in the habit of forming temporary cabals to perform ad hoc problem-solving. It's a noisy, disparate, chaotic, and utterly confusing place especially to foreign mages. Thankfully the locals are charming. The Pearl geographically encompasses parts of Metro Manila with a high concentration of Mulat who operate in the Estrada's Manila. Currently, the city has the lion's share of weirdness in the chronicle.

                      There are scavenger spirits in Tondo which alternately feast on the despair and hope of the poor. In Intramuros, Space magic falters, and the district's ancient fortifications extend deep into the Shadow. In a forgotten Spanish prison, the PCs have encountered the ghosts of conquistadors. We don't have a central Mystery yet, but I'm partial to borrowing London's 'runewalking' in the 2e corebook. Our present antagonist is a celebrity feng shui master who seeks to return Manila to prosperity. Unfortunately that involves summoning an ancient pagan fertility god, and the PCs unite to stop him. It's not that simple though as the PCs have to resolve their conflicts with one another first.

                      All the Orders are present, but localized. The Ilustrados represent the Silver Ladder, while the Maharlika share the warrior ethos of the Adamantine Arrow. Then you've got the Moriones (Guardians of the Veil), the Tanghalang Hiwaga (Mysterium), and the Sangguniang Malaya (Free Council). Historically, the first four were native Nameless Orders (roughly equivalent to the Maginoo, Maharlika, Timawa, and Alipin castes) before they disappeared during the Spanish conquest, and then resurfaced as a collection of Catholic devotional cults, masonic brotherhoods, and Enlightenment thinkers.

                      As for Nameless, one of the PCs is a Nameless Obrimos who descends from a line of Ifugao tribal priests. I'm also in the early phase of developing 2 distinct Nameless Orders as future NPCs, one based in Binondo, the other based in the Muslim quarter of Quiapo. The world-building has been thorough, and I don't want to overwhelm. If you need specifics, I'd be happy to share more.

                              Perhaps Changelings with ties to Filipino-Chinese enclaves (Binondo, Banawe, San Juan) adhere to Directional Courts? Elsewhere, alternative courts might include:

                              Myths
                              Maria Makiling could be a Court or it could be a title held ala Tokyo's Moon Princess. Imagine a Freehold of Los Baños based around Mt. Makiling, UPLB, the Makiling School for the Arts, and IRRI, each loosely represented by a court.

                              Festivals
                              Filipinos mark the calendar around certain holidays, creating cultural milieu over time. What might the Court of Passion (Kwaresma), or the Court of Jubilation (Pasko) look like? One can even dig into things like La Naval, Edsa anniversary, and the UP Lantern Parade.

                              Materials
                              The Court of Ivory Fingers harks back to old arts of carving Catholic imagery in expensive ivory, feeding on the collective piety and religious pageantry of Filipinos. What might the Courts of Acacia, Capiz, and Brass look like?

                              For Mage, I appreciate the suggestion regarding Verges and Hallows. That could tie-in with rune-walking. Perhaps the rapid pace of construction in the Metropolis is disrupting the old 'routes'? I can already see a land developer blocking roads or seizing old property serve as the front for the Seers.

                                      We haven't touched anything related to the Pasig yet, but I imagine the spirit of Pasig River as an ancient crocodilian. Perhaps decades of pollution has shrunk the spirit or shriveled it to scales and bones, a scavenger of sorts.

                                      Here's a bare bones writeup I did for a future Changeling 2e set in Manila:

                                      Overview
                                      The Republic of Perlandia is all that stands in the way between Manila's Lost and the incursions of the Magino-o. Founded in the ashes of the Second World War, it's aim was to represent a united front against Arcadia while providing a safe refuge for Engkantos.

                                      Perlandia is also the home of Manila's Monsoon Courts: the aspiring Amihan and the fatalistic Habagat. Each reigns for their season, tied to the various holidays and festivals of the metropolis, before passing authority to the other - a notion which utterly confounds the despotic Gentry. This alliance however hasn't always been so clean-cut; rivalry is commonplace as both parties seek to curry popular support with the Freehold's masses.

                                      The Amihan, Court of the Northeastern Wind
                                      Passionate and festive, the Amihan hold sway during Manila's 'dry' season beginning with the last months of the year up until the blazing heights of summer. Marked by fair weather and little rain, it is a season of rituals and revolutions when processions, festivals, and vigils rally together communities.

                                      Many in the sun court share a deterministic view of life, believing that great things can only be accomplished through acts of courage and sacrifice. Steeled by their durance and fueled by dreams of a better future, their leaders view themselves as the example other Engkantos should follow. Thus, both the political martyr and the charismatic sun king are twin aspects of the Amihan's peculiar attempt at servant-leadership.

                                      Through strength in arms and honey-tongued eloquence, Amihan courtiers mean to foment the masses; to inspire every Lost to battle the forces of Arcadia head on, and in the daylight.

                                      Popular Festivals: Christmas, New Year, Chinese New Year, EDSA Revolution, Holy Week, Labor Day

                                      Common attire: Bright, festive patterns, sun medallions, sunglassess, pins with feathers, sun tattoos

                                      Habagat, The Court of the Southwestern Wind
                                      As the sweltering heat ripens to tropical humidity, the mysterious Habagat take over the reigns of Perlandia. Their ascension is accompanied by heavy rains and the country's notorious 'typhoon season'. Floods rise to reclaim parts of the swollen metropolis, while the public looks to omens and signs; dread, anticipation, and inner resiliency.

                                      Fatalism is deeply rooted in the Storm Court's philosophy. What good can one ultimately do against the brutal elements or the fickle cruelty of the Magino-o? Not that the Habagat will stand helpless. As leaders they are hard-nosed pragmatists who make tough decisions based on realpolitik. Some find Storm courtiers too jaded but the Habagat know better than to openly tempt the Fae with brash action.

                                      Many Habagat Engkantos find a use as spies, saboteurs, and scholars - anything to pry secrets from Arcadia. For Perlandia to weather the coming storms, its citizens must be willing to commit anything and everything.

                                      Popular Occasions: Beginning of classes, Philippine Independence Day, National Heroes Day, All Saints and Souls Days, La Naval

                                      Common attire: Dark or subdued colors, deep hues, Shield medallions, umbrellas, scarves or shawls, raincoats and hoodies
