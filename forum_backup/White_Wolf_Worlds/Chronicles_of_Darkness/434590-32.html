                                Lets get a little motion in here again.

                                Today's update is another Channel. Piercing the Veil of Slumber. This one was hard to write, most of the mental influence possibilities are already covered by Call of the Depth and Besieging the Tower of Will. Especially with Call of the Depth's ability to inflict Conditions.

                                I ended up taking it in a different direction. Astral entities. It favours dreams, but it could work quite nicely on a Temenos entity provided it was already on Earth. It's in a bit of an awkward spot in that as written it's mostly useful if you combine it with Changeling for rules for Dreams and dream entities along with Mage for astral entities. Maybe I'll introduce something to the Leviathan corebook, but as a fan-game I think I can get away with a little more dependence on other books than normal.

                                The fate manipulation stuff will go into it's own Channel.


                                So without further adu
                                Piercing the Veil of Slumber (Descendant Channel)

                                The Tribe transcend the limits of the flesh. They walk the hidden corridors of the mind, inspiring, maddening, as they please.
                                The innately psychic minds of the Tribe blur the distinction between thought and flesh. Through their divine will dreams may walk the Earth, and mortals may find themselves lost between reality and dreams.
                                  • Dice Pool: Presence + Occult + Sheol
                                  • Action Type: Instant
                                • "Depth 0": Birthright of Sanctity
                                • "Depth 1": The Leviathan may observe the dreams of others while they sleep. Spend a point of Ichor and for the remainder of the scene the Leviathan may see into the dreams of any sleeping character they can perceive.
                                • "Depth 2": As above, and the Leviathan may manipulate the target’s Dream using this Channel’s Dicepool as though they had Influence 4 (Dreams), Leviathans pay no cost to manipulate a Dream beyond the point needed to open their eyes to dreams.
                                • "Depth 3": The Leviathan gains an additional ability. They may open the gates creatures of dreams and astral planes into the material. Spend two Ichor and roll to express this Channel. A Success places the Open Condition on something or someone for an Astral entity or class of Astral entities of the Leviathan’s choosing.
                                • "Depth 4": The Leviathan may target an astral entity and roll this Channel - Rank to grant it the Materialize Condition for a scene if it did not possess it already. If the Leviathan is looking into a dream and targets an entity within, it also gains the ability to walk between it’s host’s dreams and the material world. Inanimate objects can be affected, and simply appear next to an Open target of the Leviathan’s choosing.

                                The Leviathan also gains the ability to grant anyone they wish the ability to see astral entities in Twilight by expressing this Channel vs Resolve + Supernatural Advantage. However unless they’re another Leviathan they take the Madness Condition as they lose the ability to distinguish between dream and reality.
                                • "Depth 5": The Leviathan may spend two Ichor to control any astral entity. Roll this Channel vs Resistance - Rank. If the Leviathan wins the entity falls under it’s control for the remainder of the scene. Otherwise the entity is immune for the remainder of the scene.
                                • "Depth 6": The Leviathan may throw open the doors between flesh and dream. Everything and every Wake Vulnerable person within the Leviathan’s Wake gains the Open condition for all Astral entities and counts as being made of dreamstuff for the purposes of being targeted by Strengthen, Manipulate and Control Influences. If a non-Beloved mortal falls asleep within the perimeter of the Wake then every Astral entity within their minds, be it Goetica or an Incubi gains both the ability to travel between their host and the material world and the Materialize Manifestation.

                                Adaptations
                                • Visionary Dreams (OO): In Depth 3 the Leviathan takes no penalty to perceiving world around her while she sleeps. If she has Eye of the Watchful Deity she may use that channel as she dreams.
                                • Recurring Nightmares (OOO): In Depth 4 the Leviathan can spend two Ichor to implant a fragment of themselves with a sleeping person. This fragment remains within the victim's dreams and will use its Influence every night without fail until it completes the task the Leviathan sets for it. This usually creates a madman or a Beloved, albet a Beloved who fulfils their need to interact with the Leviathan in their dreams (it makes a good way to appoint high priests who’ll oppose attempts to summon you). People containing a fragment of a Leviathan have an unusually high chance of developing psychic abilities, usually ones with severe drawbacks.
                                • Prophet of Mad Slumber (OOO, Requires Eye of the Watchful Deity): In Depth 3 the Leviathan may spend 2 Ichor and nominate any character, then roll Presence + Occult + Sheol penalised by Knowledge. On a success they may see into or manipulate the target’s Dreams. Physical distance has no meaning in dreams and if the Leviathan is capable of Besieging the Tower of Will he may use that Channel on the target regardless of range.
                                • Dreams of a Future that Will Be (OOOOO, Requires Sheol 6, Besieging the Tower of Will) - In Depth 6 the Leviathan may spend four Ichor to create a contagious dream. Define the purpose of this dream, phrased as though it were an Ambition then roll for this Channel as normal. If successful for the next 24 hours everyone Wake vulnerable person who is not Beloved, sleeps within the Leviathans Wake, and has a Dream with a similar subject matter to the contagion must roll Resolve + Composure - the Leviathan’s successes. If they fail they gain a new Ambition of the Leviathans desire, along with an ability to identify who else is affected. However their minds buckle under the long term effects. Mechanically and psychologically they can be treated as the Leviathan’s Beloved and unify as a Cult, however they have no direct obsession with the Leviathan and “worship” their new cause instead. The Leviathan can leave them alone, and while they will get into all sorts of trouble they probably won’t leave the Leviathan’s name and image around for investigators to find. Probably.

                                    I just remembered something I meant to bring up here. I am still trying to work on that crossover comments/opinions/notes thing, but time and mental stability are hard for me to find at the moment.

                                    Anyway, as we all know, Lovecraft's work makes a big thing about "When the Stars are Right". But if you look at a lot of his works, they actually describe a cycle; the stars rise, then they fall, then they rise again, only to implictly fall there-after. Beyond the great apocalypses, like how the Elder Things once dominated Earth but eventually fell by the wayside and were replaced by man, the very nature of the seals of the Great Old Ones is described in a cyclical fashion; Cthulhu and R'lyeh once reigned, but then the stars fell out of alignment and they were sealed away. When the stars return to their alignment, they will be free again - but who says that they'll stay that way?

                                    What I'm getting at is this: is it breaking the theme of the setting to apply a similar motif? The Tribe is currently in its nadir, its stars are no longer in alignment. But, perhaps the stars will rise again, restoring the Tribe to prominence and power... but only for a time; eventually, inevitably, the stars will set and the Tribe's fortunes will falter once more. And so it will go, forever and ever.

                                    This, to me, seems to be taking the description of the game's mood as described in the GDoc and writing it large; that implicit cycle of attempt, success and failure writ into the cosmos.

                                    But... I don't know. I like this idea, since it feels like it further reinforces both the horror (the Black Dawn was doomed to end, as will the next, and the next) and the hope (eventually, the Black Dawn will come again, and maybe this time we'll get it right) that this game is tied into. But I'm worried I'm misreading it, and ultimately doing it a disservice.

                                        Unlike Lovecraft's creations, Leviathans are metaphors for fundamental human lizard brain instincts. That's important to this question. If you say that Leviathan's setting is cyclical you're metaphorically saying that real world history is a cycle of civilisation/reason rises -> falls -> rises -> falls.

                                        Now that might be true, it's too early to tell. We know looking at history that, to a certain extent, reason can and has triumphed over those lizard brain instincts. War is down, tolerance is up, yay us, lets hope for even more of that tomorrow. But will those lizard brain instincts eventually triumph, destroying civilisation? Followed by another rise, another fall, repeating itself until the sun burns out. I don't think so, but we can't know for sure because it hasn't happened yet, we're still in the first "rises".

                                        So if you think that history will be a cycle of civilisation rising and falling. Or you don't think that but you want to say it's true for the sake of telling a good story then by all means make the Leviathan setting into a great cycle


                                        Now what would conflict with the theme and mood of Leviathan: the Tempest is if the Tribe knew there was a great cycle. If Leviathans knew that the Tribe's star would inevitably ascend again they'd swim around looking for a nice secluded spot to go into hibernation until the stars were right; rather than struggling to adapt to the modern world but only achieving stagnation. Cthulhu just slept through the cycles where the Stars Were Wrong but unless you really want to roleplay being asleep for years the Tribe can't copy him.

                                        An individual Leviathan might try the hibernation plan but the closest the Tribe should get is believing that the great cycle garentees they'll once again triumph but they'll have to actually do the work first. So they can't just wait for the stars tor reach the right spots in the sky. Of course, the Tribe is absolutely full of promised messiahs and prophecies destined to save the Tribe; most of them are lies and the Tribe know most of them are lies. One might be true, but how would you tell which one?

                                                Alright, I promised I'd do some thoughts on crossing over with Leviathan, and I finally managed to get my thoughts clear enough to do something. As was mentioned...somewhere around here, maybe on the blog, crossovers do often result in a weakening of the "core theme", or the need to adapt a new one. For these crossovers, I chose to go with the idea of "support it" rather than trying to force it to not happen. So, yeah, this'll be done with an eye towards the positive, as much as I can explain it.

                                                Firstly, though, I want to advocate that, if it doesn't already, being a Beloved should grant immunity to "passive" mind-rape effects like Lunacy or Sybaris, and significant resistance to active mind-fraggery like the Mind Arcana, Dominate, etc. This reinforces the sheer Insane Faith (with equal emphasis on both "insanity" and "faith") that being a Beloved induces. Your mind has been broken and remade by the presence of God Most Unholy Herself; lesser auras of mental manipulation pale in comparison to that.

                                                Besides, it's embarrassing to think your drooling near-zombie cultists could turn against you just because some rookie magician waved her hands and chanted some mumbo-jumbo.

                                                Secondly, if we do end up doing a "crossing over guidebook" expansion, there should be some thought put into a School that devotes itself to studying the other monsters with which leviathans share the world of darkness, trying to figure out how and why these horrors have managed to successfully grow and change to integrate themselves into the modern era, and how the Tribe might potentially do the same. I mean, Promethean had the Argentum (? Path of Silver) in Magnum Opus for a reason, in that studying other monsters gives prometheans more of a grasp on what humanity is by virtue of what it isn't, and if Leviathan's Schools have similar nature to the Refinements - which is, honestly, how they come across to me - then it makes sense that they'd also consider their fellow denizens of the darkness to be worthy of studying for lessons in what (not) to do to restore the Tribe.

                                                Anyway, on to the splats... I'm making the assumption that the Wake doesn't affect other supernaturals, simply because such effects are generally "mortals only" - Disquiet is the only exception, was a case of "specific trumps general", and was generally received poorly to the point we've been told that's getting reworked in 2e. Besides, I don't recall seeing rules for the Wake mind-whammying vampires and the like in the gdocs.


                                                Vampire:
                                                Lets get the obvious out of the way first; leviathans can potentially scare the hell out of vampires. Between the fanaticism of their cult, the ease with which the Wake insinuates itself into the collective consciousness, and the sheer chaos they can cause even by accident, leviathans are tough enemies and major-league boat rockers. However... vampires are also nothing if not opportunistic, and the two races do have quite a lot of use for each other.

                                                Vampires are adept at playing the social game, deftly negotiating the landscape that so alienates and confounds leviathans. Furthermore, as supernatural beings, vampires are shielded from the mind-melting auras of the Tribe - never understimate just how powerful that sort of clarity can be, or how welcome it can be; many a leviathan tires of nothing but mindless sycophants, and could be played on out of this desire for someone to simply talk to even by someone less adept at head-games than a vampire.

                                                And what do leviathans have to offer vampires? Just for starters, the obvious one-two punch is that A: a leviathan's cult is an effortless supply of meals for a hungry vampire to score in trade, and B: if properly motivated, a leviathan makes one hell of a secret weapon for a vampire looking to get ahead in the world. More specifically, the Circle of the Crone and the Ordo Dracul both have reasons to investigate and find common ground with vampires. The Circle from their interest in blood magic and belief in older gods, the Ordo from the fascinating theo-biological possibilities encoded in the Leviathan's nature.

                                                Leviathans, meanwhile, can find useful allies, or just someone to talk to, in vampires. It's a dysfunctional relationship, but honestly, the Tribe has to take what it can get. There's a strange respectability in a life that refuses to quit even when all of the laws of nature say it should.

                                                I recall it was mentioned that Ichor can have strange effects on vampires. I'm all for it, but I argue that it should be a double-edged sword; yes, the side-effects can be nasty, but there's equally worthy reasons to drink it. Play up the temptation, and you further strengthen the Tribe as avatars of sin in all its darkly enticing glory.


                                                Werewolf:
                                                Past all of the initial, instinctive posturing and threat displays, if given the chance to meet each other on relatively equal terms, the Tribe and the Pack can find a strange feeling of kinship with each other. Both are races whom the mortal world has left by, remnants of the bloody age of tooth and claw before civilization. Both are species who are neither flesh nor ephemera but some otherworldly hybrid between the two. Both claim descent from mighty god-monsters who once subjugated humanity, and to have ruled a primordial paradise that was lost and replaced with this all too dissatisfying world.

                                                It's a relationship fraught with conflict, aggression and arguments... but, at the core of it all, there's an understanding between the two races. Cousins in spirit, if not in flesh.

                                                A leviathan's cult can easily become shelter and support for a werewolf pack. The Tribe have little complaint about offering their Beloved as breeding stock for werewolves who have proven themselves as allies; more of these strange, land-running, furry kindred can only be a good thing. A leviathan itself can become a monster that can help terrorize and devour those creatures that oppose the pack.

                                                Even if they do not directly ally with each other, werewolves and leviathans give each other a respectful berth. It's much like the relationship between a pack of wolves and an alpha tiger. Each subtly respects the other's potential as a killer and tries to not provoke a fight that would be bloody, potentially life-threatening, and ultimately useless.

                                                Of course, it's not all wine and roses. By their very nature, leviathans are spiritual vortices, generating spirits appropriate to their nature and cult - sin spirits, blood spirits, life spirits, death spirits, murder spirits, zealotry spirits, loyalty spirits... great choruses of the more primal and often dangerous spirits tend to flock to the leviathan, suckling from its Wake and its Beloved and feeding on the bounty of Essence. Indeed, as crazy as leviathan cults can get, who's honestly going to notice one or more Spirit-Ridden hanging around?

                                                That said, whilst an obvious source of problems, it can also be a source of help, if a pack thinks outside of the box. If human meddling is causing problems with the pack's efforts to manage particularly problematic spirits, well, isn't using the Wake to force people stop acting in a different way ultimately better than just murdering hundreds of people? Properly aimed, a leviathan can be used as the mother of all hammers to tend to the spiritual landscape of an area. Blunt as hell, sure. Effective? Unquestionably so.

                                                Naturally, the easiest "alliances" tend to be between the Pure - especially Predator Kings, who see leviathans as a way to force the restoration of Pangaea - or, more dangerously, Bale Hounds. But, with the right mindset, even the Forsaken can make peace with the Tribe, if not become friends.

                                                Of course, if you need to add a little more grimdark back in, there's always the jealousy angle; though both species hail from similarly savage origins, werewolves have (arguably) adapted better to the new world than the Tribe has.


                                                Mage:
                                                It could be said that interactions between the Awakened and the Tribe if the wizards could just remember the old adage: please do not harass the antediluvian abomination!

                                                Make no mistake, the Tribe absolutely fascinates many mages. The thing is, mages already make leviathans leery just by virtue of the stories of Marduk; any idealist wizard even looks like he's getting ideas of trying a bit of monster hunting is going to have one very angry monster try a bit of preemptive striking. Of all the groups, they have perhaps the hardest time getting past that initial hostile impression.

                                                But if they do, well, it's a rather dysfunctional relationship, but both sides have a lot to offer each other, just like leviathans and vampires. There's so very much for mages to obsess over and study when it comes to leviathans, and leviathans... well, mostly what they get out of it is some decent human conversation. The big thing one has to remember is just how hard it is for a leviathan to talk to someone, person to person. A completely normal person who can see past the scales and tentacles and just talk to them? That's an element of normality that many leviathans are happy to trade drops of blood or demonstrations of their power for.


                                                Promethean:
                                                Of all the splats, I see these two having the most in common, and tending towards the least-hostile first impressions. Both races are aberrations, things that should not be. Both bear the strain of an unearthly power within that stains the world around them, destroying men's minds and shattering the landscape. Prometheans seek the New Dawn, the transformation from monster to human. Leviathans hope for the Black Dawn, the fall of Man so that Monster may resume its place as the ruler of all things.

                                                If the Wake stifles Disquiet, at least amongst the Beloved, then I can see a leviathan's cult becoming the closest thing to a home that a promethean may ever know. There's so much potential for a promethean to learn there, about what is - and isn't - human, all while resisting the well-meaning blandishments of the leviathan that, however unwittingly, coaxes the promethean to embrace the path of the Centimanus.

                                                To say nothing of the scenario of the Marduk Society being confronted by a weeping flesh golem screaming in heartbreak about how they destroyed her entire family for being "different".


                                                Changeling:
                                                At first glance, the Lost and the Tribe seem to go together like oil and water, but look beyond the surface. Sometimes, the best place to hide is in plain sight; when you're surrounded by crazies, who's going to notice you? Plus, that's a lot of hot-running emotions to feed on for glamour.

                                                Really, this depends a lot on what 2e is going to do with Clarity (which, honestly, I always thought was one of the worst karma meters of 1e) - if changelings are still one stiff breeze away from total madness, then they're never going to mix with the Tribe, and that's a fact.


                                                Sin-Eater:
                                                Generally speaking, these two races won't interact with each other much. Sin-Eaters mostly concern themselves with the dead, and leviathans with the living. Unless leviathans develop powers that let them interfere with the dead, they're not of much concern to each other. Maybe it's kind of like sin-eaters and vampires, where the biggest reason that the Tribe tend to cross sin-eater radars is their tendency to leave a lot of mad ghosts in their wake. That said, the two "cross-pollinating" each other's respective mythologies could be interesting - imagine sin-eaters who were originally Beloved and so still fixate upon their former master to form the centerpiece of their homebrewed religion.


                                                Mummy:
                                                The fact that the Arisen and the Tribe hail back to similarly ancient times and have a mutually cynical/disapproving attitude towards society's changes provides some common ground. Storyhooks, like what happens when Beloved mistake a Mummy's Relic for an artefact of their master, or when the cult of one kind becomes intermeshed with/subsumed by the cult of another, are obvious. The big thing here is establishing "who's older"; did the Tribe arise sometime after the fall of Irem? Were their waning years before the City of Pillars arose? Or were they equals, and Mummies with sharper memories can dimly recall leviathan war-priests leading armies of gibborim and lahmasu against Iremite slave-legions?


                                                Demon:
                                                I made a post on this matter back when Marduk was under fire as too powerful & white-hatted for the setting, but I'll just summarize; though Integrators would be an exception, a match between a Demon and Leviathan is a match made in heaven-hell just waiting to happen. Each has something the other wants, and can offer highly inviting reasons to form an alliance. A demon/leviathan crossover would definitely be one to play up the "


                                                Beast:
                                                I... honestly, I haven't been able to really sit down and get into Beast at all. I'll have to beg off commenting here.


                                                Hunter:
                                                This splat's never really interested me that much, mostly because it's kind of set to antagonistic by default against supernaturals, especially the more overt races. I don't really have any ideas here.
