                Though, can I say? No Always Chaotic Evil among species native to the mortal coil. There can be, and should be, tyrannical empires, but any sentient race has virtues and faults.

                Part of this is boredom and frustration with "orcs" as a concept, but mostly because we have innately hostile supernatural beings already, an alien should come off as a true stranger - which is to say, not hostile, but thinking in terms you aren't familiar with and interaction is fraught because you don't know if you're going to make them hostile when you didn't need it to be.

                Which isn't to say that there can't be world-devouring swarms of monsters, but I'd make them explicitly mindless. There's a thread on RPGnet that has a thematic idea for this, the Apoc drone fleet; they are actually an autonomous self-constructing exploration probe designed for gifting first contact to life as the epitaph of their long-gone creators, but sadly their creators evolved on a gas giant and literally did not conceive of rocky worlds bearing life; thus, when the Apoc come to a rocky world, they start strip-mining the "asteroid" to build more Apoc or terraforming it into a gas giant for life to flourish in, and if attacked they assume they've encountered hostile hyperspace life and engage defensive protocols.

                In other words, the Apoc is a berserk, heavily armed gravestone. Very fitting for a universe where warp travel involves the Underworld.

                    You could have something that is alive and intelligent but nearly entirely hostile.

                    Imagine a hive mind organism that evolved from an ecosystem spanning colony of creatures; a continent spanning supercolony of perhaps ant-like animals. The colony itself persisted and the species evolved increasingly complex interactions over millions of years of their one colony dominating the world. Eventually evolving specialized individuals which store information allowing it to learn. Eventually this colony mind achieved sapience, though it took hours or days to think a thought as all of the little alien ants that form its body and mind move around. This colony mind was a huge advantage to the species further cementing their domination of their continent.

                    Over another million years it evolved and consolidated, instead of operating as just the results of the interactions of ants, the memory ants evolved into huge immobile masses of brain at the hearts of nests. Living cables ants evolved to connect the nests for faster networking. Eventually it developed technology, which eventually lead to it integrating computers and radio into its colony, speeding up its thought processes greatly as brains hundreds of miles away can be consulted instantly instead of taking several minutes for the signal to move along the cable ants. It eventually colonized the rest of its world's continents. Finally, it developed space travel and encountered something it had only speculated might exist, and was ill-prepared to understand: other intelligent beings.

                    The basic idea is a massive hive mind that doesn't have proper Theory of Mind and is terrified of other intelligence because it lacks any natural ability to guess what a sapient being might do. Its lived millions of years, though it has forgotten most of that as it didn't have very good memory until recently (that's what caused the explosion of tech development, an evolutionary breakthrough in long term memory).

                    It doesn't have empathy or sympathy or any natural ability to relate to another thinking creature at all. Language is a foreign concept to it. War is a foreign concept to it. Trade is a foreign concept to it. Kindness, hatred, compassion, sharing, friendship, and rivalry are all foreign concepts to it. It barely grasps the concept of individually sapient creatures, a concept that shocked and disgusted it when it first discovered them.

                    EDIT: An idea for something that might be playable:

                    Imagine a species of solitary predators that achieved sapience in an arms race with a prey species that was also achieving sapience. As minimally social creatures they never developed civilization while the prey species did and so they mostly ceased to prey upon them as their fangs, claws, and stone tools aren't really a match for even an iron age civilization. Eventually the civilization realizes that those creatures that occasionally eat them are actually intelligent and attempt to integrate and use them in their societies them (probably as slaves at first based if our iron age civilizations are anything to go on).

                    Eventually the civilization advances and reaches space and brings their tamed predators with them. The galactic stage now has an entire species of antisocial loners that have none of the things that social species have, no empathy, sympathy, compassion, kindness, morality, or anything like that. They would never have formed a civilization on their own but instead got brought to space by a species they evolved to eat. They understand the rules of society and don't just kill people for the sake of killing or whatever, but usually end up as assassins or mercenaries or just lone freighter pilots or late night clerks because they are naturally territorial and don't bond or form groups with others easily.

                            found the old Mars pitch

                            The first Mars colonies were established in the 2040s, and others soon followed. Mining operations on asteroids and Mars became big business and the corporate concerns that oversaw them bloated under their weight. Earth started to run out of clean water and food about ten years later. Decades of political squabbling over the truth of any potential problems left a world devoid of solutions to those problems, and riots began. The same story of ages past played out before – war burst out, colonies stopped imports and exports, and people stranded in the reaches of space began to die.

                            The Martians didn't.

                            They buckled down and weathered the vanishing support with fortitude and perseverance born out of their frontier ancestors and they lived – thrived, you might even say – in the cold, red wastes. Gangs became tribes, and tribes warred among each other over dwindling resources, renewable energy materiel, and found ways to integrate ever stranger things into their bodies, to better stave off the terrible climate. A century passed, and signals from Earth came again. The companies that put the ancestors on the red dust came back and claimed ownership over the Martians and their tools, and the Martian propensity for war turned up.

                            That didn't work out too well; the invaders held the ultimate high ground, and even with all their cybernetics and emancipated robots and reborn machinery, the Martians did not win the day. The companies came down and reprogrammed what they could, fixed what they couldn't, extracted hefty fees from the rebels, and locked down the colonies. Now mines and work camps are little more than prisons, and they make them more like that every day, shipping in new meat from Earth to grind up in the mines, ignoring the tribes living squalid existences in the dust.

                            These corporate concerns sit in orbit in their glittering homes with terraforming implements and gases all sitting and waiting for the button to be pressed to turn Mars into a new Earth, but they don't because the bottom line says that would lose them more money than it would save. They set lights out and exercise periods and uniform requirements for employees on the ground and implement their decisions through ruthless robotic stand-ins. It's 2150 and it's same-shit, different-day.

                            You've either just shipped in or you're in the habs already; your next place of living is Nova-Mine Habitat Facility 457-Bravo-Hotel-Papa at the edge of Argyre Basin, or, as the residents know it, Hotel California.

                            Maybe you died. You suffocated in the thin air, you got pulped by a mining machine, you were the last survivor of a Martian raid or a casualty of war. Whatever happened, you got back up, you just had something else in your brain that tells you to kill, to fight back, to let the Martian dead find their rest and their exaltation in war.

                            Maybe you went somewhere you shouldn't have. You found yourself in a winding canyon during exploratory EVA, your ship crashed someplace it couldn't have, your adulthood ritual among the tribe went wrong, and you found yourself in some other Mars, someplace with canals and angry red weeds and Martian cabbage and you took water with strange things in the depths of the caverns or hiding among the sharp-edged ruins and you know that Mars is still out there, and it wants to be set free.

                                and found my Geist pitch

                                Things did not go well on your first spacewalk. A faulty stitch in the suit tore, and you were stranded on the far side of the hab module. You tried to make the walk back, but you lost consciousness within sight of the hatch. An eye the size of a planet asked that you return to life, bearing its message.

                                You were catapulted into space when the impossible happened – a debris collision. You burned to death in the empty void before you suffocated. Humans can live up to a minute without air, but exposed to space, your own body coupled with the light from a nearby star, with nowhere to diffuse heat, will cook you from the inside-out. As your brain bloated under the strain of the temperature rising in your blood, something red and older than the stars managed to find you.

                                You awoke on the deck of a ship, the dedication plaque old and stained, with a thousand or more names etched into the wall in chalk, blood, and knife blade. It has known many names – Caleuche, Flying Dutchman, the waku wairua – and has sailed every sea. The stars are just one more ocean, and it has seen a million dead worlds. For better or worse, you are its crew now. Set the dead to rest, and search for wonder among the stars, but be warned. It's not for the living – or the timid.

                                            I'm on a kick.

                                            I think what we really need, along with splat-based ships, is alternate hyperdrives.

                                            Most people probably use the Void and the Gates to it; it's relatively safe, as most ghosts at worse will stow away to get back to a living world to resolve their anchors, and the Underworld is already partly a sea that can be conceptualized and navigated as such. But sometimes, you want speed, or an unexpected route, or flexibility in where you enter/exit hyperspace.

                                            More importantly, you want it more than you want a safe trip. Which is where you get a drive that dives into the Shadow to negotiate with the void-spirits for use of perspective-based wormholes, or flickers into the Hedge to take a shortcut because your personal story demands it, or if you're really, really desperate, sinking into Duat to find places in an Arisen's memory and idle dreams.

                                            I'd be interested in that last one especially, because given nonlinear time and the fiends, it's the most like the venerable Warhammer Warp. There's even inhuman gods waiting there to throw obstacles in your path, or find vulnerable people to mark benbenet and bring their will to the cosmos.

                                                Originally posted by Taidragon View Post
                                                This is something I've semi-jokingly noted, but something I think would be neat to see is that if there are any actual aliens humans run into (rather than simply having different splats be the 'aliens' of sorts), they'd see humans as the unspeakable horror of the universe. Why? Because humanity is so changing so gosh dang much. They can be turned into blood drinking vampires, their children can be born with their "souls" being a mishmash of human and spirits of rage, they can ascend into beings that play with the very fabric of reality, they can become fae wielding the power of their greaters, and so much more stranger and scarier things. Even their dead don't stop changing into strange and mutually exclusive things. Further, while humans aren't the only things on earth that can do this on earth, they tend to be the ones who get changed the most while staying visually the same the most.

                                                Humans may be scared of what's in outer space, but considering what any one human being could become under any number of circumstances, humanity may be the real horror in the room.

                                                ​To this, and to Rose Bailey we can only say:

                                                The oldest and strongest emotion of mankind is fear, and the oldest and strongest kind of fear is fear of the unknown. (Lovecraft)

                                                There is much unknown in space....

                                                Personally it sounds fantastic to present a far-future space setting in which humans can be considered from the perspective of other sentient beings encountering them and fitting them into their own ecology and universe-view. It mirrors what we could imagine the reaction of the creatures of earth might have, to the expansion of humanity and its civilization.

                                                There is a reason the most common wild animal reaction to man is terror.


                                                --Khanwulf
