    I think I'm most proud of the Conspirator > Agent > Operative progression; it's an organic one that allows you to cram a whole lot of character into one Merit chain, and encourages you to elaborate on the character as much as possible.

    It's worth noting that with Lore, the effective Occult dots of a Cultist is determined by their Status/Initiation, with Lore kicking in at (5-Lore) Status/Initiation. So even the most knowledgeable Cult can have only the most casual initiate comment something along the lines of "Yes, salt does help repel Ephemeral Beings" or "Yes, the fae are harmed by Iron." It's the High Priests of a super knowledgeable Cult that have a really good grasp of the supernatural world, and they only make up 5% of the membership.

    Secondly, in defense of the Second Sight Merit chain, it's actually less Experience efficient than the currently existing Mystery Cult initiation and Professional Training. Mystery Cult Initiation gives you ten Merit dots for five, and Professional Training gives you six Experiences for five plus three major advantages not listed. Eight Merit dots for twelve is somewhere in between those two, and more importantly the Second Sight Merit chain comes with some serious kickbacks. Notably, a hit to your ability to handle Breaking Points, a Persistent Condition you cannot get rid of that is quite liable to inflict serious penalties at an inopportune time, and a cap to Integrity. I do agree that exactly how many members of the Cult have access to these abilities should be outlined, which is something I'm going to edit in, but I disagree that Patron is too much of a bonus. Unless a Cultist is also a Sleepwalker, ill-gotten Rotes are still going to damage their Integrity. Disciplines, which even freak out vampires at higher Humanity levels, should almost certainly do the same. Also worth noting that a Mummy's Cultists could possibly gain Affinities, not Utterances. Affinities being much lower key, and entirely plausible if the Cultist has some way of purchasing the Stolen Pillars Numen, and even then at best they'd have a Pillar 2 Soul Affinity. I imagine most mortals never get involved in the Guilds, and as a result never get the chance to manifest Guild Affinities (but I think they would, if they did).

    Lastly, the only reason Endowment is there is if you intend to model a Hunter Compact or Conspiracy with a Cult.

            Here's an answer for your question. And some basic guidelines to building Cults.

            *****

            How Many Empowered Cultists? - Excellent question, and one that wasn’t addressed above. In some Cults, minor supernatural gifts are going to be more common than in others, while for a few supernatural powers are reserved only for the elite. The gift given by Second Sight applies to all Cultists when Lore would kick in, so upon initiation at the highest levels, and at High Priesthood at the lowest. For a good yardstick based upon a Lore 5 Cult with Second Sight through to Thaumaturgy, see below.
            o - Benefits of the Second Sight Merit (3 dots)
            oo - +2 Supernatural Merit dots (5 dots)
            ooo - +2 Supernatural Merit dots (7 dots)
            oooo - +2 Supernatural Merit dots (9 dots)
            ooooo - +3 Supernatural Merit dots (12 dots)
            However, it is worth pointing out that Theurgy and Thaumaturgy simply widens the potential pool of Merits that Cultists can choose from, they don’t directly grant those Merits. A given Cultist still only has a number of Supernatural Merits as per Second Sight, which is to say, three dots.

            Building the Pyramid- Storytelling, Players Guide, and Crossover

            Cults can get a lot of mileage in the Chronicles of Darkness- they can stand in for just about any organization, and as you can see, come with a wide variety of options. So, how does one go about using a Cult in a story? What can it add to the game? Firstly, it fleshes out the setting. A Cult comes already packed with backstory, motivation, and plot hooks and it can help abstract the power of various factions at play in a given Chronicle. Conspirators, Agents, and Operatives are fleshed out characters in their own right, significant NPCs that act as the public face of the Cult and who make great antagonists or allies. If you’re feeling really ambitious, a PC could actually be a Conspirator, Agent, or Operative. Technically speaking, if you’re a member of one of the various social Y-Splats, your character sort of already is.

            It was mentioned above, but it bears repeating here, a single Cult, regardless of Reach or Grasp, isn’t going to be able to completely take over a country. They can take over a branch of government, rule a small town, or something along those lines, but it will almost never take down an entire nation or something like a Hunter Conspiracy. Why? Those institutions are more than just a single Cult, they’re nested Cults. Imagine if you would, trying to take over Canada. There are ten provinces and three territories, plus one federal government. That’s fourteen Advanced Cults working together to govern a nation, just on the bureaucratic side. It’s a lot more if you factor in other agencies, police forces, military branches, etc. I don’t care how big your single Cult is, it is never going to take that down alone. This aspect of such massive Cults can be termed The Pyramid. The most powerful Cult in the organization sits at the top, and it branches out into smaller and smaller groups below it.

            As an example, I’ll use the Deva Corporation, a far-reaching God-Machine Cult.

            Deva Corporation: God-Machine Manual Research/New Mason Architecture: Luminous Labs/Butterfly Cryptozoology, LTD/The Apocalypse Clock

            As another example, Last Dynasty International, a corporation hunting the Deathless

            Last Dynasty International: Last Dynasty Pharmaceuticals: Killwarden Memorial Conservation Fund/Integrated Transport Solutions, LLC

            Each of the above boxes represents a single, far-reaching Advanced Cult, with Conspirators, Agents, and Operatives fleshing out its membership, giving them more power in particular cities and particular sectors. Either way, in order to get through to the core Cult behind either of these continent spanning occult Enterprises you’ll need to Investigate, or manipulate, no less than three powerful Cults, after having Investigated or manipulated your way through to an actual Conspirator, Agent, or Operative in one of the base Cults. Considering those high-ranking Agents and Operatives are likely to have their own Agents and Operatives, climbing to the top of the Pyramid is no easy task, and confronting those in charge of one of those organizations is a task worthy of a Chronicle. Much like with Investigation, you’ll want to plan out how long the Chronicle to climb the Pyramid will be based on how many Clues you can gather. This style of play won’t necessarily be for everyone, since it means uncovering layer upon layer of conspiracy and cover-up, but for some players and Storytellers Climbing the Pyramid is something that will make an enjoyable Chronicle, the kind that Demon: The Descent and Mage: The Awakening were made to handle. It’s worth noting here, however, that a Mage Chronicle climbing the Pyramid is probably going to be much quicker than a Demon Chronicle climbing the Pyramid, if only because Mage Sight can make Investigation superfluous.

                More stuff, this time on making Conspirators, Agents, and Operatives characters. I'm getting a lot of mileage out of this, but I'm already foreseeing Werewolf being a real sticking point- figuring out how powerful an individual Pack is will be... interesting. Mummy will, naturally, be the most insane and fun, with Demon and Mage a close second. The way Changeling will interact with Cults will likely be very weird.

                *****

                Brick by Brick- Conspirators, Agents, and Operatives

                Whether you’re building a Cult, or fighting against one, the public face of the Cult is always its outside contacts. The power of a Cult might be fairly limited, but a well-placed Conspirator can improve it several times over. Conspirators are blackmailed outsiders, true believer converts, bribed officials, traitors, and embedded contacts- when purchasing a Conspirator, think about who they are, and how they got there, not only will this help flesh out the character, but it will help establish guidelines as to how the Cult operates. Do they threaten those they want in positions of power? Do they send loyal members into gravely dangerous situations? Do they seduce and convert? Do they bribe?

                A Conspirator isn’t, generally, an active character. They skulk in the shadows, they wield their limited influence, and they try and keep their heads down. If they refuse to talk to one of the PCs, are they frightened of retribution, wanting to keep their job, or does their master genuinely command their respect? Are they an irritating evangelist, constantly trying to persuade the party to join them? Do they believe in their mission, and consider the party a means to an end, or a threat? Do they see the PCs as a threat to their position?

                An Agent is much more proactive. An Agent is more than just an extension of the Cult, but a power in and of themselves. They still owe their allegiance and their authority to their masters, but they have built up a lot more pull. An Agent can act alone, boldly, with the power and influence of a small Cult themselves. In some cases, a successful Agent might be as powerful as their parent Cult, in which case the risk of them going rogue is constantly on their master’s mind, but the Cult needs the power and influence of the Agent, and probably has several more. An Agent makes a good reoccurring antagonist- putting a face to the Cult paired with the power to make them an active threat, instead of just a looming shadow in the background. When designing an Agent, give a little more thought to their personal motivations and goals, because you can bet that they have an agenda beyond what their Cult asks of them. Alternatively, they could be a company man through and through, a true believer, loyal to a fault to their parent organization. When you need a starter antagonist to introduce a wider threat, an Agent is your best bet.

                An Operative is a bit different; an Operative is an Agent who’s all grown up. If the Agent is the face of the man in the shadows, the operative is the man in the shadows. Think of the difference between any number of ‘mythology’ bad guys on X Files and Cancer Man. It’s a bad time when one of the grand conspiracy’s minions show up, but when Cancer Man arrives, everything hits the fan. The Operative is a Cult and a tour-de-force all on their own, with the resources and capabilities to back it up. If they’re still attached to their parent Cult, there needs to be a reason. Maybe they’re only formally associated, but the original organization maintains only distant contact, maybe the Operative needs the Cult to help them fulfill a personal goal, maybe maintaining ties with the Cult is a matter of convenience. Perhaps the Operative is genuinely loyal to the Cult. Either way, if you’re going with a bigger story, or establishing a Chronicle climbing the Pyramid, then it’s an Operative you want dogging the protagonist’s steps to let them know they’re in way over their heads.

                Agents & Operatives as Player Characters: Can you play an Agent or Operative? Sure, no reason not to. The only thing separating an Operative from being a fully-fledged character is the decision to make them one. Mechanically, an Agent or Operative is a relatively specific kind of play- you answer to a superior, you undertake missions from your organizations, but you also get a really rather nice Experience boost to begin the game. This really isn’t all that different than playing a regular character involved in Covenant/Order/Tribe/Court politics, except that as an Agent or Operative you have a few more mechanical strings and boons attached.

                Agents & Operatives as Supernatural Creatures: In non-mortal game, or a game where you are using Cult mechanics to flesh out different supernatural organizations, Conspirators, Agents, and Operatives are likely to be members of the appropriate kind of esohuman (thanks Mage Chronicler’s Guide for the awesome term). In that case, still follow the rules for Retainer when roughly determining their Skills, but take a look at the Ghoul Retainer rules in Blood & Smoke: The Strix Chronicles. Following that same yardstick would be beneficial. What that translates out to in terms of power (for games currently updated to 2E) is outlined here below:
                • Vampire: Conspirator (1 dot of Disciplines), Agent 1-2 (2 dots of Disciplines) Agent 3 (3 dots of Disciplines). Spend Merit dots from Operative on Disciplines, Devotions, Blood Potency, and Merits.
                • Werewolf: Conspirator (1 Gift), Agent 1-2 (2 Gifts), Agent 3 (3 Gifts). Spend Merit dots from Operative on Gifts, Facets, Renown, Primal Urge, and Merits.
                • Changeling: Conspirator (3 dots of Contracts), Agent 1-2 (6 dots of Contracts), Agent 3 (9 dots of Contracts). Spend Merit dots from Operative on Contracts, Wyrd, and Merits.
                • Demon: Conspirator (1 Embed/Exploit and a Merit), Agent 1-2 (3 Embeds/Exploits), Agent 3 (4 Embeds/Exploits and a Merit). Spend Merit dots from Operative on Embeds/Exploits, Primum, Merits, and Cover.

I'm trying to get a piece in on how every Y-Splat handles Cults, but depending on the gameline, X-Splat would be more useful going in depth over.                         *****

                        What We Do in the Shadows- Crossover

                        And now, a brief word on what each kind of esohuman generally does with Cults.

                        Vampire: While there could be dozens of reasons why a vampire would want a Cult, ultimately it all boils down to one thing in particular- blood. Almost the first thing young Kindred will use their Cult for is as a Herd. Vampires favor Reach over Grasp, preferring to use Conspirators and Agents to handle the needs of upholding the First Tradition if they can’t toe the line themselves. When designing a local Domain, think of how powerful the Prince is, and how they maintain their power. Do they delegate to capable Agents and Operatives, their courtiers? Do they wield raw influence? Do they lean on the Covenants? Do they mostly use servants outside the normal Kindred power structure, ghouling significant mortals? This will influence who the Prince is, and how they rule. One with less personal power is likely to be a perfect politician, dancing along a razor’s edge to keep the sharks at each other’s throats instead of theirs. One with a lot of personal power is liable to be a stereotypical tyrant Prince.

                        Because the Kindred rely on mortal agents to do their bidding during daylight hours and to reinforce the First Tradition, most of their Cults tend to be Elite Clubs or Cults of Personality. This is no different for the Covenants. Each Covenant is designed to function like a massive Cult, though local branches will almost certainly be smaller. Traits dedicated to the Covenant itself are technically tagged to “the Covenant” but practically they are the purview of the Covenant’s local leader. Merits that rely on Covenant Status and access Covenant resources are effectively using Reach or Grasp to get what they want. Operatives of the Covenants are likely to be other high-ranking Vampires in its power structure, while Agents and Conspirators are either lower down on the chain, mortals under their thumb, or Ghouls. Consider, then, what the character of the Covenant is based on how their Cult is built.

                        Blood Will Out: Where are the mortal members in all this? As mentioned above, Reach and Grasp translates to more than just influence, it’s membership and contacts made. Simply put, for the Kindred, mortals are usually set dressing- food, resources, somewhere in the background. It wouldn’t be strange for there to be some silent humans, pulses racing, at Kindred functions- after all, everyone needs to eat and it’s an uncharitable host who doesn’t provide refreshments. However, for Cults with heavy amounts of Lore, it is not unusual to have several Ghoul Agents, or for the majority of mortal cultists to be Ghouls themselves.

                        The Invictus tend to be very top-heavy Elite Clubs or Enterprises, with the central leader of the First Estate wielding personal influence to keep their subordinates down and wielding unlucky neonate Conspirators as tools to uphold the Traditions. But consider a city where the power at the top is shared, where the might of The Old Man leans heavily upon a few equally powerful counterparts- Operatives, all. In such situations, the halls of the Conspiracy of Silence are likely to be filled with a lot more politics and backstabbing than is normal, and the bourgeoisie, Versaille-esque rules fall by the wayside.

                        The Carthian Movement is usually a Gang or Unorganized, a faceless mass of the politically radical dead and their living counterparts. The Revolution is unusual in that it doesn’t use a lot of Reach, but instead favors Grasp. As an Unorganized Cult, that’s generally not a problem, they can reorganize as they see fit, but in a Domain where the Revolution is a bloody one, the Carthians could be in trouble if they ever get into power. The Revolution doesn’t mind empowering Agents or Operatives, it allows them to compensate for a general lack of access to the higher-ups in mortal and Kindred society, but should the face of the Movement ‘sell out’? Well, there’s always the next Revolution.

                        The Ordo Dracul is a very peculiar kind of Elite Club, and a doubly peculiar Covenant, because it is almost entirely uninterested in temporal power. Dragon politics generally revolve around not being elected to the position of Koaigon, but retaining enough power and seniority to continue their research and follow their personal goals. This is most likely because, unlike the other Covenants, the Ordo Dracul is a Mystery Cult, and certainly behaves as one. The base Cult, representing the power of the Koaigon, is probably rather small, with major Operatives being the leaders of the three branches of the Sworn at the local Chapterhouse.

                        The Circle of the Crone is hard to pin down, but generally favors Tribal, Cult of Personality, and Elite Club structures. It is virtually impossible to define the Acolytes, because more than any other Covenant they are highly unique and individualistic in their beliefs. The things that unite them are a Mystery Cult structure, polytheistic and/or animistic inclinations, belief in a Mother of Monsters/Crone figure, and Cruac. Pay extremely close attention to Step 1 of building the Crone Cult, as that will be crucial to determining how to divide up the Merit dots. Because of the highly individualistic nature of the Acolytes, Operatives and Agents might be senior members of the Cult with vastly different beliefs than the core group of Acolytes.

                        The Lancea et Sanctum can be as diverse in its structure as the Circle of the Crone, but are more uniform in terms of beliefs because unlike the Acolytes, they have holy books and an existing family of religions to piggy back off of. (Neopaganism and polytheistic reconstruction are generally too new and diverse to have long-established traditions) The Sanctified, more than any other Covenant except, maybe, the Invictus, deal with mortals. Interactions with mortals are an important part of the duties of the faith, and as a result, they are the second-most likely to possess mortal Conspirators and Agents behind the First Estate itself. This helps compensate for their generally small size as a Cult.

                        The Ones Who Can’t Stop: Ghouls have an odd position in the Covenants. They are valued servants who are, typically, denied access to true positions of power due to their fundamentally human nature. Ghouls have the advantage of being able to move in daylight, while still being capable of wielding Disciplines. While unable to use Cruac, nothing stops them from wielding Theban Sorcery. They can move in the worlds of both mortal and vampire, and that gives them some serious power in the Danse Macabre. It is also worth pointing out that they are, more often than not, Vitae addicts blood bonded to their Regnant, valuable tools in the Danse for their master. For all the independent power of a Ghoul, that power still depends on the Kindred.

                                *****

                                Demon: The Unchained live in the dark place where government agencies, corporations, and religions turn into Cults with sinister agendas. They used to serve the biggest manipulator of them all, and now they wield Its own strategies against it. The Unchained are willing to pick up any tools at their disposal, but for obvious reasons lean towards Conspiracies. Demon Cults are miniscule compared to those of other esohumans, primarily to preserve Cover and slip beneath the notice of the God-Machine. A cunning outcast relies upon Conspirators and Leverage, routing them through different Covers. An absolutely crazy Demon might make their Cover a Conspirator/Agent/Operative. The primary use of a Cult is resources, access, and security for one of the Unchained.

                                Agencies lean towards being a bit bigger than personal Cults, but are no less reliant upon their Conspirators, Agents, Operatives, or Leverage. The difference being their Agents and Operatives tend to be fellow Unchained or Stigmatics instead of regular humans. Free Agencies keep hold of a lot of Leverage, Temporal Agencies tend to stockpile Lore, and Insurgent Agencies maintain Arsenals and train Militant Agents. Pay special attention to Step 1 when designing an Agency, but also remember the inherently paranoid nature of the Unchained- how big can a Demon Cult get, how much trust can they invest in someone, before it becomes a security risk? What problems plague the Agency? How much attention are they willing to attract?

                                The cults of Tempters are often bigger than what most Unchained would consider safe, but the hedonistic outcasts are terribly fond of their luxuries, and the Builders generally want the resources to construct their own Hell at the end of the Descent. Tempters favor Reach over Grasp, and will often go Legitimate, running Enterprises and Elite Clubs with shady Splinter Organizations often dedicated to supporting a Temporal Agency, supporting the primary Cult off the books, or investigating the possibility of an off-world colony in which to establish their Hell. The Builders are all about resources, contacts, and Leverage, and often possess massive webs of fairly low-importance Conspirators.

                                The Saboteurs are almost always running Conspiracies or Gangs, possibly both at once, and are generally a bit louder than most Unchained would like. The Insurgent Agencies they regularly turn into can never keep a lucky streak going forever. Saboteurs favor Grasp, Arsenals, and Militant cultists- they go out of their way to find Stigmatic malcontents who want to give a two-fingered salute to the Machine. Unfortunately, this usually gets them on the radar of law enforcement, which in turn sets the God-Machine on their trail. Leverage, Agents, and Operatives continue to be a Demon’s best friend for hiding in the shadows, though the Thugs tend to favor quality over quantity.

                                It is a remarkable feat even hearing about the Conspiracies of the Inquisitors. They’re paranoid, even for Demons, and double down on layers and layers of security. The Watcher at the center of this spider’s web is the head of a small Cult with a veritable army of Conspirators who have no idea who they work for. Occasionally, they’ll set the ball rolling on an Unorganized internet-based Cult encouraging conspiracy theorists and groups like Network Zero and sits back- vigilantly -to watch the data pour in. The Watcher hides themselves behind computer screens and chat rooms, maybe dedicating the resources to make an Agent or two, but rarely, if ever, meeting in person. Giving someone resources means keeping a closer eye on them, and the hit will be bigger when the knife goes into the Paranoid’s back.

                                No one really likes an Integrator, but they can understand them. The other outcasts might not agree, but no one can deny that an Idealist’s heartfelt faith and simple desire to return home is something they felt at one time or another… before they knew better. Generally speaking, an Integrator’s faith is honest, even if it puts other Demons in danger. More than any other Agenda, their Cults are likely to be Tribal as their faith in the loyalists and in the Machine verges on the religious. The Cults of Turncoats are generally rather visible, but that’s the idea- grab the attention of the Machine. Of course, most Integrators want to return on their own terms, so they aren’t likely to go public at first, but once they do it’s only a matter of time before the Cult is co-opted by God.

                                The God-Machine has no such inclination, It uses whatever tool is best suited for the job, and while small and unobtrusive is usually Its design, sometimes brute force levels of influence are more what the situation calls for. Cults are a means to fuel the activity of Angels and a way to maintain Infrastructure and act without the kind of Aetheric Resonance an Angel gives off. Exiles, on the other hand, tend to be reliant upon Cults as access to Essence- otherwise they would have to bargain with their loyal counterparts for the opportunity to use Infrastructure.

                                Those Who Can See: Stigmatics are the wild cards in this cold war between the Machine and its rebellious servants, recruited, groomed, created, and conscripted by each individual side for their power and knowledge. A Stigmatic’s identity isn’t at risk acting in an unusual manner, they can take risks the Unchained wouldn’t, and they don’t trigger Aetheric Resonance, making them the perfect infiltration assets for Angels. This puts a Stigmatic, any Stigmatic, under a lot of pressure to join one side or another, and the players in this game don’t have to play nicely. Stigmatics are indoctrinated, infested with programmable cryptoflora, embedded with enough wetware to make them a Lost Boy, conditioned with Numina and technology to become a Dreamer, or even worse fates. Stigmatics need to take decisive action, and they always need to do it fast. Remember rule number one.

                                        Now that sounds like something extremely interesting. I'd love to see you take a crack at writing it up, if only because I'd like to see someone try and work the system I'm developing. In any case, here's my Changeling stuff.

                                        *****

                                        Changeling: The Lost have an… interesting relationship with Cults. The attitude of most Cults often make them think of their Durance, of their servitude to the Gentry, and quite a few Lost would bristle at the comparison between the organizations they establish and the courts of Arcadia. But, the comparisons still stand. The Courts are the big ones, acting as support structures for their members with a heavy ritual aspect. There are excellent reasons for this, notably that it protects the city housing the Freehold, making it virtually impossible for the True Fae to act, and extremely tempting for any Huntsmen to follow the rules, but if people were to know the extent of their activities, the Courts would still look like Cults. The Entitlements even more so, being the more exclusive and even more ritualized groups that they are. The Lost are on the long road home, but they can also never go back. Faerie has etched its laws into their soul, and part of those laws is rite and ritual, and only like-minded individuals can understand.

                                        Consider the story of a Court, their Approaches, and how that influences their structure. Who are their Conspirators? What kind of Leverage do they hold? Does the Court have Home Turf in the form of a communal Hollow? Do they have an Agent in certain organizations to help the Hedge-fresh gain new identities and get back on their feet? Do they have access to weaponry? Do they bribe the local Goblin Market for first crack at their goods? The Courts and Entitlements of the Lost are still Cults by definition, but they are generally benevolent ones. This isn’t to say that they are perfectly law-abiding or unattached to criminal elements- Faerie knows no law save the Wyrd, and the Lost have to play by those rules. The darker side of humanity can be an excellent source of Glamour, and the desperate are always ready for a Bargain. The Cults of the Lost are support structures first and foremost, and that means access to Glamour, Tokens, and mortals they can Bargain with in order to hide from the Huntsmen.

                                        The Fairest are inclined towards Cults of Personality, sometimes without the intention of starting them. They simply produce a following where they go due to their inspiring nature. The natural inclination towards leadership, towards nobility, emerges in their Cults. They try and inspire others to be the best they can be. Some Fairest with Cults are often motivational speakers with a following, public figures trying to make the world a better place, but that doesn’t stop them from indulging in the darker side of Cults too. Sometimes, a Fairest just needs to feel like they’re in charge. The Fairest fiercely defend their followers, as they can’t stand for any of them to come to harm- it tears at their Clarity with iron talons and hurts their confidence as well.

                                        The Darklings had their loyalty betrayed and their ideals crushed, and the supposedly simple matters of “right” and “wrong” won’t hold them back anymore. Darkling Cults favor Grasp, which generally puts them in the wheelhouse of Conspiracies. It hurts a Darkling to trust someone else blindly, but they thrive off of making difficult choices for others- even if they don’t know it. An enemy of the Court vanishes, a televised criminal is found dead, some Hedge-fresh Changeling goes missing and the Darkling slaps a Token of Reprieve into a motley-mate’s hand with the implicit understanding ‘Don’t ask’

                                        An Ogre speaks with their fists, trying to hide the pain that drove them to try and become invulnerable to begin with. Ogre Cults lean towards Gangs, and that’s generally just how they like it. More so even than a Gang, the Cults of Ogres are violent, verging into something like a fight club. Of course, this can be channeled into something productive- like a martial arts group, or something along those lines, but more often than not it’s something a little more sinister. The Freehold with an Ogre Cult Leader can depend on them to bring highly effective head-smashing thugs to a fight when they need them to. However, the Cult of an Ogre is often reactionary, lashing out against perceived harm and any reminder of vulnerability.

                                        The Wizened are comfortable amongst Enterprises, and those are the Cults they build and lead. Propped up by supernatural skill and Contracts, a Wizened-run business is likely to become extremely successful, but probably won’t. The Wizened are perfectionists- anything less than their absolute best smashes a hole through their Clarity, and as such they will not tolerate sub-par workmanship. More likely than not, a Wizened-run Enterprise is likely to be a highly respected, but very small, artisanal shop. Of course, whatever it is the Wizened specializes in, the Freehold can expect the very best of it.

                                        Here’s what a Beast cares about: family, friends, the herd, the pack, the Freehold. Here’s what they don’t care about: the laws and traditions of human society. Beasts break cages, real or imagined; they test limits and hone their skills to perfection. A Beast isn’t unintelligent just because they’re animalistic- physicality can be intimately tied to cunning. The Cults of a Beast are Tribal, usually small and tight-knit, with recognized Home Turf. They’re rarely legitimate, if only for the fact that they could care less which rules they have to break in order to feel free. The Freehold can always depend on a Beast’s Cult to have a place to fall back to, a territory they know intimately where they can run circles around their enemies.

                                        The Cults of Elementals can be as sturdy as stone or as mercurial and changing as the winds- or perhaps they shift with the tides. These are Unorganized Cults, focused on an ideal, a platonic form, as embodied in the Elemental. They change when the Changeling says they change, when the circumstances necessitate that they change. The Elemental will justify to themselves, and to their Cultists, when they can. If things go well, then it was all part of a grander plan. The Freehold can depend on an Elemental’s Cult to be whatever they need it to be- a steady rock on which to fall, a mine full of resources, or a crashing storm against their foes. Even a modestly sized Elemental Cult can swing to extremes with ease.

                                        The Gentry can benefit from Cults as well, subtly influencing them through a Prop, speaking to them through their Wisps, seducing them away to Faerie or bullying them into gathering up soon-to-be slaves. Perhaps the Old God is even whispering to them from beyond the grave, through one of the Inamorata. Maybe the Stranger has ordered a Huntsman to command the Cult for them- an angry, cunning, vicious creature willing to do whatever it needs to in order to go back home. A group of privateers or Loyalists is just as much a Cult as a Goblin Market or Hob-tribe. This brings in an important factor of Changeling life- political concerns aren’t just on one side of the Hedge. If the Freehold needs to negotiate with a nearby Hedgetown or Hob-Kingdom, they’ll do what they need to do. More so than almost any other esohuman, a Changeling is part of two highly distinct worlds, and can disappear fully into one or the other, and that is dangerous regardless of whether they choose Faerie or Ironside.

                                        The Oath Keepers: The Fae-Touched are home, they can go back whenever they want to; family, friends, a job, a home, it’s all there for them. But most of them don’t want to go back, because the people at home won’t understand what they’ve seen, what they’ve been through. The Lost can understand, the Lost can help them get through it, but even then the Lost have been through so much more. The Lost don’t want them involved any more than they have to, regardless of how much they might want them to be, either because the Fae-Touched can’t really understand, because they don’t want them involved in the society the Lost have built for themselves. Whatever the reasons, Fae-Touched crave the company of Changelings, partially because they need someone who they can relate to, but also because they have seen much of the wonder, and not enough of the horror, of Faerie. The Fae-Touched are living on borrowed time, wanting to plunge headfirst into a world that could swallow them whole. They can be liabilities as much as they are assets.

                                                High praise, thank-you. And now, the bit that's probably the most intuitive to write, Mummies. Promethean will follow.

                                                *****

                                                Mummy: Cults are the lifeblood of the Deathless. Without them, only the turn of Sothis and certain Affinities can wake them from the death-state of henet. A Mummy’s primary means of mortal interaction will be with members of their Cult, and that makes designing that organization a vital part of any Mummy game. When designing a Cult, remember that for all the fact they serve the Deathless, some Cultists will spend entire lives without seeing their immortal god and master. Think of the Purpose the Mummy founded the Cult for; consider the Relic the Mummy uses the Cult to guard, and think about how the culture they’ve grown up in influences their Doctrine. They may, nominally, worship the Nameless Beasts and the Judges, but only the most fanatical and devout will adhere strictly to those beliefs in a culture that doesn’t worship them themselves.

                                                Something you might want to think about is how long ago the Mummy founded the Cult, and what’s happened since then. Even if you’ve established the Purpose, Relic, and Doctrine of the Cult with the Storyteller, consider giving the Storyteller the freedom to change and warp the Purpose and Doctrine as time has gone on. The Cult might worship the Mummy, but the Deathless god isn’t constantly around to maintain tradition. Between different Descents, the Mummy might even wake up to a Rogue Cult, a Civil War, or other forms of internal strife, and one faction or another is liable to have summoned them to handle concerns they couldn’t themselves. While a Cult will maintain itself in the absence of their master, without certain Affinities they won’t expand without the say-so of the Deathless god.

                                                A Mummy’s Cult is deeply personal, their Purpose and Doctrine influenced by the personal history of the Deathless, the traditions and symbols of the Guilds, the Nameless Beast that stands in for their Decree, and the Mummy’s own Judge. As much as a Scorpion Cult is an independent organization, it’s also an extension of the Mummy’s own character. Taking the time to flesh out Purpose and Doctrine will elaborate on the Cult’s backstory, and the kind of master the Mummy once was. In this way, the Cult functions a lot like a vampire’s Touchstone, reminding them of who they were, and who they are- hints towards their Memory. Of course, if it’s been a while since their last Descent, garbled traditions and doctored histories could lead the Mummy in the wrong direction, and they might not like what they find if it differs greatly from what they’ve been told.

                                                The Lion-Headed are impetuous, tempestuous, making the most of their short time on earth by indulging in all-too human passion. They make enemies and friends with incredible ease, swift to be offended and impressed, with a reputation for rash decisions and hopeless causes. They are animated, charismatic, and passionate people, with bright eyes and emphatic gestures. This sheer force of personality swiftly endears them to mortals, who fascinate them to the point of encouraging the Hands of Sekhmet to delay (or even abandon) their summoned Purpose. Their Cults are often Cults of Personality, rising to exceptional heights when their Deathless master wakes, and collapsing to a shell of their former might with their return to Duat. Such cults are built on personal loyalty, passed down through generations, in sects that might be a small family following (something like a 1-dot Cult) or a religion of hundreds (an Advanced Cult).

                                                The Falcon-Headed want tough, competent, self-sufficient Cultists. They favor Reach and Grasp equally, one representing aspiration, the other competence. They invest heavily in Agents and Operatives, rewarding their most loyal and accomplished servants with power and increased responsibility. Their inclination towards conquering trials often pushes the Charioteers to make their organizations particularly brutal Mystery Cults, with initiation involving things like surviving in the wild for a week. Their Foundations are generally Conspiracies, and the terse, improvisational, driven nature of Talons means they can be a bit short on their doctrine. This combination of Mystery Cult or Conspiracy means that the Charioteer will drive their Cultists to the breaking point, and at the end of that tunnel they’ll expect to find elaborate mythologies, rituals, and taboos that will grant meaning to their suffering. If the Mummy can’t provide, the Cult may revolt.

                                                The Bull-Headed foster the largest Cults of any of the Deathless, leaning heavily on Reach. (A little grasp might be needed, but they generally believe they’re doing the right thing and that society should respect their insights and dedication.) Most are Tribal Cults blatantly constructed as religions, so that their master can bask in their rituals and total obedience, but in time some have evolved into expansive Enterprises. The Millstones are spectacularly dedicated, either to a core principle or to themselves, and in either case loyalty to fellow Deathless, ethics, their masters, and more or less the entire world, comes second to that essential aspect of themselves. They are reliable and stalwart, if you’re on the same side. Otherwise, the Millstones will use their reputation for honesty and devotion to place Conspirators, gain Leverage, and otherwise destroy their rivals with disciplined imagination and ruthless thoroughness.

                                                The Serpent-Headed tend to mid-sized Cults, larger than some, smaller than most. These immortal intellectuals often attract like-minded individuals, store up copious amounts of Lore, and plant Conspirators, Agents, and Operatives at key points in the antiquities business to keep an eye on any number of vessels and hints towards their own past. They tend to recruit through those channels as well, using Agents and Operatives to seek out archaeologists with an eye for Relics, retrieve any vessels they might have found, and put them on the payroll. These Cults are Elite Clubs for archaeologists and anthropologists, sometimes with genuine Legitimacy as organizations dedicated to preserving antiquities. (“That belongs in a museum!” Well, technically, it’s a Tomb. But sure) In other cases, they might be Tribal Mystery Cults, an almost monastic religion dedicated to the pursuit of higher knowledge and/or the appeasement of entities that knowledge might unleash.

                                                The Jackal-Headed are patrons to the smallest Cults amongst the Deathless, Gangs or Tribes with a Mystery Cult structure and an odd philosophy of nihilism and restraint. The Ferrymen rarely have anything in common with mortals, and it takes an odd group of mortals comfortable with death to deal with the self-proclaimed masters of passage between the lands of the living and the dead. What few Cultists they possess are armed, trained, empowered as Agents and Operatives- they are doomsday enthusiasts and death cultists, ready for the end of the world. They tend to be isolated, invested heavily in fortified Home Turf. The Cults of Anpu’s Torches generally end up looking exactly like most people imagine a Cult to appear, the difference being that these men, women, and children will all bow to the will of their Deathless master, even if it leads them to follow their god down into death.

                                                On the other hand, the Shuankhsen make use of whatever Cults they can get their hands on- picking up the disenfranchised, the rebellious, and the downtrodden. Their Cults tend to be Unorganized, able to transform into anything they need them to be. They Compartmentalize, turning isolated groups of the Cult into fodder for Ammut’s hunger and fuel for the Fasad. They like to track the Arisen through Conspirators and Agents implanted in the Cults of their sworn enemies, and because the kings amongst the Lifeless have a much higher chance of earning a Descent Reset (by committing terrible acts, admittedly), increasing the periods they can endure. Paired with their perfect memories, a Cult headed by a Shuankhsen is quite able to predict the actions of their Arisen nemeses long before their enemy can even remember who they are.

                                                The Deceived don’t generally invest in Reach, seeing as their use of Enigma actively impairs that particular trait, and so they pour all their resources into Grasp and Lore. When the Lost Guild needs to move in places of power, they invest powerful Agents and Operatives, allowing them to compensate for the shroud of their master’s protection. These Agents and Operatives are not full members of the Cult, but merely answer to them- joining in the mystical bond of worshipping one of the Deceived would render the advantage of their Reach moot. As a result, the Cults of Deceived with high Enigma ratings rule over massive Mystery Cult Conspiracies, stuffed to the gills with Lore and power, and armed to the teeth. They are ghosts and mysteries even to those who trade in secrets and conspiracies. They are the secret, horrifying masters of the world, concealed in the darkness, announced by servants both rich and powerful who are excluded from such exalted company. To the Arisen and Lifeless who know of their existence, they are horrors among horrors, gods even to the greatest of monsters.

                                                The Witnesses: Those who live in the presence of gods are cursed and blessed beyond imagining. They are wracked by the throes of Sybaris, killed at the whims of their gods, but they can’t help but serve. Sybaris won’t let them go, belief and loyalty (and fear) keeps them in line, and in the case of the worshippers of the Deceived, they literally cannot stop without a monumental act of will. Witnesses have it worse, seeing the Deathless and Lifeless in their true horror and glory, minds unbent by Sybaris, but obsessed eternally by the lure and power of Sekhem. Mortals in the path of Sekhem and Irem’s ruins can go years, decades, possibly lifetimes without even seeing the god they worship. Picture the High Priest, used to uncontested power for years, forced to bow before a god they didn’t- couldn’t -know was a literal, physical being. Think of the Cultists who joined to be rebellious, or were part of a Conspiracy or Enterprise for meaning. Think of the Cultist who joined the wrong club. At the end of that pursuit of meaning, at the end of the search for truth, they find only curses and the horrifying, glorious power of the Tomb.

                                                            You most certainly can. This system, like the Pact system I posted a while back, are meant to be used by anyone so long as it improves the fun at their gaming table. I'm trying to make this as user-friendly as possible, but I'm still not too sure if it is just yet. Anyways, Promethean stuff now.

                                                            *****
                                                            Promethean: Empowered by a force of irresistible change that forms the foundation of the human soul, called down from the heavens by madness, passion, genius, and obsession. Were it not for the way it burns the earth and scorches minds and souls, Azoth would be perfect to center a cult around. It is immortal, transcendent, purifying… destructive. Pyros changes all it touches, for good or ill, and it has a history of inspiring those obsessive, brilliant, or mad enough to try and call it down and control it. Pyros isn’t matter, energy, or an idea. It comes in substances, notably Vitriol and the alchemical compounds infusing Promethean flesh. It isn’t energy, even though it radiates out with a measureable aura and has a tangible effect on those in its field. Pyros possesses a philosophical angle, which is what creates Azothic Memory. The alchemical reactions of the Divine Fire aren’t just physical or chemical, but emotional, metaphorical, and spiritual. It changes and purifies its subject, but does not consume that which it changes. It can, at worst, reduce it down to become something else.

                                                            Disquiet can make supporting a Cult very difficult for a Promethean, depending on the Lineage. In other cases, the proper collection of Transmutations and judicious use of Conspirators, Agents, and Operatives can make it more than possible; it can actually make it entirely sustainable. The question is: why? What would drive a Promethean to start a Cult? Perhaps it is an attempt to bring people close. Perhaps they want to understand the drive to worship. Maybe it’s entirely unintentional- the Disquiet of some Prometheans can make that all too likely, turning the unwitting Created into an unwilling idol. In rare cases, the Promethean might believe themselves to be truly divine, given the right to rule by the Azoth that gives them life.

                                                            Despite appearances the Wretched are actually idealists; their choleric humour may be fuelled by pain, sparked by anger, fanned by restlessness and irritability, but the torch lit from that flame could be turned to idealistic ends. They can be doctrinaires, enthusiastic and inflexible in their devotion. Their passion verges on the dogmatic and fanatical, but the consequences for a Wretched who finds their faith misplaced can be catastrophic. One goes into deep denial, another spirals into nihilistic depression, and a third can become equally passionate in pursuing a cause diametrically opposed to the original. A Wretched Centimanus can be one of the most sincere monsters you’ll ever meet. This inclination can drive the Wretched to build up any Cult, but they’re inclined towards Tribal and Enterprise Foundations. Because of the “Torches and Pitchforks” nature of the Wretched Disquiet, most Frankensteins who build up a Cult often develop Disquietism Transmutations to redirect that ire to more ‘productive’ targets, wielding it like a weapon.

                                                            The Galateids are among the most likely to accidentally gather a Cult, typically a Cult of Personality, due to the nature of their Disquiet. The Muse’s pursuit of mortality is, in many ways, ironic, because they possess beauty and power that people spend billions on. They cannot understand, or condone, their own inhuman perfection. The desires of the Galateid, fuelled by the Sanguine humour, are confused at best, blurring the lines between parent, child, friend, and lover. They are capable of astonishing courage and selflessness, but equally extreme obsession, lust, and bravado. Their Cults are little families they’ve built up around themselves that they guard jealously, and they react badly to anyone attempting to leave. Make no mistake, their Disquiet can sour situations swiftly, but the Muse often encourages their Disquiet, bonding mortals to them with their Sanguine humour as surely as any Kindred blood doll’s addiction or the use of Disciplines, and often to the same effect. It’s entirely possible that the uninitiated might mistake a Muse for one of the Kindred.

                                                            The Nepri build up Cults of Personality and Tribal afterlife cults. They are obsessed with mortal transitions, why people fear the loss of their body. To these immortals even amongst the extremely enduring Created, death, pain, and suffering is an idle curiosity. The Osirians are keenly aware of their divine progenitor, and hardly consider their body as being of any importance, seeing value only in the Azoth within. They can see themselves as distant from their fellow Created as they are from humanity, and they want to understand why- asking questions with a cold, unsettling, overeager curiosity. Their Disquiet and their Disfigurements remind people of impending death, inspiring a single emotion that flares dangerously when they are reminded of the transitory nature of life, when they feel as though their time is being wasted. A Nepri Cult Leader is an expert in taking this Disquiet and exploiting it, making it seem as though they have the answers, returning time and again from beyond the veil of death. The inexperienced will often confuse them with the Deathless, though not without good reason.

                                                            The Tammuz are the other Lineage inclined to ‘accidentally’ picking up Cults due to the curse of their Disquiet. Those Cults tend to be Elite Clubs, as those controlled by the Golem’s Disquiet get a perverse joy in the powerful slave they’ve taken hostage. In other cases, the Cult takes on a different character, as the Disquiet driving them to possess the Tammuz instead encourages them to take advantage of the Promethean’s goodwill and desire to save those humans who seem so fragile compared to the Created. But the Golems are themselves named for a dying-and-rising god, who had a far-reaching Cult in the ancient Near East. An important part of the myth of Tammuz is that he was sacrificed in Inanna’s place, descending to the Underworld. While she was in wrath that he was found sitting on her throne when she returned, the weeping of the people for Tammuz, who gave so much to the people. The Golem may be encouraged to try and foster a Cult, then endure through Disquiet until they are torn to pieces by their worshippers, hopeful that their sacrifice will inspire the New Dawn. That is, unless their Torment gets to them first and they tear through their cultists.

                                                            The Ulgan are ecstatics, shamans treading the boundary between mortal and spirit. These Cults are, typically, also Spirit Cults, of Tribal or Elite Club Foundations. The Riven accept their duty as walkers-between with a sort of grave dignity- shamans have always been outsiders amongst their tribes. The Spirits themselves tend to either rejoice or fear the effect of the Ulgan Wasteland, as Twilight becomes transparent and the Gauntlet starts to collapse. Because that kind of spiritual turmoil tends to attract the Uratha, and Disquiet already inspires murderous rage in humans, some of the Spirits will generally take it upon themselves to drive the Ulgan away, kill them, or protect them as it fits their goals and confidence in being able to handle the Uratha. Ulgan Cults are packed full of Lore, and they often see the Generative Act as an apprenticeship- selecting those talented in dealing with spirits for the dark ritual that will turn them into the Riven.

                                                            Their opponents are no less inclined to the making and maintaining of Cults. Alchemists build them as a means to gather Pyros, find Prometheans (and with them, Athanors) and acquire resources. In rare cases, the qashmallim start them, for reasons as unusual and enigmatic as the Dominions themselves. Far more likely Cult Leaders are the Sublimati, the most unholy of the Mockeries; Pandorans elevated to human and transhuman levels of intelligence, without the desire or inclination to become human. Their unique conditions of sentience, fuelled as they are by a force of mutation and stagnation, leads to very strange conclusions and beliefs. They often found radical philosophies, moral systems, and even full-blown religions- with themselves hiding in the shadows due to the nature of their Disquiet. Unless, that is, like the Lady of Chains they develop unusual Dread Powers in order to circumvent that restraint. Some of them start organizations so powerful they don’t just draw mortals in, but other Sublimati and Centimanus Prometheans as well.

                                                            Torchbearers: Humans caught in the path of the Divine Fire don’t generally escape untouched. Azoth forces change, for good or ill, and the raw radiance of it scorches the land and ignites in human minds. Disquiet can affect a person long after the Promethean has departed, perpetuating cycles of change and unusual behaviour against their will. Alchemists and Demiurges, however, deliberately seek it out. Pyros didn’t trigger a downward spiral (some would say it didn’t trigger an immediate downwards spiral) but instead lit them aflame, making them capable of wielding the Divine Fire to perform miracles and unleash horrors. And amidst all this chaos and madness sparked by errant divinity the qashmallim drift, performing their inscrutable quests under the command of an ineffable master. Some men want to watch the world burn, others want to light it aflame. There’s a subtle difference between the two.
