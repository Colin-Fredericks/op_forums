    The meaning of Experience

    03-10-2015, 11:29 AM
    I was reading through the 2nd Edition Errata thread a little while ago and came across someone saying that Style Merits didn't have an explicit explanation. That brought to mind some more general questions about Experience in this game (for both editions), but it seems worth starting with that as an example.

    The main difference between Style Merits and most other Merits is that you get something out of every dot and can't skip dots as you purchase the various feats the Merit allows, right? [1]
    For instance, the Fading Merit is a three dot Merit for which dots one and two versions don't exist. If you want any benefit from Fading, you use three dots at creation or spend three Experiences in play.
    The Dig Deep functionality of the Relentless Assault Werewolf Fighting Style Merit (ah! caps!) is also from a three dot Merit, but to get it you first need to get Drop of a Hat and Eye of the Tiger. To get Dig Deep, you need to use three of your starting dots (one each) or spend three Experiences during play (one each), right? [2]

    Was it the case in first edition that to get Dig Deep in play you'd need to spend 2 XP to get Drop of a Hat, then spend double that to get Eye of the Tiger, then triple the first amount to get Dig Deep for a total of 6x the 2XP cost for "new dots" of merits? [3]

    The more general question I want to get at is what this means for players. I've heard the assumption now in 2nd Edition is that you'll end up with an average of 1 Experience per play session. As such, getting a new dot in Primal Urge is something that should only happen once every five game sessions, and then only if the character saw no other advancement for that time. For a group that can only meet once a month, that seems like a dreadfully long time to not be progressing, so it seems like there's an underlying and unwritten expectation that troupes will play more often. Is that so? [4a] If so, how often is that? Twice a week? Once a week? [4b]

    What's your experience of dealing with Experience been? Have you found that occasionally it helps to have skip-ahead moments where it's months later in-game and the characters all suddenly have some extra pool of XP? How did you handle that? [5]

      Skipping all existential jokes I want to make...

      It was a lot longer to raise a power stat in the original version, assuming an average of three exp a session and seven on the story climaxes. That New Dot*8 led to a huge leap each time you bought it, and it could be near impossible to buy it in average games once you got past four, and that's if you bought exclusively that. Contrast that with the game now, where five sessions is a sacrifice, but manageable at all points.

      I prefer having in game month skips where they earn some extra exp helps create a good verisimilitude for real life in a narrative sense as well as a good way to augment the exp building without drowning them in it.

      And yes, you have it right for first edition. Fading would cost six exp to start, and the three dots would be two, four, and six respectively.

          My understanding of Style merits is that they are chains of separate merits whose prerequisites are the previous merits in the chain. This is in contrast to merits which simply have a variable cost (like Striking Looks). So, to use your examples:
          * Fading is a three point merit. It costs 3xp to get.
          * Dig Deep is a three point merit but it is part of a style merit, so you need to buy the one and two point merits first. So you'd need to spend 1xp for Drop of a Hat, 2xp for Eye of the Tiger, and then the 3xp for Dig Deep. So, 6xp total.

          I haven't run a campaign under the new rules yet, so can't speak to how fast experiences come, but you might want to check the thread on Chris' Paris campaign, which gives a tally of the Beats earned by his group per session.

                          [1]: That is correct, you have to get purchase all the dots of a Style merit in order.

                          [2] and [3]: This matter has been discussed before, and one of the excellent people at Onyx Path pointed out any confusion is a draft relic: getting all five dots of a rating 5 Style only costs you 5 Experiences total. First Edition had scaling XP costs for higher ratings of any mechanical trait, while Second Edition has flat Experience costs per dot.

                          [4]: Groups are free to fix the flow of Beats and Experiences to their liking. If you want faster advancement, it's perfectly fine to give more Beats for things; but this can have the side-effect of creating a disparity between the development of characters in the narrative and mechanically. There's only so much story you can go through in a single session, after all. Of course, this can be an issue even for groups that meet more often. In our group's Vampire chronicle, which was a weekly affair (we normally do weekly or bi-weekly game meets) we kind of ended up with accidental elders power wise due to not paying much attention to the flow of Experiences (when the characters were all Ancillae).

                          [5]: As I said above, we got a little bit ahead of what we intended and once we realized we adjusted the assignment of Beats. During the occasional weeks or few months timeskips we had, we didn't really give any extra Experiences off to spend; if there was some sort of development logical to happen during that time that would reflect in say, Merits and we had earned it, we'd work it out with the Storyteller. We tended to not spend Experiences during the middle of the action while events were occurring though. We saved earned Experiences and placed most of our character advancement during time skips both brief and long, because it made more sense that way.

                                                  1. Exactly. Well, the main difference between style merits and other merits is that you get something out of each dot. As I understand it, you can't skip dots in any sort of merits, and a 3 dot style merit costs as much to complete as any other 3 dot merit, though like you said, it can be done incrementally.

                                                  2. Yes, exactly. Same cost, just incremental in the latter case.

                                                  3. That was the case. So getting to the third dot in a merit would cost 12 experience total (meaning including the first two dots in a style merit) 2 + 4+ 6.

                                                  4. Getting a dot of primal urge once every 5 sessions is actually very very reasonable considering the importance placed on that trait. Especially in comparison to the cost in 1st Edition, where the second dot in primal urge would cost 16 experience, the third would cost 24, and further on in multiples of 8. The cost was prohibitively expensive at later levels (at all levels, really) and made it almost necessary to sacrifice starting merit dots to increase it if primal urge was ever part of the plan for you character.

                                                  I think you just learn to wait. For some people, advancing a character is one of the primary ways they derive enjoyment from a roleplaying game, and for those people especially, the wait can be very frustrating. It really depends on the kind of chronicle you want to run. There's a certain satisfaction in playing a campaign for 4 years and watching your character grow slowly from a pup into a badass monster. I played a D&D campaign for 7 years with a great, but relatively stingy DM, and I was incredibly proud of my character by the end of the campaign. On the other hand, there's also a certain frustration when your character is advancing too slowly, or is prevented from doing cool things because of a lack of experience.

                                                  There's a balance that has to be struck and it depends on the players and the storyteller. If you feel the rate of experience gain is too slow for your tastes, talk to your Storyteller about it. A lot of times, a storyteller won't mind within reason. Maybe he'll throw two beats at the end of a session, instead of one. Maybe he'll add a few more ways for players to earn beats. The rules aren't rigid. Remember it's your game, the rulebook is just a framework to base it around.

                                                  5. I favour games where experience is handed out relatively generously, because I often make characters that require a lot of experience to express their core concept. For example, my last character was the daughter of the Chinese Ambassador the United States. She basically allocated all of her dots to expressing that concept. (Language merit, Mandarin, Cantonese, Status: Diplomatic Immunity, etc.) and very little in the way of actual in-game effectiveness. We've played for over a year and speaking Chinese hasn't come up once (except the time I pretended to not speak English to a police officer), and honestly I knew it wouldn't, but I had to have it. Just like I knew I had to sacrifice a few of my skill points to Socialize (etiquette) and Politics. The only reason I was able to do that was because our Storyteller handed us 20 experience points to start with. There's a chance I would have made an entirely different character otherwise (I had a lot of ideas).

                                                  My current storyteller gives us a large amount of bonus experience after every chapter, and it definitely is welcome. I've played games where I started as a veteran, and games where I started as a pup who learned really slowly (haha), and it's all been enjoyable. It just depends on whether your storyteller gives you opportunities to shine.

                                                      Originally posted by TerraBen View Post
                                                      That's some really helpful feedback. Thanks everyone!
                                                      I figured this had been discussed at some point, but it isn't spelled out terribly well in any of the books I've gone though so far. This is especially true, as eberg is pointing out, for the Style Merits and other ones that give benefits along the way.

                                                      Given that one dot of Power Stat (Primal Urge as far as this forum is concerned) is 5XP now, which is the same as ANY 5-dot Merit (5 dots of Resources, with which you're basically a millionaire and can afford anything comes to mind as it makes little sense for people to go from 0 to that all of the sudden), have GMs done something like putting up an XP store at the end of the sessions? Something like "Oh Jenny, I see you've saved up 5XP now. Your character could totally go up in Primal Urge based on what's happend, or nail down a dot of Purity or Wisdom renown and still have two. You could firm up that thing with the cops as an extra Allies dot or two. What do you say?"
                                                      Kind of, yeah. It's always been the case that experience purchases need to be justified in the narrative. Just because I have 5 experiences doesn't mean I am automatically entitled to buy 5 dots of Resources. Unless my character suddenly came into a lot of money, it wouldn't make sense for me to make that purchase, so I cant.

                                                      In my... well, experience, it's better to have the player tell you ahead of time what they would like to get next, figure out how long it would take them to gain that many experiences (based on what ever the average Beats per session rate is in your games), and then spend the next couple of sessions dedicating that character's spotlight time to earning that purchase in-game.

                                                      So, for example, if I'm starting a new game, I'll ask the players during our session 0 how they would like their chacter to advance next. Let's say one player wants a third dot in their Auspice Renown, one wants to unlock a new in-Tribe Gift, one wants to learn the Tactical Shifting style, and one wants another dot of Primal Urge. I will take all of that into account when I plan out the next 5 sessions.

                                                      Assuming 1 Experience per character per session (that's actually a little fast for my games, but to make the numbers easier I'm rounding up), the player who wants to learn tactical shifting will meet someone who can teach it to him before the end of the first session. By that point he should have 1 experience he can use to buy the first dot. The player who wanted another dot of her Auspice renown (let's say Glory) will have a chance to do something glorious in the third session, by which time she'll have three experiences to buy it with. The player who wanted a new gift will probably spend the next three sessions tracking down a spirit that can give it to him and earning its favor. And the player who wanted another dot of Primal Urge, I will spend the first five sessions putting in situations where they can act primally.

                                                      Each time a player achieves the advancement they were working towards, I'll ask them what they would like to start working on getting next, and incorporate that into my plans as well.
