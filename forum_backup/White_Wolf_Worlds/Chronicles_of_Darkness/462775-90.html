                                                        12-15-2016, 05:37 PM
                                                        Originally posted by Master Aquatosic View Post
                                                        Even after reading through the corebook, I'm a little confused on the laws of spirits, how they come into being, eat and grow in power.
                                                        Essence flows through the Gauntlet. If it's not eaten, it becomes a spirit. Spirits devour more Essence with their Resonance and grow in power, providing they survive.

                                                        From what I can tell, bigger emotional spirits devour smaller spirits generated from just people experiencing emotions, with the emotion they represent being the tastiest to them and branching out to concepts different from them being a product of desperation. Is that about right, or am I really misunderstanding it? And how do spirits of stuff that is not emotions - like spirits of Mountains - generate spirits to eat?
                                                        People experiencing emotions creates Essence of the Resonance of that emotion across the Gauntlet. Providing it's powerful enough. A moment's anger at noticing you accidentally tipped twenty instead of ten isn't necessarily going to be a feast for spirits. And they eat things more related to them. An anger spirit won't eat anything but anger Essence or spirits, unless it's crazed or being forced, as this will change its being. The exception is relative concepts. Anger can be associated with Fire, a spirit who is powerful enough that their Anger has tinges of that can also eat Fire Essence. 'Natural' spirits have an innate benefit with this. A fire spirit eats fire Essence, but it can also eat wood Essence, as fire devours wood, like a wolf spirit can eat a rabbit spirit. Emotion spirits don't usually have advantages like these, or at least they aren't as common.

                                                        And is it more likely to have an overall forest spirit representing all the animals and vegetation within a single grove or forest, a spirit for every single tree and bush, or both with the forest spirit being the leader of the latter?
                                                        The last, you'd generally have a hierarchy. A forest spirit of that forest, with individual animal, vegetation and even locational spirits that offer it tithes of Essence. Every tree and bush generates its own Essence, just not always enough to awaken its own spirit, so they would remain dormant or just weak.

                                                        And what happens when you break apart a boulder big enough and important enough to the local mythology to have its own spirit? Does it just die, or does it becom a bunch of pebble spirits?
                                                        Depending on how powerful it would be it might cling to one special shard of that boulder. It might find another way out. It might just wither and go dormant. It might not have survived the breaking process, as that might be reflected in the spirit as rock-breaking tool spirits destroying the boulder spirit. If the spirit is just a weak spirit of that boulder, it's not going to have that boulder Essence to survive, and will go dormant or get eaten up. But if it's important to local mythology it probably has more power to it and with that some agency.

                                                        A lot of these are variable for plot elements. What happens if John goes out and kills someone? He goes to jail. But maybe he doesn't, and he kills another, and another. Maybe someone covers it up. Maybe that person comes back to life. Same with spirits. That broken boulder spirit might decide it's going to cross the Gauntlet and start haunting the place, or be captured by a human shaman and used to wreak vengeance on its destroyers.
