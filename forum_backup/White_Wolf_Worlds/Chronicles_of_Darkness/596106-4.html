                            Originally posted by VoidDrifter View Post
                            The Beast controversy has been ongoing on these forums - and the RPG.net forums, from what little I know - for a while. The initial draft release attracted lots of debate, the official rewrite attracted even more.

                            But I was wondering: for those of you who had problems with Beast, either before or after the revamp, how would you have fixed them?

                            Myself? Well, I haven't been able to memorise the thread here, nevermind the RPG.net equivalent, but still...

                            #1: Horrors wrapped in human flesh
                            One of the things I liked about draft!Beast was the presentation of Beasts as literally born monsters. This is something touched on only be three other splats in the NWoD; Werewolves, Prometheans and Demons. Of those three, werewolves are often conflicted about whether or not they are happier now, demons have to feign humanity as best they can to survive, and the whole point of prometheans is to become human. Beasts, as they were presented, were something unique: monsters from birth who were actually happier when they accepted the truth.

                            "I am a monster who dreamed he was a man. But the dream is over. And the monster is awake."

                            That's not something integral to the other lines. It was in Beast, and that was a perspective I liked for its uniqueness. I don't mind using the term "Horror" to refer to a Beast's inner self, because that actually a good emphasis of the theme; humans have souls, but Beasts are horrors wearing a human suit. However, the term "Homecoming" is essential to me; it emphasizes that a Beast is closer to a Mage or a Werewolf than to a Vampire or a Changeling. No exterior agency acted on them to make them into a Beast; it was just who they always were, and they finally accepted it.


                            #2: Eat to live, don't live to eat
                            Beasts need to feed. That's just how it is. Everything needs sustenance. Beasts just happen to feed on fear and horror. Something all Beasts must learn to live with is that, no matter what humans think, they are still nothing more than meat, singing to itself in the darkness. But that doesn't mean a Beast shouldn't exercise restraint. A Beast must learn how to balance its hunger with the world around them; do they try to be humane and feed "reasonably", or do they embrace their dark side and gorge themselves, heedless of the destruction they cause?

                            It's alright for Beasts to want to put a higher meaning on why they feed or how they feed, but as more than one complainer has noted, the revision's "Beasts terrify humanity to teach lessons" comes off as foolish and pretentious. At best, it's worth a social Z-axis foundation, but I don't think it's strong enough to be the underlying purpose of the game.


                            #3: Heroes only in their own mind
                            The biggest problem with draft!Beast was the very use of the term "Heroes" in my opinion. No matter the higher cause behind it, the basic fact is that people associate "hero" with "good guy" - it's our cultural indoctrination. And if the other side are the Heroes, then, by simple logic, we Beasts must be the Villains. This simple fact, more than anything, incited readers to sympathize with the Heroes and look more harshly upon the Beasts.

                            Personally, I would call such people "Slayers". It's a much less loaded term that emphasizes who and what they are: they are Killers of Beasts. Morality has nothing to do with it, except maybe as the excuse they use when called upon it. They just want to kill Beasts. That's all that matters to them.


                            #4: Slayers are born, not made
                            The other big problem with Heroes, from my perspective, is that they were given a mechanical creation basis. This fueled the sympathetic interpretation of them and caused the schism between Hero supporters and Beast supporters.

                            I say that if a mortal rises up against a Beast because of that Beast's depredations, that's a Hunter, more tahn anything else. It's the universal thread that underlies all supernatural splats in the NWoD: Actions Have Consequences. Bully mortals too much, and humans will fight back. It's simple Cause and Effect.

                            I think Slayers should just arise. There's no need for malignancy on a Beast's part. A Slayer-to-be sees a Beast, and she knows that they are a monster. The Beast does nothing to them; she could just be passing by in the street, buying a coffee and a donut at a local stall, sitting on the bus, and it still doesn't matter. When a Slayer sees a Beast, they undergo their own twisted version of the Homecoming, finally realizing their place in life.

                            In fact, ideally, I think a Slayer potential should be immune to the Feasting of a hungry Horror. This would emphasize how shallow and selfish their goals are. They don't suffer from the Beast's presence, they don't experience anything to push them into accepting their role, they just open their arms and embrace it, as willingly as a Beast does.


                            These are the things I think of when I consider what I would have done to make Beast the best I think it could be. How would you have fixed whatever problems you see or saw in Beast?
                            #1: Born monsters and humans transformed by another monsters are classics but lack originality. I'd have gone for a "Looked into the Abyss, the Abyss looked back" thing. Beast would be people who kept searching into dark places (literally and/or figuratively) for Things Man Was Not Meant To Know, until their Hunger for those dark things warped or consumed their Souls, transforming them into Beasts.

                            #2: In keeping with #1, I would have directed the Beasts Hungers toward darkness: tainted things, dark emotions, cursed items, debased creatures, corrupt people, shameful secrets. They'd be compelled to seek them out to feed on them. They'd expose the corruption, reveal the secrets, force people to face their ugly desires, steal the cursed jewels, attract and protect monstrous beings.

                            #3: I'd see Heroes as champions of the established order; intent on keeping the Beasts from dragging the darkness to the surface and upseting the status quo. They'd be charming, inspiring, shining beacons of hope, hiding the horrible truths, making people dependant on their strength and blind to the bleak reality, and making them think they're safe, where Hungry Beasts would instead cause waves, drawing the fear and ugliness to the surface, causing fear and unrest. Heroes would hunt, fight and kill Beasts, try to erase all traces of their existences and their work, to keep the Darkness at bay like some sweep dirt under the rug, hoping that if it can't be seen it will not matter.

                            #4: I'd have Heroes emerge as a consequences of a Beast's successes. The more people would see the darkness revealed, the more their minds would rebel against it, causing the rise of a Hero to push back the Dark and making people feel safe once again.

                            When will you rage?

                                I've only read the original kickstarter preview so this might not be applicable anymore.

                                The biggest flaw I saw was Beast doesn't seem understand it's own themes.

                                First of all, it's pretty apparent that Beast liberally draws inspiration from books like John Gardner's Grendel, using the theme: 'The Hero is only as great as the villain he conquers'. It attempts to flip the Monolith on its head to portray the Hero as a blind, moralizing crusader ala Disney's hunchback of notre dame.

                                But, in Beast, the villains (i.e. the Heroes) aren't great, they're weak, pathetic, fedora tipping m'lady men (as one tawdry example). You can practically see the spittle being flung across the pages with a high authorial voice during their descriptions. Heroes aren't even near the same power levels as Beasts and are about as sympathetic and complex as a mustache twirling cartoon.

                                How to fix it?

                                Beasts desperately needs some antagonist to fight against, something powerful, something great, something that its defeat *means something*. What can that be?

                                - It can't be the Hero, they're an unfortunate person that's been pulled into this mess through no fault of their own, their creation is a by-product of the Beast feeding with low Satiety.
                                - It can't be the Beast themselves, as there's not really a mechanic like the Shadow, or vampire's 'beast system'.

                                Instead, I would change the main antagonist to be the Monomyth itself, deemed The Story. The only end-goal, ‘win-state’ that makes sense for Beast is destroying The Story and ending the endless cycle of Beast/Hero creation (for their Beast’s soul, of course, not all Beasts).

                                A big part of the Beasts character arc, then, would be convincing the Hero to work with the Beast, as both are necessary to destroy the Monomyth. They are both sides in this unfortunate conflict, forever linked in an eternal combat against each other, doomed to reincarnate again, and again. It would organically build a different theme, a humanist theme, one of understanding your enemies and questioning why you fight against them; scrutinizing who's the real villain in the conflict.

                                In my version of the game, the main conflict and antagonist to overcome is *The Players and The Storyteller* themselves - the very ones describing and playing the game. The Beast confronts them why they need to tell stories and create characters that inflict untold amounts of damage and suffering in a world, only for their own amusement.

                                But given all that, I'm going to give it another read when they release the finished version. I'm pretty confident they'll take the player feedback seriously and create a much better game.

                                                    Originally posted by darthcoffee View Post
                                                    The biggest flaw I saw was Beast doesn't seem understand it's own themes.
                                                    Putting aside that heroes are more sympathetic in the revised and there's lots of suggestions for using Dark Heroes from Mirrors*, you also seem to be oddly reversing the inverse. You're trying to suggest we turn Beast into the heroes of its own story. The idea is to undermine the monomyth and the violence inherent in it, not support the monomyth by recasting the roles so that the Begotten is now the hero and "The Story" is now the monster.

                                                    I'm going to say that is exactly what the game doesn't need. Not to mention that edging far too close to CtL's Gentry territory; I would prefer to avoid that overlap. We've been telling that story for years now, and I'm interested in a new one.

                                                    I would also like to say that I suspect you've missed the actual central theme. While feeding your Hunger and warding off heroes is part of the story, its actually only a part of it - Vampire is far more than drinking blood and protecting your territory, and Mage has more to it than scrutinizing magic to buy up Arcana dots. The central driving story force in Beast is actually crossover. The game isn't just highly crossover compatable, its also a central drive of the game.

                                                    Beast, in many ways, is an inverted HtV game. Hunter focuses on PC splats to track down and capture/kill - there's a reason the majority of their books focus on hunting vamps, wolves, mages, prommies, fae, sin-eaters, mummies, and demons, with a side of fellow hunters (sometimes turned Slasher). Unlike most other games, crossover isn't really a potential plot hook or style here. For Hunter and Beast, its a central one. The difference is that, in Beast, you're not on the hunt to kill or capture, but to make friends and build a family. And, yes, Family is another major aspect of Beast - there's a reason we call beasts the Children and the Begotten, and a reason why we call the cotorie-equivalent the brood.


                                                    * As of the last revision, it feels like that heroes are still roughly on par with hunters in terms of power level, which means that, no, they're not a match for the Children in a straight up battle without anathema in full force. Which is why people are suggesting the Dark Heroes in the mirrors book, and combining the two. Having the D.H. powered by the Temnos/Bright Dream and their super-ability with skills actually does a fairly good job of keeping the themes of BtP's heroes, while making them a greater challenge in a direct battle. Combine with the other abilities, and its actually a pretty interesting spread of abilities.

                                                    That said, the low power of the heroes might actually be deliberate, so as to keep the whole "plucky underdog" vibe of hunters around. The whole over-confident, knowing I can crush them at any time vibe might be an important vibe.
