            Originally posted by Niknokk View Post
            If we aren't careful, we can put ourselves in a bind where we either risk going way over player comfort levels in portraying a real-world evil, or we soft-pedal the real-world evil for the sake of making it playable, which is also uncomfortable. I don't think it's a terminal problem for Hunter, but I do think it's a sticking point that deserves to be taken seriously when someone has an issue with it.
            Aspel mentioned Deviant, which is convenient because the Devoted Companion just came out a couple months ago, addresses playing members of evil conspiracies who literally become more stable when they're powered by hate, and literally mentions in its systems for creating a conspiracy in-setting that the necessary Objectives are usually distasteful, unethical, or immoral; that vibe is not unique to Deviant.

            Conspiracies in Hunter are characterized as organized from the top down in contrast to the more regional bottom-up structure of a compact. If you look around at the rest of the franchise, you may notice that organizations that are organizations rather than localized expressions of a widespread philosophical system (a la werewolf tribes, Promethean Refinements, conventional changeling courts, etc) tend to be increasingly compromised with scale and power (see also krewes, Arisen cults, the Seers of the Throne, etc), to say nothing of cults of personality like those cultivated by vampires and demons and beasts.

            My primary beef with Hunter Second Edition has been its carrying forward of the tendency seen in Mortal Remains to treat the conspiracies as known quantities among their peers instead of mutually secretive groups like the name indicates, but the fact remains that hunter organizations above the level of a cell tend to infiltrate or manipulate unaffiliated cells in their orbit to take risks they don't want to take themselves — see the conversation with the Chevalier Thélème and his interviewer in the Horror Recognition Guide — and that, even without a Supernatural Potency stat to directly fill the role (Compact/Conspiracy Status comes the closest to proxying for it), long-established hunters implicitly have worn down their Integrity to a lower level and/or altered it through the Code, having gone through experiences that damage their ability to relate to people and carry on a life outside the Vigil; note that the Merciless Condition switches the circumstances in which a hunter hemorrhages Willpower from "supernatural danger to a human being" to "a new monster's presence" and switches the rewards for certain Willpower-risking situations from aligning with their Virtue to aligning with their Vice.

            These are long-running groups whose core membership is made up of and whose guiding principles are driven by people who are very likely to have seen and done things that have made them particularly ruthless and focused primarily on the pursuit of the supernatural in the name of humanity in the abstract or the concrete particular. Even without a connection to a large public-facing organization or profession with known issues, this is a category of people who instrumentalize other people (not uncommonly without their knowledge or consent) in the name of specific goals for approaching the supernatural in a setting where the supernatural is traumatic for the vast majority of people to encounter, including hunters, and that's part of why some of them help hide the supernatural from the public. I don't expect the fucking Men In Black to be morally blameless even without the indiscretions of the United States military, and I don't assign much in the way of special moral dimension to "the Long Night believe the End Times are upon us and monsters walk the earth" because that's their institutional mythology's in-universe justification for why a collection of church-basement Bible-study groups coordinate to hunt monsters.

            This is a setting that superficially resembles the world as we know it except with magic and monsters in the shadows, and when magic and monsters have and are sources of power that means the powerful have reason to be concerned with these subjects as things to have on their side and to fear being turned against them. The real-world evils of the Catholic Church are not the driving focus of the Malleus Maleficarum in a game where you are playing a witch-hunter sent to stake and extract a vampiric slumlord, for all that that mission exists to feed the addiction of a centuries-old ghoul placed highly in the Church.The fact that the Cheiron Group is an evil corporation whose main positive moral aspect is that the monsters it sends its Field Projects Division after may be doing harm to people is not meaningfully different from other conspiracies in the practical sense of "if you belong to a hunter organization big and old and strong enough to give you cool magic powers, those tend to come with the obligations attendant to that structure and those obligations may wear on your independence and/or your scruples." The Union aren't even at that level of insitutional corruption, and their being majority Home First tells you all you need to know re: "these are only 'good guys' in the sense that they have people they care about."

                            I mean, Cheiron is not made up of victims. Taskforce VALKYRIE is not made up of victims.
                            You're starting from a top down perspective that is not helping you to understand the picture Hunter is painting.

                            The books demonstrate time and again that engagement with the supernatural is not a thing that people casually do for almost any given reason, given that the supernatural in all of it's forms its actively psychologically and spiritually stressful and damaging-most people's responses are to try and keep themselves as distant from the supernatural as they can after one or two exceptional intrustions. Mortal investigators from the Chronicles corebook are often individuals who are in the middle of one of those exceptional intrusions and are written with a general understanding that most of them will go back to not looking too closely at the shadows once a chronicle is over, with a rare few exceptions diving deeper to understand it better to better interact with it (or, more realistically, be even better at not engaging with it, or at least not without protections).

                            The sort of person who becomes a hunter, a person who not only interacts and investigates the supernatural but actively work to harm in it a variety of ways in the name of a variety of causes and reasons, is an expectation above and beyond that, and the general root of that is a resolution against the harm they dealt with in their one or two exceptional experiences with the supernatural. Hunter: the Vigil First Edition's corebook is pretty upfront and explicit about that, pretty well opening it's world building chapter at the start of the book with "Yeah, pretty much every hunter is a victim of supernatural violence of some sort."

                            So if you wanted to provide a fuller line, a non-reductive line with this, it might go something like "The Field Projects Division of the Cheiron Group and Task Force: Valkyrie are not, institutionally, victims in any meaningful sense, but there are things about them that are attractive to victims of supernatural violence that lead to making up a lot of those organizations with those victims."​
                            And as addendum to that, it's in that attraction that more of the overall point is found.​

                                                        Highlighting this because it's worth emphasizing: while we like to use the name of the conglomerate interchangeably with it, the Field Projects Division is not the same thing as The Cheiron Group as a corporate entity. It is, in fact, repeatedly emphasized within the books as a low-rung branch of the company populated in significant proportion by people desperate and/or greedy enough to hunt monsters with the specific intention of taking them alive or at least harvesting them for parts at the behest of a corporation that demands quotas and may ask them to serve as testbeds for the grody medical prototypes that haven't hit the market yet, because it pays well or it keeps their husband's cancer in remission or it lets them take the fight to the monsters with the backing of a global corporation who can keep the law off their back to a certain degree.

                                                        It is kind of a shit job, as one could probably infer from there being more than one sample character blurb in both corebooks (at least six of the eight) that boils down to "you were put in the Field Projects Division due to a lucrative job offer you didn't properly read, legal trouble you were or could be in that the company would keep at bay, or a clerical error, and now you've got monster parts in you." The comparison to the Devoted is not a subtle one, particularly with Second Edition putting a little more emphasis on conspiracy hunters going rogue.
