    As I see it, different hunters will fight different monsters, for different reasons and with different methods. A hunter might spend their entire career stealing secrets from magicians and warlocks, never facing any living dead or lycantrophe. Another might be your stereotypical vampire hunter, putting stakes in bloodsuckers without caring for anything else. But becoming a Slasher is something that can happen to any of them. The line between the burning dedication to the Vigil and destructive insanity is razor-thin. Losing yourself to the hunt and becoming just another kind of monster is a universal threat.

        Originally posted by Leliel View Post

        Several objections to the objections:

        First, Slashers are a dark mirror to hunters, it's called out even in the book. Hunters are by nature killers by necessity (at the very least); even the Loyalists of Thule all know how to operate a gun. A Slasher is what happens when they stop caring about the necessity and start killing because their ideology tells them to, or simply because it's the only thing they're good at anymore. All the rage and pain that creates a hunter, but none of the ethics or focus. Even their abilities reflect this-they don't so much gain preternatural skill as an assassin so much as the fact that they no longer have moral limits, which also turns off their mortal limitations. Not every Slasher was once a Hunter, but enough of them were to remind the Vigil that the candle flame can become an all-consuming wildfire if they forget why they lit it.


        I would argue that they are a dark mirror to some versions of the hunter storyline, not the dark mirror. I can bite necks and abduct people behind my garden hedge (I don't) but that doesn't mean that I possess some qualitative sympathy with vampire and the True Fae. Nor do I think that its entirely right to say that the slasher lies at the end of the hunter road as a likely end point for every one who takes up the vigil. The fact that slashers are monsters and their prey are mortal humans for the most part and not some type of monster, highlights for me the problematic nature of the dark mirror theory. It just doesn't ring true for the majority of hunter characters I have GMed. I don't even think that a significant minority of slasher even were once hunters.


        Second: cults already have a lot of focus in the nWoD 2E in the core, there's even a merit signifying membership in one with supernatural mojo. Making them the focus of the Chronicle would retread the book you buy to have a context for Hunter 2E to begin with. On the other hand, Hunter demons are, quite simply, boring. Not because you can't get stories from them, but because they are made for a very specific genre of stories; temptation. All well and good for anyone not a demon, but demons themselves, simply by occupying that niche? Can't really have a character themselves anymore, just an echo of a particular type of temptation (even Mr. White; all he's good at is murder investigations and asking the question if someone is willing to commit murder to solve a murder-the only thing sympathetic on a human level is his confusion as to whether his human identity was his original one, and it's pointed out it really doesn't matter that much to him). A Slasher also has a defining action, but killing and managing to get away with it contains within it the seeds for many different genres; victimization, action, investigation, politics (a clever and manipulative killer disguising their hunger for any death as death of a hated minority-which means manipulating their supporters out of the way). That, and there's a reason serial killers end up the villains so often-how a seemingly normal individual loses all aversion to death and violence is fascinating, and probably unique to each person.
        The hunter demons are a little flat, but that's more to do with the brevity of their section than anything else. I mean the slasher and vampire sections aren't exactly inspirational either and its not to we get the supplements that they really shine. I think temptation is a strong theme for demons (so is corruption, transcendence through evil and individuality, decadence and perversity for it own sake), but it's definitely not the sole defining theme of these evil spirits, especially if you look across cultures and at other dark and inhuman spirits of evil. But temptation is certainly a strong enough theme to light a fire with, so to speak. Demons, via their multiplicity of themes and agendas can access the same genres as any slasher, inc those you listed above. In fact, I think slashers are far more limited than demons, as in their fullest definition as evil spirits of the Lower Depths, their natures can be legion, if you excuse the pun.

        Third, the book is there. It's also 1E, and linked to a system that no longer exists and its replacement has completely different context (a Slasher may have any Integrity-low Integrity means their drive for death is born from trauma, a high Integrity means they're so calloused they don't care any more). That, and Avengers need an update. And probably need rules for truly supernatural abilities too instead of just the two-or-four they're born with. And if "already been done" was honestly a problem, we wouldn't have 2E to begin with. Certainly not the Strix, whose B&S version is their third incarnation. And VASCU deserves to be in the core.
        You won't be surprised if I disagree with you, partially. The rules do indeed need a good update, as Integrity is certainly a difficult one to adapt to slashers. But the fact that the core rules have changed doesn't make 'already done' a problem. Updates and improvements are always welcome. I just think cults and demons are a more pressing update since they've been neglected in the past and are a more significant gap. I mean, I can and have made up slashers with just the new MR rules updates and the new endowments. Integrity is not a great issue for my antagonist designs as it is for the player characters.

        Fourth, if you really want cults and demons, there's other supplements to Hunter in the pipeline. Those two things probably deserve a book of their own, rather than Major Splat Under Hunter Rules books (although those books are pretty good, I'll admit).
        Outstanding. I certainly agree with you regarding the separate books.

            For me, a big thing about why Slashers work as the cart upsetter has a little more practical edge to it on top of everything else. The biggest advantage a cell, of any sort, has is co-ordianation. Sure, numbers and allies help, but those numbers require trust.                                                             Personally, I'm hoping that 2e steers clear of the “tier 1/2/3” terminology. Speak of independent cells, of compacts and of conspiracies; speak of Endowments and how they come in two varieties: supernatural Endowments such as Advanced Armoury, Benedictions, Castigations, Elixirs, Relics, or Thaumatechnology; and temporal Endowments such as the Bacchanal, Prayer, Unearthed Secrets, the All-Seeing Eye, I'm Doing Science, or Your Friends and Neighbors. Where Tier gets mentioned at all, use the terminology provided in GMC: you have Local, Regional, Global, or Cosmic, not “Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3, Tier 4” — and even here, I’d steer clear of actually using the T-word, instead speaking of a chronicle's scope.

                                                            And then talk about how these things interrelate, which (in a nutshell) should be all about tendencies, and never about lock-step bindings. Independent cells are most at home when the chronicle's scope is local; but there's nothing wrong with an independent cell tackling a global or even cosmic chronicle to the best of their ability. Likewise, conspiracies tend to operate on the global stage; but a TFV cell operating in a town and dealing primarily with local concerns is a perfectly viable approach.

                                                            Supernatural Endowments tend to be in the Conspiracies’ wheelhouse, and temporal Endowments tend to be something Compacts have; but go with what makes sense rather than holding fast to these associations: as The King's Raven has mentioned, Les Mysteres makes more sense as a Compact despite being built around a supernatural Endowment; and the Loyalists of Thule have always struck me as being a global conspiracy despite not technically having a supernatural Endowment*. Heck, I'd have no problem with an individual hunter who is sufficiently religiously motivated having the Prayer Endowment even if she has never heard of, or is fundamentally opposed to, the Long Night, or for an Ascended One to lack Elixirs (either because he hasn't developed them yet or because he's more interested in the uses of pharmacy).

                                                            Again, tendencies, not mandates.

                                                            * Though technically, their heightened access to magical rituals such as found in Second Sight, Witch Hunters, or Summoners could be thought of as a supernatural Endowment every bit as much as the Aegis Kai Doru’s Relics.
