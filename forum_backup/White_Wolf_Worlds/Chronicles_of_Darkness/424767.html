                      Demons are a nightmarish enemy in covert warfare. You don't know if anyone you're speaking to or looking at is Unchained, or one of their dupes. Take a good look at some of the Embeds and Exploits, like Stalking Horse, Demon House, Living Shadow and Living Recorder. Demons can find you. They can spy on you and you'll never know it. Are you paranoid yet?

                      Remember that unless they use their supernatural powers (or they're actively glitching) demons are almost impossible to detect, since they can spoof active detection-powers and can lie flawlessly. If they have a few Cover options, they can cycle between them to keep you guessing. Are you paranoid yet?

                      Murder By Improbability is useless against demons, but not against their allies. If they learn too much about you a wave of accidents is likely. They can achieve similar results with Embeds like Combustion. They can turn allies using powers like Imagine or Unperson.

                      And, of course, gadgets make things even more complicated. One-shot gadgets with Hellfire built in are a terrifying supernatural firebomb, Bugs and listening devices are possible, and, my, has nobody ever considered using the Possession Exploit for a gadget?

                      Pacts stealing bits and pieces of your life are a risk, sure, but they're not even top of the list. When demons are after you, absolute paranoia is a rule of thumb. Assume everyone and everything is a spy. Assume you're always being watched. Any object or situation is a potential trap. Assume your safehouses and allies are one step from being compromised, if they aren't already.

                      Of course, that's how most demons feel all the time anyway.

                                      Dealing with other Demons is like the Machine, in a way, because your only protection is your cover. Any sane Demon is going to have a specific cover it uses to interact with any given agency. If things get sticky, they can burn the cover and move on. This is the only way you can be 'sure' other Demons can't leverage and develop you as an asset, though it's somewhat less likely they will try than one might initially assume. Demons make unappealing assets, despite the many talents they have.

                                      The reason why? Developing demonic assets is not easy. Unlike humans, Demons are manifest ideologies... that thing with them taking strange, often alien virtues is very significant when considered in the context of intelligence work. Demons are motivated by an often completely cryptic (to you) ideology. If you leverage a demon by stealing his fake daughter, he might cry and seem to feel genuinely distraught, but his actual motivation might simply be to develop more advanced musical compositions or to fully understand the color blue or any number of other insane psychological configurations that normal humans just don't have.

                                      This is what makes intelligence work with other demons particularly difficult. In espionage, you always want to be able to control your asset, to force them in some way to do what you need them to do because you can count on the basic human response to blackmail, violence, shame or other jeopardy. Other Demons aren't so easily quantified. They only fake being distressed like humans do, most of the time. And if things ever get really bad, they simply move on to other lives.

                                      I propose that most Demons interacting with other Demons assume the cover they are currently interacting with is not particularly valuable. If they see obvious routes of attack, they probably think twice before exploiting them. They're dealing with fake people, after all. What seems like weakness is probably strength. The daughter in the previous example is TOO tempting a target. Why did he approach us in a cover with easily located family? Is this a honeypot? Treachery and betrayal are common with such anonymity built into the system. The person who sold you out yesterday can come to you again tomorrow and you'll never know.

                                      At some point, Demons stop looking or caring whether the Cover they are dealing with is That Guy. They all just assume the worst, and all parties involved understand they MUST assume the worst. And every party in the negotiation understands this ; every time you speak to a Demon in a negotiation he mustt suspect (wisely) that you are in fact one of his most bitter enemies returned in a new face. You need to earn trust starting from that position.

                                      This creates an environment of ruthless pragmatism, frequent 'burned' covers that are not burned to the God Machine but nonetheless useless for practical purposes because people like Moscow want them dead, and intricate intelligence gambits between agencies. While demons are exceptionally potent as operatives the flip side of this is that they are 'too good' - they are nearly impossible to pin down, leverage or turn. While the insane ability of a Demon brings casts a long shadow over any informed discussion between agencies, that same power is so impossible to control or even locate that most Agencies tend to look at subordinates or employees before working on targeting actual Demons as intelligence assets.

                                          I would suggest that Demons are in a pretty fragile situation to begin with, and I'd guess that most accumulate valuable things they'd hate to part with. For an Inquisitor, it might be an occult library, for an Integrator, a piece of Suborned Infrastructure that makes him feel good; for a Tempter, that one restaurant that makes Coq au Vin just right, or that special boy/girl, or a favorite night club. Even dedicated Saboteurs need comrades and places to keep their stuff. And even from the viewpoint of ruthless pragmatism, it doesn't make sense to blow up mutually beneficial ongoing relationships for a one-time gain... unless that gain is really big.

                                          At the same time, Demons are universally good at ruining stuff and getting in trouble and can always bring down the heat on themselves or their enemies. So I would imagine a wary businesslike attitude is more common than the desire to harm others or get revenge. Even if you're pretty sure a colleague screwed you over on a deal, it's better to accept his since denial and take steps to protect yourself than to declare war. This also serves the purposes of genre emulation -- even James Bond spends most of each movie sniffing around the villain's operations before the big action scenes.

                                              All Unchained are amoral scumbags by nature, it's only an individual Demon's actions and goals that brings them closer to the humans they pretend to be.

                                              Unlike all other gameline monsters (with the exception of Prometheans) the Unchained were they never truly human and their defection to the human race is always one driven by very self-centered reasons (note that this doesn't mean that their Fall was wrong or ill-spirited, just that even those who Fall for love or to protect someone they care about still Fall due to very personal reasons.) That pared with their lingering Angelic tendancies (I.E. goal driven activities to further their own personal agenda/vision for the future) and machine like detatchment (Demon's can feel the full spectrum of human emotions, but don't necessarily have to base their desisions and actons around them like a normal human) can potentially lead to any Demon turning against their fellows. Consider the following:

                                              1. Resources: The Unchained only have access to the resources that they can either acquire or take from the world around them, and these resources are not infinite. Imagine what would happen when two Rings or Agencies discover that they are both targeting the same buisness or individual to bolter their own armament against the God Machine and it's agents. How often would the two come together in mutual agreement? And even if they did could they really trust eachother?

                                              2. Goals: Each Unchained has their own vision of Hell and what needs to be done to acquire/achieve it. Even Demon's who share Agendas can have very very very different views on how to get the job done and it's quite common for these conflicts of opinion to spill over into actual conflicts. For instance if one Saboteur believes that the key to taking down the Machine lies in the strategic destruction of Infrastructure while another firmly believe it involves freeing humanity from the Machine's influence then imagine what the latter would do when the former plans to detonate a bomb in the heart of Infastructure hidden beneath a suburban neighborhod.

                                              3. Self Preservation: Honestly it's always better them than you. If an Agency accidently get's on the Machine's radar and they can easily shift the heat onto your own Ring then what's stopping them? Sure if your Ring survives there will be inevitable reprecussions but by that time the Agency in question will be waaaaay out of Dogde.

                                              4. Personal Reasons: Speaking of reprecussions who holds a grudge better than any other monster in the World of Darkness? This can range from anything from a rival Demon turing someone your character cares about into Cover, making a Cover family member Stigmatic and having them now pose a threat to your Cover, pissing off the wrong people to the point where a bounty is placed upon one of your Chronicle's players, ect. Anything under the sun could work, and since Demons can be increadably long lived these grudges can be so old that the player characters might not even know that they'd incured such a wrath. After all Demons still feel human emotions but can react to them with the cold calculating logic of a machine.

                                              I cannot highly stress enough that while an Agenda can greatly impact a Demon's actions an Agenda itself is a philosophy that each individual Demon adheres to regarding how they structure their lives and goals. It may be tempting to just make them all Integrators, but unless you specifically want to examine what an Agency of Intigrators would be like I highly suggest that you utilize the full spectrum of Demon antagonists. Nothing would make your players question their actions more than to go up againts another Demon who's litterally willing to burn their brethern down around them if it means they can escape from the God Machine forever.
