    Conspiracies as 'evil'/antagonists factions

    11-01-2022, 10:45 PM
    I run Scion and I'm using a bit of CoD stuff to fill up the game World, without using gods everywhere ( we play on low Myth Levels ). Whatever, it's a bit gonzoo, globtrotting chronicle in first place. 😎

    On start of the game, on Origin tier - which would probably by Tier 2 or 3 of HtV - I introduced Loyalists of Thule as literal (mild) neonazis evil cult, dedicated to finding the relics and using magic for themselves.

    As we went to the full Scion: Hero tier - and I mean here stuff what would be Tier 4 in HtV, I suppose 🤔- like tries to Rising Atlantis again, discovery and destruction of the Reptilian Uber Conspiracy inside other cults,worldwide - I still need to fill the game world more, with globetrotting pesky players. 😎

    And so, I return for HtV for help. As I need a bit colourful local groups and factions, I thought 'wait, are not Hunter's Conspiracies already VERY gray groups - with some stoping on Black Hats areas'? And so, I return to you my fellow forumers, to point me how Conspiracies can really be BASTARDS. 😎

    As we are now in Egypt, I think about using Ascending Ones as at least antagonists to PCs, maybe working with evil Loyalists of Thule.

    Any ideas on 'evil' Cult of Phoenix? Return of Cult of Set maybe to much on the nose?     ( Beside that in Scion, Set is not 'evil' god - rather God of Chaos, like Loki for Norse. And true antagonists for general chronicle are Great Wyrms/Sepents/Dragons of Apep/Apothis, Bolla & Jormungandr. )

                  I feel like this thread would be better served by the Scion forum, but anyways.

                  If we're going to discuss the Ascending Ones as antagonists for low scale divine entities, it perhaps pays to remember the roots of who and how they are and keep that in context of Scion.

                  To this end, it's worth remembering that the Cult of the Phoenix instilled order where the Cult of Set instilled chaos-and those have more specific context than the modern english would bely. Effectively, the Cult of Set worked to remove the influence of the divine from Egypt, pruning where the gods influence was running rampant. By contrast, the Cult of the Phoenix cultivated the presence of the divine where they felt it was best to grow it up. The later Ascending Ones emphasis on diplomacy ala sulha is an extension of that-by having a recognized role as effective and reliable mediators in the supernatural world, the Ascending Ones has all the more influence in rooting and growing supernatural influences where they are.

                  In the context of Scion, this makes the cult on the whole a presumptuous manipulator of the Pantheons, seeking to move the powers of the gods to the benefit and intent of humanity-a position made all the more annoying because they are useful being a party that can reliably mediate between conflict between Pantheons, particularly with their long relationships with some of them (I would list the whole list of likely old relationships, but history proves a disbelief I don't want to wrestle with.) THe Ascending Ones are more a political adversary than a direct militant one-not that they don't punch, but they're more adept at manipulating the egos in play to producing things in their favor, to the detriment of people who still otherwise have reaons to rely on them. When they do punch, it's with a couple of other pantheons at their back and all the image of legitimacy.

                  Into the specific factions:

                  The Knife of Paradise is easy to get some ready ideas for how to work in Scion, since Scion has explored in 1E and 2E the idea of monotheistic cults and what they do and operate, and it pays to go ahead and beg, steal, and borrow from those for reconsidering them. With that said, it's worth noting that they are the active diplomats of the three factions in the Ascending ones, alongside their militarism. Of the three, they're the most likely to try and cultivate a single god to the consumption or all others, but that's contrasted by their willingness to be problem reconciliation between groups. This of course also makes it easy for them to play a scene into legitimate action that leads to robbing pantheons of valuable resources on the ground.

                  The Order of the Southern Temple are probably not as ambitious as their militant peers, but that's because they've got the oldest and most comfortable relationships with the Theoi, the Netjer, and the Yazata, and has earned a lot in response to their related roles to colonialism. THey've got resources, influence, and sorcery, and have traditions on poking and prodding some of the most widespread Pantheons in the world into action that shapes them for their interest. They've got deep pockets, know what the mysteries are and where the secrets are kept, and are happy to build power for power's sake-not that other ambitions can't pay out.

                  The Jagged Crescent, in Hunter being the First Response, Financial scythe, and R&D of the Ascending ones, are perhaps the most straightforward of the bunch for Scion-they're the ones who find the opportunities, both in places to cultivate for their benefit and in incidents that allow for the Ascending Ones to use conflict as a way of controlling the divine. They're also provacateurs in their own way, since the drug trade and community involvement can easily be a ground for manipulating Scions and other agents into making unwise actions that get them stepping on toes they shouldn't be stepping on. And, of course, if shit comes to blows, they're also the most unpredictable of enemies.

                  So yeah, in the context of Scion, the Ascending Ones mostly do well as a political opponent, taking advantage of conflict (or even engineering them) to get Pantheons working against their own best interest in an effort to buil their own power and reshape the world to their own image, all while working ot maintain a position of legitimacy reliability that keeps them in the game. While they can punch hard, they're more dangerous in getting pantheons to punch each other and giving them some edges.
