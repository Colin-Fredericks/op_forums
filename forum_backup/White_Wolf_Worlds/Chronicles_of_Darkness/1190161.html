    Old West/Civil War Firearms for Chronicles of Darkness

    02-28-2018, 02:41 AM
    Hello everyone,

    This is kind of an odd first post, but I registered here with the express purpose of sharing some content that I've been working on for my current game, a CoD/Hunter game set in New Mexico in 1876. I love the combination of horror and Western themes, and I set out on this campaign with fond memories of the old Savage West Werewolf game, but wanting to do something more in line with the Chronicles of Darkness "feel" and new crunch. I set out, merrily, to find what other players had created in terms of nineteenth-century content and rules, and discovered... nothing. I'm sure other people have tried this before, but if they have, I couldn't find it. The campaign itself is running and running well, but I've had to do a tremendous amount of work on making the content (particularly items) "setting-friendly." I share it here (starting with this content) in the hopes that someone else wanting to do the same might find it useful.

    What follows is my adaptation of about seventy-five years' worth of firearm designs into the CoD system. I based the original stats on the only examples that exist in the nWoD rules, i.e. the Winchester Repeater and the Colt Single Action Army (better known as the Peacemaker), and then extrapolated, deduced, researched, and invented, converting to CoD by reducing all damages by one from the Armory equivalent. Wherever possible, details are real-wrold accurate; several days were spent poring over various databases of firearm history. All of the manufacturers and models are real. Liberty was taken only to fill in a gap I couldn't fill by research. You may note that a number of special properties have been invented to simulate various quirks of nineteenth-century firearms; these are only suggestions, but my own playtesting has proved they work well to differentiate otherwise-similar models.

    Although it is not standard in CoD, I allow my players to take the Fighting Styles: Combat Marksmanship Merit up to the third dot (but not up to five; multiple attacks are way too OP) to simulate burst autofire with a semi-automatic. For a single-action revolver, this represents the Western art of "fanning," keeping the trigger depressed and manually rapid-firing the revolver by running one's hand rapidly over the hammer and allowing it to snap back, and therefore requires a free hand; for a double-action revolver, it may be done with a revolver in both hands and therefore extend the burst to medium. This is the only benefit to having two guns besides not having to reload, which, given the long reloading times on a cap-and-ball revolver, is very useful if combat lasts longer than five or six turns (although, to be fair, in WoD this is not a very common occurrence).

    One final note: I have included base prices in 1876 dollars, as I chose to use money instead of straight Resources dots in my campaign to suggest the "living from dollar to dollar" feel of old outlaw Westerns. It would be simple enough to convert these; I'd say up to $50 is one dot, $100 two, and $150 three.

    With all those caveats, I welcome your thoughts and suggestions, and hope this is of use to some or any of you. God knows I've spent way too much time creating it.

    ***Special Rules***
    *Percussion cap Takes one turn to reload each chamber (otherwise can load Composure + 1 per turn)
    *Flintlock Have the same rules as *Percussion cap and are always *Unreliable
    *Cartridge conversion Any percussion cap revolver can be converted to use cartridges at a cost of $20, or performed yourself with an extended Crafts roll and appropriate tools
    *Can take scope Can be mounted with a scope under rules for simple scopes
    *Bolt-action No mechanical difference
    *Unreliable 1s remove an equivalent number of successes
    *Volatile If more 1s than successes, the gun misfires (usually damaging it)
    *Underpowered Do not reroll 10s
    *Backfire If more 1s than successes, holder takes 1 damage
    *Rare Requires extended rolls to find.
    *Not commercially available Cannot be obtained through purchase
    *Double action Self-cocks after the first shot. Can be used with Rapid Shot one-handed.
    *Holdout shotgun Requires STR 5, -5/-10/-15 to Range, and is *Unreliable
    *Slug Default shotgun ammunition.
    *Buckshot +1 damage at short range, only takes half range penalties for medium and long
    *Birdshot All ranges halved and -1 to damage. All armor ratings of those struck tripled. Any target in physical contact with target suffers half damage, rounding down.
    *Sights Due to gun weights and structure, any weapon mounted with a scope that does not have "Can take scope" receives only half the bonus they ordinarily would, rounding down.
    *Weatherprone Do not function in poor weather.

              In terms of balance, the pistols should be okay. The most powerful of them is less powerful than many of the options available in the Armory or elsewhere; the only real question for me is whether the biggest shotguns aren't a bit OP. 4 (9-again), although equivalent to the top-tier shotgun damage in nWoD, is slightly high for CoD. If anything, this is all slightly underpowered, which is fine, because that's what I'm going for. The difference between modern Hunters trying to take down a raging werewolf with a Desert Eagle opposed to a Pinkerton Special Agent (my campaign) carrying only a five-shot popgun that effectively can't be reloaded in combat leads to a scrappy, desperate feel that encourages strategizing over confrontation and makes the decision to engage in combat that much more frightening. So far my group has only successfully driven off a Beast of Bethlehem, and despite "winning," it was a bloodbath.

              If anyone uses these, please please let me know what you find!

                          Nice list! If anything, I'd say some of the guns are under-powered. 2 damage for a .69 caliber musket? Should probably be at least a 3, they had some decent energy (though of course they dropped off fast >_>). 9 agains really arn't that powerful, especially with GMC attacking rules. Some dude throwing around 10 dice would average around 3.33 successes with 10 again, and 3.74 success with a 9 again. Ironically, it means that the guns with a 9 again are slightly more accurate under GMC rules than a modern gun would be. Since the damage from these guns is added instead of adding dice like in 1E, it doesn't work like a skill check where adding a dice only adds ~3/10 chance for a success. Its basically an automatic success that only lands if you hit the attack, so a +1 damage is worth ~3 dice assuming the attack would hit anyway.

                          I'd suggest you shave off the 9agains, and probably kick the damage up/down instead. They don't really work great as a half-way point, and they act kinda funky with the new rules.

                          ~Edit~
                          For clarification, I don't mean "more accurate" as in "you will miss less often", since you need a success for the status to take effect. But if you got in a good-old fashioned shooting competition, the guy with a 9 again would have the edge :P
                          It adds: 0.04/0.08/0.12 etc. ~0.04*n damage, with n being the number of dice you are trowing around. Some guy with 10 dice will get ~.4 extra damage on average, 40% of the 1 damage you would get from just bumping it up. Works good as long as your characters are all expert gunslingers in good conditions, falls apart the less dice you use.
