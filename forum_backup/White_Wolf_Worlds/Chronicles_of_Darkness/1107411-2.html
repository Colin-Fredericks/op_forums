                        Fair enough. But, here are my estimates for who wants to keep humans around:

                        99% of vampires (which I believe are the most common supernaturals, and need humans)
                        15% of all werewolves (half the ones who do what they're supposed to)
                        50% of prometheans (they want to be human)
                        80% of sin-eaters (they were human, and still are very similar)
                        15% of mages (being cynical here, it's almost certainly more)
                        80% of demons (as a whole, they have strong connections with humans, and need them to hide from the god-machine)
                        50% of beasts (they generally need humans)
                        15% of changelings (they also need humans, to a lesser degree, and like them. Although a lot probably wouldn't care.)
                        5% of mummies (dunno much about them, but I don't think they would care a lot either way)

                        Even with all that, that's 45.44% of all supernaturals (excluding antagonists, template merits, and hunters). It's not the majority, but this also isn't taking into account the fact that things like mummies are way less plentiful than things like vampires, so their 5% doesn't matter nearly as much as the vampire's 99% (aside from the fact that mummies are more powerful).

                        Sure, they might not want to keep humanity as a whole around. But keeping their touchstones and families safe? I think that would be enough for them to at least stand out of the fight.

                        Not all of them would, sure. But enough supernaturals are going to care enough about people that they'll at least take a neutral stance in a fight between the two. All supernaturals (with the possible exception of mummies, I don't know a lot about them), have a significant portion of their population who at least has a human friend they'll fight to protect, if not actively protecting humans as a whole.
