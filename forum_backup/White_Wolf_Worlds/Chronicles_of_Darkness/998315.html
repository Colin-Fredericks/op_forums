    11-04-2016, 01:59 PM

    Werewolves and Sin-Eaters probably have it easy, but Vampires, Mummies, and Prometheans probably are a bit harder to pull off. Either way - discuss.

                                                          Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                                          The complications you were envisioning. And how you'd envision such groups abusing their authority.
                                                          How I envision them abusing their authority is a laundry list, really. In theory, the noble cause of the Ladder would mean that police resources would be turned against the Seers and their witting and unwitting servants. This means putting police officers on investigations into things that weaken the Ladder's works and taking them off of things that strengthen it. For example, if a group of Seer minions are stockpiling guns and ammo, you can bet the Ladder is throwing police at them non-stop. On the other hand, Arrow gun runners might just be given a pass on their less-than-legal moving of firearms, so long as they put them to use serving the Ladder's purposes. If there's a corrupt politician working for the Seers, have their accounts investigated. If they work for you, have the police delay long enough for you to wipe out the evidence.

                                                          But that's the pure ideals. In practice, the Ladder would probably abuse the hell out of this for personal goals as much as idealistic ones. Have the police overlook your purchases of psychotropic drugs for your cult. If someone dies in the pursuit of a Mystery (a terrible accident, to be sure, and not at all a result of your gross negligence), get it ruled a death by misadventure. Hell, get a speeding ticket thrown out because you were chasing a werewolf down the I-80.

                                                          The leeway granted police is fairly substantial.

                                                          The only problem is when competing factions start warring for control. That can get messy.

                                                          It's actually a surprisingly simple thing for a Mage to ruin a mortal's career. When the mandatory police physical shows up, cast a spell to put cocaine in their blood stream and stick a kilo of Colombian marching powder in their sock drawer. The rest of the investigation doesn't need to hold up or turn up anything because the initial evidence is so damning and the subsequent evidence so confirming that no-one will question anything else.

                                                          But... Once you do that, the door is open. Anyone else can do the exact same thing to your candidate. A Seer drugs your candidate and puts forward a fascist thug of a police chief as a replacement. Luckily, fascists tend to have their vices so you can probably get them knocked out of their job pretty easily just by following them around enough. Put the video on Youtube and send it to a few news channels and there is not a hope in hell of the enemy stopping it; by the time they even know about it, the damage is likely done.

                                                          After that, though, it's your turn to put forth a candidate and suddenly you're both competing to put someone in power. Once that happens, there's only three possible outcomes:

                                                          1. Someone blinks, loses their nerve, and surrenders the contest.
                                                          2. Negotiation. Each party finds a candidate the other accepts and an accord is made; either one party controls the institution in exchange for an oath to the other not to use it on them (and a guarantee of resources, like grimoires, tass, etc.), or both find someone they can live with, step aside, and let mortal politics be untouched. That really means, though, that each just has to be more circumspect in their influence.
                                                          3. War. The cabals engage in direct combat. This is not going to be a pretty affair but it will be decisive, because the losers are either dead or have fled the coop. In the meantime, the organization is left to its own devices or radically destabilized by feuding sorcerers.
