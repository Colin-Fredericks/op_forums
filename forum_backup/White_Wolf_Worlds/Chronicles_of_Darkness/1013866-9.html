                                Originally posted by Diggs View Post
                                How is bigger being defined?
                                Narratively. A light pistol is a gun that serves the roll of small, easily concealed handgun. A heavy pistol is a gun that serves the role of big, high-powered hand cannon.

                                Originally posted by Diggs View Post
                                A .357 magnum is 9 mm. I can understand separated the two into different classes based on energy. Also, a .45 has been considered a heavy pistol for as long as the caliber has existed.
                                If you think a particular model is better suited to one category than the other, go ahead and run it that way. The examples are just that. Examples.

                                Originally posted by Diggs View Post
                                There is no benefit to choosing a light pistol over medium.
                                That's not true at all. Light pistols are size 1 to heavy pistols' size 2, meaning they can be used with only one hand and concealed more easily. Light pistols have a Strength requirement of 2 to heavy pistols' Strength 3, so more characters can wield one without penalty. Light pistols have an Initiative modifier of 0 to heavy pistols' Initiative -2, giving the light pistol user a better chance of acting first (and with the Beaten Down and Surrender rules, potentially ending the fight before the opponent even has a chance to use their higher damage mod weapon) than the heavy pistol user. Finally, a light pistoly may be more likely to have fewer restrictions on legal ownership and carry laws than a heavy pistol, depending on what weapons you decide fall into which category.

                                Originally posted by Diggs View Post
                                Even heavy pistols have compact versions for easier concealment. I think a similar discussion occurred during development and more categories of firearms was considered to granular.
                                I mean, Chronicles' weapon system doesn't have a ton of room for granularity. If you want to make a more granular system to use in your home games, have at it.

                                            Originally posted by HelmsDerp View Post
                                            So as previously mentioned I really dig style tags, but they have one annoying downside, namely that they don't get used in other books and we're left to make our own rulings on styles not in HL going forward.
                                            Maybe? I mean, hells. Hurt Locker just came out. We don't know if there will be any tags in future books. Changeling may very well add a Supernatural tag to Elemental Battle for all we know.

                                            Look at Secrets of the Covenant - they added in Themes just to make it compatable with that book if people wanted. Future books might do the similar for Tags.

                                            Plus, I think most Fighting Merits out there are pretty self explanatory. The core book has been covered by Hurt Locker. Mage's fighting merits all rely on supernatural powers. (thus get the Supernatural tag). The new Fighting Styles in Secrets of the Covenant are all based on having organization training - that's pretty much the definition of Tactical tags; some, like Crusader, need more tags, but that's described fairly clearly in the merit itself. The only fighting merit in Changling is clearly Supernatural. Werewolf fighting merits are all clearly Supernatural (they have Renown / Primal Urge requirements) save Relentless Assault; the latter is mentioned in Hurt Locker under Attitude. Nightmare Fist (or whatever its called) is clearly supernatural. As far as I can remember, there are no fighting merits in DtD that aren't elsewhere. Lots of Demon Form merits, lots of combat Embeds/Exploits, but no merits that apply to combat directly to combat. Vampire's Kindred Dueling is Light Melee, and Frenzy is, well, Supernatural and Attitude (Enraged). Most of these are found in the actual description of the fighting merits, or the prerequisites.

                                            Tags don't really do anything but create a short hand for what can or cannot stack instead of relying entirely on explicitly stating it in the merit itself. Most pre=HL merits are pretty self-explanatory because they had to state it in their write up.

                                                Originally posted by Professor Phobos View Post
                                                Well, granularity limits being what they are, I just figure 9mm pistols should count as heavy, then, and the example light pistol should change.
                                                I consider 9mm pistols light but the ammo optimized so they are roughly equivalent to a heavy pistol when it comes to damage. You can definitely change examples to whatever you like. Alternatively, you could keep a 9mm as a light pistol and boost the damage up to that of a heavy pistol when the appropriate ammo is used. Then again 1 damage might not be worth worrying about it. I presume the intent was to suggest that a 9mm pistol is typically smaller and lighter so would be easier to use, lower strength requirement and initiative penalty. Depending on what you consider important you can place it in either category. Maybe tweak it the way you want using light and heavy as guidelines for the range that covers common pistols.

                                                Tweaking seems to have been expected for those with the knowledge to do so with the examples made for those lacking. Examples, in this case especially, are suggestions not listed as such to save space. You've got it figured out, so go with your best judgment and you won't run a problem.

                                                I'm not trying to speak for authors. I've read what they had to say and expressing my opinion of their intentions.
