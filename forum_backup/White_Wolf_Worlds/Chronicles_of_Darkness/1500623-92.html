        Originally posted by quelsec View Post
        Do you use reach to broaden a spell in creative taumathurgy? if so how?
        Only 'sort of.' Spells are, by default, cast at touch Range, only effect one human or thing, with Potency 1, and last 1 turn.

        Potency, Scale, Range, and Duration are the spell factors. They can go up by just taking a penalty during the casting. So, a spell that lasts one turn can take a -4 hit to the roll to successfully cast the spell to last three turns instead of one..

        You use Reach to shift them from regular casting limits. Like the spell mentioned above, the Default is One turn, but the Advanced Default (Advanced is achieved by using reach) is one scene. If used with the other example, where you use Reach but still take a -4, if cast correctly, the spell now has a Duration of one week.

        The charts for the different spell factors are around page 113. Here's the chart for Duration:
        https://imgur.com/a/aKyBu2n

                    Originally posted by quelsec View Post

                    For example the spell from the main book Deepen Shadows with 1 reach allows to make full darkness and that is beyond the power of a 1 lvl arkanum. So that is what i mean by broaden, extend the power above what the arcanum dot should be able to do.
                    Okay, so for your particular example... that spell isn't creating darkness out of nothing, it's affecting the distribution of ambient shadows. The Reach effect doesn't change that, it just modifies the extent of the penalty it imposes. They're both Compelling effects in so far as they pull and direct existing darkness, just one is more... precise.

                    Now, maybe you disagree with the logic or mechanical expression of that, but it would probably match the intent of the writers. Reach effects are not intended to grow beyond the capabilities of the Practice, they just apply the effect of the spell with more precision or severity.

                    So as far as creative thaumaturgy goes, I see two points of relevance. One is that a person can certainly have the point that their custom spells can have such Reach effects, and indeed probably ought to (which I would approach from the angle of "consider the strongest applications of the spell effect and whether they ought to be put behind a Reach cost". The other is whether creative thaumaturgy can devise additional Reach effects for written spells. I would say yes if it sounds like it makes sense, but would caution against the impulse to make too many additional things rest on single spells. Indeed, I would imagine that a lot of things a person might think of as a Reach at first would actually be fine just being there own spells, with the proposed effect as the baseline.
