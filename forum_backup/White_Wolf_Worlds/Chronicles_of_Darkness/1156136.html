    True Human Conditions as Game Options

    11-05-2017, 03:56 PM
    I had this idea for quite some time, since I graduated on psychology. Fiction is ripe with oversimplifications and general ignorance on mental conditions, disabled people and the like. Of course, they have no reason to know better, and their goal usually isn't to portray things accurately, nor should.

    But on the other side, to be honest, they miss a lot of cool stuff. It isn't that fiction should be accurate, but that sometimes an accurate portrayal of things is simply full of interesting elements for a story. It adds more options, developments, and can be really fun stuff to work with.

    Recently I found out, after more than 30 years, that I'm Autistic. That rekindled my passion for researching this stuff, with obvious emphasis on my own condition, and I finally decided to put this idea to work.

    The premise is simple. It is to create Conditions, Merits, Tilts and whatnot to better represent some true conditions, disabilities and diseases. Create as much as you want, and comment on the work of others. The following rules are encouraged, but must not be strictly followed:

    1- Avoid House-Rules: the idea is to give players and STs the tools to implement those things without changing the game, nor demanding more action from other players (like learning new rules). Ideally everyone in the game can continue to use common assumptions if they like, no one should be forced to learn to be correct;

    2- Know It Firsthand: everyone can comment, criticize and offer input on other's works. But ideally the first to present a new material should do so about its own personal IRL condition. In any case, it would be good to talk about what is your relation with the condition in question (you have it, you live with someone who have, you work with it at your job, and so on);

    3- Offer More, Not Less: this is actually a Design Thinking technique. If you think something isn't good, offer a better option. Give ideas. To call out the idea of others usually contributes very little to the task at hand. Instead of trying to cut out what you don't agree, add something you do agree. We can always do the cut off on later stages, in this case at our tables.

    #NothingAboutUsWithoutUs

      Autism through Conditions:
      Hiper-Sensitivity (Persistent Condition)
      Your senses are acutely alarmed by some stimuli that are completely bearable by anyone else. You can choose beforehand what are some of those stimuli, but can also decide during play that a new stimulus triggers it. More rarely, you can drop a stimulus from the list or add a stimulus that you didn't had special sensitivity to before.
      A character can have both Hiper-Sensitivity and Hipo-Sensitivity Conditions, but not for the same specific stimuli.
      Beat: whenever you're exposed to the stimulus, you can choose between getting the Overload Condition, or to immediately shut down the sensation (covering your ears, shutting your eyes, even vomiting, yelling or seeking a "louder" stimulus) until it otherwise ceases. If you do the later, you get a Beat.

      Hipo-Sensitivity (Persistent Condition)
      Your senses yearn for some kinds of stimulus more than anyone else. You can choose beforehand what are some of those stimuli, but can also decide during play that a new stimulus is needed. More rarely, you can drop a stimulus from the list or add a stimulus that you didn't had special yearn to before.
      A character can have both Hiper-Sensitivity and Hipo-Sensitivity Conditions, but not for the same specific stimuli.
      Beat: whenever you go too much time without the stimulus you need, you can choose between getting the Overload Condition, or to immediately seek down the sensation (running to strong lights, poking yourself, even hurting, yelling or other sudden and odd behavior) until it is satisfied. If you do the later in an inappropriate way, you get a Beat.

      Stimming (Persistent Condition)
      Whenever you have energy to spend, be it for a good mood or stress, your body wants to do the same kind of stuff. It is a fairly odd and repetitive behavior, like flapping your hands, picking your skin, walking around aimlessly, or simply turning around for several minutes at a time. The degree of control over such behavior and the variety of distinct stims a character posses vary greatly and isn’t relevant for game purposes.
      Beat: whenever you feels like it, you can indulge your stim. If you can’t for any reason, stress builds up and you get the Overload Condition. If you do indulge and it isn’t appropriate at the time for any reason, you get a Beat.

      Impaired Communication (Persistent Condition)
      You have poor, if any, grasp of facial expression, voice modulation, irony, sarcasm or body language, making both hard for you to understand others and to make yourself understood. Poor control of your own body can even make it impossible to communicate in some ways, even if your understanding isn’t much impaired, impeding verbalization some or all the time. You can choose alternatives for communication if you want, but they will depend upon the others being able to interpret you.
      Beat: you can choose to fail any social roll before rolling dice. If you do so, you get a Beat. If you roll but fails anyway, it is a Dramatic Failure regardless of choice, and you get a Beat. Even without a roll, you can choose to wrongly interpret interactions and call for a Dramatic Failure, taking a Beat. If you fail to talk to someone completely when it should be done without problem by a normal person, you take a Beat.

      Overload (Condition)
      Your mind was overwhelmed, and it is hard to function now. Everything seems confusing, your senses don’t work properly, your stress level is nearing the threshold. You get a penalty of -1 in actions as if it was a pain penalty. Also, you have to roll Resolve + Composure with this penalty each round to avoid this penalty increasing, and any new instance of this Condition increases this penalty instantly.
      Shedding: you can shed this Condition by spending at least a scene in sensorial deprivation. For this, you must seek a place without any strong stimulus to any sense at all, nor emotional or mental stimulus. If you lack such option, you can shed this Condition by acquiring the Meltdown or Shutdown Conditions.

      Shutdown (Condition)
      You shut yourself from the environment. You just stop. You can stand, sit or lay down, and you can walk if guided, but nothing else. If you stay that way for too much time you can go back to your activities with far less energy, and every dice pool is reduced as if you had only 1 at the relevant Attribute.
      Shedding: you can shed this Condition in the same way as Overload, but through much more time. You make Resolve + Composure rolls each hour, starting with both reduced to 1. Each successful roll lets you recover 1 of them to its normal rating. A Dramatic Failure makes the time between rolls increase to 1 day, then 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, and finally a year.
      At any time you can choose to shed this Condition without any more rolls, but this is a Breaking Point.

      Meltdown (Condition)
      You shut yourself from the environment, but your body fights to get rid of stressful stimuli. You will act at your full physical capacity to get rid of the sources of stress, flee the scene, or fight anyone trying to hold you.
      Shedding: you can shed this Condition in the same way as Overload, and in fact you will try your best to do so.
      At any time you can choose to shed this Condition instantly, but this is a Breaking Point.

      Autistic Burnout (Persistent Condition)
      Add this to the list of options for Dramatic Failure in Breaking Points. It works as and stacks with Overload in every regard, but the rolls for increasing penalty are daily, and gaining a Beat through the “Shedding” of it only resets the penalty to -1.
