                Some random ideas:
                • There's some obvious parallels between soldiers coming home with PTSD and Changelings returning form Arcadia to a home where they no longer belong. But they're so strong that you might wonder if you need Arcadia and all things fae at all?
                • You could probably have some Geniuses hanging around the weapons labs, though it would probably work best if you set the game a bit after the war with a bunch of scientists realising that for all their brilliance they were mere pawns in the game of empires and the work did nothing to help, and much to harm, mankind. The game is about how they find closure (or how they fail to find closure).
                • For Hunter: Definitely a time for occult Conspiracies to collapse, or be weakened before WWII finished them off. Groups like America's Freemasons or Victorian occult societies are starting give way to more technological Conspiracies like VALKYRIE. Conspiracies actually fighting in the war feels more WWII than WWI though.
                • You could do a weird but potentially very interesting spin on Deviant with draft dodging, the white feather campaign, and basically replacing Deviant's overt active conspiracy with more subtle omnipresent pressure for society. Or maybe this will work better for Demon, with Covers being civilian identities with good reasons not to get shipped off to the front lines and the government replacing the God Machine.
                • Hunter again: Who takes over the vigil when just about everyone who knows how to fight is a continent away on the front lines?

                                            Originally posted by Menace View Post
                                            Where would you place a Mage setting during WW1? Seems like an interesting time, the Free Council hasn't yet reached adulthood and the Arrow hadn't yet abandonned championing of Sleeper causes, so they though against each other on both sides.
                                            What CInder saif about Italy:
                                            Originally posted by Cinder View Post
                                            Also, in Italy you have poets that convince the population to agree going to war with over the top gestures and general patriotic exaltation. A pretty wild moment that will lead to all that idealism to crush against the realities of war. The whole country was not prepared, technologically and culturally, to fight any kind of war against the other big powers of Europe: WW1 put Italy through a crucible that, on one hand, unified the population but, on the other, lead to...certain bad things a few years later.
                                            ... makes me think of Mage. This idealised sense of patriotism and glory vs the grimmer reality sounds like a possible to mirror to the Supernal and the Fallen. Perhaps an early faction of the Free Council supports the war - even in the face of how terrible it is - because they think such a crucible will provoke more Awakenings.

                                            Beast: One of the most common complains, discussions, and hacks about Beast (coming from many different sentiments) is alternate philosophies to that of lessons. WWI is an era where that could be put front and centre.
                                            • At the Front the first and most basic interpretation is what the hell Beasts can possibly have to teach among the trenches?
                                            • Away from the Front, other Beasts may be trying to use the War itself as a lesson: perhaps spreading news about the worst of it, feeding on the reactions, but in the hopes of preventing such a thing happening again; or else they think a big terror some distance away is too much distraction, and people need to be reminded that fear can still find them at home.
                                            • The War consumes every aspect of life in the nations that fight it. Perhaps Beasts who still regard themselves as altered humans are ascendant in this period, regarding the commonality of experience with the people around them as outweighing their supernature. Maybe some use their Lairs to hide people, or for strategic purposes.
                                            • Maybe some Beasts convince themselves that their feedings are ultimately a comfort, even to their victims - a personal, psychic fear providing a refuge away from the cold impersonal fear of industrialised warfare.
                                            • Some would be tempted to say the hell with it. In a world where humans can do that, who cares what Beasts do?
                                            • And what about Heroes? Do any of them doubt the importance of their quests - who cares about monsters when you're facing the world's first tanks? Or do they believe that killing enough Beasts will end the War? Do they regard the War as a tool to be harnessed? Those good Heroes who aren't indiscriminate about hunting Beasts will surely find a lot to do.
                                            • And the Dream itself will be roiliing something fierce. New Chambers, new Horrors, new Insatiables by the truckload.
                                            • And some - some Beasts, Heroes, cultists, or others - will think the War is the Mother feeding for herself...

                                            Overall theme... both Beasts and Heroes, to live themselves, have to work pretty hard at convincing themselves they're right. What happens when the world kicks all that certainty out from under you, and then throws chaos at you to thick and fast to process it.

                                            Deviant: Deviants are defined by having their souls cracked open. Shell-shock - or some attempt at treating it - seems like something that could do that. In the latter case, where it's a treatment gone wrong, the conspiracy could be motivated by getting people back to the meat-grinder - but they could also be motivated by entirely positive ends - they might even want to help the Deviants, though the Deviants could still be forgived for having had enough of their help. Of course the Trenches are a place to hide from any number of people looking to hunt you down, and where scars may help disguise Scars. And the game will have a strong dichotomy of conviction vs loyalty - the applicability of that to a wartime setting is obvious.

                                            Hunter: Any of the losing nations could make a good setting for Hunter after the War. The existing order is knocked over, depriving conspiracies of the support networks - both the larger organisations of hunters and those of the monsters they fight will be uniquely vulnerable. That works best for Tier 3 maybe.
                                            The Front itself is good for Tier 1. To quote Second Chances: "Hunters must march to war while still keeping up the Vigil. Monster hunting is dangerous prospect as it is, but in no-man's land, it's even worse." (It's also a good way to get hunters from different background and groups taken away from their networks and thrown together).
                                            And for Tier 2, to quote The Kings Raven: "Who takes over the vigil when just about everyone who knows how to fight is a continent away on the front lines?" This is a time when new compacts are needed in every nation.
