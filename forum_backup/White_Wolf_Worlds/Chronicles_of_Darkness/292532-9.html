Naturally in an Ordo-centric chronicle it'll be more valuable than not, but that shifting value applies to everything. As for the judgements, others only lose status if they defy your judgements, and the things you are allowed to pass judgement on are very limited. To quote the draft: "She may even determine an exception to normal prohibitions of heresy within the Ordo. For example, she can rule that a character is able to learn the Coil of Ziva (see p. XX), or she might allow non-Ordo Dracul characters to develop Wyrm's Nest Merits (see below)." That's it. That's what you can rule on. You can't say "Give me all your shit or loose status and be murdered!" If you rule that a given Dragon cannot pursue a certain Coil and they are discovered to be doing just that, then they lose Status.

                    I don't buy into that every merit with a benefit needs a drawback, but if you add Sworn as a prereq, then that comes with responsibility for the Dragon to stay involved in Covenant business (and also adds another Experience to the overall cost of the merit, offsetting that arguably huge cost break for the Mentor effects). I don't think every merit representing a position of authority needs to come with a stated caveat that if you abuse your shit, people will want to oust you or get some other manner of revenge. That's just unlife in the Danse Macabre, and doesn't need to be reprinted all over the place. If you look at what this merit can actually, tangibly do, I don't think it's very abusable at all. If common sense isn't good enough to say that if a Twilight Judge blanket forbids every member of an Academy from studying Coils then she's going to have problems, I'm not sure discussion is going to get us anywhere.

                    Originally posted by Maskah
                    According to 1E Covenant book there can be more than one Twilight Judge and it specifically says it is extremely rare for one to over turn the findings of another.
                    Please point out the reference you're making, because it isn't the Twilight Judge section on p. 64 of Ordo Dracul. If you mean this sentence: "Once she has said her piece, the matter is considered resolved, and it may never again be revisited — unless the Twilight Judge herself chooses to do so." Emphasis mine. That pretty clearly states that nobody but the Twilight Judge who passed judgement can reverse it. Basically, was the merit written with the stuff in Ordo Dracul in mind, or not? That would go a long way to clearing this up.

                    Originally posted by Maskah
                    It is actually bad because it goes against the theme it is trying to portray very strongly. Diablerie and Humanity do not go hand in hand as they are polar opposites.
                    I know they're opposite. That's the point. It doesn't go against the theme of retaining Humanity. It absolutely goes toward retaining Humanity, since you can suck the Humanity right out of other kindred and take it for yourself. It still risks Detachment, sure, and it builds off the Coil's lower levels that allow you to resist Detachment. It's a dark way to retain Humanity, and it doesn't come without risk. That's a very compelling power from a narrative standpoint, and the mechanics are solid. Fair enough if you don't like it, but it isn't bad.
