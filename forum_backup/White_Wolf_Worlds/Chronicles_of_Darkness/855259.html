          FWIW, there was a fifth I had to cut. Here's their text.
          Gimu
          Defiant Samurai
          I will lose my family, my station, my home for this. I will lose everything for this fight. Are you willing to lose everything to face me?
          “Gimu” is a concept roughly equating to obligation or piety. It’s the closest thing to a name this loose confederation of samurai possesses, since they are officially not allowed to exist. Formally, Tokugawa laws prohibit a samurai from serving any person or ideology other than her daimyo. Informally, Gimu ascribe to a form of bushido that puts duty to a greater Nippon higher than any other concern, including the samurai’s own family, honor, and position.
          After the unification, the samurai caste found themselves barely needed outside of formal functions. With their extra time, they began looking inward, and examining the ebb and flow of courtly life. As they peered behind these curtains, they found monsters. They found demons pulling the strings of the daimyo, and walking court halls before the shogun. For the first decade or so, they answered with inaction. After all, who were they to question their leaders’ judgment? Eventually, a few such samurai met privately, and shared their concerns. Some walked away, choosing fealty to daimyo over duty to the nation. Others, however, banded together under the auspice of the Gimu.
          This new compact hunted in the shadows, pulling favors, delegating retainers, and using their influence to fight off the darkness. Sometimes, they could not rely on cleverly targeted arsons and state-sponsored executions to eliminate monsters, so they relied on their katanas. Every Gimu knew that each hunt could mean death, or disgrace. If a noble disappears, the samurai are held accountable, even if the courts weren’t aware that the disappearance was the samurai’s fault. When a samurai loses station, this comes with disgrace to her family, and their full loss of estate and station.
          Unfortunately for disgraced Gimu, they must sever all ties to the compact. A disgraced samurai becomes a non-entity, and recognized nobility cannot be seen dealing with the filthy. Every member knows of this possibility, and most prepare for it. Members set aside a coin reserve for the eventuality. In some cases, the compact prepares collections for particularly renowned members who suffer disgrace. In almost every case of disgrace, the dishonored Gimu chooses to “walk the winter road,” marching into the lair of a great enemy, with dying in battle her only remaining purpose.
          The Enemy
          Many hunters focus on the middlemen, the minions, the monsters on the streets. The Gimu exclusively focus on the puppeteers, the demons who amass great power by enslaving, possessing, and blackmailing those in power. The Gimu consider any taint in the noble houses a blight in the path to a greater Nippon. This gives them a rather diverse hunt, but a particularly dangerous one since they choose to battle only the greatest threats. Because the Gimu compact is more important than the individual samurai, discretion is paramount. Hunters cannot take up arms against just any monster, at any time. The samurai of the Gimu know that if their bodies are found in the wrong place, at the wrong time, it could mean the end of the compact at large.
          Hunters
          You’ve killed three armed men in a duel. You realized that men were no challenge for your martial arts mastery, and this caused great lament; you considered seppuku because you knew you could never die in battle. Then, a mentor approached you. A great, renowned samurai offered you the chance to battle greater foes than any human. He was ready to convince you, to explain why this noble fight was the best course of action. He could not finish his offer before you agreed.
          Your husband died, and left you in charge of the dynasty. Quickly, you uncovered his affairs, and his murderer. Some of the other samurai were investigating this, looking for the right time to strike. You would not wait. You took up his blade, and destroyed the werewolf that slaughtered your love. The other samurai looked on your devotion favorably, and invited you to continue the hunt to bring balance in honor of your family’s loss.
          You could never fight. You were always a pacifist, which flew in the face of your family’s samurai name. Your Buddhism teaches that we are all trapped in illusion, suffering in this world. You have transcended your pacifism, now that you’ve seen true monstrosities. These devils enslave humanity, trapping them in illusory shells. You must free them. So you free your fellow humans from enslavement, and you educate them; you free them from superstition and empower them to break their own chains.
          Duties
          You must find your own answers. None can teach you this path, since it defies everything you’ve ever known.
          You must reconcile the sacrifice you are about to make. Your family will hurt for your crimes. You must never shy away from family, because that would deny you the pain you deserve.
          You must be willing to sacrifice even bushido for the greater good. Acting on impulse could mean the end of this great purpose. That cannot be allowed to happen.
          Status
          The Gimu organize in an informal mentor/student hierarchy. Each member has a teacher, and once established, one or more students.
          • You exist in a network of samurai. You will always have weapons at your disposal. You have two additional dots of Resources, specifically for procuring supplies and weaponry for the hunt.
          ••• Your nobility affords access. Take four dots among Allies, Contacts, Fame, and Status.
          ••••• The greatest of the Gimu are followed to the ends of the Earth. At any given time, once per scene, your character has the support of three armed and capable samurai who will die for her. When called, they arrive within one turn, barring external influence.
          Stereotypes
          Ascending Ones: I do not trust their philosophies. But I hear they come from the stock of Yasuke, who was a great boon to Nobunaga. I will work with them.
          Ama-San: I know little of them. They protect farmers, and theirs is a noble lot.
          Azusa Miko: They’re both wise and valuable. You would be fortunate to work alongside one.
          Bijin: One must remember to keep as light a heart as one can. The Bijin help us with this.
          The Hototogisu: They’re highly competent. I only question their eagerness; none should be so eager for murder.
          The Otodo: They know the devils better than we will ever. They’re among the greatest advisors.

          David A Hill Jr
          Freelance Writer
          Independent Game Designer

                      so, kinda first impression-

                      first, yeah, I was surprised that the Gimu didn't made it in. I assumed it was the art- but the art is awesome, and we did got them in the blog while there are two new native compacts the Doubting Souls- so I can't be too sad.

                      Also- did I said the art is awesome? Because it is. Usually, when I read about a certain group, I envision a certain character, and sometimes I get disappointed from her presentation in the book- but this time, they are just as I've imagined. All of them. The Scarlet Watch was my favorite, but they all are well done!

                      Now, about the content itself- most of Doubting Souls was already discussed to its bones, so I'll talk about what's new. I kinda hoped for a native american conspiracy, but in the end, two compacts is a lot better than a single organization. I've also kinda hoped for a Les Mysteres reference to be added, like the Knights and the Heresy got, but it doesn't really all that important. Thinking about that, having new compacts than a conspiracy works a lot better for the Era- Salem is a small, closed community, bound in its own paranoia. The region feels a lot like a 2nd tier game atmosphere, and it is not really the place for vast networks and global wars. Besides, the Knights of Saint George are already presented as the official leaders of the local Vigil- having a local power for them to deal with would just harm the paranoia theme by giving a face to accuse.

                      Also- a Crossover Sidebar! The mention of local Sin Eaters as having weird powers may be interesting, and I should explore the local Bound society sometime. Presenting vampires as forced into hiding in the fear of the Scarlet Watch is exactly what I would like to see in an Hunter book, and same for mages- the point of Hunter is showing how dangerous mankind can be when it wants too, and reinforce why the monsters prefer to stay hidden (you can also thank Acheron for that). Nothing extremely evoking from the Werewolf and Created- but it is just a few lines, and wordcount is a b*tch.

                      Now- the Scarlet Watch! I'll say it one more time- awesome artwork. They are, undoubtedly, my favorite group in the book- the all "former conspiracy" atmosphere, familial connection and places where blood and money mix together sounds awesome for me. Also=- a touch and two crossed stakes as a symbol? Nice! The Les Voyageurs are not bad either- it is just a matter of personal taste, as I prefer to play a descendant of an old and rich family than a traveling fur trader- still, each has its potential for a good game.

                      As for the new compacts- the Keepers of the Weave remind me a group I once wrote, with their network of stories used to share information. I find them interesting, for they allow you to play a Loyalist or a Network-0 style hunter in that Era (more of the later, I believe) while still having their own identity. Their stereotype over the Knights only enhance my feeling that a native conspiracy together with he Knights in the same place would do more harm than good. I like them. As for the Protectors of the Light- so they are a local version of the Union among the native american tribes? Not the most evocative name, but they are not bad either. Also, I'm not sure what I think about their Lucifuge stereotype- they are seem to be presented through the European point of view, as the children of demons, and a new phenomena. Does it means that, as far as the Era is concerned, there are no Lucifuge-like hunters in the region (like to Otodo in Japan)? It is not out of place, considering that becoming s Child of the 7th Generation is half genetic, but it makes the Lucifuge a bit more.. weird, and I'm talking about the children of the devil here. Interesting.

                      all in all, it is a good chapter. I assume I'll have a lot more to say after I'll finish Fallen Blossoms.

                                                  so do you remember that in Beast, pre-Dawn of Heroes Tokyo has sounded like a city of monsters and an hunter's worst nightmare?

                                                  So yeah, they were right.

                                                  As we all know, the Gimu are not presented in the book- which is unfortunate, yet if I had to give up on one of the compacts in the chapter I believe I would have made the same choice. Still, we got them online, so no biggie. Anyway, the way hunters are presented in the setting is interesting- each hunter is a soldier in the service of the Shogunate, and the consensus is that most people don't meet with the yokai on regular basis, which helps making the setting less "shonen anime" and more realistic. Isolation, unification and brilliant future all take place in the setting, shining so bright it is hard to see the dark shadows in which the monsters lurk- and it looks like they are everywhere.

                                                  From what I see in the book, I really like all the "Japanese culture" sidebar- things like Hinnin, Wakashudo and similar terms helps unfamiliar players to get used to the setting. You can play as any one of the different social classes in the Era, and each comes with its own feeling and themes. Yoshiwara extremely feels like a great place to have a Bijin game- although I don't think I could stop myself from recreating the Gintama arc :P

                                                  I really liked the fact that even monsters take parts of the Caste System presented by the Neo-Confucianism. It helps showing just how well monsters are integrated into mortal culture. Giving to each caste its own enemies helps shaping not only how the Vigil goes in Nippon, but how the monsters work as well, helping you to expand the setting in case you would like to do a crossover thing. Also, I remember that someone once asked what "access" does the Bijin search for. That chapter gives the answer- the access that artists has to all social classes, meaning they are the most connected group in the Era.

                                                  so- Westren hunters in Nippon. The way the Lucifuge are portrayed shown that my suspicion about them from the previous chapter is true- the Lucifuge are not a global phenomena, and they find the Otodo as weird. I wonder if other similar strains of demonic blood are out there, coming from different families than that of Lucifer (I, for example, automatically think about a Middle-Eastern family who carry powers of diseases and plagues). Another proof comes from the way the MM interact with the children of the Oni- I assume the Lady of Milan does not spend time "adopting" new relative to her family. The AKD and AO sound good- nothing weird but they have a good place in the setting. The Hototogisu and the Otodo don't have a lot of details in the "conspiracy" section, but there is no need for it to make them feel important. Also- no Cheiron in Edo? No even through the small opening for trade they have with the Dutch? God, they must be pissed as hell! So many monsters in one place, all waiting to be harvested to parts- and they are out of reach. I won't be surprised to discover the Hototogisu would stand in their way as business rival, while the Ama San would have been see them as legitimate clients. Still, if there is a Cheiron presence in the setting, it would probably be only one hunter, even then he wouldn't stay for long. The Director Board must hate the Shogun so much during that Era.

                                                  Also- a city of monsters sidebar. Nicely done.

                                                  As for the monsters, we have Ainu werewolves walking among hunters, possible tengu tamers, a bashilu plague, all kinds of undead, the Onmyodo and so much more. I must say I wondered how to adapt my own onmyoji inspired hunters to the setting, but I don't think it would be much problem- I already have a number of ideas. Anyway, I liked the Ansho in Mage's Blog and I like the fact they show up in here. Its effect and lack of effect upon hunters seems... interesting. I really like that section, because it rise so many questions- are the kappa local Nosferatu? What is the end game of the Tsuchimikado? What the hell is the Gadashadokuro? So many plot hooks, so little time.

                                                  So- about the hunters themselves: I've already reviewed them when we first got them, so there is not much to say but, once again- great artwork! The Bijin's is my favorite, and I'm torn between them and the Hototgisu as my favorite group of the chapter. Also- why the Otodo fear water? I think missed something. Anyway, love the hunted relationships between the Azusa Miko and the Otodo, makes me think about the MM and the Lucifuge. I also don't get the way the Hototogisu and the Otodo view each other- either the Otodo are extremely paranoid, or that the Hototogisu are lying through the 4th wall. Both options has the same probability, I believe. I also see that their signature quote didn't got into the book. Damn wordcount, but if something has to be cut, so be it. Also, the Otodo are the only ones with a stereotype toward a Western group- it helps you to understand how few of those are there in the setting.

                                                  The last thing we get in the chapter is a crossover sidebar- and it is awesome. Now, this is how a crossover sidebar should be! So many little plot hooks, so many name droppings related to other gamelines. I'm surprised Owatatusmi Kami wasn't mentioned- but perhaps it was before his time? I don't know, the other important members of the "monster parliament" are there.I like the none-hostile relationships between the Azusa Miko and Sin Eaters- it makes sense, and shows that not all hunters target all monsters, and makes me think about that miko from the movie Rashamon. The lack of mummies is surprising, but makes sense- a single mummy is more power than a local monster-parliament may handle, but I could imagine local Azusa keeping the graves of ancient gods protected and undisturbed. Still, my favorite plot hook in the setting? Temple guardian pandorans. Now, who has put them in there?

                                                  All in all, both chapters are great, IMO, and I have so many new plot hooks and new hunter groups to love. The Bijin, Hototgisu and the Scarlet Watch are my favorites. I must say I can't wait for the new 2E Hunter to come out, only to get some more information about hunters in Tokyo, the connection between the old and new Hototogisu and seeing which groups has survived and in what form. Also, could we please please please have the Scarlet Watch re-surfacing once more in an example 2E setting, as part of their ancient obligation forcing them back together? Pretty please, with sugar on top?
