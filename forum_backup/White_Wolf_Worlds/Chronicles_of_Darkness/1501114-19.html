                This one might not be as simple as it sounds, since it concerns a potential discrepancy between gamelines: What is the requirement for a character to be able to spend Willpower on a Clash of Wills roll, vis-a-vis physical presence?

                The majority of gamelines (Vampire, Werewolf, Promethean, Hunter, Demon, Beast) use some variation on the earliest iteration of the text, which states that characters can spend Willpower to bolster the contested roll involved in a Clash of Wills, but only if they are physically present and aware that powers are Clashing (typically followed by the note on how increasing duration of an effect adds an increasing number of dice to its roll to Clash with other powers).

                Mage doesn't mention physical presence in its iteration of the mechanic at all but does specify that the Awakened always count as being aware of when their spells are Clashing, which is pretty clearly meant to interface with the requirement (not directly stated here) that a character be aware of Clashing powers to spend Willpower; it also includes the scaling bonus dice from effect duration, as makes sense for the breadth of adjustments that can be made to the parameters of spells. By the opposite token, Deviant doesn't mention spending Willpower or scaling bonus Clash dice from effect duration at all, because the design principles for Variations specifically preclude long-term effects, though the logical endpoint of the scale is included in the form of "powers that are permanently active add +5 dice to their Clash rolls."

                Geist uses a notably different format from the most common one to more or less the same result, with the biggest adjustments being the changing of the duration-based bonus dice from a paragraph to a table and the ambiguity of characters only being able to spend Willpower "if they're both physically present and aware that two abilities are in conflict," which seems logically intended as "both of these criteria need to be true for a character to spend Willpower on a Clash roll" but has room to also be read as a different statement, expressed anon.

                Changeling and Mummy both use slight variations on the formatting found in Geist, including the duration bonus table; notable differences include Changeling and Mummy's versions specifying that having awareness of clashing powers to allow Willpower spending may require magic, and that a character can only spend Willpower on the Clash roll if he and his opponent are physically present.

                That last one is… a very different thing from a stipulation that clearly exists to incentivize characters who expect magical effects they maintain separately from themselves (e.g. Oubliette, the Boneyard, Facets of the Gift of Warding, etc) to need the ludonarrative bolstering of their being around and actively trying to make sure they remain functional. Deviant's corebook Variations are pretty much all definitionally things the character is physically present to use, and Mage is working with expensive component Traits on the Clash pools and potentially messing with time and space in the execution (to say nothing of how it's a gameline that doesn't use Beats to incentivize characters to get severely wounded), but it's not clear why Changeling and Mummy would only allow Clash rolls to be boosted by Willpower in cases where the opposition has the same opportunity.

                Is this a situation where the Geist iteration of the text pre-dates the Changeling and Mummy versions in dev time and was misinterpreted, or is Willpower chicken an intended stipulation of the Clash rules?

                                            Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
                                            It's gonna eventually happen; in the book, Immortals, the Purified will eventually lose their body; can they pick up the Body-Thief template and become, truly immortal?
                                            The versions of the Purified in both Immortals and Book of Lasting Death have the ability to reconstitute their body by spending Essence, with different stipulations on ritually preparing a chamber that allows them to restore their bodies in that location instead of where their physical remains are.

                                            There's a sidebar in their chapter of Immortals that specifies that Purified can't gain or retain any of the typical templates, and the Body Thieves chapter of the same book both explicitly states that the body thief template is mutually exclusive with most other templates except with Storyteller discretion and has a sidebar on a body-swap-style body theft ritual that specifically doesn't work on any other types of immortal except the ones in that book that involve a recurring process (i.e. blood bathers, patchwork people, and other body thieves).

                                            Mummy 2e doesn't even have to go to these lengths, as the Endless Template it uses for all its immortals has deciding the method of immortality the character uses as a set step distinct from any other rituals or resources they know or have.

                                            The Deathless are the only truly immortal worldly beings in Mummy 2e, with the Endless all dealing with some level of cost-of-doing-business problems that drive them into conflict with the Arisen since relics and Sekhem are attractive prospects to tangle for.
