                    You should really give people the benefit of the doubt more.

                    This is fairly simple.

                    Beast contradicts itself in tone, theme, and text more than once.

                    As such, any argument made for or against beast by nature has to ignore part of the book. The book is impossible to reconcile as a whole.

                    I don't like Beast. I know I'm biased against it. I'm trying to keep my discussions in this thread on the peripheral because of that. Stuff like vampire bites and the fact that we're all better off trying to understand one another instead of calling each other poopy heads and covering our ears. Not direct discussions on Beast. Before anyone calls me out, I said I'm trying. Sometimes I open my big mouth against my better judgement. This post may or may not be one of those times.

                    But Beast contradicts itself. It does so often and gratuitously. I think this is the root of 99% of the conflicts over its contents. Everyone cherrypicks the text that supports their side, whether their side is defending or critiquing Beast.

                    Neither critique nor compliment of Beast is "valid" by your definition. But I think they are valid. Why? Because such an intense divide and such utterly different interpretations of the same book and the discussion that evolves from that shows the developers where the text needs to be tightened. Where it is (I presume) unintentionally contradicting itself.

                    If you need an example, look no further than the two threads spawned by the fact that the "integrity 4" stipulation for Heroes was added late in the development, and a lot of the text doesn't take that into account, fluff or crunch. The people arguing that Heroes are tragic figures are justified because of parts of the text. Their opinion is valid. The people who think Heroes are horrible people the Beasts are justified in defending themselves against are also justified. Their opinions are also valid. Both sets of opinions also contradict parts of the book.

                    There is no One True Opinion, Rater202. The sooner people on both sides accept that, the happier everyone is going to be.
