                        Originally posted by Evil Midnight Lurker View Post
                        Unexpected as it was, the murder of Knud marked the end of the Viking Age and ripped him away from his God-Machine ordained purpose as overseer and leader of a national part of Infrastructure.
                        I don't believe this means actual overseer as much as a sort of spiritual one. It's possible that the God-Machine did mean for him to come back as a mentor figure, however (it would not be the first time the God Machine was involved in making a vampire), but the regicide and usurpation of his title kept that from happening.

                        Remember the God Machine doesn't care about Knud specifically, it's trying to ease a conversion from paganism to Christianity. So in its ideal situation: Knud goes into a cathedral, prays, 'sees the light,' humbly hands over his titles to a just heir, and dies repentant. Centuries later, people are wearing What Would Knud Do bracelets and honoring his memory. Sometimes they might claim to have seen his ghost nodding to them before they did a good deed.

                        Instead: Knud goes into a cathedral, gets betrayed (not normally seen as a good thing to be rewarded in Christianity), murdered in a sanctuary, and the folks who did it get to be the king now. Over the course of centuries, when Knud is seen, it's as a scary thing, a 'horrifying blood-sucking monster,' who often haunts the grounds of the holy space he reached for sanctuary.

                            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                            Mostly because it wasn't about crossover mechanics, it was about crossover. And, honestly, the bigger thread to a lot of making all of that is making the disparate threads of themes, moods, and languages work together than the mechanics.

                            I've said it before, I'll say it again, &quot;fluff&quot; is harder than &quot;crunch&quot; (and I say that after, like, four months of panicking over crunch for Memento Mori, though other factors matter to the conversation). Making a satisfying crossover experience involves a lot of work in making the character choice of the player feel valid while blending them all into the same product, and that's a shit ton of work, one that needs tools.

                            I'm surprised to see that it took Death to really get there, but as a craftsman, I'm not that surprised. The mechanics are much....lower on the list of things to get in order after getting the ideas and more fundamental elements of their execution onto the table, and books only have so much space-and really, the idea of the Contagion Chronicle is brilliant as a way of driving that home.
                            For what it's worth, I've never advocated for the book being just about the mechanics; I've always viewed them as a small but essential part of the issue. But just as I wouldn't want the book too be all about crossover crunch, I also wouldn't want it to be completely devoid of it; there are some crunch issues that just have to be addressed, at least in a general sense.

                            All that said, I'm fine with crossover fluff getting top billing.

                            My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
