          Need? No.

          Worth it? Depends, but probably.

          Besides condensing down to one book anyway, but CofD 2e core introduces a number of new subsystems no in the GMCRU and not always included in the supernatural core rule books (mostly because they were developed after those books). The new Crafting (not exactly what it sounds when people think about it, and not just about the Craft Skill), Investigation (also not just about the Investigation Skill),and Chase rules are all very useful additions to the ST toolkit for expanded in-depth rules for things other than combat and social maneuvering when you want them to be more than a simple or extended roll off.

              You definitely don't need it. You can absolutely play without it and you'll get by just fine. However, there are some advantages to getting the 2e core book that I considered well worth it. Your mileage may vary.

              The first, and most obvious is that it's the most complete version of the 2e core rules available. The God-Machine chronicle rrequires cross-referencing with the 1e core book, and the core books for other lines contain trunkated versions of the core rules, shortened to fit the minimum needed to play the game within a single chapter.

              Additionally, the 2e core book contains some optional rules not found in any other book. They are optional, so you don't NEED them, but they might improve your game. These optional rules include a system for crafting equipment, including non-physical equipment like plans and organizations (in my opinion, the best crafting system I've seen in an RPG), a system for running chase scenes which makes them much more dynamic and interesting than the standard footchase action, and a system for running Investigations which I don't use personally, but many people like and praise. There's also a system for creating custom monsters, kind of like the one from 1e Hunter.

              I like to look at the 2e "core book" as more of a supplemental blue book than a true core. It's not needed to play the game, but it has a lot of great options for expanding upon the core rules.
