    Originally posted by GibberingEloquence View Post
    I'm glad you liked the Condition. I have some questions about Nimbus Clashing, however. What do you mean by "a mage can only invoke their nimbus against a specific instance of a magical effect once"? If a vampire tries to use Majesty on the mage and fails, but tries again within the same scene, can the mage Clash with their Nimbus once more? What if the vampire tried in another scene? What if the vampire tries Dominate instead of Majesty?
    Allow me to clarify the entire mechanic:

    A Mage can use his nimbus to Clash with any effect that would directly alter his mind, body or soul. Life to change his shape? Clash. Dominate to subjugate his mind? Clash. The heinous grip of a Beast threatens to seize his soul? Clash. A Mage, however, cannot Clash with effects that would harm him incidentally. For instance, he could not clash with a Life spell that created, then flung and infection at him. Nor could he Clash with a Uratha's supernaturally potent jaws, lightning slung by a Promethean or a Demon crippling his social assets. Put another way, if the effect would one-shot a character without recourse they can probably Clash with it, if it merely complicates their existence, affects them indirectly, or allows them some other form of defense (like Dodge, Soak, or Health) then it probably cannot be Clashed with. In the end it's a storyteller call.

    Now, when I say a Mage can only Clash with an effect once that means that, for each activation of a supernatural power the Mage may attempt to break it with his Nimbus only once. Taking Majesty as an example, if a Vampire attempted to tag a Mage with the Charmed condition that Mage could forgo his normal Contested roll and use his Nimbus to Clash. Should he fail, the Mage is Charmed, period. He cannot use his Nimbus to Clash again even if the scene or even the story (if the effect were longer) ends. However, if the Mage wins the Clash the Charmed Condition is resisted, the Vampire could attempt to Charm him again (though I'd consider that poor storytelling) and likewise the Mage could once more attempt to Clash.

    Now, on the topic of power spam. Personally I'd never have an NPC attempt the same approach twice in a scene and I'd prohibit a player from doing the same. However, as that runs into some weird scenarios across the games (For instance, does Clashing with Dominate the Mind prohibit all Mind powers? Just that spell? Justing Ruling Practices? What if the Mage devises an exceptionally similar spell? Etc.) I opted not to put that to rules. That is, IMO, the point where the storyteller and players must deem what is best for their tale.

    Is that more clear?

    Fundamentally the mechanic is there to offer players some recourse against effects that would otherwise rob them of all agency or do serious, irrevocable harm without affording them even the slightest roll. It's intended to achieve this in a dramatic manner, which conveys narratively that Mages (and full supernatural templates in general) are not to be trifled with and that the scene just got fucking serious.

                I wrote a thing!

                It's a thing for Three Kingdoms of Darkness
                Reach Heaven By Violence

                Core Power: If You Meet God...
                Keys: Stigmata Hearse
                Dice Pool: Finesse + Weaponry
                Action: Instant
                Duration: Instant
                It is easy to see why many cultures respect the sword, when death itself dwells at the edge of a blade. This power allows the Sin-Eater to reach any opponent with but a single step.

                Roll Results:

                Dramatic Failure: The Sin-Eater very obviously disappears and reappears by the side of his opponent, but he may take no other actions that turn.
                Failure: For a short moment, the Sin-Eater seems to shimmer, but nothing else happens.
                Success: The Sin-Eater disappears in a cloud of ephemeral substance and appears at the side of a single opponent within sight and makes a reflexive attack.
                Exceptional Success: The attack is unexpected, and the opponent cannot apply Defence.

                Link 1: You Must Cut Him
                Keys: Phantasmal Curse
                Trigger: “When the Sin-Eater makes a reflexive attack...”
                ...They add their Power as additional damage modifier, once per scene as their weapon trails shadowy ephemera behind it in a single destructive cut. If this attack kills the target, the Sin-Eater's weapon becomes a Fetter with a single Numen and Key determined by the Storyteller and the Anchor of the Torn. This effect only lasts for a scene, however the Sin-Eater may spend a dot of Willpower to make it permanent.


                "There is a remedy for everything but death, a hope for everything but wickedness, and everything will lapse except righteousness."

                                    You're on point about Beasts and Demons. The intent for Demons was to give them a very potent, but somewhat conditional defense against pattern manipulation, but honestly it's too much book keeping. What I'll probably go for is this:
                                    • Demons can Clash with effects that would warp their mind or body (their souls are inviolate, so no need there) by assuming Demonic Form. While in Demonic Form a demon is wholly immune to any effect that could be Clashed with, and immediately suppress any magics which were currently affecting their pattern while in Cover. This means that it is impossible to dominate, mutate, time-warp or otherwise muck up the pattern of a Demon in Demonic Form. This does not, however, apply to reprogramming by God-Machine infrastructure, or the reality bending powers of Rank 6+ beings.
                                    That accomplishes more cleanly and absolutely the desired effect; to make Demons fucking horrifying for other splats, and render Demonic Form the power house it is intended to be. As for Beast, I went with Tilts off the top of my head, but you're right, they don't work. Though, I should take a moment to note that when I envision clashing it's not summoning waves to snuff fire, indeed the Blast Numen would not be something I'd allow a Clash against, it already has it's limits. It's more the supernatural being flaring their base nature and rejecting powers that would change it. The Beast briefly unveiling their horror in an Anima Flare/American Gods, dual presence sort of way works well. It'll need a drawback, like causing heroes to hone in on the Beast when used, or greatly increasing the chances of witnesses becoming heroes. Actually, both of those are probably sufficient.

                                    Drawback: Risk Frenzy
                                    Werewolf - Shifting Up
                                    Effect: Shift up one step towards Gauru, Clash with effect. If in Gauru add +2 to the Clash roll. Werewolves auto Clash with all Clashable effects while in Gauru.
                                    Drawback: Shifting up inflicts lunacy on bystanders. If in Gauru, risk Kuruth.
                                    Mage - Nimbus Flare
                                    Effect: Clash with effect. If successful inflict your Nimbus Tilt(s)
                                    Drawback: Sleeper bystanders suffer a breaking point and count as witnesses for the rest of the scene.
                                    Promethean - Kindling
                                    Effect: Unveil your disfigurements, Clash with effect.
                                    Drawback: Intensify disquiet in all those present.
                                    Changeling - Dropping the Mask
                                    Effect: Drop the mask, Clash with effect.
                                    Drawback: Everyone can see you for what you are, Huntsmen and Gentry can feel the mask fall.
                                    Sin-Eater - Manifest Geister
                                    Effect: Manifest your Geist, Clash with effect.
                                    Drawback: ??? (I've got nothing, Synergy risk?)
                                    Mummy - Sybaris
                                    Effect: Unleash Terror Sybaris, Clash with effect.
                                    Drawback: Mortals nearby suffer breaking points, everyone is afraid of you, you look like a corpse.
                                    Demon - Demonic Form
                                    Effect: Assume Demonic form, become immune to pattern manipulation while in Demonic form.
                                    Drawback: Suffer compromise.
                                    Beast - Unveil Horror
                                    Effect: Reveal your horror briefly, Clash with effect. If successful, and in a resonant location, you may invoke a Lair Tilt.
                                    Drawback: Heroes are drawn to your location. If there are no heroes to hunt you, the probability of a bystander becoming a hero increases exponentially.
                                    Note: These are not supposed to be perfectly balanced against one another. Mummies have very few drawbacks, Demons have a huge drawback but by far the strongest option, etc.

                                    Edit: I'm trying to find a proper wording to convey succinctly when a Clash is appropriate. Currently I'm going by "when the storyteller deems it", but that just doesn't feel clear enough. Currently I'm toying with:
                                    • A supernatural being can Clash with an effect when that effect would directly subvert the very essence of their being or personality. For instance, if a Mage attempts to light a changeling's bone marrow on fire that changeling could Clash as his bones being on fire would be a clear subversion of his essential being. However, that same changeling could not clash if the mage manipulated the ambient air to cause him to burst into flame, burning when exposed to great heat is perfectly natural. Similarly, a demon in demonic form would be immune to being personally, singularly frozen in time, but could not respond if the Mage stopped local time altogether in the area. Again, a demon being locked out of time while everything else moves is unnatural, but not moving because nothing is moving, well that's perfectly normal.
                                    I'm still not perfectly happy with the wording, but I think it gets the point across better than my current pitch. Thoughts?

                                            Originally posted by ManusDomine View Post
                                            This is really cool, but could you please clarify the Soul-Glass and Martial Keys and what they do?
                                            Right, forgot to note those:
                                            • Soulglass was devised by Metamurpho (one of my players). It involves art, mimicry, and living souls. Things like trapping people's souls in mirrors, having art stare into your soul, becoming a simulacre of someone's thoughts. Think about how primitive people thought mirrors entrapped your soul and go from there. Sample Manifestations might include using The Caul to become someone's doppleganger, Oracle to gaze through reflective surfaces, or the Curse to inflict soulessness.

Things like inciting armed conflict, making weapons more/less lethal, or giving someone PTSD. Think about the hectic, hellish experience of being on the battlefield and you're on the right track. Sample Manifestations might include using the Shroud to become immune to weaponry and firearms, the Hearse to turn a car into a tank, or Boneyard to place and entire area under suppressing fire.
                                            ​Also, it's worthwhile to note that I run Geist under the assumption that more, custom keys exist, and that those given in the book are merely examples not the be all and end all of ghostly themes.

                                                    I ended up working out a Curse for a Proximi line while working out some of the Mage 2E House Rules I'm putting together. Whatcha think?

                                                    Abyssal Bane
                                                    The Quartermaines were notable for their skill in manipulating Mana and various forms of mystical energy.
