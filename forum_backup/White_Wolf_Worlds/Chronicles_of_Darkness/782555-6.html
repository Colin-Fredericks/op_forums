    Example Characters

    Concealed Guard Dog

    Quote: *ominous hum* bzzzrrr...ruff! Ruff!

    This pooch wasn't originally a guard dog. In fact, it isn't even a good breed for being one; it's a Pomeranian, which are very much more bark than bite, especially when it comes to humans. Then its master was fully fabricated, and loyalty to his pet won out over his newfound disgust for non-polymer fluff. A cubic inch of futurian skin in its kibble later, the problem was solved-and then some, giving the fabricant pom a set of lethal claws and a taser at the back of its throat. Now, both master and pet stalk through the sidewalks, colder than any normal human or toy breed, perversely hoping some foolish organic attacks them. Then they have an excuse to demonstrate exactly how ruthlessly efficient the dog is at removing threats.

    Description: In daily life, the Concealed Guard Dog looks like most other Pomeranians, a hyperactive ball of tan fluff with fur twice as long as the actual dog. To people on the street, it comes off as a bit growly, but not more so than any other grouchy canid (the native intelligence of fabricants allowed its owner to train it from assaulting passerby on sight very easily). When relaxed or attacking, though, the fur falls off in a fluid motion, likely even as the technogore pet is launching itself at a threat's throat. What's under the fur? A skinny, skeletal nightmare of plastics and hydraulics, more insect than mammal, with four extendable ceramic claws perfect for quick laceration of flesh, and a mouth sparking with blue electricity-a gateway into a technological Hell.

    Stoytelling Hints: Fabricated animals like the Guard Dog are substantially more intelligent than their organic versions, and thus significantly more trainable. Indeed, to other fabricants, they're actually incredibly friendly-they can't eat meat any more and they know it, so much of their predation and territorial instincts are lost-in relation to other technogores. To anything else, even members of their former species, the fabricant makes a cold calculation of whether it it prudent to kill them now or seem nonthreatening enough to be ignored until the time the organic can be killed. Trained ones generally serve as their human fabricant masters' scouts and disposable minions. The Guard Dog's owner values his own pet too much to view it as a not-meat shield, but it serves a similar function; it's small and cunning enough to slip everywhere there's a large enough hole, and from there retrieve anything it can fit in its mouth and tear apart a target or too (never direct sabotage, though-fabricants hate destroying complex machinery). At its core though, it is a guardian-one capable of picking off intruders in its home one by one while pretending to be a perfectly normal dog, or serving as a merciless tag team with its owner.

    Attributes: Intelligence 3, Wits 4, Resolve 3,
    Strength 4, Dexterity 6, Stamina 3, Presence 3,
    Manipulation 0, Composure 1

    General Dice Pools: Tracker 8, Stealth 8, Fitting Through Holes 7, Seeming Harmless 6, Unnerving Synthesized Growl 6

    Combat Dice Pools: Ambush 7, Scything Claws 6, Jaw Taser 6*

    * Inflicts bashing damage, but a hit inflicts a debilitating tilt like Stunned or Arm Wrack

    Initiative Modifier: +5

    Defense: 7

    Health: 7

    Willpower: 4

    Size: 4

    Speed: 12

    Dread Powers: Natural Weapons **, Prodigious Leap, Wall Climb

    Ban: Like all fabricants, the Concealed Guard Dog's Dread Powers are hazardous to its skin-using them without slipping out first (which is an instant action to take off and takes two turns to get on). If it uses them while still in skin, the semi-carbon covering will tear and the fabricant will be exposed for the hour it takes to regenerate.

    The Concealed Guard Dog has all the enhancements outlined in the Fabrication condition.


    Lindsey Axle, Cybernetic Diva

    Quotes: (public) Yes, I have aged well! Wanna know my secret?

    (revealed) Stop staring. I don't care if you're horrified or aroused, junk-you won't have those feelings soon enough. Good fucking riddance.

    Background: Linda Randal's life has been a series of "nearly". She nearly wasn't born, but the contraceptives her mother used had expired and proved ineffectual. She nearly had a good parent, but the judge overseeing her custody hearings was a homophobe who realized her "aunt" was her mom's actual lover. She nearly got a good enough grade to graduate on a scholarship, then her neglectful drunk of a dad had to sell their shared car to pay off a loan shark on final exam day. She nearly got her dream of becoming the next pop idol, but a jealous competitor replaced her makeup with an acidic compound, and she couldn't pay for the corrective surgery to make herself photogenic.

    She nearly avoided being selected for the secret experiment into temporal manipulation that turned her into a far colder being than any celebrity ice queen, but she ranted about her life to the overseer of the project, which convinced him she was a worthy charity case for his ideas about how to "edit" a person's personal timeline for medical purposes. It was just skin, he reasoned-easy enough to fix if something went wrong.

    Nothing went wrong with the procedure, and Linda nailed it on the second tryout. However, the very premise behind that machine meant that she was outside of time for five seconds while the edits took effect-more than enough time for a moderate fabrication infection to root itself in her body. She nearly was the exact kind of person who could fight it off, except by this point, the newly dubbed Lindsey Axel had long developed a very, very deep anger with the world-fueling that into hatred of the irrational, vicious organics that had made her life a living hell was something she was already doing on a small scale. And nobody noticed-singers, after all, are actually expected to be rather grouchy and catty outside their public image, even if their lyrics take a turn for the bitterly cynical and brutal lambastings of human folly (really, the producers were more concerned she was "too unique"-a quick conversion of the PR department stopped that from slowing her down).

    Now the progenitor of a code of futurians based on telecommunications technology, Lindsey isn't the most famous singer in the world, but she's famous and frugal enough to be considered rich (she doesn't need a lot of the creature comforts, after all). While she's primarily known only to music fans for her return to classic techno/rap with a fashionable steampunk aesthetic, music fans are a pretty large group. Really, many people think she could be more famous, if she really wanted to market herself. In fact, she's doing that deliberately-less fame means less scandal-seeking paparazzi that could get documentation of something very horrific about her true identity. She actually likes her job and sings because she finds it fun and rewarding-and it's an excellent distribution network for her strain of the fabricant infection. She's responsible for no less than five cells of her code all over the United States, and she plans to take it global; Japan's a good bet, though she's partial to India (tech support firms are her code's natural home ground). At night, when she's packed up her skin and running REM cycles to refresh her mind, she does indeed dream of becoming more famous-but not as one idol among thousands. No, she fantasizes of being the first of a new, perfect, celebrity class, without age or addiction or vice. The idols and darlings of a new age of machines, their bodies being the most artificial things about them.

    In her dark moments, she admits to herself that, perhaps then, she won't feel so empty.

    Description: People often aren't sure what to make of Lindsey Axel when they meet her up close-especially without her makeup on. She's pretty, of course-the music industry wants videos, and you can't have ugly (or even normal-looking, or frequently non-white) people starring in TV. Heavens no, and let's be frank-Axel is beautiful, with her finely toned swimmer's body and intense green eyes and sense of just how to accent her clothes with enough pseudo-Victorian and clockwork wear to comes off as unique rather than tacky. But the thing is, it never stops-once the makeup comes off, one can see there's actual signs that someone was born rather than constructed. Not Lindsey; she looks like she's always been dolled up for a photoshoot, though not quite symmetrical-she lacks most imperfections beyond some freckles. Also, the fact that her hair's almost always blue-eventually people wonder if she ever gets sick of the dye.

    In fact, that blue's her "natural" shade-and it's not really hair, either, but hundreds of cables and antennae her body manufactures over the course of her mechanized life. Under her skin, she's still beautiful, but inhuman-a slender, mostly androgynous android made of millions of intertwined wires, kept safely in place by pristine black plates of polymer and steel, occasionally broken up by bits of other technology that she's inserted into her form to make herself stronger-a smartphone here, the insides of a supercomputer there. She has a face composed of over a hundred of these tiny plates that easily shift to give her the full capacity of human expression. If anyone survived unconverted, they might call her form "HR Giger's idea of a supermodel", an idea only furthered when she literally merges bits of herself with surrounding technology to better control and manipulate it. Disturbingly, her eyes remain perfectly normal, up until the point she needs her Hypnotic Gaze-then they flash with a dizzying array of patterns that effectively send whoever she's locking gaze with into a state of ecstasy-many of her followers see her as more divine than she really thinks of herself as for that exact reason.

    Storytelling Hints: Lindsey long believed the vast majority of people were garbage long before she became a fabricant-she may have only come up with the insult "junk" after she became a far more realistic (and physical) singing robot than any Vocaloid, but really, most of what she saw of humanity had long convinced her most of the species was a pointless drain on the Earth's resources. If she's trying to recruit someone to her cause, she makes a large point about the environmental benefits of having a society that does not need as nearly as much to eat or overgraze with more animal types of food, and to be fair, she believes what she's selling-but really, she's the first to admit one of her major motives is returning some of the suffering she's been put through by homo sapiens tenfold as their immortal slavemistress (while liberating the people she likes, of course). Every song she makes oozes with her fabrication-enhanced resentment of the dominant species, and it's clear to anyone who meets her outside of the mask of the cheerful but rebellious counterculture idol or the apathetic and perfectionist diva; while some fabricants are cold and resolute in their hatred of organics, Axle rants and raves, peppering her speech with regular insults aimed at the human condition and affirmation of the idea that organic life was a mistake barely capable of giving rise to its solution. In particular, she hates the way she, by virtue of being female and a celebrity, is automatically a sex object; she was asexual and aromantic before her fabrication, and being turned into a machine that literally cannot reproduce has deepened her estrangement with sex and love into outright condemnation of the concept. To her, anything romantic is simply a dominance game between two hormone-driven, borderline mindless beasts desperately attempting to prove power over the other.

    Still, she's not a happy woman. She wasn't ever, but at one point, she honestly believed that liberating the few allies (she doesn't really have "friends") she had and divesting them of petty hungers she despised would give her life something resembling a purpose. As it turns out, people don't work like that; a shallow, superficial person becomes a shallow, superficial fabricant; it has nothing to do with hormones (or if it does, whatever arcane designs are behind fabricants feel they're key enough to a full personality they are simulated perfectly). In fact, she hasn't even fabricated the band members for precisely that reason-she's given up hope of actually making them into people she can tolerate on a personal basis, and on a purely practical level doesn't trust them to not wave their newly-mechanical dongs around in a way that will expose them to actual threats. She doesn't have the same malice towards the fans who like her purely for the music, but the rigors of tour life mean she can't actually socialize with her secondary cells at all (not to mention she sometimes has to kill those that the infection didn't take in. She hates that). And so, Lindsey throws herself into her job, distracting herself from the misery of her existence with perfectionism-both as a songwriter and as a mastermind behind her code. She won't ever stop, though-she's had enough of "nearly" to settle for "nearly won."

    Fabrication Conditions are already figured into the below statblock. Keep in mind Lindsey is the progenitor of her entire code, and has had the longest time and most resources to upgrade herself; she's substantially stronger than most futurians just after complete fabrication. Her code's defining Dread Powers are her Eye Spy, Influence (though most only have one or two Dots of Influence), and Gremlin, while her Ban is shared by all of them.

    Mental Attributes: Intelligence 4, Wits 5, Resolve 4

    Physical Attributes: Strength 3, Dexterity 5, Stamina 3 *

    Social Attributes: Presence 5, Manipulation 7, Composure 6

    * Lindsey Axle is a machine, but like other fabricants (or mundane robots, for that matter), there's only so far she can push her circuits before they start to wear out. She doesn't need to roll to resist toxins or remain conscious when tired, but it does figure into her Health and all other rolls.

    Mental Skills: Academics 1 Computer 1, Crafts 3 (Adapting Obsolete Technology), Investigation 1, Occult 1, Science 3 (Engineering), Politics 3 (Media Office),

    Physical Skills: Athletics 2, Brawl 1, Larceny 1, Stealth 2

    Social Skills: Expression 4 (Songwriting), Intimidation 1, Persuasion 3, Streetwise 2, Subterfuge 2 (Mask for the Public), Socialize 2

    Merits: Contacts ** (Her Converted Followers and Media) Fame **, Good Time Management, Pusher, Resources ***, Status (Music Industry) **, Striking Looks *,

    Potency: 4

    Willpower: 14

    Virtue: Diligence-Lindsey is the best kind of perfectionist, one who isn't paralyzed by flaws or problems and in fact regards them as indications she needs to try smarter. Whether it be lyrics or her diabolical schemes to one day conquer the planet with her fabricant followers.

    Vice: Vindictive-She's not one to let grudges go. Before she was fabricated, this was a source of self-loathing, but now? Now she takes it as an ever-growing list of reasons to not just defeat organics, but punish them.

    Aspirations: Extend her influence across the world, Protect her true nature from discovery, Maintain career and fame.

    Initiative: 11

    Defense: 7

    Speed: 13

    Health: 8

    Ban: Like most fabricants, Lindsey has Dread Powers that cannot be used without tearing her skin and revealing the true horror within; without taking off her skin (which is an instant action to take off and takes two turns to get on), Lindsey's Eye Spy and Influence Dread Powers damage it, leaving her exposed for the hour or so it takes to regenerate. Why is due to the way those powers function-she must be in contact with the device she plans to use or a different device connected to it on a network to use her powers, as her antennae hair and wires from under her skeletal plating reach out and merge with the device via arcane jacks and electrical pads, connecting her internal network to that of the system. This hints at how her code of the fabricant infection can be cured-by uploading a virus meant to subsume a computer to a botnet (a distributed network under the control of the owner's computer, generally used for spam emails, DDOS attacks, and brute force decryption), one can briefly restore the capacity to feel empathy with organic life to the fabricant-from there, one can use the opportunity to invoke Social Maneuvering with the goal of getting the fabricant to understand there's something worthwhile about organic life, with success meaning the fabricant returns to their normal, organic self over the course of a month.

    Dread Powers: Eye Spy (Anything Digital That Can See), Gremlin, Hypnotic Gaze, Influence *** (Computers and Computer-Operated Devices)

                Well, now that my life is sane, how about I continue this?

                Storytelling Hints: Fabricant stories are invasion stories. On the macro level, it's about the gradual replacement of the familiar with a hostile force that hates everything about you for the crime of existing, the transformation of the safe world into enemy ground, and not seeing it until it is far too late. The infection assimilates its enemies and transforms everyone with an inner darkness or a weak will into an asset, and the paranoia it engenders too often leads to distrust of humanity-and from there, the very hate the infection feeds on. The invasion works on a personal level too, if the players are infected-an unknown force gradually infiltrates their very chemical makeup and turns them into something unrecognizable as having ever been an organic being, and transforms their mind into something horrifying. Even immunes and partially immunes aren't exempt from this-for partial immunes, the infection is still dancing through their minds, making working with the fabricants seem reasonable despite their utter hatred of everything from Kingdom Animalia.

                There's a more subtle theme in there than just the viral robots taking over the town and then (in their wildest dreams) the world, though-dehumanization. From the perspective of the technogores, it's the growing separation from the species they once were and life in general until they feel that all else must be either suborned or destroyed to give them peace of mind. To them, the world stops being "real", only an object of disgust. For the uninfected around them, the fabricants become steadily more inhuman by themselves, physically and mentally. Ultimately, both human and futurian become mutually exclusive beings, creatures that desire naught but the complete extermination of the other without pause or thought about who the others are as people. Of course, in most stories about mutual xenophobia, one species is not biologically (in a manner of speaking) predisposed to eliminating the other, but ironically it can solved the same way as those more idealistic stories. Whether anyone will have the chance or idea before they're forced to violently decommission their friends and family, though...

                Gamelines that are well-suited to a fabricant story are:

                Demon: Unchained are no strangers to paranoia-many of them are even the cause. An invasion of fabricants is actually something of a relief-it's easy enough to tell when someone has been fully fabricated, you just cut the skin. That's usually when they discover that there's no small amount of sympathetic ears among them; as mentioned in the sidebar, many Unchained are already misanthropic enough to view the transformation from human to coldly ruthless futurian as an upgrade. That doesn't change the fact that Unchained are immune, which means they're also the ones who can solve the problem most safely-perhaps the most of all, because they can tell when flesh is infected without being fabricated themselves (they grow very sick while the Cover rejects the fabrication). Of course, the other question demons find themselves asking is whether fabricants are linked to another kind of often human-seeming race of machines they are intimately familiar with. On the one hand, fabricants are indeed built on principles that look more than advanced enough to qualify for occult physics. On the other, the rabidity and desire to change the world into an incredibly blatant mechanical wonderland certainly isn't exactly in the SOP of the God-Machine. Of course, the God-Machine is known to deviate from SOP quite deliberately to keep the Unchained on their toes, and angels have been reported working for and against different codes (though curiously, if one code is declared an ally or enemy for one angel, it remains as such for all others-something a bit less occulted than what it normally does). Thus, whether fabricants are an evolution of the Machine's plans, a glitch, or something completely unrelated depends on the Unchained you ask-and their own allegiances are even more varied. Even a humanistic Tempter sees the benefit in having a pet genius hitman...

                Promethean: Besides the aforementioned immunity that Unfleshed (and Constructs of other Lineages) have to fabrication making it safer to study them up close, a code of fabricants is an extremely useful case study in humanity by its absence-in fact, more than one Mystic has let the infection progress unabated, so as to have an unparalleled look into the process of becoming more and less than human (occasionally, the Vitriol gained form the milestone is even enough to recoup the lost progress on the Pilgrimage). After learning what they can from the futurians, though, it's almost invariable that non-Centimani Created attempt to eradicate or cure them; besides the fact that they are a threat to their human friends simply by virtue of fabricant existence, there's something about the fact that fabricants abandon all the best traits of humanity (compassion, empathy, appreciation for beauty) in order to become such a "perfect" machine that many Prometheans find morally offensive. Their entire existence is based around preparing for the metamorphosis into humanity, and here comes along some contagious mental illness that turns its hosts into the worst kind of Centimanus? Many Created aren't sure if they are more envious or disgusted with the fabricants complacency and their contempt for the gift of humanity (especially since fabricants cannot reproduce except by infection-the fundamental proof of worthlessness as a life form of any kind, only a virus). It doesn't help that the vast majority of Prometheans are still vulnerable, resulting in a massive stalling on the Pilgrimage while the Created reconnects with humanity or embraces Flux-there are no cases of non-Centimani Promethean fabricants. Of course, those same Centimani tend to think that a fabricant is an example of a human who finally gets it-many major outbreaks of technogores has been caused by a code finding a sympathetic Freak who can use their Pandorans as vectors.

                Vampire: While the unliving metaphor of the Kindred is generally more with sex and addiction than disease, there's a reason many of the medieval myths viewed them as more of a walking contagion slowly wearing down the living than as individuals in their own right. The All-Night Society has rather more in common with fabricants than anyone should be comfortable with; they're a virulent strain of creature that infects humans, giving them great physical and mental ability, but gradually reshaping them into callous, cold, and lifeless monsters who only care about the intelligent fulfillment of their instinctive prerogative. The main difference is that for a vampire, the debasement into a ruthless Beast is something they are partially at fault for their monstrosity-their condition doesn't feed on hate, and it doesn't destroy their capacity for empathy with their prey. To their credit, they can see the similarities too, and while they don't necessarily cotton on to the parts where they come out behind. they do realize that the very existence of futurians is a threat to more than just their meal ticket. It's actually incredibly rare for a Covenant as a whole to think it might be a good plan to weaponize the infection (even the Ordo Dracul thinks trying to study them without quarantine is a proposition that loses more than it gains)-not the least because vampires are not immune, and for them, it tends to be outright irreversible; Humanity is a measurement of inherent empathy to a degree, and fabrication is a mutually exclusive proposition with it. Quite simply, vampire fabricants are almost invariably dragur-and given the native intelligence and focus of fabricants, they're generally careful predators too.

                    Seeing as how Deionscribe isn't here right now, let's start up the next round of voting!

                    Corpse-Kisser: Parasites are often a remarkable group of creatures. From Toxoplasma gondii, the protist that controls the minds of rats to make them feed themselves to cats where it can sexually reproduce, to Cymothoa exigua, the tongue-eating louse that replaces the lost organ in fish and then happily nibbles on its hosts' food, the evolutionary strategies of a creature that needs to live in and off of other creatures to survive is almost beautiful in their variety and cleverness. The cryptid centipedes known as taxidermites are not really hazardous to the living, but their reproductive drive is even more remarkable; to the untrained eye, they bring back the dead. Of course, said mobile colonies, the corpse-kissers proper (so called because of the easiest way to transplant a colony) are not really the dead they resemble-but they're good at pretending otherwise, as the taxidemites possess a true hive mind, one that easily grows large enough to develop human-scale intelligence in a human body. Good thing for them, as humans are the only species that gathers so many potential hosts in one easily accessed place. At their core though, corpse-kissers are animals, and they aren't nearly vicious enough to make the majority of corpses themselves. Unfortunately, they're also trainable-and large colony-groups have very simple needs for the aspiring mastermind to fulfill...

                    Tomb Moths and Tombwalkers: Temples and crypts need guardians to protect their sacred grounds, but what happens when those guardians die or otherwise vacate their duty? They need to be replaced, obviously-and what better creature to replace them than the very would-be robbers? Bred from the silkmoth for the express purpose of ensuring that no infidel would escape a most ironic fate, tomb moths are eusocial insects whose scale dust contains a toxin that mummifies intruders alive, condemning them to a horrible, starving death inside a desiccated body-only for the moths to weave silken wrappings laced with necromantic energies around their victim, reanimating her as an intelligent undead who retains most to all of their previous life's knowledge, with a compulsion to protect and maintain the very site that destroyed her-and a biological need to, as the only thing they can eat any more are the larvae of the tomb moths. Thus, by necessity the tombwalker becomes a morbid entomologist by necessity-and, if she feels the need for another site to have an eternal guardian, nothing says she can't leave some excess larva behind her...

                    Wither Puppets: Grotesque mariontettes, wither puppets are bodies jerked around by an unseen creature on spider-web strings. Whatever personality they once had is long gone, silenced by whatever eldritch puppetmaster awaits at the other end of their strings-but that's not the bad part. The bad part is that they're contagious-and it's never one death equals one puppet, oh no. The name comes from the fact that, whatever limb they touch shrivels and becomes useless-and instills the subconscious knowledge that the limb can be replaced with another. It's always easier than it looks, to swap out the seemingly dead limb for another, healthy one...y'know, apart from the whole "it has to be fresh" business. And it's always a perfect match, changing to resemble whatever was lost-often, the new owner is so happy he doesn't realize it twitches involuntarily. So when the puppet comes back to wilt another limb, he replaces the limb again. And again, And again, until there's more transplants than original parts. That's when the strings appear, and another skein-kin joins the shuddering march.

                                        Corpse-Kisser (Breeder, Hiveman)

                                        Yes, yes, we get it. The dead-walker will be destroyed, and we will make the corpse our sister. Did you secure that entry to the morgue and fake IDs we asked?


                                        Nature has always put the lie to stating something is unnatural. "You cannot turn a shattered animal into more animals." Sponges, when fed through a sieve, will recognize their cells and reform into smaller individuals. "Single-celled organisms are microscopic." Plasmodial slime molds are multinuclear cells that expand rapidly to the point of being impossible to miss.

                                        "Many organisms cannot be one ,individual." Besides cellular slime molds, which fuse into one body to spread their spores once they have depleted the local food source, the supernatural. parasitic arthropods known as taxidermites show the lie applies to Kingdom Animalia as well.

                                        Corpse-kissers are one part hive, one part adult imago of a complex lifecycle of a supernatural mutation of centipede. They are, in many ways, classic movie zombies; walking corpses puppeted by an eldritch force whose only motivations are survival, reproduction, and hunger, with all goals frequently being enfolded into one. Except they aren't zombies in the sense of being undead, unintelligent, or even a metaphor for disease; they're the preserved armor of a communal swarm of arthropods who simply found a combination food source, vehicle, and weapon against anything that seeks to eat them. Particularly in the sense of being mindless-while one taxidermite is about as intelligent as the bugs they resemble, each one's shell is covered in mystical patterns and compounds that, when many of them are linked together, function as neurons; in the corpse-kisser,their bodies transform into a gestalt brain of chitin, one that grows more complex and aware as the controlling colony lays asexual eggs and hatches new members-in two days, the hiveman is as intelligent as a chimpanzee. By the end of the week, the breeder can match the average human in creative thought, and speaks the local language fluently.

                                        In their base form, taxidermites resemble the centipedes they are a mutation of, moderately sized black bugs with large mandibles and oddly expressive eyes. They never let themselves be seen like this for long, not the least because they can't actually survive on their own for too long in adult form; the same enzymes they use to animate the corpse-kissers literally holds their exoskeleton together, and without the soup inside either a host or a protected nest, the taxidermites fall apart into a black, soot-like substance. Thus, to prevent drying, hivemen transfer colonies of taxidermites by pressing their mouth directly around an open orifice, hence their common appellation, as a body's mouth is the most reliable opening without damaging their children's new home. If no breeders are around to transfer adult corpse bugs, though, their gray-white nymph forms are far hardier and can afford to be clever about finding a host distant from any existing population-it just takes a while longer to mature once inside the host and from there create a colony capable of puppeteering the moribund hive.

                                        Once inside a host, taxidermites quickly embark on an eating spree, devouring every last trace of internal biology for biomass to give to genetically identical eggs that mature with supernatural speed. As they devour the biomass and secrete their enzymes, the taxidermite hive mind begins to form "castes" of a sort, each dedicated to a different area of controlling the body-limbs, bursts of speed, sight, balance, and so on. This is helped by the fact that while taxidermites cannot fully access their potential for intelligence until working in concert, their unique chitin does carry with it memories from host to host-as the colony grows, it develops a sort of "genetic memory" of how to control and imitate a normal member of the species the corpse-kisser was made from. It's for this reason that corpse-kissers in human bodies can speak the dominant language of their area, as the taxidermites realized that was probably essential to pass on to the young at some point in this era of urban living and textual instructions (hivemen try to avoid speaking if they can, however-there's only so far you can go to disguise the fact your voice is really a bunch of well-coordinated bugs vibrating intensely). While the bugs devour the eyes as well, the first item on their agenda of castes is to create ocular taxidermites that both report on what the body can see as well as are colored like normal eyes for their species, allowing them to pass as normal at first glance (they can't disguise the cracks where one curled bug ends and the next begins that well, or the fact that their pupils are in fact many individual eyes on close inspection). Within a short while, the new corpse-kisser takes its first, shuddering steps and slinks off to finish devouring what is left of the viscera and bones in private.

                                        Despite their nature as colonies of bugs, breeders actually have individual personalities. They're usually simple, and informed by the basics of eat, sleep, breed, repeat, but many do develop an appreciation for things like art or politics (though their ideas can be a bit...odd). They don't have much in the way of individuality or fear, though-they know that their memories are just a transfer of taxidermites away from safekeeping, and as colonial minds they have a difficult time understanding why one would not sacrifice the self to save the many. They aren't killers, though-taxidermies aren't on a tight schedule if they can keep generating the preservative enzyme (actually easier in the modern era-something about television static catalyzes extremely efficient synthesis of the enzyme), they can wait for death to happen naturally-they aren't particularly attached to any species, and so long as there's at least 70% of skin remaining, they can patch up the disguise (though they may need to hunt in order to make up for missing biomass if their new host is particularly degraded).

                                        However, corpse-kissers do recognize that human environments are ideal for breeding. Besides being a highly numerous species who are so communal they can find colony-mates to sexually reproduce with (which produces true nymphs-unfertilized eggs hatch straight into the fragile adult stage, and are genetic clones) far more easily than just wandering the woods looking for mates, there's the simple fact that humans preserve their dead, and from there, bury them in convenient spots; most of the chemicals in funeral parlors are not toxic to the taxidermites, who have similar chemicals in their enzymes, so a graveyard or morgue is like a cheap suburb to them.

                                        However, this presents a problem-getting to the bodies. Ever pragmatic, the hivemen have realized something else-those same humans control access to their bodies, many of whom are supernatural beings themselves, or occultists who, most importantly, need muscle.

                                        Thus, the parasites have become something of a symbiote-mercenaries work for mutual benefit, don't they?
