    The First Night - How Do You Handle an In-Game Embrace?

    08-11-2016, 05:17 PM
    So, I've been running a 2nd Edition Requiem game set in Las Vegas where all the characters started out human. I had each of them pick a clan, Sire and Embrace preferences, and some potential touchstones, but otherwise they were statted out just like normal humans.

    Our next session will include their Embraces and will start their new life as Vampires. While pondering this and going over the rules, some interesting questions came to my mind that I wanted to get some other opinions on! The easiest way to go about this would be to 'add the Vampire template' as it were, and each of the characters would automatically receive their Discipline dots, 3-extra merit points, their extra attribute dot, their Mask & Dirge, and start at Humanity 7. However, in going over the themes of the game (Risk and reward, quickly made decisions and their consequences, and the inter-play of loss and gain) and the focus of the game which is to intimately explore the first few nights of vampirism and how the human life fades as the vampire life begins, I think it may be more interesting to experiment with these things a bit.

    My initial thought is to map out exactly what the Vampire template adds mechanically and offer that to each player as a sort of bank. Then, at an appropriate time in the narrative, they may 'spend' from this bank to advance their character up to what a normal Neonate would have. For example, my Daeva player (who would have no discipline dots on his sheet at this point) decides that he wants to use Majesty for the first time. He 'spends' a discipline dot from his starting three in the bank to snag 'Awe' and uses it in the scene and gets to keep it as an ability from there on out. This would simulate the experience of a brand-new vampire feeling out their abilities through instinct while adding a level of thematic risk and reward on the meta-level. ("I could better survive this encounter if I spent my last Discipline dot in Resilience, but is it worth it?")

    While that works pretty well for merits and disciplines, the main two things I have questions on are how to handle Humanity and the Mask & Dirge for a new vampire. After reading over the 'Breaking Points,' Humanity 7 seems far too well-adjusted for a brand spankin' new Vampire. Using powers, feeding on the unwilling/unknowing, and watching humans do activities they can't enjoy anymore should be poignant moments in a game like this. Should I start the players out at Humanity 10 and let them slip from there and settle out where they will? Should the rules be modified slightly to make slipping down to 7 easier and go back to the regular rules once they reach that level? Any ideas on that front would be helpful.

    As for the Mask & Dirge, the Mask could likely be easily chosen based on the mortal life the characters just let go of moments before they got Embraced. The Dirge, however, is more complicated. Should the player pick the Dirge at the onset along with the Mask and roleplay accordingly or should the players leave it blank until they understand better through roleplay how their character really is deep-down and among other vampires?

    Let me know your ideas, and if you've run a campaign where the Embrace happens to a player during the game, I'd love to know how you handled it and how that went!

      I've currently got three in-game 'embraces' - though they are all Revenants, so it's a little different.

      I'm doing the same thing with disciplines. They can manifest a new dot during a scene when an opportunity arises. However, I've been trying to make it an important moment where the discipline actually makes the difference. So basically, letting that first manifestation be an auto-success of a sort rather than a roll. I also try to narrate the descriptions of how the discipline feels as they use it. Depending on how thematic / narrative you want to get, you can even tailor those descriptions to the Clans. So a Daeva's vigor might feel different than Nosferatu's. A Ventrue's Animalism is different than the Gangrel's. Just another way to hammer home Clan thematics if you are into that.

      I started them all off at Humanity 7, but I also knew they'd be likely facing breaking points pretty quick since Revenants wake up starving every night so are facing down heavy frenzy checks every time they feed. I do think it's a glitch in RAW that newly created Vampires start off at 7 Humanity, since that kind of misses those good 'new vamp' breaking points at level 8. I'd say go for the bump.

      For Dirge, I gave them all the same one to start:

      Reluctant Supernatural
      You've been brought kicking and screaming across the supernatural threshold. Your role in that world has yet to be defined. You're too busy figuring out what that world actually is.
      • Single Willpower: Regain a point of Willpower whenever you experience a new facet of your supernatural life.
      • All Willpower: Regain all Willpower when you are accepted as a part of the full Kindred Community. Choose a new Dirge at that point.
      You could reskin it pretty easily to something like Fledgling to void out the Revenant aspect. For Mask, I had them start with the closest thing they were as a human. That's worked pretty well, as it's generated built in conflict between keeping or abandoning their old life as they explore more of the setting, and lose more and more aspects of that life. Abandoning jobs, losing/scaring family members, etc. I'll give them a chance to change it once a chapter ends or during a scene when they lose humanity and more of their beast begins to slip through.

                  " Humane (Humanity 7-8): A vampire in this range of
                  Humanity is either very good at maintaining her attachments,
                  or she’s very new to the Danse Macabre. She has little difficulty
                  blending in with humans. She remembers with perfectly clarity
                  what mortal life was like. She remembers the way food tasted.
                  She remembers the way sweat felt across her skin. She feels pain
                  for those hurt by her actions and those of her kind. "

                  This is what it says a vampire with 7-8 should act like. Humanity seems like a way to blend in with the humans..They have no negatives and no positives to deal with a human. They are balanced. She remembers what it was like to be human, and feels bad for those she hurts. It seems like an acceptable starting point for a vampire. You broke your leg and you know it's broken and you accept that. Like a vampire. You know your life isnt what it was and you accept that. going for more human than the average human takes work.

                  Humanity 9-10
                  "Ascetic (Humanity 9-10): A rare vampire with this degree of
                  Humanity goes out of her way to immerse herself in mortal affairs,
                  and to feed sensibly. She’s poised and confident in her dealings;
                  she’s an expert in the way humans behave, feel, and think. She
                  manages to think and act the way they do, in the way an expert
                  method actor would. She appears as an iconic, idealized human.
                  Gain a +2 die bonus to any rolls to relate to humans."

                  I would say most humans dont fall into this. There arent many "experts in the way humans behave think and feel" that takes effort and work. Many of us just go by what we know and what we feel personally. Perhaps a psychologist or something of the like. You would have to go out of your way to immerse yourself into human affairs. Like specifically go out of your way to not be a vampire. Again that takes a lot of effort, in my opinion. Why start them off acting more human than the average human? why give them bonuses to relate to humans? What if they werent good at it in the first place. What if they were socially awkward and reclusive, now they are suddenly really good at human interaction? Suddenly really good at understanding emotions humans have? Just because we are human does not mean we understand human emotions the way a psychologist would. Are your players "Icononic idealized humans" ?

                  Again this is just my opinion on why I wouldnt start a player off at 9 or 10. Humanity 9 and 10 is more human than most humans, That is why I strongly believe starting at 7 is intentional and not a glitch. Some of those breaking points, I would think an average vampire wouldnt have a problem doing. Vampires can eat with humans, most vampires wouldnt have problems creating a ghoul or using their dominate or majesty to coerce something. So why start them at a place where they should feel bad about it?



                  I recently started out my real life group as freshly embraced. they had all their dots on paper to start, but I didnt let them use their disciplines until their mentor gave them a little training on the matter. with their 3 extra merit dots I made them take something that could naturally occur. for example (but not limited to ) enticing, cut throat, striking looks, acute senses, double jointed. Something that could be justified by the supernatural shift.

                  I did let them start with their touchstones, most of them were family members or a family doctor. Someone they were super attached to in life, and that attachment just stayed with. I did not make them find new people to attach to.

                  For Mask and Dirge I had them pick something that they were closest to while they were human or if they didnt know I picked one closest to what their back story told me. No one had issues with that. We did have to modify child a little for someone's dirge. But he is playing it great already. And Monster for one of the gangrel because in life he was pretty violent, so that fit him well too.

                      I like the idea of bumping them up in Humanity, but Vitalis makes a good point that Humanity 9 may be too high. Perhaps Humanity 8 would be better; that way there's still ample room for Breaking Points to occur to your young vampires with context in mind.

                      For example, the act of feeding on an unknowing or unwilling person would be a Breaking Point at Humanity 9, but if it's especially personal for the character (say, feeding on a close friend, enemy, or family member), then it could be justified as a Breaking Point at Humanity 8. The act of creating a ghoul would be a Breaking Point at Humanity 8 normally (say, an acquaintance); however, if it's more personal (say, somebody close), then it would be a Breaking Point at Humanity 7 - but if it's particularly 'distant' from the character (say, a complete stranger) then it would be a Breaking Point at Humanity 9. It could be interesting to see your new vampires' attempts to create double lives for themselves separate from their old ones in an attempt to cling to their old humanity; it's only when the two lives start to overlap do they risk a more poignant decline and the stakes are raised.

                      I also like Charlaquin's idea on developing Touchstones in-game. In my experience playing, it's fairly common to decide upon Touchstones as the game is actually occurring - as the important mortals to your character become clearer as the game is played - and it's fairly common for initial ideas that seemed good at the time to lose a lot of lustre and importance. A Touchstone should be an important part of the game, and if you feel like your Touchstone is less important than another mortal that's getting more 'screen-time' and is simply more interesting, I would consider making the switch and having that more interesting / important mortal be your character's Touchstone instead.

                              Thank you for the responses thus far!

                              I forgot about the 'Reluctant Supernatural' dirge! It seems like it would be a good placeholder if I went that route. However, the lack of a dirge (and the mechanical benefits of having one) provides some good incentive for the characters to dig into vampire society some and get immersed rather than stay holed up in their havens. Not that my players would do that, anyway!

                              As far as Touchstones go, I agree that developing them in the game is an important part of the experience. That's why I had the players give me an NPC or two that is connected with their character, and I made sure that in the two sessions before the Embrace, each of them was given screen-time and the relationships were seen beforehand. So, I think from there it's smart to 'earn' the mechanical benefits by reaching out to some of the humans they knew in life or someone entirely new in order to establish Touchstones, so good ideas on that front!

                              Humanity is still a tricky thing, but the perspectives presented here were interesting. I personally think the best compromise would be to shift a few breaking points and start them off a step higher at 8. My reasoning for this is that I'd imagine that, for most mortals of a solid Integrity score (all of my players maintained a 7), the first few nights of being a vampire are very shocking. Grappling with the Beast, being able to do things they couldn't possibly do before, the need to literally feed off of humans, those should be things that make them question who and what they are which is the point of Humanity Breaking Points anyway. Also, I think a newly made vampire is a little more tender humanity wise so far as one failed Breaking Point wouldn't immediately push them into the 'Balanced' mindset.

                              But I digress. I have no issue with the Humanity system for vampires as young as this according to RAW. For example, my other current campaign has vampires who have been vampires for a few months and started happily at Humanity 7 with no issue. My only reason for considering an alternative was to further emphasize the shift from human to vampire mechanically to help reflect the ideas and themes of the chronicle.
