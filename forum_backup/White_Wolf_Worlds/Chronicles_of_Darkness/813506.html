    Sell an Apocalypse fan on Forsaken; or W;tF=WTF?

    01-21-2016, 02:22 PM
    My favorite game line of the CWoD was W:tA. I've been a fan of wolves and werewolves and werecreatures of all kinds for as long as I can remember. The moment I picked up W:tA Players Guide 2ed, I was hooked. A game with werewolves as the good guys? Against a palpable force of entropy and death? Fate of the world at stake? Sign me Up! So imagine my disappointment upon reading W:tF, and finding NOTHING that I could relate to. Tribes that had no connection to any existing culture or mythology. No global treat or conspiracy to struggle against. Gone were Gaia's defenders, replaced with the spiritual equivalent of a Border Patrol. WTF, indeed! So, aside from the sandbox ruleset,(still not sure that's a feature, I miss having Metaplot as a backdrop, you could always ignore it if it didn't fit your story) what's so great about Forsaken?

    "A free society is one in which it is safe to be unpopular."

      Well, the big draws for me are that Tribes are wholly unique and don't have any connection to existing culture or mythology, a lack of a global threat or conspiracy for werewolves to struggle against, that werewolves mostly stick to protecting their territories and there aren't Septs or large-scale hierarchies and aren't environmental activists, and that there's no major metaplot to color the players' ideas about the setting or distract from the activities of the PCs.

      So what I'm saying is that I'm probably not the best person to sell it to you.

      I've got some friends who prefer Apocalypse but have some nice things to say about Forsaken, I'll repeat what they say:
      • The way the spirit world works in Forsaken is more interesting than in Apocalypse: They like that all spirits are Darwinian and alien and such, and that even spirits of "good" things can behave terribly.
      • Werewolves aren't all necessarily defenders of Gaia, and they're not forced into the role of soldiers.
      • If you want werewolf organizations bigger than packs that are based on real-world mythology or culture, there are Lodges which are nicely integrated.

      That's it.

      I like 2E Forsaken better than 1E. The emphasis on packs and territory management reminds me of Ars Magica. Wolf-Blooded got an expanded role and some more interesting build options to match. The idigam are probably the coolest enemies I've encountered in WoD or CofD because they're alien cosmic entities that are badass and can dramatically alter the tone and scope of your Chronicle, but won't derail the setting (for example, I'm running a game set in the 90s in Washington state and also a game set in 2015 Texas: the 90s werewolves can all be wiped out by an idigam, but the Texas ones won't necessarily be affected).

      Hope that helps, and I hope somebody else can help more.

          Forsaken is fun because it is so entangled in its own rich mythology; one failing of Apocalypse is that there are very few stories about Gaia or the Wyrm as anything other than forces and powers. With Forsaken, you know that you are the heirs of Father Wolf and his Firstborn, that you are daughters of mad Luna, that the Idigam you now face were banished by your father's will and the Hosts you fight were torn asunder in his jaws. The Pure don't fight you because they're evil or corrupted, but because they have a mythological need to destroy you (and they might be somewhat justified).

          Apocalypse wears a human facade. Forsaken spins a unique tale all its own.

                  Well there's nothing wrong with sticking with Apocalypse:

                  1) Don't look to Forsaken to replace Apocalypse. It's not trying to. Its it's own game, with it's own concepts. There are lots of elements in Forsaken that can incorporate themes and elements you might enjoy, but they're never going to be Apocalypse. Part of that is what you might really get into with Forsaken isn't in the same place as it would have been in Apocalypse. For example Lodges (which are somewhat analogous to Apocalypse Camps) are where you get into cultural groups. So working in Lodges more strongly can help get some of those elements back in more than default.

                  2) Read Forsaken 2e. 1e was good, but 2e completely blew it out of the water. It really ramps up an evocative, and a inspiring setting. It's not Apocalypse. It's something different. It also hammers home that what Forsaken werewolves are, more than anything else: predators on the hunt. Getting away from the Apocalypse idea of packs was a huge help as well.

                  3) Remember that Forsaken is meant to be much more of a toolkit. What's lacking, and what's stressed, can be changed without messing up the game more easily than might be readily apparent.

                      Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                      3) Remember that Forsaken is meant to be much more of a toolkit. What's lacking, and what's stressed, can be changed without messing up the game more easily than might be readily apparent.
                      This is so true. As an example relevant to your interests, OP, here:

                      Umaraha'e: The Children of the Worm - A Mystery Cult that worships a being they call the Worm (or is that "Wyrm?"), a vast cosmic force of entropy and consumption. Ties into Mage.

                      Check out the Hosts, too. Great foes for werewolves. Here's one I made up, replete with fiction to help inspire you.

                      Zibhesi: The Mosquito Hosts

                      You might notice a little bit of Apocalypse in there, which is great because WTA was my favorite Old World setting, and had perhaps my favorite playable race in any game ever, the Mokolé.

                      I've even made plans to run Apocalypse using the Forsaken 2E engine (CofD engine in general). You might find it fairly easy to translate using the Werewolf Translation Guide, for instance, bringing some of your favorite elements into Forsaken.

                      But if you want to take Forsaken on its own merits, read through the 2E core. You'll see that in losing the big picture, the game is a tightly focused, highly thematic game of supernatural hunters. The Wolf Must Hunt. Read the Idigam Chronicle Anthology for lots of inspiration. The idigam are big, powerful, alien spirits capable of warping the nature of the things around them. Defeating them often means bringing together many packs and other allies, pulling out all the stops—great for when you want to jump from a more local story to a larger-scale one for a chronicle. Their history ties into Uratha mythology in some fascinating ways: they're ancient spirits once imprisoned on the moon by Father Wolf, the great wolf-god from whom werewolves are descended.

                      Also note that you can easily inject cultural trappings in any of the tribes or even auspices, individual packs, whole protectorates, etc. In my own game, set in the Pacific Northwest, a lot of the Native American history, culture, and mythology inform the spirit world and the werewolves in that region.

                      Forsaken puts you in the pads of the consummate hunters. Werewolves descend from godlike spirits of Wolf and Moon. They are half-flesh, half-spirit beings capable of walking between worlds. Their most ancient imperative: hunt the realms as Urfarah once did. Be faithful to the hunt. Seek balance, Harmony, in all things. Keep the prey in its proper place, and maintain the fragile balance between realms. They wield spirit-magic and lupine ferocity as they battle spirits, humans, monstrous Claimed, the Hosts, and each other. They wear human skins but they are not human, nor are they wolf. They've become something different, greater perhaps, occupying a unique space in their world. They grow, they live, they love, they fight, and they die. Unlike spirits or humans, their forms are protean, yet they are not amorphous idigam. Like wolves and humans, they feel, because theirs is a life lived without reservations. You are Wolf, and the wolf must hunt. Werewolves can't and don't sit back and let the world come to them. They lope into the night, howling as a pack, taking what they need by the throat. Blood runs hot. Flesh parts and shifts, bones crack, breath steams from flaring nostrils. It's a dangerous world in the shadows, full of monsters human and inhuman ... and as werewolves, you hunt them all.

                      The Sacred Hunt calls, brother. Raise your howl along with ours!

If it still doesn't grab you, for whatever reason (including not Apocalypse enough), then it might just not be the game for you.
