    Seconding Dataweaver yet again.

    Further more you are going to run into complications vis-a-vis the theoretical ideal and the practical example of a splat. Mages, for instance, are all but guaranteed to top nearly every category. This will come as no surprise to anyone who knows Mage well, it's a game where power is a key theme, and the general response to "Can a Mage do that?" is "Yes, eleven different ways". Of course, in practical application this is miles from the truth, a real character won't have access to all Arcana, unfailing situational comprehension or a pure utilitarian personality, and that's not accounting for time, scenario and the player simply dropping the ball. But the theoretical Mage-Splat Mage does, and that's what the Tier list will reflect.

    Similarly, Vampires are likely to rank low in many categories where, in the context of a story, they would probably shine. Combat is a good example, Vampires pale in comparison to Werewolves in white room battles. We had a thread on it, it's not a good matchup...in the white room. Largely because the white room utterly neglects all the social manipulation, preparation, and sheer get-out-of-dodge potential the kindred possess. Throw in context to manipulate and suddenly Vampires will astonish you with their out of the box problem solving. Of course that's true for all splats (especially Demon, the unchained are ungodly good at out of the box problem solving), but that really only makes the tier problem worse.

    Never the less, if we are to engage in tier making I shall propose a few:
    1. Which splats are best at getting elected president?
    2. Which splats are best at sleeping?
    3. Which splats are best at cleaning up the streets? (I mean literally cleaning them of dirt and litter, not crime. Ignore crime. Except littering, assuming that's a crime where you live.)
    4. Which splats are best at eating someone's Grandmother, putting on her cloths, and impersonating her when her granddaughter comes to visit?

                        Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
                        It has nothing to do with liking it or not. It was that it didn't work.
                        One, this is an opinion, not fact. Two, it depends on edition, blanket statements are meaningless. Three, it doesn't matter if it doesn't work. What matters is that ranking power and ability is an innate part of D&D. Saying otherwise is just being in denial.

                        Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                        People have said in this thread that a tier system doesn't make sense since there are multiple different forms of power and capabilities that each possesses and each are better at different niches.... except that is true in D&D/PF and making a tier system isn't that difficult in it. I think there is a large amount of backlash against tiers in the CofD community because of things like "which would beat which in a fight" and white room scenario's and such, which can understandably leave a bad taste in everyones mouths, but seriously, tiers are not that terrible of a concept.
                        Yes, it pretty much is a terrible concept. It makes sense in D&D, because you have all these options to pick from when choosing a race and class. You have the options of picking wizard, sorcerer, warlock, bard, etc. Ranking serves a purpose there, because it informs meaningful choices, and helps prevent character overlap. Furthermore, ranking relies on specific builds. The overwhelming majority of games in the CofD aren't going to include a mix of different supernaturals, however, and the way characters are make it hard to nail down "specific builds." Even games that do involve crossover characters tend to be less worried about balance, because the innate drawbacks of each supernatural (daylight restriction, essence needs, drives of the splats) tend to be magnified and cause issues. So what if the vampire can socialize better than anyone else when he has to be asleep during business hours? How good is a werewolf in a physical confrontation if you risk Death Rage and killing your entire group every single time?

                        There is simply no justification for a tier system other than proving your favorite splat is "better" than another. Which is meaningless, because each game has its individual focus.

                        ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

                        Playtesting and personal experience reported from different people have also found that personal understanding of the rules and individual creativity in the environment, as well as ST interpretation / biases of the games, have a huge impact on how things play out.

                        Vampires excell at mind-control. If it relates to that in some way, vampires come out ahead.
                        Werewolves excell at the hunt. This often includes chases, tracking and combat as secondary features.
                        Mages are awesome at investigation and research. Studying mystries is there thing, scooby gang.
                        Prometheans are survivors. Good luck killing one.
                        Changelings are illusionists and talecrafters. Most every Contract had a temporary-dream-fading-away quality to them.
                        Demons are spies. Spy games are their forte. Want to infiltrate somewhere? Your people for the job.
                        Beasts are experts at dealing with other supernaturals.

                        This above list, which focuses on the strengths of each individual game, is the only method I've seen that really "rates" the supernaturals in any meaninful way. Vampires always beat out anyone else at mind control. Even mages need Mind 4 just to open all doors in a social maneuver system. Vampires can do that with a vinculum as an innate ability, and that's not touching their Disciplines which often bypass the need for opening Doors in the first place. Vampire Predatory Auras also relate to magical mind-influence, where a werewolf's Aspect relates to hunting. Each of a werewolf's five forms somehow helps them with hunting down others.

                        Werewolves are single handedly best at tracking - Auspex, Space magic, and Atavisms don't come close to what a werewolf's nose can pull off, let alone the right Gifts. Yes, I said Space mages lose to werewolves when it comes to tracking people. Sure, they could theoretically track someone... if they either were familiar with the person, or had the person's name and some part of their body for a working sympathetic connection; wolves aren't nearly so restricted. Mages need to investigate some first... which is only fair, because investigation is the Mage forte. Werewolf also gets more fighting merits than I've seen elsewhere - vampires get two, beasts get one, demons get... well, I suppose you could count the Advanced Form merit, kind of. Werewolves get at least eight, and eleven if you count Favored Form, Living Weapon and Fortified Form, giving a lot of variety and possibilities.

                        Mages are investigators and researchers. They seek out mysteries to study. Almost everything revolves around information in some way. Low level Arcana? Built to reveal information. Mage Sight abilities? Reveal information. Sympathetic connection? Need information to track someone. Organization? The Lex Magica relies on studying precidents and established law, and that means hitting the books rather than being good at arguing. Orders are based on mystery cults, people seeking mysteries and the unexplained in the larger world. The Mysterium is a giant network of libraries across the world. People say that, in theory, a mage can do anything. Like everything else in mage, theory rarely plays out in reality. Because mages are only as strong as whatever they prepare, and preparation relies on information of what the day will bring.

                        Demon is a spy game. Cover is their "aura" and lets them hide nearly perfectly from anyone or anything. Naturally undetectable, unable to be caught in a lie, and ability to change their faces and develop the necessary contacts or status to get into anywhere is an innate ability. That's not counting any embeds, exploits, or demon form abilities. Exposure is their weakness - start having people pry into your life, and it all comes undone. Demons seem to be the "strongest" supernatural until you realize that they have to keep everything under wraps, or they die. Even the famed ability to lie only passes cursory glances and mundane tells - abilities like mind reading can bypass to the truth easily, and you can bet the GM has psychics around.

                        Beast is a game that revolves around dealing with other supernaturals, both as allies and antagonists. They literally become stronger for it, and the overwhelming majority of their abilities revolve around dealing with these others. Hunter is much the same - with a stronger emphasis on dealing with other supernaturals as antagonists. In many ways, these two games are two sides of the same exact coin.

                        Prometheans are survivors. They're tough to kill, they can eat almost anything to no ill effect (seriously - I once had a Promethean that lived off gasoline with no ill effect), can go longer without sleep than others. Its been too long since I've played 1e, and 2e's not out, so I'll leave it at this.

                        Changelings are illusionists and wish granters. The Masks, dreams, even Contracts tend to operate on a "ephemeral dream that fades away" principle. Even Contracts of the Forge, make things that fade away. Pledges are like wishes - it lasts as long as you follow the twisted logic of the Wyrd, and eveything can turn to nightmares. Unlike other oaths and similar abilities, pledges can be tricked into. Even Clarity reflects an inability to tell illusory fantasy from reality. Talecrafting is another thing that relates to illusions/dreams, because you're bringing these stories to life, forcing reality to conform to dream logic. It always amazed me how often people underestimated Changelings. No one can match changelings with making your wishes come true. Or twisting said wishes.



                        There, that's your ranking. Focus on your splat's strengths, and you're better for it. That's the best I've personally seen.
