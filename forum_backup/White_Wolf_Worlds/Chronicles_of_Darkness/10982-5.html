        Given the way combat works after the GMC revision I'm not too concerned with knockout punch. If a player's intent is to not deal with a threat right now then it's plenty useful, but the second they are interested in using combat to effect longer lasting meaningful narrative changes that goes out the window. A player using Knockout Punch is giving up the chance to Beat Down their enemy. They do no long lasting damage, do not force willpower expenditures, and still need to beat their defense with a stacking penalty for failure which against any capable combat character is no guarantee, especially if they are built to be a melee combatant (which means prioritizing strength) unless they are willing to expend willpower to do it.

        Consistent use of Knockout Punch is a good way to avoid conflict for a time, but under the GMC rules its also a good way to not fulfill aspirations, avoid risking cover, not take any damage at all, , etc. If you don't take any risks the GMC rules you'll find yourself gaining very little in the way of beats and not really resolving situations.

        I haven't seen it in play, but I don't really see it proving to be too heavy handed.

                                                    Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                    I've no interest in white-room testing. I'm a big believer its pointless, not to mention I find it utterly boring. What I said in previous post is what I meant. I believe (the foolish optimistic I am on this topic) that people can collate opinions about things and produce a useful resource for other people.

                                                    If people have had a chance to play Demon in the last 11 days since the Kickstarter started then I agree that actual play analysis would be more useful then untested hypothesizing. Which is not to say that untested hypothesizing is completely useless, sometimes you hit the nail on the head without meaning to after all. The point is to just get a debate flowing, collate ideas and such.
                                                    For whatever this may be worth...

                                                    While I've not gotten to play or run Demon in the past 11 days (or play it ever), I did run one of the playtest groups. One of the PC's in that group had as his Embeds Strike First, Knockout Punch, Hush, and In My Pocket.

                                                    He used Knockout Punch....twice, I think. Once to knock out a guy who'd mugged another member of the Ring, and once on a cop in a bathroom (don't ask). In the latter case, I was actually quite lenient and let him chain it together with Hush and Strike First. Poor guy was just standing there at the urinal, doing his thing, and as soon as he finished, boom. There it went. At no point was Knockout Punch an actual issue (although I think I did make Defense apply, and it looks like that's in the rules now - don't remember for sure what was in the playtest material). The story moved along just fine, and nothing was "broken," thrown off the rails, or what-have-you. None of the other players felt overshadowed by what that one character was doing. Incidentally, when the Ring later got into a big combat, that character didn't end up doing much, actually - the Guardian ended up doing the most damage due to having Blade Hand as a Demonic form ability.

                                                    That's the only actual play experience I have with the Embed, so take that for what it's worth. I do agree that seeing how something works in play is much more suitable and satisfying than any "white room" scenario.

                                                        A couple things about Knockout Punch as currently presented that I feel are being skimmed over:
                                                        1) It's cumulatively penalized per miss in a scene on top of the resistance by Defense — which, lest we forget, is higher in GMC's rules than it was in the old nWoD system.
                                                        2) Even a cursory attempt to revive the victim works unless you rolled an exceptional success.

                                                        Its utility is drastically undercut in scenarios where you have to deal with any degree of numbers disadvantage that allows one enemy to take a moment to wake their fallen comrade. Meanwhile it's still very much useful as a power for quickly dispatching a lone guard.
