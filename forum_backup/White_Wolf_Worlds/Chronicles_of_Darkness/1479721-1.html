            On a different note, here's our first excerpt: the Oberlochs! An old favourite from Bloodlines: The Whichever One of Those Books They're From I'm Not Looking It Up Right Now.

            I don't have much to say about this bloodline, other than I've always really liked the Oberlochs (I apparently have a thing for aging vampires). That said, I've never liked aging vampires where the bane/weakness is that you become infirm. That always struck me as both unfun and not really something that's going to come up in most chronicles. It seemed to me that the crux of the Oberlochs is that they're utterly alienated from the rest of the All Night Society. It also seemed like joining the bloodline was kind of a hard sell, so now part of their weakness is that the Embrace confers it like a disease (there are, of course, social reasons one might want to be an Oberloch, but sometimes you want the system to reflect that). Still, apart from some mechanical quality of life updates, these aren't too different from the original version, though I feel like the backstory is a little more open to interpretation and, thus, more story opportunities:

            Oberlochs: The ones who want you gone

            "Get out or get dead. All the same to me."

            The city is a straightjacket. To live in a modern metropolis, Kindred leash themselves with human mores in the hope that they might keep the prey ignorant to the monsters lurking in the dark. Maybe that means better eating, but anyone who sacrifices liberty for gratification is a fool, a dog who’ll sniff any master’s throne for a big enough bone. The Oberlochs know better. Living off the grid has taught them many harsh lessons, but the one that took root was the simplest: Freedom trumps safety. Safety is an illusion for bloated ticks who think hiding in concrete slabs is living.

            The Kin are the most isolated bloodline in the All Night Society. Called Rats among the Kindred (at least Kindred who’ve survived an encounter), they make their havens in the hamlets and homesteads that still riddle Appalachia, whether mountain hideaways or just towns that never got around to incorporating. They nest in places the long arc of progress didn’t give a shit about, bringing kine to heel with the Blood and the boot. They’re moonshiners with secret recipes for bathtub gin and biker crank. They’re sallow-eyed villagers who turn hitchhikers into statistics. They’re mutant cannibals on the edge of town who worship made-up death gods just for the thrill of it. No two broods are alike, but they all follow the same code: outsiders stay out. This is the highest law of the grandparents, a gerontocracy composed of the bloodline’s mustiest elders, and they’ll enforce it with an authoritarian zeal the Invictus would blush at.

            That grandparent terminology isn’t just metaphor, either. Rather than the eternal summer of other Kindred, the Oberlochs suffer a ceaseless winter, aging into husks as they surpass mortal lifespans. This even carries over into other forms, though some find petty pleasures in taking on the shape of skeletal wolves and bats to scare off trespassers. All who bear Oberloch Vitae share this burden, and so the bloodline seeks out ever fresher flesh to fasten to its decrepit corpse.

            The reasons for this curse are disputed. Back in the 1800s, the mortal Oberloch family fed on death long before they became vampires. Their mining concern was the most profitable in Pennsylvania, and its coffers were slick with its workers’ blood. Among their many victims were the mortal relatives of a Gangrel wanderer called Bartholomew Ahern, who returned to his native soil only to find most of his kin dead or maimed in the mines. Without much of a grip on Humanity in the first place, the loss fueled a lust for vengeance that overtook his reason, and he bound every worker to his Vitae in order to unleash them on the family’s manor house. Fire consumed most of Millionaire’s Row that night, and the Oberloch estate was the blazing epicenter.

            Only one family member survived: Alice, the mother. Ahern was impressed as she climbed from the wreckage, gifting her with the Embrace for her desperation, and this is the lie Alice still tells her grandchilder to this very night.

            Whatever the true catalyst for her Damnation, Alice fled into the wild. Ashamed of what she’d become, and terrified of being caught by her family’s former victims, she wandered Appalachia for many years, scraping by on the blood of beasts and unfortunate travelers. When her hair began to gray and her skin to wrinkle, she didn’t realize anything was wrong, taking it as a given that the punishment for her family’s sins was to be eternal suffering. However, as the years grew longer, she became lonely, missing the pleasures of her daylight life. In Harmony, Pennsylvania, a backwater even among backwaters, Alice took back what was taken from her. Her coal baron husband taught her everything she needed to know about ruling over peasants, and years of life on the lam had ground out whatever compassion she had left.

            Tonight, Old Alice still casts a shadow over her bloodline, prima inter pares of the grandparents. Under her watchful, desiccated eyes, the Oberlochs expand far and wide across the Midwest, testing prospects for physical strength, low cunning, and an inclination to go along to get along.

            The Oberlochs believe in freedom, sure, but they’re not anarchists. Liberty has to have structure, and the bloodline’s leaders know the kind of bedlam that would befall their domains if they let their childer run wild. They are Savages, after all. Which isn’t to say there’s no room for upward mobility, but you also have to take what you can get. If someone else wants it, you’d better be ready to stake them for the sun, Kin or otherwise. If not, it was never yours to begin with.

            Some Kindred worry the Kin are siring an army, hidden from the Masquerade’s scrutiny in their rural strongholds. Rumors make the rounds that the Rats are abandoning their nests, making their way into domains they would’ve once avoided. Rural America only continues to shrink, and forced from their domains, the time may be coming for the Oberlochs to fight back.

            Parent Clan: Gangrel

            Nicknames: Kin (among themselves), Rats (derogatory)

            Bloodline Bane (The Putrefying Curse): The Oberlochs age. Due to this eternal half-life, Kin have difficulty connecting to true immortals, taking [REDACTED]. In addition, when Kin Embrace, their childer join their bloodline automatically.

            Disciplines: Animalism, Dominate, Protean, Resilience

                            Anyway, a happy Sunday to you all! Time for another excerpt, this time for another First Edition bloodline: The Childer of the Morrigan, from Circle of the Crone.

                            This bloodline was on my list fairly early on. They're much beloved in the fandom, and, like the Oberlochs, they're another one that didn't need a lot of overhauling (unlike, say, the Dead Wolves and Mystikoi, who needed a bit more work). The big change here is a simplification of the bloodline's bane and an upgrade to its relationship to Cruac.

                            The original bane, like many 1E weaknesses, was long and complicated, and it relied on the late and not lamented Derangement system. So, we took the basic concept and condensed it: Now, if they back down from a challenge/conflict, they suffer a penalty to frenzy, and the more challenges they back down from in a night, the worse it gets.

                            In terms of Cruac, in 1E, they got a free rite at Blood Potency 3 (more or less). Not very exciting, and the original book treats this stystem as optional. We figured, let's go with something fancier. Now, the Morrigans are chaos witches: any Cruac spell they know of three dots or lower, they can choose to cast as an instant action, but if they fail, they cause a side effect, inflicting them and anyone else around them with temporary banes.

                            We also tweaked the backstory a bit, partially because the Circle of the Crone's own backstory is a bit different in 2E. And, as usual, we wanted to make things a little more multiple-choice in terms of where they really come from and what they really are.

                            Tomorrow, I'll be back with another 1E bloodlne: The Dead Wolves.

                            Here are the Morrigans!

                            Childer of the Morrigan: The ones who never stop fighting you

                            "My goddess doesn't want your apologies — just your blood."

                            Something vicious this way comes. Its left-hand holds a bleeding ax, blade chipped and well-loved; its right holds your best friend’s head. You run, and its laugh pursues you, the sound of carrion crows celebrating a fresh meal.

                            The Circle of the Crone is a young covenant, not even two centuries past its gory birth. Founded from a place of oppression and persecution, the Mother’s Army’s formation was — by necessity — a violent one, and the Childer of the Morrigan were at the vanguard of that violence. They still are. Acting as unforgiving guardians for their fellow Acolytes, the Stormcrows defend their covens and cults against those who would see them broken. In domains where they’re active, they hunt known enemies of the Crone and slay them for sport, their remains put on garish display so the rest of the All Night Society gets the message: To attack the Dark Mother is to invoke the wrath of the Morrigans, and like the goddess they’re named for, their fury is endless.

                            The unifying philosophy of the Mother’s Army (if the notoriously fractious covenant can be said to have one), is evolution and creation through tribulation — suffer, endure, and grow stronger. Many Acolytes view conflict as a chance for growth, but while some are content to wait for it to come to them, others like to strike the sparks of their own trials by fire. Stormcrows exemplify the latter philosophy, preferring to make their own trouble.

                            While the stereotypical image of a Morrigan is that of a hard-bitten warrior spattered with gore, the bloodline understands modern nights have spawned a wide variety of conflicts that require diverse — and not always immediately violent — solutions. Broken teeth and shattered glass are all well and good, but you can’t always punch your problems away. It’s for this reason that hacktivists, lobbyists, academic firebrands, and more can find a home with the Stormcrows, united by their desire for direct action.
                            Others in the Circle argue over dogma; the Morrigans see a threat and snuff it out. While their Gangrel heritage does predispose them to rather reckless solutions, a Stormcrow is just as likely to destroy an enemy with a strategically leaked email as with a sledgehammer to the skull.

                            While this decisiveness and ferocity earns the Morrigan healthy measures of respect and fear, these traits cause as many problems as they solve. Diplomacy goes out the window once a Stormcrow hurls a Molotov cocktail through it, and their “shoot first, ask questions while shooting” attitude doesn’t win them many friends outside the Circle, or even within it. Childer of the Morrigan may be the Acolytes’ most effective enforcers, but their actions are often the flashpoint that escalates a minor conflict into holy war. Not that the Stormcrows mind, of course — they’re closest to their goddess with viscera in their hair and screams in their ears.

                            This reckless attitude extends to the Morrigans’ use of Crúac. Something in the Stormcrows’ blood allows them to enact rituals in a tiny fraction of the time they should need, reducing what would normally be an hour of frantic dancing and chanting to a single muttered prayer and a spray of Vitae. The Childer of the Morrigan claim this ability proves they’re favored by the Crone as her chosen warriors, but other Acolytes say it’s another reason to give the bloodline a wide berth. When these rushed rituals work, they’re just as effective as if they had been done traditionally, but when they don’t, the results are hazardous to not only the Morrigan enacting them, but to anyone unlucky enough to be nearby.

                            Despite their harsh methods (or maybe because of them), the Childer of the Morrigan enjoy a high status in their covenant due to their willingness to fling themselves into danger on behalf of the Dark Mother. Problems arise in how they’re perceived by outsiders. Rightly or wrongly, many Kindred view Stormcrows as zealots first and very little second, which can make forming ties outside of the Mother’s Army difficult. Their dedication to being the most brutal monsters in a covenant that glorifies monstrousness makes it hard to relate to mortals as well, and many Morrigans find their Humanity frays badly before long.

                            Some Childer counter this by immersing themselves in some facet of the living world: The steely-eyed night-shift volunteer at the women’s shelter who drains the blood of abusers; the ruthless campaign staffer who employs backroom intimidation and boardroom sorcery to advance her cause; the gray-hat hacker, teeth gleaming in the light of her monitor, tower anointed with the arterial spray of the last troll she hunted down. All are Childer of the Morrigan, roosting in places where they can channel their need to fight for a cause.

                            Parent Clan: Gangrel

                            Nicknames: Stormcrows, Crows, Morrigans

                            Bloodline Bane (The Warmonger’s Curse): Childer of the Morrigan crave battle. Doesn’t matter if it’s a back-alley brawl with broken bottles or a boardroom negotiation with expensive pens. What matters is the fight, and Stormcrows have difficulty walking away from one. Whenever a Morrigan backs down from a challenge, deescalates a problem, or otherwise resolves an important issue through means other than direct conflict, she suffers a cumulative –1 on frenzy resistance for the rest of the night. A Stormcrow’s Beast demands bloody victory and will howl with greater and greater intensity until it gets what it desires.

                            Disciplines: Animalism, Protean, Resilience, Vigor

                            Bloodline Gift: Banshee Crúac

                            Besides their zealous love of battle, the Childer of the Morrigan have an instinctual affinity for Crúac, able to call up power in moments where most Witches need hours. Much like the Stormcrows themselves, this power is difficult to control, and it’s prone to lashing out at anyone around it. It was dubbed “Banshee” Crúac by other Acolytes because of the misfortune it causes to those nearby, and because if you’re close enough to hear a Stormcrow invoke it, it’s already too late.

                            Banshee Crúac uses the following system:
                            • A Childer of the Morrigan can spend a Willpower when attempting to cast any rite with a dot rating of three or less. If she does, the action becomes instant rather than extended, and she only needs to achieve a number of successes equal to the ritual’s dot rating to cast it. Contested rituals are resisted as normal. The Potency of these rites is always considered to be one, and no more.
                            • If a Morrigan fails to cast a ritual using this method, it counts as a dramatic failure. In addition to the normal consequences of dramatically failing Crúac, the caster and those around her will suffer a magical backlash from the fumbled power. For each dot of the failed ritual, [REDACTED].
                            • Targets of rituals cast this way must be within line of sight, even if the ritual normally does not require this proximity.
                            • Exceptional successes on rituals cast this way always result in the Ecstatic Condition.

                            In addition, all Childer of the Morrigan with a dot of Crúac receive an additional animal form upon learning Beast’s Skin, that of a crow or a raven. This form does not count toward the number of shapes a Stormcrow can have at the ready and is always available to her. The exact type of corvid varies depending on what species an individual Morrigan considers to be typical to her part of the world.

                                        Anyway, today, let's look at the Dead Wolves!

                                        If I'm not mistaken, this bloodline is the only ones that's not from a Vampire-branded book. Though Shadows of Mexico kind of is a Vampire book with the serial numbers filed off...except it has a Vampire serial number so, uh...

                                        I digress. It's another bloodline that gets a lot of love in the fandom. Gangrel-as-werewolves is a very natural theme, and I think part of the reason the Dead Wolves are so well-liked is that they have an interesting cultural context, both the Latine context and the Uratha one.

                                        Apart from a few tweaks to the backstory, the big change was Sublunario. The old version is so unlike other Disciplines that I'm not sure why it wasn't a Merit to begin with. It actually has some fun powers, it just has a really weird format. So, we figured the natural thing to do was indeed to make it a Merit, adding in some. Auspex also felt like the natural choice of a fourth Discipline: the Gangrel Disciplines are pretty werewolfy already, and Auspex provides the spooky touch the Dead Wolves have going.

                                        Incidentally, I believe we came up with "Namus-Ur". It means...Dead Wolf (as opposed to Death Wolf).

                                        So, here are the Lobos de Sangre, back to howl at the moon once more. Tomorrow, let's finish off the 1E bloodlines with the Mystikoi.

                                        Dead Wolves: The ones who hunt you by moonlight

                                        “These? All the better to eat you with, my dear.”

                                        A predator prowls the night, but it’s not alone. Swift-running shadows lope beside it, and ghostly howls echo around it. It has allies of flesh and spirit, and they’re on the hunt. You’d best hope you’re not the quarry.

                                        Vampires are not the only hunters stalking human prey. For as long as Kindred have feasted on blood, werewolves have ripped flesh from bone in the name of their sacred hunts. However, despite sharing the night for millennia, the two monsters have little in common besides their predatory nature. Kindred are selfish, immortal parasites, obsessed with building their own temporal power at the cost of everyone around them. Conversely, Uratha have a sacred duty to guard the divide between flesh and spirit, and anything that interferes in that is an obstacle to be torn asunder. Usually, they ignore each other, but when Kindred and werewolves do cross paths, things have a tendency to end poorly, as misunderstanding and ignorance lead to savage violence that can spark decades-long conflicts. It’s this status quo that makes the existence of the Dead Wolves unique — and very strange.

                                        Originating primarily in Mexico, the Lobos de Sangre exist as a tenuous link between werewolf and vampire cultures. While they’re certainly Kindred, the Dead Wolves have a spiritual awareness other vampires lack, paired with an instinctual understanding of werewolf society that lets them interact with the Uratha and (usually) walk away whole. In fact, in regions where they’re active, it’s not that unusual for Namus-Ur — as the werewolves call them — to be made part of a pack. Uratha packs are often called upon to hunt humans in order to serve the balance between the worlds, and nothing is better adapted for that purpose than a vampire. In return for their expertise, Lobos who join packs get to experience true camaraderie and trust, which are rarer than diamonds in the All Night Society. These “adopted” Dead Wolves can also serve as diplomats and ambassadors, helping to settle conflict between Licks and wolves before violence breaks out.

                                        However, some Lobos de Sangre take their Requiems in a completely different direction, using their insight into the ways of the Uratha to become deadly hunters of their so-called “living cousins.” These mercenaries sell their services to princes and covenant heads, acting as consultants and exterminators for a high price. Wielding silver weaponry and an understanding of the Uratha mindset, such Dead Wolves are adept at isolating pack members and picking them off one by one. Their ability to interact with the Shadow gives them another advantage, as Uratha often have many enemies among the spirit world who are eager to assist in their elimination.

                                        What truly sets the Dead Wolves apart from their Kindred isn’t their relationship to werewolves, but rather that awareness of the Shadow. For the normally spiritually blind vampires, this opens up a whole new dimension of opportunity and danger, and it’s one of the reasons this scattered and independent bloodline has survived — and even thrived — for as long as it has. Of course, becoming aware of spirits also means the spirits are aware of them, and not all of them are keen on making deals with dead things.

                                        The Dead Wolves hold a singular place in Mexico’s supernatural ecology. Embraced as vampires but with a connection to a wondrous and horrifying world that other Kindred are barely aware of, the Lobos de Sangre rarely feel completely at home in the All Night Society. As Namus-Ur, they’re capable of running with the Uratha, but they’re never thought of as equals to their moon-born comrades, and they’re perpetually on the bottom of any pack they manage to join.

                                        However, this eternal–outsider status means that neither Kindred nor Uratha know what the Dead Wolves are truly capable of. Vampires who persecute them turn up torpid with missing limbs, and werewolves who decide the bloodline is prey are found riddled with silver buckshot. Ultimately, Namus-Ur walk their own path, and both sides of their bloody, brutal family have learned to leave them to it.

                                        Parent Clan: Gangrel

                                        Nicknames: Lobos de Sangre, Lobos, Namus-Ur (among werewolves)

                                        Bloodline Bane (The Moonstruck Curse): Dead Wolves don’t (necessarily) worship Mother Luna like their Forsaken cousins, but beholding the moon in all its glory does stir the Beast. When Lobos view the full moon for the first time in a night [REDACTED]. Furthermore, silver, blessed metal of the Moon, has a maddening effect on Dead Wolves. The first time in any scene a Namus-Ur takes damage from a weapon coated with silver, she must resist fear frenzy with a –3 modifier.

                                        Disciplines: Animalism, Auspex, Protean, Resilience

                                        Sublunario (• to •••••)

                                        Prerequisite:                                         Dead Wolf

                                        Effect: A combination of mystical insight and instinctive skills, Sublunario grants several distinct benefits to any Namus-Ur who listens to the howling in her blood.

                                        Wolf’s Blood (•): Dead Wolves have a subconscious understanding of how to behave around werewolves and their kin.                                                     Let's talk Mystikoi.

                                                    The Illuminated have changed a lot from their 1E version. Previously, they used their bloodline Discipline to channel the Beast in order to become closer to the Divine Unity. While this is a cool concept on paper, we had a lot of difficulty with the mechanics of their power, which is a good example of a design philosophy I've tried to move away from with these updates: the bloodline Discipline makes the bloodline bane pointless. This was a pretty clear example. Their 1E weakness was that they couldn't spend Willpower to add dice to frenzy or to spend Willpower to resist any power that would cause them to fall to frenzy. Not a bad bane, in the abstract, but then the first dot of Spiritus Sancti lets you spend a Vitae to ride the wave, which the bloodline specifically can spend Willpower on.

                                                    And while the idea of allowing the Beast closer to the surface to assume godliness was a neat idea, Spiritus Sancti mostly felt redundant with the Coil of the Wyrm, apart from the spiritual aspects, which we didn't feel were very well integrated.

                                                    So, the author did some reworking. While still scholars and monks, the Mystikoi are also now a hunting lodge for ephemeral beings. By devouring these creatures, they believe they can attain apotheosis. Spiritus Sancti is now a way to make yourself (and eventually, the world around you) more accommodating to your divine becoming. The Beast/frenzy aspects are gone, but you could easily make a Dragon Mystikoi with Wyrm powers and regain all those elements.

                                                    We also felt this bloodline was a fun contrast to the Dead Wolves: The Mystikoi are far more potent when it comes to spirits, but unlike the Dead Wolves, they have no idea what they're doing, at least in werewolf terms.

                                                    With all that said, here are the Mystikoi! Tomorrow, I'll be back with one of the new bloodlines.

                                                    Mystikoi: The ones who surpass you

                                                    "Blood? A distraction. Our true thirst is for God."

                                                    Despite being immortal parasites who feed on the living, Kindred are rather earthly, ignorant of the many different layers of reality that surround them. Closed off to the harmonies of spiritual choirs, they are blind to the thorns lurking just off the path. Vampires are drawn to spinning schemes that might take centuries to mature but will only ever affect a few square miles of steel and glass. This is for the best. When Kindred do catch a glimpse of the wider world, they naturally want to know more: to reach out and touch it, to explore this new layer of their existence — and to exploit it.

                                                    One part hunting lodge, one part Christian cult, the Mystikoi believe they’ve found the secret to (eventual) divinity. To become closer to the sacred Unity, the origin of all things, one must take pieces of it into oneself. The more Divine energy one consumes, the closer one comes to being part of the Unity. They accomplish this by eating spirits. The Illuminated (so-called because they alone see the light of the Divine) have the ability not only to perceive beings in Twilight, but to interact with them in all manner of ways, from simple conversation to tearing open their strange forms and feasting on the Essence within. Rather than projecting their minds outward with prayer or meditation, however, the Mystikoi rip bloody holes in reality, using the power of Spiritus Sancti to assault beings they shouldn’t even be aware of.

                                                    The Mystikoi’s focus on spirits — creatures the vast majority of Kindred don’t even realize exist — has caused the Lancea et Sanctum to label them dangerous Heretics, and the covenant would have hunted the bloodline to extinction if it weren’t for the intervention of the Ordo Dracul. The Dragons have protected and shepherded the Illuminated for centuries, using them as hunting hounds and guard dogs for Wyrm’s Nests. The entities the bloodline covets are drawn to Nests and often interfere with the work of Defiant who seek to study them. The Mystikoi are some of the only Kindred who can deal with angry spirits directly, and the Order is willing to indulge them with their strange beliefs if it means heightened security for their most important assets. There’s also the slight chance the Illuminated are on to something, and if that’s the case, the Dragons want to be on hand to record exactly what a steady diet of Essence does to a vampire.

                                                    Hunting is as central to the Mystikoi as their divine philosophy, and a great deal of any Illuminated’s time is dedicated to tracking and studying potential prey. Largely ignorant of the Shadow and its laws, Mystikoi can only hunt those spirits who manage to exist in the between-state of Twilight. Thus, they can’t afford to be picky: Heretics will attack anything they can sink their fangs into in their quest for divinity, and their blurry understanding of what spirits are means that ghosts and other, stranger entities often end up on the menu.

                                                    Fortunately for the rest of the All Night Society, the Mystikoi are an obscure bloodline with few members. What they lack in numbers, however, they make up for in zeal. Unlike most Kindred, the Mystikoi aren’t spiritually blind, or helplessly bound by the dogma of either Longinus or the Crone. They’ve seen the truth behind the veil, and it’s made them hungry for more.

                                                    Parent Clan: Gangrel

                                                    Nicknames: Illuminated, Mystikos (singular), Heretics

                                                    Bloodline Bane (The Consubstantiation Curse): Pursuing the divine isn’t just dogma to the Mystikoi: It’s bound to their blood. Each night, Mystikoi can only consume Vitae [REDACTED]. Their ability to feed from Kindred is not affected, but only as long as Blood Potency remains below six dots. The Essence-rich blood of werewolves is also exempt, but feeding on Uratha tends to bring about its own dangers.

                                                    Disciplines: Animalism, Protean, Resilience, Spiritus Sancti

                                                    Spiritus Sancti

                                                    Literally translating to “Holy Spirit,” Spiritus Sancti is what allows Mystikoi to perceive, feed from, and manipulate their sacred prey.

                                                    While much ritual and esoterica surround this Discipline, at its core, Spiritus Sancti is about teaching the Beast to hunt a new kind of food. As a consequence of this, an Illuminated in the grips of hunger frenzy will perceive spirits as a source of nourishment and is fully capable of using all levels of Spiritus Sancti to bring down his prey.

                                                    Cast Off the Scales •

                                                    The most basic level of Spiritus Sancti allows the Illuminated to track the Divine beings at play behind the scenes and feast upon their Essence.

                                                    Cost: None

                                                    Dice Pool: None

                                                    Action: Instant

                                                    Duration: Scene

                                                    The vampire can perceive beings in Twilight for the rest of the scene, even if these creatures have a supernatural power of concealment. Nothing can escape his divine sight so long as they remain in his presence. Furthermore, his Kindred Senses attune with his new prey, and he can detect the presence of nearby ephemera exactly as if he were attempting to detect the blood of living creatures.

                                                    The vampire can use Disciplines on ephemeral beings as he would humans and animals if they can perceive him, but effects that require victims to smell his Vitae do not work unless he uses Step Upon the Path.
