                        The origonal split was mostly because we couldn't get anything done at all without arguing forever over details; and there's lots of small differences everywhere. The biggest thematic difference is that in Vocation Princesses strongly tied to Princess led organisations (called Camaraderies); autologous to the Mage's Consillia. In Dream Princess specific social groups beyond the Nakama level are uncommon; instead Princesses tie themselves to mortal groups like Greenpeace or a political party.

                        Vocation is also supposed to be a game about war stories (through that includes things like M*A*S*H). Actually... I'm not sure if that's still the intent. Meanwhile Dream is supposed to tell any story that starts with "you're idealistic and you suddenly gain power, what do you do next?". If that means magical community activists set in The Wire's Baltimore - Dream should support it.

                        (Obviously this post is from a Dream centric perspective. Tagging in Michael Brazier so he can give his side of the story).

                            Since you are so obliging ...

                            My objection to Kings Raven's take on Princess was, basically, that it deemphasized or denatured the fantastical and supernatural parts of the game. The Camaraderie thing is the best example of both, though it came after the split. In the Dream version Hopeful social groups above the nakama level are located only in the Dreamlands - which has the double effect of removing such groups from any concerns local to an area, and of changing the Dreamlands from a setting for adventures into a knockoff of an Internet forum. Neither IMO is desirable.

                            Another instance is the different Echo rules. In Dream a Princess' Echo is an innate Inspiring Merit. In Vocation it's an aura - like the predatory aura of vampires, the hunter's aspect of werewolves and the nimbus of mages; you can use it to oppose other auras. There's a definite difference between a Princess being generally an example to others, and a Princess reaching into her better self to resist a vampire's seduction; the latter is more obviously supernatural than the former.

                            I've always found it amusing that Vocation has actual rules for Princesses playing mundane politics and tying themselves to mundane groups, while Dream (where such things are supposedly the most frequent concern) doesn't really have anything to support it, even Storyteller advice on exploiting the CofD mechanics for that purpose. But that's not a thematic difference, just KR not liking those rules very much. And really, most of the differences between the versions are mechanics, not themes.

                                                            Well, that's the thing. Stories in which a small group of magically-empowered friends, advised by an equally magical mentor, defeat a world-threatening evil with their magic powers are a dime a dozen in manga and anime. Stories where the magical heroes are the tip of a big but basically mundane organization, where mundane support plays a significant role in the plot? It's much harder to find examples from Japan; the ones that come to mind naturally are from American comics, or the pulps they descended from. The Dream version drifts the setting to Western superhero tropes; Vocation's take stays near the magical girl genre.
