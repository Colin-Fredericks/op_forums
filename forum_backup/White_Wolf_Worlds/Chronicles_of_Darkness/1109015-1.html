                    Originally posted by Reighnhell View Post
                    Re: Augmentations

                    I did a quick revision of my notes - these are fairly bare-bones mechanics that are light on examples and I'm still not certain what would be the best approach for blatantly inhuman capabilities. but have a look and lets see how we can fine tune these ideas.

                    Augmentation

                    Augmentations represent artificial modifications to a characters body that greatly enhance its capabilities, in certain cases to a superhuman degree. These augmentations typically represent cybernetic or genetic modification, but could be used to represent mystic alterations as well. The merits represent basic capabilities, how these manifest or are described is up to the storyteller and players to decide.
                    The over-all number of dots in Augmentation merits a character can have is limited to (10 + the lower of Resolve or Stamina). In addition, if the total number in dots in Augmentation ever exceed the characters (Resolve + Composure), they suffer a -1 penalty to Breaking Point rolls. There is only so much strain the body and mind can withstand.

                    Most supernatural creatures cannot take augmentation – their physiological and metaphysical natures often confound the complicated and delicate technologies needed to successfully apply augmentations. Supernatural beings that are still human (as least physiologically speaking) may be able to use Augmentations (at Storyteller’s discretion)

                    Enhancement (••• or ••••)
                    Enhancements improve an individual’s physical or neurological makeup. When this merit is selected, choose a physical or mental attribute. The character may spend a point of Willpower to gain a +1 increase to the chosen attribute, which effects derived values and may exceed the character dot limit in that attribute. This bonus lasts until the end of the scene. This merit may be selected multiple times, each selection applying to a different attribute. The 5 dot version increases the bonus to +2.
                    Drawback: A dramatic failure while using the Enhancement causes it to malfunction. For the remainder of the scene, the Enhancement cannot be re-activated and rolls using the associated attribute suffer a -1 penalty.

                    Implant (•+; Special)
                    An Implant is an alteration that integrates an item or device into your physiology. This device can be nearly any weapon or technological piece of equipment. This merit may be taken multiple times, each time representing a different implant. The benefits and associated merit costs are as follow;
                    Armor (•• or ••••): Implanted Armor gives a bonus the characters armor rating. All of these are considered to grant coverage of the legs, arms, and torso. The character gains armor 1/3 or 2/0 at two dots. The four dot version grants armor 2/4, but suffers a -1 penalty to defense.
                    Equipment (• to ••): Implanted Equipment can be nearly any item that grants an equipment bonus or special feature. Such a device must be size 2 or less, and its cost in merit dots is equal to the higher of its Availability or Equipment Bonus.
                    Merit: An implant can replicate the effects of a physical or mental merit at equivalent cost. It may also replicate the effects of the Striking Looks social merit. At the Storyteller’s discretion, an implant may be able to replicate supernatural merits as well.
                    Weapons (• to ••): Weapon Implants are typically housed in characters limbs, but more exotic locations are possible. At one dots, the weapon may be a size 0 or 1 weapon with availability 2 or less. These devices are small and retractable, giving a +2 bonus to dice rolls when concealing the weapon. The two dot version allows size 2 weapons with availability 3 or less and reduces the concealment bonus to +1. The 3 dot version allows size 3 weapons with availability 3 or less; even with advanced miniaturization, such a device typically replaces the entire limb, is not concealable, and often impedes the use of that limb for actions other than using the weapon.

                    Plugin (•to •••)
                    A Plugin is an alteration that enhances the characters performance at a certain task. These often represent implants and modifications that greatly enhance capability within a somewhat narrow scope. When this merit is selected, choose an Attribute + Skill pair (such as Intelligence +Computers or Dexterity + Firearms) or an Attribute+ Attribute pair (such as Wits+ Composure) The character may spend a point of Willpower to gain an equipment bonus to the chosen pair equal to the dots in this merit until the end of the scene.
                    Drawback: A dramatic failure while using the Plugin causes the enhancement to malfunction. For the remainder of the scene, the Plugin cannot be re-activated and rolls using the associated attribute + skill pair suffer a -1 penalty.

                                        Augmentations represent artificial modifications to a characters body that greatly enhance its capabilities, in certain cases to a superhuman degree.                                         The over-all number of dots in Augmentation merits a character can have is limited to (10 + the lower of Resolve or Stamina).                                                 The Demon Storyteller's Guide actually has a setting hack where the God-Machine has separated a city from reality into a sort of pocket reality, and the people there have cyber-augmentations that are treated like how cellphones are today. It may serve as a good baseline technology-wise.

                                                As for Infinite Macabre, it has a few...troubling aspects about it. One in particular was how the suggestions for Changelings were handled, such as making a Changeling and their Keeper into more like a typical space-opera where the Keeper was a ruler and their Changeling a lost lover...but in the conventional sense. I found it pretty squicky to read.

                                                On the other hand, thinking on how Demons and Beasts would fit into such a setting has me practically giddy. Would there be a God-Machine per star system, each as alien as the last, or are they all part of some much more gargantuan process? Could Beasts set up chambers on different planets and use that as a form of faster-than-light travel? How strange could a Demon's gadgets get and yet not be looked at as too strange by normal society - better yet, as tech advances more, could a Demon's gadgets start looking out of place by resembling older tech? Would Beasts have some kind of cosmic Dark Mother, or would they have analogues on other worlds? Could Demons or Beasts find themselves actually fitting in on spaceships for practical roles?

                                                Heck, Demons on a spaceship would have to worry about Cover as much as they would on Earth, because unlike on Earth they can't just endlessly find people to pact in new parts when their lives are picked apart. A spaceship is small, and its community smaller. A cover too worn will attract attention, and the people within may find themseles with a new, "alien" intruder where their doctor once was. You don't even need the God-Machine to make a Demon's life a struggle on a ship, though you may want to throw in some new kinds of threats to make it so they can't just suffice on Embeds and such...

                                                Beasts with their lairs acting as faster-than-light gateways may find themselves as covetted for travel purposes, taking Satiety as payment...or as the source of pirating goods along. A Beast's lair traits could even be used as a shortcut for expensive technology (Mirages as holograms, for example, or with said traits as security measures) or even simply being covetted as explorers who can get through scenarios without a scratch. That's to say nothing of how they may find their starbound cousins, and the nightmares they can provide...

                                                        I'm a big fan of Mirrors' Extraordinary Mortals and Dark Heroes, so I wanted to finally take a stab at GibberingEloquence's suggestion of an updated list of Skill Tricks for 2E. Most are buffed, sometimes by merging 1E Skill Tricks together, in keeping with 2E's overall higher power level. A few, like Butcher, are nerfed to reflect the new scarcity of Aggravated damage. Feedback and further contributions are welcome, particularly for Mental & Social Tricks; the list will be updated accordingly.

                                                        Academics
                                                        Computer
                                                        Crafts

                                                        Investigation
                                                        - Always Investigating: Add Investigation to Perception, Survival, and Streetwise rolls. When Investigation applies, score exceptional success at three rather than five.
                                                        Medicine
                                                        - Anatomical Intimidation: Add Medicine to Intimidation rolls and to attacks against targets with recognizable anatomic features.

                                                        Occult
                                                        Politics
                                                        Science

                                                        Athletics
                                                        - Fastest of Them All: Add Athletics to Speed and Initiative.
                                                        - Perfect Physique: Add Athletics to Social Maneuvering rolls conducted in person.

                                                        Brawl
                                                        - Killer Instinct: Make All-Out Attacks and use Fighting Style maneuvers without sacrificing Defense. (Maneuvers do not stack.)
                                                        - Multiple Strike: Apply attacks to all targets in reach. Subtract highest Defense.

                                                        Drive
                                                        - Expert Driver: Substitute Drive for Handling when higher; add Drive to car's Durability.

                                                        Firearms
                                                        - Trick Shot: Add higher of Intelligence or Wits to Firearms rolls when accomplishing a task (e.g. shoot out a tire) or making a targeted strike. This can eliminate penalties, but can never result in a net bonus.

                                                        Larceny
                                                        - Spot Concealed Weapons: Add Larceny to Investigation rolls to find concealed weapons on people or within environments; subtract Larceny from others' attempts to find concealed weapons on you.

                                                        Stealth
                                                        - Backstab: Subtract Stealth from opponents' Perception rolls. Add Stealth to attacks against surprised targets. Opponents' dramatic failure on Perception rolls enables you to make a Killing Blow.
                                                        - Know the Shadows: Add Stealth to Perception rolls and to Investigation rolls. When Stealth applies to these rolls, score exceptional success at three rather than five.

                                                        Survival
                                                        - Concrete Jungle: In urban environments, add Survival to Investigation, Streetwise, and Perception rolls. When Survival applies to any of these rolls, score exceptional success at three rather than five.

                                                        Weaponry
                                                        - Butcher: With an edged weapon, cause an improved version of the Bleeding Tilt (Hurt Locker, pg. 143) with any successful attack. Each turn bleeding continues, mortals take 1 Aggravated damage, vampires lose 1 Vitae, and werewolves take 1 Lethal; even accelerated healing can't entirely keep up with the geyser from an open carotid. The Tilt ends with an extended Dexterity + Medicine roll (one turn/roll; [Weaponry] successes required), or with magical healing that affects Lethal damage.
                                                        - Cripple: Inflict your choice of Arm Wrack or Leg Wrack on any successful attack targeting a limb; successes greater than victim's Stamina are not required.
                                                        - Flashing Blades (by GibberingEloquence): 8-Again to Dodging while armed, +1 weapon damage, increase Defense by (Weaponry/2, round up)

                                                        Animal Ken
                                                        Empathy
                                                        Expression
                                                        Intimidation
                                                        Persuasion
                                                        Socialize
                                                        Streetwise
                                                        Subterfuge
