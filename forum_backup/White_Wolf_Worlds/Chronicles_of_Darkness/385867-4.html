                        It'll probably be related to the Court system. Not sure yet.
                        I really hope this can be taken as an implication that Talecrafting will get some kind of mention in the book (I can't remember if you said anything on it's inclusion before). Talecrafting is one of my favorite systems in Changeling, and I'd hate for it to get left behind with the shift to 2e. Especially with the additional focus on 'stories' in the new edition, which makes Talecrafting in some form even more appropriate.

                        Speaking of, I LOVE said additional focus on 'stories.' The fact the Wyrd has always effectively been a Law of Narrative Casualty was something implied in the original 1e book, but it wasn't until Sword at Dawn I felt the concept really came into it's own. Other splats have taken notice, from what I've seen of the other 2e books. The Arcanthus Mage preview shows they're working a bit more closely with the Law of Narrative Casualty the Wyrd represents. The Cahalith preview for Werewolf demonstrates a similar emphasis on 'stories'.

                        So seeing Changeling, where it all began, embrace the concept makes me all the more excited for the book.

                        The other thing I'm looking forward to is the 'mortal splat'. Now, this had been something I'd been wondering about. Unlike Vampire (ghouls), Werewolf (wolf-blooded), and Mage (Proximi/Sleepwalkers), Changeling lacked any real 'halfway' minor template/minor supernatural Merit that could be fleshed out and expanded upon. The closest was the Fae-Touched in Equinox Roads, but one look at how they form (escaped Arcadia before the process of becoming changelings was complete) should tell you how unreasonable it was for them to fulfill the same role ghouls, wolf-blooded, and Sleepwalkers/Proximi fill.

                        But it looks like you're creating a minor template whole cloth for Changeling. I can't wait to see what you guys cook up!

                                        I made some research by myself about the more original fairy tales and decided to share some of it with you for "Changeling: The Lost". The first thing you should know is, that there is not "one" true original you can refer to. These tales where told from one person to another and so a lot of different versions exist. Some of them are very very old and go back to the middle age or even the 10th century.
                                        The brothers Grimm were one of the first people, that decided to collect them and write them down. They made a trip across Germany and asked local people to tell them these stories. Their route is still well know. Translated it would mean something like "The German Fairy Tale Route".
                                        Their first version from 1810 was never published, it was crueler than their later version for children, but also not always as cruel as some other (usually older) version. (As far as I could find out...).

                                        So referring to the Grimm tales as THE original is simply wrong!

                                        Here you can read about some significant differences of a few tales to the published version of the brothers Grimm for children. I did not write the following myself, but collected the most important parts from a few sources in English instead of translating the German ones to save some time:

                                        --------------------

                                        Little Red Riding Hood

                                        These early variations of the tale differ from the currently known version in several ways. The antagonist is not always a wolf, but sometimes an ogre or a 'bzou' (werewolf), making these tales relevant to the werewolf-trials (similar to witch trials) of the time (e.g. the trial of Peter Stumpp). The wolf usually leaves the grandmother’s blood and meat for the girl to eat, who then unwittingly cannibalizes her own grandmother. Furthermore, the wolf was also known to ask her to remove her clothing and toss it into the fire. In some versions, the wolf eats the girl after she gets into bed with him, and the story ends there.

                                        Source:
                                        Little Red Riding Hood - Wikipedia
                                        http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Red_Riding_Hood


                                        Older versions of the tale:
                                        http://www.furorteutonicus.eu/germanic/ashliman/mirror/type0333.html


                                        Sleeping Beauty

                                        In the original story of Sleeping Beauty she falls asleep due to a prophesy, not a curse. Guess what? It isn’t a kiss from a prince that wakes her either. In fact there is no prince at all in the original story of Sleeping Beauty. The king sees Sleeping Beauty and decides to rape her! She has two babies nine months later, even while she is still sleeping. She awakes when one of the children sucks a piece of flux off her finger that was causing her to sleep only to discover she had been raped and is now the mother of two children.

                                        Source:
                                        Sleeping Beauty Was a Rape Victim in the Original Story - Unreal Facts for Amazing facts
                                        http://unrealfacts.com/sleeping-beauty-was-a-rape-victim-in-the-original-story/
                                        Sleeping beauty really was raped! Click here to find out who the offender was.



                                        Snow White

                                        The cannibal Queen
                                        In the newer version, the cannibalistic action of the Stepmother is not mentioned. In the earlier version, the Stepmother orders the huntsman to kill Snow White and bring the girl’s lungs and liver, not only as proof that she is dead but also to be eaten.

                                        The necrophiliac Prince
                                        In the newer version Snow White is awaken when the Prince kisses her. In the original version, there is no kiss. The Prince insists in having the coffin. Then, he asks his men to carry the coffin with the girl’s lifeless body in it. When the men carrying the coffin stumble over some rock, the poisonous apple comes out of her throat and Snow White is once more alive. What does the Prince want with a dead girl’s body? In the original version, not only is he a pedophile but a necrophiliac as well. The fact that the seven dwarfs refuse to bury the girl and instead keep her in a glass coffin so that they can stare at her is already disturbing. The desire of the Prince to have the body adds to the horrifying aspect of this original version.

                                        Source:
                                        Snow White : The Canibal Queen And Necrophiliac Prince
                                        http://yovitasiswati.expertscolumn.com/article/snow-white-canibal-queen-and-necrophiliac-prince
                                        Snow White is a sweet story of an innocent girl fighting for her life. Do you know that the original version of Snow White fairytale is full of shocking plots?The Grimm’s collection is our only source of the original version of the story as Perrault did not include Snow White in his collection. Grimm made two versions. The first was recorded in 1810 and known as the “peasant version”. In this first version there were no stepmother and prince.
