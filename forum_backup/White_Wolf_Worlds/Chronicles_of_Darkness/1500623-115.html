    Monkey's Paw has the same caveat as Honing the Form and Augment the Mind, i.e. it's a +1 Reach effect with a Mana cost; it's just worded differently because Equipment Bonus doesn't have a variable maximum like Attribute or Skill ratings.                                 Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                That said, casting from a Grimoire means gaining to rote quality and doubled ritual duration, right? You can't memorize or use the rote mundra as a Yantra while casting from a Grimoire.
                                This is not the first time you have said this and you have still not presented more persuasive evidence than a particular reading of the initial Rotes section, but for the sake of further progressing that discussion it is worth noting that The Sundered World Dark Era has, as part of its mechanics for Neolithic Wise characters, that such characters in a pre-Order world cannot use Rote Mudras to cast Rotes they didn't create themselves without a Grimoire, which at bare minimum suggests you aren't prevented from using Mudras while casting from a Grimoire.

                                Slightly less concretely, Signs of Sorcery's section on Rotecraft makes it pretty clear that memorizing a Rote is a matter of arcane muscle memory moreso than anything that would be obviated by casting from a Grimoire, with the Sects' teaching methods being more efficient than less established traditions meaning that learning Rotes the more esoteric way prevents you from fast-casting them; as I've said previously, there's little in the way of intuitive sense in saying that you can benefit from your Survival rating from remembering your training of experiencing through precise patterns of heat and cold over the course of three hours to cast Body Control but you can't do so if you take six hours reading a manual describing that same training you underwent.

                                The Grimoire Merit's mechanical description from the corebook likewise only describes the difference between its two uses as "learn the Rote" or "gain the rote quality on casting." The text is not explicit that you can't gain the Mudra bonus while casting from a Grimoire, only that casting from a Grimoire is the only innate way for a spell to gain the rote quality unless it's a Rote you made yourself (and thus know well enough to be the source of any Grimoires made transcribing it). It is still not clear to me why you insist on this reading that treats adding a character's Skill dots to rolls to cast singular individual spells as mutually exclusive with taking lots of time to cast it with a book or a tablet in front of them.

                                        Thinking on this more…

                                        The Astral Grimoire equipment bonus only exists for the Awakened subject of the spell who is hosting the Grimoire in their own Oneiros, and specifically is not provided to characters who travel to its Astral representation or summon its Goetia if it is scribed into the Temenos instead. I suppose the case to be made for it being an equipment bonus instead of a Yantra is that, like the "appropriate form" bonus Neos pointed out, Astral Grimoire benefits (at minimum) all the rotes from the Grimoire just from the subject having it in hand, i.e. a secondary effect of becoming the host of an Astral Grimoire is that you in particular can get extra benefits from "physically" having the Grimoire when you cast its spells, which is a benefit of the Astral Grimoire spell rather than Astral Grimoires more generally.

                                        There might also be a case to be made that since Living Grimoire does not have a similar benefit for Awakened subjects even if the Grimoire is visible, the Astral Grimoire bonus is a Yantra because it's using the symbolism of manifestly possessing a text whose contents you can otherwise reference by virtue of its presence inside your soul, i.e. it's a symbolic boost that you have to go out of your way to benefit from (particularly since summoning the Goetia of the Grimoire from your Oneiros means you lose that part of yourself while it's out), but the mechanics for a Grimoire's form giving a bonus to appropriate spells uses a specific example that directly overlaps with conventional Path and Order Tools (only otherwise differentiated by the lack of restriction to symbolism appropriate to one's Path or Order) and the only symbolism shared between the spells in an Astral Grimoire is that they're in the same single-soul Astral Grimoire.

                                        Considering the conventional suggested means of repairing the Hitch Condition from creating or modifying a warped Rote (which is only gained by the character for Rotes they've learned) is to enter the character's Oneiros and fixing circumstances with the Goetia of the internalized Rote, there might be something interesting to be gotten out of a mix of Astral Grimoire and the separate Astral representations of Rotes the character has learned from that and other Grimoires.

                                                Originally posted by Neos01 View Post
                                                A question about Veiling spells.
                                                Let s take Shield of Cronos as an example.
                                                The core spells states that any spell trying to penetrate it must win a Clash of will.
                                                Does the Time active sight detect the spell presence automatically or it requires the clash?
                                                Active Time Sight would highlight Shield of Chronos as an ongoing supernatural effect relating to Time. It may require a Clash to use its supplementary effect of detecting when someone is about to act for the purposes of being able to preempt it, depending on how stringently the ST reads Shield of Chronos's ability to prevent "predicting the subject's future," (since Time Sight doesn't tell you what they're about to do) but the core effect of Shield of Chronos does not do anything except stop its subject from being perceived at any time other than the present and the basic function of Active Time Sight is simply to highlight Time-related phenomena.
                                                The reach option adds false info and require a clash to pierce the illusion.
                                                Does it changes the conditions to see the spell?
                                                And what effect could trigger the clash? (Active time sight? Focused? A time spell like postcognition? The Prime arcana?)
                                                Active Time Sight is not going to pierce the illusion for the reasons just described — the very particular overlap Active Mage Sights normally Clash for is if the phenomenon would obfuscate senses the character is using and the Active Sight(s) overlaid on those senses correspond to the principles the phenomenon runs on, and the only information Active Time Sight can give about a Time spell is that it's a temporal effect (barring more particular examples like temporal warping, time travel, and keeping track of Initiative and when people are about to act). By the same token, the Reach effect is not going to stop Active Time Sight from registering that Shield of Chronos is an active Time-related supernatural effect on a subject.

                                                Focused Mage Sight could, with Scrutiny, glean an understanding of what a given instance of Shield of Chronos is doing, but it runs on the same principles as Active Sight re: whether it triggers a Clash, and the way Focused Sight works means this is functionally a fairly expensive way to go around the need to discern the illusion by simply spending Willpower to go through the process of figuring out what the spell does in its entirety.

                                                Postcognition does not discern illusions as part of its normal functioning and thus does not trigger a Clash.

                                                Prime is the most probable culprit for piercing the veil of an advanced Shield of Chronos, via combining spells like Pierce Deception with whatever Time spells are being used to examine a subject's past or future.
