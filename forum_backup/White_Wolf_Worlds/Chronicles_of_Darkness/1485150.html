    Storyteller question: How to make players... Hubristics?

    06-27-2022, 08:18 PM
    Hello!

    Today I want a bit of though excersice and advise, mostly because I really have problems with this:

    Mage is a game about Hubris, but mages are mostly human in a lot of their nature, and human can be no-hubristic. While the act of being curious can be also a pain, but you can argue you need to be curious in order to awake, you don't need to be hubristic in order to do magic.

    Here is where my problem comes: My player (and possible future players) are very humble on their ways of magic and very secure: Never reach more of what you can do, don't say you are better than anybody, in some ways they don't even have self-stem to be arrogant in any way.

    In some way, even if supernatural curious, they try to use the less magic possible to solver their problems, only their rotes-praxis like some type of spell list, frowning the use of Improsived Spells.

    How you make your players play with the magic Hubris mages are suppose to have?

      You can't really make your players do anything. You can tempt them, probably by making it worth their while... to a point.
      I firmly believe that Aspirations are really important for Mage, just to help give some direction to the PC's magical inquiries. You can try laying out easier routes to achieve their aspirations that are hubristic enough for your purposes. If they resist or succumb to temptation, there's some on-theme drama either way. You can also underline the theme by spotlighting NPC Mages who are acting hubristically, and having it work out for them. They're getting careless and ambitious, and so far they're getting away with it. You don't want to cheat the players, but having someone they don't like be doing better than they strictly should be can be motivating, and again can lead to good drama whether they cut corners themselves to catch up, or try to straighten out the hubristic rival.
      TLDR, my advice is to come up with scenarios specifically about hubris, so they'll be engaging with the theme no matter what they actually choose to do. Contrasting the humble, cautious player characters against other Mages that do suffer from Hubris still hits the same theme and seems to fit better with the kind of game your players are angling towards.

          Present individuals not on their most agreeable behavior, whether Sleeper, Sleepwalker, mage, spirit, ghost, goetia, institution, or stranger things besides. Put the characters in a situation where they would be in the right to make a snap judgment. Model power justifying itself by its own success and hint at flaws by which a character can bring it low and cathartically supplant it. If you can, split time between scenes where the characters are in their magical personas and scenes where they aren't and emphasize how much hassle the latter contain that the former could trivially jet past if allowed to act without restraint.

          Emphasize that patience has its costs. Emphasize that some of those costs are not so much "you won't get this now" as "you won't get this ever." It's going to take intimidation or hard mind control to get that stubborn old man to let you spend some time in his ancestral home to study the ghost that dwells in the attic, and your teacher isn't too concerned about breaking and entering as long as the Consilium's agreed you have the right to the Mystery.

          Emphasize the limits of safe spellcasting, particularly at lower Gnosis and Arcana. The benefits of a Praxis come out in frequent casting such that they make for good "when all you have is a hammer" situations, and every casting is an opportunity for things to go wrong; offer the usual Beat to upgrade a failure to a dramatic failure on a spellcasting roll in these situations, and provide an interesting Condition from the result. On the other side of things, Rotes are useful, but they're singular spells, and gaining knowledge of them should not be a trivial matter — most sources of spells will expect some form of compensation for them, whether that's initiation into a Mystery Cult that needs managing or favors to be owed or a prosaic trade of one grimoire for another of equal value. Where characters have especially flexible spells, present situations that push the limits of those spells in terms of spell factors — needing to sustain spells for an extended period of time is an easy way to chew through safe Reach.

          Lean on Obsession. Offer the real possibility that a Mystery will cease to be available without magic beyond the character's current limits and won't stick around waiting for them to expand them. If they don't want the knowledge enough to push for it, they'll lose access to it in favor of a more driven querent. Your Legacy mentor doesn't care that your sister's wedding is on the Solstice, they need one of their apprentices to help with the rites that must be performed once a decade at the fairy ring on the hill across town and if you can't make it then it won't be you.

          Relatedly, present situations where there are consequences for not having enough power — the Long-Term Nimbus exists to attract complications suited to a character's Path, but those complications are not obligated to be trivial or benign. That magical disease spreading from the cracks in your Thyrsus character's Sanctum may be related to the Life Arcanum, but as a Mystery it'll require more than a Disciple's secret knowledge to figure out.

          If the character commits an Act of Hubris and picks up Megalomaniacal or Rampant, make use of the changes to their Obsessions and Vice. Give them some time and opportunity to get away with the behaviors encouraged by those Conditions. Let them be an asshole for a while.

          Above all, talk to the players about buy-in and make sure everyone understands that the game isn't just about people with magic powers. Causing trouble for yourself is explicitly how characters will most expediently earn their Beats, and failure can be fun, especially when it concerns problems that you caused yourself on purpose. These games are exercises in co-writing as much as they are in gaming.

              Sounds like they are being pretty careful, that is generally a good sign. Generally you can tempt them with their Aspirations and Obsessions. Chasing them feels good to the mage. They learn mundane skills faster via chasing Aspirations. Their magic grows more powerful faster when pursuing Obsessions than with other Mysteries and, the closer it hits to home as something that will help them or those they care about, the more they feel tempted to push it one step further.

              Plus the mages that do that will make progress faster, checks for Acts of Hubris grant Arcane beats. So these characters may seem irresponsible and requiring an intervention. Now its their strength versus the cabal's finesse. Can they keep up with only the same modus operandi ? Remember that a character like Harry Dresden is a well meaning person, but a low Wisdom mage. Not really evil or unobservant, but someone who finds it a bit too comfortably easy to apply big hammer theory to problems.

              Will they need to cut corners to help prevent a catastrophe or limit collateral damage ? Will they need to appeal to the give and take of diplomacy and its gray alliances to solve the issue ? All these and the consequences can test their Wisdom.

              Using only rotes and Praxis is going a bit too far, one of the best parts of mage is coming up with creatives spells to solve an issue. The Wisest mages, in my opinion, know how to do this with the minimal Practice needed. Why cast a sleep spell with Weaving if the target's light sleep can be made deep with a Compelling? You even have more spell factors that way. Do you really need Nightvision to explore the werehouse or just to hit a light switch ? Having Wiser mages present these options can be a part of their journey to becoming Wiser. But pushing them out of their comfort zone might be how to test their Wisdom and common sense.

              Maybe create a suspicious situation where traditional solutions may not be available, that may push them to explore their toolkit. An example was posted some time ago, a version of The Yellow Sign from the Cthulu mythos. It started driving anyone who learns about it insane and made an Abyssal incursion easier. The Hierarch made a deal with a Logophage to keep wiping everyone's memories of it to keep the city safe. So you have a Hierarch perfectly at ease, who only knows he made a deal because he got a vague statement from himself, being fine sanctioning a Left-Handed mage to do whatever. Its very suspicious and can cause a cabal that is not cautious to cause more harm than good.

              New experiences are the font of creativity, when seeking inspiration, break your routine.
