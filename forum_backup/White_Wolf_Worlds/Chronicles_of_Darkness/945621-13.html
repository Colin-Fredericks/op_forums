        It's implied in the myths sidebar of Imperial Mysteries that even the exarchs experience a form of mortality and groom ministers to replace them (Replace them entirely or feed them? Tremere Exarchs. Even less fun then regular exarchs).

        I'm fairly certain that one of the impacts of the Corpus Author's ascension was that her belief that "Magic is alive" became a Truth of the world. What the full implications of being alive means for truth symbols is unknown.

        Merlin still seems to have survived the removal of Arthur's kingdom from the timeline but the legend has been romanticised and changed a lot. Perhaps that's what death is to truth symbols now.

        My point is that if even truth symbols experience a recurrent transformation resembling the tarot description of death (change and subsequent renewal) it could provide a compelling explanation for why dying is a supernal truth, and why deviating from that necessarily risks wisdom, although your mileage may vary. You have to accept that magic is alive, that the corpus author created this truth through imperium, that it applies to all truth symbols, that that fact also translates to the Fallen phenomena of mortal lifespans and that undermining that Truth necessarily drags you from the supernal.

        It certainly makes the Mysterium assertion that "Magic is alive, and therefore, magic can die" much more anxiety inducing.

            Originally posted by Mrmdubois View Post
            I like your take, but I have a nitpick. Souls that leave when you die are not likely to be carrying any kind of relevant continuity of self along with them since they seem to be identical to the ones that can be swapped around like batteries. This would seem to poke a hole in the theory that Stygia is a place where self continuous people are refined before moving on to whatever is after Stygia. If that's correct and it's not a continuous self kind of soul/journey then Mages still have reasonable room for doubt or fear of death. Granted, this is a grey area in the game specifically because it could be a great place to hang a Mystery so it could go either way an actual refinement of soul/self as a step on the journey of existence, or annihilation of self as the soul stuff gets purified before being re-purposed. Because of this lack of certainty though, yeah, even Moros have a reason to fall for the immortality trap. My general opinion is that when you think you're about to run up against the hard facts about the cosmology of the setting you'll suddenly find yourself on not so solid ground after all.

            Reality is a debate between the gods, Mages are the arguments.
            I was under the impression that "souls-that-can-be-swapped" (let's call it stcbs from now) aren't the totality of the soul. For instance, the way mages interact with the soul doesn't seem that coherent with how other beings do (such as Demons, for instance). And I think earlier in this thread it was pointed out that the Anima Mundi as the "soul of the world" doesn't mean that it's a soul in the Death and Life arcana sense. There is something strange, also, with how some beings are "soulless" but seems functionnally not that different from humans (spirits, and I believe promethean also? And what about demons? And Beasts?). I think mummies also recognize several "components" of the soul.

            So the way I understood it is that the STCBS (or arcana souls, whatever) are a part of what we tend to call souls in the common sense of the term but not the totality. They're like an important part of the soul, true, but not all. Much like how you can swap hearth but still have the same "body".

            Anyway, my point is, there seem to be some incongruity between game lines about what the soul is so I think that the stuff mages can manipulate is only the tip of the iceberg there.
