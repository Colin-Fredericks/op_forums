                        Personally, I would like to see things change in Mage 2nd so that a transgender character could have his post-transition name be considered a true name, with all of the sympathetic vulnerabilities that come with it.

                        The situation becomes a bit hairier when it comes to learning a transgender person's pre-transition name, and whether that should provide supernal sympathy or not. I know that transgender people often have their pre-transition names used by disapproving family members and acquaintances, and can only imagine how irritating and frustrating that must be. This could be a real-life example of a name being used as a weapon against a person, which is really what the whole True Name thing is all about. So I could see reasons to make it go either way... and I would like to see the game respect both choices.

                        One thought I had was that perhaps among mages there is a "shadow name ritual" that a mage can do to erase their supernal sympathy to a name. Just calling yourself "Arctos" may work for a while, but after a while people will start thinking of you as "Arctos" and this could slowly become a new True Name for you. So to prevent this, they perform a shadow name ritual to break this sympathy. However, this ritual demands a name be presented as a True Name, and this name must already have a supernal sympathy to you. (So no, you cannot present a series of unintelligible syllables as your True Name) If you don't have one, the ritual will fail. The supernal demands that you have a name.

                                    08-04-2015, 01:13 AM
                                    Originally posted by Froggy711 View Post

                                    1. The proposed ritual protects a name from gaining sympathy, it does not alter sympathy. I opened no such door.

                                    2. I didn't even name arcana requirements for this ritual. If you would like to comment on what arcana requirements this would be, feel free.

                                    3. Is there anything about this that couldn't already be done within the rules of the game, as they are now?
                                    Dave has already indicated, including earlier in this thread, that it requires archmastery to change a true name. This is exactly as it was in the current edition.

                                    A mage may generally reduce (or enhance) sympathetic connections with the Space Arcana (See, e.g., Reach and Paradox spoiler), and by default it is far more difficult to cast sympathetic magic without knowledge of a subject's actual name, but in 1e and thus far spoiled in 2e, a mage's true name is its "first identification in the Fallen, and it doesn't change." The desires or current identity of the mage are irrelevant.

                                    As I indicated in my post, I am quite comfortable with the current rule, and believe it maintains setting integrity, consistency and verisimilitude. If a character could change a true name without archmastery, it defeats the very purpose of the true name. Of course, you may run your games however you wish, and I certainly have no intention, desire or ability to try to force you to follow any rule you do not like or offends you or your players.

                                    Edit: If you want a name-changing ritual in your games, I would suggest that it require a minimum Space 5, with possible additional Death and Prime to alter the mage's soul and supernal connection, respectively. Anything much easier effectively obviates the true name mechanic, and it would probably just be easier to ignore the rule entirely. Alternatively, you might want to consider allowing the mage to purchase sufficient Allies. Mentor or other merits representing contacts with a relevant (and powerful) archmaster who had the ability to change a true name with Imperial Arcana. That would provide the name change that you seek as well as a great deal of role-playing opportunities due to debts owed to or interference of the archmaster in your character's life.

                                                The most annoying thing, in my opinion, about the so-called "real names" is that assuming its a birth name makes no sense when you're talking about sympathetic magic. A child is born under one name, gets adopted while still young, gets a new name (even if its just a different surname) before he knows his original one.

                                                Suddenly, the sympathetic magic latches onto some name that he's got a chance of never hearing before and doesn't relate too? All the sympathetic connctions our theoretical boy has involves the Ward family, and not the Jane Doe (died of drug OD) or the unknown birth father.

                                                Its not even just trans mages that theoretically have unfortunate implications here. Its any number of people. I get married and my name changes. My new name isn't real? I find that mildly disturbing.

                                                I get that its probably trying to keep it simple, but its clear that one size does NOT fit all. In fact, its blindingly obvious that we're not talking about one minority group here where the assumption of birthnames = "real".
