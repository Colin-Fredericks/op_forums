    Child characters in regular CofD?

    09-01-2016, 03:03 PM
    So the chronicle I'm putting together is going to be more Stranger Things than Supernatural. There's some obvious Stephen King influences there, and one of the common tropes in that sort of horror is the child character (who often has some kind of psychic power or at least is more sensitive to the weirdness around them). (Examples: Eleven from Stranger Things, Charlie from Firestarter, Newt from Aliens, Jake from the Dark Tower series, etc.) And given the kinds of characters one of the players in my group tends to play, I have a feeling that they're going to want to make a child character.

    Unfortunately, the only book in the nWoD/CoD that seems to address child characters (as far as I've been able to find) is Innocents, but it assumes all of the characters will be children, and isn't really compatible with other CoD games. So other than saying "No, you can't play a kid, sorry", what's the best way to deal with this?

    A child character of between about 10 and 16 would be Size 4, and have a commensurately lower Speed and Health than an adult. But what about attributes and skills? On one hand, starting the character out with fewer dots to spend would make sense, and could be said is the price of playing a child. On the other hand, if this is playing to the trope, the kids are typically able to hold their own in a group of adults. Maybe put a cap of three dots on any attribute or skill? Since I'll be using Magic Character Beans, maybe starting the character out with fewer attribute and skill points but more MCBs would work. And I'm thinking that this should all be covered by a Condition of Child.

    I'd love to hear any suggestions if anyone has any ideas on how best to deal with this.

          Hey! I use child characters in all of my chronicles. Fifteen is really the age where I base it more on maturity, if they should use modified child stats or adult stats, but there's a number pattern in Innocents that you can extend for children over twelve, which is where that book stops. There's normally that cap of three dots for Attributes and Skills, and Innocents has child only Merits, including Merits like Mental/Physical/Social protegee that allow you to buy skills over three dots. I've found Innocents quite compatible, and every game I've ran since I started STing has involved child characters at all levels of influence within the campaign. I'm currently running a game with an eleven year old Beast Storyteller character, in fact. It can absolutely, definitely, be done, using Innocents for child characters in an otherwise adult CoD game.

              This will give an appropriate reduction in capability.

                  If you want, and the child character is supposed to be "special", you could always use the existing Innocents restrictions, but make up the difference in their special traits/abilities (Supernatural Merits, Skill Tricks, etc.).

                  Gamelines that might be applicable:
                  Werewolf, for Wolf-Blooded. Many have that happen regardless of their age (even being born that way), according to the text in some Tells.
                  Mage, if you want to go with a Proximus ("you're a wizard, Harry!").
                  Changeling (2E playtest preview) for Fae-Touched.
                  Demon, for Offspring/Fractals, or even just a Demon with a child Cover (though that will swing the power discrepancy far in the other direction).

                  The following are possible, if a bit dark:
                  Vampire, either for Ghoul (come on, man, really?) or a full vampire (the Anthology had one).
                  Changeling, for a full Changeling.
                  Geist, if the kid made a Bargain young.
                  Beast, for when they really were eaten by monster(s) in the closet/under the bed.

                  Mummy is about the only one that isn't suitable.

                  (Blue Books: Immortals, for Warden, Reborn, Visitor, and maybe Purified. Inferno, if you want to recreate the Exorcist.)

If I get into a situation where a kid would be at a disadvantage such as grappling with a grown man, or talking your way into a adult only area, I turn failures into dramatic failures. Kids are sometimes quite skilled in real life anyway. Deciding to give yourself lower stats in some parts, and higher in others can make you a very effective character. No one wants to play an average kid, but playing an exceptional child character can be fun.

                                              Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                              So, are you just trying to model mortal characters as children, how to balance a child in a mixed group, or how to incorporate more elements in when you have to account for a child character?
                                              I would be trying to fit a child in a group of adults, which is why Innocents wouldn't work. As others have pointed out, Innocents uses a different scale for Attributes and Skills.


                                              Originally posted by lnodiv View Post
                                              I would use Innocents Character Creation, and then follow the rules for translating the child character into the adult world within the book (growing up).
                                              Huh... I didn't notice the rules for aging up characters in Innocents. However, after looking them up, I don't think they'd really work since they're specifically intended to age the child up to an adult character. In fact, they sort of do the opposite of what I want to do.

                                              After thinking on it some more, I'm considering dealing with this one of two ways...

                                              Option 1: Treat it as a Condition.

                                              Condition: Adolescent
                                              Your character is physically between about 10 and 15 years of age. The character is considered to have the Small Frame Merit. Additionally, while this Condition is in effect, any dots greater than 3 in any Physical Attribute are ignored, and the character does not benefit from 10-again on any rolls involving Physical Attributes.
                                              Beat: Danger or severe inhibition due to the Condition.
                                              Resolution: The character physically ages to an adult.

                                              This would be the easiest to deal with should I decide to do some wonky things with time shifts, and could be removed from or applied to characters easily. The character gets a free 2-dot Merit (though one that has some definite drawbacks), and can potentially gain some Beats (actually Willpower, since we'll be using MCBs). The main problem with this is that a player who knows his character is going to have this Condition could basically avoid the limitation on Physical Attributes by going Mental/Social primary (which is why I added the lack of 10-again on Physical Attribute rolls.) I'm also not sure if Dexterity should be penalized, since while kids don't have the strength or toughness of an adult, they can be pretty quick and agile.

                                              Option 2: Alter character creation for adolescent characters.

                                              The child character must take the Small Frame Merit. The character gets 4/3/2 dots for Attributes and 9/6/3 dots for Skills, but begins play with 20 additional MCBs. These bonus MCBs cannot be spent to improve the character, and only as bonus dice or to purchase narrative elements (as per Mirrors p34, "Cooperative World Building").

                                              This might better represent the physical immaturity and lack of experience of a child, as well as the trope's tendency of the "kid" being able to significantly influence the plot despite lacking the abilities of the adults around him. The main downside is that it's less versatile, should I want to do anything odd with the timeline of the chronicle, either narratively (such as flashbacks or flash-forwards) or as some sort of weird in-universe effect.

                                                  Here's some Conditions I whipped up for playing children and teens.

                                                  Juvenile (Persistent)
                                                  Your character is very young - her body and mind are still developing, putting her at a disadvantage in an adult world. Her Size and Speed are both reduced by 1, and you cannot raise any of her Skills above 3 dots. Additionally, you do not benefit from the 10-again quality on teamwork actions that involve adult characters, and adult characters gain the 8-again quality on Contested actions against her.
                                                  Beat: Your are denied agency as a result of your age.
                                                  Resolution: When your character reaches physical maturity.

                                                  Adolescent (Persistent)
                                                  Your character is in an awkward transitionary phase of development - no longer a child, but not yet an adult, she struggles to find her place in the world. Adults tend to assume the worst in your character until she proves her maturity to them. You begin all Social Maneuvering attempts with adult characters at a Hostile impression.
                                                  Beat: You face prejudice or discrimination as a result of your age.
                                                  Resolution: Your character reaches developmental maturity.

                                                  I figure a young child (9-12ish) would begin with Juvenile, while a young adult (17-20ish) would start with Adolescent. A Teenager (13-16ish) would get the worst of both worlds and start with both Conditions. Thoughts?
