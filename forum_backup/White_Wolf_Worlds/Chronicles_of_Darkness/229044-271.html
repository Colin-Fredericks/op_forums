        Done with the first reading (or, well, re-reading I suppose) of Chapter 5. Still not sure what to feel about the changes to Heroes.

        The fact Beasts were responsible for the creation of Heroes was genuinely one of the most interesting facets of their conflict to me. Of course, the Begotten still drive Heroes to action - they cause the disturbances that lead them down the slippery slope, and in many ways they're still unwittingly responsible for the cycle of violence. Heroes are still tragic figures who can nonetheless be the antagonists Beasts love to hate, but I think they've lost some considerable part of what originally made them so tragic.

        Maybe it's for the best overall, since they have more room to be multi-dimensional even within the heroic mindset, and they are less straddled down by the possibility of people reading unfortunate implications into them. But the relationship between Heroes and Beasts suffers a bit for it in the distillment of its grim inevitability. Sure, their role in the cosmology is expanded and they have a wider place in things, but what was most significant about their existence seems to have suffered for it.

                    I wonder if there is narrative space for Heroes who kill Beasts in more symbolic manners? Impeaching the Tyrant, stealing the Collector's hoard, and such. Other forms of subjugation and humiliation (though that does carry the chance for things to get much darker than straight-up murder). Attempting to separate the human from the Horror, like some sort of pray-away-the-monster program. The point of it all, of course, is still to be an act of self-aggrandizement at the expense of another living, thinking, feeling being. On the other hand, metaphorical deaths might take away something important from the game. What do other people think?
