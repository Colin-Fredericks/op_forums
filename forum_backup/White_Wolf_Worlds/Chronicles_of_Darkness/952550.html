    Minor/Micro Template: Hedge Sorcerers

    08-17-2016, 11:42 AM
    So, for those who want a little mortal-Changeling magic in their games, here's a Mortal+ template, using the released Playtest rules so far.
    [Insert Intro Fiction here. Any takers?]

    Hedge Sorcerers are those who’ve made their own deal with cosmic forces, enabling them to opt into other bargains struck with the hidden powers behind reality. Through exploiting clauses in these Contracts, a Hedge Sorcerer can work their will. It isn’t always easy, making sure you or your targets qualify for the magic you invoke, but the results speak for themselves.

    Mechanics

    So, how does one become a Hedge Sorcerer? Fairly easily, as it turns out. All a person needs to do a strike a bargain with the right entity. Some seek out Changelings or Hobgoblins to barter with. Others uncover old rituals to contact the forces of reality or the Wyrd itself (often, by a variety of aliases). Those who manage that step can offer the entity what it wants (or finds interesting) in exchange for insight into the wheels of power - Contracts struck long ago. The original forgers of these contracts left in loopholes to exploit their power, loopholes that the prospective Hedge Sorcerer employs (lacking supernatural might of their own).
    Method 1: Pact Method 2: Bargain with the Wyrd
    1. Summon or find a Patron (such as a Hobgoblin, True Fae, etc.)
    2. Make a Pact.
      1. Lesser
        • Contract, 1-2 dots
        • Seeming benefit to a single Contract
      2. Medial
        • Contract, 3-4 dots
      3. Greater
        • Contract, 5 dots
    3. Use Contracts.
    1. Summon or Invoke the Wyrd (or a "representative").
    2. Bargain for access to Contracts (see Wyrd Bargain below).
    3. Use Contracts.
    Related Merits

    Below are a few helpful merits that are relevant to Hedge Sorcerers. Not all may be available depending up the Storyteller and the Chronicle’s needs. Remember that as with all such character features, they need to be justified (so you can’t just pick up Harvest Emotions because you find it useful - your character needs to have uncovered how to do so first).

    Wyrd Bargain (Supernatural, •••••)
    Effect: You’ve struck a bargain with the Wyrd itself (even if you don’t realize it, or know it by that name at the time). You can now purchase Contracts and Seeming benefits for Contracts as if you were a Changeling (see below). This doesn’t give you a Wyrd score to contribute to rolls (assume you only have a Wyrd of 1, when an effect is calculated by Wyrd alone), nor does it give you a Glamour pool, necessitating you to use the Catches of individual Contracts.
    Drawback: Your deal with the Wyrd has certain stipulations. You take on a Minor Frailty, thematic with your particular deal, representing how you uphold your end of the bargain, or a curse incurred for access to such powers.

    Seem-wise (Supernatural, ••)
    Prerequisite: Wyrd Bargain
    Effect: You have learned or bartered for the ins and out of one of the fae “Seemings”, and can now independently uncover and opt into their special clauses in the Contracts. Select a Seeming when you take this merit - you no longer need an instructor to acquire the Seeming specific benefit for Contracts (but you must still acquire that clause separately, though Pact or XP).
    Note: This merit can be taken multiple times, once per Seeming. It grants no benefit other than the ability to pick up Seeming specific clauses without finding a teacher.

    Well of Wonder (Supernatural, •••••)
    Effect: You gain access to a Glamour pool, capable of holding up to 5 points of Glamour at one time. You can spend at most one point from this pool each turn.
    Note: You still need to actually find a way to fill that pool, however.

    Inspire Thyself (Supernatural, •••)
    Effect: You can tap your own surging emotions for Glamour. Roll your Resolve + Expression - your Composure (yes, you penalize your own roll), acquiring 1 Glamour per Success. Harvesting Glamour from your own emotions may have other consequences.
    Drawback: Your tapping of your emotions makes it easier for others to do so as well. Attempts to harvest Glamour from your emotions gain a +2 bonus. Additionally, the Storyteller may apply a relevant Condition to you harvest "excessive" Glamour from yourself, or do so more than once per day.

    Harvest Emotion (Supernatural, •••••)
    Effect: You’ve managed to figure out or bargained for a way to cull Glamour from emotional surges. You can make a harvesting roll (determine Attribute + Skill, as the situation warrants) as a Reflexive action to collect ambient Glamour. This Glamour must be used or stored somehow before the next turn or it fades.

    Supernatural Potency (Supernatural, •• to •••••)
    Effect: You have an inherent tie to powers beyond mortal ken. When you invoke such powers, you command them with the same faculty as their "natural" wielders. Each instance of this merit is tied to a particular Supernatural Tolerance trait (such as Wyrd) the bearer does not possess. When using abilities that factor that trait, you are treated as having an equivalent value equal to the dots in this merit.
    Note: This simulates higher Wyrd for activated or passive abilities you otherwise possess - it is not actually said Supernatural Tolerance trait. It doesn't confer the ability to store Glamour, contribute to resistance rolls (see Supernatural Tolerance in Hurt Locker), or anything else the traits would otherwise enable. It solely serves to expand dice pools, or affect calculations that are dependent upon the trait it is tied to.

    The following Merits are also potentially suitable for Hedge Sorcerers: Fae Mein (at 3 dots), Fae Mount, Faerie Favor, Faerie Healing, Hedgebeast Companion, Court Goodwill, Goblin Vow.

    Notes on Hedge Sorcerers
    • Due to the repeated deal making, they are encouraged to pick up merits that might assist with such efforts, like Fast Talker or Spin Doctor. Assembling a list of suitable Patrons and their prefered payments is also useful, as is cultivating a positive relationship with a select few.
    • Due to Hedge Sorcerers lacking the full ability set that Changelings have, their access to the Hedge is limited to known Trods and Hedge Gates, and employing Contracts such as Dreamsteps. Some Hedge Sorcerers may have never set foot in the actual Hedge.
    • Contracts that reference the Mask or Mein, either have no effect for Hedge Sorcerers, or grant a temporary, possibly illusionary, Mein to them, as determined by the Storyteller. Or you can let them pick up the Fae Mein merit at 3 dots.
    • Since they lack a Wyrd score, many of the effects dependent upon Wyrd are limited. Assume Wyrd 1, if something uses only Wyrd when the Hedge Sorcerer lacks it (such as the Size and Speed of Control Elements). Many are encouraged to find some way to bolster their potency, such as through merits like Supernatural Tolerance (Hurt Locker) and Supernatural Potency.
