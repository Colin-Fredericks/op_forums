    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    It seems your overly technical interpretation of the rules is leading to them not working as intended. I would recommend revising your interpretation.
    Or you know, you could just apply a minor errata and fix the merit, like I recommended? Which is sort of the point of this thread. Let's be honest: falling rules work perfectly fine without Punch Drunk, damage from weapons works perfectly fine without Punch Drunk, the vehicle collision rules work fine without Punch Drunk, the explosion rules work adequatly without Punch Drunk, etc. The problem isn't with the rest of the rules or my RAW interpretation of them, it's with Punch Drunk. Which is why I recommend changing Punch Drunk not the rest of the rules.

    It's clear that the intent is that when it is no longer possible to upgrade wounds in your other health boxes, that the rightmost box should take the damage.
    If that is the intent, why not just change the wording slightly, which I recommended in my original post, to make this explicit?

    It's also clear that if a source of damage would turn someone into chunky salsa, such as being at ground zero of an explosion, or being hit by a tank cannon, they should take aggravated damage. These things don't really need to be written technically perfectly, because every game is run by a human being who can interpret the natural language and apply common sense.
    Except my whole problem with Punch Drunk (and with the second dot of boxing for that matter) is that it DEFIES common sense. This is what the examples above are trying to show through the use of hyperbole. What it does (prevents you from filling the last box of health if you spend a willpower) allows you to do absurd things because of the way it is written and I am trying to point this out. You answer to me complaining that "this defies common sense" is to say "apply common sense"? Well that's great and all, but why not fix the "defies common sense" part now, while you have the opportunity?

    Finally, this is an errata thread. A bit of time could be used to clean up the merit so that it does what it says in the preamble explicitly in order to prevent future consternation when a player reads the rule one way and the GM another. I am not asking for a twenty page legal document explaining how the merit works. I am suggesting two minor changes: make it work for bashing only and/or make it clear that if everything else is aggravated the merit does not protect the last box.

    Here:

    "Effect: Your character’s resolve is unwavering, even when suffering wounds, broken limbs, and lost blood. She can fight on past the point that her body demands she quit. When she suffers bashing damage that would remove her last Health box, you may spend a point of Willpower to keep the last box, and instead upgrade damage in her other Health boxes first. If it is no longer possible to upgrade wounds in your other health boxes, any excess damage should be applied to the rightmost health box as normal."

    ---------------------

    Typically, yes, but damage that completely destroys large quantities of tissue get upgraded to agg against mortals. If you're getting hit by an out of control Camrey at 40 mph, yeah, you take like 10 lethal. If you get hit by an 18 wheeler at 70 mph, it's safe to say you're taking agg.
    Well yes. But that is because normally the damage fills all boxes and wraps around to aggro. Getting hit by a 18 wheeler (size 25 or so) at 70 MPH (+4 Damage) will kill most people because it will do 12L damage on average (and possibly much more) which for someone with 7 health will fill most of their track with aggro instantly. The vehicle collision rules work fine (or well enough for me). The problem is when they are combined with something that defies common sense like the (as currently written) Punch Drunk merit, in which case things start to get wonky.

        I think with punch drunk if something would ultimately deal aggravated due to wrap around then it would count as aggravated damage overall. Which would negate punch drunk. You could read it as only damage that was originally listed as aggravated but that sounds a little extreme and supernatural rather than resilient.

        Could certainly use a bit of clarification though because there are several cases things don't seem so clear. If the damage would do even a single point of aggravated damage do you simply ignore the effects of the merit or do u let the merit work as intended as u fill the health boxes until you mark the first aggravated and then shift down that lethal.

        For simplicity sake. Id go with it simply saying if a attack would result in even a single point of aggravated damage before factoring this merit then this merit is ignored entirely. That puts it in the proper perspective.

        Sure, u can stay up for many punches to the head but as soon as one of those punches would wrap to aggravated you simply ignore the merit. Even the idea of someone with 2 stamina having 6 aggravated with their last box unmarked seems really wrong to me. I'll likely be ruling it as detailed above for my games.
