                                    Something really important to me is the in-universe interpretations mortals have of the supernatural world. Regarding Vampires, for example, a forum poster once eloquently said how a mortal Vampire hunter in Tokyo would come to much different conclusions about Vampirism than one in Montreal, let alone encountering Strix or even blood spirits from the Shadow. How do you keep it all straight?

                                    So if we, in real life, are actually in-universe of Mummy how can we distinguish between, you know
                                    • Arisen, Deceived, and Shuankhsen, who then might be so deluded they think they're native to Mesoamerica or just twice-arisen
                                    • Sadikh and Fasad
                                    • Random undead skeletons, zombies, ghosts, and walking corpses risen by relics, Affinities, or Utterances
                                    • Sin-eaters, Prometheans, Vampires, Mage liches, Synthesized Strix, Purified, and whatever the fuck else?
                                    So I'm with ya'll in a way. As far as we should really care that's a lot of 'mummified corpses.'

                                    But screw that.

                                    The Deathless are torn between the familiar - seeing the Nameless Empire's inheritances throughout history ('The US like bald eagles, but we invented animal symbols for our nation') - and the unfamiliar ('What is computer'). But Iremite Mummies, naturally, have a common connection. They all trace their origin to the Rite of Return. When the Arisen see a Shuankhsen they're like, "Oh yeah, those guys. We've been fighting them for 5,000 years." They, in all likelihood, all have met and all know each other, barring Memory.

                                    They also all serve. They have a Purpose. The Judges, the temakhs, and the Devourer are all their masters. They have a Purpose.

                                    Now let's picture these non-Iremite Mummies. Why are they Mummies in Mummy the Curse?

                                    Possibly because they're alien. The ritualists who created them were inspired by the Rite of Return and the Deathless, and they called out into the depths of other worlds seeking patronage for their hubris. They found something radiant, beautiful, and divine, in the same way the Deathless recall Azar and the other gods of ancient Irem, except wholly other. Not Lovecraftian abomination other. The Iremite Mummies all have a common bond. But they share no common bond here. They are other. There is no familiarity. No sense of, "Oh, what's up Shuankhsen? We meet again."

                                    But what if these Mummies had no Purpose? What if they could no longer serve because their patrons were Devoured? The Deathless anticipate Sothic turns as eras of freedom, vacations from service, and a glimpse of impossible freedom. What if these Mummies longed for it? What if Purpose reminded you - helped you reclaim Memory - what it is to be human? The Deathless want to follow their personal ambitions in their search for Apotheosis, but these other Mummies long for anything to fill the void that was stripped from them. They are torn between the embers of their patrons' former majesty and the puppeteering of their hollowed husks by darker forces.

                                    They're pitiable. They are what the Arisen fear they'll one day become, what they know they'll become: their masters obliterated, Duat lay in ruin, and so far distant from humanity that they'd be thankful just to be able to call themselves Deathless once again, for these non-Iremite Mummies are nothing but Lifeless

                                                    I have never really had a problem with the Arisen having all been created with the same rite in ancient Irem. That said, it also hasn't stopped me from getting character ideas for mummies that seem to have come from another culture. For example, I had an idea for an ancient Roman mummy with a warrior cult that eventually evolved into an underground fight club in the modern day. My solution for his Roman features and love of the culture was to create this Merit:

                                                    Resonant Descent (...)
                                                    Prerequisite: Memory 5
                                                    The memories of most mummies are extremely limited, allowing them to gain only the smallest glimpses of their most recent Descents. There is something different about you, though. One of your lifetimes echoes through the ages, coming back to you time and time again. Something about this Descent has made it stick in your memory, whether it was the foundation of your cult, the beginning of a great love or rivalry, or even just a time of great personal satisfaction. Regardless of the reason, you can remember this lifetime as clearly as you can remember your most recent Descent. For some Arisen, this may even cause alterations to the sahu, causing the mummy to resemble a member of the most prevalent culture encountered during this lifetime. A mummy may only have one Resonant Descent, and this lifetime is never the mummy's original life in Irem.

                                                    By doing that, I made it so that the character had originated in Irem like the others, but had become so attached to one of his (many) Roman lifetimes, that he'd basically gone native. I also thought that such a Merit would make sense to exist in the setting. Given the Swiss cheese Memory that the Arisen are said to possess, it had always struck me as odd that the mechanics seemed to make it so that the mummies would remember the most recent events and forget the older ones, rather than having an odd mix of ancient and modern memories.
