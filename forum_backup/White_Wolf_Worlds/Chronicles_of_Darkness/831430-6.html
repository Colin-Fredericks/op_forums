    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
    Rebuilding a Broken Bulwar -[I]"When a Bulwark breaks, the freehold can rebuild it at the next time the ritual would normally be performed. It should never be taken lightly or done too easily, even if the Bulwark ritual occurs frequently. The more quickly and easily the ritual is, the harder their enemies will strike when the opportunity presents itself."
    Should not this be the other way around? When True Fae are satisfied with Tale of Sheherezada Courts should the be less aggressive if story is not told this particular time – only that leave it on two, three days and you are never sure if Gentry did not use the time to send Huntsmen or other forces. On the other hand – if you have only one ritual a year it can hell break everything. Ritual must succeed, or you will need to wait whole year without defense of Bulwark – and True Fae can attack any time in this year period! One WIDE open defense problem against many smaller ones, but eating day to day time of Freehold members.
    The easier the Bulwark is to restore, the smaller the window of opportunity to strike at a vulnerable Freehold. When promptly fixing the Bulwark is neither possible or simple, the Others and their servitors have greater allowance and time to prepare for devasting incursions, and there is less urgency driving them.

    On a different topic, I really like how Embracing The Wyrd feels (the Tolkien examples where characters suddenly become more threatening and loom terrible and great are on point!) and I think the benefits scale appropriately with your supernatural might. And I finally get what was said about the power of dropping the Mask. That the significance of the act has the repercussions it does in regards to Hedgegates and your visibility in the Wyrd feels extremely fitting without making me think that it's always going to be too risky. I asked myself several times why I would ever want to drop the mask in 1e, given how impractical and lacking in an outright advantage it was, but in 2e? I can see many situations where it would be useful and a risk worth taking, and there's a lot more substance in it, both mechanical and narrative.

    Speaking of, I find it interesting how some Contracts seem to tempt you to drop the mask to grasp their full power, because doing so allows you to achieve some permanent advantage. It tells of that magic leaving a mark, transforming you the more you take, rewarding you with an extension of its effect. That's effin' cool and speaks volumes of the transfigurating influence of the Wyrd on Changelings.

        Glamour
        • Harvesting from Emotions – I really like those rules. I was going more on the idea of that Harvested lose on point of Willpower, not all of them – but this is much more profound and powerful. With Reticent Condition, changelings are now somehow “psychic predators”, using tricks of their True Fae once masters. I love this! <3
        • Harvesting from Emotions - Does Harvesting in this way also rep all of victims Willpower? Cause it sounds it should be a bit more like one point in this example, as they are not so “palpable”. They are just dreams, not “living” emotions. Or maybe up to point of Willpower for each Harvestered Glamour point in dreams?
        Clarity

        As I mentioned it in recent topic, just after showing of new rules – I still need to highlight that each Trigger should have two versions, Common and Specific. Common would leave penalty like -1 and Specific like -3. Common would be general events and typical life complications – Common Trigger could “romantic relations” for character that lost a husband to True Fae in Durance. Then going to romcom with friends will be Common Trigger for character. Specific Trigger would be “Seeing lost husband”, when player and Storyteller assumes that it’s totally changed person after Durance and plays of Gentry.

        EACH Trigger for any changeling should have those Common and Specific versions so players would need to have highly specialized and very general ones! In that way Storyteller will not be a dick for putting characters with Specific only ones over very extreme world coincidence so Clarity would be tested for super snowflake characters. Or the one with high Common like that could go down in Clarity five times quicker with general worded Trigger.
