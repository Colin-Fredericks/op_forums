      A geist is not a spirit and therefore any method of getting one as a totem is going to be unusual — they can only communicate and be communicated with in the context of fear and intimidation and they don't have the Essence concerns a spirit would have that makes becoming a totem an attractive offer.

      Kerberoi and Reapers are likewise not spirits and thus are not assumed to have the metaphysical qualities, knowledge, or anatomy needed to scar Gifts into werewolves.

      Book of the Dead is 1e and thus may require adaptation and judgment calls, but its section on various monsters in the Underworld is fairly comprehensive. Going from that…

      Most rites that affect spirits don't affect ghosts, but rites that affect humans can.

      Werewolves of any quantity are going to run into the same survival problems as normal living people in the Underworld with the added wrinkle that eating ghosts has the same spiritual effects and consequences as eating humans.

      Werewolves who have an auspice may also find their spiritual nature adapting to the Underworld in a similar phenomenon to what makes them a probable origin of briarwolves in the Hedge (being border-dwelling shapeshifters whose border realm no longer exists), which 2e's low-Harmony confinement to the Shadow further echoes. There's a story hook that the totem of the Lodge of Death is rumored to enable its members to avoid this degeneration, and the Pure do not suffer it in the Underworld in particular (being separated from Luna's light and the support of the Lunes being non-issues for them), but the Great Below in general is of less concern to werewolves without specific drives and plenty of time to spare.

      In general, the dead require specialist knowledge to deal with, for which alliance and patronage from spirits of death is the most straightforward route.

      I don't know what you mean by "scored the Underworld."

                                              Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                              So what merits or rituals would help, I guess that remains my question.
                                              Most of the things you are asking for do not already exist.

                                              The rite in Book of the Dead provides an example in 1e Werewolf's formatting, which you may attempt to translate into 2e's systems for how rites work, for werewolves to try to cross between the lands of the living and the dead without engaging with the systems presented in Geist 2e instead. That is largely the extent of published material for werewolves to deal with the Underworld.

                                              Merits are this franchise's general-purpose mechanic for making granular tweaks to the way a character interacts with the world in the absence of a more specially-made mechanic, but there are not published Merits for acclimating werewolves and their powers to the Underworld, because Werewolf is not a game about delving into the Underworld and its characters are not built with the assumption that this is a thing they would need to do. You might take inspiration from the Ivory Claw Merits and Predator King Merits in Night Horrors: Shunned by the Moon for the dot values accorded to certain types of effect, but you would still need to actually devise those Merits.

                                              You are asking what tools you can use for this and the answer is homebrew, which is best built for a purpose. There are no Johnny/Jenny MTG combo shenanigans to be had here. This is storytelling in a setting where the typical state of things is fairly well-delineated and weird exceptions occur often enough to dedicate a common title to the books collecting and debuting them.

                                              Why Ghost wovles and not Bone Shadows? I know the one advantage Bone Shadows have is Death Gifts granted by their tribal totem, which, while not exactly much help, is still a step above having no tribe.
                                              Because Ghost Wolves are the go-to population for having weird shit done to their template, as demonstrated by the number of variants in Shunned by the Moon that the Thihirtha Numea specifically are susceptible to, attempting to become equal in strength to a tribe is an easily defined ambition for lodge totems, and the Firstborn don't usually play quite as loose with what they can grant their adoptive progeny. If you want to enforce tribal membership in this hypothetical instead and/or grapple with e.g. the consequences of tribal patronage from a ghost-Firstborn then you're free to do so, but these are decisions you will have to make yourself to suit the story you're trying to facilitate.

                                              Also, "Find a powerful totem spirit from the choir of death, learn so esoteric abilities, and homebrew the effects of drinking from the rivers of the Underworld/surviving on Ectoplasmic ghosts" would have been a much shorter answer.
                                              And "I want help homebrewing for this very specific chronicle conceit" would be a much more effective way of soliciting aid than asking what tools exist for a chronicle conceit that completely reorients werewolves from their normal place in the cosmology when most gamelines not named Mage or Beast only really give tools for using their related otherworld to the semi-natives the gameline concerns and maybe a more reliable means of entry than mortals get to other monsters if they have space.

                                              The amount of special rules werewolves have for interacting with the Underworld fits entirely into their section of Book of the Dead, which is from the previous edition. Literally everything else is going to have to be homebrew, because the Underworld was extremely mysterious and largely only speculatively described before Geist came out and then was notably reoriented in 2e when Geist came out again. Werewolves mainly hunt within territories and most things that are dead are not pursued into the lands of the dead unless they crawl back up into that territory, with the typical exceptions being dedicated specialists seeking deep lore beyond the scope of a typical pack hunter.

                                              If what you want is a general overview of how werewolves work I can provide that for you too, but it doesn't inspire confidence or motivate me to learn that you couldn't figure out "I'll need to homebrew something to suit my needs" from the starting premise of "I want to run a game where the pack is the only surviving werewolves in the world, hiding out in the Underworld and adapting their spiritual nature to suit a place that absorbs forgotten ghosts into the walls instead of dealing with spirit politics in the primal reflections of material locations." Chronicles has not been written with crossover play specifically in mind for half its lifetime, most of its otherworlds are written as places you should be wary of lingering in, and werewolves have long been the primary window into the diverse ways in which the Shadow can bring strange trouble to bear as an extension of how spirits and resonance can do strange things even as they mistake non-spiritual phenomena for rare things within their area of expertise.
