    Vienna. Alla visits the ancient vampires of Austria, clinging not only to lost Hapsburg glory but Rome itself. Kindred considered by themselves part of a Sect long gone and a city they had never visited. But from the decedent bloodlines or Rome plots stir. The Werewolves do a ritual to protect their packmates and the innocent and the Fae meet the Spring Queen on the final days of her 1920 reign. Wolfram moves to counter his rivals schemes for Christina. The PCs walk through the bones of the old world.

    No Marek this time. I’d refer his usual readers to Dacian. His player should be back to journaling soon, their vacation means a break in the journaling.

    Session VI

    Tatiana’s Conversations

    So, volchonok, did our rite meet with your approval? If you keep making of yourself a nuisance, Ochranny will give you a smack and you will regret it.

    Why were you not part of the ritual? Because you have no Kuruth to chain, and you are too much of a nuisance to walk a protective circle.

    Marek calmed me just fine by himself. That was the point. If he could not with his will alone, he could scarcely hope to chain my rage, and if he could chain mine, his would follow.

    The little one was grumpy because there was a nuisancy volchonok...

    I am sorry, volchonok. No, you are not a nuisance, not really. Though you should probably treat Ochranny with more respect. Still, you are my volchonok. You will hunt with me to harness the cycle?

    Calm volchonok, calm. Yes, you may hunt with me, and we will make it the best hunt.

    Heh, you are a clever volchonok. No, Lizette was not grumpy because of you, since she could not see you. She was grumpy, I believe, because it was dawn. She still got the most sleep of all of us, so I do not know what she was grumbling at.

    She did not go with the rest of us to the fairy place.

    Why yes, she did go with the ones who smell of death for their meeting. They wished to look more but not too intimidating, so I myself did not go. But she did not join us even when they did.

    Why would she? Oh, you know, human things.

    Oh no, volchonok, you would be bored.

    Just a room full of women pretty like our bird. That is all.

    No, they were merely very pretty, not exceedingly beautiful. Still, it was quite enjoyable

    Why do you care so what Lizette, Wolfram and Alla were doing, volchonok?

    Ah I see. The magical necklace is too much of a human thing to be an interesting hunt, but finding a wayward vampire child who consumes flesh interests you, eh? You know what would make this hunt easier, volchonok?

    I see by you ignoring me that you do. Human things. Human things would make this hunt easier. Knowing about streets and alleys and rooftops and automobiles. It is an old trail true - it would have been quite difficult. But it was impossible, here in the city. I will get better at it though. My pack quite likes places like this.

    Alla cannot join the hunt in the day. She is a vampire, you know this. Fenric came instead. It is he who found as close as we have to a trail, a place the quarry gets clothes. Wolfram was... looking at books, something about the necklace.

    No, it is good. Human thing or no, we must move quickly in regards to Christina. The Ivory Claw tried to send a letter to her brother, asking to have her as a mate. Marek and Dacian stole it, but he will only be waiting so long before he takes her anyway.

    Because, silly volchonok, he is Pure, and should have nothing that he wants. And because Wolfram wants her for a mate.

    He is very prepared to fight for her. With guns, but still. It is better to fight as one is strongest, after all. I believe Lizette will be making silver bullets even.

    He will not shoot me, or Marek, so why should I care?

    He is not Claimed, he is something else, Marek has said and he knows these things. It is not for volchonoks to decide.

    I too think Lizette wants Christina for a mate but... it is a human thing. Yes, a very stupid human thing, volchonok. She cannot marry her, and so cannot take her as a mate officially, or keep her away from the Pure. She was not at her house just to mate though, she used fairy magic to find what the thief looks like.

    Light colors. Pale skin, blonde hair, yellow eyes. Like Lizette’s. It was a woman.

    That is all. It was magic, it cannot give a smell. It is perhaps a vampire though, or from them. One of Alla’s kind told her there was one gathering artifacts of this sort.

    The thought occurred. I will consult with Marek, but we hunt for many things,and surely one at least is worthy of the Siskur Dah.

    Soon, volchonok. Tomorrow we will hunt Lady Summer and steal her power. But not today.


    Dacian’s Book of Shadows

    21 June 1920 - Night

    Around 8:00, Wolfram, Alla, and Fenric went to meet the Prince of Vienna. The werewolves were asked not to accompany as that could be considered a show of power, to arrive with two such bodyguards. How it could be anything other is beyond me, but that was apparently not the desire of our vampire. For their part, Marek and Tatiana were not insulted but did invite Dearbháil and myself to join them for a mystic rite at dawn. The sheer excitement the Fairest and I shared was palatable.We changed to be more comfortable in Gemütlichkeit. Our Uratha companions would be more than welcome there, Dearbháil and I would make sure of that. That Dearbháil took both their hands and playfully pleaded with them may have decided things for both. We eventually found Lizette to invite her as well. She was drawing plans for what looked to be an enormous teapot modified to include weaponry. The Wizened stumbled through trying to politely turn us down as she was not a terribly social creature.

    She was rescued by the return of Wolfram. The irony of the situation was not lost on me, but I deferred my amusement. Alla received a second and mysterious invitation to meet another powerful vampire and wished for us to know of her whereabouts. Dearbháil and I would have been more interested in meeting this strange vampire with Alla, had she not mentioned that she didn’t think he would wish to keep us, but she was unsure. We wished her well in her meeting. However, Lizette jumped at the opportunity to help protect Alla from this strangely large vampire. Something about eating people was mentioned as more of a question? Vampires are strange.

    We finally arrived at Gemütlichkeit around 9:00. Unsurprisingly, the seat of the Spring Court was warm and inviting. Our werewolf companions marked their territory at a table which was out of the way and yet held a clear view of nearly all the floor. A waitress which Tatiana observed for possibly a moment too long delivered a bottle of quality Russian vodka based on the scent I caught on the way by. I spotted Queen Sophie just after we entered and she graced me with her lovely smile. I chose to mingle with the local courtiers a bit, as it will never hurt to have friendly contacts, before requesting an audience with the Queen. Sophie nearly blushed at how formal I was, but I soon had her much more comfortable with my presence. I believe my name will be well received in Viennese Courts for some time. Dearbháil joined the revelry after she felt I had acquired an adequate amount of attention. She truly is quite selfless as Fairest go. My feathered friend convinced Queen Sophie to connect us with the local Autumn Court as well. They would likely have information about powerful relics and how to dispose of them, plus we could take Lizette to something less uncomfortable for her.

    Gemütlichkeit proved enjoyable for all but as dawn comes early, we returned to the hotel around 11:00. I do hope we can continue to experience the joys of all our cabal as a group. Lizette and Wolfram seemed none the worse for their visit, if perhaps with a bit wider eyes than they left. I must learn tomorrow what was so interesting.

    22 June 1920 - Early

    If only each morning could start this way, I would not even regret waking before dawn. All, save Alla for obvious reasons, gathered in the fleeing twilight outside of Vienna. As the Uratha and Ochranńy, our totem, collected their ritual equipment, Dearbháil prepared us for the day. She warmly took my face and kissed me on either cheek. Fenric only received a warm smile, but he was affected similarly. While she moved on to the others, I was awash in Dearbháil’s potent magic. Her use of our Court Contracts was useful as well. Once we were all similarly refreshed and awed by Dearbháil’s charity and beauty respectively, the rite began.

    I focused on every detail I could detect as the Uratha took their positions. The rite was meant to “chain their rage” to help protect the pack and themselves from the bestial fury within. The four main themes I determined were Rage, Calm, Binding, and Dawn. As the first rays of light struck the ritual area, Tatiana symbolized the inner rage of the werewolf. Marek circled Tatiana while binding her hands with a chain made of iron and bone. Marek was the symbol of calm, which is not at all surprising, though I was intrigued by the chain. Tatiana is of the Iron Master tribe, while Marek is a Bone Shadow. The sympathetic connections had to be very strong given the usage. With the obvious symbol of binding, I looked on straining to see the slightest glimmer of our totem. In a wider circle, we were told that Ochranńy moved in the opposite revolution from Marek, protecting the rest of the pack from the rage within should something go wrong. The rite was as beautiful as it was powerful. All to keep others safe from the unbridled wrath within each Uratha. The assembly was speechless; whether through fatigue, ignorance, or appreciation of the display before us was not readily apparent.

    I think I will retire to my room, perhaps attempt to discuss the finer points of the ritual with Tatiana. Or perhaps I will not ask the Uratha what she did with her rage and instead review the map of the grounds for possible improvements.

    22 June 1920 - After Lunch

    While I do not enjoy being commonly referred to as a thief, I know where my skills lie. When a flustered Wolfram returned from a meeting with Christina’s Lady’s maid, he asked if I could steal a letter being sent to Christina’s brother. Apparently Hohenburg rudely accelerated his intentions with Christina by placing a letter in the post asking Gunter to instruct his sister to accept an upcoming proposal to marry. In the time I have known Wolfram, I have not seen him display such distress as his perceived helplessness. I did not allow him to humble himself toward me further, wordlessly grabbing a random courier’s bag and leaving. At the end of the hall, I turned to find Marek accompanying me. Silently nodding to each other, we continued on to the Post Office.

    Entering the building as though I was judging every brick, I announced myself the courier of an unnamed Romanian noble. The postmen immediately believed my ruse. I noted there was nothing supernatural present as we were given the full tour of the premises on the way to the Postmaster’s Office. He gave me an abundance of assurances that any package my patron might send would be in the very best hands. Marek was immediately accepted as my protector so after we left, I sent him back in to distract the front office. The Uratha stormed back in demanding (in a much thicker than usual Czech-tinted German) a trinket that was mysteriously missing from my bag when we left the building. His size and accent were more than enough to grab the attention of all present, making it much easier for me to slip back in. I used my abilities to guarantee no one could easily follow me, laughed my way past the single locked door barring my path, and grabbed the letter from the outgoing box. I ran back out just as easily and after another tense moment, Marek exited as well. The post was scheduled to leave by 2:00 pm. We returned to the hotel before lunch was brought into the main dining room. I left the unopened and uncreased envelope at Wolfram’s place at the table. I thought he might enjoy the present.

    Wolfram has since left to research what the relic could be in the storied libraries of Vienna. I hope to do something similar through the help of the Autumn Court, once I have been accepted as an ally.

    22 June 1920 - Afternoon

    Our Sin-Eater returned with only slightly more answers than questions. Wolfram asked Marek and myself to assist him in deciphering the subtext of his library findings on what we learned was known as the Eye of Iblis. That I was included in this scholarly work was flattering as I am fully aware that the other men are greatly my superior, but I contributed nonetheless. After the three of us poured over the notes, consulted tomes and each other, then cross referenced various religious, folk, and historical accounts, we were certain of the following:

    The Eye of Iblis aids in the command of some creatures, likely spirits, while worn

    Encourages acts of atrocity for what amounts to good luck while still wearing the necklace

    Increases the possibility of madness and/or debased actions

    The observed obsession comes not simply from touching the piece but from wearing it

    Theoretically enhances the use of certain hedge magics, notably those dealing with spirits

    Any effect on the supernatural, such as ourselves, is unknown

    Is fairly old as it was possessed by Pashas of the Ottoman Empire for some time before the Great War

    After considering the information and description of the piece, I determined that dislodging the gem would likely break the magic without losing value as exotic jewelry. With jeweler’s tools, I or Lizette could mark the gem around the edges with “occult” symbols to increase the value to some if they are unaware or unconcerned about magic.

    While we three understudies of the occult toiled over old knowledge, Lizette was busy gathering new information. Using her own Contracts, the Wizened learned the thief was a woman with striking yellow eyes, pale skin, and blonde hair. Sadly, we had no ideas as to who this woman might be, though I suspected Hohenberg recruited one of the Pure. This is entirely speculation, of course. Once that was disseminated among the cabal, our small engineer discussed making Wolfram silver weaponry as it seems he may encounter Pure resistance to him proposing to Christina. While most minds fall to silver daggers, it was determined that bullets make the better option. I will likely liberate silverware from several of the numerous fine dining establishments given the Pure must keep close watch on the local silversmiths. Lizette can happily melt my bounty into ammunition. But before I start such an endeavor, we must prepare for Dearbháil’s performance tonight.


    Lizette’s Plans

    22nd June 1920

    Well. I have now gotten love letters from two women, and kissed one. Well, one of the love letters spent a bit more time asking me not to kill her potential husband than on affections, but they were there. Okay, not kill. I already said I would not do that. Not take away to faerie, or turn into a frog, or horse, or something. Still, I’ll take it. Marlene’s letter expressed she was curious about my “tastes,” and curious about some things she heard about the French and their…talents. While it was not unseemly, her intentions and interest were clear.

    Okay, more (less?) important things. Herr Hohemberg has attempted to speed up the clock, trying to have a letter sent to Gunter asking permission to propose now with his backing. Marlene tipped us off, and suggested Wolfram put a move on Christina and begin making his own case as a match. She got a kiss for that. Even got to decide where it went. Meanwhile Marek and Dacian absconded with the letter.

    There was talk of blowing up the post office. Enough to get a gal all giddy, especially as Wolfram, who tends to only want to use huge amounts of explosives where necessary, was for it. Obviously he quickly altered it to no loss of life, which is understandable; the postal workers are not our enemy and don’t mean us harm, so I was okay with them leaving. Still, this deal was getting worse all the time. We discussed ways to draw them out, then moved onto a very localized explosion in the mail room, then a fire, then Wolfram decided he did not want possibly valuable letters destroyed so smoke bomb, than no explosion. Bah.

    I need to work on some sort of shaped charges that can eliminate a portion of a structure while keeping the rest intact. If I had something that could blow, say, inward or something, I bet I could have used it. Well, at least until Wolfram decided not to destroy letters.

    Anyway, my visit to Madame Esterházy’s home was not for for skirt chasing at all. Actually, the real reason was to use my Contracts of Animation to examine the box. It took a couple tries, but I did get the face of the thief, which was somewhat unusual though I don’t think she was fae. Still, there are not many yellow-eyed women, besides yours truly, so that should help, maybe?

    I’m doing this backwards, maybe sideways. Following the last journal entry, Alla had gone to meet the lead Vampire of her Court, and got an invitation by a mysterious stranger, one Maximillian Nostras. Which sounds like a made up name to me. As this vampire was a complete stranger, she wanted some backup, but Werewolves might be a bit too threatening, since they are not the hardest supernatural creatures to detect but each one is a juggernaut. Alla made a comment about keeping Dearbháil, so she backed down, and Dacian did too, which apparently surprised Alla. Someone had to girl the hell up, and that person was of course the Frenchwoman, yours truly.

    The meeting went fine. Maximillian Nostras is an enormous man, a member of a group called the Macellarius. Most of the conversation was him being side about the Prince, a lady named Magdalena, and a member of a rival lineage called the Eupraxus. He suggested she and some of her hangers on, members of another group called Gorgons who were part of the “Circle of the Crone” might have the necklace. Apparently the Eupraxus are some sort of Vampire nobility, but this one was very new. I am not sure what “new” means to a vampire.

    Maximillian was eager to ingratiate himself to Alla. He offered her that information for free, and even asked her about doing him a favor which would put him in some debt to her. I confess some suspicion about the large man’s goals. Also, of course, vampires are scary as hell. I try to avoid them when possible, and have a handy flamethrower when not.

    The next morning I watched the Werewolves do a ritual to control their anger, with the sunrise. I was not going to be up for sunrise, but that is how it goes. First sunrise I may have seen since the war. Not sure.

    Anyway, then was Marlene with the letters, me going to the house to examine the box, and the whole post office thing. Wolfram took some time to research the amulet, which is called the Eye of Iblis, which is a Muslim thing apparently from the Ottoman Empire. It makes you stronger if you do bad stuff, but is addictive, or at least makes you disinclined to part with it. Also, some stuff about controlling beings of “Smokeless Fire.” Spirits maybe? Elemental Lost? Bah makes my head hurt. I have my thing…Wolfram, Marek and Dacian will figure out that part, I am sure.

    Oh, some discussion of weapons, with Dacian taking silver cutlery and cheese knives and such, to make for silver bullets or weapons. Silver weapons are not too effective, but that issue does not extend to bullets. I was thinking about a way to have a silver toothed chain saw as well. Sadly I did not keep silver around as it seemed…antagonistic given the Werewolves. But we need to have a way to fight Werewolves if we are not werewolves. Silver bullets and a silver toothed chainsaw could work. You can’t just kill them with a silver cheese knife.

    Tonight should be interesting. Dearbháil is performing. Wolfram will be taking Christina there. Marlene’s letter suggested we meet in private while her mistress was away, but when I saw her at the house she seemed somewhat disinclined. Not that I was trying to initiate shenanigans really beyond rewarding her I had work to do. She could go to the concert then after all, maybe, though given Dearbháil really seems to dislike her maybe not.

    But honestly right now I need to get back to this duplicate regardless. While it won’t be needed if some of Dacian’s ideas work out, we may need it, and I would prefer to potentially have it if we need it. Hmmm, I wonder if I can make it explode…

    End Session VI

Wolfram, he made it obvious that they were not humans, by shooting them in the face and having them not fall over, as humans are wont to do.

        Dacian’s Book of Shadows

        22 June 1920 - Evening

        I mentioned in my last entry that we must prepare for Dearbháil’s performance. As I have not yet seen Tatiana wear anything other than her uniform, I suggested (delicately) that we investigate the tailors of Vienna. I knew that she would not stand for anything too fragile or elegant. Thus I chose a more discreet tailor to properly measure and prepare a masculine suit. Tatiana’s subtle curves were appreciated without being emphasized. The hand stitching should hold up, providing she gives at least moderate care, to a fair amount of wear. It was a handsome fit in the end. I paid to make sure the ensemble was completed before we had to leave to support and enjoy Dearbháil’s efforts.

        23 June 1920 - Dawn

        The cabal has had an exciting and eventful evening. Our Uratha were not present, as Marek pulled Tatiana to scout their ritual space for tonight. Thus my purchases today were in vain. I am certain our Iron Master will resent missing the festivities at the opera however. ...I never thought I could say that about Tatiana.

        Regardless, when we arrived, we found our seats were well appointed indeed. Christina and Wolfram shared a box while Lizette, Alla, Fenric, and myself shared an adjacent box. I was using my time to practice my forgery. It is not an art in which I excel, but could be incredibly useful in the future. I believe Lizette was working on some form of clothing design. When our Minstrel took the stage all other pursuits were put aside. Though I had had the pleasure of hearing Dearbháil sing in the past, knowing the tongue in which she sang added considerable depth to the experience. While we sat in our box, I noticed one of the Theosophists in attendance as well. Her presence was not immediately alarming, though it did serve to remind me of the tenuous situation in which we find ourselves. I would hesitate to claim we are beset upon all sides, as we have as yet not made our presence known. We came to intermission and I noticed Lizette seemed to slump against the back of her oversized seat. Alla left without comment, and I refreshed my drink.

        I returned just as Dearbháil took the stage. Her hauntingly beautiful voice filled the hall once again, but only for a few moments before dissonance arose from near the entryway of the wide opera house. A voice cried out, “First St. Petersburg, now Vienna!” Tracing the source of the interruption, a brawl of some form tore away from the doors to reveal four gun waving Communists. The upstarts fought with six men from the opera, who struck soundly with batons to restore order for Dearbháil. The Fairest ceased her performance as if the note had been severed from her throat. Replacing her melodious song came her Irish temper. Incensed that her opening night was rudely interrupted, Dearbháil roared from the stage at the offenders. One left immediately, though his associates were not so lucky. The men from the opera were quite skilled with their batons, though the gunshot which rang from Wolfram’s box finished one as another ran away. The fourth was possibly the least lucky as I struck him blind before the gunshot. He could only hear the terror of the audience and the deathrattle from the ground next to him. Though it was perhaps missed given how liberally the batons were used. A shriek from the other side of the opera house drew our attention to three large men advancing menacingly on a group of women. A chair lay rended asunder by why blade I could not tell. Wolfram seemed to immediately understand the situation and fired his pistol once again. His aim was true, striking one of the men in the face. I flinched as I was not prepared for the horrific scene that never came. I would learn afterwards the men were vampires of the Gangrel clan. These vampires either instigated the Communist attack or were incredibly lucky in their opportunism. Either way, things were not going as they intended. The women were withdrawing and the men, suddenly without targets and the center of attention, seemed to want to run away. I tried to strike one blind as I had with the Communists, but nothing happened. I am unsure if vampires are immune to my abilities, though I suspect I am simply too unskilled to perform under duress. The three Gangrel forced their way through the now hostile crowd and jumped out the window to escape.

        Our Fairest, never one to back away from a challenge, addressed the crowd directly and frankly, calming the situation by taking back the attention of the audience. Dearbháil made clear that tonight was for everyone, as no one had come away unchanged or untouched by the Great War and in this, we were all as one.                                                         Dacian’s Book of Shadows

                                                        24 June 1920 - Afternoon

This time it was to make sure we were not inside the territory of another. This ritual was for something incredibly important to the Uratha. It was referred to only as the Siskur-Dah. The purpose of this sacred hunt was to discredit the Pure of Vienna.

However the Siskur-Dah ritual was not the subtle machinations of a plotting cabal. Lizette carved a wooden mask which parodied Gunner for use in the ritual. Tatiana wore the mask and began strutting around the ritual area much like a peacock. Marek wore his rifle though it was obviously for show. Our totem and the Uratha’s companion spirits were also involved, I learned later. No sound was made as this was to be an assassination. The spirits assisted Marek in stalking their prey, as he tracked from the shadows. At length, Marek lept from his vantage point with an outstretched hand, symbolizing the reach of our cabal. The strike left a bloody splash upon the mask before Tatiana fell back stiffly.

Dearbháil did not disappoint, of course. The security tonight was higher, which I’m sure offered some small reassurance to our Fairest. In attendance, we spotted Gloria the Theosophist, though she did not seem to notice us at first. Given Lizette’s aggressive hammering on the train, it was for the best that we remained unnoticed. Additionally, Wolfram noted two sets of vampires. A man and a woman, each with a small compliment of bodyguards. Alla chose to visit the lady though I am unsure how their meeting went. I should also note that Lizette’s abilities as a seamstress are amazing. The crimson gown she wore defied her child-like frame and proved she was definitely a mature woman. It was less revealing perhaps than the merlot dress from the last performance, but who am I to complain about presentation of beauty?

Wolfram and Fenric remained in the hotel as he had to light himself on fire to become insubstantial. Fenric was to watch over the body and also turn on the shower to end the burning. I removed any trace of my passing and made it harder to be noticed before grabbing my rope and slinking out and across the street. Together we form a rather potent information gathering team. Now that I have written this and sent it back with Marek, I will accompany our Bound to the crypt where we can investigate the best way to ambush the meeting of the Pure.

While my spectral associate had little issues, I had to pick the lock of the outer gate, sneak past the guard, and then best a most formidable lock on the crypt entrance. Between my skill and my Contracts, I gained entry without alerting the guard.

I could sense the echoes of horrors I heard but never saw slithering past and lumbering through the Apothic Reaches closing in upon me. Once I could see, I found that my breath had caught in my throat and I once again began to breathe.

While disappointing, it was for the best. As we moved among the dead, a soldier from another time awoke and climbed from his casket. Speaking in a thick German that I could only barely recognize, Franz Ludwig Hollenberg asked us to leave. Thinking quickly and assuming the bearing of a true noble, Wolfram addressed Herr Hollenberg and convinced him of the infiltration of “witches” into his fair bloodline. As Hollenberg still wished to serve his family, even in death, he resolved to assist us when these fiends defiled his family’s resting place. That Hollenberg believed werewolves were servants of Satan not withstanding, he apparently felt a debt was owed to his family. Possibly related to an accident involving quantities of alcohol, a mistress, and treacherous stairways. Once Franz Ludwig was committed to assisting us in ridding his family of “the witches” and their “unclean charms,” I inquired as to his willingness to instruct me in the ways of the blade. Marek sniffed the air and determined it was not Gunner, but another werewolf which frequented the gentlemen’s club. I find that when Marek speaks, it is as though the world pauses for a moment as there was occasion for him to make sound. Like me, he is often silent but I don’t believe the world is as concerned when I speak. Intriguing. Alla took first watch, as she would have more problems after dawn and there rest of the cabal would benefit from sleep. I took second watch, and as my duties as sentry are at an end, I have penned this entry. Now I will enjoy the comfort of this warm bed.

I would scout the area to locate and remove any innocents before Lizette would plant an explosive to level the building. After that, we simply find something to take back as proof.

One of Hohenberg’s men delivered an “invitation” to Wolfram to meet Gunnar at the gentlemen’s club. It was immediately agreed, by all but Tatiana, that this was a posturing attempt to push Wolfram out of the contest for Christina’s hand. Tatiana, a bit more pragmatic, stated it was clearly a trap. Also, and I cannot overstate this point enough, that what a noble must and should do have no bearing on what a werewolf will do. Any such thought is ridiculous and dangerously naïve thinking. While I agreed, having seen firsthand everything short of the werewolf portion of the statement come true, I was also not oblivious to the seconds counting down before Christina swooned. Tatiana’s statement contradicted almost every aspect of the Esterházy world view, and there were only minor attempts from the rest of us to the contrary. Once Marlena stabilized her mistress, we decided not to let Tatiana address the subject of nobility again for a while. Beyond that, she, Marek, and I would have coffee in a café nearby in case of trouble and Wolfram would indeed walk into the wolf’s den.

                                                        I am unsure of the outcome, but as I finish this entry, I plan to take a nap in this lightless room, my shoes neatly placed at the foot of the bed. After an hour (or more depending on the nap), I will be as prepared as I can be to possibly lay siege to a Pure stronghold. Were it not for the safety of my cabal, I could never have imagined ever writing that line before this moment.

                                                        Courage, it seems, stems from having something to lose or someone to miss.

                                                        25 June 1920 - Evening

I carried a small travel bag containing my rope and a roll of dynamite, courtesy of Lizette. It was enough explosives, based on our Wizened’s calculations, to level the entire building as well as anyone left inside. The fuse was set for me to have roughly 10 seconds before the blast. I have very few things dear to me left in this world. Those in my cabal are the first on that list. Any harm that befalls them will be met with dire consequences.

But even I was aware the Archduke had outlawed such things among the nobles three years ago. Something about preserving the nobility. Now, I again concerned Tatiana’s stance and nodded to myself.

Not only did Hohenberg challenge our Bound to a duel one day after his relics were to arrive, but he also threatened Wolfram’s entire family. My grip upon my chair tightened until I heard a slight creak from the antique wood. Christina’s concern pulled me from my darkened thoughts as she felt Wolfram must accept even though it was dangerous. Marlena looked almost intoxicated with excitement. Thankfully, Alla saved us from another lesson in the passing of the nobility. She entered carrying Gustav’s skull, which shocked Christina into silence, and prompted a change in subject.

I believe Wolfram also discussed what we all actually are with Christina and Marlena. This will be difficult for them at first, but ultimately it is for the best. Perhaps it will also hasten Christina’s acceptance of how the world truly is and not how she has always been assured it must be.

                                                        Now I must change to go, against Dearbháil’s wishes, to meet a vampire.

                                                        25 June 1920 - Late

We met in a large room, which hinted at opulence, but the open space seemed to suggest past fortunes. At the center of the room sat a massive man behind an equally impressive desk. One in which the vampire suspected of collecting magical artifacts would be in attendance. From there, the meeting turned more into a social gathering. Maximilian greeted each of us, seemingly as equals, though he clearly thought he had an upper hand somehow. Not that I wished him any ill, but I noted that the pair of Uratha did not seem to cause any unease.

                                                        Eventually we took our leave, parting on good terms and without incident. I have penned this short entry, and now plan to visit Franz Ludwig for that lesson in swordsmanship.


                                                        Lizette’s Plans

                                                        25th June 1920

                                                        Upon returning to Christina’s residence from dropping off the equipment, we discovered that Gunnar had sent a letter to Wolfram…at Christina’s house…insisting at a visit at his club at five on the clock. This lead to a great deal of discussion. Should he take an escort? Would it make him look weak? Strong? Christina kept pushing that a man of his peerage cannot simply be kidnapped or murdered in a club he was known to be visiting so he should be safe. Tatiana kept countering that ultimately it was competition for mates and the Pure would stoop to that level if they felt they had to. I thought both points of view had merit. Dacian felt somewhat put off I think about the discussion of his subtlety.

                                                        Christina had an undertone of excitement. It flickered behind her eyes and kept her hands busy, much like mine tend to stay. I am not positive what it was, but I have my suspicions. Marlene passed me a letter pleading me to spend the night in her boudoir, claiming our interruption was distracting and addling her. It all created an…undercurrent.

                                                        In the end, the plan made was that Wolfram will go himself to meet Gunnar, but Dacian, Tatiana, and Marek would be there in case an extraction was needed. In order to protect Christina, she, Marlene, Dearbháil and I would shop. I hope it is a good bonding moment.

                                                        An incident with Dacian and the daggers that are silver made me wonder about what the best thing for defense for Christina and Marlene. I don’t think they can handle chainsaws, which is a shame as I would love to make them personalized chainsaws. I could put their initials on them. But I don’t think they would like it. Shame.

                                                        But this is the 20th century and a lady needs her superweapon. We can’t be depending on men to protect us. Maybe a flamethrower would work. I just don’t want them to catch fire. Maybe a teapot to ride on which sprays hot tea? Time to switch to my planning notebook…


                                                        (later)

                                                        Wolfram’s trip was a success. It could be best summarized as “A lot of noble bullshit happened and now he has been challenged to a duel his opponent will probably not survive to see.” I went shopping with Dearbháil Christina and Marlene. No clothes for me (really, I always end up making my own) but a good trip overall. That excitement I mentioned was very much still there.

                                                        But back to the whole babbling about ones noble family and dueling…these nobles are weighing on my nerves. I mean, I really like Christina, Marlene and Wolfram. I really do. I would sleep with any of them under the right circumstances, and I won’t sleep with just anyone. I consider them all friends. But their fretting about bloodlines, family honor, propriety (not personal, but noble) and all this stuff…I think we in France had the right idea about to handle the nobility broadly speaking. And it was all I could do to stifle the urge to remind them of the year. I guess they can’t help it. They were raised noble and don’t really understand the real world.

                                                        And Wolfram and Gunnar and their manly honor and duels…honor died on the battlefield of Verdun. It is so archaic, so nineteenth century…what year is it again? Why twenty years into this bold new century? Sometimes I wonder if they even know.

                                                        I should note that Christina is still getting used to us discussing our powers. Wolfram’s discussion of his ability to have guns inside himself prompted a comment about difficulty getting used to it, and various other comments seemed to take her off guard as well, including the note Dearbháil can rouse us as if we slept a full night, or Dacian’s casual discussion of being able to become invisible. Still she handled it well, even handled watching the werewolves spar outside human form well. She did ask me to stop discussing blowing up the club.

                                                        The immediate threat has ended, so it looks like a quiet evening. Alla, Dacian, Marek and Tatiana went to give the head (what century is this again?) of the fallen vampire to Maximillian (who hopefully won’t eat it) and trade it for a favor of arranging a vampire salon to talk to Lizbet. Dearbháil trained Christina, Marlene and, comically, Wolfram in the hand to hand. As for me, while we discussed making more bullets that are silver I wound up not. We discussed making some for the Mannlicher, which everyone seemed rather amused by my saying out loud, but wound up, again, not. I did make an incendiary device for Wolfram’s ghost powers. Making a device specifically to burn up my friends sits poorly with me, even if I understand this will not cause him harm in the grand scheme of things.

                                                        Afterwards, Alla took me out to show me some gowns she wanted duplicates of and provide me with money for material. They will be purchased on the morrow. She will have them by the salon.

                                                        As I considered bedding down, Marlene came to me with an issue with some of the houses new electrical systems. My dubiousness that anything regarding that would be in her bedroom was strong, and well justified. Still, she made the trouble up to me, and I fell asleep beside her. I am no Dacian, but slipping back to my rooms was simple enough, and I am prepared to work on the gown until such time as it is the time to set werewolves aflame.

                                                        Mayhem!

                                                        End Session XI

                                                        I honestly wish I wrote down all the stuff Gunnar said, but it was based on a light set of notes and a lot of ad-libbing, though my players insist I killed it. Gunnar began with a rambling story of the Battle of Vienna in 1683 and the friendship of Hohemberg and Esterházy commanders in the battle, and tradition, and an offhanded remark of something his late aunt, Sophie the Duchess of Hohemberg who he broadly described as married to the heir to the throne of Austria, Hungary, Bohemia, etc. I had Wolfram make a politics roll to accurately correct him that as she was a wife in a morganatic marriage she was not to truly be those things. By the end, he was challenged to a duel. And the snobbery continued.

                                                        The long of the short of it was men with impressive mustaches sniping each other. As I say, I hate I did not record it.

                                                        With the sparring Werewolves, the roll to resist Lunacy was not hard for Christina, who has very high both Resolve and Composure (4 in each). Somewhat harder for Marlene, who has 2 and 3. She spent Willpower.

                                                        Dacian’s lessons were not played out.
