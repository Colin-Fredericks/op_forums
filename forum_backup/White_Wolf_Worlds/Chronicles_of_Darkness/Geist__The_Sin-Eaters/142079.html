    Satch Tries Adjusting Mechanics

    05-19-2014, 01:12 AM
    Nearing the end of my corebook reread (after which I begin rereading Book of the Dead and the assorted SASes [and possibly the relevant sections of bluebook stuff like Glimpses of the Unknown or Mirrors]), I'm thinking about tossing together a few general fixes to bring Geist's presented mechanics in line with the GMC update and otherwise tweak the game while staying in line with the presentation of the fiction and other details.

    Seeing as I'm approaching a couple of time-consuming other things, though, I'm mostly going to be doing this piecemeal, attaching rationales when I can. Feedback is appreciated.

    To begin:

    Hack: Adjust the activation dicepool for Manifestations to Manifestation Attribute + Key Skill + Memento Bonus, removing the Manifestation dots from the calculation.
    Rationale(s): Incentivizes pursuit of stronger mementos at all levels of Manifestation proficiency. Additional Manifestation dots already provide bonuses in the form of new abilities or contributions to effect calculation, and the multiplicative progression of gaining new Keys and Manifestations has been observed to skew the perceived power level of the splat. Memento bonuses being Key-based retains the notion of thematic specialization.

    Hack: Adjust the basic range progression of the Boneyard to a flat [successes]*100 yard radius, retaining any conditional expanders (i.e. add [Skill] to successes for range purposes).
    Rationale(s): It's the range provided by the middle dot in the as-written mechanics. The radius measured in miles combined with having five extra dice from the Manifestation dots produces another awkward multiplicative effect that skews the perception of the power.

    Hack: The basic Rage attack is now treated as a [dots]L attack, adhering to changes for specific Keys as already established.
    Rationale: Basic GMC damage update, subject to fiddling if this off-the-cuff tweak turns out to be ill-advised.

    More to come in the not-so-imminent future.

      Tentative tweaks on the Boneyard again:

      Hack: Adjust the basic range of the Boneyard to a flat [successes]*10 yard radius for any Boneyard with a conditional expander. Conditional expanders now increase this to a [successes]*100 yard radius, which remains the basic range of Boneyards without such modifiers.
      Rationale(s): Further incentivizes use of Boneyards that affect the physical world in thematically resonant locations.

      Hack: Change the bonus provided to unlocking same-Key Manifestations within a Boneyard to a flat boost of [Boneyard dots/2, round down, minimum +0].
      Rationale(s): Makes the bonus less of the point of the Boneyard. Reduces the problematic aspects of adding memento bonuses to an activation roll more than once (add bonus to Boneyard activation to get more successes to add to other Manifestation activation in addition to bonus from same memento).

      Hack: Allow the Boneyard to be sustained over multiple scenes by spending a point of plasm per additional scene, as the Marionette's mechanic.
      Rationale: Ups the story potential of the Boneyard as a long-term investigative tool.

      And some others:

      Hack: Switch dot values of charm and vanitas mementos.
      Rationale(s): Utility and replaceability of charms are higher and lower, respectively, than vanitas. Increases value of charms as emergency healing over that provided by draining something you're not supposed to destroy personally. Clusters together mementos that have Keys.

      Hack: Allow Archetypal Manifestations to be performed once per chapter instead of once per story, hooking this into the Beat mechanics for dramatic failure.
      Rationale(s): More dramatic failures are a good thing. More encouragement to act within one's Archetype is a good thing.

      Hack: Base effect of the Shroud is now providing [dots/2 round up] points of general Armor and [dots/2 round down] points of ballistic Armor.
      Rationale: Basic GMC armor update, subject to fiddling if this off-the-cuff tweak turns out to be ill-advised.

      As always, feedback is appreciated.

                  Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post

                  At the moment the bulk of my thoughts on Synergy amount to "god I hope Idigam Chronicle comes out way sooner than expected so I can shamelessly steal from Harmony" because these are stopgap measures and I find it an amusing coincidence that the sample characters from New York have Synergy ratings that almost seem to have been set under the assumption that that was how it worked.

Speaking of which:

                  Hack: Any ghost that has made its "home" in the Underworld (i.e. has picked up a ratty black/white/brown/gray/drab shroud and has lost its original anchors) automatically gains the abilities of the Consume Offering Numen, which is treated as a part of being a ghost that does not manifest outside the Underworld for reasons of only having an effect in the Underworld.
                  Rationale(s): "Can drink from the Rivers or eat ofrendas to get Essence" really doesn't have any business staying a Numen in post-GMC where ghosts no longer naturally accrue Numina and have a limited number by Rank. The mechanics of the Numen are functionally similar to spirits' Essence regeneration, which is an innate quality, so it's only fitting that this also be an innate quality.

                                  Oh, just remembered an important one:

                                  Hack: Ghosts that have anchors can still enter the Underworld.
                                  Rationale: Anchors being changed to Influences makes losing them less of an irreversible threshold.

                                  And as more of a note/supposition than an actual definite change:

                                  Hack: Ghosts in the Underworld can change some of their Influence dots as a function of giving up memories in assorted chthonic transactions (e.g. ferrymen bribes, Old Laws, etc.) A ghost who replaces all of their Influences with things exclusive to the Underworld can no longer stave off Essence bleed in the mortal world save through rituals drawing on their personal identity in general or plot-device mechanisms such as unique cosmological confluences or special dispensation from the Kerberoi; none of their Influences work as Anchors.
                                  Rationale(s): Feeds into the notion that ghosts in the Underworld are changed by the place, draws a sharp delineation between ghosts who have completely assimilated into Underworld cultures (and the Kerberoi) and those who still meddle in the mortal realm.

                                          Hack: Ghosts that have "active" (Conditioned to Open or beyond) anchors can still enter the Underworld, but cannot cross the Rivers without losing (here defined as at least dropping it back to Anchor) at least one per River to be crossed (or all of them, whichever is lower).

                                                  I note that:

                                                  1) Avernian Gates still largely open in circumstances that are little better than chance for most ghosts.

                                                  2) Most ghosts only have one or two Anchors in the first place, which may be destroyed by simple entropy if they make a jaunt down into the Autochthonous Depths and don't get out before the door shuts.

                                                  3) Not all ghosts' Anchors represent unfinished business, and the GMC mechanics in general seem to be moving toward an assumption that ghosts are a little more durable existence-wise than earlier mechanics indicated.

                                                  4) The Autochthonous Depths are many things, but I am having some difficulty picturing them as a place the average spook would relish being trapped for an indefinite period of time, anchors or none.

                                                  5) The Underworld being a place where ghosts shed their attachments to the mortal world, combined with the earlier hack's stipulation that only naturalized ghosts pick up Consume Offering, means ghosts that still have Anchors are severely curtailed in their ability to regain Essence along with their broad-scale mobility unless time and tide erode their grip.

                                                  6) Avernian Gate is now a ghost-accessible Manifestation Effect and our one example so far who has it is not noted as having changed much.

                                                  7) Non-compulsionary ghost-summoning necessarily generates the Anchor Condition, which, while only giving the ghost the option of answering the summons, still works for ghosts in the Underworld, which rather puts the lie to "if you have Anchors at all you can't enter the Underworld" in the new context of Anchors (and the existing summoning mechanics only work on ghosts in the Autochthonous Depths from the Autochthonous Depths).

                                                  8) A keystone memento is specifically mentioned as a means of "safeguarding against the destruction of the final anchor" and geister are the most mobile things in the Underworld and its surrounding environs.

                                                  9) The hack is "you can't cross the Rivers without losing your Anchors," not "crossing the Rivers loses you your Anchors."

                                                          Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post
                                                          Are any of the Sin-Eater's inherent abilities in need of hacks? Stuff like Death Stains, infusing ghosts with plasm, etc.
                                                          Opening Avernian Gates needs the modifiers from Synergy adjusted, resurrection needs clarified and the Synergy costs given a look-over, and plasmic infusion can afford a more elegant solution than "treat them as though they had an Intelligence equal to their Power," among other things. A quick particular or two:

                                                          Hack: Sin-Eaters can automatically detect any place that has held the Resonant [Death] Condition within the last decade as a function of their ability to sense Death Stains.
                                                          Rationale(s): The penalty goes up by one for every year since the death, the dicepool for analysis is relatively small, and while the wording is vague a case can be made for "penalty greater than -10 stops the power working" being the operative precedent.

                                                          Hack: Sin-Eaters can specifically detect Avernian Gates through their morbid senses.
                                                          Rationale(s): Gives the system of marking Avernian Gates a little more nuance; Sin-Eaters are more likely to bother to mark a gate to indicate particulars about the region of the Depths it opens up into, like warning signs; more general "deadlands found here" signs are thus more likely the work of the more plentiful mortal occultists.
