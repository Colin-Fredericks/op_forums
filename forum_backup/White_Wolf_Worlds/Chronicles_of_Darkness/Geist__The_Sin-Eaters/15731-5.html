    Session LXI

    Doc’ Log

    Doc’s Log: 1645 Hours, Monday, August 2, 2010

    I was up a good bit of the night and into the morning, so we all slept in today. We wanted to make sure when we got to the Ivory Coast it would be sufficiently dark to help hide our movements. I got up around 1300 Hours and went to meditate. I knew I would need everything balanced before I returned to live combat. There have been easier things to do than will myself into a state of calm knowing I’m about to lead five of my closest friends into a death trap.

    An hour and a half later, I walked out of the Meditation Room centered, but not at peace. I met the others as they were trying to prepare. It struck me how much like a military unit we’d become. Ronin got everyone a black Camelbak. They looked suspiciously like our hydration units in Iraq, but maybe REI sells the same models now. Nightshade had her knife and was loading her pistol. Ronin was already locked and loaded. Snowflake reminded me we had some magical prep work to do. Cupcake looked as afraid as any first time soldier about to be deployed, but she tried to help with anything she could. I hated myself at that moment.

    Just then Rose came in and I pulled her into the Drawing Room to talk privately. I knew that the only time I would be able to talk with her about my concerns was while I was calm. This was the only shot. I explained that I don’t doubt her abilities at all and I didn’t want to sound like I was in any way condemning her. I remained as even toned as when I had to amputate. Describing to her how I knew her sport was predominantly a solitary endeavor, I then gave her examples of how we may need her as a team. I didn’t want to wear the sports analogy thin, so I switched to more military terminology. With her going off on “individual missions,” the rest of us were weaker. I told her that where we were going, her skills were going to be greatly needed. But she needed to remember the Krewe she left behind before she moved ahead. I watched as realization settled over her ashen features. She thanked me for explaining it to her this way. I simply nodded and told her that I wanted us all to come home as close to the way we left as possible. Then I gave her a hug. She didn’t pull back or act shocked. I think she realized how afraid I am for everyone. After that, we joined the others back in the entry hall.

    Nightshade had something to show the girls when I was leaving with Snowflake to cast our magic. Snowflake talked to me about what he had enhanced with his own magic. Given our extra equipment and the fact that most of us suck at being quiet, being lucky while sneaking seemed like the best option. I was glad he was there to help me decide. The only things in my head were bullet wounds, explosions, and death. When we finished and joined the others, all the girls except Nightshade looked like they might be sick. I decided it was better I didn’t ask. Karen was keeping Bella Donna away while we left, but when I saw her last she was a nervous wreck. Sometimes I really wonder how she can manage with all that she’s forced to deal with. My respect for her grows even faster than my pity.

    With Tavia and Chandra tasked with keeping the House “safe,” we made our final equipment check. Camelbaks and bulletproof vests for all, then any individual weaponry. Ronin also had a GPS. I had my Mossberg fully loaded and packed another 30 rounds in my fatigues. My survival knife was strapped to my leg and I was ready. Now we’re going to storm the Cote d'Ivoire.


    Doc’s Log: 0100 Hours, Tuesday, August 3, 2010

    I haven’t felt this kind of anguish and rage since I died. I want to have that fully loaded M60 again and just unload it into the patrols and guards. I don’t even care that they are kids. What these people are doing … I have to focus or what I’ll do will be just as bad as they are.

    We moved to the House Gate and I started by writing the name of the village on the stone wall. I kept writing the arcane symbols around the inside of the Gate until after about half an hour my Sepulchre Gateway finally opened. I warned everyone that we had to stay on the path. Anyone that got off would be lost in the Underworld. That made everyone very nervous. Then we joined hands and all walked in. I’d never actually used the ceremony before so it was a little surreal to move through this bizarrely silent tunnel through the Underworld. The bones making up the path did not help that sensation anymore than the three or four ghosts that stared in from the edge of the tunnel as we continued. We moved along our osseous road for twenty minutes before we came to another Avernian Gate. I looked back at everyone to make sure they were ready. Fearful nods were my only response.

    We emerged into complete darkness and were hit with a smell that made my soul ill and my blood boil. Rotten flesh. The air was thick with that putred scent. I tried to keep everyone calm as I knew this would not end well. Surprisingly, it was Ronin that puked until she was dry heaving. I moved to the sound and held her doubled over form until she could manage to stand. There were no jokes this time. I needed her to know it was okay and she would be all right. Of course she was pissed off, but not at me at least. I told everyone that we were in a mass grave and that we were going to have to find a way out. After everyone was as prepared as I could make them, I let the heat from my mounting anger ignite into the Pyre-Flame Shroud. The white-hot ghost flames wrapped around me and formed full tactical armor. But more importantly, my protection also lit our surroundings. The bodies were piled up on all sides and above us. The ravine was filled and the Gate we exited was merely a flat spot in the earth. I could feel my compassion singing from the heat of my own rage. We noticed moonlight coming from one corner. Before I could make a move, Cupcake drew her cleaver and said she’d get us out. I started to ask if she was sure, but she cut me off, held up her weapon, and told me again that she’d get us out. I stepped back and told the others to turn around. The skill that Cupcake had with hacking the bodies apart was appalling and I’m not entirely sure she hadn’t been taken over by The Cracked Cook. Relentlessly, she chopped through limbs and torsos, steadily opening an escape route from that disgraceful tomb.

    When we could finally climb the hill out, we found ourselves standing on the edge of a camp. While Cupcake wiped away as much of the gore as she could, we looked around. There were two buildings surrounded by trees. The camp looked abandoned, but given the number of bodies in the ravine, that wasn’t surprising. We heard soft whimpering in one of the buildings so Cupcake, Ronin, and Rose went to investigate. They found two little girls, the oldest might have been 16, the younger closer to 10, huddled against the far wall. They were wearing only dirty shirts and were obviously terrified. Just before they entered, I noticed Rose stopped short and they all backed away. The door had been booby trapped with a grenade. Since Cupcake could speak the language, we had her call into the building and tell the girls to turn away from the door. Then I sent Rose to flow into the room and talked her through disarming the cowardly bomb. When she opened the door, Rose held the grenade uncomfortable in her hands. I took it and clipped it to my Camelbak strap. I knew just how to use this and had no problems with doing so at this point.

    Through Cupcake, we talked to the girls and learned that those that did this were not from a familiar tribe and didn’t speak French. The uniforms they described suggested Liberian Mercs. I knew nothing good would come from here out. I had Cupcake explain that I was a doctor and wanted to make sure they were okay. I sent Snowflake away, kept Cupcake near, and had Nightshade stand behind me. By this point, it was obvious the girls had been savagely raped and Nightshade easily made it known with her very presence that no man would be able to harm these girls while she was there. This put me at the mercy of the strong women and made me less of a threat. Both girls were bruised and dehydrated but were physically intact otherwise. I gave them each some water from my pack; I could make no claim on their mental state, but through Cupcake I assured them we would make those responsible pay. Nightshade was so enraged when I stood again, she trembled. She looked straight through me and mumbled about all the zombies she could raise from the ravine. Her broken phrases were filled with half formed threats of killing anyone that stood against us. I told her that wasn’t helping, but I doubt I sounded convinced myself.

    Snowflake scouted the grounds surrounding our position and found the other building had a few supplies but it was also booby trapped. Since no one was inside and I still had my Shroud up, I moved to the door and disarmed it. That’s two grenades for me. Inside we found two AK-47s and three full clips. Ronin seized both assault rifles, threw one strap across the opposite shoulder each, pocketed the clips and then turned around. I have seen Ronin happy, grumpy, and just plain pissed off...she’s always fairly intimidating. But nothing has ever been as frightening as the look of unwavering cold she had right then. There was also some fresh clothes and half melted chocolate bars which we gave to the girls. We could all hear helos in the air but none seemed to be getting any closer. Using the GPS and my study of the maps, we determined the camp was roughly three miles south of Jean’s village. That gave us all the intel we needed right then, so we explained that we were moving to the girls and told them they had to be very quiet. There was no way we were leaving them behind even though they would endanger us even more.

    We moved about 20 minutes north through the jungle when we found the first tripwire. I disarmed it after Cupcake calmed the girls again and added a third grenade to my makeshift bandolier. After a few more minutes, we stopped again. This time due to voices speaking heavily accented English. Everyone looked to me for orders, so I sent in Rose. She looked at me with appreciation and I knew she was thinking about our talk from earlier. We couldn’t use the ear curls for fear of being picked up on enemy communications, so it was radio silence. Normally I would have been really worried and impatient for Rose to get back. But here in the jungles of Africa, I knew that silence was our shepherd. Rose eventually returned with perfect intel. Ten mercs, children really. She estimated them to be aged 10 to 18. The leader seemed to be the oldest and he also had a strap of hanging grenades. The others were all heavily armed with pistols and more AKs. They moved back and forth, which I explained was part of a perimeter patrol. Apparently they radioed in over walkie talkie every 2 minutes. The person they spoke to on the other end had a French accent. They seemed to become much more tense when they neared the highway to the west and stayed hidden. I thought it would be best to get a more complete view of the area, so we activated Snowflake to scout the greater target zone. It became very apparent that we had come at a time of a standoff. The forces in the village and all points south were apparently government and mercenary troops. Snowflake reported between 30 and 50 soldiers while they kept roughly 100 villagers as prisoners. They were separating people by gender in the hotel. It seemed to Snowflake they were keeping potential “soldiers” (ie children) with the women. The men were less likely to survive at all. There was a definite tension between the leaders and the soldiers in the south as if they were expecting two different things. We pieced enough together to determine they were looking to move. The several buses which were little more than exploded piles of scrap helped in that regard. While he couldn’t see all of the forces to the north, he could easily determine they had many more men fielded versus the superior armaments of the south. The soldiers to the north were on guard, but far less tense. The lesser number of machine guns did not diminish their capability given they outnumbered their targets. Snowflake also pointed out a Jeep driving through the mostly untraveled village. It was a patrol of southern forces and three of the riders carried RPGs.

    After processing all this information, I took a moment to think what we could do. As I crouched in the jungle, I heard the sounds of birds over the distant helos. Glancing over, the girls seemed terrified of every noise around them. And then I understood what we had to do. Mercs and governments forces alike were tribal and already very tense. In general, tribal people have a tendency to be superstitious. Yes that’s horribly stereotypical of me. I’ll be sure to ignore it the next time someone addresses me as “Hey, Mexican” as punishment. Anyway, we figured out how most of us could cause the most psychological disturbances to the morale of the soldiers while others helped secure the hostages. First we need to cross the highway to the west and hide the girls in the jungle there. Once we confirm they’ll be safe, I will call the birds into the village to suddenly act very strangely. If a few soldiers suddenly start bleeding from their orifices, Ronin and I will be pleased. Snowflake can cause all sorts of illusions to terrify and break the ranks. Cupcake can control and maybe even explode several of their vehicles. Ronin can focus her rage to break their will through fear. Nightshade can raise some of the bodies Snowflake found outside the village as zombies and I just won’t ask what they will do. Plus she can use her own magic to call in a sudden fog to help cover our movements as well as further spook the soldiers.

    I won’t jinx us and say we’re golden, but I do feel more comfortable with our 50 to 6 odds right now. We’ll see how upbeat I am once this is over. We’ll also see if Nightshade and I come home with new undying servants.

    Now it’s time to move.


    Jasmine’s Diary

    Monday, August 2, 2010, Afternoon

    We are about the head to the Ivory Coast. We spent most of the day resting. I’m quite anxious about it, because I know we might encounter some terrible things of a wholly mortal nature, and that upsets me. Doc’s warnings about the horrors of war have certainly had an impact.

    I’ve packed bottled water, granola bars and a few other things for the trip to Africa, though when I saw the preparations others were making, I felt as though I wasn’t prepared enough. Doc provided us all with kevlar vests. At first I wasn’t going to accept one, but it didn’t seem to get in the way too much, so I decided to wear it after all.

    Natalie cornered the women in the krewe privately and offered us a sort of…rape deterrent device, as well as morning-after pills. She’s quite worried that if things go poorly, the women in the group might be raped, and sadly, she’s probably right to be concerned. But I couldn’t really accept what she offered us. Well, I took the pills, but not the…other thing. Unless I’m unconscious or completely drained of plasm, I’m certain I can escape any would-be rapists. Gwen and Alexia also took the pills, just in case. The experience rattled me a bit, not just because of what Natalie offered us, but because it made the dangers we might be facing much more real.


    Monday, August 2, 2010, Late Night

    We are in the Ivory Coast, and have just laid plans for a massive rescue which we hope will free Jean’s parents and some of the other captive innocents.

    We traveled through Doc’s sepulchral gateway and arrived in a charnel pit that held the rotting bodies of a fifty or so dead. Emerging into such a horrifying place brought the reality of what we were facing crashing down on us. We all reacted differently. Simon remained somewhat detached, as always, and Doc very much became the soldier. Natalie became uncharacteristically quiet. Once Gwen got over being sick at the sight and stench, she was all business, and I think Alexia withdrew and let the Cracked Cook take over. As for me, I could almost feel something shift inside me. All the anxiety I had been feeling before we arrived evaporated, only to be replaced with a cold, steely determination that, now that I reflect on it, is a bit frightening. Once this is all over, I may very well have a breakdown, but for now I’m focused.

    Alexia moved some of the bodies and made a way out of the charnel pit. The pit lay inside a small village. It was abandoned, probably because everyone had been killed by the rapacious mercenary forces we soon
    learned were operating in the area.

    Actually, it wasn’t completely deserted. There were two girls, about ten and sixteen, trapped inside a small shack. We heard them whimpering from outside, and I quietly crept up to have a look in through the hovel’s single, small window. The place was rigged with a grenade, which would go off if the door was opened. Alexia talked to the girls from outside the shack, telling them to shield their eyes, while I phased through the wall and, with Doc’s coaching, disarmed the makeshift bomb.

    The girls were starving and dehydrated. They had been beaten, raped, and left as bait for a cruel trap. The people who did this are monsters, mercenaries who work for the corrupt government forces. Natalie seethed with barely contained anger, and kept muttering to herself about raising a zombie army to slaughter every last soldier in the country. After what we’ve seen, I can understand her feelings, but it was still a bit scary. I wonder if we didn’t made a mistake bringing her. Her post-traumatic stress could make her do something unwise, and we can’t afford any blunders here.

    We gave the girls water and food, and took them with us as we left the village and moved north toward the nearest town, where Jean’s family are. They believe we are U.N. agents. I’m not sure what we will do with them when we enter the town, but we can’t take them into that war zone with us.

    Using night’s darkness for cover, we moved through a wooded area until we heard a group moving ahead of us. Doc sent me forward to investigate, and I crept close enough to see a patrol of about ten people, mostly well-armed adolescents. Their disarrayed uniforms marked them as mercenaries. They didn’t seem to want to move past a nearby highway. When I returned to the others, Simon dropped into a boneyard trance to have a look at our surroundings and the nearby town, part of which was just inside his range.
    The opposition forces are to the north, while the government and mercenary forces are to the south. The government forces seem to be preparing for a withdrawal, and helicopters provide constant air cover for both sides (we can hear them quite clearly). The opposition forces hold the hospital, and are tending to wounded with the help of U.N.-affiliated doctors. The government forces, however, are better-armed. Doc believes they are withdrawing for strategic reasons, possibly because more U.N. support is on the way.

    Unfortunately, they have a couple hundred people, including Jean’s family, held captive in a hotel. There are about forty or fifty mercenary soldiers holding the hotel, and they were separating the men from the women and children, moving each to different floors. Gwen, Doc and Natalie suspect that they are waiting for trucks to take the women and children away as slaves and future soldiers, and they will probably kill all the men. They must be stopped.

    We spent the next half-hour deciding what to do. We decided to unleash the force of our supernatural abilities to cause a panic among the superstitious mercenary soldiers as a distraction. Natalie is going to create a few zombies after all. Even though the zombies won’t stand up to armed men with machine guns, they should frighten and demoralize the mercenaries. She also plans to use her hedge witch magic to summon a spooky mist throughout the town. Meanwhile, Simon will use ghostly illusions to keep the soldiers off-balance and scared, while Doc will call upon the birds using the Primeval Marionette, bringing flocks of them into the town as a bad omen. Alexia will take control of several military machines, possibly even a helicopter, and use them against the mercenary troops.

    Under the cover of those distractions, those of us who can will attempt to infiltrate the hotel. Gwen and I are responsible for that part of the operation, and my job will be to find Jean’s family while neutralizing any guards I encounter. The prospect of fighting and possibly killing other living people instead of supernatural horrors or spirits is unsettling, but I’m focused, and will do what I must to ensure the safety of Jean’s family.

    Team Player

    Doc spoke to me in private as we were making final preparations. We had a long talk about my tendency to operate independently—things like going alone into the Underworld to investigate the chapel or chasing after Black Shuck on my own as a flock of birds.

    Doc expressed his confidence in my abilities, but he also said that I need to be more aware of the krewe as a team. I’m a part of that team, and regardless of my faith in my own abilities, I need to remember that if I go off to take care of something on my own, I’m leaving the rest of the team “in the lurch” as they say. I realized he was right when he pointed out that my sport, swimming, is largely independent. I rely on myself and no one else…except for relays, of course, but I almost always swam in solitary events. I don’t really have much experience with team sports, and I’ve spent much of my life on my own, until I met Richard, of course. I suppose I hadn’t realized how much that has affected the decisions I make.

    I understand better now why Gwen has been so upset with me recently. I’m thankful we have someone like Doc who is observant and wise and can not only see these things, but also knows how to talk to us in ways we can accept and understand. I mean no offense to Gwen, but she simply doesn’t have that talent, not when she’s angry, anyway.

        Session LXII

        Doc’s Log

        Doc’s Log: 0400 Hours, Tuesday, August 3, 2010

        I don’t know what’s causing the most trouble here. Either we are more unskilled than I think or this mercs are special forces. At least I’m only bruised from all the bullets...for now.

        I summoned the birds of the area and gave them their orders just as Nightshade’s fog rolled into town. The birds and fog together should have unnerved most of those mercs that were pressed into service. At least, that was the plan. If they were rattled, they damn sure didn’t show it. I managed to sneak into the village, dodging patrols and remaining behind cover without any problems. My intention was to wait until we were ready to move and then sow more horror and/or pick off an patrols I could while the others prepared the hotel for our attack. Before we were ready, I heard an explosion coming from the direction Ronin and Rose headed. I didn’t give my position away, but I did find one of the Jeep patrols and they were on heightened alert. They must have spotted Ronin as I doubt anyone could have seen Rose. I cut my hand open and threw the blood droplets in the direction of the driver of the fading Jeep. Through the fog, I heard some screaming and then the Jeep stopped. That at least worked, so I moved in. When I was close enough to see my targets, I noticed the driver trying to stop the bleeding while the others had their weapons drawn and were searching. Before I could do much else, Rose appeared from the fog and started screaming at them to attack her. That definitely got their attention, but I shot one before they could level one of my Krewe. Another started bleeding and I knew Ronin was around. Unfortunately, I also heard a chopper coming. One of the other soldiers threw a grenade into the building while the others turned their guns on me. I guess Ronin was hiding in the building. I shot the one throwing grenades to keep him from catching Ronin without enough cover. Before I was completely mowed down, Rose turned into this massive wave and crashed into the two remaining soldiers. They were pretty beaten up from getting hit with that much force and bouncing around the concrete and Jeep. But not enough that they didn’t unload their machine guns on Rose. I shot the remaining guy holding the RPG, which was still loaded when he fell. Unfortunately, we were not quiet and before I could really do anything but turn around, two more Jeeps pulled up behind me. They each had 3 machine gunners, 1 RPG soldier, plus the drivers. They spread out when they realized I had my own grenades, but that wasn’t helping me any. A third Jeep with the same personnel reinforced them as two helos moved into position between the buildings above me. I knew this was not going to end well. Rose screamed for me to run just before she crashed into as many of the soldiers as she could with another of her giant tidal waves. One of the helos opened up on the remaining guns trained on me and I realized at that moment that Cupcake and Snowflake were in position. One of the mercs fired his RPG at the chopper while three more machine guns opened up on Rose. She didn’t seem to even register the bullets, though I couldn’t tell for sure. There was still several machine guns pointed at me. When the shit went down, I dove for the shop window across the street. Between the bullets, the shattering glass, and the rubble already scattered across the cement flooring, I was less than happy. I Bulwarked most of the damage, but I was grateful for the vest. I finally managed to put up my Primeval Shroud and crawled to what would have to work for cover. Someone used our makeshift click radios to let me know they were circling the building. Great. I heard the click code to hold as I was looking for a way out. I guess Nightshade’s Zombie Horde was almost in position. I had just run out the back and down the side alley across the street when the government forces ringed my last position. The grenades and machine gun fire told me I got out just in time.

        We regrouped to let Rose heal up a little. Apparently she fakes being okay better than I thought. Ronin watched over her while she soaked in a tub for just short of two hours. She could heal up that way and made me feel less worthless as I was out of Plasm again. Snowflake watched over the forces and gave us a better idea of troop movements. 36 inside the hotel, 9 outside, and all heavily armed. This is fabulous. I was waiting for the Zombie attack to start before I moved in, but before anything could start, the two helicopters moved over to the hotel and that was unexpected. What was a little weird was that they weren’t flying normally, like someone either didn’t know how to fly or was having real trouble with it.

        I knew just when the Zombies moved in because the screams from the hotel preceded three waves of gunfire. I was impressed they were disciplined enough to fire together like that. The Zombies were still up when I heard the chorus of firing pin clicks. I came out of my building and noticed one of the choppers had drifted away from the hotel. The other was still seeming to have trouble but remained over the hotel. I decided to chance it and moved to engage. Snowflake informed me that there were 11 men with grenades in the hotel waiting for the Zombies.                 Video Games

                While Alexia and I were in the video game store, I really looked at the assortment of games for sale, and was surprised by some of what I saw. Of course there are plenty of unnecessarily violent games out there, and after our recent excursion, I find it hard to imagine anyone enjoying the simulated experience of bloody combat. Or so I thought at first until I really examined my own thoughts and realized that there is a certain thrill to it that might be appealing to some were real lives not at stake.

                But what really annoyed me were all the sports games. There are games for tennis, for football (real football, not American football, though they had games for that too), for track and field, practically anything you can imagine.

                I can understand the appeal of sports games, but if a person wants to enjoy sports, then why don’t they just go out and play them for real? It’s more fulfilling and much healthier. There are plenty of facilities around to enjoy such sports for a reasonable fee, certainly no more expensive than buying all these games. If the games were more abstract, like they were when I was younger, I would understand the appeal because then they really are games, and not simulations. But moderns sports games seem to be trying to make the experience as realistic as possible, and if that’s the case, you might as well play for real.

                Alexia argued that some people didn’t have the facilities or talent to play sports for real, but I don’t think that’s a good excuse. I suppose it could be disheartening to lack skill in a team sport when you are playing with others much better than you. Video games might provide a form of false empowerment to make up for that. And Alexia is right that not everyone has a team to play with or against. But I still think there are enough opportunities out there that most people could find a way to play real sports instead of wasting their energies on a video game.

I often feel like I’m one with the sea, and I could share that part of myself with Karl in a way no one else could ever fathom. He knows what the depths mean to people like us. He understands their comforting embrace.

I told him about our terrible experience in the Ivory Coast. I leaned against him, and he put an arm around my shoulders. It felt nice.

I don’t really know what has happened between them in the past, but given Rose’s difficulty in relating the “conversation,” it sounded littered with profanity and threats.                                                         It ended up that everyone felt like I should sleep, so I woke up just before dinner. I felt better too.

                                                        Everyone came to dinner and it was pretty normal. Well except that we didn’t talk about “business” with Leslie there. We apparently don’t want her knowing exactly what we are, so there was a gag order on dinner. Once that was finished, we decided to go to Murder Ballads. Even Tavia went this time, though she wore this giant black Elvira wig. Rose stayed home as she hates clubbing but also Karl was supposed to return. That was a much better reason to stay home so we didn’t give her a hard time.
                                                        It was busy like usual on a Saturday night, but we saw several people we knew. I spotted Neon and Rebecca right off. Then I saw Sonia light up when she saw Nightshade come in. I tried to ignore her and have good time anyway. Snowflake and I noticed with a grin that Pilot Light was showing around a friend. And though Karen didn’t seem pleased by it, I noticed a few of Nightshade’s groupies from the party as well. Since we had new people with us and it was something fun, Nightshade showed us around the VIP rooms under the stage. I knew she had a VIP pass but I’m not sure I even knew there was anything under the stage, much less a set of sound dampened lounge rooms. After that, everyone started splitting off. Bright Eyes was taking it all in, so I gave her a bit of advice. I was happy to introduce her to my vampiric friend, but I wanted to her to know how bad of an idea it might be to get involved with either Rebecca or Neon. That love square is not worth the issues, plus I don’t want Truman to have to deal with more internal trouble. Once she was made aware, Bright Eyes and I went over and talked with the Carthians. I mentioned seeing the Sheriff at Alchemi and Rebecca explained that he did indeed make it a point to hustle on the weekends. This could be invaluable information the next Boys’ Night Snowflake and I go on. Bright Eyes seemed much more interested in Rebecca, even if just for casual conversation so when we all moved to dance, I asked Neon. I also felt like it was a safer bet. Eventually we moved our way through other parts of the club, parting on good terms and making more friends for Bright Eyes. Then Nightshade and Karen joined us and we talked and flirted a bit. Then Nightshade and Bright Eyes decided to dance together in the cage. That stopped quite a few people in their tracks. I had just enough brain working to grab my phone, which Nightshade insisted have a camera in it, and take a picture. Karen and I agreed that was probably the hottest thing we’ve ever seen in Murder Ballads.

                                                        When they were finished and we could all blink again, I walked over to actually speak to Leslie. She seemed at least moderately happy to see me, given we aren’t close. But with the reception she’s received already, I thought it might be good to have a positive face in the crowd. Also I wanted to make sure she was still willing to work in the shop. That seems like it won’t be a problem once we get her class schedule. I should talk to Trinh and Elsie as well. We talked about her summer and random pleasant things until Pilot Light and his friend came over. It was very obvious who he was there to see so I made myself scarce. While I doubted that Leslie would have much interest in him, I also knew that Pilot Light would get nowhere with her she didn’t want to go already. John, surprisingly Elsie’s date and Pilot Light’s friend, looked bored out of his mind until Bright Eyes came over and started talking to him. Bonnie Lynn would have been so proud to know he was very upfront with Bright Eyes about having a girlfriend. Somewhere in the night, Ronin just groaned and has no idea why.

                                                        We got back to the Bluffs around 0100 Hours and most of the couples split off. Karen and Nightshade were talking about the jacuzzi so I knew I was not needed. That left Bright Eyes and I to our own devices. Before I could really think about my situation too hard, Rose came in and told us that Karl was home before she went to bed. She was obviously tired given the time, but she seemed so happy. It happens so seldom that it stands out. I went to talk but he told me he needed to speak with Snowflake and Tavia about a private matter. I let them know I’d be around if they needed me. Not sure what’s up with that, but it can’t be good.

                                                        They let me know that later today we are going back to Glenwood so I decided to hit the Library. I realize that Snowflake and even Tavia probably already know how to deal with all this, but I don’t really know anything about handling Abyssal taint. When I came down to the main research table, I found a large box filled with bars of soap. I have no idea why it’s in the Library or on the big table. Setting it down and out of the way, I got to work. After about two and half hours, I had a better idea of what might have caused the taint. Based on the research, there could be a gateway to the Abyssal Plane opening, which would be catastrophic, but the signs aren’t really there for that. What seems more likely, if equally terrifying, is that someone or something has returned from the other side. I assume that things not of that plane eventually have to return, given the reading. The problem is that anything that returns from that terrible place isn’t right anymore. And that includes far more insidious changes than simple insanity unfortunately. But because of these changes, whatever it is no longer belongs in this plane either. If this is the case in Glenwood, then the taint is coming from the being or object “trailing” energy from the Abyss. To cleanse the taint, we’ll first have to find the source. And after that, I hope Snowflake has a plan. I don’t mean that to sound lazy, I mean I can find no way to remove Abyssal taint or deal with energy from the Abyssal plane.

                                                        And with that cheery thought echoing through my skull, I’m going to go lay down. Tomorrow will be a big day.

This almost never happens so I was shocked. But in my calmed state, I silently nodded and left her to her own journey.

This sounded like a terrible idea for Tavia and then she explained why. When Tavia was still living in Cold Harbor, Henri left subtle hints that he was interested in her in more than just a citizen or cousin. Also the mayor has never hidden his displeasure that the Fishborns of Dark Haven Bluffs were separate. He felt they thought they were better than their family. Snowflake seemed to silently agree with this assessment, but did not comment. Tavia believed that Henri wanted to take her as his own and either through this or in addition to it, claim control of the House. Apparently even Karl was unaware of these events. Snowflake was not silent in his opposition to either of these options and Karl immediately asked if Henri had ever acted inappropriately toward her. While seeing Karl’s posture nearly ripple into a protective and angry stance was intimidating, I had the same thought. Brineborn or no, no one would be claiming Tavia against her will while I was breathing. Tavia quickly assured Karl that nothing had happened, but woman’s intuition had previously told her all she needed to know. Karl informed us, possibly noticing my similar shift in posture, that Henri was taller and wider than himself in both human and hybrid forms. That was a sobering thought. Tavia continued that she was suspicious of Tzipporah’s possible involvement, even though it had been weeks since the would-be matron travelled through Cold Harbor. We really can’t discount the possibility, but we also don’t have anything to go on. We decided that when Tavia goes back, at the very least Rose will be going along. I think we should all go, so Snowflake can take the yacht and we can initiate a Hearts and Minds operation for the people of Cold Harbor. That will weaken Henri’s powerbase and reconnect Snowflake with the people that might otherwise stand against him. We don’t want to have to fight them and if we can somehow use this to our advantage in gaining strength against Tzipporah, all the better.

I wish Karl could accompany us, but it’s not a good idea to take a brineborn like him into territory belonging to the Pure werewolves.

                                                            Doc’s Log: 1845 Hours, Sunday, August 15, 2010

                                                            I knew there were worse places than I have experienced in war. What I’ve seen and heard about in the Underworld proves that. But knowing it and witnessing something from those worse places are not the same thing. I’ll try to make this make sense by starting at the beginning.

                                                            Snowflake drove Bright Eyes, Nightshade, and myself while Cupcake rode with Rose on our merry trip to Glenwood. We (read I) were very specific in our mission perimeters. Recon and surveillance only. I was very clear that we had no idea what we were dealing with and therefore we should not in any way engage our target. I got the look from Rose, but we all agreed. That’s how I should know things are going to be far worse than what I expect.

                                                            After the hour it took to drive out, Cupcake called and told us to stop. She said there was a dead body in the window of the pawn shop we just passed. This was on Main Street so we figured that would stand out. Snowflake pulled into a parking spot next to Rose and we all got out. Sure enough, there was a body sitting in a chair, on display in the pawn shop window. It was male, dressed casually, and based on decomp, it had already been there awhile. The pawn shop was open so we went inside. None of us could see the body after we walked in. Rose and Cupcake went back out to direct as Bright Eyes tried to touch the body. They saw the body move from her actions, but Bright Eyes never felt anything. After a few minutes, the teenager working the register called out to us that the chair did not have a dead body in it. He sounded just like it was something said all the time. There is no dead body in the window. Watch your step. No pets allowed. There is no sex in the Champagne Room. We asked him about it and he sighed before telling us “the weird trick of the light” started about a week ago. That’s not good. I walked back out to check, but the body was still there, just in the floor now from Bright Eyes’ pushing. Then I noticed the body had no Death Marks, which was my first sign the kid was right. I went back in while Cupcake investigated the chair itself, which was not for sale. Bright Eyes, with Rose instructing outside, moved the body out of the window and it was gone. The kid told us we were wasting our time. It would just be back tomorrow. Cupcake learned that there was not a dead body in the chair. Next she used her object reading on the window and that’s when we hit the jackpot. The window told her we could see things because “everything is broken.” We figured out that the Outsider must have returned a week ago and the taint must have been more of a blast radius at first. The outside of the window was affected but not the inside, which meant it wasn’t completely pervasive...yet. The kid was pretty apathetic, even for a teenager. We decided we had to move a little faster. Rose found a watch that could be a charm while the kid talked to someone on the phone about us. Given the discussion, it was probably the owner but the kid was not inclined to exert himself. She bought the watch and we left. I just couldn’t get a thought out of my head. Everything is broken. It was so simple but so profoundly terrible.

                                                            Rose unlocked her Primeval senses and immediately realized we needed to go to the abandoned strip mall down the street. We didn’t want to attract attention so we parked along the street and walked through the empty parking lot. Cupcake picked the lock while we prepared. I didn’t want to overreact, so I only unlocked my Pyre-Flame Shroud for protection and so we could see without alerting anything that we were coming. Inside it looked just like any other cheap strip mall except it was deserted. The space felt big and dusty. Shelves and fixtures stood empty and the escalators were turned off. The only way to describe it was desolate, like something out of an apocalypse movie. We came to something next that was bizarre at first, then mind numbingly scary. In the center of the mall, under the large skylight were dozens of manikins standing around as if they were real people in a still image of the mall in its prime. That’s when the terror hit. It was like something had tried to recreate a normal scene after forgetting what normal actually was. The cheap Greek statue reproduction didn’t help make anything better. Cupcake object read the statue then jumped. Her face contorted in horror at first, then she shook her head like she’d been dazed. She turned to us with no emotion on her face or in her voice and said she’d seen the Outsider. She couldn’t remember anything but eyes and teeth. She thought it was her mind protecting itself from things it shouldn’t know. She then moved to one of the manikins to ask it if they could move without help. She blinked then asked why they weren’t moving already. Her eyes widened just before the mall sound system started playing muzak. She quickly backed away while telling us that they could all move, we were just still too close to the doors. Under the terrible music, I started hearing an odd scraping as things were obviously moving toward us. Nightshade grabbed my arm and almost ran for the door. I managed to stay on my feet and moved with her, shotgun ready. As we retreated, I noticed not only were the manikins moving, but there were also dead people with pipe driven into them attempting to shamble after us. The escalators turned us as we ran by, but I was not interested in waiting for what came up. I covered our flank until everyone made it back to the door. The manikins were at least as slow as zombies from the movies so even Snowflake managed without trouble. Rose determined that we needed to see the rest of the place in case the Outsider was still here. I looked at her like she was nuts but I realized she was right. I told her I was coming with her, but she reminded me she could be invisible and I’d be the only target. She had seen the dead bodies too, so she knew what was at stake. I did not like it, but I agreed. As soon as she vanished and we backed out, all the manikins turned back.

                                                            I stood at the door planning how best to destroy the building for about 15 minutes before Rose walked around the corner of the building. She said there was a Goblin Market in the basement. It was closed until Monday, so she couldn’t go in. Of course it was. What respectable Goblin Market opens on the weekend? The guards showed Rose a secret passage to get by all the manikins and come in easier. Unfortunately, she also found the First Aid Station was being used to make the zombie manikins. The signs of struggle and blood meant they were bringing live bodies in; probably the local homeless. I said we should firebomb the whole place, but the rest of the Krewe looked at me like I was crazy. The consensus was that the property damage wasn’t worth it. I don’t understand that, but whatever.

                                                            We moved on to another abandoned building that Rose sniffed out on the same street. It was 1.5 stories with no signs or anything special. Looked like a few of the shops I thought about before finding my free standing building. Cupcake picked the back door and inside we went. We were immediately met with the stench of water and filth. It was obvious this use to be a druggie hangup due to all the needles on the floor. I cautioned everyone to be very careful with their footing and not to touch anything. Rose noticed something strange about the pile of trash in the floor. Bright Eyes confirmed what I suspected was a danger symbol, used by homeless people. We stuck together because splitting up was not an option here. Rose heard it first as we took our first few steps. The droning hum of some kind of machine upstairs. We started to move forward when Cupcake stated as fact that no we would have machine men instead of manikins. I stopped and looked back at her, my head filled with images of new Cybermen. When we got to the stairs, I could hear the humming too. That’s when Rose stopped short and I think she almost shivered. When she looked back to us, she let us know with a look of disgust that the sound was actually flies. The sheer number of insects needed to make that much noise … I don’t … I can’t. Nothing natural could deal with that. Snowflake recommended we go back outside and let him investigate less directly. We propped him up on a few cinderblocks and listened as he described the scene. The upstairs was just as dirty and scattered as downstairs, except for one room. We could see it from outside, but there was a heavy curtain over the window. Actually Snowflake corrected us, as that room was covered on every surface with black flies. Walls, floor, window, everything. In the center of the Room of Flies, a man sat with his legs crossed. He was also covered in flies. Because of that, Snowflake couldn’t tell anything about him, including if he was still alive. He suggested we try to communicate. I didn’t like the idea as that could lead to confrontation, but no one was physically going in so I didn’t have much to argue. Snowflake created an illusion to speak with the being. He looked up when the illusion appeared and seemed happy for the company, though he was obviously insane. He thought the illusion was just another hallucination. Snowflake learned from this man that Louisa, a member of his lodge, despaired after another member of the lodge, Stephen, sacrificed himself to banish an Abyssal creature back to its plane. His noble sacrifice was in vain thanks to Louisa using some kind of relic or artifact to summon Stephen back, her love for the man blinding her to the damnation she invited. I realize this is a little dramatic. I’m paraphrasing the words of a madman spoken to an illusion. Work with me here. Anyway, Snowflake got the address of the lodge along with a warning. Any contact with Louisa or what once was Stephen would be terribly dangerous. After learning about how this man could not go on with the eggs in his belly or the flies crawling out his eyes and his ass, Snowflake empowered his illusion to strike at the man’s mind, granting him release.

                                                            I have never believed in the idea of a mercy killing...until this afternoon.

                                                            Before we could investigate the lodge, we had to do something about the Room of Flies. We walked back down the street to the hardware store on the corner. We found several heavy duty bug bombs and duct tape. Rose and Bright Eyes used their reflexes to break the window, throw in the bombs, and seal up the hole with the duct tape without the horde of flies attacking. After a few minutes the black curtain fell away from the window. Millions of dead flies filled a room surrounding a desiccated body, a happy smile on its face.

                                                            We drove past the lodge on the way back to the Bluffs. It was a two story storefront in what might have been the nicer section of town. Other stores around it were open and everything seemed fine. There was a Closed for Renovation sign out front, but no store name.
