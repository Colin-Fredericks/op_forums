    Wraiths as Minor(ish) Template?

    01-01-2014, 11:34 PM
    I have some mechanical notes that I'm extremely uncertain on.

    I have to admit two things that are essential to deciding what's appropriate and what isn't appropriate for making this Minor Template: First, that I've read Geist but never played it, and while I'm very interested in it I don't have the sort of active experience with it that I have with other splats, so I need some help with it.

    Second, I didn't play Wraith and read a brief summary, and I'm less interested in emulating it and more interested in creating an interesting and fun way to play Ghost characters, with a lot of acknowledgement to Geist.

    With that in mind, here's a list of basic notes to describe it. Does any of it sound questionable to you guys? Do you think it's appropriate for Wraiths to have Keys & Manifestations, for instance? How should they be reconciled with the GMC Ephemeral Being rules? Eager for your help to make something fun.

    Wraith Mechanical Breakdown

    Overview

    • Wraiths use mortal Attributes, NOT Spirit Attributes
    • Wraiths utilize Ephemeral Being rules in God-Machine Chronicles
    • Wraiths can also use Numina.
    • Wraiths adopt aspects of the Sin-Eater template:
      • Thresholds, Psyche, Plasm, Keys, etc.
    • Wraiths use a modified Integrity as their Integrity Trait.
    • Wraiths may have and purchase Physical and Mental Merits, but can be restricted in what Social Merits they can buy.
    • Wraiths must consume Plasm in order to keep themselves from dissipating into the Underworld.
    • Implied connection with Geists, perhaps step in transformation from Ghost to Geist?
    Attributes & Skills

    • Wraiths follow mortal character creation rules, retaining their attributes and skills they had in life. Their advantages are also the same.
    • When they become Wraiths, ghosts regain the ability to adapt and change, and are once again able to purchase new attributes and skills, reflecting increased knowledge and a substantial alteration to their self-image.
    Ephemeral Being Rules

    • Wraiths are ephemeral ghosts and reside in Twilight by default.
    • Wraiths use Plasm in place of Essence for all required upkeep. Wraiths must spend 1 Plasm every day to remain active. Wraiths that fail to pay their Plasm upkeep are banished to the Underworld.
      • Wraiths can spend Plasm to boost their traits for a single scene on a point per dot basis, They can’t boost a trait by any more than Psyche + 2 dots; boosting takes a turn and they may only boost a single trait in a turn.
    • Wraiths are treat Rank with Psyche. (UNCERTAIN)
    • Wraiths retain their Virtue and Vice as they had them in life. They are not reversed.
    • Their Integrity is not fixed, it is slightly modified.
    • Wraiths regain 1 Willpower per day.
    • Wraiths retain their Influences and Manifestations. Additional Influences and Manifestations may be purchased. (UNCERTAIN)
    • Wraiths are their own Anchors and are the subject of their Anchor Condition, unless their Integrity is reduced to zero, or they’re sent to the Underworld. (UNCERTAIN)
    • Wraiths keep their Banes but lose their Bans. (UNCERTAIN)
    • Wraiths may purchase Numina.
    Wraiths & Sin-Eaters

    • Wraiths share many similarities with Sin-Eaters.
    • Wraiths have Thresholds. These affect their keys.
    • Wraiths gain the Psyche and Plasm advantages.
    • Wraiths have access to Keys. They begin play with the Two Keys of their Threshold, and may purchase others like Sin-Eaters.
    • Wraiths have access to Manifestations when they have successfully Materialized. They begin play with three dots of Manifestations.
    • Wraiths need Anchors and spend time in the Underworld based on Psyche?
    • Wraiths regain Plasm through Haunts, Ectophagia, Threshold Resonance and Passions. (New Mechanic?)
    Integrity

    • Functions like normal with some breaking points removed and other universal breaking points added.
    • Witnessing supernatural events is no longer a breaking point.
    • Acting to gain Plasm confers a +2 bonus on breaking point rolls.
    • Avoiding the living world for more than a week at a time is a breaking point with a zero negative modifier.
    • Wraiths with Integrity Zero are banished into the Underworld and become Spectres, empowered but utterly beholden to the resonance of their threshold. Some say that Spectres go on to become Geists.

              Going through them one by one:
              • Archetypes: “your archetype defines your relationship with and philosophy regarding death.” Mechanically, it serves as a source of Plasm, in conjunction with Virtue and Vice. My own inclination would be to replace this with one or more Passions that likewise interact with Virtue and Vice to generate Plasm. IMHO, a key feature of the most interesting ghost stories is their driven pursuit of “unfinished business”. For a wraith, this choice should be emblematic of what draws him back to the land of the living.
              • Thresholds: mechanically, these determine which Keys a Sin-Eater starts out with. It’s very logical that a ghost would have a Threshold.
              • Psyche: “a Sin-Eater with a high Psyche is a deathly being, an expression of the afterlife in the mundane world.” In addition to the standard features common to all power traits, sufficiently high Psyche grants a Sin-Eater one to three Anchors and demands that he periodically travel to the Underworld or lose Psyche. Frankly, I’m not sure how appropriate either of these are for ghosts; at the very least, a wraith would need a modified version of Psyche. That said, it’s worth noting that Unfettered Geists get a Psyche score.
              • Anchors: This means something different for Sin-Eaters than for ghosts: for a ghost, an anchor is something that tethers it to the mortal world and keeps it from being pulled into the Underworld; it also acts as a Condition for enabling the ghost’s Manifestations — more on this below. More to the point, a ghost’s Anchor is something in the material world that it can Influence.
              • Plasm: this is a physical substance out of which ghostly forms materialize. It’s appropriate for Sin-Eaters, but not so much for ghosts. It’s worth noting that both ghosts and unfettered geists are “fueled” by Essence.
              • Synergy: unless you’re implementing an Oblivion-like split personality mechanic, this isn’t appropriate for wraiths.
              • Keys: aspects of death that unlock portions of a Sin-Eater’s Manifestations. It’s worth noting that Keys work differently for Unfettered Geists, providing them with access to skills and letting them learn Numina based on Sin-Eater Manifestations. I’d be more inclined to give wraiths suites of Key-based Numina that include, but are not limited to, Geist-style Key/Manifestation pairings.
              • Manifestations: for a Sin-Eater, these are powers of death unlocked by Keys: Boneyard, Caul, Curse, Marionette, Oracle, Rage, and Shroud. For GMC-style ghosts, Manifestations are ways to interact with the material world: Twilight Form, Discorporate, Avernian Gateway, Image, Materialize, Fetter, Unfetter, Possess, and Claim. The naming issue needs to be resolved, as these are two wildly different things.

              On the GMC side of things, ghosts have Rank, Essence, simplified attributes, Virtue, Vice, Integrity, Corpus, Willpower, Initiative, Defense, Speed, Size, Bans, Banes, Influence over Anchors, Manifestations, and Numina. As Ephemeral Beings, ghosts are subject to Summoning, Exorcism, Abjuration, Warding, and Binding.
              • Rank translates reasonably well to Psyche; note that Rank 1 ghosts are assumed to be drones. I’d be inclined to say that Lucid Soul overrides this. Rank might benefit from being renamed.
              • Essence is fine as is, IMHO.
              • Agreed about using “Lucid Soul” Attributes and Skills instead of the simplified Ephemeral Attributes.
              • Virtue and Vice are reasonable names to use. I think the inversion of Virtue and Vice should be treated as something specific to regular ghosts and spectres — in fact, the inversion of Virtue and Vice would be the primary defining distinction between Wraith and Spectre.
              • I don’t think that wraiths should be defined by Integrity any more than vampires are. If you do decide to keep Integrity, don’t ditch the “exposure to the supernatural” breaking points; but consider redefining what a ghost considers to be supernatural.
              • Bans and Banes are tied to Rank (or whatever it’s called for wraiths).
              • Ghosts have an Influence rating equal to Rank that can be dedicated to one Anchor or split among several. The primary use for this is to establish the proper Conditions to Manifest; but at sufficiently high levels, the ghost can potentially do some very strange things.

              Some mechanism for determining which Manifestations a ghost has is needed. I’d be inclined to assign dot ratings to each, based loosely on what prerequisites are needed to use them. Another source to look at here would be Orpheus — specifically, its Shades: Banshee, Haunter, Poltergeist, Skinrider, Wisp, etc. Each had a distinct personality type and a preferred means of manifesting; the former could provide a wraith’s alternative to Archetype, while the latter could be adapted as a structure for Manifestations.

              Some system for assigning Numina is needed. My initial thought would be to make it Key-based.

              My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                  I'm looking at some of the systems presented in Immortals for inspiration here. I'll provide my write-up for Psyche to explain how I think it might apply to a Wraith. The chart I'm using would allow Psyche for Wraiths to be measured as a 1-5 point Power Stat which also alters the effective rank of a Wraith.

                  Also, I feel it worth mentioning that while Essence is good if it isn't well substantiated, I think Plasm is a good fit for Wraiths. For the one, it helps to further separate and make them distinct from the ephemeral ecosystem of the dead. I'm imagining Wraiths as very distinct aberrations from the rest of their Ghostly companions, which might lend itself to why they are their own Anchors, and why they have a certain physicality, allowing them to have their full traits and even physical merits. If Essence is the stuff of all ethereal beings, than Plasm might be seen as the very physical manifestation of that, and the Wraith's engagement with that very physical aspect of the world is enabled by their consumption of Plasm. Does that make sense as an explanation? If anybody has a finer understanding of Plasm, help me out so I know for certain whether this desire should be discarded.

                  On the topic of the Integrity breaking points, that's a fair point, and while I do like the idea of keeping Integrity since Ghosts generally have it, I'll happily take any suggestions on an alternative. Since it isn't necessarily a "major splat" I was thinking it'd be better to keep Integrity to minimize any confusion.

                  New Advantage: Psyche

                  The psychology of a Wraith is a complex subject, as they attempt to adapt to housing a truly human mindset in the alien form of the ethereal dead. At first there’s a disconnect between the Wraith and their existence as a ghost, but with time and contemplation, the Wraith is able to quell the dissonance within their own minds, accepting their state of undeath and the power that comes with it. Some enjoy a harmonious understanding of their nature and their mind while others wrestle a stronger connection, leveraging self over death. Regardless, this allows the Wraith to more easily channel the power of the Plasm which is an essential component of their being. With greater Psyche comes greater facility and power with Plasm, allowing the Wraith to store more of it in themselves and enhancing their abilities.

                      The rpg.net thread had suggested Identity as the Integrity replacement, using the idea of the duality between mortal and post-life identities to represent 'Mask' and 'Dirge' (replacing Virtue and Vice). With that in mind I don't see a huge problem twisting things to make Synergy (as a mixture of those Identities) the morality stat. It regulates Identity to a theme rather than a game trait but that helps provide a sounding board for the rest of the game.

                      "I was once a _______, but now I am dead. Who am I now?"

                      This ties into Geist's theme of Second Chances but with a darker, sorrowful twist. It is too late for somethings and it is hard when you lose somebody. But when you lose everyone and everything are you still you? Do you fight your way back to make good your debts or look after your loved ones? Or do you move forward, with eyes on the future, leaving behind all you knew?

                      Hindsight is said to be 20/20.

                      The dead know this better than anyone.

                      Plus, it means we have a method to bring back Spectures (in a VERY different shape) and a legit consequence for 0 Synergy: Your mortal and dead Identities are in total conflict. Some days you act as if you never died, not dissimilar from a blip. Some days you act as if you were never alive or even never born at all. And the other days you are insane, your mind and soul trying to reconcile these incompatible views of the world. Always, you fight to defend who you are.

                      Oddly enough, I think this works well with both the idea of Passions (from CWoD and M:tC) and Archetypes but since I'm not upto doing the mechanical side of things at the moment, I'll stick to fluff and concepts.
