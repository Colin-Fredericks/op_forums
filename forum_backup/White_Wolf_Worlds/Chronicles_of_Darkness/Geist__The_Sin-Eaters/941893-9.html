        Originally posted by White Oak Dragon View Post

        And it's an appropriate downside, because it ins't inherent to being a Sin-Eater(and thus something to angst over). It's because you're bad at being a Sin-Eater. The most successful sin-eaters are the ones who either get along with their geists or can find a way to vent their urges and desires into a positive direction. If you're geist hates you, you've fucked up.

        Originally posted by White Oak Dragon View Post
        Unless I missed it, there's no actual mechanics in first edition for losing Synergy by telling your geist to shut up. Unless, of course, your geist is requesting that you torture people less. Of course, if it is requesting that you do it more, then acting in accordance with the geist's wishes will cost you Synergy rather than opposing them.
        And really, that's something that should change in 2E. Synergy is supposed to be a measure of how strong the bond between you and your geist is. It should probably take your relationship with the Geist into consideration.

        At the very least, doing things that piss off your geist should be a Breaking Point or pretty much every Sin-Eater."
        Originally posted by White Oak Dragon View Post
        I'm dropping the possession thing, as that has already been confirmed to not be a part of the second edition. At this point, I will be content if the geist has teeth. That doesn't prevent a character from being able to hold a murderous geist in check. It just means that they need to be willing to endure some hardship to do so, which actually makes doing so a bit more noteworthy than if it is merely a matter of ignoring the little voice. Conditions could work nicely for that, and also act as a nice source of Beats for a Sin-Eater that is trying to make the world a better place by keeping his or her psychotic geist out of trouble.
        Having to deal with low synergy and the conditions and other downsides that comes from that, and having "finding some way to safely appease my geist" taking up one of your aspiration spots is enough in the way of teeth. It's when the Geist can literally force the Sin-Eater to act in the Geist's wishes that "keep my psychotic geist in check" stops being a viable character option.

        I'm fine with more downsides to being a Sin-Eater, as long as the deal is still ultimately one sided in the Mortals Favor, because being one sided in the mortal's favor is one of the reasons why Sin-Eaters have little reason to regret it and can just go out and have fun living life. The downsides also have to be appropriate, logically and thematically, for the game.

                                                        I don't really get where the argument that Geist is the game about not being angsty came from.                                                         On another note, I always found it odd that Geists only get an identity when you form a Krewe. I rather prefer the idea that they cobble their identity together before they even make the Bargain. It's something I houserule in my own games, but I'm curious if that might be tweaked in the second edition.
                                                        The reason why people consider it the game to not be angsty is because there's a startling lack of reasons to be angsty. You've avoided death, you're resistant to poisons and drugs in general as well as sicknesses, and you're empowered in ways most people aren't. Even the act of coming back at all is a mutually beneficial event, because you're helping a dead being feelas alive as you literally are now. Just because you're not a Celebrant doesn't mean you're not enjoying yourself in your second lease on life either. Even Mourners, people who see themselves as still dead, enjoy their new lives in a macabre way. Celebrants are simply living life to the fullest beyond what a normal person would. Besides that, in the game book it's specified that Sin Eaters regularly hold celebrations and festivals, many of their ceremonies involve alcohol/spirits or tobacco. Their culture perpetuates enjoying life as much as it does helping the dead.

                                                        Another reason Sin Eaters are a happier lot than a lot of others is because while they regularly deal with the dead, including all kinds of disturbing methods of death, they are uniquely able to completely handle these things and make them right. While they can't bring people back from the dead or fix their wounds (generally), they can help ghosts pass on peacefully, resolve last business, and take revenge in the name of the departed. They can see and hear ghosts with clarity few if any normal mortals could, they can give second chances, and if they can learn from a ghost on how to help little Suzy get her rightful inheritance then wouldn't you be happy?

                                                        Also, you kind of answered your own question regarding why fans are so adherent to the idea Geist: The Sin Eaters has little inherent angst: almost every other gameline has tons of it, due in no small part to most other supernaturals having literally no choice in the matter. In becoming a Sin Eater, you may get more than you bargained for, but at least you aren't dead. Why make that a punishment?

                                                        However, you are right; you are free to play the game however you so wish. Geists are not all saintly beings only looking out for their host's benefit, and you will not be rid of the one you have barring extreme circumstances. Ghosts know you can see them and will flock to you for it, and you can't stop seeing them. Heck, one of the adventure seeds is all about a Geist who drives his Sin Eater to suicide simply be endlessly repeating "Dem bones dem bones will walk all over you". The gameline is full of personal horror along with grotesque, and players and storytellers can indeed tell and play angsty stories with them. It's just not the default.
