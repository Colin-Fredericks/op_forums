      This was my response to discussion of what I love about Geist. I will copy and paste it for a start.


      I like that Geist has such resonance with ghost stories. Every PC is someone who has returned from death to continue living, and every PC will be a little different as to why they chose to. The Geist also makes an interesting element to the whole thing, the odd spirit who returns them from the grave. The relationship between character and spirit, more complex then the more antagonistic relationships automatic in most similar games (Wraith, Deadlands) though without the power struggle mechanics. Not bashing power struggle mechanics but it is an interesting change.

      It is also, as WW/OPP games go, less of a "you are part of the hierarchy" game. (not that I have a problem with such, mind!) The game seems focused on interactions with the dead and living, less so with other Sin-Eaters, based on their rarity and the very individual natures of their Krewes. It still is easy to have a city full of Sin-Eaters if one wishes, but it is less a part of the intrinsic game than, say, Changeling or Vampire.

      For me, the draw is you are essentially playing a living ghost. Your powers have a decidedly ghostly cast to them, and you are still drawn to haunted and morbid places. There is a lot of symbolic elements to how the powers work, such as the keys unlocking manifestations, that makes the game particularly drip with atmosphere. The powers are often ghastly or frightening without going overboard on being horrific as well, they do the theme with gusto.

      The biggest downside is that given the exponential nature of the powers, there is going to be a lot of crossover with other players. In a long running game it is difficult not to have most of your powers also possessed by one or another different PC (though not an issue with Ceremonies). Power level is also on the high side, but I run Scion this is nothing Boneyard can be a trap for an unprepared GM as well.


      Geist is a spooky little game about people who have risen from the grave and now walk with the dead. Their existence is full of ghosts and they can choose to be avengers or psychopomps or profiteers, or all three, in a game that can have moments of The Crow or Nightmare Before Christmas, or The Innocents, or Beetlejuice...possibly in the same session.

      From an investment perspective, it is also two Books, Geist: The Sin-Eaters and Book of the Dead. A Ready Made Characters book is coming down the pipe but it does not involve a large investment to start, especially given BotD is cross game useful with a bit more focus on Geist.

          The powers are really open ended with allows for some "abuse" of them if you are creative. I don't mean abuse of the minmaxing, overpowered sort. But more you can use them in really fun ways. Its flexibility is right up there with Mage, with only a fraction of the complexity.

          Something hilarious and spooky that I did in our last session involved me using Industrial Caul to absorb a fan and motor from a walk in freezer. I then regrew the fan where my character's mouth was, giving me a lethal headbutt attack. After the enemy went running, I then used the fan to increase my movement, which resulted in a scary fan headed man chasing down a rather frightened opponent while whirring and gurgling.

          We dubbed this the Mighty Fan Man.

                  Another fun situation with my Sin-Eater: My character had to break into some hospital records. (Well had to is a strong word. He is a serial killer who was researching his next victim) So in order to gain access to where he needed to go he decided he needed a distraction. So of course he decided the best course of action was to start by killing a hooker. By using cold-wind caul, I was able to sneak up on and freeze to death a random street walker. Then I used a ceremony called Mortis Mask in order to change her apparent cause of death from freezing to a drug overdose.

                  So then I took her to the hospital, raised a commotion about finding her like this. Then while the doctors were treating her my character was able to slip into the back with no difficulty. The next problem came in when the room he needed to get into was filled with nurses. So he poked around the hospital for a ghost to help him out. He didn't have to look long, as apparently this hospital was some kind of international airport of ghosts. He found the ghost of an old man desperate for attention. Then using another ceremony called Message From Beyond, I was able to have the ghost use my camera phone to transmit a creepy message to the computer terminal the nurses were working at. Thoroughly creeped out, they finally left.

                  The only thing left to do at this point was to use the computer to get at the records. But it was password protected and I was short on time. So my character used Industrial Caul to fuse his fist with the computer and access the records mentally.
