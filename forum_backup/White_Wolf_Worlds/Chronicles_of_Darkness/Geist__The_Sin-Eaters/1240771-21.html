                    Memoria requires that you be at the location of a meaningful event *or* have a ghost’s anchor. Because some Anchors are portable, and Sin-Eaters can very easily recognize them, it’s likely that such a character will want to carry around an Anchor with a “useful” scene.
                    [Note: the third dot let’s you “bottle” any memory and take it with you, but only characters who drink from the bottle gain the Memoria condition... but as this isn’t the Sin-Eaters condition, it isn’t entirely clear if they can add Actors who didn’t drink from the bottle or not.]

                    With 4 dots, the Sin-Eater can /force/ any character to participate in that memory scene /as an instant action/. The text specifically cites mortals and ghosts, and that the contesting roll is Resolve+Synergy, implying Bound and other templates can also be drawn in; and does not state any exclusions. At 5 dots, the character can furthermore change how characters in that scene acted, to completely alter the outcome (but should keep it to a coherent scene; it can’t pull of the same transformations as, say, Dream Weaving [CtL]). These illusions are ‘real’ to the actors, potentially applying damage or conditions - although lethal damage and breaking points can cause them to snap out of it.

                    So, what I think you should expect is this character pulling out an anchor, binding people to a scene, and dictating their actions - particularly as a way to resolve combat or remove obstructions. This is probably the most “broken” way this power can be used, even with a good faith reading of the rules.

                    My advice is to work out the specific details of any memory that character carries around in an Anchor - and thus deciding on its use and limits. I recommend doing this collaboratively with the player, well beforehand.
                    You may want to consider having a carried anchor for this purpose costing merit points: memento might be a good starting point (maybe with a discount, especially if it doesn’t grant a Key); Retainer or Familiar (the ghost tethered to that Anchor) might also work.
                    You will also need to rule on whether a bottled memoria can let them draw in actors or not. As I mentioned above, the text doesn’t make this very clear. If it can, they have to activate and spend all the relevant essence beforehand (and you have to rule on what limits that has during downtime, and how much plasm they can get back to use on scene) but skips over the complication of a ghost hanging around them all the time, able to easily manifest and do it’s own thing (and thus the costs for a retainer/ally).

                    Specificity will be your friend here. I don’t think you should try /preventing/ the PC from using it in this way, but you should probably avoid having an auto-win button. I don’t think it’s reasonable to restrict them to only the Memoria they encounter during the Story, with how the Haunt is written; but if you do consider that route, go out of your way to include them early on, and definitely to use them at numerous dramatic points.

                    Also, keep in mind when a Clash of Wills would apply to this (only when 2 supernatural powers can’t happen at the same time). Grant powers that would Clash to the opposition only sparingly - and make sure they get other uses relevant to the Story, beyond just resisting this power. Have an enemy bind the other PCs in their own Memoria, for example, or have one goon bound by Dominate, or a Fae use an elaborate Tale Spinning as part of their plot (although that might not be at thing in 2E).

                    In general, I think you can “get it right” by buying in to this power: let them use it to auto-win a minor encounter or two, only directly counter it with awesome powers relevant to the plot, and go out of your way for them to come across Memoria during the story (at least one early on, and probably a few more at dramatic points). But, do work out very specific details (including how many actors can be bound, how many turns it lasts, what actions occur “by default”, and how far these can be twisted) - collaborate with your player on this as much as you’re comfortable with; write drafts of these out on notecards that can be referenced or something. Providing a handout in the style of a movie script would be really awesome, actually. Then make sure those specific details are useful but limiting throughout various encounters.


                    Hope this helps; Good Luck,
                    ~ Seraph Kitty

                            Gonna just follow up with some notes to keep in mind:

                            Mystery Play builds on Dénouement and Break the Cycle puts the ability to go off-script in the hands of the target. That means, among other things, that even within the specific confines of having a portable Anchor with a memory you know at least three details of, you're still spending Plasm based on the size of the scene you're making visible to everyone, then spending more Plasm to conscript no more than five people to play parts in the scene, and if you want to mess with an Actor that the scene didn't mess with then you have to spend yet more Plasm and hope they can manage to fail one roll and succeed the other.

                            All of this pretty much guarantees the Haunt will need to be Unlocked, which means the character will either lose Willpower, limit their applications of this trick to circumstances resonant with their Keys, or deal with the gameplay loop the Doomed Condition(s) bring into play.

                            Before getting into concerns about Clashes, remember that many of the Echoes the Memories grants access to end in the subject's death, that this is a very blatant explanation for the Haunt being on the Vengeful's list of affinities, and that taking lethal damage or suffering breaking points (or crisis points) prompts rolls to break out of the Actor Condition. The Memoria is a highly conditional power most useful for detective work and leveraging the past in specific ways; if you need to scare off living people or wreck a person, it can serve that purpose too, but there are more efficient means of doing so.

                            Resident Lore-Hound
