      First Story of my Geist game revolved around an ambiguously rich and crazy old man who lived in a "Haunted" house. It turned out that the house wasn't really haunted, and that he was drawing in Mediums, Sin Eaters, and Ghosts in order to syphon off their death-energy in a plot to tl;dr effectively create a mass-driver to punch a hole into a deeper part of the Underworld. This included driving his very powerful Medium-wife insane from the constant drain. As the players were discovering what was really going on, a silent child-ghost was helping them and vaguely befriended the PCs.

      It turned out that Goldstein (the old man) is a Redeemed Promethean that frequently went on adventures in the Underworld. He got trapped in the realm he was trying to get to, and had to trade away "All humanity he'd ever create and hold" in order to escape. He thought that'd take away his ability to become Redeemed, in truth it was his last Milestone. The Deathlord came for his first-born son instead. Goldstein's thaumaturge friend saved the boy from hell by binding his Ghost to the house, but since Goldstein had to lose the child he could never know--or the Deathlord would send servants to claim his prize. So, Goldstein was trying to punch a hole into the underworld to get his son... who was the ghost the PCs befriended.

      In the end, the PCs talked Goldstein down. They knew an Abmortal, Mac, who achieved immortality by fusing with ghosts (The ghost's personality and anchors would perpetuate in Mac's body, causing it to regenerate back to full from any amount of damage). They bonded the child to Mac. Mac and Goldstein became best friends.

      Not your standard story, and decidedly a "Grey" end, but there you go. Hilariously, all the PCs still hated Goldstein, but didn't want to hurt the kid by offing him. Besides, they got to more or less keep his "haunted" mansion.

          Geist was the first game I ever played a full campaign in. I was Nan, a young Changeling (we were young and did not know to beware crossovers) attached to a krewe known as the Calaveras. She had known the leader of the krewe when they were young, and looked to him for security when she found that she could not return to her family.

          One adventure ended in personal disaster; Nan was a freshly-appointed Scarecrow Minister, and decided to test her abilities out on the krewe under the cover of them investigating the "Midnight Man" creepypasta (in short; you do a small ritual and supposedly summon a pagan god who plays hide-and-seek with you until dawn; being tagged torments you with your worst nightmares). She hunted the krewe and showed them exaggerated versions of their own deaths as illusions.

          It did not go over well. One member of the krewe took it especially hard, and Nan had adopted her as a sort of mother figure. With tears streaming down her cheeks, Nan begged for forgiveness and was rejected. She asked if she was loved, and told she was not. That Sin-Eater soon left the krewe and moved away.

          Nan was devastated. She fled through an Avernian Gate and into the shallows of the Underworld, and bound a handful of ghosts to serve as her new family. They could not refuse her, and told her that she was perfect. The krewe could not allow either this abuse of the dead nor a girl who was barely a teenager to live in the Underworld.

          The leader of the krewe got her to back down with love, giving her a grand embrace. She let the ghosts go, and the krewe returned to the surface world.

          Remi. she/her. game designer.

              My character was a lonely teen connected to two hunters. She thought that she was chatting to a young lad in the local area over the car's 2-way radio which had recently been replaced. It turned out that the kid was actually haunting the 2-way radio. She found that out when he warned her about something he couldn't possibly know otherwise. Since they'd been friends for so long, and she wasn't a Geist at that time (not until after she died a few years later), it was quite a heart-wrenching moment. She tried to hide it from the hunters but eventually they figured it out. They didn't destroy the radio, thankfully, but later on when she became a geist freed him from that radio she had to send him away ... only to find that she had become his anchor in the interim.

              I cried in real life.

                  So, in our campaign, which is currently on hiatus due to Demon being AMAZING. We managed to have a heartfelt moment just about every session. Most of them came from the local Ashwood Abbey division (NPCS). Our first real interactions with AA (Or the Alpha Betas as their fraternity was known) was when one party member was pledging with them. So, the party, was going up to a mountain with AA for various reasons, one member was pledging, one was dating a member of Ashwood Abbey, one was there so all the party was together. So, the first thing that happened was the two party members who were pledging were made to leave so that only the pledge was with the Abbey. When it was just AA and the pledge they went into the woods and we learned what they were up to [dramatic pause] hunting werewolves as part of initiation. So the pledge just shrugged it off and lead the hunt like they were supposed to and managed to track down the werewolf. And so what happened was most of Ashwood Abbey was hit with lunacy and didn't really do anything, but the leader of it still could kind of manage to do stuff. The pledge wrestled the werewolf to a standstill while the leader of Ashwood Abbey shot him in the face with silver buckshot. This happened for a couple of rounds. Werewolf would escape the grapple and then would get grappled again and take silver to the face. After this happened the werewolf managed to run away and everyone went back to the camp. Later the party regrouped and followed the werewolf, who was a teenager, back to his house. The werewolves told us to go away, and threatened to call the cops on us so we, reluctantly left. BUT, we came back later, to be the good sin-eaters that we are, and broke into their house, stole some stuff from them and left a note that, despite being as good intentioned as possible, still was probably pretty threatening. It was all really touching. There's more stories if people would like to hear them. LIke how we nuked the Hisil after hiring a werewolf blood dealer and how we helped out this really nice unbound geist.

                      *Thoughtfully wanders by, dropping a few eaves as he does; his brows lift merrily, and he looks back over the chasm of his time on the Ball o'Mud*

                      Not necessarily a scene, so much as a continued pursuit from a collection of Sin-Eaters I co-story-told for. At one point, I was an actual player in a multi-genre game, (The same one I mentioned about The Cat Lady, to those that may remember.) and I played a somewhat-completely-crazy homeless guy. (I know, everyone's favorite concept right? The freedom that goes along with such a role should never outshine the responsibilities of it either though. After all, who else is going to fight The Man, yell at lampposts, and throw other characters out/in windows during combat?) I had lost the brunt of my memory in the accident that killed my character, (A Sin Eater) and was currently on-quest to find some gosh-dang-answers. I searched for them everywhere, and eventually completed more than just a few circles, and caught the cars I had been chasing in traffic. (My character would still pray at Stop-lights, if he drew breath.)

                      But I digress: Apologies. *soft sigh*

                      As it would be, I played such an apparently memorable character, that I had several friends and allies. (And it was true; you could not find a more dependable crazy-man, than my own.) And he was actually a very die-hard advocate of helping ghosts. In the pursuit of one of those ghosts, I ended up having my character step through a door to the Underworld that I was not completely familiar with; and the character was lost. (As an important side-note: This was the period in which I volunteered to become a Storyteller: so we had to write my character off in some fashion.)

                      What was truly heart-warming, was the amount of DT submitted by our players thereafter, with folks attempting to locate my former PC; despite knowing ooc that it was more or less futile. One character in particular, (one of my precious Sin Eaters) never actually stopped searching for my PC; and made an active effort the first time he visited the Underworld to ask several ghosts if they had ever heard of him.

                      *A bow of his head, and he begins to slide without sound or reason; to stage left.*
