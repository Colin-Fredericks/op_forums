    Originally posted by Rater202 View Post
    My reading makes it very clear that Geists that are happy to just sit around are the rule, rather than the exception.

    That fits with the theme--the world is a dark and scary place, but life is worth living, you're alive, and that's worth celebrating.
    Yes, life is worth living. So much that it is worth chaining yourself to a horrific embodiment of death just for another sweet taste of it.

    Originally posted by Rater202 View Post
    The Bargain flat out does not happen if the mortal doen'st agree. this is explicit. The mortal is the one who decided that the Bargain happens.
    The Bargain also doesn't happen if the geist does not offer it. I can walk into a car dealership, sign the offered loan application, and drive away in a new car (well, some people could, at any rate), but payments still need to be made on the loan. If that scenario worked the way the Bargain is being interpreted here, then I would be able to do that, the car would be mine, and the worst that the dealership could do if I decided not to pay would be to make angry phone calls at all hours of the night. That would mean that, not only does the Bargain have fewer negative consequences than any other supernatural agreement, it also has fewer negative consequences than even ordinary mortal agreements. It makes the Bargain more akin to winning the lottery than entering into an agreement with a supposedly helpful entity. That doesn't really seem fitting in the horror genre to me.

    Originally posted by Rater202 View Post
    The Geist can not force the host to do anything. Completely refusing to bend can lower your synergy, but that's but it. The geist can yell and scream and cuss, but it can not posses you, can not take control of your body from you, can not force you to do shit, unless you're one of the Wretched. This is explicit. The Bargain is explicitly not possession. The closet thing to this is a single NPC who was completely spineless and let his giest bully him into doing what it wants.
    A Bargain should be binding to both sides of it, not just one. If lower Synergy is going to be the only downside to violating the Bargain, then lower Synergy should be a bit more problematic than just a penalty to doing Ceremonies or other ghost-related things and navigating the Underworld. If a Sin-Eater is just wanting to go about his or her everyday life as they had been before, then low Synergy is largely meaningless, at least until something goes awry and they end up dead again. As an aside, I think that Synergy should remain high if the Sin-Eater is acting in accordance with the geist's wishes, even if the geist desires horrific things. A geist of torture that makes the Bargain with a torturer shouldn't find Synergy plummeting because the two of them go on a torture spree. High Synergy with a horrific geist should be terrifying to everyone.

    Originally posted by Rater202 View Post
    Idle Hands, going by the strict rules, flat out can not take control of the Lazy Stoner by the strict rules of the game and by the themes of the game. You played the game as much darker than it's meant to be. Which is fine, it's your game you can do what you want with it, but that is not the default and should not be the default. The default should be the one game with no supernatural angsy, as this is the selling point.
    This last point is where we disagree. Sure, the rules of the game make it so that Idle Hands would have little to do besides drive the Bound insane, but I disagree that that should be the way of it, and I disagree that the selling point of Geist is that there is no supernatural angst. Yes, it has an atmosphere of celebrating life in the face of death, and that is awesome. That's not the same thing as there being a lack of downsides to one's condition. If anything, it should be embracing the good and the bad of life, accepting it all together and saying that it's worth it.

    Originally posted by Rater202 View Post
    The game makes it clear that most geists, most of all, want to be alive again.

    The game mentions one Sin-Eater keeping a serial killing geist locked up tight in her soul, and the host of the Burning Woman is explicitly stated to tell her geist to shut the fuck up whenever her geist tells her to pour gasoline over a human being and set them on fire. If the geists could force their hosts to do hat the want, then neither of these would be possible.

    All of this together makes it abundantly clear that the mortal holds all of the chips before and during the Bargain.

    You want to play a Sin-Eater who gets bullied into doing what it's geist wants? Fine, there's sup[port for that in the rules, but that is not the default assumption of the game, and your characters experience sucks far worse than the default simply because he doen'st realize that he has most of the power in the relationship.
    Yes, that character's experience sucked worse than a lot of others. That was kind of the point to the character. But the Bargain should be binding regardless of the terms. A geist that bargained for its Sin-Eater to sample a new food or experience a new pleasure when the opportunity arises should have every expectation of having the Bargain honored. As a general rule, the player gets to decide what the terms of the Bargain would have been, so it would be up to the player how much friction there is between the geist and the Sin-Eater. I think that that is an area that should be explored more, and part of that is making it so that the Bargain is binding. I'm not saying that the Sin-Eater shouldn't be able to violate the Bargain, but that there should be consequences to doing so.

                        Here's the thing: Geists do not posses their hosts. The bargain is explicitly not posses. This is stated, flat out, in the introduction. The closest thing to possesion is the host reverse possesing their geist,

                        If the geist can posses you, the geist will posses you. You can't play a character like the example host of the burning woman or the woman who keeps a serial killer locked up in her soul, because the geist will just flat out posses you if you don't set your enemies on fire or go out and randomly kill people. And that sucks worse than Prometheans, because Prometheans at least have a way out of their "the world itself literally hates you" state.

                        Furthermore, and I've said this before, but it makes no sense for the Geist to be able to posses it's host. If Geists had the power to posses mortals at all, then why would they even bother with the Bargain? They would just posses any convenient mortal and ride them until they broke down. The fact that they make the Bargain instead of posssing mortals tells me that they can't posses mortals.

                        Finally, actually reading the book, the vast Majority of Sin-Eaters are not all angsty like other splats--It sucks to be a Vampire. It sucks to be a werewolf. It sucks to be a mage sometimes, it sucks to be a hunter sometimes, the fact that being a Promethean sucks is the motivating conflict of the game, it sucks to be a changeling... or at least become a changeling. It kind of sucks to be a Demon and it kind of sucks to be a Mummy. It kind of sucks to be a beast unless you're very good at finding alternative feeding methods or you're an asshole, and you can tell just from the previews that it sucks to be a Deviant. Every last one of those splats has a good reason to be all angsty about what they are.

                        Sin-Eaters do not. Sin-Eaters, actually reading the book, when they're not laying spirits to rest, banishing murderous specters, or using their powers to earn some cash, they're partying. They're celebrating. One of the Archetypes is literally all about partying. They are living life to the fullest and enjoying every last second of it. That's what Sin-Eater culture is about. If that's the culture, then it's reasonable to conclude that the vast majority of Sin-Eaters, well, if they don't like their awesome powers and the fact that it's very hard for them to die, they at the very least don't regret making the bargain.

                        And why would they? They're not dead, and there are no major drawbacks.

                        So, think of it this way: Instead of saying that the Geist should be able to posses the Sin-Eater or otherwise punish them in a way other than lower synergy(which would be major draw backs, which Sin-Eaters lacking is a feature, not a bug,) you should ask yourself this: Why, why is this alien super ghost so willing to give up their freedom to someone else, just to live again, when they have no way to make the host keep their end of the deal once it's over, other than lying to them or hoping that the host is week willed enough to bully into compliance? Is it because such a deal is literally the only way thy can feel alive again? Is it because there are rules that compel them to make deals so unbalanced in the mortal's favor? Or is it because bad things happened to the first geist who tried to force a bargain under it's terms?

Or is it because bad things happened to the first geist who tried to force a bargain under it's terms?
                                That is exactly my point. Why should a geist want to enter into a relationship like this if they're going to be giving all their power to someone with no reason to support their interests? Why should this extremely potent ghost choose to become a prisoner in someone else's head with no way to influence anything? I don't think that this exchange should be the default:

                                Geist: Okay, you're about to die. You don't have to. I'll allow you to live again, in exchange for x, y, and z.
                                Sin-Eater: Okay, agreed.
                                G: It is done. You are alive, and x is now fulfilled.
                                SE: Great. This is awesome.
                                G: Okay, now for y and z.
                                SE: Yeah, those aren't happening.
                                G: But, you made a deal...
                                SE: My promises are worth less than toilet paper, dumbass.
                                G: If you don't hold up your end of the deal, I'll make you suffer...
                                SE: How?
                                G: Well, if you die again, I might not bring you back a second time.
                                SE: Oh well. I have a second chance at life already, and thanks to these super powers you gave me, it's not going to be easy to die a second time.
                                G: Ah.... Well, then I have other ways of making your life miserable...
                                SE: No you don't. One of my friends is actually a Sin-Eater, and he told me all about it a couple years ago. You can't do jack shit.
                                G: Crap. You're right.
                                SE: Yeah. Screw you, you stupid ghost.

                                I'm not saying that Sin-Eaters should be unable to refuse their geists. Hell, I'm not even saying that they shouldn't be able to blow off their ends of the Bargains. I'm saying that there should be advantages for Sin-Eaters working in accordance with their geists, and disadvantages for ones working at odds against them. Even if possession wasn't one of them, there should be something that makes a Sin-Eater think twice before telling his or her geist to fuck off. Maybe the geist can call the attention of malicious ghosts to the Sin-Eater. Maybe it could disrupt the plasm flows in the blood, causing confusion. Maybe the earworm is bad enough that the Sin-Eater will suffer penalties to concentration. Maybe instead of full possession, the geist could cause a limb to lash out at an inopportune moment. I just think that low Synergy should be something that a Sin-Eater would want to avoid, but might accept instead of the alternative.

                                I have often felt that a lot of the horror in Geist came from the answer to the question, "What would you do for another chance at life? What sacrifices would you make? What would you agree to?" If the Bargain has no teeth, then the answer to that question doesn't truly matter. Agree to whatever you wish, because you don't need to carry through. The geists are nothing but paper tigers with two major abilities - the power to take an ordinary dying mortal and transform them into the one type of entity in the entire Chronicles of Darkness that are completely lacking in any form of downside, and the power to be mildly to extremely annoying, depending on the individual. In my opinion, any Bargain that has that kind of power should be something one hesitates to break, not something that is upheld or broken casually depending on the whim of the moment.
