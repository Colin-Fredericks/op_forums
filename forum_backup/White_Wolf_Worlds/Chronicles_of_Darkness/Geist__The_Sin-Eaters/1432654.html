    Memoir of Minor Obsessions [Personal Musings Thread]

    01-19-2021, 01:36 AM
    As with Mage and Deviant, I've had some thoughts percolating about everyone's favorite flesh-ghost game and decided to commit them to text.

    • As a starting character, crisis points are one of your incentives to pursue your Burden Aspiration early on: gaining two more dots of effective Synergy bumps your Intercession dicepool up to three dice from zero, which in turn allows you to start trying to daisy-chain the Bonding Condition for Synergy Beats (and also adding another die or two to the dicepool from having an effective Sympathetic relationship while you're pursuing your first Touchstone).

    • On the other hand, the way Catharsis works means it's slightly preferable (in a game where your primary aim isn't just that particular endgame, at least) to resolve your geist's Remembrance before your Burden: completing the former before the latter means you've unlocked your Specific crisis point trigger and can still come back from the dead, while doing it the other way around means you jump from a Vague trigger to none at all, lose the ability to come back from the dead (and no longer automatically risk manifesting your Rank 5 geist to defend you at low Health), and effectively stop contributing Synergy Beats to the shared pool.

    • Given the above, the Indebted Condition is a concrete concern for most Sin-Eaters of any level of establishment: the alternative to swinging the pendulum back toward the other end of the Bargain is leaning into your Keys, which a) can only mitigate the mounting penalty to Haunt rolls for so long, and 2) will start cutting into your Willpower soon enough unless you're particularly lucky with resonant conditions, which either makes the problem self-correcting or encourages you to lean into your Root and/or Bloom.

    • Speaking of Keys: the Dooms of Blood, Cold Wind, Disease, and Pyre Flame all make a character a resonant target for their respective Keys's use. Funny, that.

    • Speaking of Willpower: It's an important currency in this game, between the Liminal Aura and its adjustment, the deferment of unresonant Dooms, and the cost of the Wavering Condition, as well as the Anchors of your character, their geist, and their krewe; in many ways it's co-equal with Plasm in its significance to the template.

    • Speaking of Indebted: It's therefore a prudent move, in any case, to resolve the issue at the heart of at least one of your Touchstones before you reach Synergy 6, because that's probably going to be the harder one to resolve and having to juggle between three different focuses is a recipe for disaster in Bound circles; if your krewe-adjacent Touchstone only has to compete with your personal vendetta or your geist's unfinished business, the rebound will be that much easier to resolve.

    • Talking of krewes: You're really encouraged to be celebrants early and often, between the Krewe Beats for resolving the Regalia Condition, the Krewe Beats for exceptional success on Ceremony rolls, and the end-of-chapter totals for advancing/living by Doctrines, and the Willpower/Essence/Plasm from fulfilling the krewe's Virtue or Creed. (Underworld tableaus, ylem trances, punishments from the Kerberoi, and being Dead are slightly more rarefied fare, but definitely ought to be kept in mind later on as Catabasis approaches.) Since that first one isn't available to a freshly-made krewe, the bonuses for taking extra time or using costly symbolic elements are a major thing to leverage for characters who're having trouble with their Doctrines (whether because they've set the bar higher than they can immediately reach or because they simply haven't figured out all of them yet) unless someone wants to pour their efforts into building up the krewe with normal Experiences.

    • Presence is an important Attribute for the Bound, between its place in the Aura Flare roll, its place in the Intercession dicepool, its place in half the Innate Sin-Eater Ceremonies, and the Key of Blood in general. (Manipulation slightly less so, but only because it doesn't contribute to Aura Flare and the Doom of Deep Waters is a little stickier than Blood.) The Bound are Social creatures!

    Discussion welcome.

          Originally posted by Primal Zed View Post
          While it's good to consider the game theory of what the rules ultimately incentivizes from the players, this analysis seems to lean further into min-maxing without regard to what would make for a good story in a given chronicle.
          "You should focus on building your krewe and pursuing your starting Touchstone early in the game," "the book is not joking when it says you should pick your Burden Aspiration based on the expected length of your chronicle," "it's prudent to get your affairs in order in a game where conflicts of interest are baked into your template as you rise in power," and "the ways Willpower is gained and lost in this game affect the narrative pressures on the characters through the mechanics" are not what I would call "min-maxing."

          Your thoughts here seem to be based on Catharsis coming into effect when the Burden Touchstone is resolved, regardless of whether the Remembrance is resolved. The book indicate Catharsis occurs after both Burden and Remembrance are resolved. Is there something I'm missing here?
          The first sentence. You reach Catharsis when both are resolved, but resolving one before the other has different results.

          Resolving your Burden first gives you an extra Rank and makes resolving your geist's Remembrance trigger Catharsis. Resolving your geist's Remembrance first gives you an extra Rank and the narrowest personal crisis point trigger while still allowing you to engage with the Synergy economy. Doing either deprives you of one source of Willpower and temporary Synergy for the remainder of the game.

          If your intention is to stick around to help your krewe and you don't want to lose the template's strongest recuperative boon while you're doing so, it's wiser mechanically (and more compelling narratively) to put off tying up the loose end that made you Bound in favor of working on the core problem of your ghastly patron.

          That this tension and perspective makes it easier to see how their Burdens can make the Bound monstrous (or even Tyrannous) is icing on the cake. Whether it's their legacy, their loss, their regrets, their revenge, or their attachment, any given ghostwalker hypothetically has an excuse to put their business above that of the source of their magic and thereby prompt conflict, and the harder their Burden is to put down, the more tempting it becomes to take the Plasmic power of the geist (and Esoteric power of a krewe, to a lesser extent) for themselves.

          Meanwhile, a Sin-Eater with an easily-resolved Burden strengthens their Synergy both on the way and in its conclusion and has little reason to stick around after they've upheld their end of the Bargain by resolving the geist's Remembrance; the focus becomes less about clashing Touchstones and more about figuring out the geist's history and nature, and maybe helping change the nature of the Underworld if you're up for it.

          I also think Key resonance should be more psychological than literal.
          The Keys in 2e are literally 1e's Thresholds plus the four Elemental Keys. They're forms of death. They're supposed to be pretty literal, given that they're the things geists and Reapers lean into to become more inhuman. Psychology is for ghosts and the people dealing with them, not the power of Death Itself.

              Unpacking crisis points a bit more because go figure, they're important.

              1. If you don't want your geist to be Unleashed, you intercede by rolling a dicepool based on your relationship with it. The results of this roll can adjust the core dicepool up or down on later rolls without applying modifiers, but the baseline is determined by your Synergy, which increases by two or three if you're pursuing the underlying promise behind one or more of your Touchstones. At Synergy 1, without those adjustments, intercession rolls use a chance die. The sole source of Synergy Beats that isn't routed through crisis point triggers involves deliberately Unleashing your geist and having it be discorporated, which stops you from using Plasm for two scenes on top of emptying your reserve, so knowing your geist's Ban, Bane, and personal trigger is pretty important as a co-author in the narrative of a character learning to deal with this thing.

              2. Failing an intercession roll means the geist is Unleashed or you suffer the Wavering Condition, which doesn't go away until you make a physical or material sacrifice in the pursuit of your geist's Remembrance; in the meantime, you have to spend a point of Willpower to roll any dicepool that includes Synergy. That set notably comprises:
              • Every Haunt.
              • Every Intercession dicepool that is not a chance die.
              • The roll for the geist to let you stay dead.
              • The Aura Flare ability, which already costs a point of Willpower to use normally and gives other Sin-Eaters a Condition that cashes out with a free point of Willpower.
              • Break the Cycle rolls to allow an Underworld tableau to move on.
              tl;dr If your geist is displeased and you can't quite talk it down then your options are Poltergeist Activity or an onerous diversion of your focus onto your magical half's affairs (the ignoring of which will be quite a headache if you want to Be Magic for the sake of other pursuits).

              3. If two of your character's Touchstones have come into conflict, they have the Indebted Condition, which applies a mounting penalty to rolls for Haunts, rolls using Remembrance Traits, and rolls to intercede in crisis points. That last part is important, because Indebted supplies the main actual dice modifier that applies to crisis points and it only goes away when you rebalance the scales; given the bump in Synergy from pursuing a Touchstone is the same regardless of which one you tilt towards and the Condition kicks in regardless, a character who experiences this conflict is almost guaranteed to have (for the moment) at least a Sympathetic relationship with their geist, which means they're sufficiently aligned that any ensuing crisis point allows the Sin-Eater to pick the geist's response as long as they forgo intercession, which neatly avoids the penalty that would risk giving you Wavering or having to sacrifice Synergy. (The problem being, of course, that you need to pretty quickly settle that debt if you plan to do anything other than pursue your Touchstones or avoid hemorrhaging Plasm.)

              4. Succeeding at an intercession roll gives your character an effective +3 Synergy for the sole purpose of determining which dicepool you roll on your next Intercession attempt (the number of dice from Synergy doesn't change, but going from zero dice to one or adding a better Attribute instead of a worse one would otherwise require spending Experience, so a temporary boost is important and the main reason why the Relationship chart doesn't just break down the dicepools by Synergy ranges). In addition to opening up the possibility of an Inspired-esque daisy chain effect, the Condition is (as mentioned) one of the only sources of Synergy Beats in the game, and the least onerous one to resolve besides — both of the resolution criteria that don't give you a chance to get the Condition again involve raising your Synergy.

              5. On the other end of things, dramatically failing an intercession roll forces the issue to such a degree that stopping the geist from Unleashing itself costs you Synergy, and even if you don't do that, your next crisis point is resolved as though your relationship was a step worse. Since encountering a crisis point resolves the Bonding Condition that raises your effective relationship level, this means that anyone below Synergy 5 is rolling a chance die, and anyone below Synergy 8 can't forgo rolling to choose how the geist responds. This isn't as likely to result in a death spiral as Bonding is to facilitate a chain of Synergy, but it does increase the odds that your geist will manifest in an uncontrolled fashion or that you'll pick up the Wavering Condition as described above, which in turn pushes you toward your geist's Remembrance and potentially into conflict with your Burden.

              6. On the other other end of things, if by some miracle you roll an exceptional success, you also add two dice to your next Synergy roll (for which see point 2); this is, in a sense, your incentive to still make Intercession rolls at high Synergy even if you somehow aren't worried about how people will react to a terrifying specter of death manifesting in their presence.

              7. On the furthest end of things, Flatlining at Synergy 0 cuts you off from basically all your powers except Ceremonies and prevents you from improving the relationship by buying Synergy or rolling well on Intercession rolls, but turns crisis points into a source of normal Beats like some other breaking point equivalents do and offers resolution from the limited number of Remembrance scenes you've yet to complete. Otherwise, this is how you die mad or dive into Tyranny — your Burden's not gonna make your geist re-rethink the Bargain, and if it's got what it needed or you refuse to give it then the relationship has broken down beyond the scope of mere mutual exploitation.

              In conclusion: hey it turns out this game where Self Vs. Service is a listed theme has strapped into its core an engine for struggling to negotiate that conflict. who knew?
