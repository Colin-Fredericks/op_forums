    Could use some help homebrewing Ceremonies

    05-27-2022, 03:20 AM
    So, in a game I am running I have this Absent npc called the Could-Have-Been-King, who was an artificially created ghost(made by a Mage). Anyway, he desires to actually live and as such has been gathering lore on ceremonies most fair and foul in his pursuit of power and the knowledge that can give him "birth". In my chronicle he is trying to approach one of the characters and entice him with Ceremonies that can give him an edge. Basically, he wants to turn one of the Changelings in my game into a "Necromancer-King"...and I would like help with making ceremonies that could be used for such a thing. As I don't find many meant for Sin-Eaters that I find...the most useful for that purpose. Or if any of the Geist writers are here, I would love some design tips on how to make these.

    (I have not yet found any Ceremonies that uses Politics as part of the dicepool, so if any of you have ideas for ones that could feasibly use the politics skill that would be nice )

      I suppose this can vary a lot depending on what you think a “Necromancer King” entails…
      What comes to my mind would be things like:
      • see and communicate with ghosts
      • summoning
      • abjuration, exorcism, and warding
      • bind to a particular spot, object, or task
      • steal a ghost’s powers, or compel them to use them
      • help a ghost materialize, possess, or claim
      • plumb knowledge from the dead
      • undead minions
      • heal or harm ghosts
      • steal or grant Essence
      • boost or alter the traits of a ghost


      …Although I might also recommend looking at D&D spells, as “Necromancer King” kind of fits more in that genre.

      For modeling these abilities, Geist has a few Ceremonies and Haunts that relate. In particular, “Momento Mori” has a ceremony that binds a ghost to a task - specifically guard duty, iirc. I’d also suggest looking at Reapers as a model; having, or replicating, a Death Mask can be used to enhance ghosts and bind them to a task.
      “Second Sight” has at least the basics ( seeing, communicating, summoning) as Merits. It’s 1E, but still very usable - and I think there’s been a fan update.
      I believe Mummy also has a few applicable Utterances, and 1E had a section on playable ghosts, that included new Numina for replacing an anchor with a Mummy’s commands, augmenting abilities, and claiming.
      And, of course, Mage has a bunch of these as sample spells.
      Oh, and Chris Allen did a write up of “Spirit Noble” Numina that would be very applicable:

      For costs and mechanics, I’d usually point to Sorcery (Mummy 2E). It lets you convert basically any supernatural ability into a ritual, and then provides merit costs for learning them. [pp. 272-275 & 120]
      Most dice pools can be converted into Resolve + Occult + Wyrd - Rank / Resistance, or something else appropriate.
      I do think requiring the Rule of Three in addition will help it feel more ritually… In general, have at least 1 symbolic object & 1 action, with elements that represent the subject, effect, and a source of energy. This can largely be whatever feels appropriate in the moment, or even provided by the player, as it should be more evocative than restrictive.

      But… “I can make you a Necromancer King” sounds a bit more like a demonic Pact to me - I suggest looking at Inferno for bargaining with a demon that can just arbitrarily grant you knowledge & power (in the form of a dozen or so rituals, and maybe a power source). It certainly seems more appropriate for your situation, as the gist I interpreted was “you can immediately get amazing power, but this entity will get to enact its super terrible plans” - and that’s much more of a plot-driven thing than worrying about merit ratings and EXP. ["Inferno" pp. 84-93]

      But if it is the player’s goal to gradually accumulate occult knowledge to become a Necromancer King, I’d definitely just use Sekhem Sorcery for costs, possibly allowing the “sorcerous knowledge” merit to have no maximum number, as long as all the abilities fit that theme. Note that the “sorcerous prodigy” merit effectively lets you decide if a specific power is Major (1 dot : 1 effect) or Minor (1 dot : 2 effects). Which isn’t quite the same as the wide range of costs for “proper” ceremonies. But. It does require the player seek out knowledge, experiment, making terrible bargains, etc - and Changeling doesn’t offer the supplemental EXP Geist does, so that cost might be more manageable, especially if they buy up other traits from time to time.
      And this can still be supplemented with the Infernal Pact rules, of course.

      Hope something in that is useful to you,
      ~Seraph Kitty

              As far as Politics goes, the following effects come to my mind:

              Extending an Old Law of the Underworld over an area in the living world. The ritualist must make the law known, since ignorance is a defense in this case. Transgressors become targets of Kerberoi and their agents.

              Being recognised by the Underworld as a Reaper, with corresponding benefits and duties. The ritualist gains freedom of movement through Avernian and Irkalla Gates, but must take Ghosts into the Great Below.

              As for rebirth of the Could-Have-Been-King, my only idea is to mark a pregnancy to allow him to reincarnate upon its completion. The Ghost's identity is submerged within and blended with the host's subconscious, affecting their personality and eventually granting memories and Skills.

              Aside from that, you can look into Ceremonies from the 1st Edition, if you haven't already.

                      I don't have the books at hand, so it's difficult for me to determine the right power level for Ceremonies. I think that replicating official Haunts is fine, as long as the Ceremonies are more involved and narrow in use. However, if you're looking for effects that cause trouble in the Underworld, I can try to think of something. As a general consideration, you can decide whether the negative outcome is the goal, means to an end, or a side effect. Also, consider that you can steal powers from across the entire CofD and beyond, as long as you figure out how to make them deathly and how they make mess of things for the dead.

                      For instance, a simple Spirit Exorcism can be made to banish the possessor into the Underworld, to be forced to prey on Ghosts to survive. Under most circumstances, getting rid of the Spirit is the goal, and threatening the Underworld society is a side effect. The Necromancer may not even know the consequences of this rite.

                      Another Ceremony may attempt to steal some of a Kerberos' power, to immunize oneself against a very specific form of death. However, that suppresses the Kerberos' ability to return from destruction, making its Dominion vulnerable to collapse. Endangering the Kerberos is means to an end, and the Necromancer can justify themselves that it's unlikely that the worst comes to pass, not knowing that Ferrymen actively transport people to Domains easiest to destabilise.

                      Yet another Ceremony may sacrifice an unhealthy Aspiration, just to imprint it upon a random Chtonian, making it interact with the Underworld in an unpredictable manner. Another Ceremony still, may confuse the Guardian of an Irkalla Gate into forcing people through without payment. Convenient if you want to cross for free, no so much for Ghosts kidnapped from their established homes.

                      I didn't list any outright malicious Ceremonies, because even benign rituals could be used for evil, if the Necromancer is so inclined.

                          So the thing about Ceremonies is that, as ritual magic that can be performed by the unaware, to a certain degree they're plot devices. Necromancers can threaten the Underworld with them in part because necromantic cults are a thing, people affect the Underworld through their deaths and the way they think about death, and community and sacrifice back the most powerful Ceremonies — it's the same principle the God-Machine uses to make Infrastructure work through permutations on the three pillars of knowledge, natural resources, and manpower, except Infrastructure is pulling that kind of stuff off at an industrial scale, which is why most Infrastructure's effects are designed as plot devices with narrative linchpins instead of systematized rolls and dot-ratings.

                          It's also worth noting that Ceremonies in Geist are designed to be difficult to pick up a lot of without being part of a krewe or similar Mystery Cult, designed as they are to benefit the collective-action side of Geist; if your intention is to come up with weird occult shortcuts for a changeling character specifically to use, unless you intend to have this Absent teach them how to perform these tricks reliably, you can just relay those effects to the character with the rule of thumb that effects with a bigger scale or more overtly supernatural outcome take more involved efforts.

                          Like, to be clear, as Seraph Kitty points out, the thing Ceremonies represent is a broad category and learning them takes investment — what you've asked for is basically just "what are some weird things about the way the world works that this changeling character can take advantage of that aren't Contracts?" and the answer is "theoretically anything."

                          The Absent's goal is to be incarnated, and they're attempting to accomplish this by enticing a changeling with occult knowledge. Ludonarrative efficiency being what it is, the best bet is that this offer and/or the tools in place are directly relevant to the process of getting the ghost incarnated, or at least finding out how to manage that, and the changeling in question is suited to that process in some way, whether by their metaphysical qualities or their desires and placement in the world. What's the changeling likely to want to accomplish that being a Necromancer King would facilitate?

                              Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                              What's the changeling likely to want to accomplish that being a Necromancer King would facilitate?
                              Yeah, that was the approach I was taking in my post. I wasn't trying to brainstorm a new template or whatever. That list of powers is what I expect a 'Necromancer King' to be able to do with a collection of ceremonies - most of which are modeled by one form of published power or another, that could be turned into ceremonies via Sorcerous Rites.
                              Sorcerous Rites are really the only toolkit we have right now for inventing new ceremonies. I'm pretty underwhelmed by it myself (I much prefer 'Second Sight' and 'Witch Finders'). But it's at least a system, and one where you don't have to sweat under tricky mechanics of assigning dot ratings. If you want something mechanically meatier, maybe sketch it out any given effect as a mage spell and give it a dot rating based on that.


                              If you're looking for ideas of what a Necromancer King could do, steal ideas from other splats, other game systems, books, movies, etc.
                              Or let the player tell you what they want, have them research it as a downtime action, and spend your prep between sessions finding a comparable spell somewhere. Bam, now it's a Closed Rite.
                              It being a 'necromancer' ceremony mostly just means it messes with ghosts or dead things some how - so anything (that you're willing to deal with in session) can be on the table, which makes guidelines kind of hard to write. Beyond that... I don't know what you mean by "less Sin-Eater-y", apart from maybe "exploits ghosts rather than helps them".

                              As far as developing details to each specific ceremony ("stab an eye of newt under a full moon while chanting the victim's name")... those possibilities are also endless, and mostly arbitrary, and very much tied to your personal understanding of symbolism and the occult logic of your chronicle. Mostly, don't sweat the details; improvise them, or let the players. If you really decide that particular effect needs to be 'hard to pull off', come up with one (or more) complications that takes at least a scene to pull off - collecting exotic agreements, coming up with a significant power source, securing the aid of a cthonian, whatever. Otherwise, do not stress about it beforehand - forcing yourself to come up with creative details leads to frustrating writers block. Let your subconscious fill it in, or steal it from fiction you like. I guess you could do a free writing exercise if you really, really have to, but I'm pretty sure that way lies madness - madness you don't need, and probably won't help much with what you're trying to do.

                                  So picking up from the Kickstarter thread-

                                  -The general good guidelines for Ceremony power revolve around the likely frequency with which a ceremony is going to committed to versus how big of a plot device they're execution can be, with the fact that the most powerful Ceremonies do things like open Avernian Gates and create Anchors. 1 and 2 Dot Ceremonies tend to have, at most, a particular effect that can be felt for the Scene, maybe a Chapter, 3 and 4 Dot Ceremonies tend to cast a shadow over the Chapter, maybe the Story, and 5 Dot Ceremonies are supposed to lead to changes that'll be felt for at least the whole Story or the next, with some bursting to affect Arcs. No single Ceremony has the singular power to completely define a Chronicle, barring Catabasis (which you could reasonably debate isn't a Ceremony, but c'mon.)

                                  In turn, on an operational basis, the increasing dot scale is akin to religious functions and how often you would commit to them. 1 and 2 Dot Ceremonies are frequent rituals that help to regularly keep the faith of the Krewe and keep up attrition against the ideological enemy(/ies) of the Krewe, 3 and 4 Dot Ceremonies are more done as needed or on more monthly/seasonal premises, and 5 Dot Ceremonies are intended large commitments and attempts to shape the Underworld, like holidays intended to spear the world.

                                  I'll have to dig up my design doc from after I finished writing Ceremonies and have a public forum on it some time. In the meantime, I will note that while I tried to keep to some coherence with Regalia and Ceremonies, that entire writing process was hectic and strained as fuck, so if some of that advice doesn't jive with what you see, that'd be why.

                                      Building on that operational basis, it may help to think of dot rating in terms of how major a figure in a Mystery Cult (and how major a Mystery Cult) would be expected to be needed to perform the Ceremony.

                                      If you haven't personally learned how to create an Avernian Gate, you need to be basically one of if not the leaders in a krewe whose mysteries teach that process — a krewe as mystically potent as most krewes will ever get — in order to have the knowledge to manage it. On the other end of the scale, a one-dot Ceremony is known to every celebrant in good standing in the krewe that teaches it — which can be any krewe that hasn't collapsed and might be little more than a half-dozen ghosts and mortals who meet in a church basement for philosophical discussion about the nature of death that you might have over casual drinks.

                                      Mummy's Open Rites include the same categories of actions that Geist describes as Ceremonies whose dot-rating is functionally zero due to how widespread they are, and as a game without as much emphasis on collective action versus solitary sorcerers who might or might not also be immortal is less concerned with time and institutional knowledge than it is personal costs and esoteric reagents. Ceremonies present a take on common magic for which you might need a priest — if you want to use Geist's systems for this process, initiation and possibly Regalia would be important things to think about.

                                              Originally posted by Anyoneatall View Post

                                              That's how sorcery works in mummy?
                                              It lets you convert any power into a ritual?
                                              The Sorcerous Knowledge merit (p.120) states:
                                              “With the Storyteller’s permission, you may invent a new Closed Rite to fill this slot.”
                                              The sections on sorcery (pp.272-275) don’t actually say what qualifies as a Closed Rite - only that they are “the fruit of years of study and sacrifice”, follow the Rule of Three, and can be made easier with Pillar points. We get 6 particular examples from the sample antagonist cults, and we have the common Open Rites to contrast with, but nothing else to really gauge what’s appropriate.

                                              I would say it’s an open invitation to invent anything, or replicate anything, that makes sense as a ritual - at ST discretion. Most NPC sorcerers will probably have only 1 or 2 tricks, very thematic to their occult traditions. PCs should probably have a “theme” they’re based around, but I’d advocate for them being able to learn a handful of powerful effects. Especially if they're mortals or minor templates in a game with major template PCs.

                                              There should clearly be limits. For example, if a power would be accessible to your template in another form (or to a fellow PCs template), don’t make it a ritual to sidestep the cost. A lot of instant-action effects don’t make sense for a ritual. But, for example, some of the GM Infrastructure could be read as Closed Rites on an industrial scale (and iirc they even follow the Rule of Three explicitly), and just about any Magic Ritual you find in popular fiction would probably work.
