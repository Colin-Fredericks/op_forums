    Originally posted by Tessie View Post
    It's mechanically trivial to activate them and most if not all of them are trivially easy to end their Conditions prematurely. Often by spending the single charge you paid Plasm for.
    On doing things. You're not getting returns from singing the Dirge to empty air for three seconds or extruding the Caul to ignore wound penalties you aren't suffering or consulting the Oracle about absolutely nothing. This is a storytelling game that is played with other people — the rules are written under the implicit assumption that you're going to use them in a way that makes sense with what's happening in the story and not waste the other players' time on inconsequential activity in the name of raw numbers. Using a supernatural power is not a trivial act in a game where supernatural powers are a narratively significant element of the setting.

    I have heard that this loop will be changed in the released version just because it's so easy to abuse, though.
    Yes. That is my point. Even if there weren't a clear distinction between refilling your Synergy-derived Plasm capacity with cyclical use of Haunts and having the necessary stock to actually funnel dozens of points' worth of Plasm into Enhancements in a single application, "You can abuse this blatantly exploitative reading of the mechanics if you're willing to completely ignore self-evident design intent and treat the game's representation of above-average Attribute ratings as unexceptional" is a poor attitude to bring into a discussion of the game.

    You lose excess Plasm from Keys at the end of the scene and buying off the Doom before Synergy 6 limits your gains to three Plasm per use. When your "trivial" application of the mechanics to use nearly the full Plasm capacity of a starting character calls for using the same power on nothing three times in a row before actually doing anything with it, it's time to take a long hard look at your approach to RPGs.

                        I'm not impressed with the argument from narrative significance. People are real good at rapidly turning wondrous discoveries to mundane practical use. As a species we figured out flight a little over a century ago. Airplanes are pretty damn phenomenal but ask most people who use them what they think and you're almost certainly going to hear about what a hassle security is, or how uncomfortable the seats are. Hell, we can have a real time video conversation from hundreds of miles away using two assemblies of plastic, metal and glass, and that's just a normal thing for a lot of people. Expecting people to not use magic powers for stupid exploits is expecting people to not act like people.

                        3 in an attribute isn't at all uncommon and it isn't just a PC problem. Very, very few of the published NPCs restrict themselves to even starting character stats, where it's impossible to have fewer than two such attributes. The majority have significantly higher values than that. Yeah, most aren't supposed to be "average" people, but even the ones that are often have three, four or even five attributes at 3+. When literally every PC and NPC significant enough to have a stat block is above average 3 dots does not feel out of the ordinary. They can make the claim that it is all they want, that's not how they treat it when it's time for examples.

                            A character who is using a Haunt is using it for something. That is why the recovery loop is a loop and not a point that says "Sin-Eaters with the right combination of Keys, Haunts, and Attributes refill their Plasm between scenes without consequence or remark." The people with magic powers in this hypothetical are not doing a stupid exploit, they are engaging in nonsensical behavior that they would only arrive at through out-of-character knowledge of the way the game is written in a particular iteration of the rules.

                            A character is not going to conclude that the thing they should do is merge with their geist for enhanced physical plasticity, do nothing, dismiss their geist from their body, and then do this again three to six more times without the presupposition that your borrowed powers can be tapped for literally no reason except to cram what would be multiple scenes' worth of gradual sipping at the power of the Underworld into a laughably short timescale.

                            The loop is made such that you're incentivized to use Keys with Haunts so that you can get more effect out of them with the Plasm that you're drawing from them, and most Haunts are minimally things you have running for a scene. If you are refilling your stocks in this fashion, it is pretty definitely intended to be comparable to the returns you would get from rolling an exceptional success in a resonant scenario or accepting the Doom to not need to lower your net gains by two Plasm. It's very clearly not made to have characters Unlock their favorite version of the Caul and then dismiss it five times in one scene for a top-off.

                            When literally every PC and NPC significant enough to have a stat block is above average 3 dots does not feel out of the ordinary. They can make the claim that it is all they want, that's not how they treat it when it's time for examples.
