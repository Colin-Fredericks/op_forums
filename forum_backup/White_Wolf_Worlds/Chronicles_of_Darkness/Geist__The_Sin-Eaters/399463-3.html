    Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
    nWoD Wraith wouldn't work very well. It works in oWoD because each gameline was very much entirely self contained - it could easily get away with keeping the play area predominately to its Underworld. nWoD games all use a common framework (mortal baseline, before adding supernatural template; uses the "World of Darkness" primarily, with additional "worlds" being optional add-ins), and ghost mechanics have already been established. To accurately replicate Wraith, it would basically be ignoring the rest of the world and need to build up the Underworld far more. And if you start using a mortal baseline... you pretty much have more Geist than not.
    Considering the differences between oWoD Changeling and nWoD Changeling, I very much doubt that Wraith couldn't be incorporated into the larger universe alongside Geist. The underworld is very much left open for GMs to create new domains and kereboi, meaning there are a lot of places that Wraiths and their domains could be established, if far and away. That's also discounting the fact that nothing is known on what is under the oceans in the underworld - Wraith could easily be settled there.

    Thats said, I agree with Baroness Nerak on this - Geist and Wraith are two different entities. Just because you like one or the other doesn't mean neither of them deserve a second edition. Tonally they're completely different as well - while they both involve the underworld, Wraith is dark, depressing and pretty much all about existing in a world that would sooner see you subsumed than get closer to actually living. Geist is about bringing balance to the dead and the living however you like and simply getting to live again after dying - that's not to say there aren't dark or depressing moments, but the game is open for being a struggle or being about helping however one can.

    Heck, if you really wanted to make Wraith its own thing without disrupting Geist, then why not make it a realm outside death? The place where ghosts go when they are destroyed instead of being allowed to pass on? Perhaps the remnants of what is lost over time ends up in their dominion, where they reconstitute into a new being? I don't know. At least one group has tried (and is still trying) to make a nWoD Wraith fangame, so there's appeal in it, but it doesn't gather my attention given how dark the game is. If it does get made, great. If not, at least the classic game exists. I'm all for a second edition of Geist to emerge though, or discounting that a new source book. As extensive as Geists' powers are, a new manifestation or key that isn't quite as damning as the Pit or the Stygian Key would be cool (I'm all for them having a cost to get, but I love getting new powers).

                                        Originally posted by Taidragon View Post
                                        I know that Baroness Nerak considered houseruling that the Shroud, rather than giving 1/2/3/4/5 armor, instead gave 1/1/2/2/3 armor per level, as 5 armor is apparently quite a beast to fight against.
                                        I play a vampire Hunter that became a Sin-Eater, so the ST and I had to houserule some things for sake of compatibility with 2E. This was along the lines of what I worked out. I also got a look at a draft of Mage 2E at GenCon this year and picked out something neat for protective abilities like the Shroud. In that draft's rules on Mage Armor, Arcana that could generate a physical barrier granted 1/2-Arcanum dots as armor, while those that made you a more difficult target to strike granted +Arcanum dots to Defense.

                                        So for Geist, Keys that actually generate a protective barrier, (like the Elemental Keys), you get a 1/1/2/2/3 armor rating. For Keys that make you harder to hit (Passion, Phantasmal), you get +1/+2/+3/+4/+5 to Defense (even against firearms).

                                                            I think if I was developing Geist 2E, I'd mostly just refine what was there. The core premise of the game seems great, and the presentation works well for most things. Bring the Underworld into the core book as much as possible. It's been a while since I read the core book, so I don't remember how often the Sin-Eaters go therein, but I'd make the transition about as integral as the Shadow Realm is to werewolves. Kingdoms of bone and ash, rivers of blood and grave-water, etc. Whole realms lost to the Underworld, histories etched in gravestones and black towers. The Underworld is a really fantastic place (in the classical sense of the word), and I'd want to capitalize on its use as much as possible. Sin-Eaters are both at home and foreign there, one part living mortal and one part deathly spirit from the Underworld. Walk in both worlds, the duality of life and death, of forgotten remnants that linger on.

                                                            Maybe have them go further at times, into the Lower Depths? That could be cool.

                                                            Oh, and in the same way that packmembers can be Wolf-Blooded and even mortal now, fully playable, I'd have ghosts be part of the extended Sin-Eater "family." Including being playable. They have some advantages and disadvantages over full Sin-Eaters, but it makes them unique.
