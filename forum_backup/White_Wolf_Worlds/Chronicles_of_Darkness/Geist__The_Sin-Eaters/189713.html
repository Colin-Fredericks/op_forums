    Extended Family for the Littlest Gameline - Crossline Book Recommendations

    07-06-2014, 08:28 PM
    As I may have mentioned elsewhere, I'm in the process of reading through as much of the nWoD as I can; I finished the current corpus of published Geist books about a month or two back, opted to read some of the bluebook material before I moved on to Promethean - chiefly Antagonists and Second Sight - and found a decent amount of useful material beyond that specifically pointed out in the Geist corebook, i.e. the zombie rules and the psychic powers.

    For starters, both Second Sight and Antagonists contain information on cults, though with different focuses - the Entropic Things and Demons of the Outer Darkness from Second Sight's Cults of Things That Must Not Be both seem particularly fitting for chthonians and other strange things out of the Underworld, while Antagonists' more detailed look at the types and structures of cult give a general idea of how the more hardline krewes (or the mortal hangers-on thereof) might operate.

    Then there's tidbits like Antagonists' fourth chapter containing what seems a good example of weird things one might find in the Underworld - toxicum mold is worrying stuff - or Second Sight's emphasis on how psychic powers tend to impact their possessors' psyche in the having of them - the opening fiction and sample story in particular do a good job of highlighting the effects of being host to a power not entirely under your control.

    So now I'm reading Immortals, whose utility in constructing abmortals should be fairly obvious; the mechanics for how societies of body thieves work seem to call back to Second Sight's mechanics for thaumaturgy paths, and of course blood bathers are practically archetypical in terms of "immortal who stays immortal by maintaining a body count," though Pig provides a good example of how that can get more complicated and Shawn's frankly worrying genesis hooks in well enough to the matter of things that make Reapers ask serious questions.

    All that said, does anybody have any other bits of recommended reading for Geist that isn't part of the gameline proper?

    Resident Lore-Hound

          Dancers in the Dusk has some info on what the dying dream about, along with a Morphean called the Pale Brother who might be an incarnation of Death.

          One of the pieces of microfiction in Chicago could be interpreted as a dying man meeting a geist. The one with the narrator screaming "I'm not dead!" as the thing approaches him.

          Mummy: The Curse has a bunch of new ghost Numina. Some Utterances that affect ghosts could also provide some bits of cosmological necromystery for Sin-Eaters to puzzle over, like the one that sucks ghosts into a lake of fire until the Arisen vomits them up again to do its bidding.

          The Moros chapter in Tome of the Watchtowers has some cool stuff on various philosophies of death, like whether or not Hiroshima cheapened the concept.

          There is, of course, Ghost Stories, but that basically goes without saying.

          I recall the corebook for Geist mentioning Midnight Roads. Might be worth a look.

          Finally, the only other thing that comes to me right now is the chapter on Sin-Eaters in Mortal Remains.

                  My Geist game has made heavy use of Second Sight and Antagonists. I will second Summoners as being very good...I definitely have gotten some good use out of it. The Cthonians are useful, the Numbers Station stuff works for Geist, and the Men in Black work for dealing with a Krewe that wants to solve every issue by overpowering it. Some ghost stuff there is good too.

                  WoD: Immortals I have also gotten a fair amount of use of. Also, WoD: Slashers does not make for even powered antagonists but also make for antagonists Sin-Eaters might fight. I would say that with the above three mentioned earlier in thread are my big five recommendations.

                  Honerable mention to Glimpses of the Unknown, which has a short Sin-Eater section with a Ceremony.

                  Secondarily, as noted above Midnight Roads and Ghost Stories are useful. Ghost Stories is hard to use out of the box due to Sin-Eater powers but has some use.

                              The Mystikoi from Ancient Bloodlines are a small bloodline of vampires originally from Armenia who practice a sort of twisted form of Gnostic Christianity significantly influenced by Manichaeism. They regard the Beast as a sort of spiritual guide, and have a unique discipline that allows them to perceive and communicate with entities in twilight while riding the wave of frenzy.

                              The Sta-Au, from the same book, are a strange North American bloodline originally associated with the Ktunaxa and Blackfoot tribes who are unusual in that they can "adopt" new members from any of the clans. Like the Dragolescu and the Mystikoi they have unique abilities that allow them to see and communicate with ghosts, but they can actually go one step further and temporarily "fade away" into twilight, becoming ghosts themselves for most mechanical purposes.

                              These days the Sta-Au have spread out from their place of origin, making their territory in barren places that they call the "Land of the Worms", which is made up of isolated pockets of land that seem to attract ghosts and are inimical to all living things besides vermin and parasites. Areas that are part of the Land of the Worms have a number of unusual properties, not the least of which is that any human that dies there invariably becomes a ghost, with the area itself acting as their anchor.
