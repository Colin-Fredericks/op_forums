    Ghosts and such...

    03-14-2015, 12:25 PM
    I was pondering on Ghosts and I wondered on the theories behind them. I'm still not 100% sure on what exactly they are when it comes to the question of souls. This is perhaps more of a GMC subject, though I think the topic is fitting for this board.
    Are they souls of the departed,.. Or are they sort of memories left behind that clung to some unfulfilled purpose, or perhaps a part of the soul left behind due to unfinished business?

    Also can someone who is soulless (not a Promethean or similar, I mean someone like a Mortal that has the Condition of it) generate a ghost if they die with unfinished business to attend to? Such things may potentially tie into Mummy, what with it's Lucid Dead Numen and such... I'm not sure though.

    Also, whats the thoughts on where potentially a Ghost may Pass On to... Would it be possible that a ghost reaches the realm of Stygia in the Supernal? Or do you think that is where a destroyed ghost may end up, due to the shades and spectres of that realm?
    I tend to think of the Underworld as having a similar purpose to the one in Wraith, as an area to help ghosts pass on and keep the balance of souls moving along, though it's been some time since I've read through Book of the Dead and I'm not sure now really.

    So, what are your opinions on this subject?

      They're very definitely not uniformly souls, much as rumors about ancestor spirits and Lunes in Werewolf don't signify that all spirits are souls.

      Promethean 1e Core includes mention of ghosts of previous Prometheans on a list of story ideas an example ST jots down in its Storytelling chapter, Book of the Dead introduced a mechanic that deposits the Created in the Underworld upon their first death which Matt McFarland's explicitly carried over to 2e as a universal milestone, and nowhere in the last three and a half gamelines I've read has anything come up to indicate that ghosts only come from ensouled beings.

      Dave Brookshaw has also pointed out that ghosts are semi-psychic in nature and that there are supernatural means of making ghosts from people who are still alive.

      Geist's mention of how geister might be something else exploiting a ghost's memories and ties to the mortal world to fulfill an agenda is an aspect I've hooked into the inferred necessity of very old ghosts exploiting younger ghosts to get back to Earth (younger ghosts can still manifest and communicate with the living, after all) and similarly tied to chthonians through similarities between Fae Charlatans and the necessity of vampiric Touchstones.

          As always when it comes to a Geist game, I haven't setteled on any hard and fast metaphysics.
          The mortal Necromancers general believe it is the nothing but the fragmentet memory of the person given unnatural life due to our shared memory of that individual. Really just a thought-form given life purely by our own wills. They partically use this line of reasoning as an excuse for their abusive behavior towards ghosts, but they claim to have reasonable evidence to support it.
          Some Sin-Eaters even agree with them. This is supported by how a persons ghost can change by how society at large percieves him. Think of the ghost of Jack The Ripper, who has become something akin to a god of murder, because thats how society percieves him.

          Other Sin-Eaters believe that Ghosts are literally the soul of the dead person, freed of the body which previously forced the world to remember and appriciate them. Now that they've been deprived of that body, its everyones job to give them a ghostly body with which to sustain themselves long enough for them to move on. Kinda like how you would throw a homeless man some spare change, to get him back on his feet. This is supported by how certain Sin-Eaters and mortal Necromancers can unshackle their souls and wander about as ghostly reflections of themselves, while their bodies lie in a comatose state.

          Some occultists believe a ghost is neither of those things, but rather a persons magical essence freed of the restraints of his body, to take whatever form it desires. This is supported by how certain Awakened Ghosts seem to retain their mystical abillities, and some might even grow more powerful in death than they ever were in life. A Geist then, is merely an occultist merging his own magical essence with an archetypical concept of death, in a gambit to gain an increased measure of controle over said aspect. A Sin-Eater might then be that occultists attempt to reverse his own death, and become alive again. This is supported by the Geister who try to cruelly subjugate the mind of the Sin-Eater and gain full control of the body.

          A few ragged and half-insane people whisper that its actually a cruel parasitic thing from somewhere deep down in the Underworld come crawling up to wear the ethereal essence of the dead person as a shroud. Thats why a newly born ghost mostly repeads its own death or some menial task endless in a cycle. The fragment of the dead persons mind if desperately trying to fight the alien intrusion, clinging to what it faintly remembers it once did or the few painful moments that it remembers that its mind and body were still wholly its own. By this admission, Geister are the next evolutionary stage of this unliving parasite. It has wholly mastered the ghosts etheric essence, and stepped its game up to attempting to overtake a living host. As its power increases, the Deathlords of the Underworld notice the dreadful monster wearing the human skin-suit, and attempts to drag back down below, before its insidious pressence grows too powerful. Thats why a Sin-Eater with Psyche above 5 must spend some time in the Underworld occationally.

          This is mostly how I like to organize my metaphysics in Geist games. Just like the Krewes themselves, the truth should be a mish-mash of differing opinions and labyrinthine half-truth.
          Thats my opinion, anyway.

                          Originally posted by wander View Post
                          Mind explaining what you mean by Infinite Reliquary? Its a term I've not really come across and can't find anything on... Though I admit its been a while and memory fails sometimes.
                          It's a phrase culled from VtR: Mythologies's "The Second Death" section that's stuck with me over the years:

                          The Underworld is not truly a “place” in the sense that most would understand it. It is a kind of Limbo, a vast, perhaps infinite reliquary where ghosts banished from Twilight dwell. Ghosts whose anchors are destroyed, who are exorcised or are destroyed themselves end up in the Underworld. It is not the afterlife, per se: individuals who die and pass on without leaving a ghost behind, or ghosts whose anchors were resolved, allowing them to “move on,” do not go to the Underworld. Where such souls do go is a mystery.
                          I'm not sold on the dead being tools…
                          I use the wording because "chthonians are Differently Conscious and this appears to be reflected in the way that more alien ghosts behave" is wordier and less straightforward. I'm saying the dead are at their base given over to patterns and that the dead among the Underworld are increasingly rendered uniform and stripped of the means to retain individual personality without effort.

                          The more talk about them though does seem to place them as being more astral body taken from a person rather than the person (makes sense that this astral body would get to personify something beyond the person and more based on what purpose it ends up with to, perhaps?). A kind of construction of someone's willpower, in a way (different from the stat, Willpower, you understand). The Underworld to that point seems a place that sucks up those ghosts that would be forgotten. Without anchors to keep them linked to the world of the living, they fall to the Underworld, brushed under the metaphysical carpet, so to speak.
                          And anchors are built on memory, which fades in the absence of sustained attachment. Recall that ghosts with anchors can't enter the Underworld, that many things in the Great Below ask for memories as payment, and that the part of the personality described in 2e as a comforting distraction is showcased and strengthened there.
