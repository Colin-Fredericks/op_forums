                            Two weeks ago was Christmas Day, so there was no game. I do nonetheless have a couple short stories for you relating to the game. One is just a scene with Natalie, Karen and Tatum where we get a look inside Karen Knight’s head. The other is more about Dale, who came up in a previous Sara journal.

                            Intermission

                            Meanwhile, with Natalie

                            Danse Macabre

                            Priests and cannibals, prehistoric animals
                            Everybody happy as the dead come home
                            Big black nemesis, parthenogenesis
                            No one move a muscle as the dead come home

                            The furniture had been moved from the middle of the room. The dim illumination came from by a curly black light bulb, and the sound of the closing lines of Nemesis were not only audible, but you could feel it in your teeth.

                            Karen Knight leaned against the wall, watching her wife Natalie, dance. The Goth thrashed around violently, literally throwing herself one direction than the other. Tatum Gillis sat on a chair near Karen, breathing heavily, wiping sweat from her forehead with a towel. Despite being nearly a decade younger and in a leotard rather than her mistress’s boots, leather skirt and leather vest, she was utterly exhausted.

                            Karen’s mind was full. Natalie and Sara had put themselves in harms way. Sara had died, temporarily. Natalie had means of dealing with the pressure, some better than others. Drugs. Submission. Dancing. Karen not so much. After all, it was not her place to show weakness. The youngest woman in the room was the adult here, and usually she relished the role. Usually.

                            Priests and cannibals, prehistoric animals
                            Everybody happy as the dead come home
                            Big black nemesis, parthenogenesis
                            No one move a muscle as the dead come home

                            Karen did not want to admit it, but she was thankful not to have seen Sara die. Coffee had too, and Noriko. She brushed her nose. Noriko seemed to have everyone’s attention these days. Usually she didn’t mind, but recently she was not sure if it bugged her more Natalie had slept with Doc again, or Noriko. Still, Noriko was hard to hate. Usually.

                            Her wife’s face was blank. Green and Blue looked ahead and Natalie’s aggressive dancing continued. She had asked them to come down with her to dance while Sara needed some time. Tatum had been brave through Cities in the Dust and Shake Your Blood, but Nemesis had proven too much. When Karen saw the song selection she knew she would not be keeping up with Natalie tonight. It probably involved a little cocaine. At least it wasn’t heroin.

                            Priests and cannibals, prehistoric animals
                            Everybody happy as the dead come home
                            Big black nemesis, parthenogenesis
                            No one move a muscle as the dead come home

                            Natalie showed no sign of stopping. Karen blew bangs from her face. It was, at least, enjoyable to watch the danse macabre taking place in front of her. The final strains of Nemesis faded away, and Tatum began to stand, ready to go back out. But then, Temple of Love began to play, much longer than Tatum’s endurance could bear. Ofra Haza’s keening vocals filled the room from beyond the grave.

                            “Fuck This, I am out. Up for a shower.”

                            With the fire from the fireworks up above me
                            With a gun for a lover and a shot for the pain at hand
                            You run for cover in the temple of love
                            You run for another but still the same
                            For the wind will blow my name across this land

                            Karen’s eyes followed Tatum leaving. “See you soon, love.” Then turned back on Natalie. She had slowed down a bit, to match the music, before speeding up again as the song did.

                            Karen thought about getting another drink, but Natalie or Sara might need her. Sucks being the adult sometimes. But a small smile crossed her face as Natalie danced across the room. Her little family was a lot to deal with, but Karen wouldn’t trade them for anything. She’d go up for Sara in a moment, but for the moment the devil in a black dress watched over.


                            Meanwhile, with Sara

                            He was alone. Oh, he could still hear the slapping sounds of his father’s studded belt coming down hard onto his calloused hand, but it was far off and not precisely comforting. As he took step after step into the fetid swamp, the slapping sounds faded and he was fully alone.

                            It was hard to take a breath in the thick air. He moved slowly, wrapping his arms around himself. The swamp was silent, not even bird sounds or buzzing insects to break the isolation, just the wet schlorping noises his bare feet made in the waterlogged ground.

                            He picked up the pace before he heard the sound of another set of footprints, a slithering noise intermittent with the four legged motion. He knew what was coming. He knew he couldn’t escape it, but he had to try, no matter how his lungs burned, no matter how hard it became to drag his feet from the sucking mud, no matter how the tree branches whipped his back and stomach in that same pattern, that same pattern over and over and over again.

                            The large gator snapped at his heels. He bit his tongue to keep from screaming and his foot caught in a root. He hit the ground hard, rolling in the mud. He scrambled forward on all fours, just waiting for the winged monster to swoop down at him. He wouldn’t scream. He wouldn’t...

                            He sat up in his bed, gulping air. His panic didn’t subside as he looked around the small room, not recognizing anything. A small sound escaped him when he realized someone was at the door.

                            “A nightmare, buddy?” The strange man’s voice helped him remember how he’d gotten there. The man had picked him up from the side of the road and fed him dinner as they drove to the nearest town. The boy had fallen asleep in the bed he’d been given, despite his best efforts. It had been a long day of walking after all and his first full belly in a long time.

                            He didn’t respond to the man. It was too late to pretend he was asleep, but still he slowly laid back down and curled up under the covers, his whole body shaking. “It’s OK, everybody has them sometimes. Can I come in?” He paused, waiting for a response that didn’t come. He did notice the boy wince and pulled the covers closer. He frowned. “This is your room now, son, and as long as it is, nobody comes in without your permission. Alright?”

                            The boy frowned, confused. He let go of the covers, remaining still and tense. The man smiled and sat down just outside the door. “You know, when I have nightmares and get scared, I get all warm under my blankets too. It always makes me feel better to be all warm and snug in my bed.”

                            The man kept talking, moving from topic to topic, most of which the small boy didn’t understand. But his voice was warm and soothing, and the man never moved past the doorway, so gradually the boy relaxed. The man sighed when he heard the kid’s breathing move into the regularity of sleep. “Ah, kid, what are we gonna do about you?” He got to his feet stiffly and looked sadly into the room before turning and going back to his own bed.

                            -----------------------------------

                            The boy sat on a seat outside the loud man’s office. He was in there with an old woman, speaking about him. He kicked his feet idly - the seat was tall enough for an adult to sit comfortably - and listened.

                            “Can you believe what that waitress told us? Who could do that to a child, to his own flesh and blood?” The man was incredulous. His tone turned angry. “Leaving him on the side of the road, swamp in either direction. And who knows what else! The poor kid is terrified of people. He hasn't even said a word in the past two days.”

                            The old woman hummed but didn’t say anything. The boy pulled his legs up into the chair and wrapped his arms around them. The anger crackling in the other room drove out the meaning of what he heard from his mind.

                            “Evil! He thinks the boy is evil.” The man’s voice rose to a yell. “What kind of foolishness is that? To be afraid of your own son.”

                            The old woman waited until the man finished stomping around the room, speaking in a measured tone. “He surely only saw his own evil reflected in the eyes of the child.” The man began to speak but cut himself off shortly. The old woman continued. “But only a fool does not fear that which peers from the darkness.”

                            There was a pause. “So, Grainne, you think he is something to be feared?”

                            “He will be.” The old woman’s voice was matter-of-fact. “But he is someone who needs to be loved, right now.”

                            The man chuckled. The boy frowned, confused. He wasn’t used to anger dissipating so quickly. “He has come to the right place then.” The man took a deep breath. “He will be making a home with us then? Provided the boy is willing, of course.”

                            The old woman stood up. “It is your decision, Emcee, of course. He will find a way to the path he needs to take, whatever you decide, but fate has led him to us.”

                            “My decision, of course.” The loud man had a smile in his voice. “The boy will stay, until he chooses a path that differs.”

                            “Very good.” The woman moved towards the door. “I will speak to him.”

                            The old woman walked out of the office and closed the door behind her. She reached out towards the boy and placed a hand on his head. After a moment, he started, realizing he hadn’t instinctively flinched away from her hand. She drew her hand back, slowly and calmly.

                            “You will find a good home here, boy. Trust the one who sees.”

                            He looked up at her inquisitively, head tilted and eyebrows high. She chuckled.

                            “Yes, I am the one who sees.” She held out a hand. “Come, boy. You should meet my granddaughter.”

                            He smiled hesitantly at her hand, unmoving. She waited patiently. Eventually, he reached out slowly to tuck his hand into hers, slipping off his seat to stand up. They walked together out of the trailer.

                            -----------------------------------

                            Tommy stretched, rolling his shoulders, before climbing into bed. It had been a hard day of work setting up camp, but he was a strong 18 year old and well used to that kind of work. After that had been the celebration of the 13th anniversary of his being picked up by the troupe. All in all, it had been a tiring enough day that he was asleep quickly, falling into the deep sleep of dreaming right away.

                            He was alone. He could hear crowds of people muttering indistinctly, a few of the gasps of horror he was so familiar with his face provoking, and some jeering laughter, but they were far off and not at all comforting. As he moved hesitantly forward into the fetid swamp, the faint sounds faded completely and he was completely alone.

                            The air barely moved around him, suffocating and thick. He moved slowly, his hands clenching into fists and relaxing, timed with the beating of his heart that pounded in his ears. His heart was one of only two noises in the wide swamp which was abandoned by animal and insect life alike. The other was the thick wet noises his booted feet made as he pulled them up out of the marshy ground.

                            He slowed to a stop, turning in place to look over the wide expanses of humid trackless wilderness. Right on cue came the slithering, four legged footsteps, always behind him, whichever way he turned. He knew what was coming. He knew he couldn’t escape it, and he had, all of a sudden, decided that he wasn’t going to try. He wasn’t going to run, the fetid air burning his lungs, his feet taking more and more effort to be pulled from the sucking mud. He wasn’t going to charge through the scratching, clawing tree branches, dodging every upturned root but the last one.

                            The large gator cocked its head inquisitively at the still young man. His chest heaved, betraying his nervousness as he looked the winged monster in the eye. It yawned, its mouth opening wide as it moved closer and closer, taking flight off the nearest hillock to glide around him in circles. He kept his eyes on it, waiting for it to strike. When it did, he didn’t scream.

                            -----------------------------------

                            Tommy waited in the shadows, feeling his Horror’s Hunger as it grumbled. He needed to feed, but he wanted to right target, the one he was sure was here. The corners of his lips upturned slightly when he saw his mark, finishing up a soda as he stepped out of his apartment building. Tommy moved out into the street, walking purposefully towards the young man, his head down.

                            The Beast sidestepped at the last moment so that they collided, the force of it spinning them around a little. “Hey, guy, watch where you’re going!” his target yelled. When he caught sight of Tommy’s scaled face, he added in an derisive undertone, “Freak.”

                            Tommy’s eyes darted over the man’s sneering face. This was definitely the one he wanted - he recognized the scar down his cheek and remembered who left it and why. He said a few words in Gaelic, “Tá tú i d'aonar.”

                            The man shoved him away, hard enough that Tommy lost his footing and hit the ground. “You’re in America. Speak English.” He threw his soda can at the teenager and turned to walk away.

                            Tommy winced away from the thrown can, so it bounced off the side of his head instead of his face. He sat up, but shifted over while still crouched down to pick up the can as he watched the man leave. A sinister smile crept across his face as he crushed the can.

                            A few days later, the man was stumbling down an alley. He looked bedraggled, his tie loose and suit rumpled as he glanced around wildly. He heard the swooping noise of a large pair of wings behind him, but when he turned, nothing was there. A clicking noise came from over his shoulder, something like claws tapping on the asphalt. He turned again, sharply, and Tommy was there in front of him.

                            “You! I keep... You’re following me.” The man’s chest was heaving and his eyes widened suddenly with a sudden realization. “You did this to me!”

                            Tommy chuckled, a deep, rumbling sound. The ground beneath the man’s feet grew soft and damp. He took a step back and froze, caught in Tommy’s gaze. His eyes rolled around in their sockets, letting him catch glimpses of fluttering cloth and brightly colored banners. After a moment, that gypsy brat who’d cut his face a couple of years ago danced in the corner of his eyes.

                            Perched on a short wall nearby, Tommy grinned viciously, sharp teeth showing in an impossibly wide smile, when he saw recognition finally appear in the young man’s face. He moved behind the man to whisper in his ear. “You shouldn’t have done that.”

                            The man bolted. Tommy followed, gliding from dumpster to windowsill to wall, circling around his target. Occasionally, he would dive down at his prey, provoking another burst of fear-fueled speed, always letting him stay just ahead of his claws then leaping away, his heavy body sliding through the thick air. Until the last time, when the man collapsed in place, curled up into a ball and quivering. Then Tommy stepped back, slinking further into his swamp, his Horror sated.

                            End Intermission

                                        Session CLXXIII

                                        Sara’s Stories

                                        Sasha -

                                        How about that? Two emails in a row with nothing but good news. We had our meeting with James today, and it went well, all things considered. He seems like an alright guy, dealt a lousy hand. And if he’s not worked with it like a perfect angel, well... that’s a hard standard.

                                        His experiences with Team Tomorrow (we’re presuming) left him with a bad case of PTSD and, as I’ve indicated, a fascination with fire. Combine that with the brain damage they inflicted intentionally, to kill his inborn respect from human life, his own and others, and it makes his story of losing control, of thing getting out of hand believable. And while they’re not an excuse for what he’s done, they’re an explanation, at least. Underneath it all was a good man, a man who wanted to set things right, who wanted to be strong enough to do that, and to protect himself from the ones who hurt him.

                                        Rawl was a kind brush of fate, for certain. There was no question that the two men were brothers - Rawl knew just how to prick and pinch him, to keep him off balance and less twitchy without *just* being a dick. Natalie has a theory that he may have learned his smart mouth skills early, to protect his younger brother by drawing the ire of the kind of father that would send him to a facility to be experimented on. It certainly paints him in a good light, and I do like my rakish good looks with a side of heroism, so maybe I just want to believe it, but regardless, Rawl is good with his words. And apparently with his hands - he brought up a couple of times that the car he drove there in was technically only nine tenths his.

                                        There was a little tension when we mentioned that Miranda had agreed to be healed. I’ll admit, that was a surprise. I figured if someone he knew had agreed to it, that he’d be more comfortable. But Natalie caught an increase of tension there, and figured out that it was cause he was afraid Miranda was orchestrating this - he really is afraid of her psychic abilities. We put on a little bit of a show, to make the point that wouldn’t get very far with us. It was true, but the way I went about it was a little bit of misdirection, so I suppose I do owe Miranda for not pulling aside the curtain, as it were. Not that I mind owing her.

                                        Anyways, Doc got them fixed up as well as he could - the damage to their brain is reversed, and the rest is up to them. Rawl stuck around to talk with James, and Miranda came back to talk to Catherine. Doc offered to discuss things with James, some meditation techniques and the like, and I do hope he takes him up on that. Therapy isn’t right for everyone, and it is certainly more difficult for those of us who are Touched in ways most therapists aren’t trained to understand, as Coffee illustrated with a rather disjointed story that really tested her record for how quickly she could possibly get intelligible words out... But, having someone who understands the kinds of mental and emotional responses you’re having, and learning some coping techniques could be helpful for him. And it’d mean he’s around more, which is fine by me.

                                        I did also offer to help as I can with the Team Tomorrow people, tracking down who they are and figuring out their deal. I’m sure James’ end game is a little less... knowledge based than that, but something should be done. Unfortunately, we don’t have a lot of leads. The facility was shut down and abandoned. They have little to no web presence (Natalie checked). We do know where his dad is, and he might very well know about the place he sent his son to. I would hope so at least. At my most charitable, I can imagine what he knows is all lies, that he was misled into sending him there... but even then, maybe there’s that nugget of truth in there. And realistically, I find it hard to believe the man that has been described to us so far was that naive.

                                        Still, until we can get something like that worked out, we have a bit of breathing room. Tomorrow evening we’ve made plans to meet with Ildefonso - for one, we’d like to take the Underwater Story Weavers up to the park, as I’ve mentioned, and he’d like to meet them. For two, Tatum doesn’t believe us when we talk about how handsome he is, so we’re just going to have to show her. Such a hard life I lead, Sasha.

                                        Sara


                                        Natalie’s Diary

                                        April 20th 2011

                                        Ten Days till Walpurgis

                                        The situation with James and Miranda seems to have been solved. We met them at a café, with Coffee, Noriko (as a kitten) Rawl, and Miranda. James was defensive. I understand. Rawl took point, and while he was a dick much of the time, it appeared to be brotherly banter. Miranda and Sara and I stepped in too. He was cagy, but we did not press him to admit much. He was paranoid, and suffered from PTSD. I don’t think any of that was why he actually committed the crimes, but Doc was willing to let it go. Even with Miranda also agreeing to the process he was reluctant, but we talked him into it.

                                        We went to a little townhouse and Doc fixed both of them. Both seemed a little out of it. Rawl stayed with his brother, we took Miranda back to speak to Catherine. They spoke for awhile and we hung out. We promised James to look into Team Tomorrow and I will. I am not optimistic after I did the digging, but we will see.

                                        Karen has been harping on the women that I like. This is recent. I’ve been thinking about it. She never cared much before, usually. Granted, we were not in a committed relationship. I mean, she did not like Sonia, but no one I date likes Sonia. Doc does not like Sonia.

                                        I still love Sonia. It’s just not a good idea. Bad for both of us.

                                            Sara held two mugs carefully in one hand, using the other to knock lightly on the door. She waited until she heard Tommy’s low rumbling “Come in,” before opening the door and slipping inside. He had been in the middle of taking off his boots, and looked at her with some surprise as she set the mugs down on the desk. “I thought...” He glanced out towards the hall, in the vague direction of the room Sara had told him was her and her wives’.

                                            “I caught that. The comment about being nice?” Sara nodded. “He would have taken me down quickly if you hadn’t recognized me, or had a bad reaction, wouldn’t he have?”

                                            Sara got an exasperated look on her face. “Probably.”

                                            “Good.” Tommy had a serious look on his face and Sara rolled her eyes at him. “No, I mean it. It’s good for you to have someone watching your back. You think too little of your own safety.”

                                            A shadow crossed over her face and she looked sad for a moment before she shook her head and smiled. “So what about the rest of my family here? What do you think of them?”

                                            “Well,” he began slowly, “I have never met anyone whose guilt tasted so delicious.” Sara cocked her head at him questioningly. “I did not see, but I presume it was Alexia who... reacted to my face. Fairly mildly, honestly, but I could see a kind person’s guilt in her eyes as she pushed cookies on me.”

                                            Sara chuckled. “It is her way, to feed everyone, especially in delicate situations.”

                                            Tommy took a sip. “I’m glad I had such good timing, to show up right when there were fresh baked cookies to be offered.”

                                            Her grin widened. “You wanna know a secret?” Now it was Tommy’s turn to look questioningly at her. “She has a stash of ‘emergency cookies’ pre-cooked and kept in such a way that a few minutes cooking makes them seem fresh baked. It comes in handy, especially when peace offerings are needed.”

                                            Tommy nodded understandingly. “Clever and creative.”

                                            They sat in silence a few moments, enjoying the tea. Sara kept her eyes on him, waiting. “So...?”

                                            “Her wife and girlfriend seem nice, though they did not talk much.” His face acknowledged the irony of the statement.

                                            “They’re somewhat more shy than the rest of us, they’ll warm up to you.” Sara smiled fondly. “A problem for time’s hands.”

                                            “Lady Tavia is an excellent host, though I feared for her balance up some of the stairs.”

                                            Sara laughed lightly. “She does enjoy being the lady of the house. And she is tougher than she looks, that one.”

                                            “So she said.” Tommy inclined his head respectfully. He went back to his tea, ignoring Sara’s impatient eyes. After another moment he looked up, unable to completely hide his smile. “Tatum and Coffee are an interesting pair. I think Tatum is more of a kindred spirit?” Sara nodded. “Coffee is, well...”

                                            “Enthusiastic?” Sara suggested. “Would you believe she is from Georgia?”

                                            “Truly?” Tommy let out a surprised rumbling chuckle at Sara’s nod. “I had a thought to ask her how long she had been cursed with silence, to have saved up so many words to use so quickly.

She eventually caught her breath and took his head, lacing her fingers in his. “I believe you would confuse her.” She took a deep breath and squeezed his hand, a more serious look coming over her face as her giggles faded. “Tommy...”

                                            “Need? No.” Sara shook her head, letting her hair fall forward. “Want? Very much. You see true,” she said as she brushed her fingers lightly over his eyelids, “and you know me better than anyone with Gran and my father gone.” She let her hand trail down his face and rest on his neck. “I’m sorry, Tommy. I should have written you. I just... needed distance, at first and then once things... I... settled down, I didn’t know if anyone would want to hear from me, or what to say...” She took in a breath to say something else, then let it out, unsaid.

How have you been, love?” Sara brushed his collar off a little, resting a hand on his neck. “Can Sasha and Emcee spare you for a whole month?”

I don’t think you’ll be in the way though, another pair of eyes is always helpful.” She cocked her head and looked at him. “Would you like to hear the story?” He smiled and nodded, settling into his chair with his mug held in both hands. Sara drew both her legs up onto the desk, sitting tailor style. Her voice took on its storyteller cadence:

                                            “Once upon a time there was a small girl, only six years of age. She lived with her parents in a large house, and lived well, until the time came for her to leave home and go to stay with her aunt. No explanation was given to the child, but her aunt was kind and, having just lost her husband, was welcoming of her company. So she was content with her new life.

                                            “And so she lived for nearly a score of years. Things changed, as they always do. Her aunt remarried, but her new husband was also kind to the girl. She grew up, as all children do. In fact, she was beginning her adult life when another change came, one much less happy.

In her 23rd year, however, she received word that there had been an accident and they were dead. Strange circumstances, indeed - a burnt out boat, a missing lifeboat and two bodies washed up on shore. She had come back to deal with the business of death, such as it was, staying in her old house. Her old bed even.

                                            “From the first night she slept in her old bed, she began to have dreams. Vivid dreams, dreams of menace and death. She dreamed of a woman, somehow familiar, being choked to death by a man. A small girl, only six years of age and similarly familiar, watched and as the murderer turned, his deed done, she fled. Unfortunately, her flight ended as she tripped and fell down the nearby stairs, landing at the bottom with a broken neck. The only thing that wasn’t in sharp detail was the man’s face, which left nothing but an impression of menace.

                                            “The dreams were strong and grew in intensity over the fortnight she slept there. The young woman hesitated over reaching out - she didn’t feel threatened herself by her dreams, and she felt nothing but safety in the house. The feeling of being trapped in it faded as soon as she woke up. But still, with the rising intensity, she was pushed into searching out the ones who know, someone who might discover why she dreamed, and what she dreamed of...”

                                            Tommy set down his empty mug. “So how’s the story end?”

                                            Sara unfolded, shifting so that one leg hung off the desk. “We kicked around some ideas. It might be that she witnessed something and repressed the memory, and this is it coming back. My theory is that she has repressed memories, but of these dreams, not of the murder itself. I think the dreams are visions granted by a ghost, or ghosts.” She sighed. “I’m afraid we’re going to find unfortunate things about her family. Hopefully, they just sent her away because of their travelling, or because she started having disturbing dreams. But the fact that the man is the only non-detailed bit of the dream... Natalie suspects it’s someone she can’t handle admitting is a murderer. Alexia remembered the family name, and there was some kind of sex scandal related to Teresa’s father about 10 years ago.”

                                            “Complicated stuff.” Tommy’s face was hard to read.

                                            “Yeah. And that’s not even getting into her parent’s death. We’re going to be doing some research tomorrow morning before visiting the house and seeing if we can make contact with any spirits that might be there.” She tucked a strand of hair behind her ear. “So, wanna tag along?”

                                            “I’d like that.”


                                            Tommy woke up as Sara shifted in his arms. She whimpered, pulling in on herself and further back into him. She shuddered again. “N...”

                                            “Sara. Sara, mo chroi, wake up,” Tommy said softly into her neck, running a hand down her shoulder. She started awake, taking some deep breaths. “It’s alright, you’re safe. A nightmare?” Sara nodded, and Tommy put his arm around her pulling her close. She placed her arm over his. “Those are my thing. What’s wrong, a gra?”

                                            The dark haired woman was quiet for a moment. “It’s... complicated. Our last job... no, not the last, I mean the one with the stalker, on the college. It didn’t end as well as I let on.”

                                            “Oh?”

                                            “People got hurt.” Sara took another deep breath. “I can’t... It’s complicated.”

                                            Tommy buried his face in her hair, holding her tightly. After a moment, he shifted back a little, reaching up to tuck a piece of her hair behind her ear. “Does it have anything to do with you dying that night in Arlington?” Sara turned around quickly to look at him, a question in her eyes. “I haven’t always known.”

                                            “How?” Sara’s brow furrowed.

                                            Tommy considered a moment as her eyes danced over his face. “Have you met any Begotten, in your travels?”

                                            Sara nodded. “Dulcinea. The red-head Alice met during the Bright Shadows job. She said she was one of the Begotten.” She turned over and levered herself up on one arm, running a finger down his cheek. “You’re...?”

                                            “A Beast. One of the Begotten of the Dark Mother.” He nodded. “And she gives us knowledge of all of the Family.” Sara heard the capitals. “Your krewe - Natalie, Doc, Alexia - they all died and came back, didn’t they?” Sara nodded. Tommy paused, watching her face carefully. “I didn’t know, until I saw you, but...” He smiled slightly. “Your stories do a decent job of masking the truth from those who don’t know it, and any slip ups were covered up by your known penchant for fairy stories. But when you know, once I knew, I hoped, maybe you would,” he looked away, thinking up the right word, “understand. Help me learn.” He looked back at her.

                                            “Of course.” She smiled, and leaned over for a kiss.

                                            When they broke apart, Tommy kept his hand around her waist. “Now, about those nightmares.”

                                            Sara burrowed into his embrace. “I did die, that night in Arlington, but my Geist brought me back. And that night in the radio station, I died again.”

                                            His arms tightened around her. “And the person killed you?”

                                            “I don’t know that she was a person, more a weird machine thing. She’s gone.” Sara sighed into his chest. “She blew herself up, with an odd sort of resignation... I came back, obviously. She’ll always bring me back. But it’s a life for a life, Tommy. The gods don’t allow such things for free.” She bit back tears. “He was just trying to defend his wife.”

                                            Tommy stroked her hair and whispered soft words of comfort, letting her cry. When her breathing became more regular, he asked her quietly, “Feel better?”

                                            “Some.” She smiled up at him weakly but a smile none the less.

                                            “A problem for time’s hands, hm?”

                                            She felt his voice as much as heard it, resonating through his chest. She nodded and relaxed in his arms, allowing herself to drift back off to sleep.

                                                So to summarize what we know, Lauren Gleason was a sitter/nanny for Theresa Redcrow and possibly Emily Turner, though I don’t know for sure. She had an affair with both employers William Redcrow and Angela Redcrow, who did not know about each other. Her friend Felicia Newsome discovered this, and told someone, leading Lauren to beat her up and hit Felicia’s head hard at the foot of the stairs, probably causing at least a mild concussion. Sometime thereafter, Angela was blackmailed (about the affair? Something else?) and Russ Turner, Emily’s father and Angela’s brother, assaulted and strangled Lauren. This was seen by Emily, who ran away and fell down the stairs, cracking her head open in the same spot Felicia hit hers, becoming a Sin-Eater. Russ regretted his daughter dying, but she came back and Lauren was dead nonetheless. Russ believed Lauren was blackmailing Angela, but she was not. She may have realized who was just as she died. Theresa was sent to live with her aunt in Pennsylvania, while Emily was sent to the Netherlands for unknown reasons. Theresa has a happy life in Penn. Lauren becomes a ghost somehow in the Primordial Dream.

                                                William, seeking to avenge his mistress, shoots and kills Steven Turner, Russ’s brother and best friend, who refuses to sell out Russ as the one who killed Lauren. Russ at some point is romantically involved with Felicia, before marrying a girl named Louise. Felicia a few years ago shoots her boyfriend dead claiming he was about to attack, but witnesses say otherwise, so she is now in prison. William is involved in a sex scandal with underlings.

                                                Emily comes from the Netherlands about a month ago and kills William and Angela on their boat in a spectacular and blatantly supernatural act of revenge. She is vague on the finer points of why. Theresa returns to the house and begins having intense dreams of the murder of Lauren and death of Emily, though it has been so long she recognizes neither though both are familiar. Beloved Uncle Russ is replaced in those visions by a Horror that wandered into her house in the Primordial Dream, so the message is lost. Also, Lauren apparently never realized Emily got back up. Russ has visited several times. We don’t know if only Theresa got the dreams, or if they tormented the other Redcrows. Theresa may be connected to the Primordial Dream somehow, I know exactly jack and shit about it.

                                                My jolly band of outlaws enter the fray, having been hired by No Homo Theresa at the advice of Trinh Phillips, a former client and ex to a messy relationship. (Probably best, Trinh does not need someone other girl’s heart to fuck with) We poke around the house in the real world, and then travel to the Dream as Sara’s childhood friend/new lay is a dream alligator, where Lauren yells at us as intruders then the Horror attacks us, so we back out. The investigation continues.

                                                    Sara’s Stories

                                                    “So anything else to do tonight?”

                                                    Alexia, Chandra and Catherine left together, shaking their heads. Natalie, Coffee and Karen left shortly after, with a wink from Sara. She lingered behind, watching Tommy, who was still sitting at the table, looking thoughtful. His nostrils flared, taking in the warm scent of the herbal tea that was warming his hands.

                                                    “Everything alright Tommy?” She took a seat across from him.

                                                    “If he’s... grown up, these past few years. If he’s more under control. Then he gets off scot free? Is that how it works?” He looked up at her, his expression dark.

                                                    Sara raised an eyebrow and took a deep breath. “People can change, Tommy. They can become better.” They sat in silence for a moment. “Killing him won’t change what he’s done and if his place in the universe is now having a positive impact, killing him would only compound the sorrow.”

                                                    After a moment, Tommy looked away. “If he’s changed, I won’t kill him.”

                                                    Sara heard the slight emphasis on the word kill, and saw his eyes glint. She thought of a thousand things to say, and decided on a simple, “Thank you.”

                                                    They sat in silence again, with Tommy subtly watching the rise and fall of her shoulders with her breath. He broke the silence this time. “Is it always this complicated?”

                                                    Sara chuckled. “Not always. The soap opera of love affairs is particularly complex here, and we don’t even know who the blackmailer is yet! Poor Emily - I so wanted to ask if she knew she had a sister. But with how agitated Catherine said she was whenever the Redcrows came up...” Tommy nodded solemnly. “At least Theresa herself seemed to have a positive association for the unfortunate girl.”

                                                    “You feel sorry for her?” Tommy’s face was inquisitive, without judgment.

                                                    “I do.” Sara shook her head sadly. “Six is young to be so immersed in the world of the dead. The brain damage does not help.” She smiled sadly. “The Virgin Sacrifice and I don’t always see eye to eye, but I was a strong willed near-adult when we joined. I can’t imagine how it would feel if the entity I had shared existence with since I was six disapproved of me growing up.”

                                                    “Your Natalie is afraid of her.” Tommy swished the dregs of tea around in his cup.

                                                    Sara looked regretful. “With good reason. She’s powerful and unhinged. Even if it’s just fugues and confusion isn't a code word for psychotic break, she’s dangerous.” The dark haired girl bit her lip. “I wish we knew someone who could be the guardian, guide and friend she needs.” Sara sighed, but after a moment looked up at him, head cocked, realizing something. “You like her.”

                                                    Tommy nodded, shrugging slightly. “I feel like we have a lot in common. Minus the fugues. And the incest. Probably.” He withdrew slightly at the implied mention of his family. After a moment, he gave Sara a strained smile. “She could put on a hell of a light show.”

                                                    Sara groaned. “Oh, the ideas Emcee would have if he knew about out supernatural powers...” She grinned at him. She changed the subject. “The Primordial Dream is interesting. I was distracted last time, but the swamp, at least, is very pretty.”

                                                    Tommy looked down, an embarrassed smile playing over his lips. “I don’t - I don't think your friends liked it as much. Well, except for Alexia.”

                                                    “She was fascinated.” Sara leaned forward over the table, peering into his cup. She glanced back up at him. “I’m glad we didn’t have to fight that Horror. It was a close thing.” Tommy nodded. “I was a little surprised you didn’t crocodile up.”

                                                    “He doesn’t leave our Lair.”

                                                    “Oh.” Sara looked mildly surprised. It was easy for her to think of Tommy as a strange Sin-Eater that just dealt with dreams instead of death. He was family, after all. “I see.” She looked deep in his eyes for a moment. “You are an interesting entity, Tommy Sanger.” As Tommy dropped his eyes back down to his cup, Sara hopped off her seat and circled the table to give Tommy a kiss. “Good night. Sleep well. Know that I love you.”

                                                    As she turned to leave, Tommy spoke up quietly. “What did you see, in the leaves?”

                                                    She turned back, arms akimbo, with a mysterious smile. After watching him a moment, she said, “Forgiveness. Redemption.”

                                                    He didn’t look over at her, but Sara smiled at him anyway, sure that he could see her out of the corner of his eye. When she was at the door, he spoke up again. “Forgiving him won’t change what he’s done either.”

                                                    Sara paused, a hand on the door frame. “But it can change today. Good night, Tommy.”

                                                    “Good night Sara. I love you.”
