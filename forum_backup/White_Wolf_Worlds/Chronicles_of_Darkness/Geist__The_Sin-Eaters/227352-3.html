            Originally posted by Aspel View Post
            They don't have to be antagonists for you to struggle against them. You can still have conflict with your friends.
            Indeed. What if the Geist wants you to go have sex with a random person, and you're just not into those kinds of relationships? What if the Geist wants you to enjoy a saucy steak, but you're on a diet? What if you're a Vegan? What if the Geist wants you to go out and dance like its 1999, but you'd rather enjoy a quiet drink here in the WIP lounge?
            None of these are strictly antagonistic in nature, but still a situation in which your wishes are in conflict with those of your Geist, reasonable though those suggestions might be.
            What about an introverted person who becomes a Sin-Eater? That person might prefer his days to tick by playing World of Warcraft 24/7, but that is hardly the life experience the Geist signed up for. In this situation, the Geist even has the objectively correct idea (Dont play WoW to the detriment of your social life and career), yet the Sin-Eater might not agree. Although those kinds of situations are entirely manageable by a Storyteller winging it, I feel like we need something - Anything! - to mechanically repressent those kinds of disonnance, outside of "Roll Synergy".

                                    When I first read Geist, I skimmedi t like three times looking for where the rules for eating people's sins was. I was rather weirded out that they weren't there.

                                    Another issue is that there is no Masquerade / Lie equivalent. When I first read the book, I thought this was awesome. In fact, I presented to my players as, "And unlike every other splat, you may feel free to tell as many people as possible that you can speak to the dead. If you're really good at it, maybe you'll even get your own talk show." Implying that it was people's general attitude towards the afterlife that created a quasi Masquerade rather than any effort on part of the Sin Eaters. People maintained their own Masquerade and fooled themselves because to do otherwise would be to accept death, which people have a hard time doing. Everyone thought this was pretty cool.

                                    Then it turned out Geist's powers include turning into a flying incorporeal tentacle monster and eating state senators on national television and dropping the same exact old car from a 10 story building. Repeatedly. Out of thin air.

                                    One of these things does not belong. Either a Lie equivalent needs to exist, or Geist powers need to be built from the ground up as "things mortals could explain away if they tried hard enough." The issue with the latter is that its harder to make the powers fun/evocative/cool.
