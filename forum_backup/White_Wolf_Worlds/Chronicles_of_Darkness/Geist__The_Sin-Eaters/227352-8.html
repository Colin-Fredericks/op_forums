    Originally posted by Aspel View Post
    Yeah, but that's the problem; ignoring food has game play consequences. Ignoring the Geist doesn't.

    Synergy is going against your new Underworld centric morality. If your Geist is crazypants and thinks that eating ghosts will make it stronger and that's it's obsession? Well too bad, you become crazy, lose Synergy, and the Geist takes over all because you did what the Geist wanted.
    I think this is where we disagree, and it's the crux of what's upsetting you. I think you're getting too wrapped up in the examples of breaking points and not in the actual description of synergy.

    Imagine you are forced to share your body with a man from a lost tribe that has never seen society. Suddenly you have two very different (alien if you will) viewpoints of the world. Imagine you can't communicate with him very well but he's in control of the body... and he's thirsty. He's thirsty and there's fresh, clear water in the room down the hall. You tell him not to drink that water but have a hard time explaining why. See, he's never heard of a toilet before.

    To him, not drinking out of the toilet is some weird abstract morality that the civilized world brought on: to you it's just a disgusting act that makes your stomach turn. This is what synergy seems to be meant to represent and why we have the suggested breaking points we do. Your example of a crazy pants ghost that wants to eat ghosts is actually the exception to the rule: I'd argue that Geist would either not be against eating ghosts (and would therefore not have that breaking point - like a vampire doesn't have a breaking point for sucking blood) or else would trigger that breaking point because he blames his host for allowing him to commit such a disgusting act.

    Either way, if you stop thinking of it as a hard and fast morality rule and start thinking of it as 'how well your mind is tuned with the Geists', the rest of the book makes a lot more sense.

                Look, if you're going to throw extreme cases at the system until you break it and then complain that its horribly broken, you're going to break every single system. The problems you're pointing out aren't arguably the Sin-Eaters ruleset fault because it quickly becomes negligible when you use a god machine reading. So what if a crazy ghost eating geist breaks the synergy system - a serial killer PC breaks the morality system the same way. You know how you deal with that problem? You don't allow serial killer PCs.

                Specifically, the undeniable intention of Synergy is to be a measure of how well you're able to get along with the strange alien personality that now shares your body. The breaking points you take offense to are meant to represent things it (the vast majority of geists) finds abhorrent - eating ghosts and generally not living. If you want a Geist that wants to do things that Geists normally find abhorrent, you're attempting to play the equivalent to a mass murderer mortal. Before GM, your response to ideas that wouldn't be represented by the rules as written because the authors hadn't considered them would be to reinterpret the rules and try to keep the original intentions in mind. Not to throw the books down in disgust and move on.

                But here's what's really upsetting me about this argument: you're presenting these edge cases as if they are front and center in every game and make Geist unplayable - which simply isn't true. If you demand that everyone use your rigid interpretation of the system to prove your argument, you're sabotaging the argument. By definition the new chronicle book will reinterpret the rules with a god-machine philosophy in mind and, like integrity, synergy will become a bit more flexible to accommodate for the crazy edge cases that storytellers wont want to allow most of the time anyways. I'm sure everyone agrees that the god machine rule set vastly improves the existing rules and, while it may not perfect them, it'll likely make all the systems more streamlined.

                So lets focus on things a new chronicle book wouldn't by definition fix - things like how we'd like Krewes to change, or the overall ambiguous nature of a lot of the npcs in Geist. What do we actually love about Geist and what could use some looking at that isn't explicitly going to be fixed by the new ruleset? What constructive criticism can we give to whitewolf to improve on Geist?

                        In the spirit of the thing, some foundational bits of hope:

                        That the state of the Underworld's overarching nature be made more clear - the focus on subterranean visuals tends to lead the mind away from the more unusual vistas like those Book of the Dead mentions (dense dead woods, flame-spewing metal Doom corridors, narrow bridges that drop into a seemingly bottomless abyss, etc.) - basically, emphasis on the Underworld as the Big Black over the Literal Giant Cave Network; it leaves space for things to happen in, further justifies the general shallowness of charted maps, allows for some ambiguity in mapping the borders of the Dead Dominions, and hooks nicely into the bit from Vampire's Mythologies about how there might be several Underworlds.

                        That the ways in which ghosts can advance in Rank within the Underworld receive some attention, factoring as those things are likely to into things like powerful geister representing multiple types of death and the metric by which ghosts might be drawn deeper into the dark now that age is no longer the primary measurement of ghostly potency.

                        That the Kerberoi receive a consistent portrayal in terms of role and cognizance that makes it clear their proxy-agency in the sunlit lands is a thing that happens with something resembling notable frequency and builds on the trend loosely set by the Triptych and the Clockwork Hive of the Dominions as creatures who -with some regularity - develop an agenda above and beyond the scope of their duties as the executive branch of their slice of Tartarus over time. (Things like the fate of the Scissorman or the story seed from Glimpses of the Unknown about Kerberoi going to war and what this means for the victor are also worth some focus, I think.)

                        That ghostly Twilight be properly outfitted with inanimate elements besides particularly morose specters - the unquiet dead have a handicap on the amount of evocative imagery that can be brought to bear in their slice of Twilight thanks to the Rank-hobble, so things that aren't there in the solid half of the land of the living deserve some emphasis, particularly if this can be made to tie into the way lost places and objects sometimes find their way into the Underworld.

                                There are a few things I'd like to see a little different in Geist, though I find the game as is more or less just fine (I'll touch on the big Synergy debate here at the end of my post)...
                                One thing fluff-wise is to reverse the power where a Sin-Eater can see a person's age... I'd like to have it as a kind of timer hanging over people's heads where a Sin-Eater can literally see when that person is due to die. I think you could do alot with that power and there could be some tie-in to the God-Machine's Infrastructural plans... Imagine this: You as a Sin-Eater spot a person who has about a minute left on the clock... They walk out into the road and a car is gonna slam into them when all of a sudden they get saved by somebody (its a Demon or an Angel due to Fall, but you probably don't know that) and all of a sudden, their clock boosts a couple of years before counting down again... Thats a story arc right there as this is likely something that you've never seen before.

                                I think the Pass-On ceremony should be an inate ability also... It seems to me (YMMV of course) that it's pretty much a compulsory buy unless your Archetype is more focused on dealing with the living, such as the Celebrants or the Pilgrims.

                                Synergy: My take is this is how united you are with your Geist. The Geist is an unknowable near eldritch abomination despite it's origins... It has a weird link with death and aside from it's clear Influences and what it wants from their Bound host (need a better word than 'host', as the two are basically joined by the soul and the Bound has general control, it doesn't fit in my minds eye for the relationship the pair have) some things will likely erk it. Destroying Fetters (remember in G-M rules ghosts possess via fetters now) is a sin against Synergy 7... Generally messing up the natural order of death or trying to renege from The Deal drops Synergy. Synergy dropping leads to mental issues and also brings the Geist closer to saying 'To heck with it, I'mma just gonna take you over now'.

                                The Geist isn't there to snatch control away though (until it decides enough is enough at Synergy 0 of course...) The Deal is that the Geist is bringing the Sin-Eater back, to feel what life is like and live it. The Core gives examples of Geists wanting to do things people generally wouldn't do and not acting normally... If it came up and was told to live with no viewpoint or reference, it'd likely wreck it's body rather quickly. It seems by the crunch at least perfectly happy to sit back and let the Sin-Eater do their thing, living life for the pair of them. The Sin-Eater also has leverage on the Geist with their Keystone, so it's not immediately gonna mess round with the Sin-Eater until pushed into it when Synergy drops enough. Its only when Synergy is out that the Geist itself reneges on The Deal, even though it can be malicious and ressurrect a Sin-Eater when it promised it wouldn't. The system of Max Synergy dropping along with the point drop could represent the Geist being annoyed at the Sin-Eater ending up dead... The Deal is to live after all...

                                Perhaps a rename to something representing the level of patience the Geist currently has with their Sin-Eater would be better?

                                    After some thought, I've decided what I think would help the Geist books the most. I may do up a houserule document for this...

                                    I want the Chronicle to be called the Cthonian chronicle - and I want it to detail new rules for building ghosts, Geists and Cthonians - basically a handbook to twilight creatures. Unlike vampire, we don't need a new creature to fear, just a better description of the ones we have.

                                    To be more specific - something I intend to do for my game is document some abilities that ghosts, Geists and Cthonians might manifest in their twilight forms that they cannot show to the average mortal. I'm going to pull inspiration from Demon in writing these: monstrous forms that will make physical combat with a yellowbones a scary proposition if you have a form in the twilight. I'm thinking things straight out of beetlegeuse - monstrous ghost worms, or deformed creatures with claws instead of hands and eyeballs on stalks. Big ones, fast ones, tough ones - think of Demon's true forms but without any sign of technology.

                                    Then I'm going to add rules to differentiate a ghost, a Geist and a Cthonian. It'll likely differ based mostly on powerlevels but I'd really like to give a reason why a Geist might actually want to be careful around a particularly old ghost - call it yellowbones and tread lightly. Rules for Geists are a bit easier - they've already got Keys and manifestations to use so their changed physical form will likely mostly be for looks when it comes to combat. Cthonians will be the ones that truly need to be alien - it'll be a challenge to give examples of them without defining them.

                                    This has actually got me a little excited...
