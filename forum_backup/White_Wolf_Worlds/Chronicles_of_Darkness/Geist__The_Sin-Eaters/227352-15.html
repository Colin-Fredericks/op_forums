    Yes, but we apparently took away different meanings-having to get used to creepy stuff is considerably less sucky than having to eat people to survive or having literally the entire world wanting to kill you, me thinks, and making it suck bad enough that the majority of Sin-eaters would have legitimate reason to angst about their condition would seriously detract from the gameline's celebratory tone.

    As for the deal" Hey, you died. I can bring you back to life, but you have to let me piggyback in your soul so I can experience what it's like to be alive again."
    "I don't know"
    "you get super powers out of the deal"
    "sold!"

    It's a deal. Even fairy bargains tend to tell you the details, even if it's in a rhyme or in wording that makes it unclear. The vary nature of the Bargain as a bargain means at least some details have to be shared, and it's in the geists best interest to make the mortal see that the deal is better than dying. For that reason, incidentally, it is logical to conclude that exceptionally monstrous, insane, or otherwise inhuman geists don't get hosts very often, simply do to not being able to communicate why taking the bargain is better than dying.

    Creepy sometimes is not enough to say that being a sin-eater is bad. Sin-eaters arguably have it the best of any splat right now, what with having no reason, by default, in general, to angst about being a sin-eater. Anything they could angst-angry ghosts, and the like, is external to the sin-eater. They'd have the same problems with the ghostly version of the unseen sense merit.

    And again, an alien intelligence whose best interests include you being happy and healthy, that you willingly agreed to let piggy back into your soul, that can't control your body unless you die at least seven times, that you are the dominant partner in the relationship with... Might get creepy sometimes, but considering that you let the spooky alien intellect into your soul willingly and doing so brought you back to life, you'd kind of have to be the kind of person who'd complain about anything to angst about that.

        You're right, its a bargain. It's The Bargain. But as it is, it feels more like The Gift. What are you giving up for your second chance? What could compel you to refuse it? With the exception of a few types of hunters (and arguably Demons), Sin-eaters are the only splat to choose to become a supernatural. Everyone else is generally a victim of circumstance. Literally every Sin-eater is a Sin-eater because they chose to be one. That's huge. But it loses a lot of its emotional power if its just this blind, snap decision without any real consequence. With the way The Bargain works now, all I know about a Sin-eater is "They were dying." If that deal becomes a more weighty thing then it automatically informs the base nature of every Sin-eater. So rather than "They were dying." you get something closer to, "They want to be alive that badly." Look at how we view, say, a drowning person grabbing a lifejacket versus someone going through chemotherapy.

        Every Sin-eater should absolutely know what they're getting into, and what they're getting into shouldn't be always be pleasant, because that just means that what they want is that much more meaningful. It's the difference between "not dying" and "living."

                I agree with Soulgambit that any splat referred to as Sin Eaters should have something to do with eating sins (or at least something that would inspire the notion in ages past).

Still, keeping with the overall themes of the World of Darkness, it is perfectly reasonable for Sin Eaters to have a celebratory outlook on life overall while still being urged to commit (and committing) some potentially atrocious acts on occasion.
