            This may have been covered earlier in the thread, though I see some mention of there being really no downside to the Bargain a Sin-Eater enters into as is in the rules. Perhaps not at first glance, but when you look into it... There are specific things that make a Bound a walking target for ghosts... They can interact more clearly with ghosts than normal mortals and as such, ghosts are going to hound them to have them help the ghost with their unfinished business or bother them in some way. If the ghosts knew that Bound blood can rejuvenate them into an echo, then the Bound is going to find themselves being attacked by ghosts to get to that plasm-enriched blood.

            Also... I think it'd be hard for a Bound to find connections in the mortal world once they've received their Geist... They can see when a person is going to die and their general health conditions... Meeting someone you really like and seeing you have 'this much in years' time with them can be somewhat... morbid. But nope, you don't K.O until the reaper comes calling.

            I don't think Bound are all Blessed with Awesome with no downpoints at all. Of course having more mechanical ways to represent these downs with the ups would be great.

                        Originally posted by wander View Post
                        I don't think Bound are all Blessed with Awesome with no downpoints at all. Of course having more mechanical ways to represent these downs with the ups would be great.
                        Blessed with Awesome is the selling point of Geist, though.

                        Each game, after the main trio, departs from the standard WoD core themes in at least one way, which makes it's thematics distinct

                        Prometheans are allowed to have a happy ending, even if they have to work their stolen asses off to get it: Hell, it's even got rules right inthe core book.

                        Hunters flip flop the setting: You're not the creatures who go bump in the night, bringing darkness to the world even if you're good intentioned, you're a relativly normal human bumping back, serving as candle in the darkness even if you're intentions are less than pure.

                        changeling is another flip flop: what in other games would be a sticky end is just part of your backstory: You've already lived through the worst there is, so the only options are heading up, or not moving on at all.

                        Mummy reverses the power scale: You're not a new anything: You're an ancient and powerful being. You don't fear you're elders, you fear losing your powers and being forcibly reclaimed by the sleep that robs you of your memories.

                        In most games, nobody has any clue how crap works. In Demon, you've got a pretty good idea, because you used to be part of the machine that maintained the world, and mos tof your rivals and allies are the same.

                        In Upcoming Beast, instead of the isolation of the other gamelines, you've got a huge family themes, to the point where cross splat interation and friendlyness is both encouraged and being built into the system

                        What makes Geist Different from the Blue Book and the Big Three? Most o fthe other splats, it sucks much bad to be one. The Bad always, in the long run, outways the good unless you work your ass off to avoid the bad. With Sin-Eaters, the good ultimately outweighs the bad: Given the choice between being dead or being alive, you got to choose alive. To sweeten the deal, you've got some fucking sweat psychic powers and a head guest who has it in their best interest to keep you happy and healthy. Yeah, ghosts can tell you can see them and will try to make you help them, but you get great rewards for helping them move on and you can always ward your pad to keep them out. and Yeah, sometimes you're head guest can be annoying or have disturbing requests, but unless it's an unusually crazy one, you're a spineless bastard, or you've died a lot, you can negotiate it down to something better or just tell it to shut the fuck up.

                        The World sucks, nothing has any meaning to it, nothing lasts, and you're all going to die someday, but that day is not today. The closest thing to dying you experienced only made you stronger.

                        You're alive, and that's something to celebrate, so pull up a chair and crack open a beer.

                        Lack of Sin-Eater suckage is a feature, not a bug. It's what makes it distinct from the other game lines.

                                                            I dont think you're entire wrong in your acessment, but I think you might be forgetting that contrast is what ultimately causes emphasis.
                                                            We dont determine a games impact by where the line is, but by how much it wavers. As such, if we have no "Life kinda sucks" emelemts, the impact of the "life is good" thematic will be entirely lost, because thats just setting a conssistently high horizontal line. It'll have no impact.
                                                            Conversely, if you take away the "life is good" thematic, the "Life kinda sucks" thematic will be weaker for it, because thats also just a horizontal line, set at a different altitude.
                                                            You need contrast because of this.

                                                            Also, a game where "Everything is good and life is awesome" might be a very unique World of Darkness game, but it wont be fun or interesting. What interesting stories are you going to tell in a game like that? My own life is allready kinda good and everything is allready kinda awesome, so why would I interested in a game that portrays that, but with a supernatural twist? I make the assumption here, that peoples whoose lives are shitty and sucks, have better things to do than play tabletop roleplaying games, of course...

                                                            Interesting stories rise from adversity, and I honestly think its alittle derailing to call this internal adversity "angst". Honestly, the very principle and core of Geist is that you have another person living in your head! I get extreamly fed up with my room mates, and I dont see them 1/10 of the time I would be forced to put up with a Geist. How is having another person living in your head, not something that lends itself to a bit of internal adversity?
                                                            Sure, the good should still outway the bad, I agree on that. After all, having a dude live in your head is still better than dying, but it certainly wont be fun all the time, even if the dude is lovely.
                                                            Of course you could always make the arguement that such can be handled by storyteller fiat on a case by case basis, but the thing is, if there isn't any concret systems to it, it gets forgotten.

                                                            As I said, saying; "I want Geist to be a darker game" would be missing the point. Saying; "I want systems for the thematic elements that was allready part of the game, but was left out of the mechanics of 1ed, for some obscure reason" certainly is not.
