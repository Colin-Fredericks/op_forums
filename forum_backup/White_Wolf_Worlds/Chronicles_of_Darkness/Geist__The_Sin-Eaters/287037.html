    My question is are Sin-Eaters capable of fulfilling this role? Or am I better off picking up a different game Archetype instead? If so, suggestions on either how to build the Sin-Eater or which other Archetype I should pick are greatly appreciated.

              Depends on the scenario. Sin-Eaters can be great informants and has a wide variety of ways to solve a number of diverse issues and disable enemies with curses and phantasms. However, in a combat scenario, a Sin-Eater does best when he is killing things or preventing things from killing him. He cant place his Shroud on his teammates like a Mage might and the Curses he can place are more annoying as long-term issues than immidiate threats. While he is able to heal his allies, he doesn't do it quite as effectively as a Mage might, nor is his phantoms as effective at way-laying people in the short-term.
              Honestly, if you're gunning for a support role in terms of a combat scenario, a Mage would be the way to go. Preferably something the lines of Mind/Space/Prime/Fate.

                      Outright murder of someone helpless is an automatic Synergy drop. Sin-Eaters do not lose Synergy from killing almost any other time, unless it is completely accidental or purposeless. So they are among the few who can kill all they want without losing Morality unless the subject is quite helpless or they have no reason.

                      Mages do make better "Support" characters, but that does not limit you to them. The question is what are you looking for as "support"

                                  Sin-Eaters have several options for those kind of support roles, and can do a few of them very powerfully, but they kind of become a lot more specialized than other templates do. Starting characters only get 3 dots of manifestations (which are pretty expensive to purchase up), and the super-useful abilities tend to be unlocked at 2-4 dots. Three dots in Oracle [particularly with the Stigmata, Industrial, Phantasmal, or an Elemental Key] can get tons of information and bonuses to Social rolls or using objects, etc - but that's about the only trick a starting character will have.

                                  Boneyard, I think, has a lot of the power's you're looking for in a support-style role. With enough dots and the right keys you can turn your allies invisible, harass enemies with illusions, learn all of the details of a location, toss objects around... It has the limits of only being a static radius around you, and you remain comatose (thus vulnerable) while boneyard is active, but it offers a wide range of "haunting effects" across a large area.

                                          Incidentally, the problem with that rule is it is tight and vague at the same time. It's in one place and not in Synergy (which makes more sense for it) and goes somewhat against how Sin-Eaters are discussed (Angel of Death being a viable concept in theory). Conservatively, killing anyone who does not attack you first or you kill accidentally (a Synergy check, by the way) fully heals you and automatically removes synergy. Liberally, it has to be murder, so you essentially don't apply it to people if you can justify it somehow. The middle road seems to be if you use it to kill some helpless person it counts, but "I am the winning combatant" does not apply. It IS something you want to discuss with your GM, but I would say any deliberate killing at all makes it very difficult and would be something discussed in the book. After all, say Diablorie which is generally an automatic loss is discussed repeatedly as a major sin and has that backing it up. But Sin-Eaters as angels of death is treated casually, and that is not even referenced outside that section. Ectophagia is mentioned in worse terms, and it is a mid-chart roll. One HR (and it is an HR, not interpretation) I saw several times when it came out was if you deliberately kill someone and decide to take the heal, it is a Synergy loss while if you kill someone and don't, it is not a loss. I am mid-road to liberal, so never implemented it as it never seemed necessary. That said, given my druthers, I might make that HR the way it worked in a hypothetical 2e depending on how Synergy-as-Integrity worked.

                                          In addition to that rule, discuss Plasm with your GM. Plasm can be hard to come by if you are not regularly resolving ghosts. In 1.0, haunts give 1-5 per week, in 1.1 per night, it is good to know which way your GM is going there. Also consider your Threshold's way of getting it back. But Plasm is harder to come by than, say, Glamour or Vitae.

                                          Boneyard, Curse, and Pit are excellent for Support in terms of surveillance and impairing foes. Oracle is very useful for info gathering. Marianette is useful for dealing with ghosts, making zombies, manipulating people, or similar. But don't forget your Ceremonies! Sin-Eaters get real flexibility from those and some are very useful! Also very ghost focused, but you know.
