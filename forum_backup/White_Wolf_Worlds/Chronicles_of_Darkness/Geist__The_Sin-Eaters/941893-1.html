                Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
                Remember that the "[thing] Chronicle" is more about something that defines the line than just being a threat; Mage was the Fallen World Chronicle and Promethean was the Firestorm Chronicle.

                Ideally, a Geist 2e would be built along the lines of a Krewe Chronicle, with more effort put into building your krewe's mythology and growing together as you all increase in power and understanding of death. My old group had the headcanon that Tier 3 krewes ascend beyond our reality to fundamentally alter the nature of the Underworld, and while that idea need not be stolen, I love the concept of doing archaeology on the krewes that have come before. Give the Twilight Network more legs and toss some example krewes out there, as Sin-Eaters seem to communicate with each other much more than the original book suggested.
                personally, I don't think that Geist's 2nd edition would feature something like "Krewe Chronicles". I don't mean that there shouldn't be a lot of emphasis on krewes in 2e- there should- but usually, the "X Chronicles" thing is something which both clash and reinforces the themes of the gameline. Each splat has a tendency to circle around a certain refuge from the darkness- until something force it back in, showing that there is nowhere to horror of the world. Vampires play sophisticated political games against each other, when a strix come and break all the rules. Werewolves hunt as they must, when an enemy which is not recognized by their old traditions rise and being chaos to both flesh and spirit. A mage is busy in her occult research and is on the verge of a breakthrough- when her magical senses starts tingling, and they won't let go until she'll investigate the new mystery. A promethean is in the middle of his pilgrim when the Fire descends and the world change, forcing him to change with it. Changelings manage to re-create their small place in the world, until a huntsman come and tries to drag her back to a mad story of beautiful madness. Hunters follow a series of cruel killings, and as they expect to find a monster they find a person- crazy and twisted, but still a person. Even regular people, who are just on their way to live their small and regular life, may stumble upon a strange machinery, and since that moment their life just won't be the same.

                Each game has a story to tell, and the featured Chronicle is meant to help you tell it- it shakes things up and change the status que, throwing the characters into a situation not well understood and which could change everything in their small corner of the world. Suddenly, vampires are reminded of their own personal horror, werewolves discover why they must hunt, mages discover the price of their own hubris in search for truth...

                the question we need to ask is this- what would make a sin eater remember that it is a game about Second Chances?

                                                    Personally, the concept of Krewes as described in 1e never really grabbed me. For one, I agree with the general consensus that the mythology aspect was just... weird. For being the splat's equivalent to Court, Covenant, Order, etc. it felt... tacked on. Like instead of developing even a general Sin-Eater society, the book just left it up to the players. I mean, that's not a terrible idea in and of itself. A system for creating your own organizations along the lines of a Covenant with knobs and levers to build the benefits thereof based on the group's common beliefs would make for a great supplement, but it just felt odd and out of place in Geist. I mean, I know such a subsystem must have taken a lot of time and effort to build, but its inclusion as a core part of the game, to me, came off as, "We didn't have time/interest in building a 'Sin-Eater Culture.' Here, you do it." I'm sure that's not the intent, just what it felt like to me. It felt like getting a Vampire core book, and in the section about Covenants it saying, "Vampires often organize into sociopolitical factions based on common ideals, goals, and mythology. We're not going to give you any examples of what those are or how they interact with each other. That's for you to do. Thanks for paying for this non-content!"

                                                    Again, I know that couldn't have been the intent.
