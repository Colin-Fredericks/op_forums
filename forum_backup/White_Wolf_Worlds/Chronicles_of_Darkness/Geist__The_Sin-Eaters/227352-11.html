    Originally posted by SunlessNick View Post
    I would favour this as a major difference between the Depths and the Dominions. As in, keep the cave network (with occasional underground lakes, and the twisted underground structures close to the Gates) for the Depths, and go into the alternatives in most of the Dominions, to the point where some Sineaters might see them as more like parallel dimensions accessed via the Depths than all part of the same world.
    My main point is that it would be preferable to see the Underworld framed in such a manner that the default assumption of "what's on the other side of this wall?" be "empty space that might contain something unusual, from artifacts to bound monsters to a whole other otherworld" rather than "rocks, I guess."

    There's existing tidbits of this like the occasional sound of traffic and construction equipment or the toxic gases and other hazards that keep one from getting the best look at a given stretch of tunnel, but it bears emphasis that, static maze or not, Tartarus is in large part an unknown quantity. More than a few of the more esoteric antagonists could stand to benefit from having the Great Below described in terms that help readers imagine ways in which they might influence the living and the dead - the prison is porous, after all, and chthonic acoustics carry strangely between worlds.

            Have a look at page 274 and 275. The book explains that once a ghosts purpose is resolved it is free to pass on of its own free will - a sin eater doesn't necessarily have to do anything. This looks contradictory but note that the ritual specifies the ghost has resolved all his anchors. I'm not sure if this was a mistake on the writers part (you could ask for clarification I guess) but my interpretation is this:

            An anchor is a ghosts connection to the world, while the purpose is why it remained. An example might be that the ghosts purpose is to get vengence for his murder - in which case getting that vengence would resolve the ghosts issue and send them packing. A more noble ghost might decide that this isn't enough and have a purpose of stopping that sort of murder from ever happening again - its purpose is now too broad to simply resolve short of wiping out the entire race. Anchors on the other hand are specific connections to the mortal realm - A beloved toy or the place that the ghost resided maybe. These are places that the ghost has some specific connection to and provide a way for the ghost to continue to attempt its purpose. If these are gone the ghost is forced into the underworld - where it can either give up on accomplishing its purpose or hope to find some other way to form a new anchor.

            The ritual then is how you force a ghost with no anchors to move on even though its purpose isn't truly complete. It clearly hasn't stopped anyone from ever getting raped again, or helped everyone who's lost in the woods get home safely: but since it no longer has a way to connect to the mortal realm there's not much point in trying. It's done everything it could and its time to move on. The ritual is essentially the assisted suicide for ghosts that a few might be interested in. Most however will choose to fight on for the slim chance of finding a new way to resolve its purpose - perhaps by convincing sin eaters to do it.

                    Instead of talking about it, I'll just post this instead:

                    Originally posted by Me
                    The corridor is a short one, at least as far as long curling tunnels to the realm of the dead are concerned, slopping downwards. The Witch leads the group through a doorway that looks like you'd find in an old access tunnel, though the letters and markings are in strange sigils. On the other side of the door there's an actual stairwell, made of stone and rusted iron, and with pipes that seem to pulse. At the bottom, the door opens on to more earthen tunnels. There's a noise of commotion and of all things life and after a while the flashlights go off as a glow comes from around a curve. And just around the bend the tunnel opens into a cavern...

                    The cavern is rather large, with the ceiling fading off into darkness. The top half of it looks like a cave should, with stalagmites coming down from out of the darkness, dripping water every so often. Sometimes a strange pale batlike creature will flutter around from the dark. But below that is a different story. The tunnel they come from leads out into a rundown subway station, at the top level. Lights flicker, but they aren't electric, they're little bugs trapped behind the glass. People mill about, living in little sheds and hovels, all of them glowing a pale greenish white. In the distance there's the sound of something running along the rails, but there are people--ghosts--down on the lower level with their little plywood huts right on the tracks without a care. Strips of mostly faded cotton and gemstones decorate their houses, some with smears of dried blood or flaking paint. There is a lot of colour here, amongst the grey, even if most of it is old.

                    All in all, there are perhaps hundreds of them living--in a manner of speaking, at least--down in this place, most down on the bottom level, but some talking up where Sabrina and Tacitus are. There's a rumbling in the distance, and the sound of pistons.

                    "Ah, 8:15, right on time," Sabrina murmurs as the ceiling shakes and drips more. Some of the strange white bat creatures fly away like birds startled out of a tree as a giant millipede runs across the top level track. It has an almost human mouth and it makes an unnerving high pitched whine as it rushes by, thousands of legs pumping in a wave motion across the raised track. The head disappears into the tunnels, but the legs keep going for almost a minute until getting to the end, which looks like the back end of a subway car, only with oversized human eyes instead of windows. After it's gone, it's like it was never there, and the bats start to fly back to their roosts.

                    Heedless of the monster, Sabrina just leans against the rusted iron railing as it passes. "Tacitus Lupo," she says with a bit of a grin, opening her arms to motion to the strangness around her, "welcome to the Autocthonous Depths' Little Halicarnassus Station. The first last stop on your tour of the Underworld."
                    The Dominions, though? Those are vastly different. Some even have their own strangely shining suns, like the washed out reddish sepia of Dead Man's Hand, with it's Martian sands and warm, dry weather. Or the strange Egyptian city of Akar Hal, which had a night club, because I'll admit I panicked and just threw something in there. Or the Forge of Orcus, with it's massive ironworks. The Depths have their own strangeness, beyond just being rocky caverns, but it's the Dead Dominions where "Something is uncomfortably uncanny" goes to full blown "We're not in Kansas anymore".
