                    I think I see the problem. You've failed to roleplay this out and you're using extremely non-realistic extreme examples.

                    First of all, the revelation of the channel is not a Geist only thing from how I read it. It's a combination of a discovery of how the universe works combined with how the Geist/Sin-eater interpret it. If the end result is that the Geist is Santa Clause, their beliefs before the fact damn well better reflect that it was a possibility in some way. If I was the storyteller of a game where the Krewe decided that one of the Geists was Santa or something ridiculous I'd demand they roleplay it out and give me a damn good explanation. What's happening is the characters are getting new information regarding how the universe works and revising their religious views, not being brainwashed into thinking up is down. They would never come up with something one of them thought was ridiculous because it's their own interpretation of it.

                    By the way, I should mention that even if your characters are athiest, they still have a religious view. It's the idea that even if you refuse to choose, you've still made a choice.

                    Second is the idea that it's an immediate transformation because that's how the rules play it out. It's just like leveling: yeah, on paper it happens immediately, but realistically the Krewe is having the revelation and probably spending some time testing the limits and revising their opinions.

                    So, as I said before, we could all benefit from a better example. Allow me to provide one.

                    Tom is in a Group and they decide it's time to officially make it a Krewe: they've learned the ritual and have three Sin eaters who wish to be joined. Tom is an Athiest, Cindy is christian and Zach is a Buddhist.

                    Out of character, they decide to come up with three channels that they can agree on: Suffering, celebration and balance.

                    Tom chooses celebration since he believes religion is a lie used to manipulate people, and they should drop it and simply enjoy life. He believes thanks to his time as a sin-eater that there are beings that are truly more powerful out there (perhaps even god-like) but, like him, they simply exist and he owes them nothing and should spend his life living it for himself. His Geists channel is not the embodiment of Athiesm however: it's the embodiment of independence, free will and occasionally rebellion.

                    Cindy chooses suffering: she believes there IS a higher power and that we should live our lives for him or her. Her Geist is also not the embodiment of Christianity: it's the embodiment of hard work, dedication and fighting for a greater cause, in slight opposition to Tom's.

                    Finally, Zach will represent balance: believing that there's a time and place for both suffering and celebration. His Geist represents balance, peace and tranquility.

                    Now, out of character the players have come to an agreement: their in character religions are not the same to be overly specific, so instead they decide to come up with something sensible: the Geists discover they are aspects of the Judges: the ones that wrote the old laws of the underworld and are divinely mandated to judge the living and dead. Their creation myth is that the universe came into being when a great surge of power caused creation. Other options would be that the universe always existed and is simply self perpetuating. Or maybe they get more abstract and say the universe was once a very simple spirit: the only thing that existed. It became sentiant and the paradox of its existance caused it to splinter into three main ideas: Suffering, Celebration and Balance. These three entities set about building the universe as we know it out of themselves in order to discover the solution to the original paradox of its existance. Knowing what they know about the universe, the players agree that whatever they chose fits in with their characters belief systems.

                    The end result: the Geists are more or less who they were before, but with a slight tilt to their sense of existence. It also gives the storyteller new themes to work with: perhaps their in an unbalance in the world and the SEs have to investigate why a town is suddenly too much balance in South Dekota, or not enough suffering in the underworld.

                                    I think I'm with Satchel on this, and I like how Baron put it, but I feel it needs more explanation. As an aside: Aspel, I think you're allowing your personal views of religion seep into your opinion. If you are unable to handle the religious aspects of Geist, I recommend you don't try: but don't phrase it as 'fixing the system for everyone' if you do. It tends to annoy people who know the system a hell of a lot better than you do. Anyways, to the heart of the matter:

                                    Originally posted by Aspel View Post
                                    Whether your Geist says that it's Santa Claus or Marqui de Sade, though, the fact that the channel is something that is verifiably false within the setting--I once again point out the Lucifuge saying the Ghostwalkers are deluded--bothers me. It isn't something like the Atlantis myth or the origin of the Lineages
                                    Here's where you get it wrong: it's not verifiably false. It's just an interpretation of reality from someone who, although they might be a little unhinged, has seen a lot more of the picture of reality then you have. Think of it like this: Understanding reality is a giant puzzle, and you don't even have a fifth of the pieces. Humanity comes up with science principles and understandings to piece together the pieces it does have and assumes the finished product is all there is... then someone becomes a sin eater and finds a few more pieces (the underworld and ghosts) - they suddenly believe something that is - according to you - verifiably false. But slowly, they accept this and come up with ways to justify connecting it to the puzzle pieces humanity is pretty sure we've put together.

                                    Then one day, the Geist comes out and reveals some more of the puzzle pieces that the sin-eater needs to know - just a bit, and distorted by our narrow human perspective. Hell, they might not even be connected to any of the pieces you've put together so far as part of your understanding of the universe. To make things worse, you can only interpret it based on your personal perspective of the universe: so the Geist puts it into terms the Sin-eater can understand: 'I am an aspect of Michael the Angel, here to guard the gates'. Or 'I am the Marqui de Sade - here to push boundaries and teach people to celebrate life'.

                                    And of course different people react differently: some take it literally and assume that their Geist is the one and only Santa Clause. Others might be willing to interpret it in a more abstract sense (he is an aspect of a force of the universe that is personified in human lore as an Angel of God sent to guard the gates of heaven - whatever that actually means is still beyond my understanding.) Some will accept that they really can only see some small part of the big picture, while others will say 'OK, yup. I now know everything. Anyone who says anything that doesn't fit into the picture that I have is lying and deserves death, or at least a lot of ridicule'

                                    Why do the Geists do this?
