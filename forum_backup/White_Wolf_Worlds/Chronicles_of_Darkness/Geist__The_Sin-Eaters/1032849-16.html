        After discussing with a friend, I fleshed out my distaste for the Burdens a bit more.

        I understand it's too early to really judge, and this has been a bare-bones preview for the splat. I will reel myself in after this and wait until we get more info before writing Burdens off. Taken in isolation with the language used, however, they make it sound like Burdens are a quest or an agenda, not a character aspect. For other splats, the starting points (clan, auspice, path, etc) are background aspects that influence a character's life just like any mortal background aspect, but it's not something that controls what they choose to do with their lives. A Nosferatu who wants revenge on his sire will still be a Nosferatu with all his Nosferatu-ness after that sire is dead, and he will still use all his Nosferatu-ness in whatever future endeavors he pursues, no matter how un-vengeful those ambitions end up being.

        What happens when a Bereaved finds that person they were looking for? What happens when a Kindly achieves justice for the wrong committed? What happens if the Hungry's anchor is destroyed? Do you pick a new Burden or are you forced into the passing-on endgame scenario? I really hope future previews expand more on Burdens as traits rather than goals.

                            Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post
                            The only thing Burdens conceptually marginalize that would have been a thing in 1E is someone who (to quote someone in my group who is iffy on the changes) "just doesn't want to be dead". There's definitely a stronger sense now that the Bound need to be particularly driven to live for some reason - that the Geists don't offer the Bargain to anyone who who meets the metaphysical requirements at random (woudn't be surprised if the "touched by death/otherworldly-sight/etc before" requirement isn't there anymore). Just wanting to live isn't enough, you need to want SOMETHING.
                            "Just doesn't want to be dead" is perhaps the most elemental expression of the Hungry burden--in essence, the thing you refuse to leave behind is yourself.

                            Originally posted by Darkfoxdev View Post
                            I like the burdens conceptually but I worry they will be resolved too easily without the storyteller bending over backwards to draw out the goal. All of the burdens have a clearly defined goal that realistically would not take long to resolve in most cases. Avenging your own murder is a great example: take 'The Crow' for instance, dude comes back with a bird spirit (aka a geist in this game) and hunts down his murderers, whole thing took about 2-3 days. While some issues will take longer, resolving those situations for other ghosts is the main theme of the game so you'll have alot of practice when you do it for yourself...and then what? Sure you need to resolve your geist's issues before you can move on but your character's main motivation has been resolved

                            Maybe I'm overthinking it since we don't see as much of sin eater society, it could be that finding yourself after this resolution is a big thing and the main reasons krews form in the first place. Okay i'm done... now what?... I know I'll be a spooky ghost person! It just seems that doing that would be like making changeling types based around a fetch. An important part of the character sure but not one that will last the entire chronicle nine times out of ten and not for every player. Hoping to see the next preview address this.
                            Eric Draven was a character specifically designed for a short-form chronicle, so his specific expression of the Vengeful Burden was straightforward and direct. In a longer-form game, the ST would encourage his player to create a more involved Burden. Basically, just like Aspirations can be short-term or long-term, so can Burdens, and we're going to give you advice on talking to your ST about how lengthy a process it is to fulfill yours--but we do expect that most PCs will fulfill their Burdens in a typical game, and that "well, what do I do now?" is very much a question that cuts to the core of the game's themes.

                            Travis Stout
                            Geist 2e Developer
