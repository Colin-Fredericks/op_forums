                I've been kicking this around in my head for a while, and this is what I came up with. How I'd do Geist 2E, were it up to me:
                Themes:
                Living in the Face of Death - Geist is about being constantly reminded that death is an ever-present shadow over life, and choosing to embrace life in response. It's about helping the living and the dead accept the inevitability of mortality. It's about ghost stories. It's about not being a ghost story while you're still alive. It's about developing bonds of love and friendship and helping people through the darkness. It's about throwing glorious fucking parties for the living and dead alike. It's about grief. It's about celebration. It's about fear. It's about fun.

                Ties that Bind - The Bound are aptly-named: they're wrapped tight in supernatural chains that link them to their Geists, to their Krewe-mates, and to their Anchors. Each Sin-Eater sits at the center of a web of deathly energies that spread from her into the worlds of the living and dead, and its from these bonds that they draw much of their occult power. Such bonds, however, require at least as much of the Bound as they grant, and maintaining them is often dangerous for everyone and everything involved.

                The Dark Passenger - Each Bound has forged a dark bargain. For a second chance at life, she's taken on a ghostly presence, a presence she can never escape, that pushes her to indulge its desires and pursue its drives. The Bound must learn to exist with their Geist, distracting it with endless earthly pleasures or giving in to its dark needs. The Geist, meanwhile, pursues its own agenda, whispering always to its Sin-Eater, encouraging her power in order to further its goals.

                Bridging the Ultimate Gap - The ideal Sin-Eater isn't so much a guardian policing the border between the living and the dead, but rather a link between those two states. She helps the living remember the dead and make peace with their own mortality. She reminds ghosts of the pleasures of life and the import of letting go and moving forward. She is a grief counselor whose effectiveness is rooted in the fact that she knows grief only too well.

                Depthless Mysteries - Ultimately, Death is unknowable. Even the Geist's knowledge of what they truly are is muddy and inconsistent. Are they ghosts that have taken on spiritual import? Spirits that have descended into the Underworld and fed on ghosts? The echoing remnants of dead gods? Somehow all of these? The depths of the Underworld shift with time, populated with impossible creatures and the graveyards of broken ideas. The caverns descend forever downward. And then there's what's beyond - what awaits those dead who aren't grasped by the cold halls of the doomed. Krewes often form for companionship and support, but as the Geists within a krewe interweave, they inevitably push the krewes to delve into the mysteries of their own dread being.


                What Sin-Eaters do:
                Sin-Eater stories are ghost stories with a twist - the haunted are on equal footing with the haunters. Sin-Eaters often have a good sense of what they're dealing with in any particular haunting, and they're in a strong position to defend themselves from spectral assault. Geists don't terribly like destroying ghosts, however, and strongly favor the Bound ridding the world of problematic ghosts by resolving their Anchors. This is how the Bound "eat sins:" they take it upon themselves to correct whatever imbalance keeps ghosts in the world of the living. Sometimes this is as simple as making sure the ghost is remembered by someone important. Sometimes it can involve murderous vengeance. The rush of plasm they gain when resolving a ghost lends weight to the metaphor of sin consumption.

                Sin-Eaters know that the world is filled with death. Ghosts linger on every corner, in most buildings. They're omnipresent echoes, only a handful of which have the wherewithal to inflict themselves upon the living. Even the most thorough Sin-Eaters, those wholly dedicated to moving the dead along, have to pick and choose their battles. As a result, Geist stories are intrinsically personal. They're stories about what matters to the ghosts. They're stories about the effects that death has on those left behind. They're tales of frightened faith, shaken hope, abject grief, desperate denial, and - just sometimes - acceptance. Sin-Eaters get to know people. They make friends and lovers. They cultivate Krewes. They collect Anchors.

                Occasionally, Sin-Eaters delve into the Underworld. For the most part, this involves going into the Depths to recover a destroyed item, deliver a message, escape an enemy, pursue a banished ghost, or help the living move on. Sometimes it's to indulge their vices or abuse the dead. Older Bound find themselves increasingly drawn into the depths or risk their deathly potency infecting their Anchors. The Bound generally try to avoid crossing the rivers or passing the gates they find there, but sometimes sacrifices must be made. Sin-Eaters who enter the Lower Mysteries witness entirely new vistas of mortality that often force them to face their own inner shadows, leaving them - and their Geists - forever changed.

                Geist stories sometimes become epic. Bloody-handed necromancers raise ancient ghosts with powers that shake mountains. Entombed godkings rise in search of plundered relics, stirring even normally docile ghosts to acts of violence and opening the gates of death to release half-dead monstrosities into the world. The boundary between life and death frays, and entrances to the Underworld begin appearing throughout a small town in the mountains. Dead gods ascend from the depths to claim a city as their personal dominion. Epic Geist tales add a gonzo flavor to the game, challenging even powerful characters and driving them to forge disparate cults into larger factions or even unearth the secrets of long-forgotten conspiracies.


                2E Upgrades:


                Do away with Archetypes.
                Instead:
                Willpower is regained through Purpose - the player's reason for being in the face of death. This is the reason they choose To Be rather than Not To Be.

                Making major progress towards the character's Purpose in the face of significant danger returns all spent Willpower.

                Making minor progress towards Purpose in the face of minor obstacles returns a single spent Willpower.

                Characters also possess Aspirations which grant beats, as per core World of Darkness characters. Each character furthermore possesses a Deathly Aspiration, a short-term goal of the Geist, which grants a Synergy beat. (See below.)

                Bound characters regain Plasm primarily through two other unique traits: Drive and Desire. (Plasm can still be harvested from ectophagia, Haunts, Flesh Fairs, and the resolution of hauntings.)

                Drive represents the Geist's raison d'etre. It's almost always something too huge to actually accomplish. It may be something like "Punish all rapists" or "Learn all secrets." This is the thing that keeps the Geist bound to the world, the thing that keeps it from passing on. Were it ever to be truly resolved, the Geist would move on to its final destination. Fulfilling the Geist's Drive in a minor way restores a single Plasm. Making major progress towards the Drive in the face of tremendous difficulty restores all spent Plasm and grants a Synergy beat.

                Synergy beats are tracked independently from other beats and Experiences. When a player earns a number of Synergy beats equal to her current Synergy, her Synergy increases by 1.

                Desire is the Sin-Eater's primary tool in distracting her Geist from its Drive. It's an earthly pleasure that the Geist particularly responds to. It may be drinking, dancing, fucking, reading, gorging on food, or playing videogames. It is, by its nature, something that the Geist can't experience when not bound and does not further the character's Drive, Purpose, or Aspirations. In short, it's an unproductive distraction. But that's part of living life. Indulging the Geist's desire nets the Player a single spent Plasm. Doing so in a manner that significantly undermines the character's goals or endangers her restores all spent Plasm and a spent Willpower point. Most importantly, a player can avoid experiencing a Synergy Breaking Point resulting from ignoring an opportunity to fulfill (or - rarely and at Storyteller discretion - actively opposing) her Geist's Drive by engaging in her Desire within the same or following scene. Using Desire this way costs a Synergy beat, however.


                Synergy Re-Imagined:
                Synergy is rated 1 to 10, and player characters begin at 5. Synergy is gained by engaging in the Geist's Drive, pursuing its Deathly Aspiration, and otherwise behaving in a manner appropriate to the Geist. Synergy is lost by ignoring the Drive or performing actions inimical to the Geist. (These latter actions are not tiered - any of these actions can trigger a Breaking Point, regardless of Synergy rating. Some actions are particularly destructive to the bond between Sin-Eater and Geist, however, and levy a penalty to the roll to avoid discord.)

                Discord Breaking Points include: Closing an Avernian Gate (with a penalty of -2 if the Sin-Eater destroys the gate). Destroying Mementos (with a -2 penalty if it is a Deathmask or Keystone). Destroying a ghost (with a penalty of -2 if the Sin-Eater performs ectophagia). Inadvertent killing (-1 die). Reviving the dead (-1 die). Destroying a Geist (-2 dice). Mass or serial murder (-2 dice). Attempted suicide or attempting to destroy one's own Geist (chance die).

                Synergy grants various bonuses at high levels (and penalties at low levels as the angry wails of the neglected Geist distracts its host), but the most valuable of these is Keys. For every second dot of Synergy (1, 3, 5, 7, and 9) the Sin-Eater gains access to a Key. This is the only way to earn Keys. Keys are lost when Synergy drops below the threshold necessary to maintain it. The Synergy 1 Key is always appropriate to the character's Threshold, the Synergy 3 Key is always appropriate to the Geist's, and the Synergy 5 Key is open to player choice. Either the Synergy 5, 7, or 9 Key must be the character's second Threshold Key.



                Anchors Re-Imagined:
                All Sin-Eaters possess Anchors, the parts of the living world that keep them returning to it. These have a similar role to Touchstones in Requiem, but rather than being something the player uses to prevent bestial descent, cultivating Anchors reinforces the Bound's connection to the world of the living, allowing her to channel greater deathly power. Each Anchor is associated with either a Lock (see below) or her Psyche trait, and the character's relationships with these characters, organizations, or locations deepen as she grows in power. These relationships are not without risk, of course - the loss of a valued Anchor can cripple a Sin-Eater.

                Just as Anchors tie the Bound to the living world, the Bound touch their Anchors with the energies of death. Anchor locations become haunts or even Cenotes. Anchor characters become mediums or attract ghosts. Many of those offered the Bargain were once Anchors for a Sin-Eater or ghost themselves. The appeal of Anchors as beings with a connection to the Underworld (or as leverage against a Bound) often brings them to the attention of Sin-Eaters, ghosts, unbound Geists, abmortals, living human magicians, and things stranger yet. The dangers faced by Anchors often drive Sin-Eater stories.

                When a Sin-Eater's Psyche rises above 5, she must begin spending a set amount of time in the Underworld to keep her power concentrated in her own physical form. If she doesn't do so, her Psyche seeps from her, becoming reduced. More troubling, that energy flows along the invisible lines tying her to her Anchors, infusing them with the morbid energies of death and decay. This can ruin an Anchor's life as they suffer nightmares, experience visions, inadvertently open Avernian Gates, and become shining beacons of power to ghosts, the Bound, abmortals and necromancers.

                Each Anchor functions as a Merit, and each level of the Merit grants bonuses to Manifestations involving the Lock the Anchor represents (such as a bonus die at first and increased dice tricks later on). For the Anchor that represents the character's Psyche, the Merit provides bonuses to harvesting Plasm. Sin-Eaters possess Mementos that represent these bonds (generally an object given by the or taken from the Anchor), and without them, they lose the benefits of the Merit until they recover or replace the Memento. If an Anchor (or the Sin-Eater's relationship with her) is destroyed, the Bound not only loses the advantages granted by the Merit, but suffers a significant penalty when using the trait the Anchor was tied to.

                Characters begin play with three 1-dot Anchors (one for her Psyche and one each for two of their Locks). They may increase the rating of any of these Anchors with Merit dots during character creation, or even purchase a fourth Anchor if they have a third Lock.

                At high Psyche, Sin-Eaters become a danger to their Anchors if they don't spend time in the Underworld. Anchors bound to high-Psyche Sin-Eaters who don't bleed off their deathly energies in the stygian depths gain increasing penalties to actions as they become plagued by unearthly sounds and visions. Worse yet, ghosts gain a bonus to rolls to notice such individuals, to affect them with Numina, and to manifest in their presence.

                Going it alone: the lone wolf character is still a viable playstyle for Geist, though it somewhat flies in the face of the themes of the revised game. Loner Sin-Eaters tend to focus on Psyche and Synergy rather than Anchors, and thus have more Keys and fewer Locks than more social Sin-Eaters.


                Krewes Revised:
                Do away with tiers.

                Many small Krewes are informal groups of Sin-Eaters who meet up to talk shop and generally support one another. They're trauma survivors, uniquely situated to understand what one another has gone through, and that gives them a common ground that often leads to bonds of deep loyalty. Most Krewes take this a step further, performing a ceremony that binds them together with metaphysical chains not entirely unlike those between a Sin-Eater and her Anchors. This binding results in a profoundly deep communion between the Bound involved, a communion accompanied by flashes of blinding insight. Krewes exit the binding with a deeper working understanding of the mysteries of death, the relationships between their Geists, and the role their Krewe plays in the living world. This allows the Krewe to purchase Merits as a group, much as they might share a Safe Place, and even Krewe members who join later or have not contributed to the pool gain the benefit of these Merits.

                Those members of the Krewe present at the binding, however, are known as the Founders, and they gain a handful of supernatural benefits from the experience. These Founders gain additional powers as their Krewe expands and evolves. More importantly, their Geists change as their cult grows and their mystery deepens. They reveal more about themselves and their origins, revelations that wholly alter how they are perceived (and even what they are called) by the Sin-Eaters.

                Characters who join large, established Krewes may purchase a one-dot Merit to represent their membership. (This Merit is a two-way street - it both allows the owner to benefit from the Krewe's pool of Merits and it adds the owner's Anchors to the overall number of Anchors associated with the Krewe.) Additional Merits can be purchased by non-founding members, and the types of Merits available are based on the Psyche of the Founders. As the Founders grow in mystical power, their channel and mythos evolves, granting greater advantages to their followers. Mechanically, this is handled as a prerequisite for Merits based on the Founders' average Psyche.

                The Founders, on the other hand, gain access to additional Merits as their base of allies and followers grows. Founders Merits have prerequisites based on the number of total Anchors possessed by all of the members of the Krewe.


                Manifestations Revised:
                Manifestations are powers that combine a Key (a manifestation of a deathly archetype embodied by the Geist) with a Lock (an aspect of the living world embodied by a Sin-Eater's understanding of one of her Anchors). This combination of life and death manifests a ghostly power.

                The goal of revising Manifestations is to make the system more coherent. Locks should provide a specific set of powers while the Keys alter them in reasonable ways. Abilities that fall too far outside of the logical combination between Locks and Keys should be recast as Ceremonies.


                Ceremonies and Mementos are largely unchanged. The latter could perhaps be fiddled with a bit to build Mementos out of traits rather than having set traits at set levels. Any other changes will be focused on making these cool, collectible aspects of Sin-Eater existence. Players should want to load up on them, and the game should support that.

                                    No, that's correct. Being a German word, you can find it in the Oxford English Dictionary (English being a Germanic language) where it retains it's "spirit" meaning. In ordinary colloquial English you only see it used as part of particular loan words, e.g., poltergeist, zeitgeist, etc. The original German usage is closer to what the ancient Romans meant by the term "genius" (as in genius locus). The problem for the game is that "Geist" is too vague of a name, which ultimately translated into issues of thematic focus. It's hard to play a game that doesn't really know what it wants to be. It must also be hard to write such a game as the quality of Geist 1.0 was so poor a 1.5 edition of the book was ultimately necessary to patch up its many problems.

                                    IMO, if the goal was to channel spirit (and thus canonize how Claimed could / should be played) then it should have had a broader scope than just ghosts. That it doesn't have a broader scope argues that it's goal was to bring into being a "Ghost" game (and they could have just used that name and saved alot of trouble) that would serve as a nWoD spiritual successor to Wraith (by scavenging most of its popular themes -- Ego v Id, Underworld, Fetters, etc.). I would have preferred an actual nWoD Wraith but since Geist took so many themes from the oWoD one, it would necessarily only resemble the Oblivion in the broadest sense of topicality, i.e., players play non-Vampire Undead.

                                    Now there is a question of how best to fold Wraith's into the existing PC splats without breaking the existing spirit rules. IMO, the best approach would be if Wraith's were Claimed dead who (exception to normal Claimed rules) did not deteriorate over time (because they leech life from their surroundings).

                                    A nWoD Wraith game would need some new themes to focus on too. As Geist has pretty much already staked out the whole 'resolve one's past life' theme, I'd suggest that a new Wraith not focus on that topic. Picking up on some of the military themes from oWoD Wraith might be appropriate in its stead (the dead are legion). Wraiths are captains and dukes (again the Ancient Roman meaning -- general) of the dead. To avoid power creep and metaphysical problems, the long "war" they've been party too is over. Maybe they won, maybe they lost, whatever the case, the truth is lost to history. Maybe the wraiths were the heads of an invading force, maybe they were there to defend the world from an invading force, who knows... The point is they are stuck in the material world (the Romani are a good precedent for this kind of thing -- anthropological evidence suggests that their ancestors were a "lost" army from the Indian sub-continent) and they have to find ways to cope. This steals rather well from ghost stories like Beetlejuice and Being Human, as finding a way to co-exist with the living is a central part of the game. Of course, like Demon, there are those who think that the whatever war is still going on...

                                    Of course that's what I'd do / hope for in a nWoD Wraith game, the topic was Geist 2.0. Mainly, I think a rebranding to something like Revenant: the [whatever] would do some wonders for thematic unity. "Geist" is such a rarely used word it's hard to tell what the game is going to be about (without jumping to the ghost conclusion). I should be able to read the title and instantly know what kind of game it is (like Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, Changeling, Hunter, Mummy, Demon, and Beast). Promethean is a bit of an exception but Frankenstein: the Stitching sounds ludicrous. Others have already laid out suggestions for what things in Geist should be changed / updated and I refer you back to the top of the thread for those suggestions.

                                                I really like the idea of Drive / Desire replacing Virtue and Vice. I think Touchstones can keep their traditional place and name, representing the reason(s) that you came back and your ties to this world. If you had become a ghost instead of a Sin-Eater, your touchstones would have become your Anchors.

                                                Speaking of Anchors. I think the core innate ability of Sin Eaters should be to "eat" the Sins (basically Anchors) or Ghosts or the dying. They can store one Anchor per Psyche. While a Sin Eater has an Anchor they can tap that ghost's related memories (possibly other things, possibly other traits via a merit or something). This should be an active experience. No combing the Ghost's memories randomly, you have to ask specific questions (that get answered with memories) and you get bonuses for things like holding an item related to the anchor. Notably, Ghosts who have only some of their Anchors become extremely violent towards the Sin Eater, encouraging Sin Eaters to work in tandim to get all of a Ghost's anchors very quickly. Resolving Anchors should be an Aspiration. Successfully resolving an Anchor should have some decent rewards.

                                                Synergy is a tricky thing. It should be a case of "higher is universally better" and it should represent not a balance between life and death but how well you work with death (as personified by your Geist). Do you walk across the rivers in the underworld hand and hand with death as an old friend, or do you struggle against death in the world? I think this should stay away from murder being good or death being allowed to run rampant, but high synergy should have a very somber and solemn "tone" to it, so to speak.

                                                The major powers (manifestations)... not going to lie I don't have much in the way of good ideas for. I'm also not sure how to really fit the doors and key metaphore into the scheme of Sin Eaters. Mechanics aside, I'm not sure what they really add to the game. I feel like "Death is the door, you have the keys" is very evocative until you sit down and thing "okay but what do you do with that?"
