                          *slips in under the door*

                          You know, I've frequently pondered that very same manifestation-use, bigwhitt. And, *a polite tip of his hat to Satchel* I have concluded the suspended animation as an assumption myself, frequently. I do wonder though, as an opposing, (though by no means argumentative) point, considering the fact that they do age, that perhaps the body withers as/if they reach a point past their normal lifespans. After all, it does say in some of the flim-flam wherein, that only damage done to the body beforehand is healed upon the wakening of said Sin Eater; and no damage taken thereafter is considered.

                          In this point, if we considered aging to be a damaging effect, ("Ravages of Time", as it were.) which is a constant variable (that invariably leads all creatures not inherently Immortal into death anyhow.) then no, you would not be protected by the power. And as an aside, under "Success" in the Elemental Oracle, you can read that the person falls into a "death-like coma". Coma, not stasis or suspended animation, necessarily. And people in coma's can die of old age. *he does hold up one of his fongs; an appendage much like a hand without fingers, and surprisingly animate and dexterous nonetheless: to forestall words momentarily* But that is merely myself lending to the Devil's Advocate, which I am licensed to freely exercise, by-the-by. *And displays the license for those that would dispute his claim: it looks as though he will need to renew it soon.*

                          I tend to agree with dear Satchel though; more on wishful thinking and my own preference than anything purely logical. It's just fun to think of it that way anyhow. The wording in both versions of Geist has troubled me. A body in a coma would eventually die of old age; and a body would rot regardless, as time passed.

                          In games I have ran, I often go with the suspended animation line of thought. *Another nod to Satchel* For simplicity sake.

                                  *A slight nod at D.C.*

                                  As usual, White Wolf lets our imaginations fill in the myriad gaps; purposely I wager. All the more reason they go about making many parts of their books vague: they don't want their rules taken to Court, as it were, and fought between Rule-Lawyers. *A slight smile, full of mirth* As always, it's up to the interpertation of the Storyteller to dictate how it's played in the game. I imagine you can play it any which way you like. Using your example of a cave-in, we can assume that all those variables are only relevant if someone is going to come and actually rescue the Sin Eater in question. If he doesn't have a Krewe, or anyone nearby wherein he can target for his abilities, I would think it would be safe to assume that he/she runs out of their stores of various traits, and then eventually withers. (Or their Geist sees how hopeless the situation is, and abandons them: a likelier scenario, whether it's exactly true or not. Geists can be fickle.)

                                          I agree with Satchel and the majestic posting ninja Shake, but I would also like to bring another little thing up. While not expressly stated to the point of being a mechanical system, it is alluded to several times in the core book and couple times in Book of The Dead that while they are not immortal and still age, Sin-Eaters who are not repeatedly killed do tend to live impressively long lives. Not like that of a Changeling with high Wyrd, but it is (vaguely) said that they typically have something of an extended lifespan, though it is nebulous in that regard. There is a story hook in the core about an aging Bound forestalling death by living the bottled memories of ghosts, during which time his body did not age, acting as a hinted method of how players might be able to stave off death by old age. So while it is true that unless they discover a ceremony or a sort of secret or power in the Underworld that reverses, slows, or stops aging, they will eventually grow old and die. But at the same time, it isn't a bad thing, and I for one enjoy a bookend that has the character passing on after living out the life that his very determination served to preserve through calling out to his Geist. And it is unlikely that the Geist is going to be gone for good, allowing for the player to still hang onto that character in spirit, so all in all I am content.

                                              The thing is, infinitesimally few people really die of "old age". What happens is that their bodily systems for healing injury, fighting disease, and processing toxic substances slow down enough for something else to kill them. Usually it's disease, or cancer, or a bad fall, or cholesterol buildup around the heart. None of those things are "old age", and they also all fall into the category of "things that you're basically immune to as a sin-eater". We don't actually know how long the average person can live before their bodily systems just fail under their own power, without some pathogen or circumstance hastening it. So it's reasonable to assume that even with no immunity to age, a sin-eater could live for what is (by human standards) a really, really long time. They just won't necessarily be the picture of health and fitness while they do it.
