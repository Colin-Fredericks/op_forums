    Session CXXXIV

    Doc’s Log

    Doc’s Log: 1150 Hours, Wednesday, November, 17, 2010

    I went in a different direction than trying to find, steal, then alter a rite of the Pure. Something in my head told me that Mallard would somehow sense the taint and Angelique would suffer for it. Plus it would take Axe or Rose, or both, to ninja into Glenwood without backup and it’s not worth all that.

    But I did hit on another idea as I entered the Library. I grabbed my sketch pad and made a really fast drawing of a cocoon. It was the most simple symbol of change I could think of quickly. Drawing in hand, I headed down to the GMC Room. I used the picture to focus my summoning of a Change spirit. The protean nature of the Uratha, wanting to alter an aspect of someone, and being a spirit of change, I figured this was a better option. Plus any rite made with the assistance of a spirit would be cleaner than bastardizing something from the Pure anyway.

    The spirit was incredibly helpful, whether because I wanted to “do something (TM)” to an Uratha or because I was furthering its Influence, I’m not sure. Either way, I was given a recipe of sorts for how to do exactly what I wanted. That’s a little scary reading back over it. Anyway, I’ll need to:

    -Summon at least 1 Lune Spirit, probably several

    -A powerful enough spirit could make the change itself

    -Control the change so that it only does what I want

    -Need Earth-oriented items for grounding to do this

    -Guide the spirit

    -Silver weapon (check) to make the mark, metaphorically cut out the infection

    -Be ready for Kuruth

    -Axe can help hold Angelique down, but we may need to investigate sleep magics, anchor chains, and bear tranquilizers

    From what I know, Lunes can only be summoned while the moon is in the sky at night and they tend to be hard to control. Based on what the Change spirit said and what I understand of True Names, the mark will need to be either on the forehead (nope) or between the shoulders at the base of the neck. That fits more in line with the lunar cycle tattoo I had in mind so that Angelique didn’t just have some random and wicked scar. We can’t always make healing beautiful, but there’s nothing that says we can’t try.

    All that was left was for me to build a ritual. I headed back into the Library and started working. It was hopeful going at first, but then I seemed to have almost lost the point. I either refocused well or just pulled everything along with me by sheer determination because then everything seemed to fall into place.

    Now I’m finished an hour later and I can actually have lunch with the Twins to talk about finding Mariah’s tub. As long as that goes well, I think I’ll practice First Tongue after lunch to keep in shape for summoning the Lune.

            Session CXXXV

            Doc’s Log

            Doc’s Log: 2045 Hours, Wednesday, November 17, 2010

            Wow I’m apparently a huge asshole. This might not be a major surprise to anyone other than me, but I didn’t realize how much of a jerk I’d been to Axe. Yes that’s what I lead off with after several hours of First Tongue practice. And yes I’m skipping things already.

            Axe came back in from what should have been an afternoon run about two coughs away from hypothermia. Bright Eyes hugged her while I grabbed a blanket from the coat closet to wrap her up. Cupcake had worked most of the afternoon on a locally sourced Bouillabaisse, which she was very proud of, so while Axe took a quick hot shower, the rest of us set the table. We filled Axe in on the plan for the ritual and what her role would be over fish soup. She was very happy to provide security and hoped that this would finally fix the issues with Angelique. The way she said that made me think something was up, but I decided not to be nosey at the table.

            When we were shuffling out, all content with the incredible food Cupcake made, Axe pulled Bright Eyes and I to the side. She wanted to let us know how relieved she was that we found an answer for the whole werewolf vice monster issue haunting Angelique. Actually it was more she was glad she could help the Bone Shadow and then move on. Apparently Axe isn’t really as into Angelique as I thought. According to our Little Red, as Bright Eyes calls her, the werewolf has basically taken up speaking of herself as Axe’s property and become unbearably clingy. Axe believes it’s mainly to be free of her Patron, but that’s still not okay. That veers deeply into the realm of using Axe instead of loving her in my book. Even Bright Eyes looked annoyed at that revelation. And then, things went pear-shaped. Axe started talking about how relieved she was because she wouldn’t have to worry about her fear of keeping up with Angelique intellectually. I thought that was ridiculous, as if Angelique was that smart, she wouldn’t be in the mess. But Axe took it differently and opened up on me with both barrels. She let me know, to my shock and shame, just how little she appreciated my not so subtle hints about how dumb she was. She listed, far more than I could bear to hear, the numerous times I’d been snide or condescending about her common sense or intelligence. At one point, Axe reminded me of my comment to, “read a book.” The incident with free climbing the Bluffs left a particularly barbed memory for her as I felt her words cut and slice as she recounted my mockery in excruciating detail. The look of mortification on my face eventually (mercifully) stopped her. Stumbling through thoughts, struggling to find any string of words which may adequately work for an apology (and finding none), I staggered through the most uncomfortable and shame-filled attempt at atonement in my life. I remembered the book comment, but it was only ever meant as a jab as a way for her to pass the time. Not that that, given current context, helped anything. Eventually, with Bright Eyes standing by being incredibly helpful with her nods of, “yes you did that,” Axe finally ended her litany of justified grievances with an edict “that from now on, trust me to know what I’m capable of and don’t assume that I’m an idiot.” Obviously I agreed, but I did remind her, sheepishly, that the medic in me will always come out when people endanger themselves. That brought up another sticking point that Axe believed we were all academic scholars. When I told her that as far as I knew only Cupcake had ever finished (or attended) college, I really thought Axe might punch me. Her disbelief melted when Bright Eyes gave her the “helpful” nod.

            Before anything more awkward could happen, we were distracted by what sounded like a whispered argument between Tavia and Noriko in the hall outside the Music Room. The issue seemed to be whether or not Noriko was eavesdropping on our conversation. Once it was obvious we knew they were there, Noriko showed me a picture from her cellphone. An hour ago, a homeless squat was hit with what looked like a wall of fire which killed the three scorched bodies in the image. The image in the phone triggered a momentary flashback unlike I’d had in a while. I immediately heard the screams, smelled the blood and burning flesh of the explosion. Just for those few seconds, I was dying again.

            Now that this is written, Axe, Noriko, Cupcake, Bright Eyes and I are going to find who did this.

            Doc’s Log: 0030 Hours, Thursday, November 18, 2010

            I will regret this, but I can’t be as cruel as I should be in this case.

            I’m out of order again.

            We drove out to West Haven in the van to what amounted to an abandoned construction site. Most of the exterior walls for a building long forgotten served as a temporary squatters’ settlement. Noriko shifted into a housecat while Axe just turned invisible before both went in to investigate. The rest of us stayed at the van as the police line had already moved everyone else along. From where we waited, I could see one of the inner walls. The burn marks suggested there was no blast radius, or even a blast. Nothing continued to burn because it was so hot, everything turned to ash. The fire seemed to just move across the inside of the structure without any accelerant or really anything that is normal for a fire. Remote controlled is what I thought of first.

            Axe found a scent and followed it to a dead end. Other than the police and the dead homeless people, there had only been one person here that didn’t fit. The scent was of a young caucasian male, who was oddly not sweaty given the heat needed to cause this level of damage. He walked to a car parked at the curb and left. We had a lead, but not a good one. But something else struck me. Officer Tart was the lead investigator on the scene. For all that Tart is crazy, he’s probably also a sensitive. This was almost confirmed tonight. The whole time we were there, he continually walked an odd circuit around the perimeter, stopping exactly where Axe send our kid stood. I went to distract him on the other side of the building while our sensitives read the area. He opened up to me as I was a face he trusted. He told me that he probably wouldn’t be lead on this case for long as his superiors didn’t believe this had anything to do with Satanists. I suggested that maybe he not mention the Satanists without proof of Satanists. I don’t think he’d gotten that far in his procedure for all the paranoia. We talked for a while and I talked him up a bit about trying to keep from bringing up the “other” before finding evidence of it, because we needed decent cops to stay on the force. I was absolutely distracting him, but nothing I said was false. Tart is a good man, just doesn’t understand what he’s poking at. I got his card to give to Mouse. Maybe she can help me figure something out.

            When I made it back to the van, Cupcake led the debriefing. A pyrokinetic started the fire on purpose; stared at a lighter before forming the wall of flames that he then pushed through the structure. She got his license plate off the lamp post and his description of crimes from the spot Tart kept returning to. Bright Eyes noted that there was also a ghost around randomly scaring people. That set me on edge. A pyromaniac with his own juice is bad news, but not something we can’t handle. A ghost that is scaring the hell out of the living is exactly what I’m supposed to keep from happening. Bright Eyes used one of her ceremonies to track down any nearby ghosts. She found two, but one was a child so not the one they saw in the visions. Without any other leads we could follow on our arsonist, we tracked the ghost to a nearby townhouse that looked like it might be condemned in the next ten minutes.

            The door was unlocked so we called inside. No sense in walking into a homeless squat and causing even more disruption. That was not actually the case this time though. A woman’s voice called from deeper inside. My senses said she was a ghost, but the sound wasn’t in Twilight. When we walked in, we all felt that we were standing in a haunt. And the pretty woman in the white flowing gown was standing in the living room of a cheap Elvira-wannabe set. There were movie and band posters from the nineties as well as a plastic stack of skulls from either a Hot Topic or Halloween store. The Beetlejuice poster was displayed the most prominently. Though the perky ghost didn’t look much like it right then, with her bright smile and bouncy attitude, I could see she died due to a combination of heroin overdose and starvation. Marcie, as she introduced herself, was not “the scariest ghost on the East Coast,” but she obviously had aspirations. The talk of Scare Raids like in the Casper movie set me on edge. Marcie had faded back to Twilight for her own comfort but was happy to talk to us about the pyro. She thought he was from New York but noticed him around several buildings. There was also a coffee shop he went to that the waitress’ like him. The two things that stuck out the most of me in her story was that our target wasn’t afraid of anything. She had checked him since Fear/Terror was apparently one of her growing Influences. That was the other thing. Marcie was becoming a geist and very quickly. That our pyro was apparently a sociopath which explained his lack of concern for committing a triple homicide; well that and no proof that would stand up in mortal courts. But we had proof and he’s not a normal mortal. Axe had tightened from the moment we heard the description of the fire and this time I can’t really say I disagree. Only Bright Eyes seems to think there was anything but malice used in the fire. She also mentioned he was cute. I don’t get that woman sometimes.

            Anyway, Marcie was very helpful but she continually talked about looking for people to possess or terrify. It was driving me nuts so I finally started asking about her life so maybe I could find out more and learn a way to help her move on. She had moved her body into one of the rooms so her main anchor was here. That was where things got really hard for me. Marcie has some pretty big dreams, if somewhat twisted by silly movies, to become the scariest ghost around. She can already sense the fears of those around her and wants to lead Scare Raids. That’s a pretty creative thought for a ghost. However getting her to talk about herself caused her appearance to change. The more she talked, the less she was the pretty blonde in the flowing white gown and more the track marked, dirty waif in makeshift clothes. Her demeanor changed from excited and happy to tired and depressed. It was then that we could see exactly who had died, forgotten and alone. I could feel my guts tighten as for a split second I saw Nightshade’s face instead of Marcie’s. Nightshade and I have shared most of our nightmares and hardships. I knew the anguish and disgust she went through before. But things turned around for Nightshade. Marcie was a reminder of exactly what could have happened to one of my closest friends. What I should have said was that she needed to stop interfering with the living unless they interfered with her. It is my responsibility to stop that kind of activity. What I actually said, while fighting back tears of my own, was that I knew some people she might be able to work with to do her Scare Raids. I’m going to call Grin and/or the rest of the Autumn Court. Marcie died without fulfilling any of her dreams. Maybe in death, I can help her become something more without losing what made her who she was. I realize this is basically experimenting with the creation of a geist but I can’t bring myself to crush the glimmer of hope Marcie has finally gained.

            Marcie gave us her key so we could come and go to check on her. I’m also going to use my ceremony to create some ofrenda and books so she has new context on the world since she died. Beetlejuice should not be the height of her goals. But first, we have to finish with the magic island sleepover.

            It’s going to be a long night.


            Sara’s Letters

But we took care of our Little Red when she got back, me with a hug for warmth and Doc with a blanket. A hot bath (alone, sadly) and a piping hot seafood stew later and she was good as new and confronting Doc about looking down on her.

Probably when she had strongly implied that it was only her depth of feeling for Alice that let her resist her mentor’s patron’s commands. It might have ruined Angelique’s chances with Alice, but it did make it so she didn’t want to break it off so long as her mentor’s patron was an issue. Which has my back up, and puts me solidly behind Alice’s decision. Not that I wouldn’t have been whatever her reasons, but... *shrug* “You have to be with me or I’ll become a horrible murder-beast, starting with the people you care about” is not that way to start things off, even if there is no malicious intent.

It needed to happen eventually, and should have sooner, probably.

But I definitely ought to talk with him, before I let assumptions get in the way of the cohesion of the troupe.

To be fair, it’s sorta a side thing - Noriko told us about this very strange fire and we went to check it out, her, Doc, Alice, Alexia and me. Alice used her Sight (technically, I should probably say her Sense of Smell, but Sight is much more mystical sounding - I haven’t forgotten all you’ve taught me of showmanship) to find where the arsonist had stood and gone, and the Alexia and I used our Sight on those areas to get a glimpse of him. He was kinda cute, to be honest,a little on the preppy side. The others seem to have written him off though. He did kill 3 people, it’s true. Conceivably by accident, though that is a stretch. But all we have of him is a single vision of one (admittedly, without context a pretty bad one) action and the word of a ghost that he is sociopathically not afraid of anything and from New York. Hopefully the others will at least humor me in attempting to see if there is anything redeemable or mitigating in the situation. Hopefully by talking to the supposedly cute barista that has a crush on him if nothing else.

Hers was a sad story - a young runaway who died of a drug overdose and starvation. When I searched for her voice in the spectral howl, it even said she was still addicted to heroin. Which made it a little strange to find a bright, happy young woman, in a flowing white dress, surrounded by accumulated detritus, including wind chimes, movie posters, newspapers and plastic Halloween decorations, talking about being ‘the Scariest Ghost on the East Coast’. She was even planning on a scare raid, like the trio of ghosts from Casper. Casper’s uncles, I think, or older brothers?

It became clear that she was well on her way towards a different kind of fate, where she will become a new thing, a conceptual being, instead of returning to the cycle. We mentioned this to her and she latched onto it, even the idea of losing her Self into whatever concept she will embody (something to do with fear, certainly). This bugged Doc particularly (as did her spooking habits) because, I think, he was as charmed by Marcy as the rest of us.

            A Self is a hell of a thing to sacrifice on your way to becoming, but it is a harder thing to sacrifice for some than others. Unfortunately, the Marcy we were charmed by was the result of her already having lost much of her living Self. When Doc discussed finding her body to give it a proper burial, and her old life was brought too close to the surface, she shrunk in on herself, looking gaunt and starved. Her flowing white dress became ragged barely-clothes and her ghostly arms were covered in track marks. She was clearly jonesing for a fix. I comforted her as I could, and we managed to get her a little stable, a little more like the Scariest Ghost on the East Coast.

            Doc was clearly conflicted between his dislike of the idea of sacrificing even one’s own Self to become something greater and his desire to make it so ghost Marcy never hurt the way junkie Marcy had. We’ll be seeing her again though, and helping her out one way or another. I think we’re all agreed on that.

            So, after our pleasant and lazy afternoon and kinda hectic and heart-wrenching evening, it’s about time for our sleepover in the sympathetic magic room. Sleeping in a room surrounded by creepy faceless manikins and Ziploc bags full of viscera is not really how I imagined that cultural staple that is the sleepover. But one of my favorite people will be there with me and two that I am quite fond of. I’ve fallen asleep in less pleasant circumstance and I have a magical dream island adventure to look forward to.

            Sara


            Alice’s Reflections

            After Angelique leaves, I’m fairly restless, so I decide to take a walk along the beach to gather my thoughts. My walk periodically becomes a jog, and I end up staying out for the next few hours even though it’s ridiculously cold and there’s a bone-chilling wind coming in off the ocean. I really hate this freezing winter weather. Even bundled up against it, it still gets under my skin.

            I miss Arizona.

            The weather’s good for something, though. Thoughts of Angelique clutter my mind, and the cold helps give me a sort of clarity. Angelique seems so different now than when I first met her. I feel for her, and I’ll do anything to help free her from the bonds forced on her by her late mentor, but being with her is making me increasingly uncomfortable.

            Maybe it’s the cold, or maybe it’s the stress over Angelique, but I really want to see Eris right now. I guess that means I’ve made my choice.

            How the heck am I going to break the news to Angelique?

            * * * * *

            By the time I return to the mansion, I feel like a popsicle. I take the time to enjoy a long, hot bath before dinner, and I feel much better afterward.

            Over dinner, I learn that the others have been busy this afternoon, working out plans with the twins for finding Moriah’s blood-bathing tub. And while I was out walking, Doc was cloistered in the library, looking for answers to Angelique’s problem. He might have found them. He’s developed a ritual that will help Angelique change her true name. It doesn’t sound pleasant, but if it works, then hopefully it will sever her ties to her diabolic master. That is the best news I’ve had all day.

            After dinner, I draw Doc and Sara aside to get more details of the ritual. It will involve branding Angelique with silver and weathering her werewolf death rage, but I’ll be there to absorb her fury if necessary. With the right Shroud, I should be able to handle it for a short time at least.

            I thank Doc, and then begin to explain how relieved I am to know we might have a solution to the immediate problem. I explain that I don’t think things are going to work out between Angelique and me, and that it will seriously help me break the bad news to her if she’s not relying on me as an anchor against the lure of her demonic patron. But the discussion goes off the rails.

            “It’s scary how insistent and needy she’s been. She’s been saying the she belongs to me. It’s creepy, almost like a stalker,” I explain as Doc and Sara listen. Sara in particular seems troubled by Angelique’s behavior. She’s almost angry that my werewolf friend has been using her plight to make me hers, but I try to defend Angelique. I don’t think she’s maliciously forcing me into a relationship. She’s just desperate. That doesn’t make it any less uncomfortable.

            “It’s not just that, though,” I continue. “When I first me Angelique, I liked the mysterious, coy, confident woman I saw. Even then, though, I was intimidated by her sharp mind. She’s so intelligent and beautiful, a part of me worries that I can’t really keep up with her.”

            “Wait, why would you be worried about that?” Doc interrupts.

            I’m confused. What’s he getting at? In the back of my mind, I start to feel defensive. Is Doc making fun of me?
