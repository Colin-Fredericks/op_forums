                            I'll here point out with a certain degree of vitriol that you are insisting that I prove a negative and preemptively crowing about lack of emphasis. This is semantically knackered.

                            I learned Aristotelian logic a couple years ago; conveniently, the variables used for such are S and P, so let's say S = Sin-eaters and P = former Psychics and sit down for a primer/refresher:

                            "All S are P" is what's called an A Statement; in the square of opposition, an A Statement is directly incompatible with what are called O Statements, such as "Some S are not P"; if one is true, the other has to be false, as might be gathered by the relationship those two statement-types have being called "contradictory." Likewise, "Some S are P" - an I Statement - is subaltern to - that is, implied by - the corresponding A Statement. O Statements, as you might guess from the thing being called the square of opposition, are subaltern to corresponding E Statements - "No S are P" - and thus if the universal statement (the one that addresses an entire category instead of a nonzero set of instances) is true, then the particular (the one addressing the aforesaid set of instances) must be true as well, but the particular can be true while the universal is false.

                            There's two more relationships besides contradictory and subaltern in the square, and they're nicely symmetrical in what they mean. There's contrary, which is the relationship between corresponding A and E Statements - as could be inferred by universal statements that mean opposite things, "All S are P" and "No S are P" cannot both be true at the same time. (They can both be false - such as if the criterion P is something that can be found in some but not all members of group S.) Their subalternate I and E Statements, meanwhile, are subcontrary - they can both be true, but they can't both be false; the only way this could happen is if you were talking about a set of zero members, which you can't do with particular statements, so if Some S are P is false, then Some S are not P has to be true.

                            So let's unpack this:

                            It's logically impossible, within the confines of this argument, for All Sin-eaters to be former Psychics and for Some Sin-Eaters to not be former Psychics, but it's possible for Some Sin-eaters to be former Psychics and for Some Sin-eaters to not be former Psychics. (We can extrapolate from pre-release talk about Geist mentioning the involvement of other supernaturals in the gameline, word-of-writer, and the lack of mention in sample characters and anywhere else in the line that the bit in Chapter One about every Sin-Eater being Born With A Caul is an artifact of an earlier iteration that didn't get through the book's notorious editing rush rather than a Definitely Factual Thing - it's not exactly unprecedented, given the corebook's directly opposed statements on Kerberoi forms.)

                            On the other hand, "No Sin-eaters are former Psychics," if made a true statement as you propose,* means "Some Sin-eaters are former Psychics" cannot be true, since its contradictor is not false, while "Some Sin-eaters are not former Psychics" stands untouched.

                            In other words, going from the evidence of one true particular negative statement - the sample Sin-Eater characters provided by the books - and filtering it through the square of opposition, you lose more by adding a universal negative* than by not doing so.

                            * I'll here note that if you're not proposing that No Sin-eaters are former Psychics, then you've opted to literally argue for absolutely nothing, painted over a core of "I hope that the book is edited in such a manner that we don't have blatant contradictions in setting lore like we did in 1e core."

                            Lastely, telling people to "read the books" are alittle insulting, given the fact that you're either implying I'm of poor memory or too dense to read the material before I speak my mind. This is further aggrevated by the fact that we're not really discussing the old book here. We're discussing my wish for a possible future change/addition to a new book.
                            It is my sincere hope that I do not need to explain how "I hope [change that is not a change] is made" is aggravating cruft that I've spent a sizeable portion of my posts in two other more-confirmed gamelines' What-do-you-want-to-see threads snapping more verbose versions of "read the books" at and thus not something I possess especial reserves of patience for.

                            I dont think it was your intention to insult, but I'd be pleased if you would consider what you're implying a little more carefully in the future.
                            I would be pleased if I didn't have to struggle with communicative difficulties imparting the understanding of authorial intent that I've synthesized for this game from its own books and bluebook mentions, posts by the writers, and inference from the latter-day fluff to people who haven't taken literally weeks and months of their time deliberately processing the material like I have but don't understand how to properly leverage a position of ignorance by actually asking sincere questions.
