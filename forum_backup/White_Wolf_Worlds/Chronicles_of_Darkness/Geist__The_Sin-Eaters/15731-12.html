                    It's the off week. Let's meet Alice

                    Lost Footing

                    Alice Campbell
                    Threshold: Prey
                    Archetype: Reaper
                    Virtue: Just
                    Vice: Relentless

                    LOST FOOTING

                    What have I done?

                    I don’t know how long I’ve been repeating that to myself.

                    There’s so much blood. It’s all over my hands and arms. I’m pretty sure it’s splattered all over my face too. The carpet is soaked in it, and I’m kneeling in the puddle because I can’t stand. All the strength has drained from my body. I guess I’m in shock.

                    Arizona State House Representative George Powell lies dead on the floor in front of me. His body is twisted awkwardly, one leg bent painfully under the other. One of his forearms is nearly severed. I can see the pale white bone peeking out of the ragged flesh. His stern blue eyes are staring at the ceiling, wide open with surprise. There’s a wood axe planted deep in his chest. My axe.

                    What have I done?

                    * * * * *

                    I never imagined my life would turn out this way, but I guess there were signs all along. I was born and raised in Casa Grande, Arizona, on my parents’ small dairy ranch. I was the youngest of three children; both my elder siblings were brothers, Jacob and Ethan, and they picked on me a lot, so I learned to be tough. When push came to shove, though, they always looked out for me.

                    The ranch work wasn’t easy, but that never really bothered me. I don’t mind hard work. I helped take care of the cattle, making sure they were fed and milked. When the cows got sick or were injured, I tried to take care of them. I didn’t like to see them suffer. Sometimes the sickness was incurable or the injury too great. In those cases, I still did my best, and somehow my presence seemed to help the poor beasts die more comfortably. Maybe even more quickly.The same thing happened when my grandfather passed away. He had terminal cancer, and was in a lot of pain. In his final moments, I held his feeble hand and all the anguish drained from his face. He died at peace, with a gentle smile and a soft, contented sigh. My mother said I had a gift for bringing peace to people and animals that were about to pass on, like a little redheaded angel. I always felt more like an angel of death.

                    My family was pretty religious. Church on Sundays, prayer before meals and bed. I had a harder time with my faith than they did, though, especially once I became a teenager and discovered that I had no interest in boys. In fact, I developed a serious crush on another girl in my class that lasted through high school. I never acted on it, though. I was terrified that my family and classmates would discover that I was a lesbian. To this day, my parents don’t know. My brother Jacob does, but he’s cool about it, and he helps me keep the secret from the rest of my family.

                    We never had a lot of money. It was all we could do to keep the ranch going, but in the end it wasn’t enough. By the time I finished high school, we were in debt and forced to sell our land and cattle. The family moved to Phoenix after I graduated, and I got my own apartment. I made ends meet working as a waitress. Compared to the farm work, it was a breeze, and the tips were pretty good. I used to dream about saving enough to attend university, but even then I knew that college was a futile hope. As it was, I spent most of my disposable earnings on camping and climbing equipment. Whenever I could, I’d head out into the desert or the canyons to go rock climbing, caving, kayaking or just camping with my friends. Phoenix was great and all, but I never felt more alive than when I was scaling a sheer rock face or exploring a twisting cavern.

                    In Phoenix, I was more open about my sexuality and even dated a few other women. It was nice to be myself for a change, even if none of the relationships worked out. And then I met Katherine Powell.

                    She walked into the restaurant where I worked. It wasn’t an expensive place, but it was trendy. She was there with her friends, and I was their server. As soon as I laid eyes on her, my heart skipped a beat. She was beautiful, with gorgeous brown eyes and a great body. Her mouth turned up slighly at the corners of her lips, giving her this confident, cat-like smile that just made me melt. Her friends called her Kitty. It was love at first sight.

                    But of course I couldn’t say anything to her. She probably wasn’t into girls, and I didn’t want to embarass myself. When I found out she was the daughter of George Powell, an influential conservative politician, I figured I had zero chance with her. But it wasn’t like that at all. She was really alternative and open-minded. She came to the restaurant about once every week or two to eat with her friends, and I ended up serving their table most of the time, so we sort of got to know one another a little. I think one of her friends picked up on the fact that I was crushing on her pretty badly and must have said something, because one night Kitty started flirting with me. The next thing I knew, we were dating.

                    Those were some of the best weeks of my life. Even now, knowing how it all turned out—knowing what a sham it all was—I still can’t help but remember my time with Kitty fondly. I was in love.

                    We had been going out for a few months when we decided to go caving. Kitty had never been before, but she was always eager to try new things. She was athletic and enjoyed camping and spending time outdoors, so one weekend we headed to a cave I’d been wanting to explore. We had all the right equipment and took all the right precautions. But sometimes tragedy strikes, and there’s nothing you can do about it.

                    I was scaling a deep shaft in the cave, trying to see if there were passages at the bottom. It was rough going. Kitty was managing my safety rope, but something went wrong and the rope came loose. I should have been fine, anyway; I’m a good climber. But I made a mistake and lost my footing. I fell thirty feet down the shaft and landed badly on the uneven rocks at the bottom.

                    I don’t know exactly how bad it was, but it was pretty bad. I broke my back, one of my legs, and several ribs. I had severe internal injuries. Each breath was excruciating. I was dying.

                    Kitty frantically screamed down to me. I could barely manage to call out to her. I told her to go find help. She couldn’t do anything for me by herself, so she ran out of the cave. That’s the last I saw of her before I died.

                    If she had brought help, she could have made it back in time to save me. I didn’t expire quickly. I lay there for over a day. Every moment was agony, punctuated by brief, blissful blackouts. And then I died.

                    * * * * *

                    What have I done?

                    I know I should stand up, leave the house. Flee before I’m discovered. I’ve taken my revenge on the man who killed me. Justice has been done. The hunt is over.

                    The hunt was good.

                    Her voice echoes in my head. My shock starts to give way to a sort of determined conviction. It wasn’t easy getting this close to Mr. Powell. It took weeks of investigation and waiting before I found the right moment. The Huntress encouraged me, guided me. She’s done this before. And it all paid off. Powell was an evil, unscrupulous man. And now he’s dead.

                    * * * * *

                    I woke up slowly. I felt like I had been dreaming. In my dream, a strange woman came to me. She looked like a Navajo witch. Grasping arms reached from the shadows behind her, clutching at me, at her and at things they could never reach. I somehow knew that she was called the Grasping Huntress. She told me that Kitty wasn’t coming back. She had abandoned me. Her father made her leave me to die, and no one would ever be the wiser. Unless…

                    As I woke, I realized that I was no longer in pain. I could move again. My spine was whole, my leg unbroken. I could breath without effort. I knew then that it wasn’t a dream. I’d made a deal. The Grasping Huntress brought me back so that I could make Representative Powell pay for leaving me to die.

                    I free climbed out of the shaft and found my way out of the cave. It was the middle of the night, and the desert was bathed in moonlight. I had to hike several miles to the closest highway and hitchhike back to Phoenix. I didn’t want to believe that Kitty had really abandoned me, and my phone had a little charge left, so I called her.

                    When she answered the phone, her voice was trembling. She never expected to get a call from me. My voice was dry and it must have sounded strange, but she recognized me.

                    “Alice? Oh my god, Alice! Are you okay? How did you—?”

                    “Why did you leave me to die?” I asked, quietly.

                    “I didn’t want to…but it was too late! I couldn’t do anything. And if it came out that I was involved…my father said it would ruin us!”

                    “Ruin you? His political career is worth more than my life?”

                    “No…it’s just…I didn’t know what to do! So I just did what he told me!”

                    “…”

                    “Alice? Are you still there?”

                    “How could you leave me? I thought you loved me.”

                    “I…I’m sorry. I’m not really into girls. I was just curious and you seemed nice. I guess I was just rebelling against my dad. He’s always so concerned about his political reputation. It’s annoying! He hated that we were together! He thought it was a stain on our family. I mean, he couldn’t stand the fact that I was dating a lower income lesbian.”

                    The torrent of her words swept through my heart and broke it.

                    “That’s why you were with me? Rebelling against your father? I love you, and you don’t feel anything at all?”

                    “I’m so sorry, Alice! I shouldn’t have used you like that. I was going to tell you…but…”

                    “You killed me,” I whispered.

                    I don’t know why I said that. I think I was angry because I knew that she really had killed me, more or less. Ultimately, though, she was just a scared twenty-year-old. It was her father’s fault that I died. But I was angry and heartbroken. I wanted her to know what she’d done, and that was all I could think of to say.

                    Kitty was silent. I could sense the terror rising in her as she realized she must be talking to a ghost.

                    “Alice?” She could barely get my name out past her fear.

                    That was the last thing I heard before my phone died.

                    * * * * *

                    What have I done?

                    Only what you had to.

                    I slowly struggle to my feet. I feel sick, but manage to keep from vomiting. It’s time to leave. I turn to the door…and Kitty is standing there, staring in horror at the corpse of her father. At the axe planted in his chest. At the blood-splattered girlfriend that she never loved. She’s in shock and can’t even speak. She doesn’t believe what she’s seeing.

                    She left you to die.

                    All the anger and betrayal and heartbreak fills my head. I grab my axe and wrench it from Powell’s chest. It makes a horrible wet cracking sound as it pulls free. I grip the axe with both hands and take two long strides toward Kitty. She screams, but the scream is silenced when my axe digs halfways through her neck. She falls to the floor and another puddle of blood begins to blossom beneath her.

                    What have I done?

                    Only what you promised.

                    I collapse to my knees again and break down into sobs. My tears mix with Kitty’s blood, leaving bitter streaks that run down my face. The Huntress tries to comfort me, to justify what I’ve done.

                    There are bad people. They deserve to die.

                    I realize in that moment that I’ve committed myself to this murderous path. I’m only alive so that I can bring justice to those whose crimes deserve death. I feel my innocence shatter like glass, its jagged shards slicing at my heart and leaving scars that will never heal. This brings on a new round of sobs.

                    Eventually, I collect myself. Now that I’ve killed Kitty, it will be easy for the authorities to connect the murders to me. I’ll have to mask the evidence, and the Huntress shows me how. We find a pistol hidden in a closet and I use my plasm to conduct a ceremony that makes it look like Kitty shot herself. The police will think it’s a murder-suicide. I have to leave my axe behind, but I can get another one easily enough.

                    Before I leave, though, I notice Kitty’s bracelet. It’s Navajo, made of silver and turquoise. I gave it to her not long before I died. I’m surprised she’s still wearing it. Maybe she did have feelings for me. Or maybe she felt guilty and kept it as a reminder of her mistakes. Either way, I’m flooded with remorse when I see it. Kitty didn’t deserve to die.

                    Inwardly, I scream at the Huntress. It’s all I can do. But she’s silent.

                    * * * * *

                    I left Arizona as soon as I could. I just emptied my bank account, got in my jeep and drove. I ended up in Portland, Oregon. I still didn’t completely understand what I’d become. I knew that I’d been brought back from the dead for a reason. I knew I could sense death and see ghosts, and I decided it was my duty to help them seek justice against their killers. That definitely seemed to be what the Huntress wanted.

                    As far as I knew, there weren’t any others like me. I didn’t know the term sin-eater. The Huntress always referred to us as skinwalkers, but that made me uncomfortable because I remember hearing Navajo legends about skinwalkers when I was a girl, and they were always portrayed as evil witches. That description might’ve applied to the Huntress, but I didn’t want to think of myself as evil. Still, one of the first Keys I learned to use was Primeval, which seemed a lot like skinwalker witchraft to me.

                    It wasn’t too long before I ran into other sin-eaters, though. A little girl had been murdered, her body thrown into the Columbia River. The police hadn’t been able to track down her killer, but I heard her ghost still wandered the river banks, so I went to talk to her. That’s when I ran across the Dead Judges. They were a young krewe of sin-eaters dedicated to making sure that dangerous ghosts were eliminated, imprisoned, or destroyed to pay for their crimes. Of course, they did it mainly to protect the living.

                    The krewe only had three members. Jonathan Marshall was a handsome thirty-something Stricken Advocate, joined to the Crying Woman. He was the krewe’s leader, and used to be a lawyer until he died of cancer. Then he gave up his practice in order to serve the dead. Cassidy O’Rourke was an angry Torn Gatekeeper, and she was tied to a geist called the Red Hood. She’d been raped and murdered by a man possessed by a psychopathic ghost and was determined to make sure no one else had to suffer her fate. And then there was Randy Goddard, a Forgotten Bonepicker who used to be a P.I. until his fascination with the paranormal discredited him. His geist was the Plank Walker. In the krewe’s mythos, Jon was the Judge, and Randy and Cassidy were the Jury.

                    I ended up joining the krewe, and the krewe binding gave me a new mandate. The Dead Judges had always just focused on dangerous ghosts, leaving living killers to the mortal authorities. But that wasn’t enough. I became the Executioner. If someone needed to die to avenge and resolve a wronged ghost, I was the sin-eater for the job. I’m not proud of it, but I took to it easily enough. That still scares me. I could blame the Huntress, but it wasn’t just her. Something inside me needed to seek justice.

                    * * * * *

                    What have I done?

                    It’s been a long time since I’ve caught myself thinking those words.

                    He was worthy prey.

                    The body of a park ranger lies sprawled on the ground before me, bent uncomfortably over moss-covered tree roots. His eyes stare at the sky, wide with fear. His shotgun rests on the ground nearby, its shells spent. Blood stains the moss red and soaks into the moist earth. It drips from the head of my axe.

                    Rain begins to fall.

                    Somehow, this doesn’t feel right. This wasn’t a just kill.

                    The hunt was good. That’s all that matters.

                    * * * * *

                    I liked being a part of the Dead Judges. I felt like I belonged for the first time since high school. For a few years, we did good work helping ghosts and making sure that monsters, both living and dead, wouldn’t hurt anyone again.

                    Cassidy and I fell for each other and we ended up in a relationship. She had a hard time with men since she died, and we shared a desire to see justice done. We were good for each other, and I think she understood me better than anyone. Probably still does.

                    I could go on and on about the things the krewe did, like the time we destroyed a geist who had grown into a huge ghost tree, or the time I stalked and killed a thrillseeking cult of rich college kids who got their jollies from drugging and burying homeless people alive in homemade coffins. I could describe the time we investigated a haunted mine and helped free the ghosts of several miners. But those days are past, and I don’t feel like reliving them.

                    In the end, I screwed up. I lost my footing. My perspective became skewed. I killed some people who didn’t deserve it. An alcoholic who killed a family of three in a drunken car accident. A young woman whose marital indiscretions led her husband to commit suicide.They’d made mistakes, sure. They were selfish idiots who deserved punishment. But they hadn’t set out to do wrong. They didn’t deserve to die. The other Dead Judges became worried about me, Cassidy especially. We started arguing a lot. She was afraid of what I was becoming. She warned me that the Grasping Huntress was leading me astray, but I refused to see it.

                    * * * * *

                    “Oh fuck me,” Randy exclaims, out of breath from running through the forest. Cassidy and Jon are standing there too, staring at the ranger’s corpse and my bloodied axe. The look in Jon’s eyes is cold and hard. He’s angry. Maybe angrier than I’ve ever seen him.

                    But Jon’s angry look is meaningless compared to the mixture of rage and hurt on Cassidy’s face. The rain has plastered her straight blonde hair to her forehead, but she’s still beautiful. It’s over between us, though. I can tell.

                    “What the hell, Alice?” Cassidy shouts. “Why the fuck did you kill him?”

                    He was dangerous. He was a challenge. The hunt always ends in death.

                    I close my eyes and shake my head, trying to clear the Huntress’s voice from my mind. I reach up and tear my deathmask from my face as I begin to argue. “He shot that camper. He killed her! Her ghost is suffering!”

                    “It was an accident! He didn’t mean to shoot her! The gun just misfired. You know that!”

                    “He didn’t deserve to die, Alice.” Jon says. There is a chill in his voice. “You were there when we made that judgement.”

                    “But the camper’s ghost is crying out for revenge! We can’t just let her linger on in anguish. How else are we supposed to help her?”

                    “That girl’s ghost is confused and angry!” Cassidy is screaming at me now. “She’s just furious that her life was cut short. She doesn’t care about justice. That’s our job Alice. That’s what we do. And this?” Cassidy gestured angrily at the ranger’s bloody corpse. “This isn’t right! It’s just cold-blooded murder.”

                    Cassidy turns her back on me, wrapping her arms around herself as if to stave off the cold. “You’re a monster,” she says.

                    As soon as the words leave Cassidy’s mouth, I know she’s right. I’ve lost my footing. I’ve fallen and I don’t even know where I’ve landed. The Grasping Huntress has ahold of me, and I’m losing my mind. I lower my eyes and see the ranger’s corpse.

                    What have I done?

                    “You went too far this time, Alice,” Randy says, shaking his head.

                    “She’s been going too far for awhile now,” says Jon. His voice is like stone.

                    Cassidy begins walking away, her back still turned. Randy and Jon share a look, and I can tell they’ve talked about what comes next.

                    “You’re too dangerous, Alice. You have no idea what justice is anymore.” Jon hesitates, then sighs deeply before continuing.

                    “You don’t belong in this krewe. The executioner can’t be judge and jury too. I’m sorry, Alice, but we’re done.”

                    Randy and Jon turn and follow Cassidy into the forest.

                    * * * * *

                    I didn’t want to go on living after I got kicked out of the Dead Judges, but I’m a survivor. I had to move on. I was angry at the Grasping Huntress, but I couldn’t lay all the blame on her. We’re a part of each other now, even if we both want different things. Somehow, I have to find a way to live with her without becoming her. I need to regain my footing, get back to solid ground, and figure out how to do my job as a Reaper without losing perspective again.

                    I thought about making a go of it alone, but that’s a bad plan. I can’t do this by myself. If I’m alone, the only thing I have to guide me is my geist, and I know exactly where that road leads.

                    I need a krewe. And preferably one as far from Portland as possible.

                                                            Doc’s Log: 0045 Hours, Tuesday, November 9, 2010

                                                            I’m mentally exhausted.

                                                            I didn’t think everything would be cake and ice cream when we dealt with The Hair, but fuck me the rabbit hole is deep.

                                                            Nightshade let us know around 1930 Hours that the meeting with The Hair was set for 2030 Hours at Murder Ballads. We had all healed up from the trip to Glenwood and I had looked online for some panels to replace the sides of the van. Nightshade had been suspicious missing since we got back, mainly because Karen sent her to their room. The level of Dom/sub that goes on there is a little baffling to me sometimes.

                                                            Anyway, we got to Murder Ballads a little early to make sure we handled any unexpected complications before meeting The Hair. Right as we walked in, a complication came up. I will call the complication Jaggling as I don’t think she has her own Craft Name. Jaggling is The Hair’s roommate, confirmed lesbian, more than mildly jealous, and moved to unwise actions. Jaggling reached from the random people in Murder Ballads and grabbed Nightshade’s wrist to confront her. Given the earlier events of the day, I felt my verbally aggressive response in taking control of the situation was far more calm than other ways. I mean as far as we could tell, Jaggling was about to attack Nightshade and I didn’t even strike her. Nightshade ran into conflict with Jaggling after The Hair took her home.

                                                            Instincts and being on edge had me ready to destroy the girl where she stood, because I take care of my own. Logic screamed loud enough to get my attention however and we learned that Jaggling was also a Hedge Witch. More importantly to us, she knew that The Hair’s family were and are Banshee. Because she has an affinity with spirits, Jaggling was aware of a Darkness spirit following The Hair. This spirit was incredibly weak but was sent to keep watch over The Hair without her knowledge. The spirit was apparently loose lipped with the witch as she knew the family line was dwindling and The Hair had to be preserved to pass on her birthright. Jaggling was fully convinced of this “truth,” which at least partially explained her opposition to Nightshade “interfering” with The Hair’s legacy. I guess because I had originally placed myself between Nightshade and Jaggling, I was allowed to do most of the talking. That or Nightshade just enjoyed seeing how Jaggling shrank away from my anger. I suspect I know which one it was. Eventually, I gave Jaggling my card and told her to call me if she needed research assistance, basically giving her an out to ask for help, as she clearly jumps in over her head with things she didn’t know anything about. Then I ran interference for her when The Hair showed up so Jaggling could get away unseen.

                                                            I directed The Hair to the private rooms to meet the others, then headed over to the bar to ask for a steak knife. The bartender looked at me for a moment and asked if I planed to kill anyone. I decided against making the joke that I didn’t need a knife for that and promised not to make him regret helping me. Then I walked back into the private hallway behind the Krewe, cut my hand to pull my Stigmata Shroud around me, and walked directly into Twilight. The little wingless bat with red beady eyes jumped when I stepped into its reality but I found it could speak to me directly. English is not usually available to such minor spirits so I was sure something had meddled with the tiny Darkness spirit following The Hair. The others were keeping tabs on me and stalling The Hair while I argued with Vlad the Informer to give us some privacy. Finally, I just grabbed it with the intention of walking out of the room and continuing our argument. Sadly, it started shrieking like some horrible invention that did not just drag fingernails down a chalkboard, but fired real nails across a chalkboard the length of a football field. I still left the room, but Vlad never stopped screaming. I didn’t want to hurt the spirit but after about ten minutes, my head was splitting. Before I could lose my temper however, the Vlad stopped screaming. It was strange because something was still screaming almost as loud. I glanced down and realized that while the spirit had been afraid I would eat it or whatever, it was now terrified. I ran back into the private room and stepped back through to our plane of reality. The Hair was floating, her mouth was big enough Nightshade could have crawled in, and she was unquestioningly Keening! Axe looked at me funny before I realized I was still holding the tiny Darkness spirit. I apologized and let go, instantly sending it back into Twilight. It stayed across the room from me after that, but seemed to be okay with me otherwise.

She had taken no breath since I arrived and did not seem aware, while her hair was thrashing around as if she were in gale force winds. Axe ran out to see if she could find out who was dying. Nightshade went to go handle the bouncers and ease the terror growing in the club. Eventually The Hair collapsed, though her hair with, assistance from Bright Eyes, caught her before she hit the floor. Axe came back in with a mirror fragment from the car accident outside. Apparently there was a t-bone crash and the passenger of the struck vehicle “was turned to paste.” Axe has a way with description, though based on the wreckage I saw later, her assessment was probably closer than I originally thought. The mirror fragment will make a nice charm necklace.

                                                            Once The Hair came to, she seemed nearly high or possibly more drunk on power. She became very dominant of Nightshade, a fact that Karen did not miss. They moved to the dance floor while Axe, Bright Eyes, Karen, and I all headed to the bar so I could return the knife. We took our seats as the bartender mixed our drinks and talked to relax like normal people. Karen and I don’t get to hang out very much due to schedules and interests so tonight we talked more about her mysterious watcher. She has only ever noticed the sensation at night and only while on the second or third floor. Never the first floor for sure. The more I hear about this the less I think there’s anything innocent going on. The first floor of the House is very easily navigated and also heavily utilized at most times of day. The upper floors are murkier as people are occasionally around but not usually awake at all times. Plus it would be harder for us to know exactly where to go right away if we heard a disturbance. Before this, I didn’t worry too much as the House has always protected us. I don’t think there is a physical intrusion because of that. That makes me more worried that someone is scrying on Karen specifically. That opens up many possibilities since we know it isn’t through a Boneyard. I don’t like unknown possibilities when it comes to our home. I had a mental image of Ronin flipping out because the sanctity of our sanctuary has been defiled.

                                                            I didn’t get very far into my own personal rabbit hole before Nightshade needed Karen. I was right beside her so I couldn’t help but hear the fact Nightshade was asking permission to leave with The Hair. I could feel Karen stiffen at first, but then she turned on her barstool, stood as high as her platform boots could go and, while staring directly down at The Hair, grabbed Nightshade by the hair and kissed her ...savagely? I don’t know if The Hair got the telegraphed message, but I heard the psychic scream of “MY BITCH!” loud and clear. After Nightshade could breathe evenly again, she left with The Hair. Karen sat back down at the bar, but the time for conversation was over. I left after a while as I still had work to do back at the Bluffs.

                                                            Around 2300 Hours, I settled into the Library to finally get back to decrypting Bone Dry’s book of damnation and slavery. I read back over all my other notes as I poured over the occult Mandarin tome for a third time. I had just finished the last section describing the final ritual as the massive grandfather clock struck midnight when the answers came rushing into my mind. It was satisfying, but also slightly disturbing, given the situation. I double checked my information against the various vampiric information I knew and had available in the Library. My epiphany seems to hold up. Sadly there is no way I can find to unweave or reverse the spell, which is disappointing but not surprising.

                                                            I already knew but confirmed that a Jiangshi could become a more traditional (to my Western sensibilities) vampire through consuming the soul of another vampire. Though I did learn that this would apply to any other type of vampire, Penanggalan, Aswang, etc. Useful to know for other situations I guess. The next option, from most unhelpful down, is have another mage try to take control of the Jiangshi. This would, for the mage, simply be a contest of wills with Bone Dry. But even if the new mage won, we would have have to trust that she would then release her new slave. I hate to judge groups of people unfairly, but my track record with mages is not really making it easy for me to assume they are sane, much less trust one. Option 3 is to send the Jiangshi into a truly holy site. Once there, Guang would have a chance to throw off Bone Dry’s control. Unfortunately there is almost an equal chance that he, or any other Jiangshi, would burst into white flames due to the power of the holy site.

                                                            In the Chinese culture (and many others), 4 is the number for Death. That works out to be favorable to a Sin Eater blessed with Luck Magic. Option four seems to be the most likely route to freeing Guang. My research indicates that if a Jiangshi can drink the blood of another type of vampire, enough to spiritually represent her strength of will, she can try to throw off the bonds in which Bone Dry has mystically bound her. Assuming the might of blood and force of will is strong enough, the Jiangshi will shatter the bonds and serve no other master. If not, the Jiangshi can drink twice that amount of vampire blood and try again. Should she fail again, she must drink a frighteningly large amount of vampire blood and make a final attempt. I have to guess that the three tries option stops there because of the risk of Vinculum, though I’m only peripherally familiar with that concept. Still, this seems like our best bet and I believe we could call enough enough favors with individual vampires to make this possible. The rest is simply up to Guang. Though, thinking about it, I can grant him fortune in pursuing his freedom once we have the rest together.

                                                            Either way, we now know there is hope for Guang plus I know how to free the Jiangshi slaves of Bone Dry. His days are numbered and the pendulum swings back toward the light.

Cupcake was a little concerned but her wife was fairly determined that she would help the man tricked into becoming a hopping vampire. I also got the point from her wording that she was willing once. If Guang failed after the first attempt, we’d need to find additional blood donors.

The Hair said that Star was alive and that she wouldn’t be around for a while. That message, given what we knew of The Hair’s family, was ominous enough. But Jaggling added that it was not like The Hair to not say goodbye. This time she just hung up abruptly. I immediately felt my hackles raise and went to find Nightshade and gather the Krewe.

Given the control of the Darkness spirit, I immediately instructed Jaggling to come to Dark Haven Bluffs.

                                                            Jaggling arrived shortly after we got off the phone, but she seemed incredibly frightened and not just of the House. She said as she started walking up the steps that she thought something had happened. The shadows had been playing tricks on her. Worried that more than just paranoia had affected Jaggling, I commanded her to halt. I think that might have temporarily made her more afraid of me. Then I gave her a brief warning before calling for Lily. The doll happily ambled down stairs and looked the young woman over. I turned to Bright Eyes and told her to get the twins here before taking Jaggling into the TV room.
