    Non-Bound supernaturals generating ghosts?

    12-05-2025, 02:36 PM
    Not all supernatural have souls, or else possess them no longer. Examples includes Beasts (whole soul is replaced by the Horror) or a mummy (whose soul fractured five ways).

    But ghosts aren't souls!

    COULD a supernatural generate a ghost? By dying. An NDE? Bizarre magical ect? How would such a ghost diverge from the norm? Would they 'remember' being something else?

      There's offhand mentions of ghosts of supernatural creatures in 1e's books for Vampire, Werewolf, and Promethean, and more involved discussion in Mage; a case can be made for 1e Changeling including the Gravewight kith with no special notes that changelings might similarly be valid there, whereas it's a pretty distinct metaphysical point for demons to not have anything like a guaranteed afterlife (though the human former tenants of soul pacted Covers can potentially reappear as Echoes); mummies being what they are, it's unlikely for them to produce ghosts that are actually bits of themselves, and the Unfettered Inheritance kind of takes the narrative space a ghost would for Beasts; Hunters are mortals and thus theoretically as capable of leaving ghosts as any human is, while deviants are a sufficiently broad umbrella that they might or might not create one depending on their circumstances.

      In 2e, most things that don't run on Integrity or Wisdom can probably be assumed not to directly make work for the ghost-specific gameline now that the Underworld and its primary interfacing splat are known to exist, much like how we haven't gotten "hunters" as a dedicated slot in the list of Antagonists in a splatbook since Hunter came out as a gameline. Any exceptions are by definition going to be exceptional, but the trends of ghosts as a whole are such that their memories are at least a little Swiss-cheesed as is typical.

      Ghosts created by someone's own death use the character's own Integrity, while the ghosts of objects and animals derive their initial power from the Integrity of human referents; it may be that there's a similar phenomenon to the lack of directly human-derived spirits in the Shadow, or there could instead be a shortcut like the Graveyard Geist phenomenon that allows something that doesn't have Integrity but does have humanlike intelligence to leave a ghost. The salient point for Geist's purposes still largely boils down to trauma and loss, and any supernatural qualities beyond echoing the thing's former powers are going to depend on the storyhook that's responsible for it.

              First: Mummies don't have fractured souls. Aside from being claimed as slaves by the Judges, their souls are just fine. The Pillars aren't a fracture, but an understanding about souls. Everyone with a full soul has the five of them, as far as Mummies understand it, it just doesn't matter.

              That said, the game isn't completely clear on what ghosts in general are. They're memories, but also something else, and they can be either memories of the destroyed person/thing or memories about it, which complicates things even more.

              In the end the answer is yes, although they would be rare and mostly defined by the ST. A supernatural is far less likely to leave a ghost and this ghost won't be anything special compared to other ghosts.

              Overall ghosts form as a response to Integrity, and the type of trauma it represents, so entities without Integrity are unlikely to produce ghosts. The presence of a soul may also be a factor, even the ghost not being the soul itself (or at least not all of it, that's still under debate). Leaving something else behind also probably reduces the likelihood of a ghost forming, as those memories can still "coalesce" on something. A torpid corpse, an echo that can come back from death, a dream of itself, things that in a sense already fit the trope of a ghost.

              Yet, humans can, very rarely, form ghosts without dying. So there's precedence for anything to happen, even if not an appreciable probability.

              My personal take is that the least likely splat to leave a ghost of any sort, even if completely destroyed, are Demons, as both their memories and the memories about them are too tied to their Covers. It's possible, though, that a Cover do leave a ghost sometimes, if not one likely to know the nature of the Demon (but who knows?).

                      I don't know if Mummies can for, and their near death experiences seem to end with them returning on another mission for the Judges. So I'm a no for them.

                      Mages have always had a thing about ghost-mages able to use Numina which looks like the rote spells/praxes they had in life, but losing most of their power. So at least having a possibility of leaving a ghost seems guaranteed.

                      Vampires are arguably already dead, and may or may not leave a ghost, though iirc in both editions they can survive in the Underworld without blood (though there is a river of blood) by eating ghosts... Though it can become addictive. So I'm fine with no ghost of vampires - but you could have a ghost left after the embrace changes them I to a vampire; this one is entirely contestable, but I'm happy with my answer.

                      Werewolves have no reason not to leave ghost, imo. They are said to be disconnected from the human collective unconscious, and sometimes dream their way to the Anima Mundi, so arguably their half-spirit nature (and I don't think spiritsleave ghosts) may disqualify them. So if you wanted you could have it depend on their harmony (like make a roll where very spirit leaning werewolves are unlikely to leave a ghost, but human-leaning werewolves are likely...).

                      Hunter are human so why not? Slashers have lost their humanity so I'm liable to make them like Vampires (having created a ghost when they lost it) but I suppose they could create a ghost if a human's integrity was affected by their death...

                      Changlings are in a similar boat to werewolves, their souls are twisted but they are still fairly human. Maybe high Wyrd changlings are more likely to become some kind of hedge entity/goblin? I'm not sure what fits them best.

                      Demons can not leave ghosts, but as above, i love the idea that their covers can.

                      Which leaves Deviants and Prometheans. Deviants i don't know enough about, but again, those that were human have had their souls torn apart, your milage may vary. Prometheans are sometimes may from human bodies, so theremaybea ghost ofwhatthey were previously, and they can become a normal human as their reward/after-life. But they can experience death and return from the Underworld (at least once again) so I am leaning towards no.

                      Also, Bound are described as already being ghosts, so they can't leave a ghost, though may become one of they manage to die.

                      EDIT: but disregarding all of the above. The correct answers is, what makes the most sense for your game.

                      Is it more fun of you have Vampire ghosts moping around? Then go for it!

                                      Originally posted by orathaic View Post
                                      So I'm a no for them.
                                      I'm not a hard no for any splat, really.

                                      Edge cases and weird mysteries are the staple of the setting. Whatever the standard situation is, or the likelihood of something, in principle there's always a way for it to happen if it isn't outright impossible for some very specific reason.

                                      And given that ghosts are formed by memories, and even objects, animals and still living humans leave ghosts, there's no reason for a ghost of any splat at all to be flat out impossible, as unlikely as it is.

                                      That said, I would have the most unlikely cases be Mummies (there's no shock in their death and they keep living), Werewolves (they're more likely to become something in the Shadow rather than a regular ghost), Bound (they're already ghosts) and Demons (their own identity has too little weight compared to their Covers, even for themselves).

                                      Hunters, Mages and Changelings I think would have a normal-ish chance. Changelings may have a harder time because Clarity instead of Integrity/Wisdom, but otherwise no big deal.

                                      Prometheans and Deviants are variable. They have unstable souls, which isn't a defining factor, but has some influence. Some are arguably more human than humans, others are almost lost to inhumanity, but overall their journey is really marked by relationship with their threads of humanity.

                                      Vampires literally have Humanity as a trait, so the same applies, but one important thing is added: memory. They're immortals with a close relationship to history and memory, theirs and from others in general. I think the older the Vampire, actually the more likely they are to leave a ghost.
