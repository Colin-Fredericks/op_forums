Its not much of a bargain if you get to come back and get superpowers and you can be a ghostbuster and you have a new buddy whos kinda creepy looking but hey they're good people.             Originally posted by Illuminated Minutia View Post
            And at the end of the day the answer should be "Yes."

            I agree with you for everything except this last line. It's way more interesting if this answer changes between yes and no.
            I like Geist's attitude towards this to be honest: at the end of the day you're running around with a powerful ghost running shotgun - as opposed to Inferno where the demon has the opportunity to run the show if it really wants to. I think, as cool as Inferno's mechanic might be, we should treat Geist as a completely separate beast. This is why I suggest using conditions rather than a tug of war condition to show a Geists influence on a humans mind.
            What I think Geist could use though to improve on this? How about a guide for creating and balancing a ‘bargain’ that actually affects players mechanically? It’d have to have two effects: it’d modify your synergy rolls - either changing a few of your synergy rolls or adding bonuses to them if the breaking point is a direct or indirect result of that bargain. I think it’d also have a second effect - a punishment if a Sin-Eater went to long without finding some way of satisfying this bargain regularly.
            An example might be a Geist who’s bargain is to ensure ‘all dead are honored in death’ - This gives the SE three unique aspects:
            • Synergy breaking points are modified for this. Any breaking point that was reached as a result of fulfilling the bargain goes up 3 ranks in the breaking point chart. Thus, destroying a ghost that was desecrating the dead (by say, upturning a mausoleum or simply making a dead person look bad) goes from a rank 6 synergy breaking point to a rank 9.
            • If the SE goes for more than a month without somehow finding a way to honor the dead, they gain the ‘disjointed’ condition - meaning -2 to all manifestation rolls. This condition worsens at six months and eventually leads to synergy breaking points. (Note: the time range should be adjusted depending on the difficulty of the task. A Geist that wants you to hunt down dangerous spirits might be happy if you catch one a year, as opposed to a month)
            • Finally, the storyteller inflicts conditions of the character any time he hears of someone desecrating the dead - literally or figuratively. This leads to any story of someone desecrating the dead leading to a story complete with beat awards.

                                                    I did a quickie conversion of Geist to GMC rules here. I think, on the whole, Geist is good enough, and doesn't really need a HUGE change. The quickie conversion is meant to be just that, quickie to make Geist playable under 2E rules, not a full-on update. That said, it pretty much includes a lot of stuff I'd like to see explored more in-depth in a 2E Geist book.

                                                    Primarily, that's the relationship between a Bound and her Geist. I love the concept, and Synergy is a REALLY interesting "Morality" stat, but I feel like the connection between the Bound and the Geist was glossed over quite a bit in the main Geist book. Why I came up with the idea of Ties, both to mechanically reflect Vampire Touchstones, but also so Bound players can think more in-depth about WHY this particular Geist chose to revive this particular person as Bound. I also really liked the idea of Geist's as "part-spirit," in that they Geists are emblematic of certain concepts, and just do things related to those concepts. The core book has a passage talking about a Geist of car crashes, who causes car crashes not because it wants to, but just because that's what it is. So I came up with Compulsions (still looking for a better term) that represent a Geist's, shall we say, anti-social tendencies that the Bound has to try and keep in check. One of my favorite passages from the core book was talking about how Bound with more badass Geist's are considered more badass themselves. Someone keeping your regular old vengeance-y Geist in check is good, but someone who's holding in Spirit of Vengeance Itself? That guy's got to have some SERIOUS stones.

                                                    I don't really want to see the Geist become "the Beast by another name," in the Bound have the potential to be "taken over" by the Geist and forced to do things, by highlighting those times when the Bound and Geist's interests align perfectly, and those times when the misalign perfectly, would add a lot of interesting fodder to an already fantastic game.
