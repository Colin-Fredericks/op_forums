          Yes. I like to deploy a theme of allmost "Gallows Humor" in my game. The world is bleak, dark, depressing and we're all dying very very slowly, with each day marking another inconsequential step on the eventual entropic end of everything.
          But thats not nessesarily a bad thing. The temporality of the world is what makes it matter and gives it its beuty. Every Sin-Eater has died atleast once, so they not only see and know this fact, they live it. Its a game that looks upon the chrome blackness of death, and instead of being struck down by the pointlessness of it all, sees the reflection of the life that was, and the impact that it had.
          Its a game about death and life, and how embrace both, without marginalizing either.
          Its a good game.

              I've been thinking about this and I've come to realize that Geist IS kind of flexible in what theme you play it as. I came to the decision when setting up and running my game that I should decide on a few themes for myself and run with them. I'm launching my game later this month so things are pretty flexible at the moment, but what I've come up with personally (to provide examples of themes I hope most people will find fitting) -

              Second chances - The go to of Geist, This can be interpreted as a second chance to resolve wrongdoings by the players, or to right wrongs no one else can fix in the forms of helping ghosts fix the things they couldn't resolve before death. This one unfortunately is largely up to the players to bring out in my games, but I ended up writing it out in a couple of different forms before realizing that this sums it up nicely.

              Gang warfare - Geist provides a lot on Krewe politics, so my game will involve surviving in a world where you have territorial Krewes with ties to gangs in the area (my setting is Detroit) and extremely corrupt cops on the other side - hopefully pushing the players to prefer to live in a legal grey area thematically

              Secrets of the underworld - Essentially, I want to bring in lots of imagery of death spirits wearing funeral shroud hoods and reaching out with long, bony fingers. My campaign will have elements of investigating weird underworld shenanigans and figure out how they tick.

                                          Let's see some of the concepts I could have for this.

                                          I could do a variation of Heigou where the bloody little girl ghost (her elder sister) is her Geist. That would be separate character though. More similar to the Sa variation. Heigou isn't supposed to be aware that she's haunted. The ghost appears to other people to encourage them to protect Heigou, but never appears where Heigou can see. Heigou herself is rather more like a changeling, having been kidnapped and transformed by a "witch". Sa by comparison was the normal world variation where witch is replaced with serial killer looking to teach one or more protégés and kidnapping some potential sociopaths for an academy. The ghost was replaced by hallucinations. Making the hallucinations back into being a ghost and go back to a single supernatural killer, I can also re-intro the two "voices" that competed over Heigou replacing Ilmater and Shar with the elder sister and some less benevolent Geist could be good. This version would not necessarily have Heigou or Sa's self-hating, self-destructive manner or they could. If she kept Sa and Heigou's vigilante/black dog habits, she'd beca reaper.

                                          I could adapt Eija Semezou from my novels. She does have the near-death experience in the form of being nearly sacrificed by Balor cultists. (Knife missed her heart and she received medical attention soon after...the cultusts had been distracted, there was another ten year old girl slaughtering them at the time) So there's minimal changes necessary. She'd definitely be an advocate. Eija is more comfortable with ghosts than with people, minor fear of crowds and other people holding sharp objects. Granted Eija is also a magus and apprentice sorcerer, but those abilities are encapsulated in the Keys and Manifestations. Eija is a combat medic, substantial medical knowledge for a sixteen year old (she passed Iceland's paramedic tests and apprenticed in Chinese medicine) which combines with her death-seer abilities and martial knowledge into highly effective Dim Mak. In Divine Blood's population of human death-seers, she is something of a pariah since she uses her knowledge to kill when she needs to, though she hates doing see and hates "opening her eyes fully" because then she sees the blood on her and her family's hands. Eija is set to be a Goddess eventually, with a Domain of Blood.

                                          Hel is another DB death-seer. She's also a 12k+ year old Demoness. Though at current moment she is sixteen years into her current incarnation (deliberate return to childhood to purge mental clutter and reconnect with mortals, Demons and Gods do that occasionally) so is only granted access to a fraction of her power and memories. She is also a Yomi citizen death seer and has no education regarding her death-seer abilities. She's also rather terrified of ghosts. Manifestations and Keys aren't as versatile as a Demonic Realm, but the central concept of being the Demoness of Prisons could be dealt with via boneyard and marionette Manifestation. Hel is also likely a variation of an old D&D LG necro of mine, down to the chip on the shoulder, the crippled body and hating dead things, so I could reach further back to Yeighdra Blackthorne as the model. Near-death experience could be due to her body's poor health or, going back to Yeighdra, an attack by ghosts bond to a Necromancer. She would definitely be a judge.

                                          Another character that would be a good translation is my daughter of Yanluo in scion whom I never got to play really. She was intended to be the Goddesss of Beautiful Death and in mortal society was a psychiatrist, grief counselor and part time para naturalist. Most of her powers were support and her main offense came from the spider demon she could summon (a ghost promoted to demon to punish corrupt bureaucrats. I based that spirit on Jet Li's character in Hero) she also had a ghostly guide, but that was more a mentor thing. She had no particular near death experience but I could come up with one. She would likely be an advocate, necromancer or mourner. Her concept of beauty paralleled the concept of useful and beneficial. A beautiful life benefitted the people around a person. A beautiful death was one that produced a glimpse of serenity, nobility or hope. Ugly lives were selfish and ugly deaths denoted cowardice, foolishness and pointlessness. Acacia Liuzhi had spider, healing and death related powers.
