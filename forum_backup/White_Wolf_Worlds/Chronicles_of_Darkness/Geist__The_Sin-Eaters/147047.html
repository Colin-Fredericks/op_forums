    Help me build a setting?

    05-23-2014, 04:09 PM
    As a quick blurb: I'm putting this post up for two reasons: Firstly, because I welcome feedback and advice from those who have more experience than me in WoD (which basically means any!) and secondly because writing things out helps me think. For those worried about stumbling across spoilers for their own game, my group is based in southern Ontario. If you live here, you might want to think twice about reading on, but otherwise feel free. This might be a good mental exercise for you, or you might suggest good ideas or help me with problems I'll run into creating this setting. I haven't decided whether this post will end with any meaningful spoilers anyways.

    I will try to summerize the actual setting in this doc:     https://docs.google.com/document/d/1...it?usp=sharing

    On to the juicy stuff: I want to run a Geist campaign (yes, I'm coming from a D&D background) - eventually. I'll probably run a few short term mortals games first to get my group acquainted with the system, and so I have a basic understanding of the GMC rules when I try to adapt Geist to them. The short term mortal campaigns might also help to establish the setting or some plot points leading up to the actual game, or they can be just one off games with the Mortal system. I want my players to roll up disposable characters for this to get them roleplaying in the new system, but I'm not above having a long and horror driven precursor to becoming Sin Eaters. I've thought about doing this over summer and having the game transition around Halloween just for the extra atmosphere - the game will likely change from out and out horror to dark humour and a touch of anatomical horror.

    My first problem is I am not travelled enough to write about other cities or places, but the local area doesn't really lend itself to urban horror and gang warfare like a city like New York might. I'm not really liking the idea of basing it in Toronto and hearing people make jokes about Rob Ford, or else basing it in another nearby city and having a lot of rural adventures happen (searching through bushes and the like)- so I want to create a fictional city that - while still in Ontario - is a blank slate. The other problem is gun laws - they're much different here and having a gun is fairly out of place - but I'm thinking I'll just have a large black market that makes it so even mortals can get them - albiet illegally. Without them it seems like Mortals would have a hard time becoming interesting NPCs.

    My second problem (or rather, issue I'd like to address) is I'd like to keep my players serious. I'm hoping to do this by mixing in a lot of sobering themes - As part of the plot I plan to stomp on my players emotions just enough to make them want to win. Show them the orphan that is already dead and forgotten due to neglect while the people responsible enjoy carefree lives and get them to do something about it. Any advice in this Vein? Where do I draw the line?

    I intend to continue expanding on this and developing this thread.

      1) The thing to do if you don't want to go local is do research. My own campaign is set in New Haven, and I am running other sorts of WoD games in Prague and Malibu/Santa Monica, neither of which I have been to. If you have time for it, do some research on the city. Wikipedia is not a good be-all-end-all but it is a good starting place. I'd also look for sorts of stuff on the web like you think players might frequent. For my current Changeling game I used Yelp to get some local nightclubs, restaurants, and similar. You could also check out Urban Explorers websights, or ghost hunter society websites, for creepy and/or abandoned places.

      1a) I don't see a connection between Mortals having a gun or not and being interesting NPCs. Some of my most interesting NPCs don't fight at all.

      2) The only way for this to succeed is for you to discuss things with your players are far as mood and theme you want to maintain. If they are used to goofing around in games simply putting serious stuff in won't change that most of the time. Make your expectations clear and make clear why you have those expectations. if they like levity, work out a compromise so that they can play as they want and be serious when needed. People often try to use system mechanics or just having grim events to drive player behavior, and that tends to lead to frustration for everyone.

      I've never been to Ontario, so lack any specific suggestions for local places/ideas.

          I gave it some thought, and I think I'm going to take your advice a little and base it in an existing city - I'm just going to go slightly further away. I've decided to place this in Detroit - which seems like it'll be an amazing setting with all the guns, drugs, death, violence and day of the dead shenanigans I could ask for. Apparently they have a place called Mexicantown - which I can only assume is Detroits version of little Italy or Chinatown - and Dias del los meurtos seems like its a thing there. There also pretty close to the Canadian border so I don't think the culture will be that alien to me. As an added bonus, of all the cities I can think of, Detroit is the only one that I can think of as 'in the process of dying' - a point of which I want to integrate into my setting. I'm thinking it's not just metaphorical death - I'll throw in some God machine mechanisations to help explain how it went from such a thriving city to what it is now.

          I think I need to start thinking about notable NPCs for the area. My thoughts are:

          Krewes - I like the imagery of clowns as sin-eaters, so I'm going to make one Krewe in particular based on this. I want to contrast this away from characters like the Joker and Harley Quinn, but I also like the imagery of a sexy female Harlequin. I also don't like the imagery created by Insane clown posse for this, although they ARE a major gang apparently so perhaps some of their members will be from that area, but others from straight entertainment aspects. I've got three members in mind so far:
          1. A sexy Harlequin - I'm thinking a lithe black woman with white face paint who is also a skilled acrobat
          2. A muscle bound clown who does more of the heavy lifting grunt work - polish background I think
          3. A black as night Mime who is the 'speak softly but carry a big stick' leader of the group. I'm thinking his Schtick will be to coat himself in full body paint and perform as a statue - waiting for donations. I'm still deciding what his true ethnicity and history is - but mostly I just want him to be the big bad scary leader that no one really speaks directly to.
          Still debating what other Krewes will be in the area. Drugs will probably be a big theme for one, and Religion for another.

          Police - I mentioned before that I want to add Inferno demons - I think the police will be where they make their presence known. Some will be mortals, many will be cultists and a select few will be possessed humans controlled largely by Inferno demons. Mostly, these will be direction-less, in it for the hell raising boys club - I just want the police to actively be the cruel bad guys so far.

          Tengu Corp - of note will be a corporation called Tengu Corp - which will deal with everything from foreclosures to investments in Detroit -a front for the God Machine. I don't want the GM to be a major player in my campaign - but it will be the mostly oblivious backbone that caused all the ruckus. The thinking is that a particularly powerful demon was captured here in the underworld and several angels - along with a large cult - now maintain infrastructure that keeps it mostly contained. The demon itself is not all that dangerous now, but its passive influence is largely to blame for Detroits recent ruin over the last 30 years. I might rename the corperation if I can find a more appropriate real life corperation that helps color Detroits local scene. If it's not obvious by the way: I'm referring to inferno Demon being contained by infrastructure - not GM demons. The infrastructure is like a damn holding a force of nature at bay.

          I think next I need to come up with something to make the setting a bit more colourful and cheerful. I'm really second guessing the whole 'lost and alone' theme - this is starting to feel too depressing. Perhaps I'll make one or two of the other local Krewes a bit friendlier and more cheerful than I originally envisioned.

              This generally looks like a good start. I hate Inferno with the fire of a thousand suns, but Demon the Decent is very good. If using the God Machine anyway, Decent Demons may work well/better. The only advice I have about including the God Machine in a non-Mortals game (or any not focused on it) is opposing a fundamental aspect of reality itself can make whatever else you are doing seem like small potatoes and take some of the wind from other baddies. It is not necessarily bad, but it is something to be aware of. Some players don't care, some players find it a distraction.

              If you are looking for deeper roleplaying and a deeper game, having helpful NPCs the PCs care about helps. If everyone is against them, the world is a dungeon crawl with more rules.Friendly NPCs can provide romance options (which often make people think about their characters more, I have found) and can be targeted by bad guys since they are probably easier to kill than Sin-Eaters (a T-Rex is easier to kill than a Sin-Eater). You just really, really have to balance that last part. You don't want players worrying about them being potential liabilities.

              I'll note here you need to look a lot into Haunts and ghosts for them to deal with. What you have is cool (and Balance of Shadows, my game, deals with other line stuff a lot) but many of their powers work either best or only with ghosts, and ghosts and haunts are how they get their juice back. You need to be sure to have a good deal of that for them to deal with. Just don't expect most ghosts to be much challenge...all of them can punch a ghost into submission, and if any of Stigmata Key ghosts will be nearly helpless. So plan ghost related challenges more around roleplaying and such than combat, mostly.

                          I don't think that there's really a need to tone done the "lost and alone" though. In a way I think that sense of isolation and alienation is core to what Detroit has become. Especially when you look at the history of racial violence and white flight in the city, there's a great deal of "us vs. them" survival mentality almost bred into the city's bones.

                          What Baron Samedi says is all quite true, though I'd argue that there are ways to make the world feel alive and engaging while keeping every powerful NPC an enigmatic and dangerous enemy. It's just a matter of fleshing out the NPCs like people. Maybe they all start out wanting the party dead, but they have their reasons, and with work and negotiation, they can be turned to see the party as assets, allies, or even unlikely friends.

                          My personal advice for running a Geist game is to make a bigger bad out there that the sin eaters have to stay concealed from, much like GMC Demons and Angels. Otherwise I find it's really hard for the players and their characters to give even the slightest care towards concealing themselves from the mortal populace... they have no mechanical reason to do so, and almost no fluff written as to why they would do so. Sin Eaters with the right manifestations can shrug off missile strikes, and in the off-chance that you kill them, they'll just get right back up again 24 hours later. It feels like asking Achilles to stay home from battle - the only incentive he has is one of his own making, not fear of his enemies, no matter how numerous.

                          P.S. Inferno is wonderful and I think the point of it is that Demons *aren't* interesting... but they give incentive to people, who are interesting, do interesting things. I'd be interested in hearing why you don't like it so much Baron, but this topic I think is not the place to do so. =P

                                                  Back on topic, it's interesting to note that there are vast salt mines and deposites beneath Detroit. Salt, in current occult practices at least, is often used in wards and bans... just what, in the world of darkness, might need to be contained beneath that much; and what might have started leaking out with the mining company's operations?
                                                  As for possibly sinister companies, the Illitch family owns a startling number of businesses in and around Detroit. The family was presented the Key to Detroit in 2008. Now, obviously, in the real world there's not much sinister about the family... but this is WoD, and there's always fun to be had with influential, apparently respectable, and incredibly wealthy families.

                                                  Yes. People that form cults tend to be attention-hungry. Maybe not on the grand, celebrity scale, but even then most aren't content to build a cult and say "Y'know what guys? I think we're big enough. I think we've spread the word to as many people as possible and should just sit back now."

                                                  Errr... most ghosts *want* to be seen and acknowledged. That's the only way they can find resolution or continue the works they had in life. Depending on the lore you're drawing from, their existence can depend on others remembering them. I very much doubt that a ghost who wants to be left alone feels very well served by nobody acknowledging her existence, in as much as a ghost has the agency to "feel" in a conventional sense.

                                                  As for the Geists themselves, they're insane, yes... but secret-keeping? That depends on how you choose to portray them. I think there's plenty of evidence in the book to support that they only appear to keep secrets because they're too mad to really know what's relevant information for their Sin-Eater... and a "Game of Second Chances" applies to both members of the bargain. With the charicterization of many Sin-Eaters as being driven to hedonism, the best place to find that lifestyle is as one of the rich and famous.
                                                  ...and what of the benevolent Sin-Eaters, the ones who want to make their abilities known for the medical and scientific breakthroughs they might inspire? Maybe they don't want the celebrity, but being globally famous is the safest way to ensure that you *aren't* packed off to a super-secret lab as a super-secret lab rat. What about the brooding author who's never been published? Become famous and publishers are killing each other for the rights to sell your work... and so on.

                                                  Suspicious of the government and media? I'm not sure what that has to do with anything. Either they have enough evidence of Massive Malevolent Government Conspiracy (tm), which falls under my recommendation above, or they don't.

                                                      One thing you might want to look into is local crime statistics by area: they're a good way to get to get a general layout of the SES of a place, especially if cross-referenced with demographic information (although in Detroit that might be a bit tricky, since the percentage of black people has been rising steadily, since they frequently make up a decent amount of the poor in America, near I can tell, and those who can afford to have tended to leave Detroit. Still potentially useful, though).

                                                      Also, a common...mistake by STs new to WoD: if you want an NPC to be a major antagonist, don't just make them a physical powerhouse. In fact, I'd recommend making them slightly physically weaker than the PCs. WoD isn't like D&D, where (barring the save-vs-die spells), the amount of damage you can do is capped, or is more-or-less easily predictable. If your players know much about the game at all, and if you allow stuff from non-core books like Armory and Armory: Reloaded, they can put out amounts of damage that would, according to the guidelines in the core, totally obliterate vehicles multiple times a turn. And it is much, much easier to stack a characters damage output than it is to stack their ability to soak up damage, unless you give them Attributes or Skills or similar in the 20+ range. Good antagonists in the WoD are hard to deal with either because they can't be found, thus forcing the PCs to capture and interrogate underlings or follow clues, or they can't be attacked in the conventional sense because they're in the public eye and attacking them will bring waay too much heat down on the PCs, or they're not something that can be punched. Golden rule of antagonists: the moment you stat them out and put them in a situation where the players can thwack them, they'll thwack them, and almost certainly thwack them hard.

                                                      I'm reminded of a Changeling game I ran once where the weather was going nuts, and the players were tapped to investigate it, eventually discovering, after much legwork, that the problem was that an anti-Fae shield the local Freehold had put up broke the ancient pact between the Seasons and the Fae, so the only way to fix it was to go into deep Arcadia, encountering their old Fae captors in situations that, even with iron, they were near-powerless in, and reforge the pacts to secure the stability of the seasons for the world. If the solution was "find X, thwack X", then it wouldn't have been nearly so satisfying or interesting.

                                                          I'll note something else I do, may be helpful.

                                                          I often tend to have fewer "villains" and more, to borrow a fellow ST's term, "agendad NPCs" Agendad NPCs are trying to do something. They have a plan, which they are carrying out. The PCs may decide to help, oppose, or ignore them. But more often then not, an agenda's NPC has other Agendad NPCs they are eventually going to clash with. It's a thing. Unless the players ignore them, they will eventually clash with one or the other. I like the situation because it gives the players the flexibility to make the decision of who to back, and puts the onus on them to find out what the Agendad NPCs true goals are...they could wind up working for someone who seems pretty friendly but is pretty nasty.

                                                          Now, I do have some outright villains, who will not work with the PCs for any reason. But I try to have a reason for that beyond "the are evil" Ivory Claws, from among the Pure, could be good villians due to their hatred of Necromancy, and the inbreeding and abuse are just more reasons to hate them. A Keberoi could work as well. Looking at the (excellent, BTW) post above mine, a Keberoi could fill the place of "true fae" here.

                                                          A few books have some ideas for stuff that may not be where you would think to. War Against the Pure for Werewolf: The Forsaken has some very interesting shifters that could make for good antagonists or agendad NPCs. The PCs are tracking a slasher, only to discover it is one of the Colony, a Werecat who hunts humans with low Morality, but must kill one per month or go mad...and she has no way to know if a human is evil or not, she can only kill a human and hope. Or the wereroaches who send spirit eggs into food all around them and make more wereroaches, or those fishmen that live in isolated villiages. Good stuff. World of Darkness: Slasher should be very useful in a Geist game if you want a more mundane slasher. I really, really like Summoners for Mage, it is a great book I have used a lot despite never having run Mage. The Men in Black are delicious answers to the question of why bother with a Masquerade...Oh just let the PCs try and fight them. The Numbers Stations section of that book is rather interesting as well, and the book is one of the few with Cthonians in it. Also the Abyssal entities which are, for my money, the scariest things in New WoD.
