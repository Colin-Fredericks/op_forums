    Geist Chronicles: Integirty, Synergy and Conflict of Minds

    09-18-2014, 01:24 PM
    MugaSofa has asked me to start posting work from my sadly unfinished Geist Chronicles book (which could arguably be the Krewe Chronicles, or Geist 2.0 I suppose) So to start with I present the rules for Integrity, Synergy and Conflict of Minds. If this thread gets interest there will be more to follow.

    These three are what I consider the thematic core of the game. That's not to say I think they'll dominate the play-time, rather I think of them like feeding in Vampire. You may not run many feeding scenes, but the need for blood defines vampire society. In Geist terms they represent why a couch potato who comes back is going to get off that sofa and size his second chance by the throat - he has to invest in his attributes to defend himself against the Geist. He has to go out and indulge his Geist's Passions. He has to found a Krewe and live by it's mythology. Or he has to unearth his Geist's Past.

    Because if he doesn't do at least one of those, the Geist is going to take the body for joyrides. Now this dosn't automatically require interacting with the dead, but it easily can with the right Passions, Krewe Mythology or if Ghosts are the best witnesses to an event deep in the past.


    So without further adu:
    Modified Traits: Integrity and Synergy

    A person is bound to a Geist. There are two minds and so there are two traits that affect the Sin-Eater’s mental fortitude: Integrity and Synergy. The Geist’s own mind is too inflexible to be represented by a 1-10 scale.

    Integrity

    Integrity measures the mental state of the human Sin-Eater, in this it remains mostly unchanged. The Bound have died and returned with a Geist glued to their mind and soul, that’s a pretty extreme situation and it can change a person, but it is a change caused by trauma and circumstance. The Sin-Eater’s mind and morality have not been metaphysically altered by their reincarnation.

    Mechanically Integrity is unchanged, however when an effect depends on a character's Integrity rating Sin-Eaters use Integrity + Synergy (maximum of 10) unless stated otherwise. The actual array of Breaking Points is different. Sin-Eaters are not nearly as shocked by the supernatural as a typical mortal and Some Sin-Eaters can kill without suffering a Breaking Point, others are quite proud of the fact they retain the usual instinctive revulsion at the thought of ending someone’s life.

    Synergy

    Synergy measures the working relationship between the Sin-Eater and the Gesit. At high Synergy the pair could each control half of the body, acting in perfect unison, more importantly both consider themselves to be on some level parts of the same person. It is hard to maintain a strong sense of self, a firm Integrity, at high synergy.

    A more modest Synergy would represent a solid working relationship where both get time to pursue their own interests and avoid pushing each others buttons or a pair who are lucky enough to have common interests.

    A partnership with low or no Synergy usually means that the Sin-Eater is in charge - it was originally their body, they have the home-turf advantage - but punctuated with frequent clashes over control.

    Mechanically Synergy increases a Sin-Eater’s maximum Support pool, each dot in Synergy allows one extra point of Support. Synergy is also added to certain dice pools, most noticeably Conflict of Minds in which Synergy provides a bonus when the Sin-Eater initiates a conflict but a penalty when the Geist initiates.

    Sidebar: Rumours on the Twilight Network

    First Krewe I ever ran with, we had this guy with a hundred romans in his head. Believe it! He started turning Roman himself, wearing armour and talking in latin. So the other day I saw a fresh, same Geist only my brv was one of the romans.

    Support

    All Sin-Eaters have a pool of Support equal to 3 + Synergy. Support is an abstract value which can represent many different things: It could represent your Geist feeling gratitude or liking it’s partner. It could represent your Geist being willing to put aside it’s own needs when the pair are in danger. It could even represent your Geist being afraid of your Sin-Eater, maybe she’s threatened one of the Geist’s Anchors.

    Spending Support

    The player can spend one point of Support as a reflexive action to automatically win any Conflict of Minds roll. The player decides whether this represents the Sin-Eater defeating the Geist or if the Conflict of Minds never happened.

    Regaining Support
    • You regain one point of Support if the Sin-Eater helps the Geist indulge it’s Passion or indulges that Passion on behalf of the Geist.
    • The Sin-Eater may opt to automatically fail a Conflict of Minds roll in exchange for one point of Support.
    • If you uncover a new and significant piece of information about your Geist’s Past, refill your Support pool completely (Geists tend to forget about their human life in between partners).
    • If you resolve one of your Geist’s Anchors refill your Support pool completely.


    Sidebar: Morbid Passions

    Some Geist’s have Passions so abhorrent that their partner would never willingly allow them to indulge. A player can opt to declare their Geist’s passions Morbid as an out of game declaration. If they do so the Sin-Eater can never regain Support from indulging their Geist’s Passion. As a consolation prize they gain a Beat every time the Geist initiates a Conflict of Minds while their Support pool is empty. (This is in addition to the usual Beat for losing a Conflict of Minds)

    Morbid Passions do not have to involve eating babies. Anything that the Sin-Eater personally finds repulsive could qualify, even if a hypothetical average person would consider it perfectly normal. It is recommended though, that Morbid Passions are only invoked when there is little chance of the Sin-Eater changing her mind.

    In essence this rule exists for players to declare as a matter of narrative intent that they are interested in a story about their character fighting their Geist. As a corollary the group should be forthright in saying no if the story is supposed to be about exploring the Underworld and doesn't want the plot to get waylaid by a rabid Geist. Role playing is a communal activity, it is important that everyone is on the same page before play starts.


    Conflict of Minds
    Sidebar: A Convenient Reference
    Here’s the base dice pool for a Conflict of Minds.
    Torn: Strength + Stamina
    Silent: Resolve + Stamina
    Stricken: Manipulation + Composure
    Prey: Intelligence + Wits
    Forgotten: Wits + Composure

    Geists are not loyal lapdogs who dispense advice and provide occult power. They have their own goals and Passions. If the Geist feels that they aren’t getting enough out of the relationship or that the Sin-Eater is making a really bad decision they may try to seize control themselves.

    The Torn attack their partner to take control. For the Silent, sometimes they just can’t endure any longer and when they give in the Geist is left in charge. Stricken Geists favour persuasion and trickery. The Prey cannot be opposed, Sin-Eaters must anticipate their desires and give them no reason to insist. Finally The Forgotten are varied and random.

    Yet for all this diversity a Conflict of Minds is simple. When your Geist attempts to take over roll your base Conflict of Minds pool - Synergy with any situational modifiers.

    Dramatic Failure: The Geist takes over for a scene, it’s control is so absolute that the Sin-Eater blacks out for part or all of the scene. Take the Dude, Where’s my Body condition. Also: take a Beat.

    Failure: The Geist takes over for a scene, leaving the Sin-Eater as just a voice in their own head. This is usually less traumatic than it sounds, something about being bound at the soul helps ease the experience. It’s not enough to make watching yourself go on a murder spree palatable, but it helps. Also: take a Beat.

    Success: The Sin-Eater remains in control, the Geist may not initiate any further Clashes of Wills this scene.

    Exceptional Success: The Sin-Eater remains in control, the Geist may not initiate any further Clashes of Wills this scene.
    Before rolling the player may choose to spend a point of Support instead. The player decides if this represents defeating their Geist or retroactively declaring that the Conflict of Minds never happened. Either way, spending a point of Support mechanically counts as a Success.


    Sin-Eaters can also initiate Clashes of Wills. Any time the player wishes the Geist to act in a certain way and it does not want to the Sin-Eater can declare a Conflict of Minds. Roll your base Conflict of Minds pool + Synergy with any situational modifiers.

    Dramatic Failure: The Sin-Eater erroneously believes that the Geist will behave and lets it loose. Roleplay the result.

    Failure: The Geist’s mood remains unchanged.

    Success: The Geist is persuaded or threatened into playing along. It will do what the Sin-Eater wants.

    Exceptional Success: The Geist and Sin-Eater come to an accord and act as one. Take the Teamwork! condition.
    As always, a point of Support can be spent in place of rolling for a single automatic success.



    A common motivation for Sin-Eaters to invoke a Conflict of Minds is if they want to Manifest their Geist to fight off some threat. When the Sin-Eater invokes a Conflict of Minds while in danger the results are different.

    Dramatic Failure or Failure: The Geist is willing to help defend the Sin-Eater, but it will do so it’s way. It might carry the Sin-Eater away from the battle or ignore pleas to fight non-lethally.

    Success: The Geist is persuaded or threatened into playing along. It will do what the Sin-Eater wants.

    Exceptional Success: The Geist and Sin-Eater come to an accord and act as one. Take the Teamwork! condition.

    As always, a point of Support can be spent in place of rolling for a single automatic success.

                                  I was thinking about this and - considering the directions of GMC - I'm thinking there's a far simpler answer that involves making the players do the extra work rather than the ST.

                                  Firstly, my interpretation of Synergy is that it's a more passive score: it represents the health of the bond you have with a Geist who is attempting to be essentially your subconscious personality. Basically, a high synergy score represents to what amounts to one mind: although your Geist might disagree with your choices, it'd never truly override them because you take the perspective of the rational mind.A low Synergy score to contrast would be about the equivalent of a person with multiple splintered personalities- two minds that share the bare minimum amount of ground and contradict eachother, with the Geist even sometimes taking control.

                                  That's why I'm gonna suggest you use conditions rather than a direct contest of wills.

                                  To use an example that I really like that's on the boards, lets use a Geist that gets caught up in the thrill of the hunt. During play, the Sin Eaters come across a dangerous predator than the ST might apply the following condition:

                                  Bloodlust

                                  Your character is titillated by the idea of hunting worthy game. You take a -2 to mental rolls unrelated to tracking, hunting or killing of this prey.

                                  Beat: gain a beat whenever you make significant progress in hunting and killing the target.

                                  Resolution: this condition is resolved when the target is either killed or no longer seen as worthy game. Gain a beat if you resolve this condition by killing the target. This condition can also be removed with 24 hours of rest and meditation.
                                  Just a thought.
