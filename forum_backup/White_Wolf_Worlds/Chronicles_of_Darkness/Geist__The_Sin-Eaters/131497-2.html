                                As the guy that first proposed (and later wrote) the idea of making Sin-Eaters nigh impossible to kill for good, this thread is highly entertaining.

                                My thought was that actually managing to kill a SE should be a big deal, and should come out of the story. I also wanted to give STs a chance to play a bit harder with their PCs than normal, and to more fully explore the death/resurrection theme.

                                Personally, I favor ideas that trap SEs in lose-lose situations that would make the Geist want to bugger off, rather than catch-all methods of simply killing the SE. Killing someone is boring, trapping them for all eternity (maybe) seems much more interesting to me.

                                                Nyx is right. You could do all this stuff with chaining them up or exorcising the geist after you've downed them (according to Hunter: Mortal Remains, not possible without a specific ritual like the one described for Sin-Eaters), but it's much easier to just cut off the Sin-Eater's head. Or at least after you kill them, just sever their head. Removing a vital organ like the heart or brain is not necessarily enough to keep it from regrowing (see Null Mysteriis on the matter of Fog Men in Mortal Remains), but severing a being's head entirely is generally a good catch-all for permanent death. The real trick is to make the geist give up on repairing its host's body.

                                                            Disclaimer: Of course this is all my opinion and perception of how geists and their Bound work.

                                                            It would probably take an extraordinary level of genuine partnership between a geist and its host to make the geist work that hard to keep them. Most have a working relationship with their Bound, but only to the extent that they can use their Bound to fulfill their own drives. In the end a Sin-Eater really is just a vessel, one that can (and will) ultimately be replaced.

                                                            In any case...I'm not sure a geist could leave its host to get help in that situation, or get help at all? That seems to be part of the Bargain: As long as a geist is attached to its Bound, it can stave off the energy of death to a certain extent because it's sharing its own deathly nature with its Bound. That's not something other ephemeral entities could "help" with; they're not bonded to the Sin-Eater in a way that allows them to manipulate his energies. Likewise, the only geists capable of traveling on or between planes by themselves are unfettered. The whole point of joining with a Sin-Eater is to have a physical body, and the downside is that they're tied to that body until they let go of it. Once they let go, they can't stave off its death anymore; the Sin-Eater reverts to being a simple mortal and passes the threshold of death fully, unable to return except maybe as a raised zombie. If zombie Sin-Eaters were a thing, then geists wouldn't need to keep their hosts alive.

                                                            I've always seen the Bargain as a kind of osmosis - the geist has a deathly nature that "bleeds" over to the Sin-Eater when they bond. The more the SE's physical body is damaged, the more energy the geist has to bleed over to fix it. At a certain point, fixing it either isn't feasible or just isn't a worthwhile investment anymore, and it's better to let go and look for a new mortal instead.
