    Interjecting Wraith into Geist - The Sea of Sorrow and The Ocean of Fragments

    08-23-2014, 09:35 AM
    I recently got to reading up on Wraith, and despite only having gotten about 40 pages into the thing, I had an idea based on what I've allready read.
    Wraith talks about something called The Far Shores which lie beyond a grand ocean, which is basically a mimicry of all the mortal religions afterlifes, Valhalla, Nirvana, Paradise and so forth. Doesn't this sound sorta Dead Dominion-esque?
    Additionally, The Book of the Dead conviently fails to mention anything about the opposing shore of The Ocean of Fragments, while having a Dead Dominion which practises something very much akin to the Soulforging/Moliating that Wraiths practise, while the dread Tempest shares a few properties with The Ocean of Fragments itself.

    So my idea for interjecting Wraith into Geist "cosmology" (if one can call it that), is the following setting hack:

    __________________________________________________ __________________________________

    When something - anything - dies, it usually fades into Oblivion and/or Trancendence, if one believes those are seperate things. However, sometimes something shackles a would-be dead-thing to reality. It can be an emotion fealt so deep down and burning so terribly hot, as to defy the very imaterial laws of Death itself, a love so powerful that no lack of pulse will sate the desire or a magical spell woven by a mad warlock. Whichever the case, when something that should've by all natural laws died but lingers, something happens.
    That something is the splintering of the object or person into two things; The Noumen and The Phenom.
    The Noumen is the splinter of the thing which expresses how the object was seen by others and behaved in accordance with its enviroment. The Splinter of Ideas. It is the emotional bond between lovers, the imaterial kinship between an artist and his tools. The Noumen sustains the Shade via the shackles that its mortal life imbued it with, and creates Anchors that traps the Ghost in a quasi-material state, not enough to facilitate material existance - usually anyway - but not weak enough to allow it to fade peachfully into Oblivion. Those Ephemeral Shades bound by the Noumen are what the Sin-Eaters call Ghosts. Due to the fact that the world usually remembers a person imperfectly, the Shades bound by The Noumen usually have a one-track mind, and are possessed of much less agenda than those bound by he Phenom.
    The Phenom is the splinter of the thing which expresses how the object saw itself. The Splinter of Form. Its burning Passions and desires come to unnatural expression when the unholy will of the dead-man keeps the Shade from reaching Oblivion. The sheer weight of the Shades lust and desires are enough to ressist the entropic pull of the great nothing, which lies below the Underworld. Though they are far less numerous than their Ghost brethren, the Wraiths are vastly increased mobility and power.

    It is entirely possibly for a being to leave both a Wraith and a Ghost behind upon death, though this is rarely the case. What would happen if these two dead halves of a persons mind were to be rejoined post-mortem, is anyones guess. For even though both are constructed of Ephemera, they share very different - albietly equally tormentet - existances.

    Just as these two concepts have different metaphysical meanings, they have very different metaphysical realms of belonging aswell. Outside of physical reality lie more than merely the Twillight, into which The Bound are permitted vision. Twillight exists are a strange frequency of existance, somewhere between the world that is, and the world that was. The dread Shadowlands exist by virtue of the memory of the Wraiths that inhabit it. When a Wraith enters the Shadowlands, he brings with him the memory of how the Skinlands looked when he still walked the Earth, and his dead-memory of how things should be, colors how the Shadowlands are by virtue of his desire to see the world as he remembers it. Their memory is imperfect however. Death forever warps the perpective of even the most level-headed Wraith, and the buildings that take rise when a Wraith remembers them are colored darkly by his intangible nature. It is a shadowed place born of the stygian minds of its inhabitants in which no sane living person will ever feel at home.
    Twillight then, is something akin to a stepping stone between worlds. Not quite material existance, and not quite the gaunt realms of the Shadowlands.

    What lies beneth is the dread Underworld. Just as the Phenom and Noumen color the shackles that the Shades wear, so too does it color the great stygian deep that they are forever pulled towards.
    The Noumen, being the manifestation of ideas and perspective, are colored most closely by the human culture of the Quick. Ideas bleed through the Noumen that bind the Ghosts there, and only after something as become - and stayed - endemic of the living culture, will it drip down into the Autochthonous Depths.
    As one goes deeper into the dead-realm of the Noumen, things become more heavily mired in thoughts and ideas. Culture from centuries ago lie there, half forgotten and perverted by the entropic pull of the Void. Rivers of Death flow through the Domains, to seperate one idea-world from the next, and stop the realms from flowing into each other, just as cultural barrieres have preventet the same for the cultures of the Quick.
    The dread-scape of the Phenom are colored less by the ideas of the living culture above, and more by the weight of the desires of its lifeless inhabitants. Here lie the Dark Kingdoms, given purpose and form by the lusts and Passions of the Wraiths who lord these shadow-vaunted lands. These dead empires are forged from the stillborn dreams of the Wraiths who come here and the unfortunate Thralls who become little more than forge-material in the dread Soulforges. The lusts, desires and Passions of the Wraiths burn strong here with stygian cold fire, and if a Wraith isn't careful, he might easily become the foundation upon which the blackened Citadels are build.

    Between these two Underworlds, lie a great subterranean ocean. The Sunless Sea, The Sea of Sorrow, The Ocean of Fragments. Whatever one might choose to call it, it lies as a great divide between the realms of Phenom and Noumen, The Dead Dominions and The Dark Kingdoms. Only a single ship sails these black waters; The feared Freighter.
    Only this most feared and respected of ships is strong enough to withstand the Tempest, which roils endlessly out on the waves of this vast ocean. Some Wraiths speculate that the very waters of this ocean are the condensed fluid of the dread Tempest, or perhaps vise-versa. Perhaps the Tempest is the evaporated version of the sea. Between the Dark Kingdoms and The Dead Dominion lie the island that holds the mist-shrouded Labyrith, which all Geister and Wraith alike, avoid. Since the Labyrinth leads unknowably down into - and perhaps underneth - the black sea, some whispers have started, that perhaps within that hellish place lie the wellspring from which the entire sea spring. Some blackened abyss leaking pure nothingness? A bleeding god? None have yet returned to tell of what they found.
    Many fear that the deepest pits of this ocean holds nothing save Oblivion, and while the Ghosts of The Dead Dominions respectfully refer to its guardian, the vast Leviathan, by the name Kerberoi, the Wraiths have a different name for it; Malfean

    That same Tempest rage most terribly in The Dark Kingdoms, fore they lack the Avernian Gates that Geister and sorcerer alike use to navigate it in The Dead Dominion. Perhaps an enterprising Sin-Eater could make a name for himself, forging such Gates where now exist only Byways?
    Speaking of Sin-Eaters, they hold a curious position within all this. They are a merger between the Noumen-driven mind of the Geist and the Phenom-driven mind of the Sin-Eater. Not quite ghosts or Wraith and not quite material living, these beings may walk both realms relatively unmolested, as the Noumen of the Geist feels at home within the Dead Dominions and the Phenom of the Sin-Eaters feels at home in the Dark Kingdoms (as much as one can in a black hell of stygian cathedrals forged of wailing ghosts, of course).
    Remors are whispered between The Bound Necromancers, of Avernian Gates that lead directly into the Shadowlands and perhaps even ones that circumwent the terribly Tempest of that place, and into the Dark Kingdoms themselves. Though it doesn't come naturally The Bound, with practise and a determined mind, they can learn to navigate the Shadowlands and Dark Kingdoms as readily they do Twillight and The Dead Dominions.

    __________________________________________________ __________________________

    Puff. That was a mouthful. Alot more than I had initially imagined, but meh. I've tried to write it as vague as I could, so as to not give everything away and do anyone the disservice of spoiling the Wraith book. Unless this gets deleted for spoiling Wraith, I might return and update it, as I read through Wraith and find more inspiration for the setting hack.
    I hope you can excuse my massive wall of text :P
