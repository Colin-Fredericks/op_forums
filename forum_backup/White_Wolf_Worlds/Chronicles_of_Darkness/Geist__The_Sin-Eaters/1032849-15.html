        Just not wanting to die is a basic qualification for all of these Burdens. The supposition that the Burdens seem to offer is that there's always a why that's more sophisticated than just wanting to live, which is something I arguably agree with. Why do you want to keep living? Everyone has an answer more specific than just wanting to live, even if all they can manage in the moment is that they don't want to die.

        Maybe your 1e Sin-Eater who didn't want to die just came back, but whatever they did once they got back is probably suggestive of what their Burden actually was.

        Like, wild guess... I think a lot of people who just "don't want to die" probably fall under the Abiding, the Hungry or the Kindly Burdens.

        I was initially just gonna say the Hungry, and I contend that most of the motivations you'd think of probably fall under it. "I have more countries to see! More food to eat! More life to live! I don't wanna die!" et al.

        It's a weird shift to from a question which is reactive in nature to a proactive one, but I'm certain that it wouldn't actually be hard to address any first edition Geist character in this second edition framework in a compelling way.

        It's also possible that this might enhance a character by providing them with a greater motivating force than in First Edition, since I recall "What's my motivation?" being a consistent criticism of Geist 1e. :P

        An interesting thought: Your Burden might be more honest than you are.

                    Originally posted by White Oak Dragon View Post
                    I see where you're coming from, with Burdens making them seem more like ghosts. That makes sense to me, though, given that I believe it's been said that it's the ghost that makes the Bargain with the Geist. I'd imagine that those probably aren't the only Burdens that exist, though. There are probably others that might show up in a Players Guide in the future. I'm curious, though, what kind of individuals would you see as being unrepresented by the available choices? The only ones that have occurred to me so far would be the Frightened, those that can't let themselves rest because they are too terrified of what awaits them on the other side.
                    For me, it's more this than anything:

                    Originally posted by Darkfoxdev View Post
                    I like the burdens conceptually but I worry they will be resolved too easily without the storyteller bending over backwards to draw out the goal. All of the burdens have a clearly defined goal that realistically would not take long to resolve in most cases.
                    CoD already has an Aspirations system. Making Burdens the place where the splat starts seems to suggest that Sin-Eaters don't have much depth beyond whatever drives them to stay on this side of the grave. In 1e, the character determined the Threshold, but here it sounds like the Burden will determine the character.

                    As for additional Burdens, I understand the concept of forcing Bound to have specific reasons for wanting to come back rather than an open-ended "just don't want to die yet," but really that is a very valid thing that could drive someone to want to live. As a name, I suppose it could be called the Unfulfilled, those who regret not being able to experience everything life has to offer, but a problem I already see there is that it doesn't fit in with the other kinds of "drives" Burdens give. It's not a purpose to complete, because the act of living itself becomes the purpose. It goes beyond Hungry, because Hungry limits itself to a single anchor of some kind. This is what I mean that it feels limiting.
