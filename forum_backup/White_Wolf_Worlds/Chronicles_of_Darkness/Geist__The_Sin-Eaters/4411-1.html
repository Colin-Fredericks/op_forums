    That, and what I'm looking at is not so much bans as they are embodiments of what the Geist is. Much of the Geist core talks about how Geists seem "part-spirit," having replaced their regular ghost anchors with concepts, like fire or car crashes. Bans are things Spirits CAN'T do, what I'm going for are things Spirits (and, at least in this case, Geists) HAVE to do. Like they say in the core, a Geist of car crashes causes car crashes because that's what it is. It's the duty of the Bound to keep the Geist from getting way out of control and turning every road into an eighteen-car pileup. Rather than things the Bound can't do, I'm looking at things the Geist needs to do, because it's their nature, but that the Bound doesn't want to let them do.

                Oh, one change I made to a Manifestation (less a GMC update and more of a house rule) is to Phantasmal Marionette. The Bound's body and Geist's body actually "switch places," with the Geist body manifesting physically and the Bound's body "vanishing" within it. Both because I felt it thematically appropriate (and also because it's awesome) for the Bound to actually transform into her Geist. I also altered the additional attribute cap for Phantasmal Marionette, so the cap is your dots in Marionette, rather than a flat two. It's still logistically difficult to max out a single attribute, as well as bringing about potential crippling overspecialization.

                    Originally posted by ErikModi View Post
                    Compulsions (Looking for a better word.)
                    Geists are more than just ghosts. Some of their Achors have been replaced with concepts, like spirits. As a result, Geists do things. Not because they want, not because they like to, but because they have to. Under normal circumstances, a Bound can keep her Geist's more. . . anti-social tendencies in check. But as Synergy drops, it can get more and more difficult to keep the Geist's behavior confined to the Bound's own psyche. Sometimes, the behavior gets out.
                    When a Bound loses Synergy, she may take a Compulsion and a Beat. Doing so means she never has to roll against that particular Breaking Point again, however, each Compulsion applies a -1 modifier to further Discord rolls. A Bound may only have three Compulsions.
                    Compulsions are closely linked to a Geist's Archetype. As such, the Compulsions tend to escalate withing a sphere of influence, rather than branching out into new archetypes (though rare Geists who do embody more than one concept may do so). A Geist of vengeance, for example, will push her Bound to ever-increasing extremes in the cause of punishing wrongdoing, rather than making her give her money to the homeless. This is reflecting by selecting multiple closely-related Compulsions, or the same Compulsion more than once, increasing the stakes of fulfilling the Compulsion. For instance, a single Vengeance Compulsion may be satisfied by seeing the wrongdoer arrested. Two Vengeance Compulsions may require taking an active role in bringing the target down. Three Vengeance Compulsions may only be satiated with the wrongdoer's death at the Bound's hands. Storytellers and players should work together to determine the exact limitations and drives of each Compulsion for the purposes of their stories. Examples are listed below, but you should be encouraged to make up your own, tailoring them to your conception of your Geist and its relationship with your Bound. Compulsions generally require a character to perform an action, which may be risky or socially unacceptable. At the Storyteller's discretion, a Bound may earn Beats as Compulsions force her into dangerous situations, or regain Willpower as the Geist's purpose is reaffirmed.
                    A Compulsion can be resisted for a scene by spending a Willpower point. Each compulsion triggered in a scene must be resisted seperately.
                    Charity: Your Geist follows the Golden Rule to a fault. When you see someone in need, and you have the means to help them, you must do so.
                    Fire: Your Geist is tied to the primal element of fire, and will not allow you to extinguish any burning flame.
                    Vengeance: Your Geist is a spirit of vengeance. Anyone you perceive as having wronged you or someone else and getting away with it must be punished.
                    Fell in love with this, the moment i read it. I decided to try and incorporate it into my ongoing campaign, but with a twist. I'm running it more Hunter: The Vigil style, by the way they can sculp their own morality. To put it bluntly, whenever the Sin-Eater and Geist agrees very strongly on a topic, which would otherwise be a cause of Discord, i allow my players to take a Complusion, and replace that Discord trigger with the Complusion.
                    As an example of this having happened, was when one of my players decided that her slightly feminististic Sin-Eater had to kill a known serial rapist, and murderer. Her Geist, being something along the lines of Geist-of-hate-crimes, decided this was a perfectly reasonable action and no Discord was triggered. Instead, i asked her if she would be ok, with her mind merging more closely with her Geist. She thought the idea was pants-droppingly amazing, and so i gave her a Compulsion. I'm not quite sure what it's gonna amount to, but i'm thinking something along the lines of "Let no act of prejudice go unpunished.", though I'm alittle worried thats gonna make the Geist feel too alturistic and beneign.

                    Anyway. Loved your idea. Shamelessly stole it. Love you for it.

                    Cheers ~ Krobeles.

                                    I keep thinking to the Geist of Car Crashes mentioned in the core rulebook. They state that a Geist of Car Crashes doesn't cause car crashes because it enjoys it, or wants people to suffer, or wants their insurance premiums to go up. It causes car crashes because that's what it is. In the case of Compulsions for such a Geist, the Compulsion would be to cause car crashes. Starting with minor fender-benders, then more serious accidents, and culminating in multi-car pileups. Ten or more one-ton hunks of steel colliding with each other at high speeds, and one entity being responsible for it, seems like a definition of "mass murder" to me.

                                    To me, this underlines the central conflict between Bound and Geist. Your Geist embodies some concept, and as a result it wants to do things. And these things are probably not nice, like cause car crashes. It's the responsibility of the Bound to tell the Geist "no." We won't cause a car crash today. Or tomorrow. Or the day after that. Compulsions represent loosing that control, in small ways at first, and then in larger ways. Fighting your Geist isn't fun, but sometimes its necessary to keep it in check.

                                    Also remember how Bound afford each other respect based on the "badassery" of their Geist. Not necessarily because the Geists are more powerful, but because keeping their anti-social tendencies in check requires a more exceptional person.

                                            Ok, I'm going to describe something, and to be clear: I'm saying, in no way, that this is representative of all feminists, most feminists, or anything but a vanishingly small proportion of a particular kind of person. Kay? Kay.

                                            If people immerse themselves in a way of viewing the world, or a kind of attitude or similar, it can come to affect the rest of the way they view the world. Think of us and how we can sometimes start to view the world through as a story, or how teachers can sometimes start to treat everyone like kids. Stuff like that. So if a particularly angry feminist views things through the lens of seeing sexism in things, it's possible for them to start to generalise and see sexism/power struggles/discrimination where perhaps nobody else would (I think this is where those really angry and more extreme feminists you see sometimes come from). I'm thinking the Tumblr-crowd level of crazy here. And that's just normal psychology, what if you had an alien, amoral being of death in your head whispering all the time? Of course that guy is supporting rape culture by eyeing off that young woman in the short skirt. Of course that shopkeeper only didn't short-change you because you checked. Of course those parents over there pushed their son to play with transformers and their daughter to play with Barbie. Just becomes the paradigm you work in.

                                            Now, imagine the "Let no act of prejudice go unpunished" in that kind of a situation, and I think you'll start to see it's quite dark. Now, nobody (well, nobody sane) would say carving out the eyes or hands of a guy who eyed off a woman in a short skirt is any kind of appropriate and measured, let alone productive, response. But an alien being from beyond death, sharing the mind of someone who already views things at least partially through the lens of patriarchal control of women, who has recently suffered a pretty major breaking point?

                                            Just a thought.

                                            Again, in no way am I saying this kind of attitude is representative of feminists or feminism. Most are quite nice people. Strictly talking the extreme, somewhat lunatic fringe.

                                                There's fanaticism in every branch of thought, and its generally the fanatics who give the movement as a whole a bad name.

                                                To use your example of feminism, I have no problem with the concept that women are just as good, valuable, and worthwhile as men. I start having problems when the attitude becomes "women are superior to men" or "women must claim their rightful power by taking it from men." That's just prejudice of a different stripe.

                                                The Geist in question could start with punishing those extreme acts of fanaticism, but grow more and more fanatical itself as the Compulsion builds.

                                                However, Krobeles is saying that the Geist in question isn't a Geist of writing wrongs or anti-prejudice, its a Geist of hate crimes. It feeds off the negativity of hate crimes. That going to be a. . . distasteful thing to play out any way you slice it. But they way I intend Compulsions to work, they start out as small manifestations of the Geist's nature, and build. So this Geist may start by encouraging hate or prejudice around itself. Again, to use Allan53's example, a man looking at a woman in a short skirt may just be admiring the view, and have no intention of acting on it at all. The Geist may sense this, the Compulsion would be to alter that thought into something hateful. The Geist would want the Bound to use a Manifestation to encourage the guy in question to actually think about that short skirt as an invitation, to consider that old bad wrongness of "she's wearing that, she's asking for it."

                                                Now, a feminist sees this guy oggling this girl. While she might normally just have a mild negative response, think of him "pig" and move on, again, the Geist wants to encourage that hatred to grow. He again wants the Bound to expand that minor negative thought, changing the feminist's perspective on this guy from "just another bad man" to "a serial rapist pedophile waiting to happen."

                                                The Compulsion for a Geist of Hate Crimes, at the start, may be just as simple as "encourage hatred and animosity in people where it already exists." Just that little nudge, that little boost from "slightly annoyed" to "really annoyed," is all it needs, for now. As the Compulsion escalates, the Geist wants more and more. Moving into "creating hatred where none exists" and finally to "encouraging action based on animosity and hate."

                                                I bring up Manifestations here because of the way in which Geists act. A Bound with a Compulsion can fulfill it in any way they see fit, but Manifestations are a potent tool for doing so. A Geist of fire whose Bound has gained enough Fire Compulsions that they're a serial arsonist could start fires simply with gasoline and matches, or cold use Pyre-Flame Boneyard to spontaneously start fires over a potentially very wide area. A Bound who has the Geist of Car Crashes could just hop on the freeway and start engaging in criminally reckless driving, or could use Industrial Marrionette to crash cars while staying out of the way.
