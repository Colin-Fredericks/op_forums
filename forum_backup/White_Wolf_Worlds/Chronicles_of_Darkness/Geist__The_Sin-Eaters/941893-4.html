            Well you can say the Bound are a type of Claimed (they kind of are, in a way...). Yes, I know the Bound don't have Essence (Ban and Bane also only come in through Krewe formation also), I'm not being completely literal by stating that, just that there are some similarities (Synergy represents the level of patience a Geist has with their Bound to me)... Anyway, you could look at the rules for Urging that a fresh Claimed tends to go through mid-fusion, though after that the pair are bonded into one soul.

            I'd play it that it's not that the Bound is in complete control, the Bound's personality you're rping is actually both the Geist and it's former mortal persona fused (this is going that your character starts the game as a Bound). Synergy thus represents the level of hold the mortal/Geist half has, though both the Geist and mortal together as the Bound have the singular personality you're roleplaying as the normal character. As Synergy dropped in 1e, a Bound could get disorders (same as Morality drops gave them) and this could be the rp element of the Geist's persona taking over. It wasn't so much of bouts of being Urged rather than a slow shift over to fully Other.

            Now how to represent this in 2e Geist, I'm not exactly sure. I like the idea of a somewhat more fluid and see-sawing Synergy though a Bound doesn't have their Geist possess them until Synergy 0, it's more like they're Claimed and Synergy is the level of shift between the fused's personalities as one side or another becomes more dominant.

                I'd blend Beast and Werewolf morality. High synergy creates a condition that improves ghostly manifestations, but saddles you with a Ban and Bane or makes it impossible to use the Virtue analogue to regain WP. Low Synergy improves physical manifestations, and a condition that is a persistent penalty equal to psyche because the Geist is trying to take over. Middle ground gives a bonus to ceremonies, but no penalties.

                                Regarding the influence of the geist, the Chrod editions expand what each line's virtue and vice analogues represent about a character. In the case of sineaters, vice could represent a principal drive of the geist, giving the sineater a cheap and easy way to built up willpower by doing what it wants - while the virtue is rooter in their former life, harder to fully pursue with the demands of their new existence, but more fulfilling.

                                That would also illustrate the unusual nature of geist "possession," in that following either their own or the geist's drives can fulfill a sineater's sense of self.


                                On a different subject, some of the writing for keys and manifestations implies that the first level power of a given manifestation has to be activated before any higher level can be - so high level powers are two actions and two rolls. Assuming keys and manifestations are structured the same way, I'd like that to explicitly be the case, because it goes well with the idea of unlocking, and somewhat offsets the versatility of sineater powers.
