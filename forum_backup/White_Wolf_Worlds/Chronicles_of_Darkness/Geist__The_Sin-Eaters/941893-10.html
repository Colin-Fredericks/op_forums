In some ways, Sin-Eaters have it worse than most... having made the Bargain, they no longer even have the option of suicide.
        And as I noted, the price you pay is having a partly insane presence in your head constantly, the inability to stop seeing ghosts in all their grotesque states and attracting them to yourself. You can't shut your Geist up, you can't stop seeing it, and if your relationship with it deteriorates enough it becomes hostile, even outright stealing control of your body away. Any place someone has died leaves a Death Stain, which constantly gives a chilled sensation to Sin Eaters all around. Do you have any idea how hard it would be for a Sin Eater to find a decent place to live without these death stains causing problems? Trouble finds you whether you like it or not, and that is the price for being a Sin Eater.

        The reward is having a second chance at living. Again, why have angst about having a literal second chance of life, even if one with strings attached? You don't need to worry about burning at sunlight nor the task of sustaining yourself with blood; any fury that comes is inherent to yourself, not a bloodline of lycanthropy; using your powers does not cause the universe to hate you, nor are you harmed by your curiosity (anymore than a normal person); the world doesn't hate you for merely existing and trying to discover yourself; you don't have the beings who changed/created you constantly hounding you down, with anyone around you a potential backstabber (again, any more than a normal person); you don't have to hunt down creatures of the night with just your wits and a gun at your side unless you want to; you are yourself when you come back, you are not bound to collect artifacts you have few memories of, and you don't fall asleep after a set amount of time; and do not cause humans problems by merely existing because you have a horror hunger.

        Simply put, being a Sin Eater is not a walk in the park, but it's nice being able to play a splat whose existence doesn't suck in some innate way. If that's not your thing, you're under no obligation to play the game. However, if you're interested in having a second chance wrapped in the macabre, why not enjoy it?

                        It's a game about coming back from the dead, it would be wrong to say that there is any one mood to capture it, it's all about ambivalence. There will be people who will treat their second chance at life as the best thing ever, a burden, a responsibility, or with apathy. It's an always changing mood that is completely reliant on the characters themselves and their environment (their geist, their setting, the ghosts and stories they deal with). Fate gave them a second chance and the power to make things different, it's ultimately their choice what to do with it. Someone can be changed from the event and squander it by becoming a crazy cat lady that never leaves their house for fear of dying again or try to be active and use newfound powers to better life for themselves and those they love.

                        Life is like a box of chocolates.

                            I've been reading this and just wanted to share my thoughts, since there seems to be quite a bit of contention in terms of what people want for the sin-eater condition, particularly the relationship between Sin-Eater and Geist.

                            Let me start off by saying that Geist was the first tabletop RPG I've ever encountered, so it will always have a very special place in my heart. That also means that I didn't know better when it came to Geists flaws. There are indeed a lot of them, and while I don't agree with some of the issues people have, I definitely agree that the Geist needs more teeth. As it is, they really don't have anything to do and it's not hard to ignore them out of game. That being said, one thing I absolutely agree with is that one of the appeals of Geist is that, all told, it's pretty great to be a Sin-Eater. You died and came back with magical powers. It's meant to be upbeat and celebratory, providing a new spin on death as a theme. So I definitely don't want being a sin-eater to become something harsh and unpleasant, but with regards to the Geist, they do need more teeth, and Sin-eaters need more mechanical reason to...be sin-eaters.

                            So I'll offer some ideas I've had in that respect, using the examples offered above to illustrate them. First off, the example of the red-hand killer being essentially sealed away by his sin-eater. That should be a totally viable option. It should be possible for a sin-eater to ignore their Geists wishes if they really wanted to. But, as many people have pointed out, it takes a lot of weight out of the Bargain if ignoring a Geist has no significant consequences. And honestly, it takes away from the accomplishment of it too. In the core book, it says that sin-eaters nod in respect to her, she's clearly considered something special for being able to do that. So the mechanics should show that.

                            My thinking would be that totally ignoring a Geist should have some negative effects after a while. Whether some tilts or conditions, combined with whatever penalties low synergy would bring. Also, make t so that after your synergy drops to a certain point, you actually have to expend willpower, maybe even plasm, illustrating that yes, keeping this beings demands in check requires serious effort. Because shutting down the urges of a demanding, quasi-death deity should absolutely be HARD. It should be a heroic task. There should be a reason why, to bring up another example, the burning ladys sin-eater makes the effort to go see the forest fires every year, or the risk of burning down targets to cleanse them instead of just doing something easier or just ignoring those desires outright. But here's the thing; those conditions she keeps getting? resolving them gets her beats. Constantly losing willpower and plasm means she has to indulge her virtues and vices regularly. It means she has to help ghosts pass on and find potent cenotes to keep herself filled with plasm. These ideas show that not everyone could do what she does. It demonstrates that your Geist isn't just a passenger, but someone you need to acknowledge and appease, and it does so in a way that doesn't mean being a sin-eater in general sucks, though it could suck to be a sin-eater with a particularly harsh or demanding Geist. It means your character has to work even harder to just go about their days with an angry spirit screaming in their heads. But it also means the character is stronger for it. It forces them to become a better sin-eater, because otherwise they'll crumble under the strain. It means that there's way around it-either you compromise with your Geist, dealing with the dead to at least some degree, or you deny it and have to face the dead so you're strong enough to keep overcoming it, but either way you have to be a sin-eater.

                                        Vampire, werewolf, changeling, and hunter all have somewhat "antagonist-focused" second editions, what with the "X Chronicle" format, and I'd predict something like that for Geist, because one of the biggest complaints about first edition was there wasn't really that much motivation to do anything, or any really compelling threats or mysteries or overarching plots. So that's something I'm one hundred percent sure 2e is going to give us. Cthonians being the focus is something else I could definitely see, since they were something about first edition that didn't get much explanation but that people really were interested in. "Cthonians are trying to invade earth and break the boundaries between life and death" is a good, immediate threat that players can engage with, and it fills the same niche as the strix or the Idigam in that it's something you can easily leave out of your chronicle if you want, but it's also easy to use as the centerpiece of one.

                                        I'm hoping we get some nods to Mummy, because it gave us some neat underworld stuff. I know there's a lot of argument over including crossover stuff right in the corebook, but just a few mentions of how well the lines work together would be nice. Mummy introduced sapient ghosts, and I'm hoping Geist picks that up, maybe even giving us some player options in that vein. It would be really neat if there was a ritual to grant full sapience to a ghost, plus some mechanic for sapient ghosts having to constantly hang on to their identities or fade into the two-dimensional flanderized shades most ghosts are.
