    Musings on a new Wraith

    12-31-2013, 11:11 AM
    Recently, I've had some musings on how a new Wraith might work in the nWoD, specially after having read Mummy, Geist (finally), God Machine Chronicles and Blood & Smoke.

    Here's how I would do it in a way that mashes well with other games (specially Geist, because you can't ignore it) and reinforces it's themes. Just doing it for fun.

    I remember reading in one book (might be misremembering) that ghosts don't have souls. The ghost is the energy that stays, but the soul goes away, no one knows where to.

    Then, a Wraith is what happens when the soul stays with the ghost. It's rare, but it does happen.

    Losing the body, though, has an effect on the mind of the individual. It is not as contained as it was. As the mind is not confined by the limits of the brain, it might fragment, grow in stranger ways. It is easy to lose it's sense of identity.

    So, the main power stat of the Wraith is still Psyche, like in Geist. And the fuel is also Plasm.

    Morality/Integrity is replaced by Memory, like in Mummy. However, a Wraith starts with Memory 7. They also have anchors, that work like Touchstones. However, a Wraith has an Anchor for each level of Memory and don't attach it to specific levels of Memory. When losing Memory, player choses which Anchor he loses. Once an Anchor is gone, it's gone for good. Players can't get new Anchors, but instead may buy (Like a Touchstone merit) a Fetter.

    Anchor: Anchors are related to the Wraith's life and are specific. People (my wife), objects (the knife that killed me), places (the house of my childhood). When an Anchor is destroyed, the Wraith loses a dot of Memory.

    Fetter: Fetters are related to the Wraith's death and are not specific. Objects (knifes), places (alleyways like the one I died), feelings (hatred). You can't have more Fetters then your Psyche. Also, Fetters change the Wraith's appearance in some way. (knifed fingers, bloodied steps, red eyes with steam coming from them).

    When you have more Fetters then Anchors, you lose your name and chose a new, more archetypical one. You might still remember the old name, but you don't resonate with it anymore. You start resonating with the Archetypical one.

    Some new Memory sins:

    7 - Doing your Shadow conditions (dirge) or Aspirations.
    6 - Not using your own name (remember who you are) - This is not a Sin once you have more Fetters then Anchors.
    5 - Using your own name - Once you have more Fetters then Anchors.
    4 - a week without talking with other ghosts, or without seeing an anchor (Et in Arcadia Ego)
    3 - Interacting directly with the living (remember, you're dead)
    2 - Pretending to be alive (remember, you died)

    Wraiths have a Threshold, like in Geist.

    Self and Shadow: Work like Mask and Dirge from Blood & Smoke, respectively. However, Self is your ideal self, how you think you should act. Shadow is against it, how you don't think you should react (It's not necessarily bad. It's just against who you think you are. An Authoritarian might have Jester as a Shadow. You think you have to be a hard man, but man… those jokes just keep coming in your head. They won't respect you if you tell the joke, but you're witty. It might escape!).

    Keys: Given by the Threshold, like in Geist. they might determine the types of Numina the Wraith can get. Firestarter is Pyre-Flame, for instance (this might need some work, still).

    Aspirations: Wraiths choose Two Aspirations, like normal, and a third, Shadow Aspiration. This is related to his Shadow Archetype. They also chose two Resolutions. They are major things that need to resolve of their deaths, and are related to one of his Anchors (help my wife move on, revenge on my killer, get the house of my childhood back to my family). When you resolve, you lose that specific Anchor (and a dot of Memory). However, when you fulfill a Resolution, and it was your last Anchor, you get to transcend! Whatever that means. :P

    Getting Plasm:

    Every time someone mention your name (the one you resonate with), or remember you died, next to you, roll Resolve + Composure + Psyche.

    When next to an Anchor or Fetter, you roll Resolve + Composure + Psyche. You can only do it once a day.

    That way, for it to gain Plasm, the Wraith has to remind people of it's death in some way. Perhaps next to his wife, he uses Telekinesis to knock his portrait so that she has to get it from the floor. When he is more Archetypical, he might have to create an urban legend in some way. ("I heard that the ghost of Knife-hands stalks these alleyways and kills everyone that has too much rage in their hearts!")

    You fill your plasm when you fulfill a Resolution.

    Shadow Experience: Fulfilling a Shadow Aspiration and Getting a bead by getting a Dramatic Failure instead of a Failure gives Shadow Beads and Experience. It can be used to buy Fetters and Psyche.


    This is it, in a nutshell. Loved the Stygian parts in the old Wraith, so I would probably do something like that. Perhaps some places in the Underworld that only the TRULY dead can enter.

    Also, a Wraith with more fetters then Anchor is, for all intends and purposes, a Geist.

    Opinions, suggestions, anything?

      Overall, I like it. I’d do a few things differently, myself…

      A minor bit of renaming: Eidolon instead of Self. Eidolon was a Background in Wraith: the Oblivion that represented the wraith’s “higher self”.

      I’d make wraiths more desparate. A wraith is a ghost that has had the spark of life reignited in him, possibly right after his death and possibly after centuries languishing as a ghost; but while that means that he is once again a full person instead of merely a remnant of one, it comes at the cost of burning through his life (adapt the rules for Sekhem loss for this purpose; but apply it to Memory). When they lose their last dot of Memory, they succumb to Oblivion and cease to exist.

      They have the ability to stave off Oblivion by indulging their Passions. Passions are Conditions, except that the beats of experience earned are dedicated to buying dots of Memory. Passions also interact with Eidolon and Shadow: indulging in a Passion while satisfying your Eidolon lets you regain all of your Plasm as well as all of your Willpower (once per story); doing so while indulgng in your Shadow earns you a single point of Plasm (once per scene) — or you can forgo the “Memory Beat” and refill your Plasm instead. Passions themselves are neither good nor bad; it’s all a matter of the end to which you apply them. Each Passion that you Resolve brings you one step closer to Transcendence; but it also removes one of your bulwarks against Oblivion.

      A wraith doesn’t have an Anchor for each level of Memory; he has an Anchor for each Passion. Resolving the Passion eliminates the Anchor associated with it; Anchors are not themselves Resolved.

      I’d have Aspirations work like they do for mortals: no division between “good Aspirations” and “bad Aspirations”.

      I like the idea of organizing a wraith’s Numina by Key, or at least by Threshold.

      I also like the “become a geist” option, though I’d treat it as a sort of Threshold-based z-splat and I wouldn’t use Fetters: once you have sufficient Psyche, you can choose to bind yourself to some aspect of death that resonates with your Threshold; doing so interferes with (or outright prevents?) your ability to Transcend, but also immunizes you from Oblivion: once you lose all of your Memory, you become an Unfettered Geist. It also grants you Influence over that aspect of Death.

      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
