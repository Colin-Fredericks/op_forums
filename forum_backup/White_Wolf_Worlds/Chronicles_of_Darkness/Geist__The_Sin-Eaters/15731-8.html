                It has been going awhile. How many readers have followed us 100 sessions I wonder? We began in the winter of '10 (when the game was set in the future by a few months!) and it has been a strange trip. Glad you are enjoying it though.

                Session 100! It is time to see what the Krewe has uncovered at the Haunted Hotel.

                Session C

                Doc’s Log

                Doc’s Log: 1445 Hours, Friday, October 22, 2010

                We decided not to leave any one of us less rested than the others and simply slept in our rooms. I suspect Bright Eyes and Nightshade stayed together all night, but after Nightshade’s trauma from yesterday, that is not unusual. What I did find unusual was that I was actually relaxed. I knew it wasn’t anything about the sleep, the hotel beds aren’t that special. But it was almost like I had a plan for how things needed to be. This was further backed up by the fact it was very easy to get centered this morning. Meditation took about half an hour, though I did go to the pool for security.

                When I came back into the kitchen, Cupcake was raving worse than usual with the Cracked Cook. She was having some kind of argument about eating herself into a stupor. I asked what was wrong, since she had to have woken up the others with the screaming. She launched into a fit about The Shining before getting a hold of herself. Something had provoked an idea with the Cracked Cook and he wasn’t willing to let it go, though it seemed to me like he was trying to look out for her in his own demented way. The Dog of War was more relaxed this morning than yesterday as well. I usually have to get into a fight for him to get that calm.

                Mysteries of Death are not always about violence Sadeeuk, but there will always be conflict.

                That isn’t at all ominous. Anyway, Cupcake made bacon biscuits for breakfast but Bright Eyes and Nightshade didn’t come down throughout all of the meal. Eventually I got nervous and went to check on them. Nightshade called back from behind the door that everything was fine. They both came down together a few minutes later. Nightshade looked a little flustered but Bright Eyes looked down right hungry, and I don’t think her meal was on the table. While it’s not at all a problem for beautiful woman to pay attention to me, it was weird that Bright Eyes was so openly flirty. Once the Krewe was assembled, we made plans to check out the maze and below the basement if possible. Rose wanted to just splinter into a pile of spiders and look for a way down, but we have so little information and no way to get to her if things went bad, that was just too risky. I wasn’t the only one to think so for once either. While the others got ready, I went to the third floor balcony and sketched the maze. It wasn’t very involved but neither was the maze honestly.

                When I came back down, Bright Eyes suggested we split into groups to solve the maze and investigate anything we could. Even Rose could tell that wasn’t why Bright Eyes wanted to be alone with Nightshade. It was weird given she is normally so considerate about things like that. Cupcake was trying to ignore the obvious when she mentioned that she had string to use like Theseus in the Labyrinth. With Bright Eyes attached to Nightshade, it was obvious they would be a group. Rose and Cupcake decided to move together for safety but also for their strength in investigation. That left me to travel with Lily. Rose strapped one half of a pair of cloth sheers to Lily’s right hand before putting her on top of my pack. She shifted around until she was straddling my pack with her back against my neck. It was informed that she was now watching my back. She could slip down into my pack of those that wouldn’t understand came out, but still have freedom to move and help. Now my best day of picking teams, but for what I was doing, I could have done worse.

                Lily and I started off based on my sketch of the maze and were making pretty good time when a little girl that might have been ten stumbled out of the hedge. She was wearing a ridiculously short skirt, even by today’s standards, and was sloppy drunk. I knew this had to be Daisy and she quickly confirmed things as her introduction was also a pick up attempt. I offered her my coat since she was showing so much skin it made me cold (and a little sick). I suggested we go to the pool in an attempt to play along. She didn’t know about a pool, having died in the ‘20s, but she was willing to keep walking...well stumbling with me. The pull in the maze and right out of it was no different than inside so I knew the pool was my only shot. Lily was confused as to why I was acting interested in what was blatantly a helpless little girl. I could feel the grip of her sheers pressing into the back of my neck, I guess to get my attention. I tried to mumble over my shoulder for her to trust me a few times, but that didn’t work very well. The churning in my stomach from what I was doing wasn’t getting any easier to deal with either, so I can’t really blame her. I scooped Daisy up and carried her since she was almost falling every few steps from all the cheap booze she’d had. Things got very difficult when we got to the pool. Daisy panicked when she saw it, given that it should exist for her. I reached back and had Lily cut me so I could bleed on the ghost, but that didn’t go as I’d hoped. Daisy got very quiet as she relived everything from life in her mind, then called me a pervert and fought to get free. She wanted nothing to do with the pool. My gag reflex was tightening so I set Daisy down. I couldn’t bring myself to force a ten year old to go somewhere with a stranger that she thought was going to molest her. She bolted as soon as her feet were down. I called after her but she red phased as soon as she hit the edge of the woods. I gave a brief recap to the others through the ear curl as I continued to the woods. There I took out the curl and puked in the trees. I don’t care if it would have been for her own good, that little girl had experienced things that no child should ever know. I couldn’t be a part of that even for a moment. Once I was able to stand again, I explained things to Lily. She was happy to know that I was still the person she thought I was, but then she was sad that Daisy had to live like that. For once, we were in complete agreement.

                Once the queasy feeling in the pit of my stomach was settled and Lily didn’t want to stab me anymore, I put the ear curl back in. Rose and Cupcake were talking to someone but I couldn’t figure out who. Bright Eyes came over the curl to let me know it was Troy, since I missed the start of the conversation. She seemed more focused now so hopefully we won’t have any more uncomfortable issues. They were leading Troy from the maze back to the pool. Once I was a little more aware of what was happening, I realized Troy felt something strange with the pool. They convinced him to come inside just before Bright Eyes, Nightshade, and I got to the pool entrance. The ghost was immediately alerted to something being different, even though I could tell this was ‘40s Troy. Probably felt the changes from Nightshade’s trapping ritual, but also seeing Molly there could have done it. For her part, Molly moved to me almost immediately as Troy tried to talk to her. She was obviously afraid, as if she’d seen a ghost. Rose bled on Troy to bring him back to us, as it were. There wasn’t the same change for him as with the others. But when we tried to ask him questions, he became evasive and suspicious. I thought I could see what was happening, so I sent Molly to go talk with Rose in the other corner of the pool area. Then I took Troy to the side and explained that I knew. I was a little worried that he might become violent, but instead he admitted to me what he’d done. More importantly however, he told me how it happened. Much like my sense of rage drove me to get away from my Krewe, the entity had incited Troy after some slight by Sondra and later Wynona. He explained about how he hated it but after they were dead, it was like all his cares were lifted. He relaxed and was happy with his life (outside of being a murderer). Troy had even tried to stay away from the Hidden Hillside after Sondra was killed, but he said it was just completely natural for him to come back. He had attacked Wyndham in the maze after Thomas witnessed his murder of Wynona. However he had no idea why Wyndham accused Marvin Tomilson, as everyone knew he was innocent. There was a very real sense of remorse from Troy, for committing the murders, attacking Wyndham, and for Tomilson’s unjust execution. There was no attempt to explain why these things had to happen, no rationalizing, nothing that suggested the type of personality that could take another life on purpose. We moved back to the others and Troy agreed to be bound in the pool room.

                After giving Molly some reassurances, Cupcake and I went to get some tables, chairs, cards, and really anything else we could to help make for a more comfortable prison. While we were away from the pool, Nightshade shared her suspicion that Sondra was lesbian. At first I was confused as to why that would matter until Nightshade pointed out Sondra was more likely to follow her back to the pool. I realized there is a certain trust among compatriots of which Nightshade was looking to take advantage. That same trust could probably exist between Marvin Tomilson and myself given our backgrounds. Given that idea, we decided to split up again, as we tended to meet ghosts in smaller groups. Cupcake announced that she had found a typed letter in the bar, since she could finally go through it properly. It was from Bertrand according to her Oracle vision. The letter was written after she left the bar last night. It had only a single line.

                They need your help.

                We don’t know what’s special about the bartender, but he definitely knows something. Nightshade, Rose, and Lily went to look for Sondra while Cupcake and Bright Eyes came with me to search for Tomilson. Before we could get to his room, Rose began a characteristically awkward conversation with what turned out to be Yvette. Nightshade stepped in to make things less uncomfortable and they convinced Yvette to follow them in search of Molly the maid. We managed to all get back to the pool within a few moments of each other, and Yvette became incredibly suspicious as soon as she came in. Yvette panicked and drew her pistol. Rose locked down her arms, while Troy snatched the gun away. Nightshade was feeling a bit prickly as she put her own pistol to Yvette’s head. Probably unnecessary but it immediately took the fight out of her. It was more fear than anything else from them on. That was amplified when Molly saw who we brought back. She flipped out, pointed like a Hangin’ Judge from the movies, and shouted about her killer. Molly got so worked up that I had to catch her before she attacked Yvette. Eventually I talked her down while Troy positioned himself directly between the two women. Bright Eyes stepped up for her turn to donate. When blood was applied, Yvette calmed into a notably depressed state and listened without struggle. She seemed to accept this fate and agreed to be bound to weaken the entity. When Rose let go, Yvette collapsed and sobbed her wish to end it all....again. Before anyone else could offer comfort or even a pat on the shoulder, Troy moved to stand in front of her. Looking down with an expression of pained truth, he pointed out to Yvette that they both had debits to pay before things could be set right. She could only nod and sniffle.

                Now if we can keep Molly and Yvette from killing each other again, I think our plan might work. Each time we bind a ghost to the pool, the pull in the rest of the hotel fades. But we still have at least six ghosts, and that’s assuming no others show up. We also still don’t know what to make of the bartender. But after this, I think most of us could use a drink.


                Jasmine’s Diary

                Friday, October 22, 2010, Noon

                It took be awhile to meditate this morning, in part because Alexia was making quite a fuss arguing with the cracked cook about the movie The Shining. When I finally made it down to breakfast, I found that Doc and Lily were already there, but not Natalie or Sara, and eventually Doc had to go up to fetch them. Sara was acting peculiar. She had a sultry, wanton look in her eyes and was practically clinging to Natalie all morning. She’s normally a bit more discrete about these things.

                Over breakfast, we discussed what steps we should take in the hotel. It might be possible for me to perform a more thorough investigation of the ruined basement by turning into a swarm of spiders, but the others were worried about me being alone down there. So our main plan at this point is to get as many of the ghosts as possible into the pool area so that they would be free of the entity’s influence. Then, hopefully we can draw out the entity and destroy it. To that end, we needed to search the hedge maze behind the hotel, as some of the ghosts occasionally appear there and it is where Thomas was attacked. So we split up to do just that. Natalie and Sara headed off together while Alexia and I accompanied one another. Doc took Lily. I disassembled a large pair of sewing shears and gave her the blades so that she would have a weapon of sorts of use if necessary, and we taped one of them to her right hand. She looked very brave riding on Doc’s backpack. I don’t think Doc was happy to be baby-sitting Lily, but he handled it with good grace.

                Doc and Lily were the first to encounter something. They found Daisy’s ghost wandering the maze, drunk. Daisy was a young girl, no more than ten or eleven, who sold her body to make ends meet in the early days of the hotel. Doc tried to lead Daisy into the pool area by pretending to be interested in her services…and it made us all very uncomfortable, most of all Doc. According to Doc, Lily disapproved very strongly and nearly stabbed him because she didn’t understand that he was simply trying to get Daisy to safety.

                Unfortunately, Daisy didn’t remember the pool; it was built after her time, so she thought Doc was trying to take her somewhere she didn’t know. It spooked her, and she started to panic. It didn’t help when Doc asked Lily to cut him so that he could bleed on Daisy. When he did that, she became very quiet as though remembering all the horrors of her life and suicide, and then she fled as fast as she could. I almost transformed into a flock of bird to follow her, but Doc reported that she disappeared when she hit the edge of the yard, leaving only a momentary red silhouette like the other ghosts.

                Not long after that, Alexia and I encountered 40’s-era Troy. He offered to walk with us, and we cautiously guided him back toward the hotel. While we were still in the maze, though, he stopped as though something
                were bothering him. He said he had a chill, so we looked around and could sense the deathly residue in that spot. Someone had been killed there, probably in the 20’s, though I don’t know who. Was Sandra killed in the hedge maze?

                Troy was a perfect gentleman, though, and as we approached the outdoor entrance to the pool, he got a bad feeling about something and told us to wait while he checked it out. He seemed honestly concerned for our well-being. Despite his misgivings, however, we got him into the pool, and then he realized that something was off. He became very defensive, ready to lash out at us if necessary, but I approached him as calmly and gently as I could and took his hand after leaving a little blood on mine. He calmed down as he realized that he was safe there in the pool. All the while, Molly looked on anxiously.

                Doc, Natalie and Sara soon joined us. Once Troy realized he was safe, he was more talkative. He didn’t know what was going on in the hotel, but he knew that something there was trying to control him and the other ghosts. As far as he knew, it was the hotel itself. The entity seems to be fed by people dying there, but Troy didn’t know why people who didn’t die there, like Yvette, were also trapped.

                Troy asked to speak to Doc privately out of earshot of the ladies, but thanks to the ear curls, we could all listen in. He confessed to Doc that when he was alive, he had killed Sandra and Wynona, but he didn’t know why. Something bad would happen, or they would do something he didn’t like, such as turning down his earnest advances, and then something in his head would take over. Anger blinded him and he killed them. Afterward, he felt relieved; all of his anger and tension and frustration just melted away. He tried to stay away form the hotel but always ended up coming back like it was the most natural thing in the world. He seemed to feel sincere remorse over what he had done. After what happened to Doc yesterday, it’s easy to believe that Troy would not have become a murderer if the hotel weren’t fanning the flames of his wrath. Troy seems to be unique among the ghosts because he fought against the entity’s influence even while he was alive and was at least somewhat aware of it power. Perhaps that’s why he is so strong against it now.

                Then the entity started to pull at Troy even though he was in the pool. It must have a strong influence on him. Troy started to flicker a bit, but fought against the pull long enough for Doc to bind him with Stigmata. Then Natalie performed her ceremony to seal him into the room. The ceremony severed the entity’s pull, so Troy is safe for now.

                Meanwhile, Sara tried to encourage and comfort Molly, who is still scared out of her wits. The rest of us went to get a few things for the ghosts to entertain themselves. A card table, cards, some books and magazines and other things. I filled the pool and got it ready for swimmers.

                Our next step is to split up and try to draw out the ghosts, as they seem to appear more easily when we are in smaller groups.


                Friday, October 22, 2010, Afternoon

                Alexia decided to go back to the bar after we had things arranged in the pool. She did not get much of a chance to search behind the bar last night because the bartender, Bertrand, ran her off. This time, however, she found a typewritten note that simply said, “They need your help.” I recalled seeing an old typewriter in one of the offices yesterday, and Alexia activated her Oracle in order to read the note’s past. She discovered that Bertrand had typed it last night after meeting us.

                Whatever he is, he knows more than the other ghosts. But is he a guardian spirit or a trap?

                We split up to wander the hotel in search of more ghosts. Doc, Sara and Alexia split off into one group. Natalie felt that she could probably get Sandra to follow her to the pool, as she believes Sandra is attracted to women and felt that would help her gain Sandra’s trust. I accompanied her along with Lily. If we encounter Sandra, I can excuse myself and then become invisible in order to keep an eye on Natalie without making Sandra feel uncomfortable.

                We ran into Yvette, not Sandra, though. She was a bit anxious to run into us again, but Natalie soon put her at ease. Yvette said she was looking for Molly, so I kindly offered to help her find the maid. I suggested that I had a complaint about my room, and needed to speak to Molly anyway.

                Natalie and I led her into the pool. As soon as she saw Molly there, she knew something was wrong. She opened her eyes wide and drew her pistol. I was on her in an instant, immobilizing her arms while Troy took her gun. Natalie put her own pistol to Yvette’s head, which was a bit excessive but got the point across. Faced with her “killer,” Molly screamed incoherently and tried to attack Yvette, but Doc held her back and eventually calmed her down.

                Sara bled on Yvette to grant her clarity, and then we explained the situation. Both Molly and Yvette seemed to accept that if we could keep them there in the pool long enough, we could defeat the force that has bound them to the hotel so that they could pass on. Yvette collapsed to the floor when I released her and moaned that she wanted to die and end it all again. She apparently feels a lot of remorse over the things she did.

                Troy stepped in at that point and told Yvette that they both have debts to pay, and they will only feel better once that’s done. Yvette seemed to accept that bitter truth.

                So now we have three ghosts. Now we just have to collect the rest. We should be fine as long Yvette and Molly don’t attack each other, and I don’t think that’s going to happen. Not with Troy in there, anyway.


                Footnotes

                Hats

                The Drowned Bride has decided that Karl would look good in a hat, something with more class than the flat cap he often wears. I discussed it with Natalie a bit while we split off to search the hotel. I’m not sure if a fedora is right for him, and certainly not a trilby. Natalie says that trilbies are fashionable among arrogant hipsters, whatever that means, and that Karl doesn’t want to make that sort of impression. I don’t think there’s much threat of that. I get the feeling that these hipsters tend to be rather dapper sorts, and as much as I love Karl, he’s a bit rough around the edges to be considered dapper.

                Still, perhaps a bowler?


                Troy’s Guilt

                Troy did kill Sandra and Wynona, and he tried to kill Thomas Wyndham. But he was extremely upset that Marvin Tommelson took the fall for the murders. Thomas accused Marvin, and Troy didn’t understand why since he thought Thomas had seen him when he attacked. Maybe Thomas made a mistake. Maybe not. Next time we run into Thomas, we should find out. In any event, Troy was very upset that an innocent man took the fall for his crimes, though obviously not upset enough to step forward and confess his crimes before the man was executed. Or perhaps he was away and did not find out about the conviction until afterward.

                Troy claims that he did not kill Albert Finnigan or Daisy, and I believe him.


                Our Darker Selves

                The hotel entity seems to be able to draw out a person’s darker self, causing our inner demons to take over. That’s what happened with Doc’s rage yesterday, and I suspect something along those lines might have been happening this morning with Sara and Natalie. It’s how the hotel got Troy to commit murder.

                Our geists are quite aware of this possibility as well, and I caught Alexia arguing with the Cracked Cook about her own vice. He’s worried she will give in to her gluttony while we’re here and stuff her face until she’s sick. But I know Alexia is very careful about how much she eats. She’s very sensitive about her love of food, which is one reason the Underworld makes her so anxious. I do feel a bit guilty, though, because it was the Drowned Bride who put the idea into the Cracked Cook’s head in the first place when she realized how the hotel was affecting some of us…

                End Session C

                Several notes here

                First, was a good session 100.

                Second, being session 100, the first hour was spent with the PC’s Geists RPing a conversation through the channel. Everyone described and played their Geist, which was quite exciting. That’s where the whole “hat” note in Jasmine’s journal came from.

                Third, Sara’s behavior was due to the Entity empowering her vice, much like Doc’s was the previous session.

                I will also note there was some confusion regarding Yvette’s behavior. She drew her firearm when she saw Molly, with clear intent to fire. Jasmine wrestled the gun away, Troy interposed himself, and Natalie put a pistol to her head, which made her calm down.

                The Krewe is putting together the mystery of the hotel. Next session they add 2 more ghosts to their little “family”, discover a powerful Memento, and have an unexpected visitor arrive at the hotel. Come back in two weeks for the continuation of the story. Comments and Questions welcome.

                                        Session CI

                                        Doc’s Log

                                        Doc’s Log: 1715 Hours, Friday, October 22, 2010

                                        Things just get better and better around here. It’s more like Dark Shadows here than back at Dark Haven Bluffs. I really hate that Snowflake isn’t here. He could get some great context from this place.

                                        Once we got Yvette settled in, the Krewe gathered to determine our next move. Before we could get very far, Troy told us he knew of something that might help us against the hotel. He said there was a shed on the other side of the hedge maze. Buried under that shed was the axe he used to kill Sondra and Wynona. This brought our conversation back to who died in the maze. James “Jimbo” Gibson vanished investigating Sondra’s murder, but Troy swore he was in Indiana at the time. Gibson was either FBI or a Marshal so it seems odd that no one else would come looking for him. But then we don’t know how the bartender got here either. Daisy came up next and Rose mentioned maybe trying to be a matronly figure to try and draw her in. Troy said that was a very bad idea. Apparently Daisy does not like women. The way Troy described it, I suspect she was either abandoned or abused by her mother or a mother figure. Given her age at death, this made the depravities Daisy endured all the more disgusting. I can only hope the people that did this to her have all found a particularly violent Dark Dominion to spend eternity. If not, I may have to track them down and help them move.

                                        We followed Troy’s directions to the shed and there it was. Wiping the dirt away, I lifted what I knew to be a very powerful piece of Memorabilia out of the ground. The bloodstained fire axe, Troy Donovan’s Axe, was a Passion Charm, but also made chopping wood or targets much easier. Still that’s better than suddenly having the urge to beat down a close friend for a slight that was based on my own assumptions anyway. ...There was a tangent. This wasn’t the entrance to a monster’s lair I was hoping for, but it was a clue. It also meant we were probably dealing with something not from our realm...again.                                             Jasmine’s Diary

                                            Friday, October 22, 2010, Night

                                            When night fell, we all went to the bar to see what ghosts we might rescue there. The plan was for Alexia to speak to Bertrand, the bartender, while I spoke to Thomas Wyndham since I had already established a rapport with him. Doc would talk to Marvin if he was there.

                                            We were pleasantly surprised to see that James Gibson, the investigator who was asking around after Sandra’s death, was also in the bar. He disappeared mysteriously in the 20’s, though Alexia and I suspect it was his death mark we sensed in the hedge maze earlier in the day. Sara volunteered to speak with him.

No sooner did I sit down to speak with Thomas than I felt the old, familiar weight settle in my heart. The loss, loneliness and heartbreak I’ve suffered over the past nineteen months welled up inside me and I felt so utterly hopeless that even the thought of Karl did little to comfort me. Richard’s death felt as fresh and terrible as it did the moment I found out he had killed himself, and I lost all will to go on. I buried my face in my hands and began to sob. I don’t think I’ve cried openly, when others were around, since Richard’s funeral…but I was so depressed I couldn’t help it. I think that Thomas was trying to speak to me, but the only thing that pierced my morose fog was some other disturbance in the bar involving Alexia. I looked up at her with tear-soaked eyes and she looked back, and I could see that she was also taken by some strange urge and wanted nothing more than to carve me up then and there. She was muttering to herself more and more loudly until at last she was shouting “The flesh will come back, but how do I know it will come back?” Everyone’s attention was focused on her…but I didn’t care. I just wanted everything to end. Even knowing what might lie in store for me beyond the threshold of death, I just wanted to die. But Alexia’s insane shouting was helping her fight her urges, so I couldn’t rely on her to kill me. I knew I would have to end it myself.

                                            So I pushed myself up from the table and slowly staggered from the room, barely able to set one foot in front of the other. All the while, the Bride was pleading with me. She kept reminding me that I’ve found love again, that Karl is waiting for me to return to him. She practically screamed at me to stop what I was planning, but all I could think about was how much I missed Richard and how ironic it was that the Bride, who represents the Resolution of Death in our krewe mythos, would be railing against my own deathly resolve. And for some reason I forgot that as a sin-eater, I would come back if I did manage to kill myself. Or perhaps I simply planned to kill myself again and again until I couldn’t come back anymore.

                                            Then Natalie was there with her arms around my shoulders. She said she would help me. In my depressed state, I thought she was promising to help me end it. She guided me toward the pool room, and I thought how fitting it would be to drown, like I did the first time when I should have stayed dead. But once we were in the pool room, Natalie looked me in the eyes and reminded me that I had to have hope. And as she said that, she did something to me that counteracted the hotel’s influence. My depression lessened until it was nothing more than the melancholy that has haunted me since Richard died, and then even that faded. I remembered Karl and how sweet and wonderful he is, and I felt good again, so sad at all.

                                            It still took me some time to get my bearing again and shake off the last shreds of sorrow, but before long I was back on my feet again. I stayed in the pool room a bit anyway, though just to make sure.

She had a terrifying urge to kill and eat someone and struggled with the chilling idea that slaughtering and eating another sin-eater would be okay because we would come back, so she really wouldn’t be killing us. Fortunately, she managed to fight against the urge until Sara and Doc helped her to the pool. Doc, being a clever man, managed to get Marvin’s ghost into the pool room as well by asking him to help guide Alexia there. Natalie sealed him in the room in short order, so that’s one more ghost captured.

Marvin had no idea, but Wynona suggested that it was because Marvin had been stalking her. He flatly denied it. I’m more inclined to believe him than her, because her judgment of things has proven decidedly suspect. Given Thomas’s opinions on Hitler versus Stalin, I wonder if he isn’t especially racist? Then again, he did not seem particularly averse to me.

Perhaps he was a hit man sent to kill whoever murdered Sandra, in which case she too must have been involved with the mob somehow. Natalie suspects Bertrand might have killed Gibson in order to protect Troy, but if so, he must have known more about what was happening at the hotel even then. It’s quite mysterious. I can’t help but remain suspicious of Bertrand and his connection to the hotel.

                                            We once more ventured from the pool room to find more ghosts, though really Alexia wanted to visit the kitchen to whip up a few snacks so that if the hotel targeted her again, she would have something to eat in order to help stave off her darker cravings. She felt drawn to the oven, and that is when we made a terrifying discovery. Daisy’s corpse was inside the oven, curled up and cooked like a gruesome roast. It was sickening. But it does explain why her remains were never found.

But we did not want to upset the other ghosts with the horrifying vision of her cooked flesh. She was quite unconscious, effectively “dead” as a ghost. Doc wanted to try and heal her, but doing so would have drained his plasmic energies to almost nothing, so I suggested that we take her into the pool room in a laundry cart, covered with some spare towels. Then, once there, we could shield her with one of the folding screens we had brought in while Natalie sealed her in a bottle. That way we wouldn’t have to worry about her waking up or suffering any more until we were finished with the hotel, and the other ghosts wouldn’t have to see her gruesome state.

                                            While we were gathering the laundry cart and then moving back to the pool room, Sara and I realized that James Gibson was watching us furtively from the music room doorway. He seemed suspicious of us, and we both had the impression that he planned to follow us to the pool to see what we were up to. So Sara and I hatched a plan. She followed the others to the pool room and made it a bit obvious so that Gibson would know where we had gone. Meanwhile, I cloaked myself in my Shroud and disappeared. I quietly followed him as he crept carefully and silently toward the pool. He seemed unsure of what to do once he was outside the door, so I suggest through the ear curls that he needed a little motivation to get him into the pool. So Natalie did her best damsel in distress scream, and in response Gibson burst into the pool, drawing a gun in each hand and ordering everyone inside to freeze.

                                            Our ploy worked, and we had gotten him into the pool room, but he was still dangerous, so shifted into my Tear-Stained Caul, then reappeared and grabbed him from behind. He recovered from the shock quickly, and I must confess that he got the best of me. He threw his weight forward and threw me off balance, shrugging out of my hold quite handily. I quickly recovered, of course. He leveled his shotgun at me, but with my Shroud on I was not too concerned, and made a move to shift his arm out of the way and lock him in another hold. Before I could do that, though, Sandra ran up and forced herself between the two of us.

He was so shocked that she was still alive that he dropped his guns to the floor and put his hands on her shoulders. Sara said we should bleed on him to help him remember, so I cut myself and smeared some of my blood on his back.

He told us that he was sent by the mob to kill Sandra’s murderer, and we explained his current situation to him. He seemed quite taken with Yvette. I think her short skirt made an impression on him. I was also flattered when he asked if I was a professional. “It’s been a long time since anyone’s gotten the drop on me like that,” he said. I assured him that I was, in fact, a professional, and then complimented him as well. It’s been a long time since anyone’s been able to shrug out of one of my holds like he did.

It seems he was good with numbers, and fixed accounts for the mob. While he was on the run, he was supposed to be well-armed. We stopped by his room on the first floor, but there was no sign of his ghost. I don’t think we’ve seen him at all yet.

I think that after all we have been through, they really needed to hear Karen and Chandra’s voices. I would have spoken to Karl, but he’s not due back from Cold Harbor for another day or so. What pieces I could find looked like a summoning or binding circle to me, but a few fragments looked like they had been damaged before the newer floor was laid over the original. I suspect that whatever entity is trapping the ghosts here is an outsider that was summoned decades ago, perhaps while the hotel was being built. Since the hotel has proven adept at influencing all of us, we have decided to alter the rooming arrangements. Natalie, Sara and I will lock ourselves in a room together since we know our darker sides aren’t going to hurt anyone else, and so that we can all keep an eye on one another to make sure we don’t do anything foolish. Doc and Alexia, who have proven to be a bit dangerous when the hotel gets to them, are locking themselves in their own rooms so that they won’t be around anyone else. And if they do come for the rest of us, the locked door should prove a nice deterrent and give us some warning of their approach.

Not because of untold horrors or otherworldly rituals, but because it hit too close to home. Two people must bury the hatchet over a vice-related grievance. I was controlled by The Entity through my anger. I was still holding the pain and rage I felt toward the breakup with Nightshade inside. An event that was every bit my doing based on an issue that only I had. And to make it even more ridiculous, I had already found another loving relationship without the kinds of problems that led to my issues to begin with. Not only was that unhealthy and dangerous, it was completely stupid. Nightshade was just as uncomfortable as she didn’t want anything brought up. Rose started to suggest we talk when Nightshade mentioned we could call Chandra and they could make up. That wasn’t fair, though it was amusing (now). I told the others to get ready for a huge fight. At first, I think everyone thought I was joking about Chandra. Then they realized I was preparing for war. Once my Stigmata Shroud encased me in my scarlet armor, I walked to Nightshade. I apologized for letting my own issues get in the way and be projected on our relationship. I admitted to her and myself that she was free to love who she wished, the way she wished, and I should have never thought I had a say otherwise. Leaning over, I kissed her on the forehead then shook Bright Eyes’ hand as I apologized to her for getting her involved in my problem. It was a strange feeling to have my emotional turmoil splashed out in front of everyone and laid bare in such a way. But before I could think about the embarrassment, we heard and felt a terrible roar coming from the lobby. We rushed in to find a dark tree creature stooped over in the three floor opening. The beast was massive with eleven damaged pods hanging from the tendrils. I spotted the four faces of the investigators Cupcake described molded into the bark. It was time to bring the end.

                                                        Before The Entity could attack, Nightshade smashed the bottle freeing Troy while Rose held the doll’s head up and ripped on her hair. Physically, nothing happened other than a very scary scream tore through the hotel. In my mind however, I got the image of every nerve in The Entity’s body being shredded horribly and healed over and over, every second. We wasted no more than that split second in attacking the creature. The Krewe unleashed Hell.

                                                        I remembered the anguish I caused Nightshade when The Entity tried to turn me against her. The flame ignited inside and filled my chest with malice. The blaze of my emotions exploded from my hand as a gout of wicked Pyre-Flame hatefire. But my rage was my own this time and my fire ravaged The Entity’s soul as well as its form. Strength, flexibility, and endurance all burned away just like its wooden body. I followed with Stigmata Rage and forced its boiling, tar-like sap from every crevice. But I also hinder its mind from the agony. After three more blasts of my damning Pyre-Flame, The Entity broke free of Mr. Ox’s Gift of Spite. It was not finished yet, but a sickening burnt smell filled the hall and it was covered in wounds. The first attack came to me as The Entity lashed out to impale me as it had the bearded man. The impact on my chest hit like a fallen tree, but it only managed to slide me backward from the force. The others continued with their attacks. I had lots more anger to throw. The sap continued to pump from its wounds thanks to my Stigmata Rage so I continued twice more with my Pyre-Flame assault. The gunshots and other mystic attacks had more trouble damaging it. But a creature made of wood will not stand against the rage of a Pyre-Flame. Rose and Bright Eyes combined the final strike to finish it. The Entity shrieked then started to vibrate as its bark seemed to rip apart into itself. Something inside was effectively consuming the outside. When it was over, all that was left was a burnt husk in the odd shape of a tree that was pulled out of the living realm. I wrapped my Shroud tighter around me and stepped into Twilight where the tree fell and proceeded to pummel the trunk to smoldering bits. Rose and Nightshade shouted and snapped me out of my fugue of vindication.

                                                        Molly was calling for us so I ran to the Pool Room and phased back in. Bertrand seemed distant but allowed Molly to speak. She thanked us for keeping our promise. Then she turned and walked out the back door. As soon as she crossed the threshold, she vanished. Yvette, who had quietly sat watching behind Molly, stood and followed. She also vanished at the threshold. Troy staggered in about the same time, but most of the other men were gone. Bertrand asked if the hotel would be safe and made me promise that it will go on. I told him that the new owner hired us so that she could reopen the Hidden Hillside Hotel. Once he knew that, he smiled and took on a more peaceful expression before walking out the door as well.

                                                        Wynona noted that “all the boys got Raptured.” Looking around, only she and Sondra remained other than Troy who basically collapsed. We had miscalculated when to open the bottle so he was affected by Mr. Ox’s gift as well. Once we are sure that nothing else is lingering, we’re done. After that, we just need to clean up, rest up, load up, and go home.

                                                        I still don’t know exactly what this thing was, but I do know one thing: It fell to the Middle Shadows of Dark Haven Bluffs.


                                                        Jasmine’s Diary

                                                        Saturday, October 23, 2010, Afternoon

                                                        This morning, Alexia and I were the first up and about. Fortunately, there were no further incidents during the night; I did not become suicidal, and Alexia’s unfortunate cravings did not surge again. After meditating, I joined Alexia in the kitchen to prepare a spot of breakfast, and eventually the others joined us.

                                                        We decided to check Finnigan’s room again before returning the basement to begin clearing the a path through the rubble. Natalie, Sara and I went, since we thought he would be less threatened by women. Finnigan was there this time. I kept my distance and let Natalie and Sara do all the talking, and they managed to convince Finnigan that Yvette had brought in backup and was coming with some mob goons to kill him. They promised to lead him to safety and we got him into the pool room. I flicked blood on him to make him lucid and we explained what had happened to him. When he finally saw Yvette, who had been hiding behind the folding screen, he managed to hold it together well enough.

                                                        We spent much of the rest of the day clearing the rubble and debris from the basement so that Alexia could get to the remnants of the summoning circle. When we finally cleared away enough, Alexia crept in and examined the circle with her Industrial Oracle while Sara performed a ceremony to read the emotional resonances left by the people who demolished the basement recently. Together, they discovered quite a lot about what happened here.

                                                        Long ago, when the hotel was being built, a distinguished-looking man with a dark beard attempted to summon a benevolent, plant-like feminine spirit, probably in an attempt to turn the hotel into a sort of garden-house to grow strange fruits with miraculous supernatural qualities. However, something went wrong with the summoning (as it so often does), and instead he summoned a terrible outsider that resembled a gnarled, twisted tree with tentacle-like branches. At that moment, a young Bertrand interrupted the ceremony, and the outsider broke free of the summoning. It became a part of the hotel, twisting it into a trap for those who die under the effects of their vices. The bearded man’s soul was devoured by the outsider.

                                                        This does explain Bertrand’s strange connection to the place, as well as his loyalty to the hotel throughout the years. He seems to have understood what he had inadvertently caused, and was trying to take responsibility for it.

                                                        More recently, a group of people who worked together like a close-knit military squad came to investigate the hotel. One of them used some sort of ritual or power to locate the tree-outsider’s hidden roots, and then the group set explosives to destroy them. They succeeded, and that is what collapsed the basement. With its roots destroyed, the outsider could finally be killed, so the group fought it in the hotel yard. They were not prepared for its influence, however, and they fell to jealous in-fighting as it slaughtered them all save one, a woman named Cindy. It planted some sort of seed in her, forcing it down her throat and allowing her to escape alive. Sara seemed quite disturbed by the strength of emotion that lingered from the confrontation.

                                                        Alexia did note that the face of the summoner was formed in the gnarled bark of the outsider’s trunk amidst many others. Sadly, there is little we can do for him now.

                                                        I’ve just drawn a bath, so I suppose it’s time to drown myself a bit in order to recover some of my plasmic energy and then heal and rest until night falls.


                                                        Saturday, October 23, 2010, Late Night

                                                        We went to the bar again at night to find the last two ghosts, Thomas and Bertrand. I was responsible for luring Thomas into the pool room, so I sat and talked with him awhile in the bar. He was quite charming as always, though I’m beginning to get the impression that he’s too charming, if that makes any sense. I eventually convinced him that there was a party for guests going on at the pool, and expressed my surprise that he hadn’t received an invitation. He was more than happy to accompany me to the party, however, and once we arrived in the pool, I took his hand and bled on him. Naturally, he was horrified to realize the truth of his current state, particularly when he saw Marvin’s ghost. Though he had survived the axe blow that nearly killed him, it had paralyzed him from the waist down, and he still blamed Marvin, which convinced us that he really did believe Marvin killed Wynona. Not only had he encountered Marvin in the hedge maze just before he was attacked, but it also seems that Wynona thought Marvin had been following her. She had expressed her worry to Thomas, which is why he assumed Marvin was the murderer. Marvin denied ever stalking Wynona, and I’m more inclined to believe him than her, as she has a rather egocentric and warped view of reality.

                                                        Thomas had a hard time believing that Troy was the culprit, but eventually accepted it. Natalie sealed him in the room with the others. Then Alexia and Sara returned to the bar to gather Bertrand. Alexia used the code phrase Bertrand told her: “It’s time to bring the end.” He immediately understood and solemnly joined us in the pool room, where we bled on him and then sealed him inside as well.

                                                        There was an odd moment of quiet disappointment then. I think we all expected the outsider to appear in a huge show of force once the last of its ghostly captives were torn away from it, but there was… nothing. Bertrand told us that the group who had fought it before kept notes on the beast, but once they were killed, it used Sandra to try and burn them. He was, however, able to rescue a few fragments, and they revealed a few means by which the weakened, vulnerable outsider might be summoned. Unfortunately neither method sat well with us.

                                                        First, if someone dies under the effects of a vice, then the outsider will manifest in a spiritual reflection. Second, if people act very strongly in accordance with their vices, it will appear.

                                                        Neither of those prospects were very appealing to us. We’ve all had quite enough of our darker sides, and I for one don’t want to kill myself in order to draw out our enemy.

                                                        I suggested that Alexia examine the fireplace in which the notes were burned. She might be able to gain glimpses of the past in which she could see more fragments of the notes before they were claimed by the fire. Bertrand showed us the place and Alexia unlocked her Oracle again, and she found that there was yet another option.

                                                        If two people earnestly overcome some sort of vice-ridden conflict between them, then it will anger the outsider, causing it to manifest. We all looked at Doc and Natalie at that. It’s no secret that their doomed romantic relationship and Doc’s tendency to be possessive of Natalie has made their friendship very tense of late, and the trouble has only fueled Doc’s frustration and anger to the extent that Natalie is honestly frightened of him.

                                                        Doc sighed and nodded, realizing that he finally needed to put aside his anger and come to terms with the fact that he and Natalie are close friends and nothing more. It was a touching moment. Doc’s apology was heartfelt and true, and I could sense the simmering fires of his rage subside. He even admitted that he had no reason to be angry anymore. He’s happy with Noriko and he still cares deeply for Natalie, and she for him. Natalie apologized for her selfishness in everything. Yes, her emotional issues are partly to blame, but she had to take some responsibility for hurting Doc, and I for one was glad to hear her do so. I know it was hard for both of them, but as they finally forgave each other and embraced, the hotel shuddered and there was a terrible groan from the lobby. It sounded like the roar of a hundred creaking trees. The outsider had arrived.

                                                        We rushed out to face it. Doc tossed Troy’s bottle against a wall and Troy swirled out of it, rapidly coalescing and ready to fight. Then I snatched Mr. Ox’s doll head from my belt and tore at its hair. It unleashed a bone-chilling scream, and the outsider shuddered as though every part of it was struck with utter agony. Unfortunately, Troy reacted the same way. He fell to his knees paralyzed by pain. I felt bad about that. We should have waited to smash the bottle, I suppose. In any event, we took advantage of the outsider’s distress to attack it brutally. The creature was immense, filling all three stories of the lobby. Heavy, cocoon-like pods grew within each of its tentacle branches, and we could tell that each pod held the essence of one of the hotel’s ghosts. Countless faces screamed with wooden groans from its trunk. Natalie sank into the earth with her Grave-Dirt Boneyard and began throwing chunks of rubble from the basement at it, denting its tough, bark-covered hide. Sara flitted about like pyre-flame in her Caul, stabbing deeply the remnants of its roots. Chips of bloody, wooden flesh flew away as her knife plunged again and again. Clad in my Primeval Caul and Shroud, I leapt into its branches and slashed at them with my fierce claws, but the thing’s bark was so tough that even with my great strength, I struggled to pierce it. Doc unleashed his fiery wrath on the thing, blasting it with fire and using his Rage to tear open its bark so that its thick blood ran free. Alexia, however, was most impressive. She was clad in her Phantasmal Caul and wielded heavy cleavers in each hand. Her many arms hacked away at the beast’s trunk, carving deep wounds.

                                                        The outsider managed to shrug off the effects of the doll’s head far sooner than we expected, but by then it was too late. It lashed out at us, but to little effect, as we literally tore it limb from limb. Its body fell apart and disintegrated, leaving only a shriveled husk in Twilight. Doc took care of that by stepping into Twilight and hacking at the husk with Troy’s axe until it was nothing more than splinters. Even then he kept at it. It was cathartic, I think, and Doc needed an outlet for his anger. I hope that he’s a bit calmer after this.

                                                        Troy recovered from the paralyzing effects of the doll’s head and accompanied the rest of us to the pool room to check on the ghosts. I apologized for putting him through such agony, but he shrugged it off. He understood, and the important thing was that the outsider had been destroyed.

                                                        The back door of the pool room was open, and a soft light shone from it. Only Molly, Yvette, Sandra, Bertrand, and Wynona remained. The others had already departed though the door as soon as the outsider was killed, and I slightly regretted being unable to say goodbye to William. Molly came to thank us for helping her as we entered, and after she did that, she turned and walked calmly through the glowing doorway. Tears of joy welled in her eyes as she turned to walk out, and I was glad to see that there was no more fear lurking in her eyes.

                                                        Once Molly left, Yvette sighed as though a great weight were lifted from her, and she too walked out the doors and disappeared into the light, no longer shackled by guilt. Bertrand lingered a bit longer. He was concerned about the hotel, but we assured him that it would reopen now that the horror was over. He seemed satisfied, and then he too passed on.

                                                        Troy, Sandra and Wynona, however, made no move toward the door. Wynona seemed frightened of it, honestly. She seems to think that eternal damnation awaits her if she passes through it, and she may be right. At the very least, her she has a bit of karmic cleansing to undergo, and her next incarnation probably won’t be so posh. Troy, on the other hand, just seemed comfortable where he is. I don’t know what, if anything, is holding him here. Maybe nothing. But he doesn’t seem ready to pass on yet. I’m not sure what will become of him, but we need to discuss it before we leave the hotel. And as for Sandra, I’m afraid she’s too attached to Natalie to move on now, and that’s not good.

                                                        I fear we need to find Cindy quickly. The outsider was certainly trying to create another of its kind by planting its seed pod in her, and we fear that it will germinate inside her and grow a new terror elsewhere if we don’t stop it. It may already be too late.


                                                        No Prejudice

                                                        William Gibson was quite upset to learn that Marvin had been wrongly accused, and I almost feared he would attack Thomas. So while Doc checked on Marvin to make sure he was okay, I spoke with William. He and Marvin has struck up a friendship, and seem to be getting along famously. William told me that he respects people of other races. He was raised by a black nanny, and she was as much a mother to him as his biological mother. He learned from her that racial differences exist only in the mind, and we’re all people just the same. It was pleasant to hear that someone from his time held such a view. Having suffered a bit of prejudice when I was younger in Taiwan because I’m half Kiwi, I found William’s outlook quite pleasant and refreshing. I think I would have liked William very much when he was alive, and I regret that we couldn’t spend more time together. The Bride seemed quite taken with him as well. He’s no Karl, though, and I do look forward to getting back to Dark Haven Bluffs and my boyfriend again.
