                        atamajakki:
                        I could see a strong case being made (I may well type one later; I'm sleepy and confined to my tablet) for the krewe itself as the plot motivator.
                        I agree. One of the reason's I've heard for the dearth of tier 3 krewes and trash-culture feel of present day sineaters is to allow PC krewes to bring about significant and lasting change in sineater society. Even if they don't, others will try to.

                        The Kings Raven:
                        The whole war in the underworld concept completely misses the point of what Geist is about. A second chance at life.
                        This is why I brought up the notion of "ghosts of krewes past." Rather than a malevolent conspiracy to be warred against, it's an echo written into the geists that were changed by serving as its channel. And since such an echo could only be carried through those geists, and geists are in their ideal state when bound to a sineater and walking both worlds, it naturally becomes something present in both worlds.

                        But geists don't organise much by themselves, so on Earth it's going to be more like a pattern of Sacrosanct than a big enemy krewe. And yet it will pose many of the dangers of a big enemy krewe. Meanwhile, PC sineater society is the way it is because it's the isolated geists and the trash-culture Bound who are the ones who tend not to get sucked into those patterns of the past.

                        But leftover channels of a dead conspiracy isn't a good way for geists to be. It's not good for the sineaters they're bound to. And it's not good for the humans or ghosts - or other sineaters - they steamroller over, either. So they have to be taken out. But taking them out really means laying them - just like ghosts. And giving them a second chance - just like the PC's have had.

                        And that's something that could be at the heart of a krewe chronicle too.

                                                            Apologies if I'm reiterating what other folks have stated they wanted already;

                                                            The Synergy system overhauled; the hierarchy of sins never made any sense to me to reflect much of the Geist/Sineater dynamic. Not enough focus on the individual Geist at all. It resembles an early draft artifact of the Sin-Eaters being a heavy Psychopomp type without the rest of the material really supporting it that well (by that I mean in presentation; resolving a ghosts issues is the best way to get plasm, but it gets such a TINY reference, and many of the archetypes don't quite jive with it). Needs fixing. It's been the most asynchronous "morality" I've ran into with the WoD line thus far, which is very odd.

                                                            -Some mechanic to reflect the influence and impact of the Geist; mentioned plenty of times. It doesn't do enough for my tastes, but this is skewed in part due to my love of Wraith; I'd much prefer to see something like "Shadow dice" to reflect getting the cooperation of your Geist empowering you, but working against it actively posing more tangible issues. Synergy still doesn't quite do enough here.

                                                            -More focus on the Geist itself; I didn't feel that reading and then playing the game. It feels utterly neutered and for all the spectacular things it gives the character, treated to much in presentation as an afterthought after it creates the Sin Eater.

                                                            -Less blather about Spirit/Ghost hybrids; seriously, no. Treat them as their own thing, even if -sometimes- they -might- be the result of a ghost eating a spirit or vice versa. It's not very evocative, it doesn't really give them enough oomph. Since Book of the Dead, Mummy and even GMC, I think there's much more room to present these things as something interesting and unique. Tacking them off as some meta-mechanical hybrid doesn't really evoke how fucked up and interesting they are, let alone the potential they have. It feels too much like a major facet of the character is more of a meta-mechanic afterthought. We don't need justification for why these exist Out-of-Character because they weren't originally part of the other setting books; it should be enough that they do.

                                                            -More emphasis and focus on the Archetypes; there's a consistent complaint that there's a lack of "focus" in the game I've gotten from my play-groups; until I started reading (and re-reading) Archetypes and just how important they are to -giving- your character that big Purpose with a capital P, I had the same issues. This is a -big- area of the game I didn't feel got presented as being as important as it is. Primarily this is a formatting issue; I think they need to be at the forefront more given they pretty much are the way to mould where your character is going, what they're doing and even a means of regaining Plasm.

                                                            Off the top of my head. I have the book, but only got to play it a couple of times and haven't since. We never got the chance to get the 1.1 version (missed out it seems on the, show your book, get it on Drive Thru thing that went some time back) when really that should have just been handed out as a free errata. Might have helped, but there's some things I definitely know I'd like to see stemming from what we ran into.

                                                            Werewolf: the Forsaken Overhaul: A W:tF Shard and House Rules, Pre-Idigam Chronicles and taking different route. Constantly being Updated.
                                                            New Bloodlines and Blood and Smoke Updates: Homebrew Bloodlines and updates to prior ones.
                                                            Belial's Brood Blood and Smoke: An updated take on the Forsworn.
