                            Originally posted by MachineIV View Post
                            Sorry if I haven't made it clear; the Seemings have all been sort of... format spitballing? I'm considering a few different routes here. I'm leaning toward:

                            Benefits:

                            1) Coping mechanism/Clarity gain thing.

                            2) A mechanical boost to your escape thing.

                            Drawbacks:

                            1) A thing that risks Clarity, adds to your list of risks.

                            2) A thing that makes your Changeling experience a little more complicated mechanically.
                            As far as spitballing goes, this feels pretty solid; what defines you and what puts that at risk are good cores for the whole "Claiming your identity" aspect. The issue that concerns me is the mechanical boosts, because mechanics help give definition to characterization if absolutely nothing else.

                            It's somewhat easier to do penalties than benefits, because penalties take away from a universal, so the characters don't gain specialization in a particular area. The Beast penalty, for example, of having a harder time slipping manacles won't limit the number of potential Beast escape artists. Frankly, the opposite is more likely. In the end, it doesn't mechanically incentivize one kind of Beast. A mechanical benefit of some sort encourages a particular field of endeavour, and people won't really slot that into place if it doesn't aid the character's area of focus.

                            It's something to think carefully on. For the Wizened, I think this is well done, because what their creative endeavour is doesn't have broad limits. A Wizened who acts as the alderman for a freehold may spend their time better organizing its affairs. One with a more social bent might take time to write a speech. Ultimately, the benefit is tied into the concept of the Wizened's self-definition, rather than defining them in a specific way.

                            Not sure if this is helpful or something you have long since considered but, with 1e, if you wanted to play a social character, you played a Fairest. If you played an Elemental or a Wizened, you put yourself at a disadvantage when doing social characters. 2e looks to be trying to avoid this and I sincerely hope it manages it.

                                                On the Wizened Seeming: I think I just fell in love.

                                                “Art” isn't a matter of what you do, but how you do it. The cook in a MASH unit isn't an artist; he can't afford to be, as he has the much more important responsibility of ensuring that everybody gets fed in a timely manner. OTOH, a chef on a luxury liner can use the same skills to prepare a meal that his patrons would die for — or kill for. Both are cooks; but one is concerned about quantity over quality, and the other is concerned with achieving excellence in his chosen profession. The Wizened is a would-be chef who's forced to prepare meals for a MASH unit day in and day out, until he finally finds a way to achieve greatness even under those conditions by tossing away everything that isn't part of the finely honed chef he wishes to become.

                                                I think it's also worth noting that just about anything can be an art. There's a reason why fighting styles of the orient are so often referred to as “martial arts”; parkour can also be thought of as an art. And then there's the art of the deal. As long as what you're doing is done with style, it's an art.
