    Hey guys, I think I might have something positive to contribute to this Project. (Unless this has already been brought up already. There are a lot of posts between here and the start.) But first I really think focusing on the whole "No one is in charge" thing is another step backwards. Especially since the way things are now the real theme of Genius actually is: Bitter Dissapointment. But enough of that, I'm here to actually contribute in a positive way.

    Genius sorta has the same problem that Requiem 1st Edition had when it first came out. It has no real mysteries to explore. Requiem 2nd Edition remedied that with both the Strix Chronicle and the magnificent World We Die In section that's just chalked full of unique ideas and setting. I think Genius need something like that, a jumping off point for players and STs to showcase what's possible and what interesting stories can be told.

    For instance, you know all those Splintered Timelines that Mage, Hunter, and Demon introduced? Well, what if we tied those more directly into the Genius gameline as a whole? They already function similarly to Bardos already, why not just use those instead and give them an intimate link to being Inspired. Imagine what the game would be like if when a Genius Catalyses they cognitively perceive and comprehend an infinitude of possible realities. Each one either a divergence from how the world formed (different timelines, life forms, physics) or a possibility of how the world could be (hyper technology, Utopias, Hellscapes.) From that infinitude does the Inspired comprehend physical laws and scientify properties reality has either rejected or isn't ready for, and that is how they build their wonders. But those laws and properties are not of the current reality, so the Genius' inventions are prone to Havok as reality tries to eliminate the anachronism that taints it. But more than that, the Genii are able to comprehend how to make reality like the possible Utopias of the Infinitude. They know how they can make the world better, kinder, safer. But in Infinitude they also see all of realities indifference and mankind's folly, equally able to see how things can go wrong and fall apart. The game then becomes about the Genii gauging how much they're willing to transgress in order to bring about their vision of the future against what costs they're willing to make to get there. I call it: The Hollow Earths Chronicle, using the term Hollow Earths to refer to the Infinitude of Splintered Timelines.

    Aaaaand that's all I've got for now. It's been on my mind for the past couple of days or so, so I hope it helps.

    *pokes head back through portal*

                            Wow, busy night that I missed.

                            In regards to the Peerage and Lemuria, we don't have to do away with their Foundations and Baramins, just downplay the results of the Invisible war and who 'controls' the world. The trend in 2E is having less overall backstory to allow Storytellers and players to craft their own histories. When it comes to what each splat defines a Genius have as the Y-splat options they are still useful. Each Foundation offers a How for the Genius with its grants and outlook, as approaches towards the Mad Arts & Sciences exist within them. The Catalyst determines the Who as common foibles, quirks, and personality types for the Genius. As for Fellowship/Field expansion that is the What of what branch the Genius is more actively engaged in.

                            ------------------

                            When it comes to themes for Genius I've felt there should be a shift towards Untruth and Uncertainty. An attempt to embrace past criticisms of 'amounts to nothing' and 'bitter disappointment' and 'no one is in charge.' Mage has it easy for a game where their Truth is the existence of Supernal magic. And compared to other game lines one with no straight answers up front is refreshing in my eyes.

                            Do we actually know what the next scientific breakthrough or the next big invention is going to be? Sure you can following current trends, development blogs and cutting edge science but even within the last few decades there were plenty of surprises. Who would have predicted the iPod, Youtube, Pokemon Go and a variety of other platforms? Further back in history, when the proponents to Alternating and Direct current were duking it out who knew, at the time, what the future harnessing of electricity would look like?

                            For a Genius, the are posed between the world as it is and the world that isn't. Untruths, false realities, splintered timelines and stranger dimensions all vying for existence. Why should reality have all the fun? Why should what is currently known of science be absolutely true? After all Science is a process, a methodology of learning about the world that has shifted radically as understanding has increased. But still, you have the disproven theories, the technology far ahead of its time, and the countless alternatives to 'Truth' vying for attention. Inspiration and Mania is less about guiding humanity towards greater eras of enlightenment and more the chaotic stream of all ideas, good and bad and weird and ludicrous and failed ideas, all pushing for existence. In the mind of a Genius, with the inspired as their conduit.

                            Uncertainty comes from the questions the Genius has when they have a chance to stop and think. Sure, your advancements in Metaphysics may have earned you a flying telepathically controlled entropy flipping car, but unforeseen Faults can creep in without your awareness. The next stroke of Havoc could destroy it completely, or the next Umada Field or Bardo goes against it enough to destroy it. If your Fellowship has gathered much consistency, why are their still holes in your theory that can rip it apart? What if you pegged your opponent herbologist as to how her Wonders work, yet her paradigm comes blindside?

                            Isn't it a common roleplaying experience where you're put on the spot with an unfinished plan, unexpected arrivals, and underfunded equipment? The Peerage warns that for all the parts where Mania starts making sense, it doesn't, to remind Genii that they may not be able to plan for everything. This encourages lateral thinking, something that Genii are masters of (for how crazy their logic sounds on the outside). Lemuria and similar Inspired antagonists have rejected the warning of there being no hard answers, taking to their theory of everything at the cost of being unable to divert from it strongly, of preventing variety amongst their Wonders.

                            The question from an Uncertain future isn't what is right or wrong, the question is who will act.

                            ------------------

                            Building from the Untruths idea, since Bardos do require Mania to survive you can draft up as many antagonistic invaders as you do more neutral Bardos.

                            The other set of adversies are the Clockstoppers, or Hollow Men. The people who can cause works of Mania to not exist, predisposed towards Geniuses but if left unchecked can undo technological advancement as we know it. This is only my taste but their name never impressed me, and their span of Voids seemed arbitrary. But it is useful to have adversaries that are as anti-Genius as the Fetch's abilities are anti-Changeling. Definitely could use some expansion and more prominence.

                                    Not so much ignorance as rationalization, I think.

                                    Just as brilliant people can end up constructing brilliant reasons why their biases and prejudices are right, a Genius' superhuman pattern-matching ability can turn inward on itself and create spurious reasons why X is true just as easily as ways to make rocket boots.

                                    That instinct to justify ourselves ... who hasn't seen some news story or argument and wanted it to be true, even if we suspected it was false, because it would make us look better? One of those jerks did something that perfectly conforms to our idea of them; someone tried out our pet Thing and it worked brilliantly; there's some brilliant argument why an idea we hate is self-contradictory/illegal/ruining everything.

                                    And then it turns out to be wrong.

                                    It's where urban legends come from. Who really wants to check if Winston Churchill said "the fascists of the future will be called anti-fascists", or a Societ nail factory once filled a quota for "12 tonnes of nails" by making a single 12-tonne nail, or Christians are up in arms over Starbucks' red coffee cup? Wow, 8 spiders a year? Really? That'll really gross out my friends ...

                                    Skepticism can turn inwards too. Conspiracy theorists have piles of evidence, detailed debunkings of the truth, all of it subtly wrong. Homeopaths have peer-reviewed studies "proving" homeopathy works. Academics cite papers that they haven't read that supposedly debunk myths.

                                    It's that impulse to be "right" even when you aren't right that creates cranks, and that's what Geniuses are - weaponized cranks, ones whose Time Cube-esque delusions produce useful results.

                                    That's what Unmada represent, I think. That temptation to rationalize.

                                    Or at least that's what I think they should represent. Right now they're more generalized mental-illness-derived delusions, since they're basically just the Mad nicked from Awakening.

                                    Not "we don't know what's going on, we need to figure it out" - "you don't know what's going on, you need to stop pretending you do". But at the same time, you can't abandon everything you believe; that way lies solipsism and madness.

                                            Originally posted by malonkey1 View Post
                                            I've been thinking that Inspired society isn't as open to non-scientists as I think it could be. Perhaps we could expand what the Foundations' members do?
                                            • Artificers: Instead of being Mad Engineers, they're makers and crafters of all stripes, who believe their Magnum Opus is best served by making concrete physical things. They're Engineers, Artisans, and Industrialists.
                                            • Directors: Instead of Mad Psychologists, the Directors are the Genii that believe that in order to effect their dreams, they need to control the underlying systems of society. They're the Sociologists, Economists and Politicians.
                                            • Navigators: Instead of Mad Physicists, the Navigators are people of action, who follow the old adage of "Want a job done right, do it yourself." They're the Explorers, Experimentalists and Warriors. Tony Stark (Marvel Comics) and the Joker fit in here.
                                            • Progenitors: Instead of Mad Biologists, Progenitors are Genii who want to change the world by changing the people. They are the Doctors, Educators and Transhumanists.
                                            • Scholastics: Instead of being Mad Philosophers, they're the ones who believe that they can achieve their vision by perfecting an idea. They're the Philosophers, Poets, and Occultists.
                                            I like this. Diversity should definitely be emphasized.

                                            The change to Progenitors is interesting. If Internalized Wonders are changed to emphasize Inspired ideas more clearly, not just surgical implants, it's a natural fit for them to be Educators with their Grant.

                                            Originally posted by malonkey1 View Post
                                            With Clockstoppers, I think they fall kind of flat because all they are is a literally empty-headed luddite. To me, I think it'd be more interesting if they had something like Inspiration, but instead of wanting to make a future, they want to bring back a lost past. If the Genii are futurists and innnovators, then the Clockstoppers are Romantics and nostalgic. They may not even be malicious to Genii by default, but they want a time that's been lost and may never have really existed, and the constant march of change and progress scares them and pushes them to fight back.

                                            For example, you may have a man who wants society to go back to the "white picket fence" ideal 50's he remembers, where everything was simpler, family mattered, and people still worked for a living, ignorant of how terrible that time was for many groups of people. He's normally a sweet old grandpa, but when he sees society move away from the values he remembers, he starts to get spiteful, without being able to control or really notice it. He starts posting hateful memes on Facebook, and then he starts berating and yelling at young people for not living how he lived, and as time goes on, that anger at the world changing around him twists in on itself until he snaps and "un-Catalyzes" into a Clockstopper. He's not empty, he's scared and angry and lashing out at what he thinks needs to be destroyed. He just wants to make America great again.
                                            I've said this before, but Clockstoppers have always worked for me because "luddite" is such a classic type of protagonist. And really, can you blame them? Technology is scary, Inspired technology doubly so. If progress went awry, has gone awry, how do we stop it? Are we even capable of stopping it?

                                            It's not about being politically conservative. It's about a very specific fear that mad scientists represent, of a person who manages to impose some flawed idea on the world and break the world.

                                            The demagog whose ideas overrun the globe in a 1984-esque nightmare. The weapon of mass destruction that kills billions. The faceless new technology that lets the rich lay off workers ... hell, R.U.R was about vat-grown slaves becoming an oppressed underclass.

                                            But also stuff like Jurassic Park, Terminator, Alien. The scientist may be well-meaning, but the hero knows deep down it'll go awry and kill everyone. And so it does. So it always does. (Because science is evil.)
