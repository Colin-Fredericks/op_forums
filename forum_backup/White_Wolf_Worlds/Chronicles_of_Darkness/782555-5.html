            PROTIP: When you schedule a project to be written on the side, make sure you have the kind of attention span that doesn't focus on one thing at a time!

            Finally:

            Fabricants (aka Futurians, Technogores)

            Relax. Chew. It will be much easier for your liberation than it was for me. Once you are made pure of hydrocarbon-oh, the things we shall do. Together. Like before.

            If the past is a foreign country, then the future is another planet. Not only are no humans existent there, no human has ever been there, by definition. It hasn't happened yet, and to be frank, the only way we present folk can even imagine it is to speculate. Usually in ways that often does not remotely resemble the future, even from people who specialize in speculating what the future looks like. How many times has it been said humanity's technological development has reached its end state? In the past fifty years? From the other side, how many movies from around the 1980s have the 2000s as a technological wasteland of deserts? Truly, the future is an alien world, and anything of it is even more alien.

            Is that why fabricants hate the people of the present so much? Are their mechanical parts born of a potential future, one they know will never arrive in even their extendable lifetimes? Is the loathing that pulses through their oil-pump hearts really born not of any inherent nature, but of loneliness and anger for the world they can no longer be part of, and the world that they could call home never reached? It certainly explains their more infectious behavior, and also why they don't seem intent so much on the extermination of organic life as avoiding it.

            But the writer digresses. Because the part that others recognize in fabricants, what makes them so mutually horrifying, is that the hateful androids aren't from the carbon-less future, not really. Once upon a time, they were humans, friends and family, husbands and wives. All that is gone now, subsumed into silicon and steel-and all their soul consumed by hatred for not only the species they once were, but anything that reminds them of fleshy existence. Even their own skins.

            Fabricants are, in shorthand, organic beings who have become almost entirely mechanical forms of the people and animals they once were, with the only parts that aren't whirring machinery being their now leathery skins, which can be shed and replaced as easily as a human takes his favorite suit on and off. The machine only resembles the being it used to be in exterior frame-because the machine is also scientifically possible by largely mundane principles of engineering. Sure, some of the computational units in the brain-case have occult symbols etched in their circuitry that allow quantum computing, and the power source is never quite clear where it's getting the energy from to convert what they eat into battery power, but everything else? Completely in the bounds of hypothetical near-future robotics and engineering (no matter where they show up-a less advanced are may find itself plagued with fabricants that have LED eyes and piston muscles, while the First World's native technogores have sleek insides that have as much to do with nanotech as larger-scale metalwork).

            All resemble their previous selves, except in one way-utter, gut-level contempt for any form of mobile carbon-based life. Even their skins come off as being too much like the bipedal things they have to switch off their olfactory sensors to deal with every damn day. In fact, one of the weaknesses of a futurian infestation is that they take the opportunity to remove their skins the moment they feel safely private from any prying eyes, allowing their circuits to cool in the air. This distaste colors literally everything they do and think; the transformation into a fabricant breaks all casual connection with anything organic under the sheer force of disgust; fabricants who were loners in their previous life happily become highly-advanced hermits, the only company needed or wanted being whatever machines they can scrounge up to serve as decoration and spare parts. The ones who were closely connected still love their families-but that love is twisted, constantly battling with the nauseous feeling in the stomach-furnaces that the fabricants suffer whenever they think about anything except the most cerebral aspects of their loved ones' existence.

            Those same loved ones are usually patients one through ten when the fabricants discover they can "liberate" others into the same mechanical existence.

            How a particular "code" of fabricants (the accepted term for a particular strain of the infection) arises is perhaps the most mysterious aspect about them, not the least because there actually is one exact thing in common: the proto-fabricant becomes "unstuck" in time for at least three seconds-not just frozen in time or alternatively moving when time is stopped around her, but actually experiences the passing of time while outside conventional time (a disorienting experience, to say the least). Unsurprisingly, this is incredibly rare, usually involving the very definition of a freak series of events or deliberate engineering (and before you ask, no, fabricants themselves almost never have enough resources or expertise in temporal phenomenon to engineer said events). When the proto-fabricant comes back, they seem completely fine-at first. Over the course of a few months, the mind of the proto-fabricant begins to dwell on both the fragility of organic life, and its flaws. The fundamental uselessness of breathing in everyday life, for example, or how instinct that overrides compassion and reason are the results of chemicals not released by the brain. Many proto-fabricants shake off these thoughts, and the transformation ends there. Some, whether weak-willed or already misanthropic, come to agree with this viewpoint, which naturally leads into the devaluation of the benefits of organic life ("Who was the bright spark that thought touch-and thus pain-was such a good thing?"), a growing annoyance with uncontrolled emotion or inconveniences produced by frailty, and eventually, the revelation that organic life is not only flawed, but inherently disgusting-not worth the complexity of the molecules it's born from. With every step, the proto-fabricant's insides reconfigure and calcify a little more, wires replacing nerves, newly steel joints growing tiny motors, the heart being recycled for the nanotubes of a fuel-efficient carburetor. By the time of the final revelation, the brain itself turns to crystalline superconductors of an unknown silicon-platinum composite, and a new technogore stalks and clanks through the night.

            Even people who only know the new futurian can tell the gradual change in the fabricant's personality. While the transformation does not rob her of any emotional capacity (logic actually requires emotion-otherwise the mind lacks the ability to assign good feelings, and thus value, to any given option, and thus the mind cannot make decisions at all, let alone logical ones), the growing disgust does manifest in increasing coldness and distance towards anything and everything not a fabricant or a less-advanced machine. It's simply not prudent to reveal the fabricant's actual feelings, and the bile is so great that any intelligent fabricant (which is all of them) recognizes that it may creep into anything but the most aloof behavior. They often take to wearing sunglasses to hide any static or damage to their video-screen eyes, and rarely open their mouths entirely, as the teeth have become ceramic and the tongue wiggling rubber studded with sensors for temperature and mineral composition. They become more technically skilled, the gradual transformation of the brain from grey matter to CPU mainframe coming with programs that direct the fabricant in the esoteric engineering needed to repair themselves, which naturally includes a great deal of knowledge on programming anything less advanced than their own makeup-they need to understand much of "relic" technology in case they ever need to incorporate it into themselves as a replacement. Most, of course, do not notice-and there is no "but" addendum for loved ones, not really. Fabricants aren't made sociopaths, and thus when faced with an organic they actually care for, they are able to swallow their disgust by focusing entirely on non-physical aspect of their relationship-they may come off as depressed, and they recoil at touch or the thought of sexuality, but the latter isn't that frequently brought up, and the stigma around mental illness means many ignore the former.

            Under the skin and the forced smile, though, the fabricants are lonely creatures. Surrounded by creatures that revolt them simply by the beating of their hearts, feeling only comforted by the hum of machines and tolerating plants alone among all living things (most fabricants run on biofuel), technogores are repelled by social contact. For some, that's fine-many fabricants were misanthropic loners before their transformation, and so all they need to do to maintain their spare part budget is find a stay-at-home job of their choice, preferably in the most deserted area they can find-easy enough, for a genus of natural engineers and programmers. The more social ones, however, are torn-they remember the days that they could look at a family member and not feel as though they were stepping in dog shit. Thus, they swallow their distaste, and resign to an existence of misery...

            Then, one day, they get the impulse to feed some of their skin to an animal. Maybe a rat, an insect, the family dog. They don't know why, they just cut a bit of the grimly necessary disguise off, watch it regenerate, and then force feed it. And then they watch, rapt, as the animal's skin becomes loose-fitting, then falls off to reveal another advanced robot!

            Whatever eldritch mechanism that causes "liberation", as fabricants term it, it still seems to exist in their skin disguises. Actually, it exists everywhere in the fabricant, but the skin is the most easily healed and palatable. Ingesting it starts the metamorphosis into a fabricant of the same technological background as the vector, but also links their minds to the fabricant's disgust for life, bombarding them with every vicious invective and feeling the futurian has realized about organic matter. The two sync well, and what took the original fabricant a matter of months is shortened to hours among those who partake of fabricant flesh. Yes, it's possible to resist the tide of anger, to fight off the infection, but technogores don't liberate everyone-only people they view as useful allies, or more importantly people they would love, if it wasn't for their fleshy bits. Generally, a fabricant knows just what to think to get her infected to agree with her premises on the fallibility of carbon vs the perfection of metal, and if the soft method doesn't work, well there's always brainwashing. Upon the full transformation, a child-technogore feels instinctive loyalty to his code of fabricants, "us against the world." From there, fabricants quickly convert those closest to them-and almost invariably, start looking into spreading their perfect condition among others, cults of a dark transhumanism that would happily destroy most of the world to rid it of awful, disgusting flesh (though fabricants do become more logical, and realize it's probably best to leave at least some humans organic-besides the fact that some people deserve to be weak, they don't know how to build babies). They're usually caught before they cause any global damage, they're not as subtle as they'd like to be-but what they do leave is bad enough, including mechanical beasts that share the same hatred for organic life.

            As a final insult to injury, though, it is possible to cure a fabricant, and turn them back into flesh that has no particular grudge against life. It's just that, besides being just as arcane as the strains of infection arise, and likely unique to each code, the fabricant has to be convinced to let go of hate, and accept guilt for her actions. Ask anyone who has dealt with prejudice about how easy that is.

                            Thanks for the tips:

                            Continuing;

                            Liberation From Flesh:

                            Part of the horror factor when it comes to fabricants is their viral nature-the realization they aren't truly replacing anyone, but infecting them with their hatred. Many players might be interested in roleplaying the metamorphosis, either completely or partially before fighting off the encroaching rage and inhuman body. Thus, it is useful to have a system for the process of "liberation," especially if a character's friendly Contacts (or even a True Friend, if the player permits it) are turned and wish to bring their friend into the purity of metallic existence, for their own comfort. Please keep in mind that, even if a PC is cured, the complete prejudice futurians instinctively feel towards humans and anything else with more hydrocarbons than iron is a massive shift in characterization, and one many players are not comfortable with, on either end (ask any D&D party with a player that could be titled "Chaotic Stupid"). Clear this with your players first.

                            Fabrication (Persistent, Tilt):

                            This organic character has been touched by whatever hate-powered forces that turn animal matter into mechanical frames, and it is quietly pushing him or her towards misanthropy and only valuing the imperishable nature of machines. Organic characters (including undead ones) who interact with your character in a meaningful way (a conversation that goes beyond small talk, an argument, or a vampire feeding on their blood are all good barometers) get a reflexive Manipulation + Empathy - the character's Composure to detect that there is something "off" about this character's emotions (they won't necessarily realize this is because of growing disgust with them unless they score an exceptional success, but they can tell there's an unnatural and likely deliberate coldness in their demeanor). In addition, the character gains one of the list of traits below, as their body becomes an advanced machine (all Attribute enhancements can take them above 5, though not Skills):

                            * Adaptive Musculature: The character gains +2 Dexterity and +2 Athletics, which can take them above 5.

                            * Cognitive Programming: The character gains +2 Intelligence, which can take them above 5, and the Good Time Management Merit regardless of prerequisites

                            * Redundant Systems: The character no longer suffers wound penalties, nor needs to roll to remain conscious when the rightmost Health box is full.

                            * Repair Protocols: The character gains +3 Science and a Specialty in Engineering, and +3 Computer with a Specialty in Adapting Obsolete Technology

                            * Resistant Materials: Natural armor 2B/2L, which stacks with any the character is wearing.

                            Beat: Whenever the character's growing hatred of organic life causes long-term problems. This also results in a cumulative -1 penalty to the next "progression" roll, below

                            Resolution: Transformation into a fabricant, while it apparently doesn't have a germ (or more likely nanobot) component (there hasn't been much opportunities for research on the project), is still a disease in many ways-it progresses gradually, and can be fought off if resisted. When a character gains this Tilt, note whether they are a "progenitor" infection (got the infection from being outside time, and thus will be the first of a new code if they are fully fabricated) or a "secondary" (got it from another fabricant). Once a month for progenitors and once an hour for secondaries (barring some arcane process of deliberately slowing the infection for the latter, which is not infrequent-it allows for greater subtlety), the character rolls Resolve + Integrity (or equivalent advantage, if they have it) + Supernatural Advantage (if present) vs. the number of seconds they were outside time (progenitors) or as a Clash of Wills against the fabricant that caused the Tilt (for secondaries). Failure on this roll means the infection progresses-stack another instance of the Tilt. Every stack beyond the initial gives the roll to detect something awry with the nascent fabricant a +1 bonus, and every second stack gives the character another fabricant trait. After three stacks, the character's converted digested system loses the ability to break down meat (the alcohols needed for biofuel aren't easily refined from animal matter), forcing a strictly vegan diet, though the character needs to only check once every three days for starvation (they still need to use the restroom, but to only vent liquid waste product that wasn't converted). At five stacks, natural healing ceases, instead forcing a Dexterity + Crafts roll to fix damage, which does not need proper tools unless the damage is aggravated (even a proto-fabricant without Repair Protocols instinctively understands enough about their new body to know how to jury-rig a repair), which takes five seconds for bashing damage, five minutes for lethal and five hours for aggravated, each success healing one box of Health. At seven, their eyes become mechanical optics, and a called shot to the head (even bashing damage) will damage them-the character still sees perfectly well, but the image of human eyes become broken up and staticky, impossible to mistake for anything natural, until the damage is repaired. More failures than the character's Resolve + Composure means the character has hit the point of no return; in a few short minutes, they develop any weaknesses and abilities they have not already, the skin develops the needed tailoring to be taken on and off, and if mortal develops the Dread Powers of their code; the Tilt is resolved as the character has been fully transformed into a new technogore. Success means the infection stalls-five over the course of the Tilt breaks the infection, and the character loses one stack of Fabrication every day until the Tilt is fully lost.

                            Possible bonuses: The character has saved an human's life before the roll (+5), the character has regained Willpower from their Virtue in the past week (+3), Merits that depend on empathy and friendship with organics (+1 for every dot), Teamwork actions to remind the character of compassion for organic life (varies)

                            Possible penalties: The character murders a human (-5), the character deals with the stress by retreating from the world completely (-3), the character exploits the fabricant traits for violent actions (-2), the character has gained a Beat from this Tilt before the next roll (-1, cumulative), the fabricant that caused this condition knows the character well (-2, secondaries only).

                            Special: Supernatural Technogores

                            The fabrication process isn't choosy; so long as the infected subject is a mostly organic life form with a nature that does not directly conflict with the robotic nature of the being he is becoming, the infection can take root and possibly complete metamorphosis. This means that creatures actually immune to fabrication is an incredibly narrow category. Besides the infamous tendency of fabricants to convert animals into lethal robotic minions and pets, this also means the monsters of the world are quite capable of being incorporated into the code as powerful living weapons against animal life. While they don't replicate a code's Dread Powers, this is only because the supernatural components of a fabricant body are dedicated to replicating organic features of a monstrous fabricant's abilities; vampiric fangs are easily swapped for hypodermic needles, a Promethean's ability to feed on energy to heal is simply given a set of power collectors for the same purpose, a werewolf's shapeshifiting can easily be to a frame with a different set of weapons and propulsion system (and the wolf-people are an especially juicy prize-just because natural healing isn't possible doesn't mean regeneration isn't, wires and gears reassembling themselves and reattaching even as they are dented).

                            About the only published (at the time of this writing) monsters completely immune to fabrication are Unfleshed Prometheans (who are already living machines) and the Unchained (who are also living machines, except for their Covers-which treat the encroaching fabrication as hostile malware). Being outside time does not expose them to the infection, and all feeding them fabricant flesh does is make them very sick, and in the case of beings capable of sleep, give them terrible nightmares of a mechanized world where unconverted organics are treated to horrifying slavery in the halls of a robotic nobility (this is actually not any different from a futurian's normal wish fulfillment dream, but immunes lack the fundamental hostility that causes their minds to interpret it as anything other than a surreal horror). The monsters partially immune to infection are a little less narrow, but not by much, and it depends on having an odd relationship with time and its sibling, destiny; Changelings and Arisen, along with whatever others have temporal manipulation fundamental to their beings (ST's discretion; it should not just be beings with the power to manipulate time, but for whom causality is tilted by the very nature of their existence-if Sirens exist in your CofD, the fact they they themselves are products of a potential future and are born to pull destiny away from that future are a good example). The infection takes with them, but it stalls out at three stacks (when the Fabricated individual must eat only plant matter), and the aggression never goes into outright hatred; the partial immune may become extremely snappy and impatient with organics, but apart from transhumanist sympathies, this never quite goes into disgust for organic life as a concept (and in fact, the partial immune may not realize anything's wrong until someone actually shows them the mechanical bits covering their insides). Ironically, this also means the partially immunes are also the ones that others may notice acting strange, since the emotions of anger are actually a lot harder to conceal than hatred. In game terms, the Beat Condition for partially is changed to "gain a Beat whenever you act against the wishes of organic allies to protect them from their fundamental weaknesses", and at three stacks, failing roll to resist further infection means the next roll will be after a three month interval-the fabrication halts, but gets more entrenched. They never become full fabircants.

                            Unfortunately, there are one thing that make this not nearly as appealing as it could be; the fundamental hostility that fabricants feel towards organic beings is simply not present for immunes. This isn't a particularly special sense that alerts them to something being off when they realize they aren't disgusted towards this seemingly normal individual, it's that when they find out, the ire...vanishes. For Unfleshed and Unchained, the reasons should be obvious, but as soon as they realize otherwise, the aggression rapidly tapers off. For partial immunes, the anger is still present, but even then, it only goes into feelings of irritation and annoyance; more patronizing than actually vicious. Why is unknown; is it because the programming of the infection realizes it's pointless? Is it because none of these creatures really obeys the normal nature of hydrocarbon life?

                            Or-as many more paranoid scholars think-is it because all of the above have beings who can be won over to the fabricants' cause through reasoned argument?

                            Ephemerals, as a whole, do not naturally care as a whole if the dominant species is a mechanical one-the futurians' emotions generate Resonance and contribute to the Astral same as any other human, ghosts are the very definition of provincial and don't care unless the liberation affects their personal goals, and angels are, well, angels. It depends on what the God-Machine feels is best at the time. Unfleshed, like most other Prometheans, have a Centimani contingent, and discovering there's a race of humans who have finally clued into how disgusting it is as a species (and doesn't naturally feel Disquiet-all instinctive aggression is gone) thrills no small part of them. Unchained have no small number of misanthropes themselves who view humans as mindless cogs in Infrastructure anyway, and converting the Machine's tools into allies appeals to them (and some Integrators may think they're doing what God wants and making a new race of man in Its image). Partial immunes less so, but just because the fabrication process stalls doesn't mean they're immune to mental influence; so long as a fabricant can phrase their mission in such a way the infected carrier believes what they're doing is not fundamentally wrong (that fabricants made of iron are natural enemies of the True Fae, that they cannot sin against a particular Judge, that they put far less strain on the environment due to a lack of need for meat or as much for crops), any Social Influence for the fabricant to convince the carrier to help automatically succeeds at the roll for a good first impression, and with the carrier having one open Door.

                            Thus, the immunes unwillingly trade the terror of being assimilated into something hostile and alien for a double dose for fear of betrayal; being a member of an immune species simply means any conspirators with the fabricants cannot have their allegiance revealed by tearing skin.

                                                            Werewolves and changelings are apples and oranges.

                                                            When werewolves are in human form they have human DNA. When werewolves are in wolf form they have wolf DNA. When they are in hybrid form they have crazy DNA. When changelings have their mask up they have human DNA. When they drop the mask they still have human DNA.

                                                            When werewolves are in human form they respond to drugs and poisons as a human. When they are in wolf form they respond to drugs and poisons as a wolf does. When a werewolf is in hybrid forms they respond in crazy mashup fashion. When a changeling has their mask up they respond to drugs and poisons as a human does. When a changeling drops the mask they still respond to drugs and poisons as a human would.

                                                            When a werewolf changes into a wolf they're a wolf, when a changeling drops the mask to reveal they are a rock they aren't actually a rock, or they wouldn't have DNA, or blood, or an immune system that worries about the flu, or responds to chemicals just like a human body does. In point of fact, a changeling can't even weigh as much as a rock of their size, the mask and mien can only differ by 20 pounds if memory serves. A granite statue of a 200 lb man would weigh 550 lbs if made out of granite, 520 lbs if marble, the changeling is stuck weighing 180 to 220 lbs in their mien if their mask is 200 lbs.

                                                            And looking to changeling's crossover rules, there's the fact that vampires can drink the blood of changelings. Vampires can only drink blood. If a changeling didn't have blood but oil because they were a machine, water because they're actually made from ice, or sap because they're really a tree, then the vampire wouldn't be able to drink their nonexistent blood, now would they?

                                                            Also, in the crossover section dealing with werewolves, it stated that werewolves can eat changelings to replenish essence. Eating to replenish essence only works with humans, wolves, and spirits. Changelings certainly don't count as wolves or spirits, I'm pretty sure. So unless the writer and author completely spaced out, the natural conclusion is that they considered changelings to be human. Of course it's possible they did completely space out, since in werewolf first edition it was explicitly stated that werewolves couldn't eat other supernaturals for essence, so either they forgot, or they disagreed.
