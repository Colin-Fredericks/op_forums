                    Depth 1 are Loci, if memory doesn't deceive me. A region in the mundane Wilderness means more symbolic resonance with the Border Marches, so they have greater Depth. The wilder, the better. The Uratha like the shallows because that's where it's most likely things of the Flesh or Shadow will attempt to cross over because it's a short walk, a matter of seconds or minutes, and that's really tempting for a Spirit or one of the Wise.

                    The Depths offer greater benefits to hunting, but it can take weeks to cross them and gods live in there, making them far more dangerous. You can just walk into and out of them though- Wits + Survival I think. Fail a regular navigation roll and you can stumble into Pangaea by accident. Because transition is easier, you also need to make rolls to avoid running into any Spirits that are hanging around the Flesh.

                            Not quite. The Gauntlet is the Border Marches, crushed into some kind of massive scab or scar across the entire world, and the reason the Gauntlet is thickest around human inhabitation is because it has the least Resonance with Pangaea, which despite being a whole other realm was also the primary means of going back and forth between the worlds. In the Sundered World, where humans build there are no Marches, and because there are no Marches there's no way to cross over from Flesh to Shadow- the route is completely blocked.

                            This eventually transitions into the modern status quo. Human building = Basically impossible to cross. Wild Places = Easy transition due to Resonance with Pangaea. Loci = As always, quick crossing points between worlds. In the Sundered World, a Locus is even more dramatic than normal. There's no need to Reach, no need to perform some sort of ritual to gain access, you can simply walk into a Locus, spend thirty seconds walking through the Marches, and end up in the Shadow.

                                05-24-2016, 01:14 PM
                                Well this has been a long time coming, but I really wanted to get this right. Hopefully everyone likes it. Ladies and gentlemen, the Unchained of the Sundered World.

                                *****
                                Sundered World Demons

                                The Machine: There is more than one god wandering the world before the Sundering, and that means the God-Machine needs to work with an extraordinarily light touch. Pangaeans are more than a match for all but the most powerful of its Angels, the Diharim howl from the Hisil, the High Chieftains of Faerie and the first Gods of the Thistle can walk the world freely before it becomes Ironside, and in the lands that will be called Kemet the temakh still serve Azar. Even long after Irem vanished from the world, the Machine will order Its servants to avoid the ruins and relics.

                                Areas of Infrastructure are as devoid of the presence of the Border Marches as anything constructed by man, Facilities can be confused with Ruins of the Time Before, and time splinters are mistaken for Emanation Realms. It is in these places, where the Machine can be sure It is alone, that It manifests in all Its power. The few Angels that exist spend most of their time in Facilities and time splinters, but some are still sent forth in order to perform Missions and oversee Infrastructure. These Angels are different than the ones that come later, a little more self-aware, a little more creative, given full license to use their Incepts whenever and however they see fit in order to be able to effectively defend themselves against the other creatures that may be watching over or using a given village.

                                The works of the God-Machine don’t reveal themselves quite as much in clockworks and oil, but in ceramics and stone. A village is arranged just so, ceramics with the words of a distant deity are buried under homes instead of the god-words of the Wise, a menhir carved with strange symbols, rivers and forests shaped with extremely specific earthworks to produce occult matrixes of their own, fires that refuse to burn out. An Angel could bend an entire village to its will, using them to mine gold and copper to produce circuitry for the God-Machine’s more complex projects. The bodies of Angels come in many forms, in pottery and statuary and copper ornamentation, in sacrificed humans and animals, in obsidian and wood.

                                The most common of the God-Machine’s servants in these days are Imperatives, flocks of them will descend upon a village It intends to subvert, often leaving the local Wise baffled. Imperatives are simple Ephemera, too simple to even be touched by any of the Arcana, and make excellent Mysteries. However, they are virtually impossible to halt or command without outright destroying them. Many Wise, apart from the Blood-Wise who are most familiar with the Shadow, will probably dismiss them as an irritating but relatively harmless spiritual phenomenon.

                                The Outcasts: The Unchained are rare, but not unheard of, and in a world where the God-Machine isn’t nearly as omnipresent their numbers start to accumulate over time. They are all drawn to Infrastructure, both as insight into their Ciphers and as one of the only sources of Aether they can find. Fortunately for most of the outcasts, Infrastructure is often close at hand, and it is usually the Infrastructure they established themselves. Where the Unchained do walk, they have a tendency to either hide or rule. It is a smaller world, with tighter communities, and becoming trusted enough to start making Pacts or gain a Cover that could enable them to live a relatively eccentric life is a difficult thing. Among the most common Covers that demons seek out are traders. Because the People, like many cultures of the time, have trade based on gift giving, Pacts are easily concealed as business deals. As a result, Patchwork Covers are common, with demons arriving from the wilds as a lost, but familiar, stranger.

                                The generous trader wanders their route, giving gifts of pottery with strange symbols, or textiles with unusual weaves, and asks for nothing in return but abstractions- relationships, memories, misfortunes -while heaping blessings on the heads of those who take them up. How could they say no, when their work is so obviously lucky? How could they say no, when they expects so little in return? Everyone loves the trader.

                                The hunter disappears for long stretches of time, alone, as is their custom. Everyone thinks they’re mad, but they always seem to come back. They are a master of beasts, served by strange monsters that can only come from the Border Marches, marked by peculiar scars, and changed by their visits to places of the Time Before. They might be feared, but they’re also respected. When they say a terrible spirit is coming to end their way of life, they are believed, and a hunting party heads out with them. Many often return with the same odd scars and unbelievable stories. They rarely speak of what they have seen, and spend more and more time with the hunter.

                                The elder will not be moved, and no shaman or Wise One will dissuade them from changing some aspect of the traditions. These ceramics should be buried here instead of there, they should be marked with these new signs, that earthwork should be torn down and a new one built here, we should honor the gods with these sacrifices instead. No one understands how they get away with it all, but they are just so persuasive.

                                The potter is a strange one, living on the outskirts of the village in a hut that has been blasted by lightning and scorched by flame. They have no Wise One, so the potter makes due as their shaman. The ceramics they make resemble nothing the People have ever seen, but they must work, because gifts of them have pleased the local spirits. The power this potter puts into the ceramics is like nothing the Wise have ever seen, for it does not trigger the Sleeping Curse and anyone can wield them. Their village is well armed against what is in the dark, but the potter doesn’t cease making increasingly elaborate and powerful works of art and some fear they know something’s coming and aren’t willing to speak of it.

                                Those Who Can See: Stigmatics are, understandably, a little rarer in these days. But where they appear, they appear in force. Whole villages are co-opted by the God-Machine for a project, and the inhabitants either become active parts of the project, or are warped by the proximity to active Infrastructure. They are not ember-bearers as understood by the Wise, and a god, but not one that the People normally worship has touched them. Villages of Stigmatics are peculiarities and terrors to the rest of the People, with strange traditions and frightful marks, they are mad oracles marching to the beat of an eldritch drum, following the path of a wheel even the Sky-Wise can’t see. These settlements are actively avoided, but in time a Circle of the Wise will be called to go and investigate. When they arrive, likely expecting a discrete being to appease, they find instead nothing to placate and only an otherworldly herald, an Angel, one of the God-Machine’s hands and feet. It is the Storm-Wise who are most likely to grasp what the God-Machine is, not a being but a force, something that moves and is moved in turn.

                                In a village that has a resident Unchained, however, the situation is often rather different. The demon recognizes others who are touched by the same hands that made it, and sees in them potential allies that can be told just enough to be made dangerous to their enemy. Those Stigmatics blessed with an Unchained patron find belonging and community of a human sort, and not the clockwork harmony of the Machine’s order, often organizing into a sort of cult around the demon. They tame cryptids and battle Angels; they make deals with their patron for power, and take up fantastical works of pottery that function as the hardware for the first Gadgets. One demon may slowly gather up enough Pacts to make a Patchwork Cover or several, preparing lives for decades and even centuries to come.

                                The small god embodied in a demon surrounds itself with children who resemble its form of perfection, and they’re groomed to attain desirable aspects to become the next incarnation of the village’s god. Some may only settle by taking tribute in the form of Pacts for desirable qualities of these children who will be heavily compensated. Others may become so enamored with a particular life that they want it all for themselves.

                                The cult of Stigmatics surrounding a demon beat down low-Rank Exiles, caging them with Bans and Banes. In time their patron will ascend, regaining its immortality and some of the raw might of its angelic Incarnation. Perhaps it will even find a way to become one with the Infrastructure it subverted, becoming a small God-Machine in its own right.

                                Wonders and Horrors: For the most part hidden from the God-Machine’s gaze by virtue of distance and Its need for concealment, the Unchained have a little more freedom to act. They can walk about in their true form and wield Exploits, but their greatest works remain the construction of powerful Gadgets. Most use ceramics, as unusual, magically potent, pieces of pottery are common amongst the Vinca. Others will raid Infrastructure for rare iron and gold, while others will use the ornamental, but readily available, copper. Gadgets installed into Pacts are more common in these days, when a Pact is liable to be a worked stone or a piece of pottery, and a village supported by a prolific Unchained Gadget-maker is in some ways more powerful than one supported by the Wise. Many Gadgets can be wielded by anyone, and a Gadget can be in anything suitably symbolically resonant. Perhaps the carved menhir symbolizing the villagers’ oaths to their Unchained patron is a Gadget a dozen times over, with different effects installed into each individual Pact, making it an unspeakably powerful Lambda. Perhaps a grove of sacred trees protects the village, or lidded pots can become traps for spirits.

                                The Demon-Blooded are more common in these days as well, especially since in a tight-knit community relationships are a little more expected. At a distance from the God-Machine, they can more freely communicate with their parents, and many grow up proud of their natural capabilities and what they can do. The most powerful of all the Demon-Blooded are the Nephilim, an unexpected consequence of the Unchained condition the God-Machine will later produce Infrastructure to rectify. When a Demon-Blooded is born, roll their parent’s Primum. If they are a Fractal, roll the lower of their parents’ Primum. If any successes result, they become a Nephil, with all the benefits and drawbacks that entails. This can stack with the traits of ordinary Demon-Blooded, so you can be a Nephil Offspring, or a Nephil Fractal. A Fractal who joins the ranks of the Nephilim is likely the closest thing to a natural-born demon that exists in the world, and in some ways they are something… more. Able to overclock Embeds and even determine when one of the Unchained is lying, capable of wielding the power of Exploits and transform into a Demonic Form of their own, a Nephil Fractal is an unholy terror, a demigod astride the earth.

                                The Cipher: For the Unchained, the Cipher is an answer, a puzzle, a mystery, a pilgrimage, and possibly a trap. For the Demon-Blooded, it is a chain they wrap around themselves. Unexpected result of the Fall or not, the Demon-Blooded are of the Machine, and their power stems from it. If the Unchained Cipher is something unexpected that draws them away from the Machine, then the Demon-Blooded Cipher is something unexpected that draws them to the Machine. The Nephilim possess unstable souls, the crackling core of Primum within them liable to shred their sense of identity. However, they can stem the effects of their Unstable soul… by choosing to spend a point of Willpower and suffer a Compromise instead. This functions as per Flowers of Hell, advancing their Cipher.

                                Some Unchained, early Pentagramists, are unhappy with a limit to their power, and some even see the Cipher as another limitation imposed by their creator. They force a fifth Key into their Cipher, breaking it, reforming it, potentially discovering true freedom and becoming utterly unchained. While the mysteries of the Cipher make the beliefs of Pentagramists mysterious and dubious at best, the Pentagram is the salvation of the Demon-Blooded. In forcing in a fifth Key, they shatter their Cipher, and in doing so shatter the God-Machine’s hold on them. Forcing the Pentagram immediately forces a Breaking Point with a -5 penalty; with all the risks it holds for the Unchained. For Offspring, the burden is relatively light- a single point of Aggravated damage and a permanent catastrophic glitch. For the Nephilim, it is far riskier, and potentially fatal in more ways than one as it tears at the flesh and the soul alike. But if they survive the procedure, they find themselves freed. The Activated Condition resolves, and they no longer suffer the modified Demon-Blooded form of Compromise, instead using the Unchained version (minus the loss of Cover).

                                A Pentagramist Demon-Blooded is a free agent in the Sundered World- unique, unspeakably powerful, and a possible origin for myths of demons, angels, gods, and demigods. They are truly the great men of old, heroes of renown.

                                                    I'm also liking the idea behind the Pentagram as well. Giving Demon-Blooded a potential out from the God-Machine's influence is a really interesting concept.
                                                    Looking back, I think making them a separate type of Demon-Blooded would probably be wiser, but I like the idea of Fractal Nephilim, if only due to the fact that they're basically flesh-and-blood, natural-born, Unchained. Tying in the Pentagram (which I can't take credit for, it's a hack from the DStG) to the Demon-Blooded just seemed natural to me. The belief that the Cipher is a controlling influence from the God-Machine is just conjecture for the Unchained, but it is reality for the Demon-Blooded, and I liked the idea that what is- possibly -a trap for one might be the salvation of the other. The Pentagram freeing the Demon-Blooded just feels... right, somehow. Like it's a satisfying ending to the story.

                                                    I also just loved the idea of the Pentagram for its extremely Gnostic attitude. Even if you're free of the God-Machine/Demiurge, even if you've expanded your Primum and completed your Cipher and truly anchored yourself in the world and established your Hell away from It, you still are of It, and the Cipher is still a relic of Its design. How does one become truly free then? In kabbalah, crossing the gap between the lower seven sephiroth and up to the Supernal Triad is referred to as "crossing the Abyss". When it's a qlippthotic, or Left-Hand process, "crossing the Abyss" is still a test, but the end result isn't union with the divine, but total separation from it and establishing yourself on top. Utter separation, utter freedom- the true definition of Hell, a place away from God.
