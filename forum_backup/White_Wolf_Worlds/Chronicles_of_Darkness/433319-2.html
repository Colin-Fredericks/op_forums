    Originally posted by Xenorath View Post
    It probably isn't a great idea to pin the Judeo-Christian God down as one of the Old Gods and a bunch of the prophets as Mages. Not only do you start getting into the whole "Jesus was a vampire or possibly Lucifer, who was also Buddha" that cWoD sometimes suffered from, but you also undermine a bunch of other splats, like the Malleus Maleficarum and possibly the Lancea et Sanctum. This is all before bringing the God-Machine or Principle into the equation either.

    This is a can of worms that should not be opened
    Originally posted by LostLight View Post

    about Disbelief- the number of people believing in the supernatural is not relevant for creating Paradoxes or using magic. The name itself is going to be changed at 2nd edition to avoid that confusion, if I'm not mistaken. This is the effect of the Abyss, and as long as that black void stands between the Supernal and the Fallen, magic may fail even if every single person upon Earth would believe that you can throw fireballs.

    Second, the bible doesn't forbid only Necromancers- but all kinds of magic workers. Many forms of Divination are included, and so are many other magical practices- no diviner, no enchanter, no witch, no charmer, no medium, no wizard, no necromancer, no cloud reader or astrologer. Going by Paths, then you have no Moros, no Acanthus, no Thyrsus, no Mastigus and maybe even no Obrimus. Unless you claim that the priests has decided to monopolize magic and worked to destroy competition, it would be weird to claim the Prophets as mages. Even if you decide they banned only Arcana, you won't have Time, Fate, Death, Spirit, Mind or Forces at the very least- which is harsh for a society of mages. Also, the prophets weren't the leaders of Israel- you can't count the number of times the Prophets raged that the people of Israel defied the will of God- but in the end, they didn't punished them or forced them to follow the rules. Those parts belonged to God and the Kings.

    In short, I think that while the idea could be interesting- it would require a lot of work so the Suspension of Disbelief would not be kicked out of the window- and that is without mentioning the screams about "stereotyping" and "bigotry" which may rise.
    Originally posted by ZealousChristian24 View Post
    Kind of agree with a lot of the others, though the idea of a secret Nameless Order within the priesthood of ancient Israel sounds like it could work.
    Originally posted by glamourweaver View Post

    That's not what I'm saying. I'm saying Priests and Prophets aren't Mages - because Mages wield agency over their power and Prophets do not. They just deliver messages from the force that wields said power and takes the supernatural actions on its own accord. Solomon is the only Biblical figure who traditions treat like a Mage. There may be others Awakened CLAIM, but it's going to be highly disputed.

    Also the alienated nature of the Awakened from society is kind of a fundamental theme in the game. They don't get institutionally elevated as a group.

    Any general presenting any real society as magically enlightened relative to others strikes me as patronizing and silly - not to mention touched with ethnocentrism. We elevate the Ancient Judeans because the dominant religion in our society claims them as forebearers. It's the same reason in any media portrayal of the Greco-Persian Wars in our society the Greeks are Good Guys and the Persians are Bad Guys.

    The Kohanim were a priestly bloodline, no different from any Indian Brahmin (which is to say, some individuals could be Awakened - sure, but it's not the nature of the group).

    I'm saying this as a Jew myself: Our ancient forebearers were not special in any way (outside of their relationship to YHWH in the context of our religion, but henotheists who believe themselves favored by their god are not unique or special in a global sense). Mage's cosmology is Gnostic - it in no way jives with the theology of Tanakh.
    Eh, I'm about as devout as anyone, but I can separate fiction from reality. Concerns about blasphemy don't bother me, since I'm proposing it all as a work of make-believe.

    Anyway, yeah, I was kinda looking at it in terms of ALL ancient societies being the product of some supernatural splat or another. I mean Egypt was the successor-state of Irem's Nameless Empire, which was the work of the Shan'iatu; Sumer was founded by sorcerer-kings who rivaled the Shan'iatu; my pitch has Assyria and Babylonia serving the God-Machine and the Principle, respectively; etc. So Israel doesn't become special except in the sense that they were specifically a nation founded by Mages rather than one founded by some other splat.

    The whole idea is that Abraham, Moses, et al, when they made their covenants with YHVH, got some magical mojo injected into the bloodline that allowed for a higher rate of Sleepwalkers and Awakened (originally this was to be nationwide, however the covenant was modified to restrict it to the Levites specifically). However, part of the setting would be explaining how Israelite Awakened are different from standard Mages - they are explicitly SERVANTS of YHVH, not the dilettantes of modern Awakened society.

    Keep in mind also that the Tanakh was probably compiled post-Exile and reflects a particular POV which may not be in line with the literal truth (speaking both in terms of the real world and the WoD perspectives.) We know that Israel was a bunch of stiffnecked, wayward children who frustrated YHVH to no end; the only reason He put up with them is because all the other nations were even worse. Any prophet whose preachings were unpopular was put to death and their writings were destroyed; and the Priesthood had a tendency to usurp political power instead of serving as they were supposed to.

    As to the prohibitions on divination, witchcraft, etc.: The holy men of Israel prophesied (Time,) cursed the enemies of the Lord (Fate,) discerned thoughts (Mind,) commanded beasts (Spirit,) and called down the power of the elements (Forces.) The difference between that and the prohibited practices? Well, if you're doing it according to the rules (by the power of and in service to YHVH), you're okey-dokey, otherwise (calling on foreign gods, communing with ghosts, going against YHVH's orders, etc.) you get stoned.

    Regardless, I think changing it to "A lot of Levites were Sleepwalkers/Awakened" rather than "All Levites" is a good idea.

                            Enough of me being negative, it's time I posted some of my ideas.

                            Setting: Germany, 1626–1631 AD
                            Game line: Vampire/Werewolf Crossover

                            Between 1626 and 1631, the Bamberg and Würzburg witch trials took place. This all went on at the same time and area as the 30 Years' War, which was one of the most devastating wars in the history of Europe. Now I know Hunter or the Possessed from Inferno are the obvious choice (or Giest if you want to focus on the war itself), but that is why I would rather see what depravity the Bale Hounds and Belial's Brood would get up to in a time like this. It would be interesting to see how these two similar groups would interact with each other. Outside of that, massive Wounds would be forming which would be a huge problem for werewolves and I don't think we've ever seen how vampires deal with such brutal wars. This war was heavily fueled by hostility between Lutherans and Catholics, so it would be interesting to see what effect this would have on the Lancea et Sanctum, which would have largely been made up of former Catholics.

                                                    Well in case you don't already have one, here's a list of pre-Colonial African kingdoms.

                                                    http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of...olonial_Africa

                                                    I'll skim over a few and see if anything jumps out and screams "World of Darkness plot hooks!"

                                                    Edit: Carthage circa the Second Punic Wars sounds pretty interesting. It's close enough to Egypt that you could get some Mummy action going, and of course the conflict with Rome lets you work the Camarilla in there. Numidia would also be in the same region at the same time.

                                                    Ancient Carthage - Wikipedia
                                                    http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthaginian_Empire

                                                    Numidia - Wikipedia
                                                    http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numidia


                                                    Oooh. Engage comparative religion/mythology geekery The high muckety-muck god of the Carthaginian pantheon was Baal-Hamon, which has obvious phonetic similarities to the Egyptian deity Amon and the angel Amoniel of Lancea et Sanctum mythology. Amon, of course, was assimilated with Zeus as Zeus-Ammon by the Greeks. And his consort was Tanit, possibly cognate with Egyptian tA-Newet, "tA" meaning "Earth" and Newet being Nut, the sky goddess, so a rough translation would be "Earth-Sky" or "Earth-Heaven", which has some interesting parallels in Sumerian mythology via the primordial couple Anshar and Kishar (Carthage was a Phoenician culture, which ties it to Mesopotamia). Or possibly TAw-Newet, "Breath of Nut/Breath of the Heavens," which has possible connections to Hebrew beliefs (Genesis 1:1-2 references the creation of the heavens and the earth and says that the "ruach", which means both "spirit" and "breath", of God, brooded on the face of the waters).
