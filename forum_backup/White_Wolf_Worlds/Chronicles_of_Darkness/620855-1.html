    Its terrifying when your home is invaded. Of having them know where your loved ones are, the things you hold dear. Don't fear for yourself. Fear for others.

    Be afraid of alerting the Hunter's police connections. Just because you can easily kill a hunter when you're one of the monsters doesn't mean the backlash won't be terrible.

    Hunters generally aren't afraid of going to extreme lengths to take on the supernatural. The supernatural, ironically, tend to have more restraint (save when we're talking about things like the Brood, Seers, and the Bale Hounds). In a game of chicken, the hunters will generally have more guts to stay the course.

        Depending on how well established the Hunters are, I think you draw upon the fact that the Hunters are likely to be well informed, well funded, well equipped and well organized.

        The PC's wont be fighting 5 random dudes with guns. They're being stalked by 5 trained killers, backed and funded by a world spanning organization of other trained killers. They know where you live, where you work, where you go to get drunk and where you shit. You're never safe. You can never let your guard down.
        Hunters are ruthless, relentless and resourceful. Use that. Have them burn the PC's houses, send them pictures of their loved ones homes, get them fired or put in jail on false accusations of rape or pedophillia, hack their bank accounts, rig their cars with bombs the list is infinite.
        The actual "they're gonna kill us"-part is just facet of the sustained and coordinated campaign of terror that a Hunter is likely to unleash to take down a powerful supernatural foe. Even when they do come for the kill, they're not gonna walk to you with a knife and slash away. They're gonna be sitting half a mile away, with sniper rifles and machine guns and you're in a wide open field, because you were lured there by the Hunters.

                Originally posted by Michael View Post

                That's true in a narrative sense, but I find it conflicts with the fact that this is a game of make-believe. The extreme behaviour of NPCs can very easily just drive players to their own extreme behaviour, and generally as you're still trying to keep a degree of plausibility in the world you've created, the players are going to be much worse that the NPCs.
                Honestly this is where the Morality/Integrity system comes in. Not to "punish" certain behavior mind you, but to bring out the effect of the social contract in game where player's might be inclined to disregard it because of their separation (being real rather than the fictional characters) from the consequences. A Hunter who is comfortable with easy murder should have fairly low Integrity and run the risk of scratching at Slasher impulses.

                        Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                        Shock and Awe: have a seemingly untouchable NPC get 1-shotted out of nowhere. That'll rattle your players pretty quick.
                        There is a fine line between making it scary, and making the players just feel frustrated and hopeless. The first is fine, the second is just going to ruin the game for the people at your table. Besides, if you one shot a NPC, and then cannot do the same with the characters in the same situation, you tend to harm their suspension of disbelief. At the same time, no player is going to like to be killed by a rock from the sky either. Unless you have a pretty good reason for why that one shot kill worked, and why it only worked once (and the characters learn the reasons), there is in my experience a pretty big chance such tactics backfire.

                            Tactics. Corollary to the 'go after the money' from earlier, most hunters are fully aware that they're not going to win a toe-to-toe without serious hardware. So they do things differently. One thing I'm fond of is 'poisoning the food.' Vampires, particularly well-established ones, don't think too much about feeding. If a vampire goes to feed and gets his brains splattered across an alleyway, then suddenly the rules have changed. My favorite setting I ever did for a Vampire game was a Ventrue prince who used the Masquerade as a McCarthy-style bludgeon -- anything bad happens in the city? Must be a breach, sanctions for everybody. If a hunter kills a neonate somewhere and the entire metropolitan infrastructure reacts, that's a pretty good indication some serious shit went down.

                            Also on the matter of tactics, 'divide and conquer' is a good one. If the PCs' allies start coming after them, murder in their eyes, the PCs are like as not curious as to why. Let them investigate, and discover that the hunters have been working on a frame job, and PCs' allies were suckered. That does a really good job of establishing what kind of juice hunters have.

                                I don't know if this was mentioned already, but there's a chapter dedicated to hunters-as-enemies in the Antagonists splatbook for 1e; in addition to the advice here, I highly recommend checking it out.

                                Something to remember is that, since your group are playing supernats, the best way to play up hunters as antagonists is to avoid the "whitewashing" they get given in their own gameline. Hunters who actually hunt are desperate, vicious, and half-deranged. These are the hunters who will send the PC threatening notes and incriminating pictures. They will torture and murder the PCs friends, family and lovers for information or just to terrorize the PCs.

                                Ultimately, being stalked by hunters should feel like being stalked by a serial killer, or living out a slasher movie. Because, from a supernat's perspective, that's what hunters are.

                                Use traps, ambush, headgames, hit and run, try to drive them into a killzone or whittle them down in order to make them vulnerable to a final attack. If one player (or a cannon fodder NPC) falls, emphasize the brutal violence of their death, especially at the hands of the hunters proper. If your players are willing to play along with this, that should really help drive the horror home.

                                That said, don't be afraid to embrace it if the PCs lash out. The fear response is called fight or flight for a reason, and supernats have a lot of options to fight. If possible, show them how dangerous just blindly fighting is - but, if they win, then use that to drive home that they are still monsters. Describe the visceral slaughter left in their wake, have dying hunters plead and babble apologies to their loved ones... let them feel ashamed at how readily they have reduced living, breathing beings to so much cooling meat.

                                Then slap them with the realisation: what do we do now? Gotta get rid of the bodies, or doctor them, or else the law will come after them.

                                But, ultimately, be prepared for players to not play along. Even in a horror game, many players don't like like to feel helpless, particularly when it's something that they "know" they can deal with. There's a fine line between horror and nihilistic frustration; push some players too far, and they'll deliberately lash out, ruining your planned scene because they would rather your NPCs burn with them rather than lose.

                                    There's some good conversations here, and I would add that it might be worth thinking about expected vs. unexpected outcomes. For example, if the Hunters understand what the monsters fear, is that part of their trap? Say their plan, all along, is to frighten the monsters so badly they bear claws/fangs. Why? Is it to study them? Is it because they'd been hunting them for months? Is it to trap or doublecross (e.g. Draw in a second monster type to have them kill each other.) them? Right now, it sounds to me like the expected outcome is a big battle. I'm suggesting that it doesn't have to be that simple, and that might give you other options to introduce Hunters as an antagonist. Too, there are monsters that throw their lot in with Hunters on occasion to fight monsters. This could draw some connections between the PCs and the NPCs in an interesting way. So to sum up: I'd think about motivation, because depending upon why the Hunters are going after your PCs, that has the ability to make your scenario pop.

                                        My proposal... First contact has the players and hunters BOTH on the SAME SIDE.

                                        And during that point, you show them do things like have a willingness to do acts that bother your players. Maybe they call in an airstrike against a sludge monster and are armed to the teeth... or are flatout inhuman in their actions, such as playing the zealotry and conviction to terrifying levels. Let them have harsh treatment of the first bad guy's cultists, let them cut down those involved but unaware. And show them how dedicated and willing they are to throw themselves into the line of fire to achieve their goals. They're a different sort of inhuman then.

                                        If it works, they'll hesitate fighting them. And if you go far enough, be scary.
