    08-16-2016, 02:49 AM
    Unnecessarily antagonistic opening statement. If you don't want to be treated with hostility then don't start by being hostile in your opening line. The group attack against the Promethean forum is not OK. You should avoid using red text as well. Darksider

    Right, now that's out of the way, here's some stuff I've been thinking about:

    ​There is a force in the universe, an energy, that defies normal definition. It is a force of Creation, Dynamism, and Change. Under certain circumstances, this force will respond to those who are Devout, Obsessed, or otherwise fixated. This force can be channeled into a dead body, or the likeness of a person, imbuing it with new life. Beings thusly animated, and the ones who make them, are... changed by the process. Sometimes physically, sometime mentally, sometimes spiritually. A little energy stays behind in those who channel it. and becomes an integral part of those who are animated by it. They feel compelled to seek answers about this force, and the Supernatural in general.

    That's what I'm willing to start with. It's late and I'm tired. What kind of game would YOU have made, based on the same set of inspirations that Onyx Path used?

      I don't really have something to add here in terms of original ideas, but there are a few potential bits of inspiration I can point out.

      First, there's Kindred of the East. It was an Old World of Darkness take on playing as eastern vampires. Simply put, you died and a part of your soul got sent to Hell while your body was still fresh. However, Hell isn't exactly the best guarded place, and your lower soul, aka the P'o, managed to return to the Hun, the higher soul, that remained in your body. You come back to life, can feed from the Chi of other people. Perhaps the only problem is how the Kuei-Jinn (what they're known as) literally require Dharmas (kinda like Humanity) to function. However, a Dharma can be completely different from Humanity, the only similarity that remains with Promethean is that most Kuei-Jinn feel the need for an ascension through the Dharma. For example, following the Devil Tigers is a Dharma based on enlightenment through pain and violence and you supposedly ascend to demonhood at the end.

      The next were a more fansplatted idea that was never followed up on the Rpg.net forums that might be liked. The idea of playing as a Skeleton or a deceased being. The ideas present seem to suggest that the need for living pleasures was desired, as well as and almost need to hold up a facade of humanity, but without the Promethean aspects of "must need humanity at the end"

      https://forum.rpg.net/showthread.php...6#post11040286

      Through this link, posts 49-58, then abit more on 72 and 74.

      Now that I think of it to throw out one last bit of inspiration, there was an oWoD fangame called "Zombie: the Coil". So far, these ideas reminded me abit on how there isn't a nWoD gameline for zombies and this could potentially give some inspiration for your ideas.

      http://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.ph.../ZombieTheCoil

      Hopefully this stuff helps.

                  Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                  Some of us dig the Alchemical, transformative, back-from-the-dead, and artificial intelligence themes, and even the alienation to some extent, but just don't like the "humanity or death" part.
                  While I have issues with the phrasing there, I'm going to go beyond that to a much more vital question:

                  If you cut out the central driving force behind what compels these beings to do stuff, you need to replace it with something equally vital. So what do you (and your players) want the driving theme of the game to be?

                  "My existence is a mystery and I'm magically compelled to explore it," doesn't really work on two levels. First, it's fairly flat. Any newly created supernatural being wants to explore their new nature, but it lacks direction. Mage works because it's driven by obsession where you keep devouring new and more complex mysteries. Beast works because exploring your Horror grows your Legend, and helps you make your place in the world what you want it to be. There's clearly a, "what next," in those games after you get past the opening mystery of, "what am I and how does this work?" Second, it's skimpy on inherent dramatic conflict. Life doesn't have to "suck" but it needs to have conflict to be interesting as a RPG. Why should the characters do anything challenging or interesting to play out? They don't have some end goal to push towards any more, and any situation that seems like too much trouble can just be skipped for easier and safe investigations into the world around them. If the Dynamic Force that powers them magically compels them to get involved with dangerous stuff so they learn more, that just gets right back into a different form of "being a Alchemical being sucks."

                  What kind of game would YOU have made, based on the same set of inspirations that Onyx Path used?
                  Probably the same thing, but with more dials included in the core book instead of saving them for supplements; though I admit that the WoD 20th books have spoiled me a bit by having massive page counts to allow them to cover more modes of play in one book. I'm not arguing the premise of the thread (you did ask this question as well), just that I think that PtC is easy enough to expand without the massive changes that you feel it does.

                      Mangle77, just to be clear, are you looking for alternatives to the Pilgrimage itself, alternative ends to it or just new ideas on Promethean in general?

                      For the first, i'm not sure I have a great deal to offer, other than an idea about perhaps the Promethean needing the help of his creator in some way or there being rites or processes which a Promethean can use to dampen the effects of his Disquiet and Wasteland to a degree. For the second, here's some:
                      • Qashmal A Promethean who comes to understand the true nature of Pyros or Flux might be able to harness that power within himself, transmute his flesh to fire and become one of the Qashmallim, whether as an Elpidos or Lilithim. If you intend to keep playing this character after doing this, i would run it so that he is (mostly) free from the control of the Principle, although it might send messages, directives or missions.
                      • Metamorphosis With an understanding of Pyros and another form of supernatural power, a Promethean might become able to transform himself, at least partially, into another kind of supernatural being, perhaps re-making its Azothic soul into something more reminiscent of a strange ghost with the knowledge of Plasm or damping its fire with the dark power of Vitae. I'm not sure whether this would be able to actually swap templates fully (as in, turning the first one into a Sin-Eater and the second into a vampire), whether it would make them into a minor template (abmortal and ghoul, respectively).
                      • Control Perhaps a Promethean could, through intense self-disciple and control, gain the ability to completely suppress his Azoth, rendering him effectively human for a limited time while not suffering the effects of Disquiet and the Wasteland.
                      • Apotheosis The Promethean makes his Azoth burn both bright enough to work his Transmutations and sustain himself and dim enough to spare the land and souls around him. He perfects the furnace within himself, becoming able to perfectly regulate his own power, an immortal, Pyros-powered physical god, capable of working the most powerful transmutations with ease. The price for this state, though, is that the Principle takes a 'personal' interest in their doings and they are often plagued by Qashmallim with messages and missions. Even worse, the Principle's opposite number may take notice, and take measures to contain the threat it its Infrastructure... (This end would probably be as rare more more so than a mage's Archmastery, and would probably come with comparable power).
