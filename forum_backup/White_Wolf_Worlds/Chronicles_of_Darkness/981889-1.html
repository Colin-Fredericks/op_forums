        I kind of feel her pain. The magic system in Awakening remains my gold standard. I've never seen anything quite get the mix of creative thaumaturgy and hundreds of example spells exactly right, and every time I try to conjure one from scratch I can't escape its influence. I've been tinkering with game systems for decades, and no game seems quite so perfect for hacking, modifying, and repurposing to meet my current gaming goals. It's just so...comprehensive.

        I had fun toying with Buffy and Ascension, BESM and FATE look pretty good on paper, and I'll always have a bit of a nostalgia for D&D (2nd Ed and earlier), but I keep finding myself returning to Awakening's combination of endless variation and (mostly) clear guidelines for building new spells. I'd rather take that engine and build a completely new car around it than shop around for a dream setting only to find that their mechanics either actively discourage creativity or abandon me in a sea of GM fiat with nary a hand wave.

        But yeah, the system is pretty intimately tied to the setting, and if you want to make major changes the latter, you're going to have make some changes to the former. And you might come up with some clever and cool mechanical solutions to the problems your setting changes create, but some of those problems aren't easy to solve, and not every mechanical patch actually works the way you intended. It can indeed feel a bit like how an Obsession must feel to a mage.

                        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                        *sigh*

                        You might want to review those two posts for anything that might come across as pre-emptively defensive, or attacking styles of play or preferences that disagree with your own. If you can't see the problematic parts of these two posts without being told what they are, I don't have high hopes for this thread going any better than the last one.

                        This. One of the most glaring changes in tone between the the oWod Mage first edition and revised was the way they tried to make it about 'street level games' by making the horizon realms far less playable. The person who introduced me to Mage said that it ruined revised for her and she stuck with 1st edition. Learn to embody a 'yes, and' mentality, and if you want to run a game a certain way get agreements from the players on how the game will run rather than force it mechanically. For example if you don't want to run a 'Pink Mohawk' Shadowrun game, spell that out before hand rather than saying "Lone Star will come down on violent criminals with excessive force." That way all the players and the GM will be on the same page as each other, that way if one player has different desires or expectations you can work with them and try and come up with a way to satisfy all parties.
