                                The Lancea (et) Sanctum believe that vampires have a divine mission from God to be the wolves to his shepherd. They are supposed to prey upon humanity in order to keep them in line and drive them into the arms of the church. They believe their founder to be the biblical Longinus, the Roman soldier who pierced Christ's side with a spear. The LS believe that while doing this he accidentally ingested some holy blood straight from the source, and became a vampire as a result.

                                Now, the Ordo Dracul have the basic mission statement of "transcend the vampiric condition". Note that this is not the same as becoming human again. They want to keep all the perks but get rid of all the weaknesses that come with them. Their primary approach to this goal is to conduct experiments of questionable ethics.

                                The Circle of the Crone believe that the above two are doing it wrong, and that the best way to be a vampire is to just be a vampire. To revel in the power and the blood without trying to cleave to human values or tinker with the equipment.

                                    Okay, I have one big request now: could you guys give me a short summary of the beliefs of Lancea Sanctum, Ordo Dracul and Circle of the Crone? The 1E rulebook is kind of frustrating in that matter, as it's very vague. It's mentioned that CotC are about acceptance and redemption, but it's not explained what does it mean exactly. Overall, I have no idea how to portray these groups in-game...
                                    The typical Lancea Sanctum had a hierarchy and practices that emulate or mock the Roman Catholic Church. Their general premise is that you did something in life for God to damn you to existence as an undead blood drinking monster. You might be able to return to his grace or gain some kind of reward after Judgement Day if your predation on humanity turns them or keeps them on a path to God or convince and convert other vampires in the righteousness of their cause. Theban Sorcery is a gift granted to them from God to assist them in ensuring vampires think twice about mocking their calling and assist them in their divinely mandated role. How devout or impious the members are towards that agenda and the rituals varies from setting to setting.

                                    The Circle of the Crone emulate human pagan blood cults that primarily (but not exclusively) serve a female deity, and typically elevate vampires to a religious status as the embodiment of their preferred deity or at least servants who share a higher level of communion with said goddess than humans could never reach. They don't have any uniform hierarchy or set of beliefs, as they tend to be more of a (perverted) interpretation of the primary interests of whoever they worship. Cruac is generally considered a blood magic gifted to servants of the local chapter's deity to provide insight and power to their practitioners.

                                    The Ordo Dracul studies various facets of the vampiric condition and have developed a series of pseudo-scientific experiments, exercises, or meditation techniques to alter their own body sufficiently to make those vampiric powers function more efficiently permanently, called Coils of the Dragon. They have a byzantine hierarchy that revolves around the mentor student relationship in conjunction with the number of Coils they have proven to master with the penultimate objective of being able to completely transcend the vampiric condition into something else. How these changes are accomplished is not as important so long as they can be replicated by someone else.

                                        Gladly!

                                        The Invictus' philosophy centers around protecting the Masquerade. In 2e, one of their nicknames is "the conspiracy of silence", because they infiltrate human power structures and use their influence to keep the herd under control. They also fancy themselves the spiritual successors to the Camarilla, and believe that gives them the right to rule over the dead as well, though in truth their reach is far less ubiquitous than they would have you believe.

                                        The Lancea Sanctum, or Lancea et Sanctum if you want to use proper Latin grammar, are the largest monotheistic Kindred religion. At its most fundamental, their dogma revolves around the belief that the Kindred condition is a curse from God. All vampires are inherently and irredeemably Damned, but that doesn't mean they don't have a place in God's plan. As God's chosen predators, they take it upon themselves to cull the flock.

                                        The Circle of the Crone began as an association of various Poly-theistic vampires that banded together against religious persecution by the Lancea et Sanctum. They welcome vampires of any faith, so long as they uphold the organization's central ideal of religious freedom.

                                        The Carthians are to the Invictus as the Circle of the Crone are to the Lancea et Sanctum. A coalition of political dissidents united under the common ideal of political reform for Kindred. Anarchists, fascists, socialists, libertarians, communists, capitalists, you name it, all banded together because they can at least all agree that the current system doesn't work, even if they can't agree on a better alternative.

                                        The Ordo Dracul follow the ideals of Dracula, who believed, as the Lancea et Sanctum believe, that he was Cursed by God. But rather than humbly accepting his role or seeking forgiveness, he sought to spit in the face of the God that Damned him. He sought not only to rid himelf of the Curse, but to add insult to injury, turning its weaknesses into advantage. Some modern Dragons share Dracula's belief that their condition is a Curse from God, others take a more scientific view, perhaps seeing it as a disease or mutation, and some are even agnostic about the nature of the condition. But all share the goal of rising above it and becoming something greater.

                                                If a newly Embraced vampire decides the best thing to do is walk into a police station and turn himself in (which I would consider pretty unlikely, but let's roll with it), if he's lucky he's going to be held in a holding cell and thought a complete whackjob until word gets up the chain to those in the know and he's released into the company of a blood-bound lawyer and shuffled in front of the Prince. Not the most prestigious beginning for a character, but nothing a little Dominate can't fix.

                                                If he's unlucky then something happens that forces the issue, like the sun coming up forcing him to sleep (or worse, burning him alive) or he attacks cops in a Frenzy, or a million other ways that can go wrong. Then even if he somehow escapes the police station, he's going to be pursued by the cops AND the local court for Masquerade breach, and his days are numbered.

                                                Also, one thing to keep in mind about executions in Vampire: there are probably plenty of places in the world where private executions are carried out via staking and giving the torpid Kindred to the elders who can only feed on Kindred blood. Who fears a blood bond to a truly dead man?

                                                    If you want a covenant who's least cruel, you'd probably need to go with the Carthians. They, at the very least, recognize that they need to adapt to modern human society in order to survive in the world. The Lancea Sanctum view themselves as substitute Satans and that it is their divine mission to terrorize and punish sinners. In the Bible, the being known as Satan actually works for God and is specifically tasked with testing people's faith, punishing the sinful and strengthening the faithful. However as humanity became more and more abundant, Satan found his job to become increasingly difficult until he could no longer satisfy it on his own. The Lancea Sanctum picks up the slack, performing Satan's duties while on earth. They are Christian zealots who believe medieval justice is perfectly acceptable if it is carried out in the name of God.


                                                    To answer one of your questions, yes, they do prey on sinners. They even like to embrace them sometimes as punishment. They are extremely protective of the pious and virtuous, however.

                                                            Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                                                            To clarify something: which covenant is less cruel to humans?
                                                            They're all mixed bags, and it can vary from domain to domain. All the covenants have more sympathetic contingents and more monstrous corners.

                                                            The Invictus is probably the most moderate by this metric. Their nicer guys aren't as nice, but their nastier guys aren't as nasty. They're a very pragmatic lot, with an ideology arguably closest to the human perspective: they like stability and temporal power. Fuck you if you get in their way, but if you don't, they're usually content to leave you be. Made men, middle managers, power brokers and peacemakers. Ultimately they just want everything flowing smoothly and predictably so they can comfortably skim off the top.

                                                            The Lancea et Sanctum are a modern religion. They have all the positives and all the drawbacks that implies. They believe in a just God and the sanctity of the righteous. Milder Sanctified might interpret this as a motive to protect innocent and upright mortals from the predations of the likes of the Invictus. More severe Sanctified view it as a ticket to visit horrors upon those they view inferior, and to put the devout through hell as a sort of "test" to weed out those whose faith isn't strong enough. The rank and file attendees of Midnight Mass lean upon the Spear as a comfort to themselves that their unlife drinking the blood of the living isn't so bad as they manage survival night by night.

                                                            The Carthian Movement are extreme. From a mortal perspective, that can be good or it can be very bad. They believe in mortal ideologies applied to undead life, and that can encompass anything from democracy and rule by consent, to anti-coercive libertarianism, to socialist cooperation, to a fascist vanguard. When they're busy fighting the other covenants for influence over domain policy, they're a moderating force, at least as applies to mortals: Kindred society comes first, mortal affairs secondary. (Carthians are known for not being afraid of collateral damage when attacking other vampires' power bases, though.) When they take power and the other covenants consent to their ideas, they dive straight into the banality of evil, setting up systems and engines of industry to serve the people (vampires) with what they need to thrive and prosper (the blood of the living).

                                                            The Circle of the Crone are a wild card. They're insular, and they value power, wisdom and willpower. They also value worshippers, who can probably expect to be treated the most kindly of any of the covenant's human servitors – keep in mind that's relatively speaking, now. They're iconoclasts, and other iconoclasts can usually expect to receive at least a mild nod of respect and be passed over. The rest of the herd, those who don't demonstrate power, wisdom or willpower in sufficient degrees to defend themselves? The acolytes don't hesitate to shed their blood in honor of their own glory.

                                                            The Ordo Dracul is a small cult of psychonauts and experimenters, really only held together by one constant: they hate the Curse. Some of them hate it because they loved life, and want to climb back to something resembling it. Some of them simply chafe at any kind of restraint. The nicest saints of the Kindred are probably split between the Dragons and the Sanctified. The devils are here too. Members of the Order are secretive and practical, so they're unlikely to prey on humans in large numbers in most cases, but they're liars without restraint. A fiend of the Sanctified or the Acolytes will tear a human apart with a feeling of triumphant glory. A fiend of the Dragons will tear a human apart and feel nothing at all except mild curiosity. Which is worse?
