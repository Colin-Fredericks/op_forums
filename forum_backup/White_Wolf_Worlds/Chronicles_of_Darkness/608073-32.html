                        Originally posted by Hexmeister Vic View Post
                        Clearly, we aren't using the same definitions of gothic.
                        Probably not. Of your examples only Stoker really fits as an example of Gothic literature. Alien is always going to be 50% science fiction and many of the key aspects of Gothic literarure (like romance) are completely absent from it so it favors horror more strongly than terror.

                        I see were you're trying to invoke "Gothic" as a literary genre but I don't agree that it needs to be the centerpiece genre of a game like Hunter, which IMO has a great deal of survivalism in its DNA. "The monsters are everywhere. And your characters can fight back."

                        If you want something more specifically Gothic then you have to go back to Hunter's roots -- the Chronicles of Darkness core game. Games like Vampire and Promethean have more Gothic DNA in them (as they lend themselves to the romance aspect necessary for true Gothic literature) than games like Werewolf and Mage which mix in other themes (like crime drama in the case of Werewolf and urban fantasy in the case of Mage). And really in the final analysis, if all the games were all Gothic, all the time, they'd all be the same game, which makes for a dull experience.

                        Final thought, the inspirational material sections of these games is always a good tell of what literary genres and themes the game is going to mix together. In the case of Hunter, I'd expect more Van Helsing (the movie) or Helsing (the anime) than Stoker's Dracula.
