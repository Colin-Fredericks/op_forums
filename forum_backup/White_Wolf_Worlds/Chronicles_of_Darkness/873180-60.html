                    Some prior information first, I've been familiar with the Chronicles of Darkness books for a while although for one reason or another, I've never been able to play in a game. I started with Geist, then went to Changeling because the concepts in both looked interesting to me and when I saw that 2nd ed was coming out, Beast appealed to me most. I love the lore and the idea of both playing an actual monster where the details are more or less left up to the player and being able to infect people with nightmares sounded very interesting.

                    Where I'm getting hung up, though, is the Lair and, specifically, the Chambers.

                    So, let's say I'm playing a literal Gorgon. The Heart of my Lair is a cave adorned with statues fitting what my Horror is. Over the course of play, I add a Chamber using an abandoned hospital and then later on, I'm able to add another Chamber based on an old, 19th century factory. The Burrows between them are simple. The cave is the basement of the Hospital, stairs leading down into natural, rocky corridors and there's a road connecting the hospital to the factory. As a Medusa-esque figure, my Namtaru glides easily from nightmare-scape to nightmare-scape, hunting the unwary in her home. From what I understand, the real-world places that those Chambers are based on heavily influence the place within the Lair that they exemplify, so while my Medusa may alter the hospital so that it, too, has statues of doctors or patients, maybe being dark or maze-like in it's construction, it still has to look like a hospital and same goes for the factory.

                    Now, let's say that instead of a Namtaru, I want to play an Anakim. Perhaps a 20-foot titan of moss and stone with a mouth colored brown from it's ground-up victims. It's Heart is a dense, rocky forest, but otherwise, over the course of play, I still accumulate the aforementioned hospital and factory. There's still the road, but maybe the hospital is set deeper into the forest, closer to the Heart with a path leading into the woods. However, the Anakim is 20 feet tall. How does this giant navigate the insides of the hospital? How does he even get into the factory?

                    Lastly, what about a Makara? Some huge leviathan the size of the hospital or the factory itself. How does that thing stalk the hospital's corridors or the rickety machinery of the factory? In addition to it's size, the massive whale-like creature needs an ocean to swim in.

                    So, my ultimate question is this. How do I reconcile a Horror that doesn't seem to suit the area a Chamber is in with the Chamber itself? Does it actually have to be connected thematically to the real-world location? If not, how much can I alter it?

                    I'm sorry if this is too long winded. I didn't want to create another thread to busy the forum. I'm also sorry that this is so long-winded. I feel like the core question is simple, but maybe I went overboard, here.

                    Any help would be much appreciated, though!

                            (Also Lair Traits infect chambers, so going with a Makara whom say, has the trait for dimly lit and flooded, so long as that hospital is dimly lit while you claim the place, Flooding gives you oceans to swim in.)
                            See, I don't think that's a very satisfying answer in this case. For the giant, it'd definitely work, but for the leviathan, a new problem is caused.

                            For example, saying that the size of the Horror depends on the location makes a huge difference when the size of the monster is supposed to be part of what's so frightening about it. The leviathan being small enough to fit changes part of what makes it scary.

                            On the other hand, saying, "Yes, the monster is the size of the hospital and yes, it's inside the hospital with you." Is all fine and good dream-logic-wise, but while a bit of that logic can help reinforce the strange reality of a situation (a door on one side of a bent hallway opening to the start of said hallway for an endless room), if you set up a situation that can't even be realistically imagined (what does the leviathan occupying a space far too small for it to fit in even look like), then it doesn't really help describe what's going on.

                            I mean, obviously, it works better for the giant. Realistically, even if it stooped, it wouldn't fit into a twelve foot tall corridor, but when that thing's chasing you, you're not super worried about the approximation that is the four or so feet in size that'd make a difference. It's not the same for the building-sized leviathan, the house-sized dragon or the truck-sized eagle (especially when that last one predicates it's fear on the target being exposed, not in small, maze-like hallways).

                                                            Originally posted by Op3999 View Post
                                                            Dreamborn and Mother's Land? I've been reviewing my book trying to find answers, but those terms aren't super familiar to me. Could you give me some page numbers?
                                                            "Dreamborn" is Beast's name for what Mage calls "goetia" — natives of the Astral Realms. The Mother's Land is Beast's name for what Mage calls the Anima Mundi — the soul of the wider world, which the collective human soul is divided from/connected to by an edifice of stone and fog and language that exists in the depths of the Primordial Dream.

                                                            The Mother's Land is where you find the souls of deserts and forests and oceans, and also where the Dreamborn that claim an empty Chamber to become Horrors come from. Things there feel more real than anywhere else, and its residents often possess a goodly measure of actual influence over the physical world. The thing is, the protean fear-goetia that take shape as they pass through human legend and language are Rank 2 — roughly equivalent to the average sentient ghost, conversational spirit, or low-level angel.

                                                            They develop their starting Atavisms and their first dot of Lair as the seed of starting characters' magical aspects in a place where size is often correlated with power and significance, and — despite theories to the contrary — generally die with their human halves unless they are freed by Inheritance. Most Horrors' size is "just" a function of their fearsomeness, particularly at the lower end of the scale; being Actually Bigger comes at the opportunity cost of being stronger, defter, or more resilient.

                                                            Maybe it's just poor word choice on the part of the writers, but that law allows for real creatures like the Blue Whale, the biggest animal to have ever lived.
                                                            It is an incredible stretch to go from "as semi-imaginary creatures giant Horrors don't have to worry about the material/biological constraints of a massive form" to "a thing whose powers include 'scary because it's big' can conceivably have actual trouble moving through a place that is part of it and responds to its needs," particularly when "orders of magnitude bigger than a human" does not actually contradict "can fit into a building that they're allowed to damage."

                                                            Plus, that "effective" size increase affects real health dots and with satiety expenditure, speed as well. It doesn't seem that mutable by Dream Logic if it's reinforced in such a way.
                                                            That "effective" Size does not affect Dream Health, is the thing. You're dealing with a psychic construct that draws its power from the soul of the world — that suggests more viability for nightmare-logic movement options, not less.
