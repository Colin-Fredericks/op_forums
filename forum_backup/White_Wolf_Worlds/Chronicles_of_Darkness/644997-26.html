        Originally posted by The Lone Gunman View Post
        So, quick question, and I apologize if this has been addressed elsewhere: If you're using a spell that's withstood by stamina on a vampire, and that vampire has Resilience (which adds directly to stamina), would the combined stamina+resilience value be used for the spell, or is it the attribute alone that's counted?
        Yes, absolutely. Resilience modifies the vampire's actual Stamina trait; it'd be the same case as if you had buffed a target's Stamina with a Life spell, only the Resilience is always active. Further, they can spend Vitae to increase their Withstand rating, unless Dave or Rose think otherwise. I say that because of this:

        Originally posted by Vampire: The Requiem Second Edition, p. 91
        With Vitae, any Kindred can accomplish massive bursts of physical prowess. By spending a point of Vitae, he may add two dice to rolls involving his choice of Strength, Dexterity, or Stamina for the turn. This does not increase traits derived from these Attributes. However, it will increase relevant resistances.
        The reason I bring it up at all is that you could read the bolded bit as being equivalent to spending Willpower to boost a Resistance trait. I know that because I specifically brought it up during development; by 1E rules-as-written, spending Vitae to strengthen you in, say, a grapple, would only work when you were actively rolling—it was useless when resisting. Well, that doesn't make any sense. So I know that the passage I highlighted specifically calls out that you can boost resistance traits with Vitae. Can you do it against magic? Well, it doesn't say you can't, and vampires use hostile magic on one another all the time, so....

        I believe the rules as intended would allow vampires to boost their Withstand ratings by spending Willpower and Vitae. The Kindred can be remarkably resilient, which fits well enough to me. Of course, groups are encouraged to discuss how they want to handle this in their own games.

                Vamps also get +Blood potency to resistances while in frenzy, which should hopefully count. I do like the awesomeness of the power here for mages - that each Master has an obliteration spell at the ready - and the only barrier to using it is *politeness* - it brings the horror of the setting to the fore. Why not kill your wife's lover? Heck, int' she guilty too? Aint like nobody will miss someone who *never existed*. Almost like you never killed anyone at all...

                However, I also want an occasional shift at horror in the other direction; not just "look what I have done" but also "shitohshitohshitthere's-a-fucking-monster-coming-for-me." I want there to be some threats that aren't put-downable with a single roll. It sounds like creatures from the abyss and lower depths may provide this (as will any more convuluted mystery). Having a vampire in frenzy crashing through the barroom and resisting your first couple of spells as she flings herself at you will keep the edge of threat in the narrative present as well as in the cosmic backdrop.

                    Originally posted by The Lone Gunman View Post
                    So, quick question, and I apologize if this has been addressed elsewhere: If you're using a spell that's withstood by stamina on a vampire, and that vampire has Resilience (which adds directly to stamina), would the combined stamina+resilience value be used for the spell, or is it the attribute alone that's counted?
                    Taking your Stamina off Withstood spell's Potency is not a derived trait, but a function of Stamina - resiliance is written the way it is so that vampires don't add it to their Health dots.

                    So yes, vampires would add it to Stamina for withstanding stamina-withstood spells. Similar with mummies burning Pillars, werewolves changing form, mages buffing Attributes, etc. Given that you don't get to add a Power Stat on, it's only fair - and supernatural means of boosting your attributes become very, very important when fighting mages.

                    Problem is, can you boost them all? And can the mage come up with a way to kill you that ignores what you're good at to hit your weakness?
