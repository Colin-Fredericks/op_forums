                    But back to Genius...

                    I'm seeing Obligation or its replacement as a measure indicating how "together" a Genius has it. High, and they are basically functional, Low and they are the stereotypical Mad Scientist or Evil Genius. High, and they are confident in public perception, Low and they know they are rejected and they scramble for recognition. Illuminated, and they just don't care anymore (provided their mindset even includes the notion of outside sentient actors).

                                        Obligation 2.0 (until a new name is settled) serves as the basis of how well a Genius interacts with humanity. High, although eccentric they are respected or at least heard out. Low, they are a pariah and stereotypical evil genius to the nth degree. Transgressions now extend to what end the Genius will disturb previous perceptions of themselves. The quirky if strange old man refitting a DeLorean in his garage is not the guy who would kill people for their body parts. Although he is willing to burn down his house for insurance money and trick terrorists into giving him plutonium, events his [Obligation 2.0] rating may not survive.

                                        Alongside Obligation 2.0 is a set of inverted boons and penalties. Geniuses high on the scale are Paragons (like the Science Hero merit and Paragon terms from 1.1e) better able to manage their reputation and trust, respect or believability. What they say makes sense. However, their Wonders are at a disadvantage as Havoc is all the more forthcoming as Inspired and actual science clash. On the flip side those low on Obligation 2.0 can have their Wonders bettered at the cost of distance from humanity, Jabir plaguing their interactions and performance around the non-Inspired.

                                        I like Vent0's alternate way of looking at it, as Obligation 2.0 is fundamentally how the Genius perceives their connection to society around them. A Genius stuck at one end of the scale could be surprised for how others' perspective changes from their own, like the Paragon finding horrified reactions from something that was 'perfectly safe' or the MAD scientist finding begrudging respect from someone he didn't think he had helped.

                                        As for bonuses and penalties, it would look much like having two flipped sets of Kenning that Changelings have from Clarity.

                                                I don't have any ideas for Obligation 2.0 at the moment, but I do like the sound of Ever Professional's Association.

                                                However, I'd like to share some ideas to make Geniuses more powerful.

                                                1) Spending Mania to boost Mental Attributes grants +2 per point. The same goes for the Grants of the Directors and Navigators.

                                                2) By spending a point of Mania when they get 3 Successes on a Mental roll, they may turn it into an Exceptional Success. They may do this a number of times per scene equal to Inspiration.

                                                3) Add Inspiration to all Build Equipment rolls, including Plans. This stacks with number 2.

                                                4) By spending 1 Mania, you may decrease the normal time taken by a Mental extended action into singular turns. This includes Meditation, creating Plans and Investigations. The cost must be paid every turn.

                                                5) Do not bind Mania to create Wonders. Instead, Binding Mania unlocks additional features once you have surpassed the minimal Axiom requirements for a given Wonder. It also adds dice to Clashes Of Wills involving the Wonders. Certain high Inspiration Merits may allow the Binding of Mania to add extra functions even to 5 dot Wonders, and dice tricks to Clashes Of Wills involving the Wonders.

                                                6) Contesting a power meant to turn a Genius away from their Magnum Opus receives the Rote Quality.

                                                7) The extra Mental Attribute dot given at character creation may take that Attribute above the normal limits imposed by Inspiration.

                                                8) Geniuses now have access to a trio of aura mechanics similar to those of vampires, but meant to represent Inspiration as a force of change, progress and transgression. Here is the draft:

                                                Roll a Mental Attribute + Inspiration. This may not benefit from Mania expenditure. Each Attribute causes a different Condition (see the spoiler below) and represents a certain archetypal aspect of the Genius.

                                                Action: I am unsure. Vampires use simple actions that take up a turn at most, but these might take multiple turns.

                                                Intelligence: The Professor. It invokes the Genius as a figure of wisdom and guidance, or authority and dominance. Success on the roll inflicts the Instructed Condition.

                                                Wits: The Visionary. It invokes the Genius as someone filled with intuition and big plans, or a methodical and analytical machine. Success on the roll inflicts the Shared Vision Condition.

                                                Resolve: The Adventurer. It invokes the Genius as the brave soul who fears no danger. They can risk themselves for the greater good of humanity, or use their gifts for personal gain at the expense of others. Success on the roll inflicts the Condition.

                                                Instructed
                                                Your character has been taught to act in a certain way by an expert or authority, or at least someone who acts the part. They find that following this advice feels natural. When following the instructions to the letter, they gain +2 and an Exceptional Success on 3 Successes. Attempts to turn them away from this course of action suffer a -2 penalty and lose 10 Again. This Condition fades after a number of days equal to the Inspiration of the Genius who inflicted. After resolving Instructed, your character cannot be subject to it again for a full month.

                                                Resolution: Follow the instructions to the end. Make considerable effort to openly challenge them.

                                                Shared Vision
                                                Your skills have been carefully inspected to make you an efficient part of a plan, and you are properly motivated to see it carried out. All revelant rolls you make as a secondary actor in a Teamwork action receive the Rote Quality. If you are the primary actor, all rolls which benefit from the bonus dice given from secondary actors are considered an Exceptional Success with 3 Succcesses. This Condition fades after a number of days equal to the Inspiration of the Genius who inflicted. After resolving Instructed, your character cannot be subject to it again for a full month.

                                                Resolution: Carry out the plan to the end, significantly change its outcome with personal alterations, or sabotage it for your own gain.

                                                Impressed
                                                You have been awed by the achievements and stories of a larger than life figure, and you find it difficult to get in their way or do them harm. All rolls meant to do so suffer a -2 penalty and lose 10 Again, including supernatural powers. Their Impression level with you is one step higher than it would normally be, up to a maximum of Excellent. Their rolls in Social Maneuvering against you gain Exceptional Successes with 3 Successes. This Condition fades after a number of days equal to the Inspiration of the Genius who inflicted. After resolving Instructed, your character cannot be subject to it again for a full month.

                                                Resolution: Ask to join them in an adventure, challenge them to a competition.

                                                Bye.

                                                        There's one aspect of Genius I wanted to discuss: Funding. Being a genius is expensive since they rely completely on technology to do their thing. I like how the game acknwoledges this, but as some people have pointed out in here a player shouldn't be worrying about balancing numbers and businesses. As such I have come with a system that I hope will balance both aspects. Please, feel free to share your opinions and suggestions on it.

                                                        -Funding.

                                                        In game terms, funding refers exclusively to the characteristics and origin of the resources (Money, personel, tools, materials, buildings, etc...) that the genius needs to create and maintain the infrastructure that allows him to build wonders. Each type of funding will have advantages and disadvantages that the player will have to deal with at the discretion of the storyteller.
                                                        In game mechanics Funding is a way for the storyteller to create drama and adventures for the player character by introducing complications. It's not intended to be a hassle or another thing to worry about. It's just another way to add fun to the game. The storyteller, with the help of the player, is the one that decides when the complicatons enter the game and always with the intention to enrich the adventure.
                                                        During character creation a player character must chose between one of the following fundings, or come up with a new one with the approval of the storyteller. (These are just some examples. If you have another one, or think one shouldn't be here, please feel free to say it)

                                                        - Trust fund.

                                                        Someone in your family or close to you was very rich, liked you a lot, and recently died. As a result, you have inherited a vast amount of wealth that you're using to fund your research and wonders. However, there's an obvious catch: it's limited. Sooner or later your funds will run out, and many young geniuses underestimate just how expensive the live of a genius can get. This type of funding is great for players who don't want to worry about funding, at least in the begining. As the campaign advances, and depending on the character's actions, the storyteller can tell the player their funding is running low and they need to find an alternative source of income or risk running out of cash at a very inconvenient moment.

                                                        -Freelancer.

                                                        The genius offers his skills and inventions in order to fund itself. The genius is already a stablished freelancer with a list of regular clients. It's important to specify what type of client and jobs they take since it will affect their reputation and the kind of clients and jobs they will get in the future. This type of funding offers the most independence. The big catch is that, even with regular clients, it's a very unstable source of income. Also, taking into account how chaotic the life of a genius can get (and let's be honest, their creations are not famous for being very safe or stable) planning becomes nightmare, with projects often delayed or destroyed, and there's the risk of angry clients coming back for the genius.

                                                        - Business.

                                                        You own a business that provides you with a stable source of funding. The player has to specify what type of business it is. It's large and succesful enough that it runs itself, so the player doesn't need to worry about management. The main advantage is that it allows the genius to be independent and gives access to the resources of the business. However, apart from the regular problems that may assault any businesses, the genius has to deal with the simple fact that his or her business is ill prepared to deal with what enemy geniuses may throw at it, which makes it a very vulnerable point of attack. Here's an example, batman and iron man are used to battle supervillains, but the people that work at wayne enterprises and stark tech are not. A genius must be prepared to defend their business.

                                                        -Organization.

                                                        A big and powerful organization is the one providing the funding. The player has to specify which one (Government, corporation, etc...). Working for an organization has the advantage of not having to worry about anything except the research and building of wonders, the organization provides anything they may need. The main catch is that the genius has to justify their expenses, answer to the organizations demands and help it reach their goals, whatever those may be, or face the wrath of their superiors. In conclusion, it's a secure source of income and security in exchange for independence.

                                                        Well, that's it for now. If you have more ideas feel free to share them.
