      First - check Path and Order combination. In 2E you can get Legacy from other sources, but it's more difficult. Then look for Arcana Attainments - in RAW of corebook you get Ruling Arcanum of Attainments as Third Ruling to character, but popular is House Rule to get even one from Optional powers if Legacy's Ruling is your Paths Ruling. So check with your ST what is permitted in game. Other than that? Pick something cool and working with your PCs idea.

                  So, there are a lot of legacies and there are a number of them that use Death, Matter or Life in different combinations with each other. It's also possible to join a legacy without knowing the arcana for the Optional attainments and developing that arcana for the optional attainments later.

                  You also qualify for Tamers of Rivers both main and optional arcana for instance, they're Matter and Life, a legacy of healers. Think Waterbenders really. That's just one example.

                  Whether or not a recruiting table exists kind of depends on the legacy and it's methods of initiation. Some legacies advertise and some make you figure out they exist on your own before considering you. Researching most legacies should be pretty standard library research work for most Mages for surface level info at least.

                              I'm of the opinion that you should honestly have a Legacy in mind when you start a character and seed their symbols with ones appropriate for whatever Legacy you want them to join, because otherwise it'll be very difficult when the time comes. Truthfully, as a friend pointed out to me, Legacies are so personal anyway, the idea of Mages joining one rather than all simply founding their own is a bit of a curious feature of the setting.

                              That said, it is possible to work backwards; to design a Legacy for your character and then have the ST introduce it as something that already exists; having them stumble upon a mentor or a daimonikon or what have you.

                              Also, take a hint from Mrmdubois here too: A Legacy represents a metaphor for how a Mage approaches the Supernal but the Awakened have this habit of making metaphors very literal. Consider a method by which your character resolves issues or how they view something, and then translate that into an action.

                              If you wanted to carry on with the Navajo theme, why not have them join the Yee Nahgloshii or the Four Foot Lodge? A Legacy of skinwalkers traditionally considered Left-Handed because so many of their attainments exist to sicken or harm. Perhaps the Four Foot Lodge really is just a bunch of bastard-coated bastards, but perhaps they're also trying to do good; acting as a threat and impediment to those who sin. It's more of a Guardian idea than an Arrow one, but it still works.

                              Just making that up off the top of my head, but it's an obvious path to go down.

                                  Upon reading your first post I assumed your character would want to find a way to resurrect his sister by emulating his mythological namesake. My mind went to a trickster Legacy, possibly one that utilizes either Ghosts or laws of the Dead with the goal of confronting an avatar of Death and conning it to get his sister back. Less gloriously, he could raise his sister with an Indefinite Death 5 spell, and patch her up with a stolen Soul, but that doesn't require a Legacy.

                                  For the record, Legacies in the 2nd Corebook are just a fraction of all written Legacies. If you want a wider selection you can check the thread below. Not only are the Legacies listed, each gets a summary and a commentary. It's reasonable to assume Mages have access to lists just like this one, in-character. You can ask your GM for a session where your character learns of an interesting Legacy and travels abroad to meet its adherents:
                                  I read a Legacy a day

                                  Consider that, even though you choose between a preexisting Legacy and making your own, wiggle room exists. You, as a player, can design a Legacy and your Game-master can make it so that it existed in the setting all along. Not only that, your character can modify an existing Legacy to his liking by developing different Attainments. It can be done in-character, no less. The book doesn't say anything about changing the optional Arcanum to a different one, but nothing should break if you do. Just ask your GM for approval.

                                  As far as re-skinning goes, consider the following Legacies:
                                  - Katsinam Suukya
                                  - Scions of God
                                  - Keepers of the Covenant
                                  The former two develop themselves after a spiritual patron, and the Coyote is a valid one. All of them interact with Spirits. You could modify any of these to interact with Ghosts and Death-Gods instead. Those are just as an example.
