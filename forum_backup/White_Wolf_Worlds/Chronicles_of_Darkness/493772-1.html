    well I think it's unreasonably unfair to calculate the base usage of vampire vitae as 1/night. After all, any wound requires vitae, a lot of disciplines require blood too. And also, someone in these forums have said this, and I've kinda stolen it to use in my game, but it's really a great point on this topic. It basically comes down to this: with the vampire longevity, most of them see a war, or two. Or famine, or disease or something equally widespread. That's when the vampire population must be able to survive without breaching masquerade. Thinking that such a time won't come is naive at best (history tells us that almost all centuries had a war... and elders know this. That's why they don't want overpopulation, because they know that once catastrophes roll around back into town, they'll need to survive in those harder conditions and preserve the masquerade.

        I use the 1:50k as a benchmark. A very rough benchmark. As others have said, population density is more important than the raw numbers, and be sure to keep in mind transient population. A college town with 60k permanent residents can actually support quite a few more vampires comfortably when the three universities in town are cracking along.

        As an example, the Chicago metropolitan area is about 10 million people, giving you a rough vampire population of 200. In my Chicago game I have around 80 vampires in some level of solid existence. That accounts for characters who are only very rough sketches of a one or two sentence blurb and a name, to staple NPCs the players interact with regularly. That gives me a ton of wiggle room to introduce new characters, seed stories, and have faces in crowds for a dramatic Elysium, all while remaining within the bounds of suspension of disbelief.

        Yeah, dropping 5 vampires into a rural town of 15,000 is not going to work in most circumstances. You can still have those kinds of situations, you just need to take a few minutes to think about, "Okay, how do these yahoos feed themselves without blowing their cover?"

        I like Griautis' point above, as well. Bear in mind that while everything is plugging along with no problems you can get away with packing more vampires into a given population. When something happens like a war, natural disaster, whatever, you're going to have a huge problem on your hands. That would be an interesting situation to run through. It's like avoiding making a budget because you have plenty of money, and no problems paying off whatever you need. One breakdown where your car needs major work and you may be screwed. See kids? Always make a blood budget!

            Using Boston as an example New England city, you've only got about 650k population in Boston itself to work with, but when you take the surrounding area of suburbs into account that number jumps to 7.6 million. That's a very workable number. Remember that you can have vampires living in relatively close proximity as long as feeding is more regulated or at least careful. Maybe in your city feeding grounds are ruthlessly patrolled, and Kindred citizens of the domain are only cleared to feed in certain areas, ensuring that the drain on the blood supply is sufficiently spread out.

            Herd poaching, vessel smuggling, feeding ground claim-jumpers, Prince-edict smackdowns on the folks who do those things, these are things that make vampire go 'round.

            As for other supernaturals, I honestly don't worry about the strict population numbers so much when I'm running a vampire game. I keep it vague as far as the players are concerned, and I only bother with a very rough sketch of what is where for myself. If you really need hard numbers to keep things tidy, I don't think there's too much overlap in supernatural population as far as any kind of supers-to-mortal ratio. None of the other supers are competing with the vampires for blood (territory and such is another concern, but lets stick with the main issue of feeding without shredding the Masquerade) so the ratio isn't impacted by mages, or changelings.

            That said, I love having other supernaturals present in my games, no matter the game I'm running. The focus stays on whatever that game is, of course, but I love the cosmopolitan nature of the shadows in the WoD, and I think it's important that the characters realize on occasion that they're fish in a very big, very dark pond.

                It depends if you want your players to feel like a conspiracy of supernatural folk or like isolated monsters. Dwindling the vampire population lets you focus on the more monstrous aspects of your player's characters, whereas a larger vampire population makes the story more political / conspiring.

                That's what I find, anyway.

                                    I like the idea of Vampire populations being small. It really gets across the point of claustrophobia (think Sartre's "No Exit", hell is a dozen other Kindred for eternity), the desire to create more vampires for companionship (because all the other suckers suck), and the problem of having made enemies with one of the 5 elders in the City. It's like working at a really small company, with a boss that hates you, and co-workers who want to see you fail. You want to quit, but being unemployed won't work for you, you have a serious health condition (needing to drink blood) and you can barely afford it even with the 'health insurance' it provides.

                                    Add to this the fact that Vampires in a small City/less densely populated region are essentially boxed into their domains makes things even worse. Over the centuries you come to know every street corner and alley. You know all the best hunting spots..... and that's it. That's your world from now until you meet your demise sometime between tonight and eternity.
