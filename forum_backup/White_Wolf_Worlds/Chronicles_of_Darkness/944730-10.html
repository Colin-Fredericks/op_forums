                                    Originally posted by falco1029 View Post
                                    With 2nd edition, well, I can't see that working anymore, I admit. They no longer have just better utility; their powers are now superior at similar levels too (if they're willing to risk paradox), to the point of completely overriding other splats in the same field. In 1e, a Mage needed 4 dots of Mind to do what a Vampire could do with Dominate 2, but now they have a 2 dot spell that's far better than Dominate is (again, if enough Reach is spent), for instance, in addition to the half dozen other spells they get at that level (plus CT and attainments). Paradox is more of a risk now, but not enough to override the complete advantage they have in both breadth and depth.
                                    I don't think that's true. Mind spells are fairly weak until your character is significantly advanced. Psychic Domination is, I assume, the spell you're talking about. It is Withstood by Resolve, has to last for sufficient duration for the action to be completed and has a primary factor of Potency. A starting mage with Gnosis 1, Mind 3, has one spell control slot, so having someone do anything that takes a while is going to eat it, has a base four dice and may have to spend 2-4 of them to even make the spell have an effect depending on the target's resolve, has to spend at least a reach for making the duration scene long if the action can't be completed in a few seconds, and may need to spend even more dice if a longer duration is required, and has to contend with paradox on those tiny dice pools if they just want to make the spell instant cast and sensory range. Iron Edict is a much surer much more powerful ability for a starting character. Once you've hit someone with the Mesmerized condition they're pretty much yours. The mage is constantly running risks to duplicate the base effect, and success is far from guaranteed.

                                    The thing with mage spells in 2e is that because effects are placed in the appropriate practice, lower level Arcana dots *seem* really strong, but it's because even their lower dot powers scale with the mage getting stronger, unlike the other splats, not because they're really powerful the second you get them. The system has balancers that keep power level in check.

                                    Mages are also slow to cast things now because of this; having to incorporate Yantras so that you have enough dice to boost spell factors to an effective enough level means the werewolves or celerity/vigor vampires are able to contribute far more relative combat effectiveness than a simple glimpse at Mage's spell list would suggest. They also get far fewer useful innate abilities; a vampire reflexively boosting strength or dex can cause a lot of damage with a mundane attack, and you're probably dead if they successfully get you in a grapple and start feeding, a werewolf can shrug off massive amounts of damage reflexively, etc. It's very easy to create situations where the mage is completely outclassed and functions best by providing support.

                                    2e Mages aren't broken power level wise. They're maybe broken information gathering wise. They'll break PC groups where they're allowed to set the tempo because they're absurdly good at getting the upper hand with preparation. But that's an issue with the ST losing control of pacing, or failing to move the spotlight around so that individual character strengths and weaknesses are properly highlighted.

                                    That being said, mixed groups are headaches no matter what splat you're talking about. There are ways to work around any issue, but I've had situations where having a single Vampire in a mixed game caused me more logistical issues than almost anything else simply because of the sleeping during the day thing. You can run into difficult to deal with limitations caused by any splat in a mixed group. So much so that I'm generally of the opinion that they shouldn't be attempted unless the ST and the group are very good at improvisation, there's a really strong social contract, and they have a lot of system mastery, or if the game is intended to be ridiculous and rules light. They are messy and difficult to pull off well.

                                        Eh, I disagree with your assessment there.

                                        With Gnosis 1 + Mind 2 (we'll go even lower than assumed), you have 3 dice, but to be honest, it's fairly easy to get +2 from some yantra or another on that, giving you a base of 5. Unlike a Vampire, you don't need to worry about the opposing dice pool possibly being lucky, you just need to account for the opponent's resolve. By default, with Mind 2, that does mean you probably need a -2 penalty to deal with an average opponent (mundane or otherwise), putting you back at 3, though you can spend willpower to bring it to 6, where it's no longer a concern. If you're dealing with someone above average, sure, you'll need to up it some more, maybe leaving you with 4 or even 2 dice (but still at least a 50% chance). This does mean when you first get it, people do have a shot of resisting (or rather, you have a chance of failing, if you overcompensate and thus dont have enough dice afterward).

                                        Even if the chosen yantra takes you an extra turn, from using, say, high speech, that's still combat-time, which as I recall dominate 2 still has trouble performing during. So, the whole 'time taken' thing still gives the Mage the advantage, and if we're assuming similar use circumstances, you aren't caring about combat time anyway as you have conversational time to work with, so can more easily incorporate yantras, and for that matter, can probably recast the spell a few times if it's withstood or the roll fails.

                                        As for paradox, just contain it and you dont need to deal with penalties to the casting. Might have a deleterious condition and some bashing damage, depending on how much 'dox you accumulate, but even assuming you need the reach for all of casting time, duration and range, and for the fun of it, we'll say you're causing suicide (Which Dominate can't even do) for +2 more, that's 4 dice for the gnosis 1 mage, which means their Wisdom of 7 probably can handle the containing roll. And if they want to be more careful, they can probably rely on an aimed spell instead and depending on the end goal, maybe even use basic duration.

                                        This of course is all assuming you have NONE of the following: A praxis (likely able to beat the Withstand just by exceptional-ing and ignoring it, plus you'll have less paradox to deal with), a rote (mudra bonus AND you have 3 more reach, meaning your paradox pool is now only 1 die), or the sympathetic range attainment (if you can cast sympathetically, 90% of non-mage opponents are f'd as you can take all the time you need to add in yantras and work ritually for less paradox risk). Any of those means even that starting Mage with 2 dots of Mind will have a much larger chance of performing much more competently (admittedly the space 2 scenario isn't always available, if dealing with a threat you weren't expecting).

                                        So, yes, that Gnosis 1 Mind 2 Mage does have some difficulties, but overall, there's a varied number of ways they'd still be able to make easy use of that powerful spell, just not as easily as an adept would. Plus, there's the rest of Mind 2 that they get with that same dot, including a mental shield to prevent the vampire from dominating them to start with (yes, I know, we dont have to assume it's a direct conflict, but still).

                                        As for innate abilities, Mages have attainments, and that means Mage Sight and Mage Armor, as well as a Utility attainment or 2, no matter what. Mage armor is very useful in a lot of cases, mage sight you admittedly already mentioned, and the utility attainments vary, but definitely provide heavy advantages (one possibility, again, being sympathetic casting).

                                        So, overall, while I'm not saying a starter mage doesn't need to account for things a bit better than an adept or master, I still think they have the advantage over a similar 'level' of opponent.
