                            Special April Fool's Day Entry:

                            Cows From Space

                            <<Pathetic human farmer. In time your meat will serve the People as protein, and you shall labor to till our fields. The cud of the world is in our mouth, and the udders of wrath shall drown you in the tears of our mothers and our righteous vengeance! >>

                            Soviet Russia was not a country known for scientific ethics. Enough of a lack of ethics they stopped actually making many scientific advancements (to use a historically dubious but characteristic example, telling the guy who wants to create a race of human/chimpanzee hybrids off is just as much a matter of common sense as it is morality). But ethics do get in the way of good (in the sense of actual potential for scientific gain, not in the sense of not causing queasiness among people with consciences) ideas, sometimes. In October 1958, a strange reading appeared in the sky above the Ural Mountains, not far from where, for no apparent reason, nine otherwise experiences hikers vanished, leaving only heavily damaged equipment behind. Not trusting this to be a coincidence, the Soviets immediately assigned a clandestine project to watch the phenomenon, including at least one psychic of the notorious Iron Comrade project. Upon said arrival, said psychic, an unassuming Lithuanian rancher by trade named Emilis Januitis immediately reported feeling emanations from the softly glowing "comet". describing it as "the vellum scent of profound age, but also the heartbeat of an unborn fetus" (Emilis really wanted to be a poet). Long story short, when the anomaly remained there, the Soviets realized their meticulous observations were going nowhere, and so, with characteristic pragmatism, they decided to poke it with a very high-tech (at the time) stick and see what happened.

                            Of course, if you don't have something on your stick that can be affected by the thing being poked, you can't tell exactly what said poke-ee is doing. So, to test for effects on living organic matter, the Soviets put one of Emilis' cattle on the rocket they were using, along with several varieties of inorganic matter-lead, iron, sand, gold, and an oxygen tank. The experiment proved to be a success, but not in any way the Russians wanted- as soon as the rocket touched the comet-like anomaly, both vanished utterly-along with all of the pictures of the comet. The team lurked around Ural for a few months after that, but eventually other dead-end projects called, and the team dispersed.

                            The cow regards this as one of the first times she saw how despicable a species humanity was. What she can recall, anyway-her brain was not meant for remembering faces at that point. Or words. Or really anything apart from where the good grass was, but she's pretty sure she saw Emilis laughing in her face...she thinks.

                            The anomaly, as it turns out, was a passing angel of the God-Machine, there to examine the strange energies unleashed in the Ural incident, and was so lost in its work that is didn't notice the rocket coming at it until it hit the angel in the olfactory detector. Surprised, the angel instinctively teleported back to its lunar Infrastructure, taking the rocket with it. While the angel was recalled on the basis of discovery, the Machine's sensors didn't detect the cow, which was thrown into the storage Infrastructure and the temporal stasis field meant entirely for inorganic matter-rather than sending her into an unmoving stasis, it vastly accelerated her personal timeline, 30 years in an hour-but she was lucky. Her biology proved compatible with Aether, and within her body, new biology formed-an infinitely better set of organelles for repairing the damage caused by age among them. Her brain grew exponentially more complex, developing an enlarged frontal lobe. Nerves throughout her body grew into tangles of unique pseudo-organs capable of manipulating space. Her growing intellect grew fat on the streams of information within the Machine's heart-and she grew wroth with rage. Over the course of, from the Earth's perspective, next week, she studied the God-Machine's intelligence on the species she blamed for sealing her in space for thousands of years, eventually mastering a hack by which she tricked the Machine's Infrastructure into cloning her, with enough genetic modification to be considered unrelated members of the same species. To these fetuses of the newly dubbed Bos supremus, she taught her knowledge of the world that slaughtered their dumb cousins for meat, and stole the milk meant for their calves. Finally, she used her growing knowledge of spatial relations to open portals all over the world-and all over the world. ranchers shrugged at the sudden addition of new heifers and quietly added them to their expected profit margins, unknowing of the cold and alien intellects behind their unexpected fortune.

                            The Age of the Cow From Space has arrived, and soon, all the world shall bow before their bovoid supremacy!

                            Any day now.

                            Despite them not putting any plan into action

                            For the past half-a-century...

                            Okay, let's face it; hyper-intelligent, psychic cattle with omnivorous diets are still, well, domestic cattle. They aren't exactly the most aggressive species on the face of the planet, with even undocked bulls only charging when you provoke them. Even the Supreme Oxen Empress herself is mostly motivated over anger as what she perceives as a personal attack-now that Emilis is dead (from falling out of a hole in the sky), she's lost a great deal of her rage. For the most part, Cows From Space prefer the quiet life, chewing cud and dreaming of the inevitable victory when they finally put their plans into motion. But provocation is something that creeps up on humans very easily, and they are psychic, omniverous, and hyper-intelligent. How many meat packing plant employees have died for seemingly completely normal reasons recently?

                            ​(will continue, have other obligations)

                                Cow From Space:

                                Attributes:

                                Mental: Intelligence 8, Wits 2, Resolve 7

                                Physical: Strength 4, Dexterity 3, Stamina 5

                                Social: Presence 3, Manipulation 4, Composure 1

                                Skills:

                                Mental: Academics 3 (Agriculture), Crafts 2 (Slave Oversight), Investigation 4, Occult 1, Science 5 (Local-Scale Doomsday Devices)

                                Physical: Athletics 3, Brawl 2 (Have Skull, Will Use), Stealth 2 (Perfectly Normal Cow), Survival 2

                                Social: Animal Ken 1 (Barnyard Animals), Intimidation 3, Subterfuge 2

                                Merits: Common Sense ***, Eidetic Memory **, Patient *, Thief of Fate ***

                                Potency: 4

                                Willpower: 8

                                Virtue: Ambitious

                                Vice: Lazy

                                Aspirations: Take over the world (someday), avenge cruel treatment, have a nice, sedate life.

                                Speed: 12

                                Health: 10

                                Initiative: 4

                                Defense: 5

                                Bans: Cows from Space are blowhards, and are psychologically incapable of not boasting to an apparently helpless or seemingly allied human. Roll a d10 and divide by 2, rounding up-the number is the amount of pertinent facts the Cow unknowingly reveals. In addition, they will not actually attempt to kill someone except as part of their "avenge cruel treatment" Aspiration.

                                Dread Powers:

                                Emotional Aura (Numen), Influence (Spatial Anomalies *****), Know Soul. While Cows from Space are telepathic and capable of understanding all languages of people they've used Know Soul on, it their aggressive telepathic abilities are covered by Emotional Aura-they can't actively dominate someone through sheer psychic force.

                                    Storytelling Hints: First-give up on having an entirely serious mood if you are using Bos supremus as figures in a story. Any attempt to make intelligent cattle seem terrifying is going to be undercut by the fact they are, indeed, cattle. If your troupe happens to have anything resembling a sense of humor, all trying to sell the Cows from Space as legitimately horrific is going to make the actual concept seem more and more like a farce. That's all fine and dandy if that's the purpose, but really, you don't invoke cattle in dramatic moments and expect people to not acknowledge they are, well, cattle.

                                    Still, this does tie into the overall theme of a story featuring Cows from Space, that of hiding in plain sight. The very fact that the idea of cryptid, megalomaniacal barnyard animals is so utterly ridiculous nobody takes it seriously out of universe means that nobody in the CofD takes it seriously either-even before the evolved bovines start bombarding the players' potential allies with complacent feelings, proving that Bessie is in fact an evil genius with a talent for weapons design is an uphill battle, to say the least. Add in the fact that Bos supremus is a species born capable of playing with time-space in ways that even Mastigos wizards at the peak of their powers find jaw-dropping, it's quite likely that the instant the Cows get wind of their imminent exposure, they'll teleport to the other side of the world and leave normal bovines behind, leaving their would-be revealers to look insane, stupid, or most likely, both. Assuming they don't use said spatial manipulations to interfere without ever tipping their hooves-it's (not particularly) surprising what you can do with gravity and the ability to make heavy objects suddenly several feet high, directly over an investigator's car full of evidence. On the other side of the story equation, the Cows are the very embodiment of stupid smart people-they have a lot of raw brainpower, but they're horrible at guessing at what a human is going to do next. Know Soul is no substitute for emotional intelligence, and thus they are repeatedly flabbergasted by humans behaving in ways not directly driven by Aspiration, Virtue, or Vice-daily life, in other words.

                                    Still, there is a mood that lends itself to a more somber undertone when dealing with Bos supremus; seemingly harmless things having terrible implications. On the one hand, this applies to the evolved bovoids themselves, supervillains that look like completely normal meat stock, but it also applies to their origin story; who in their right minds would say putting a living cow on a rocket, like many other early experiments into reaching space, would result in such creatures? It also figures into what snaps them out of their instinctive complacency-as implied by the more pacifistic side of their Ban, Cows from Space are generally shaken out of their complacent instincts by someone behaving viciously. When it comes to farms and cattle, that generally means the excesses of the meat and dairy industry. While Cows from Space accept the fact that their dumb cousins are meant for protein, and sadly accept that (they don't eat beef for the most part, but it's more for the same squeamishnes humans don't eat chimp meat than any spiritual sense of kinship-they don't really object), they despise animal cruelty, especially the kind that incidentally targets the evolved bovoids. Thing is though, most animal cruelty caused by industrial farms is caused by obliviousness rather than malice; products of money cutbacks, inattentiveness, and just plain laziness. This does not excuse behaviors like leaving animals to stew in their own filth for months simply because the staff cleaners didn't feel like it, but it does emphasize the fact that a simple act coming more from lack of effort results in a great deal of misery for a bunch of innocent livestock-and not-so-innocent farmers, if the Cows from Space happen to be grazing there at the time. Who knows how many disasters that struck meatpacking towns are as much the product of revenge as ill luck?

                                        New day, new cycle of votes. This theme is "body horror":

                                        Darklings: Not to be confused with the Lost Seeming of the same name, and in fact they're probably the opposite of those Darklings. Rather than being a rebel against alien slavers whose guilt over her actions has caused her to absorb something of the fretful, frightening dark, Darklings are the still human servants of an alien dark-and most of the time, they don't know. The "darksome", it seems, recognizes a cult of organ thieves who willingly signed up and are fully aware of it are probably not subtle enough for its purposes. So instead, it finds people of particular social groupings (which depends on brood to brood-the darksome isn't choosy) with a hidden darkness in their past. Through that darkness, the darksome reaches and stains the souls of the humans that are members of it, creating a nocturnal split personality in all of them with three goals: Serve the darksome, serve their Vices, and steal internal organs to implant in gardens to farm (yes, they replace the organs with shadowy prosthetics, but still). This isn't due to the darksome's grotesque sense of aesthetics; Like any crop, the viscera gardens are meant for feeding, and in time, the power sent to the darklings' shadowy master will allow it to swallow them, and anyone nearby.

                                        Fabricants: Battlestar Galactica was right-there are machines clothed in human flesh about, and they hate us. Of course, they were wrong about their origins-fabricants weren't created by men, they are men-or were. Perhaps it's a side-effect of existing outside the normal flow of the fourth dimension, perhaps it's the Abyss, perhaps AM exists in the future and wants at least somebody who understands him, but occasionally, someone organic will become "unstuck" in time, usually due to a disastrous attempt at time travel. Over the course of a few months their internal organs turn to circuitry, silicon, and metal, and their minds turn towards disgust with anything built from hydrocarbons (to the point where they are always looking for opportunities to take off their disgusting "skin" disguise and let it all hang out). Some just turn to hermitage and tech support over the phone, others decide Skynet isn't being invented fast enough and start methodical extermination of anything that harms a poor, innocent machine. Most dangerous of all are those who retain some empathy, still care about some people and animals-they're the ones who "liberate" their organic companions into fabricant existence, and from there form cabals of vicious, technically gifted cyborgs.

                                        The Flayed: People tend to underappreciate skin. It's an understandable error-it's there from the day you were born, and unless there's a whole in it, you get used to it. The Flayed appreciate skin all too well-they're constantly in danger of losing all of it. Victims of a supernaturally mutated form of leprosy, the Flayed lose a few square inches every hour, on the hour, it literally peeling off and falling off almost painlessly. Almost, because eventually, some skin that's keeping things like "muscle," "guts,", "your heart" in is going to fall off, and the Flayed dies a horrible death. Thankfully for them, there's a way to treat it so the disease does not progress for a few blessed hours, a day, or even grow some skin back. Unfortunately for the planet, that's "drive people insane with fear"-the only thing that sates the Flayed disease, it seems, is trauma, and given how Flayed become utterly fearless in a short while, that means anyone nearby should probably pay for PTSD treatment in advance. The disease is merciful in one way, though-it gives the Flayed exactly the powers they need to do that...


                                        Special vote: I also own GURPS Fantasy II: Madlands. If people want to, I can convert a monster or two (whether they be Footless, Soulless, or Heightless) from there at the end of this cycle.
