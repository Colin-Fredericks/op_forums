    Training Wheels: Order/Path Aspirations

    09-29-2016, 04:16 AM
    One of my persistent difficulties with the Chronicles of Darkness - as well as games with similar mechanics, like some of the *World games - is the centrality of Aspirations/Obsessions or Aspiration-like mechanics to experience-earning. I like the idea, but in practice, I feel like players often struggle to come up with ones that they're happy with, and I think this problem is magnified when they're new to the system or setting. They ask, quite reasonably, how they're supposed to know what their character wants when they don't know enough about the world or the people in it to make that call. And I think Mage is probably the most daunting CoD setting of them all, even for people who are familiar with the system from other contexts.

    This is, of course, magnified by the eternal truth that players don't always read the book.

    "What does my character think about the Abyss? Fuck, I don't know. It's bad? Maybe?"
    Okay, Player, I'm nothing that down. Now, what do you think she might think about the Astral Realms? Interested? DIsinterested? Obsessed?
    "The Astral what?"
    ...we'll skip that for now. Do you think she might want to study an Exarchic Verge?
    "'Exarchic.' Is that even a word? How many more of these things do I need to learn about?"
    Oh, only about thirty-five or forty. It's not too bad.
    "Fuck you, Jacob. I can't believe you talked us into this."

    I also think that the flavor of the Paths, and the goals of the Orders, are kind of esoteric at first, relating as they do to mages' view on magic rather than mages' view of the world, but we ask people to choose them at chargen.

    So! For my current game, where half of the players are new to Mage and half of them are new to the Chronicles of Darkness entirely, and one has never roleplayed before, I sent out this list of "Persistent Aspirations" and "Persistent Obsessions" that award Beats and Arcane Beats respectively. I tried to make them flavorful, but broad enough to be usable. I leave it up to people here to decide if I succeeded at all. I'm also curious about other solutions people have devised both for GMing Aspiration-like mechanics in general and for helping ease their players into the setting.

    RECURRING REWARDS
    A player character may earn one Order bonus Beat per session by fulfilling any of the listed conditions for their Order.

    Adamantine Arrow
    - Act to defend someone other than yourself from a threat.
    - Oppose an adversary of at least equal skill in a contest, fight, or duel.
    - Learn an enemy's weakness or a new tactic.

    Free Council
    - Act in defiance of the commands of an undemocratic hierarchy.
    - Oppose the Seers of the Throne or their agents and pawns.
    - Learn a significant secret about Sleeper culture (the identity of an anonymous artist, the origin of a new trend, the location of the last copy of a film, etc).

    Guardians of the Veil
    - Act to prevent another mage from risking Hubris.
    - Oppose any action that might lead to Paradox or Quiescence.
    - Learn a significant secret about another mage's sins against Wisdom.

    Mysterium
    - Act in defense of any knowledge threatened by Pancryptia.
    - Oppose the sharing of knowledge with those who are not ready for it.
    - Learn a significant secret about the Awakened world (the location of a lost Hallow, the nature of a spirit's Bane, etc).

    Silver Ladder
    - Act to uphold the Lex Magica.
    - Oppose an instance of the Exarchs' tyranny: war, greed, slavery, surveillance, etc.
    - Learn a significant secret about what a potential ally desires most - or what they least want exposed.

    A player character may earn one Path bonus Arcane Beat per session by fulfilling either of the listed conditions for their Path.

    Acanthus
    - Use magic to alter a foregone conclusion.
    - Accept an unpleasant consequence.

    Mastigos
    - Use magic to commit a transgression.
    - Confide an uncomfortable secret.

    Moros
    - Use magic to irrevocably change something's state.
    - Allow something important to you to end.

    Obrimos
    - Use magic to burn away something unclean or untrue.
    - Do the bidding of a higher power.

    Thyrsus
    - Use magic to feed something or someone.
    - Surrender control to a primal emotion or desire.

      For Aspirations I just ask a player to give me a short list of unresolved conflicts, something they want to do, but haven't been able to do yet. After that I also give them a cheat sheet for Beats they can earn over a session which includes actions that exemplify their Order and Path that I award after the session. I leave those open to negotiation instead of nailing down what's "IC" for a particular Order or Path though.

                          I haven't had a chance to play a 2nd ed game with Aspirations and Obsessions yet, but if I was starting a game with players new to the Mage setting, I would perhaps consider letting them start without all their aspirations or with fewer than normal. Then in the first few sessions you throw a ton of plot hooks at them, have mentors introduce them to their OWN pet projects and encourage their interest. Have strange and curious anomalies appear near things the PC cares about like a place of work or family.

                          When introducing these plot hooks to new players don't describe it as an Exarchic Verge ,etc, but instead have the strangeness and effects of the Verge impact on the players life and let them decide if they think its important. If the PC was a teacher, I would be tempted to have some small part of their school manifest strange symbols of oppression, tyranny, and fear. While the schools staff don't notice or seem to be effected, it has a variety of strange and sinister effects on the students which manifest under mage sight. Then sit back and watch as your player goes out of his way to chase down the mystery. You can then introduce the concept of a 'Exarchic Verge' in game AND in character as the teacher PC finds some clues then goes to his cabalmates/mentors/local experts for advice.

                                  Just to throw this in there, the Adamantine Arrow book actually gets into a lot of storyteller and player advice. While it mentions the obvious ways a member of the Arrow can remain just a member, there's also a section which details how the storyteller can make the Arrow have more of an impact on the character, and advice for the character on how to, basically, do things that help gain Status in the Arrow. Things that make you stand out and get noticed.

                                  There's protecting Sleepers, fostering inter-Order cooperation, threat identification, which often leads to becoming a Sentinel, and obviously gaining notable martial prowess.

                                  I'm down to one player for this game. Their first two Aspirations are pretty mage-generic, 'Learn a secret about her order,' and 'find a magical item.' The other is defeating a more powerful foe. We haven't done Obsessions yet.

                                              By the way, I want everyone to know that I do appreciate the concern that too many Aspirations and Obsessions can bog things down. I agree! I think it's straightforwardly true that asking players to mentally juggle 5 or 6 or 8 personal goals is a great way to stretch their mental CPU resources thin, and can potentially pull the story in (5 or 6 or 8 x (number of players)) different directions that may be difficult to elegantly resolve.

                                              I guess my feeling, which is probably informed by my experience with certain *World games and various other systems, is that the Aspiration mechanic doesn't just have to be a list of concrete IC or OOC goals to consciously steer toward during the session but can be a codified way to tag certain RP behaviors for XP. Like, in practice, the way I often find myself handling rewards at the end of the session is to go down the list of everyone's Aspirations and Obsessions and ask if anyone can put forward a convincing case that Trevor accomplished X this session and Amanda accomplished Y this session.

                                              This is also how I handle Arcane XP; I've got a big checklist of "new experiences," including meeting various monster types for the first time, directly experiencing Arcana outside the character's purview ("wow, so that's what Forces feels like!") and, again in the service of pedagogy, engaging with game systems (the first time you scour your Pattern, gain an Arcane Beat; the first time you expend a Mana while scrutinizing a Mystery, gain an Arcane Beat - and so forth). This isn't something I expect the players to know about, but is kept in a big document that I refer to during play, and I point out occasions where doing something, particularly something risky, could yield a reward for that character ("Niall, you haven't been bitten by a vampire yet ...").

                                                  When I have a group of players new to something like Mage the Awakening, I ramp things up slowly, generally with a game where the awakening happens up front and early at the start of the campaign. That way I can explain setting conceits to the players as they come up. I also bring in things like Obsessions, Nimbus and the like as the game goes on. Just casting your first spell can be daunting enough without the players needing to worry if dancing shadows is an okay Nimbus. For example, for a new player, I tend to talk to them, and then build the Aspirations for them, not to be a control freak, but to help the player understand what they're for. Short examples can be stuff like "Cast a Spell", "Defeat an Enemy", "Absorb a Backlash" etc. while the longterms will be ones that aren't especially long in this case (two or three sessions) such as "Join a Cabal", or "Find out where you Mentor is". This way, once they've earned a few beats, they can see what they're meant to do with Aspirations. (Originally I found a lot of players would put things like "Discover why magic works", "Create a rank 5 hallow" type things for their short ones and "Become an Archmage" for their long term ones.
