    It was the middle of the night when the luxuriously hand crafted doors to the exotic shop were reduced to splinters as they were blown off their hinges. From the wreckage emerged a young boy, possibly between the ages of 12 and 14. The owner of the store, a handsome looking man with a full beard and dressed in middle eastern style apparel didn't even bat an eyelash at the destructive display. Rather, he folded his arms over his chest, leaned back in his chair from behind the counter and waited as the boy stormed up to him and slammed a fist on the counter, cracking it beneath his strength.

    "What did you do!?" the boy roared, his face contorted into raw fury as he was struggling not to tear the entire building apart. The store owner quirked an eyebrow at him before unfolding one arm and absently waving it through the air.

    "You wished to be a super hero, so that's what I made you." he said nonchalantly, "Are you unsatisfied with the results?" he asked, his demeanor was completely devoid of snark but rather suggested he truly didn't understand why the boy was so upset.

    "My parents!" the boy bellowed, slamming his fist again, this time splitting half way through the counter. "WHY!?" he demanded. The man folded his arms over his chest again before he responded.

    "Oh, that." he quipped, "Well, from my research, super heroes always have an element of tragedy in their origin stories. Soooo in order to make things more authentic, I had to kill your parents." the next strike from the boy was aimed directly at the owner of the store, but just as it was supposed to make impact, the man dissipated into a cloud of smoke.

    "Be careful what you wish for, boy!" the man's voice came from within the cloud before it and the entire store began to vanish out of existence. Soon, the super powered youth was left standing in a vacant lot, with no evidence to the store or its owner having ever been present.

    Horror: The Malicious Djinn

    Family: Eshmaki

    Hunger: Ruin

        'Honest" Gregory Loomis (the car salesman from hell) Eshmaki Collector

        Gregory Loomis had just inherited his father's car dealership when he underwent the devouring, and quickly began to indulge his collector nature by tricking people in need a of a car into paying large sums of money, or even better, trading beloved older vehicles for cars that were often lemons. Despite what you might expect, he is actually more likely to feed by screwing a customer on a new car than a used one, which he will tell other beasts is because a new car has more middlemen that need to be paid off than a used one, though he does love nothing more than selling a used lemon, even a recalled one, to someone he feels deserves it.


        His horror is a 6 eyed, four nostriled serpentine dragon wearing ostentatious jewelry, with leathery wings flapping and his forked tongue flicking as he scents out potential new vict... I mean customers. Gregory's horror wears jewelry made from hood ornaments, hubcaps and other notable parts of famously bad cars. The deadly fuel tank of a pinto converted into a bag he keeps his treasure in, the doors of a Deloren become armor for his wings as the scandalous grill of an Edsel became the base for a cod piece of all things for the serpentine Horror, made all the more sleazy by the addition of the lethal steering column for a 1961 Corvair.

        His Lair is something of a museum of horrible cars, the famous lemons carefully put on proud display on alters in a junk yard littered with the sharp rusty ruins of much better cars taken from his victims, with flows of battery acid, large angry junkyard animals and flaming rivers of gasoline providing hazards to any intruders along with with a security system his son Alan, a Beast himself with a similar horror (though Alan is more of a computer salesman from hell) made from the infamous computerized electronics of the 1976 Aston Martin Lagonda and the clunky iDrive system of a 2002 BMW 7-series.

        Gregory, being at lair ten, has decided to seek the incarnate inheritance via the spawn a legend method, in which he plans to drive one of the great auto makers out of business by tricking their executives into making the worst car EVER and letting all their out of work former employees and dealers know who did it.

        Lair Traits: maze, corrosive, toxic , slick, burning , jagged (representing scattered junk on the ground) and razored.

                Alan Loomis (the computer salesman from Hell) Eshmaki Ravager

                Alan Loomis, like his father before him, is an eshmaki who feeds by being a sleazy salesman who sells shoddy products. The difference being that while Gregory inherited a car dealership to work out of, where as Alan had to make do selling computers at the mall as a teenager in the 80s. It was slow feeding at first, but then he discovered the Coleco Adam, a computer that only routed the power supply through the often defective printer, but went so far as to release an electromagnetic pulse that would wipe any floppy disk inside it when turned. After modifying the store's stock of Adams to make the EMP stronger, he sold them all to the city government and fed deep as the EMP wiped all the data that wasn't written down and fried the radios emergency responders relied on.

                Over the next few decades, Alan repeated that strategy on multiple scales, selling computers that seem too good to be true to households, businesses and governments, and then, as soon as the extended warranty the customer paid oh so much money for runs out, they begin crashing, losing files and dropping connections, and then the customer is horrified to learn that the backups failed, all their precious photos, important financial documents, drafts of their screenplays, all of it lost forever.

                He also does a side business in spyware, putting various software out on the net that inexperienced users install to turn their cursor into a dragon, only for it to send all their personal information to Loomis, who then milks it for all it's worth and leaves the victim in rags. In these days of much more reliable computers, the identity theft strategy for feeding is his go to, but he is also expanding into shoddy smartphones, having recently unloaded a warehouse full of those exploding Samsung phones to various suckers.

                His horror is much like his father's, a great serpentine dragon decked out in finery made from the parts of famously bad computers, transistor earrings hang from his horns and a power mac G4 cube hangs from a heavy chain necklace as the chiclet keys of a Texas instruments TI-99 are set into rings on his clawed wings as his bronze armor, made from copper taken from the faulty power supplies of Dell Dimension 4600s, protects the horror's wings and body.

                His Lair is a computerized urban sprawl straight out of an 80s/90s era cyberpunk novel, electric currents fry intruders' bodies as EMPs from the various Coleco Adams in the lair fry any electronics they bring' into the lair. CRT monitors provide insufficient light for intruders to see and constantly ringing smartphones deafen them as the walls made from the aluminum casings of Apple threes and the heat from the various devices slowly cook them

                Lair Traits: Poor Light, Thunderous, Electrified, Extreme Heat, Disruption.

                            (Might be in bad taste, but I slept on it, and still had a nagging urge to write this character up.)


                            A confirmed sex offender and child killer, Harold Kreemz was on his way to either a life time in prison, or a very brutal death at the hands of other inmates. Or at least he would have been, had he not used the insanity plea to get himself acquitted. of his crimes. Instead of the rigors of life in prison, he was placed under mandatory therapy sessions to help him overcome his sick cravings. But instead of curing Harold, it turned him into something much, much worse. Discovering more about his inner self with each session, Harold's last visit to therapy resulted in his homecoming, finally transforming him into the monster he's always been on the inside. He has always targeted children, but now he reaps more tangible benefits for doing so. And now, with the ability to escape into the realm of nightmares, the police will have a significantly harder time capturing Harold then they did before!


                            His horror is a distorted blob of vanilla and strawberry ice cream. Large, bubble gum eye balls are set within drooping eye sockets as his partially melted features congeal puddles on the ground beneath him. Sprinkles, nuts, gummy bears and other assorted toppings are embedded throughout his creamy flesh like sickly sweet skin blemishes. Licorice hair dangles from his scalp like dreadlocks, limp and slick with chocolate syrup dribbling through them. His gaping mouth features splintered popsicle sticks for teeth, while his fingers and toes are tipped with waffle cone claws. Its voice is disturbingly high pitched and cheerful, singing enchanting music that causes children to flee from their parents in the hopes of getting a refreshing treat from the ice cream man.


                            Horror: The Ice Cream Man

                            Family: Namtaru

                            Hunger: Prey

                                    They call her the crazy cat lady, because of all the cats she keeps around herself. But she is no collector of felines, no matter what her victims might tell you. Anybody who stops to observe her pets will notice that they consist entirely of black cats, solid black cats! This elderly grandmother is a bringer of misfortune to the community. When night falls, she and her beloved kitties go prowling the streets, inflicting bad luck upon anyone unfortunate enough to cross their path. The lesson she imparts upon those she feeds from is simple in its deliverance: whether your good or bad, shit happens, and you can't be prepared for everything life throws at you.

                                    Her horror is an old witch sporting the ears, tail, claws, eyes and fangs of a black cat. She walks with a hunched gait, dressed in patchwork rags and always surrounded by a group of her black coated pets. Her passing causes supersitious people to throw up every counter hex their grandparents ever taught them as a child in an attempt to counteract the bad luck. Sometimes, if she's feeling coy, she'll leave them alone for doing so... unfortunately, that playfulness seems to be what draws in the Heroes who think they can ward off bad luck.

                                    Her lair is a superstitious nightmare full of jinxes and bad omens. Ladders, broken mirrors, roads leading past grave yards, spilled salt shakers, cracked mugs, burning ears, anything and everything that people associate with bad luck can be found in abundance. Visitors to her realm will find themselves hard pressed to maneuver through it without contracting 7 years bad luck, possibly more if they're careless. But it's not just the decorations that haunt them, the lair itself appears to be cursed, causing all manner of unfortunate accidents to befall unwelcome guests who dare to intrude upon her territory. Even should a Hero manage to find their way into her lair, the Beast will laugh as they are made to look utterly incompetent at their mission to destroy her.


                                    Horror: the Crazy Cat Lady

                                    Family: Eshmaki

                                    Hunger: Ruin

                                    Kinship: Undecided

                                    ---------------------------------------------------

                                    Everybody knows the story of the Little Mermaid. A young mermaid sees a man for the first time and makes a bargain with a sea witch to trade the only life she's ever known for the chance to spend the rest of her life with him. She does this before they even have one line of dialogue between them, claiming that it's love at first sight. In the Disney version, this story has a happy ending, with the mermaid marrying the man of her dreams. But the real story is much more tragic. The man was already betrothed and had no romantic attachments to the little mermaid, thus prompting her to die as a result of the sea witch's magic. This particular leviathan thought the original was much better, and taught a powerful message that young girls and boys can both learn from. Love at first sight doesn't exist!

                                    Having chosen to model herself after the popular folk tale, the Sea Witch's Horror is one that most people would easily recognize. She possesses the head and torso of a woman, but instead of legs, she has a mass of octopus tentacles with which to propel her through the water. She freely associates with Changelings, and has acquired a bit of mystical knowhow from them. She uses her magical expertise to offer bargains to those she feeds upon. Typically, such bargains are horribly stacked against her victim's favor, but the potential rewards are too good to refuse. Or at least that's what her victims think before signing away their livelihoods for the chance to follow their dreams.

                                    The Sea Witch's lair is a homely and welcoming place at a casual glance. An underwater cave with an air pocket for those land lubbers who don't have the gills necessary to survive otherwise. Potions and other magical reagents are lined against the wall on top of coral shelves, with an enormous cauldron in the center of the room, bubbling with a mysterious concoction. In the corner of the cave, one can see a side pathway leading into a bedroom of some kind, complete with a vanity mirror and make up kit so the cave's resident can make herself look presentable. Nobody who knows the resident personally comes to visit, however, not unless they need something from her. Then she welcomes them in with a cheery disposition and begins to negotiate the terms of their contract.

                                    Horror: the Sea Witch

                                    Family: Makara

                                    Hunger: Ruin

                                    Kinship: Changelings


                                    -----------------------------------------------------

                                    Lovecraft once wrote that the oldest and most powerful fear of mankind is fear of the unknown. Perhaps that may have been the case for a long time, but now mankind is getting wiser, more advanced, and with that advancement comes knowledge, and with that knowledge comes a brand new fear that threatens to shake man to the core. Nihilism, the realization that all of existence is ultimately meaningless. With all their scientific achievements, mankind has predicted that somehow, someday, the universe is going to die and everything in it will die, too. This Beast is a constant reminder of that knowledge, always looming over the heads of his victims with the realization that no matter what we do, we are all born to die.

                                    Their Horror is a pathetic and tragic sight to behold. A withered old king dressed in tattered robes and wearing a rusty crown upon his brow. His features are sunken and hollow, a clear sign that the rigors of time have stripped him of the hope he once carried in his youth. He sits apathetically upon his throne, watching the universe decay around him. He doesn't chase after his victims, there's no reason for him to do so, anymore. But when an enemy shows up trying to kill him, he rises from his seat and prepares to teach them that their struggle is futile. Nobody can fight death forever.

                                    His lair is a dead world within a dead universe. There are no stars in the sky, they burned out long ago, covering the world in perpetual darkness and freezing temperatures. All species of plants and animals have gone extinct and any food that might have been left has long since decayed, or been eaten by scavengers who eventually died off anyways. The land itself is a towering monument to mankind's failures. A graveyard built on top of a city, built on top of a graveyard, built on top of a city, built on top of a grave yard, built on top of a city, and so on. The king's throne sits on the upper most lair of this cycle of death and rebirth. He can't build the next lair anymore, he has no more subjects left to aid him!

                                    Horror: the Fallen King

                                    Family: Anakim

                                    Hunger: Tyrant

                                    Kinship: Geists

                                        The Deep One's desire likewise cannot be quenched. Despite not looking great, he has charisma, connections - and money. He somehow became a trusted advisor for a few celebrities, and used to work a number of jobs before finding his "calling".

This manipulated 'realization' of being responsible for your own misfortune, having no one to blame and having to face the consequences by yourself is what sustains his Horror. His cult is growing, and one of his cuckoo kids just recently had a homecoming and joined his brood and cult.

                                                        This is something of an evolution of my first real Beast character; only in the past while have I gotten the inspiration to really get Cormack to a more complete whole, and I figured I'd share him here. Let me know what you guys think.



                                                        Cormack was originally just a football player; he was big even at a young age, he was strong and eager, and his dad pushed him to become part of the big leagues. Everything seemed to be going well, and he got interest from various scouts looking for talent. This ended when a rival tackled Cormack into a back breaking injury that sent him into the hospital. Recovery went well, barring a lasting pain in his back, but in that time Cormack learned about the injuries various football legends went through, particularly with damage to their brain. That, more than anything else, killed his interest in football and through it ruined his relationship with his father. With his monetary and family investments in ruins Cormack tried to reinvent himself by using his looks, but though he had some talent acting he had problems remembering the lines, and he found himself verging on homelessness for it.

                                                        His luck changed when the dreams came back to him. Throughout his life, Cormack had been subject to odd dreams he could never place. What had started as spirographs that spiraled out of control, and turned into wild fractals that hurt his mind to remember, had re-manifested in his time at the hospital as a face. It never said anything, but it appeared in his the dreamscape in place of others' faces, in reflections that should have been his, in the dark. Every other time he woke up in a sweat; however, the night of a particularly bad interview, he dreamed of the stage he had been thrown off from. In what seemed like an eternity, the spirographs and fractals returned, subsuming the stage and its props, tearing the audience and seating away, and leaving just him standing in madness, with just the face staring at him from the void.

                                                        That is, until he realized he was looking at himself from that void, from the face. His face.

                                                        The next day, Cormack returned for another interview. The difference in his performance was staggering, and he was taken on, albeit in a background part considering his first attempt. His reputation swiftly grew from there.

                                                        Even compared to other Beasts, Cormack finds his Horror, The Fractal King, rather unusual. Outside of its body being tall, warped and angular, the Fractal King's form almost looks like a piece of animation rather than something flesh and blood. Light and shadow doesn't play quite right across it, an afterimage trails after every movement and a layer of something like static skitters over its form. It is obviously three dimensional and yet flat looking, though the distinction between the two varies when it slips through cracks in its domain. Painful ripples of white, black and red run across its body, with wings like blades protruding from its shoulders and its face is a smooth, rounded but expressive trapezoid decked with two horns on top of a similarly shaped head. Its realm initially appears to be a normal room, though one colored much like the King itself, with eye straining patterns that make it hard to tell where things actually are, and what is actually a door and what isn't; chambers accepted into its lair adopt these same properties, though those welcome find the patterns shift to something easier to bare. The Fractal King actually enjoys company and, provided it is respected, indulges in their pleasures with its illusions, but it is equally eager to share the terrors of its lair, leaving guests wondering just what is real even when they've been allowed to leave.

                                                        Concept: Charismatic Stage Actor

                                                        Horror: The Fractal King

                                                        Family: Ugallu

                                                        Hunger: Power

                                                        Legend: Seductive. Cormack's good looking, and he's long known it. When normal and even supernatural avenues fail, it's not too hard to find someone receptive to his charm to.

                                                        Life: Noble. Cormack may be seen as a flirt, but he'll listen and help however he can to make life a bit better. Being able to tell lies from reality helps.

                                                        Lair Traits: Poor Lighting, Sealed Exits, Mirages

                                                        Atavisms: Needs Must, Mimir's Wisdom

                                                        Nightmares: Flying and Falling, You Must Obey
