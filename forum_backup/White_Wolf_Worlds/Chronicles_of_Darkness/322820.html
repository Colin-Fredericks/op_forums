                                  Bone Shadows, Iron Masters, and Blood Talons don't hunt every single spirit, human, or werewolf they run across respectively. They hold that in reserve for trouble makers, though I can imagine they make it a point to find targets rather than just waiting around for them to show up if they're taking their job seriously. Storm Lords and Hunters in Darkness, however, don't have the same problem; Hosts and Ridden seem to be ubiquitously intolerable.

                                  I can see, easily, how an Iminir would look at a Sin-Eater and mistake it for some kind of exotic Ridden. A particularly wise Iminir might know the difference and give the Sin-Eater a pass, but given that it's a sacred oath and the tendency for Uratha to follow their instincts this seems like it'd be the exception to the rule. When zoomed out, I would imagine that Iminir are considerably more aggressive to Sin-Eaters than any of the other tribes.

                                                  Well from what I have read so far it seems like each Tribe of the Moon's culture revolves around hunting a specific prey. However, how they hunt that prey and what they do with it once it's been captured is entirely dependent on the individual Pack's traditions and the mentality of their own Totem. For instance a Pack of Storm Lords might successfully hunt a Ridden mortal and bind the individual far from the Spirit's resonate energy and perform a ritual that exorcises the Spirit from it's mortal host to honor a Totem related to rehabilitation or starvation. After all hunting lone Spirits are the preview of the Bone Shadow's anyway so it's not their custom to hunt a Spirit once it's no longer riding a human or beast. So some may consider Sin-Eaters to be a form of Claimed and hunt them like any other Spiritual abomination, while another would consider them foreign creatures that would best be avoided so long as they remain off the Pack's territory.
