    Lost Clans- A study into the fallen Kindred

    07-26-2016, 04:38 PM
    Strigoi- The Ones Best Forgotten

    "What could be said of the Clan of Traitors? Nothing good unfortunately. Alas, I suppose it would be prudent to perhaps first explain the peculiarities of this loathsome species before decrying them so swiftly, wouldn't it? Very well; First and foremost it should be stated that the Strigoi were Kindred like any other Clan that performs the Danse Macabre,no different than the Gangrel or the Daeva or my own Clan -they were undead monsters ruled by the need to balance Man and Beast like the rest of us. In fact some might even go so far to say that the Strigoi and Gangrel possessed a great many similarities ( though one would be wise to refrain from suggesting such a thing in ear shot of the Savages, provided one wished to keep their entrails within their bodies), both Clans having a stronger tie to the Beast than the others and being possessed of a rather abrasive pride in their inhumanity - not to mention both were master shape-changers.

    But this is where the similarities begin to fade, for while the scions of the Outsiders through their gift of Protean could assume the aspects and forms of living predators, and even nightmarish and alien features of the Beast itself the Wracks possessed an altogether more.. visceral ability. For you see the Strigoi could freely manipulate their flesh, and the flesh of others as if it were clay, and they could use that clay to craft many fascinating and grotesque additions to their very bodies - or they could completely reshape their bodies to appear as whatever they wished as long as it was vaguely human-ish in stature. Numerous extra limbs, protective plates of bony armor, sinuous tendrils crafted from looping lengths of intestine, these were but a small number of the alterations the Strigoi could inflict upon themselves.. or others. This ability allowed them to be either fantastic healers or terrifying torture-artisans in equal measure, and there were a great many stories of Wracks keeping particularly hated enemies alive for years, decades even of this agonizing surgery-torture.. denying victims the death they so sorely wished for until they had finally grown bored of their prey. But above all else the Strigoi exulted in gift of shaping flesh, and it was said that no two members of the Clan possessed the same features - so obsessed with experimentation and artistic expression were these vampires ,for they believed that by sculpting their forms as they saw fit they grew ever closer to being something truly unique in the All Night Society - each Strigoi was a unliving work of art among st the drab and uninspired sea of Kindred and Kine around them.

    This wasn't to say that the Wracks were without weaknesses mind you; for while the Clan was capable of terrible and amazing feats thanks to the gifts of their Blood, this power can at the cost of a near ravenous hunger for not only blood, but flesh itself in order to fuel their dark miracles. The Strigoi were cursed to be carrion-eaters, stuffing mouthfuls of meat down their gullets with each swallow of blood in order to gain Vitae - either living, undead, or dead. Thus it is said that the Strigoi oft had a reputation of being cannibals and sickening gourmands of the flesh, for like with every other facet of their existence, the Wracks were determined to be as creatively brilliant with their frailties as they were with their strengths.

    Truly, if they had persisted to the modern nights I have no doubt they would of come to exemplify the tenants of the Ordo Dracul, being near perfect ( hah, as if there is such a thing as perfect in this world. Perfection is the construct of an unimaginative mind, nothing more.) Defiant s every last one of them.

    If it were not for one small transgression.

    The Strigoi were a powerful Clan to be sure, but they were also vain and wicked in ways that would make even the most jaded and debased Serpent appear to be almost virtuous in comparison. It was this darkness that called to the hated and feared Strix, to the Strigoi, and through some horrendously auspicious twist of fate the Owls found kinship with the Wracks. Hand-in-talon, these to races of monsters conspired to topple the other Kindred from their pedestal that was their Humanity, to debase them, to lord over them, to crush each and every other vampire who would dare spurn their own true natures and pretend to be like the mortal cattle. Thus began a war in the mountainous regions of Europe between the Owls, their Strigoi hosts/ supposed allies, and the rest of the Kindred. But The Strix are fickle allies indeed, and for reasons never discovered they abandoned the Wracks to the mercies of their fellows near the end of the conflict. Perhaps the Owls had merely became bored of the Strigoi and tossed them away once they were finished with them, or perhaps they had always planned for the Wracks to be destroyed by their fellows. Whatever the case, the Wracks were exterminated in their entirety, and the proud lineage that were the flesh-shapers was no more.

    And yet... there still seems to be some traces of the Strigoi that survived their annihilation. It is said that the Pijavica were an offshoot of the Strigoi that grew into a clan of it's own. And some claim that the strange Discipline-contagion that is Viccissitude and the foul bloodline borne of it known as the Tzimisce were the products of a final gambit of the Wracks- a self sustaining Threnody that persists to this night. But this is just the conjecture of an old Dragon.. take of it as you will."

              Baersark- The Ones Who Resist

              " Ah, the Baersark.. truly no greater confusion is spawned than the ever present debate on whether or not the Baersark were truly a Clan or a particularly rebellious Bloodline of the Gangrel, or the Nosferatu or possibly my own. Personally, I firmly believe that they truly were a Clan of their own; the Reavers were simply too unique to be a simple Bloodline of any of the others. But I digress, you want to know what they were like and why they are no longer prowling the night with the rest of we Kindred, not to be bored to True Death by the philosophical musings of an aging librarian of The Dying Light. Very well, where shall I begin...

              The Baersark existed during the time of our Golden Age, when the Camarilla was strong and not so close to collapse that the thought of it's potential Fall ever crossed the minds of those venerable dead of the Senex of the Cult of Augers. There are some who say that the vampires who became the Baersark were the unfortunate remnants of one of the many tribes of Germania that had been crushed by Rome and her legions proper while the Legio Mortuum had their fun with the survivors - the callous acts of murder and feeding causing a number of the corpses to eventually rise from the embrace of Death as the Curse seeped into them. Where normally such an event would only spawn Revenants or worse, Larvae.. when the people of this forgotten tribe awoke they discovered that not only were they now blood craving undead things that withered in the sun and could barely even stand the sight of flames without panicking, they possessed a fierce inner rage. It was in these first few nights after their genesis that the nascent Baersark nearly destroyed themselves for good in the resultant Frenzies and due to the bizarre weakness that all of these people inexplicably shared in equal measure - a weakness I shall return to at a later point.

              But for those few that survived, they were able to learn how to retain control of themselves and even expand upon the gifts within their very Blood within a remarkably short amount of time by Kindred standards. After that it was not long before these new vampires were able to piece together, bit by bloody bit, what exactly happened to them as well as who -and what- the Camarilla and its masters were, along with a consensus on what to do about this horrifying society. Legends are unclear on what exactly went into deliberating the course of action they eventually took, but the intention was clear; they would do whatever they could to drive the Camarilla from their lands or, in failing that. make it so miserable that those preening bastards in Rome's Necropolis would no longer wish to stay. There are some Scholars who hypothesize that these fledglings were the unwitting founders of the vampire-killing Covenant VII, and while I do not agree with those who think this way it is quite clear that the clan possessed some of the most effective kindred slayers in history, and this was likely due to the Discipline they called their own - one that was said to of never been wielded outside this fearsome Clan.

              One would be forgiven in thinking that the Clan possessed Vigor if viewing them in battle for the very first time, so great was their physical power the legends say. For while the vampires of this strange variant of the Curse did not possess the Discipline of Vigor as far as records indicate, the powers they did possess more than compensated for it. You see, The inherent powers of this species of vampire allowed the user to increase their strength as they became injured, it's potency increasing with the severity of the wounds sustained by the vampire. This ability made fighting them a nightmare for most opponents, as the closer these upstart Kindred approached Final Death, the more they gained strength. While such a power would surely lead to Final Death in very short order, the hardiness of the Kindred body and the ease in which a vampire can recover from bodily damage ensured that such occurrences were surprisingly uncommon. They had others gifts that their Discipline bestowed upon them, they were able to gain vitae through blood-to-skin contact - perfect for a race of vampires who were constantly fighting while unarmored- provided that the blood splattering them was not their own, and they could enter a sort of controllable "battle Frenzy" ( not too dissimilar to Riding the Wave or the Red Surrender of the Gangrel it seems) where they could benefit from the bestial fury and its assorted attributes while remaining conscious to name but two.. which sounds suspiciously similar to another Clan that is still extant tonight. My Clan? Now that would be telling, wouldn't it?

              At any rate, these powers served very usefully in keeping these Kindred alive, for it played to not only their strengths but also helped compensate for their weakness - which was very compromising indeed. The Curse that plagued these vampires was both simple and potentially deadly at the same time; Should the vampire be wounded intentionally by another creature they were unable to offer or accept surrender until the person or creature who wounded them suffered injuries of severity that equaled their own. And seeing as how violent and aggressive these Kindred were on a good night, this Curse made it very difficult for them to peaceably interact with others.There were methods of working around this quirk of the Blood, their Discipline allowed them to weather inevitable conflicts that would arise nearly night after night, emerging no worse for wear, and the Clan itself had devised a intricate set of doctrines and rules regarding fights between clanmates in order to reduce the potential number of members lost to Final Death merely because one Clan member had playfully smacked the other. These rules of engagement did not extend to outsiders, however, and it was almost unheard of for members of this Clan to back down from a fight or offer accept the surrender of other Kindred should blood invariably be spilled - leading to how the Clan came to be called the Baersark in part.

              Which leads to a rather amusing piece of lore that illuminates how this word became the name of the Clan. For their entire history the Clan itself for one reason or another never saw a need to choose a name for their Clan. If they could be bothered to speak to other vampires at all they would either decline from giving a name to their race aside from the word 'Reaver' or would simply refer to themselves as the scions of the various tribes they came from ( for these vampires had quickly taken to embracing tribesmen and women from other peoples, seeing as their own tribe had no living people left thanks to the Legion of The Dead.) It is unclear of who was the first vampire from Rome to call this clan The Baersark ( though I suspect that it was a Gangrel- they themselves hailing from similar regions), but the name quickly gained prominence and stuck - though it is unknown whether or not the Clan ever appropriated the name for themselves.

              Alas their fate is quite similar to the Strigoi who would emerge centuries after them; The Baersark were hunted down and destroyed nearly to a man, it's many ( arguably tiny) bloodlines exterminated as the Camarilla instigated a systematic campaign of genocide after grudgingly enduring two centuries of guerilla warfare conducted by the Baersark. Some say that due to their Curse, the Reavers never attempted to make peace with the Camarilla and accept assimilation because they were physically unable to do so. Others say that their hatred for their Roman oppressors was simply so great that they would rather of died out than submit themselves to slavery. There are a few, however, who believe that the Clan as not fully eliminated, at least not by the Camarilla. There are stories that suggest that a single bloodline of the Baersark did escape annihilation, where they wandered for centuries before being incorporated into the Vitae-ic legacy of some other Clan - living on through them and even, some whisper, enduring into the modern nights with the rest of us.

              Whatever their ultimate fate, the Baersark serve to exemplify the important lesson for vampires everywhere: Sometimes the worst monsters that plague us come not from Owls or other denizens of the Night; but from our our carelessness and callous disregard for consequences, and we must always be vigilant in our Masquerades lest another threat like the Reavers emerges and seeks to destroy us from within. Perhaps it is already happened, and we are simply too blind to stop it before it becomes too late.

              Food for thought."
