                First off, glad to hear it! Thaumaturgy took a long time to come together (like, 3-4 major revisions), but the outcome was worth the headache.

                Now, to answers:

                Originally posted by Overbound View Post
                1) Are Urd's magic arrays onetime thing or is it possible for these guys to go around with spell-books with all their fancy magics needing only to be charged? Cause Spellcasting examples and "This Land Is My Land" give somehow different approaches.
                An array can be used as many times as a Sage pleases. Spellbooks, engraved relics, reusable summoning circles and occult tattoos are all common of the Scholars.

                Originally posted by Overbound View Post
                2) There is notion, that ST may include other Spheres into College's purview, but is it possible for thaumaturge to get access to other Spheres through his practice? Something along the line of studying out-of-clan Disciplines and old Mages' "not specialized" Spheres.
                Thaumaturges may research and invent new Spheres, with storyteller approval of course. You could invent a Divination Sphere for the Scholars of Urd, or a Necromancy sphere for the Wayfarers, etc. Research and discovery is the name of the Thaumaturgy game.

                Originally posted by Overbound View Post
                3) Following previous point: is it possible to cast foreign Spheres through frame of another Colledge's Ritual? Something like thaumaturge, who has some degree of familiarity with both Scholars of Urd and Wayfarer's Society using later's mind control through his runes.
                Sphere's aren't definitive things like Arcana. They are broad wrappers Mages use for a variety of spells that fit a certain theme. So no, a Thaumturge cannot improvise spells of a different College to fit their own, not without completely reinventing them (i.e. buying the spell again in a different College). This is why cross College Thaumaturges are so rare, it's a lot of work without a whole lot of benefit.

                Originally posted by Overbound View Post
                4) Did You consider adding some kinds of "special magic maneuvers" to different Colledges? Something along the line of Scholars of Urd creating onetime-use rune arrays for getting more power for this particular spell (die bonus), but with that write-up losing all its reality-altering weight for later uses; the Warlock going for a certain break of usurped soul to get more power right here, right now - automatic loss of integrity in Phylactery in addition to following roll which provides bonus for spellcasting; skipping the ritual part completely - Scholar visualizes his rune array, Monk remembers the feeling of thousands used katas, etc - at the cost of penalty to the cating roll (spell level or double of it, probably).
                Skipping the ritual is a no no. The ritual is integral to Thaumaturgy, the only way to skip it would be, well, Awakening. (Awakened Thaumaturges are likely to cast in the manner you described).

                Everything else there sounds pretty cool, but for the interest of simplicity I don't want to be adding a whole bunch of fiddly bonus mechanics. But that shouldn't stop you from using them at your table.

                Originally posted by Overbound View Post
                5) Did You consider following in footsteps of V:tM Thaumaturgy & V:tR Blood Sorcery and having more generalized rating of "[Colledge name] Style Mastery" in addition to Spheres' levels? It would give clear idea of thaumaturge's general proficiency in particular form of Art and could be used to determine other advantages, or something. Like, having the guy with 4 score in Style, through which he performs magic right now, casting level 1 spell and giving him 3 die bonus on the roll; or the guy who has "Urd Mastery" rating of 3 and "Sibilines Mastery" of 1 (with appropriate Sphere) cast the later's spell through the frame of first's ritual, cause he is just that much better in it, etc.
                I hadn't really considered it, no. Mostly because it's not the default assumption that a Thaumaturge will be a member of several Colleges. (Seriously, just learning the magic of one College is ​humongously demanding). If you did end up needing to track it I'd go by the highest Sphere in a given College.

                Originally posted by Overbound View Post
                6) Did You consider giving to thaumaturges Supernatural Advantage? Synthetic one, but still.                 Considered yes. I was originally going to give all of them Supernatural Advantages, but was convinced otherwise. This is, after all, supposed to be about mortals who've attained magic through other means (Proximi get a pass specifically because they're not normal).

                That doesn't mean Thaumaturges are defenseless. Every College has at least one Sphere that can be spun in a defensive manner (i.e. Mage Armor), and a variety of magical options for self defense.

                Originally posted by Overbound View Post
                P.S. Am I right in expecting imbued to be somehow similar to Corvo from "Dishonored"?
                That's a reasonable inspiration source of inspiration, yes. Though they're slightly less grab baggy in their abilities.

                Ah, what the hell, a little spoiler:
                • Imbued are Sleepwalkers who have their souls tampered with to give them a small set of innate powers. The Adamant Arrow is largely responsible for the practice, using it as a way to give zealous acolytes a way to "get in the fight" and contend with Supernal forces.
                • Imbued are grouped into Brotherhoods, each with a creed and purpose. As a part of the Imbuing process they are given a Geas, a compulsion to make certain they don't stray from their intended path.
                • Mechanically Imbued are essentially humans with a Legacy. They possess Attainments (called Investments) that can be called upon at will. Compared to Proximi and Thaumaturges Imbued are very specialized, possessing great expertise in an extremely narrow field. However, they have the advantage of being able to grow rapidly in power, and access their Investments with little risk. (i.e. Investments are free to use, and not subject to dispelling or disbelief.)
                • Imbued can also purchase enchantments, which represent spells Awakened Mages have cast into them, much like Imbuing an item. They can use they're limited internal Mana reserves to activate these abilities, though they require the assistance of Mages to recharge them.

                                    "What's this? I thought this project was abandoned," they say.

                                    "He hasn't updated in so long he's most certainly forgotten," they think

                                    But no, as it turns out I was just overworked and mentally exhausted. Take that fictional naysayers!

                                    Now I'm back to hacking at the Imbued, Sleepwalkers invested with legacy like powers by rare sub-sects of the Adamant Arrow. The Imbued draw their inspiration from comics and super heroes, rare humans imbued with narrow abilities and higher purpose. And while the project is still quite incomplete I am at the stage where a little feedback will go a long way. Mind you what I'm spoiling is a bit out of context, so I'll try to breakdown the mechanical bits left out:
                                    • Investments are free, and often permanently active. They cannot be dispelled, are not affected by quiessence or disbelief, and cannot be suppressed by any means other than the Imbued Geas (see spoiler)
                                    • Enchantments cost 1 Mana to activate for a scene. They are awakened magic and therefore suffer quiessence, disbelief and dispelling. However, they cannot be removed from an Imbued completely without Prime 5, only suppressed until reactivated.
                                    Spoilers ahoy:
                                    Heirs to Olympus

                                    Origins: Borne in ancient Greece, even before the height of its power, Heirs to Olympus are founded on a singular philosophy; for man to rise to the challenges beyond this world, they must first rise to those within it. To this end the Brotherhood sought to gather and empower the finest of humanity. Generals, artists, philosophers and explorers. Throughout history stood Paragons whose legends continue to inspire to this day, and though not all of history's great luminaries were Paragons, all Paragons are luminaries.

                                    Recruitment: To inspire man a Paragon must lead by example. In a world dominated by symbols of submission, this is a daunting task. The Heirs to Olympus pick from only the most exemplary to join their campaign, looking for force of will, clear vision, and a larger than life presence. Of course that means most Paragons are extremely driven towards causes of their own, but that suits the Heirs to Olympus just fine. So long as a Paragon continues to fearlessly inspire others to stand for themselves against corruption, it doesn't really matter what specific cause he is trumpeting.
                                    Once selected a prospective Paragon must submit herself to a series of grueling trials, designed to test her every facet physically, mentally, and spiritually. Though she is never informed of this during the trial, the potential Paragon needn't succeed in every task, but she must show an enduring spirit and a drive to overcome each obstacle. Any sign of backing down is grounds for dismissal. At the end of the gauntlet the sleepwalker emerges spiritually prepared for the final ritual. She is brought to a secret ruin and blessed in an enchanted pool, emerging a Paragon for all humanity to see.

                                    Geas: As a paragon of humanity Heirs to Olympus mustn’t show weakness or debauchery. Whenever a Prodigal Son gives into his vice he loses access to his Investment for the rest of the scene. Whenever he fails a breaking point he loses access to his Investments for a full day.

                                    Enchantments: The Heirs to Olympus favor personal enchantments rooted firmly in the material. They empower a Paragon directly as the Brotherhood’s creed outright forbids the use of magic to directly influence or affect humanity, but a gentle nudge is generally acceptable. Mind, Life, and Fate are the most common Arcana, while Death and Spirit are rare.

                                    Common Enchantments: Winds of Chance ••, One Mind Two Thoughts ••, Alter Aura •••, Fortune’s Protection •••, Augment the Mind ••••, Lucky Coin ••••, Forge Destiny •••••, Supreme Augmentation •••••

                                    Enchantment Dice Pool: Presence + Paragon Skill (see Below)

                                    Sobriquet: Paragons

                                    Paragon Investment

                                    A Cut Above •
                                    Paragons represent the pinnacle of human achievement, they are the best of the best, seemly blessed with talent and gravitas. He constantly benefits from the spell First Impressions and never succumbs to fear, mundane or magical. In addition, when he gains this Investment mark the Paragon’s two highest ranked skills (if several are tied for highest, he picks two from among them), these become his Paragon Skills. Paragons gain 9-again on all mundane rolls involving their Paragon skills.

                                    Cream of the Crop ••
                                    People look to Paragons for leadership and inspiration, and defer to them in times of need. Mortal bystanders will assume the Paragon is a figure of authority, and follow his instruction with little prompting. He increases the general impression of all mortals towards him by two steps, and reduces the number of Doors (GMC pg. 192) in Social Maneuvering by two. Finally, he gains 8-again on all rolls involving his Paragon skills.

                                    Exemplar •••
                                    A Paragon’s excellence becomes almost palpable, his presence radiant, larger than life. He seems to fill every space he enters and inspire simply with his presence. To act against such a figure seems almost unfathomable, and to many, impossible. Those who intend to act against him in any respect save self defense, must succeed a Resolve + Composure roll, penalized by his Presence. Similarly those he acts against must succeed the same roll, or give way in his presence. In addition, he gains the Rote Quality on all rolls involving his Paragon skills.

                                    Miracle Worker ••••
                                    The Paragons excellence expands beyond mundane reason, presence and expertise so great as to defy reality. She may now accomplish acts using her Paragon skills that should be flat out impossible, or tremendously unlikely. Ricocheting bullets to strike hidden targets, jury-rigging functional aircraft from household implements, performing comprehensive psychoanalysis based on tiny indicators on a person’s clothing, etc. Non-combat actions treat a single success as Exceptional Success, while damaging actions (like called shots) reduce penalties (including defense) by 5. Extended actions halve, rounded up, the target number and the interval between rolls.
