                        For inspiration for the CDC compact, I'd highly recommend The Strain, since it's about a CDC employee (and eventually an entire Hunter cell) dealing with a viral vampire outbreak.

                        I like the idea that the compact initially thought that things like vampirism and lycanthropy were viruses, and has only recently made serious efforts at supernatural outreach, now that they have enough information to know that a lot of the "player splats" are fighting the same things they are, and they don't really have a chance doing it without working with them to some degree. That would be an interesting hook- how did the organization find out? How did the leadership finally conclude that vampirism wasn't viral and that vampires actually attempt to prevent disease outbreaks whenever possible?

                        Cognitohazards, memetohazards, and LRDs (localized reality distortions) are also part of what they deal with (since they often exhibit similar qualities to viruses and share the trait of being major threats to humanity as a whole. See the "zombies" from the movie Pontypool for an example. And a certain plot element/flimsy boob excuse from MGSV which may have been based on them), though these are poorly understood and they're only recently developing formalized protocols for dealing with them. Some of the members have also picked up the term "x-risk" from transhumanist and futurist communities, and have started using it to describe their targets instead of just "magic diseases and parasites".

                        They're currently dealing with a need for competent personnel who can handle the more action-y side of things. They try to get ex-military or ex-law enforcement personnel when they can, but due to TFV's headhunting of those areas they often just recruit emergency personnel of various kinds and send them to the shooting range and tell them to focus on learning how to make headshots.

                        They can get a fair bit of funding and support when they need it, because they deal with threats that most people (and government agencies) understand and fear. Vampires and werewolves don't really scare most people, but "biohazard" and "radiation" do. They say a clipboard and a lab coat can get you anywhere. A hazmat suit is even better- and most people do whatever someone in a hazmat suit tells them.

                        Also, to the directly above poster: you don't always need an entirely new Conspiracy. You could simply have the Chambre operated by France's branch of the Malleus.

                            Not really serious, but I'm imagining something like frat boys who have no idea what they're doing, but they're having a blast. Two parts Maiden's Blood Sisterhood, one part Ashwood Abbey, called something like the Sons of Broseidon. They get themselves absolutely hammered on some homebrewed booze and offer themselves to a vampire. The vampire takes a bite, get affected by the booze somehow (it's really strong stuff), gets dragged off, staked and tied to the roof of the frat house waiting for the sun to rise.

                            Or they throw a kegger at a place sacred to Werewolves. When the woofs show up to object the frat boys start chasing them in their trucks. All that chrome decoration on the bumpers and such? That ain't chrome. It's silver.

                            Or they run into a possessed person whom they tie down and essentially waterboard with beer until the spirit leaves. Which somehow works. They are also somehow immune to Disquiet and the radius of the Wasteland is reduced, but only after consuming enough booze. These hunters refer to the created as "Brometheans".

                            They are lead by a bro shrouded in legend and mystery. Only whispers refer to the legendary Broseidon, who lives somewhere on the coast of Californea where he in tune with the ocean and stuff which grants him the deepest of wisdoms. The only ones who have ever spoken to him are Brad and Charlie Renolds-Handelsen. They travel the west coast to visit the chapter houses of the Sons of Broseidon to convey the wisdom of Broseidon and attend the best parties. There's something odd about them though: Brad and Charlie look like they're in their mid-twenties and have done so for at least the last two decades. They are currently engage in the most epic game of gay chicken the country's ever seen: so far they've been happily married for almost five years.

                            The Sons of Broseidon have made some progress here and there, but because of their fratboy antics are not as effective as they could be if they got their act together. They share a few institutes of education with the Maiden's Blood Sisterhood, who see the Sons as an unruly bunch of losers while the Sons see them as uptight creepy wiccan chicks who need to relax for a change.
