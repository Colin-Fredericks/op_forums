        Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
        Good point, but I'd just have them sign a pledge to stay back and not cramp the new monarch's style in exchange for something nice.
        A pledge is definitely a good start. It would also need to include provisions for not betraying the Freehold and it would need to be renewed regularly. But what do you do if they refuse the pledge?

        I'd be more concerned with what their relationship to the Freehold is like. Are they expected to participate in the Freehold's activities? If not, are they allowed to? Does the Freehold protect former monarchs or do favors for them? If so, how much energy is dedicated to that? Is it like former Presidents, who get Secret Service protection long after leaving office? Who gets stuck being the Changeling Secret Service? Is it an honored and sought after position or is it a shit job pawned off on new Changelings? If not, what's to stop enemies they made while serving as ruler from coming after them when they're no longer in charge?

        And, on another note, what would the Freehold do if the dream selection process got them a mortal who really, really didn't want to be the King or Queen of a fairie community. "You're joking!? That was just a dream! If I'd have known it was real, I never would've taken that crown! This is crazy!" Hell, how often does a human monarch run away or kill themselves? What does the Freehold do to stop that? Does it break the Bulwark if it happens?

        Please don't take these as criticisms of the idea. I'm almost certainly going to use it at some point. I'm only thinking up all these questions because I like it so much. These issues aren't flaws in the premise, they're just interesting and awesome plot wrinkles that having a mortal monarch for a year at a time brings up. I just ike to try and work out all the angles.

                    (Freehold brief) The Gatehold
                    Location: Louisiana State Prison (population 5100 prisoners, 1700 staff members)

                    Story: The descent and ascent, the archetypal story of a hero descending into an underworld and then, eventually, forcing his way out,but at a mighty cost

                    Bulwark: The Suffering - a member of each court is nominated every Solstice and Equinox, and they have to go through a painful endurance test. The last changeling standing wins.

                    Courts:
                    The Court of Stone - The court of perseverance, the court of the bloody-minded, the court of those who struggle through the realities of prison life and seek to make themselves more comfortable through domination.
                    The Court of Chains - The court of resignation, the court of lifers, the court of those who will never taste the 'real world' again, as far as they are concerned. Seen as a strange mix of apathy and serenity.
                    The Court of Keys - The court of hope, the court of new fish, the court of those who peer through the bars of their windows and know that one day they'll be free. they seem unconcerned with their surroundings, knowing them to be a temporary roadblock.

                        I really like the idea of each court having a Godly name, preferably in relation to the old pagan gods that would have been involve in France's upbringing(the Celts, and in particular the Gallic gods), but I can't find neat parallels. Still love the idea of a Diana Court, but if I can't get the gods thing to work, then it'll be the [Color] Rose Court as the main name. Anyways, the following are brief pitches for the Rose Courts of Paris, not fully capturing their idealism, cynicism, or reasons for their interpretation. Still, should be good.



                        ???, The White Rose, the Court of Pensiveness, the Songbirds and Distant of the Closed Aerie
                        Keep your friends close, your enemies closer, and yourself far away-This is the motto of the Closed Aerie, changelings who embrace the lessons of empathy and caution from the freehold's stories-Beauty learned to love the Beast by understanding him, and yet Little Red Riding Hood's compassion only earned her the belly of the wolf, and so she learned to be wary of those with sweet tongues. Courtiers of the White Rose as a result tend to become openly friendly and understanding, making friends and digging up secrets of all around them, while being reserved with their actual selves, and as such make for great investigators and movers in Parisian culture, knowing who needs to meet who and playing the social games well without giving leverage on themselves.

                        The Ideal of the Roles
                        The Maiden is marked by her open-mindedness, her willingness to consider ideas and engage in activities foreign to her, to give Beast, Wolf, and Woodsman their due. White Maiden Duels tend to include an element that forces the duelists out of their comfort zone to test their ability to move into new realms.
                        The Wild One is marked by her expressiveness, her ability to communicate an idea or emotion convincingly to another, regardless of whether they actually feel strongly about it or not. White Wild One Duels tend to test the Lost's ability to be vulnerable or to give the wrong impression deliberately.
                        The Woodsman is marked by her callousness, her emotional distance from the people around her. White Woodsman Duels push the participants to do things that would make lesser participants squirm with discomfort-after all, the Woodsman must kill all manner of creature, regardless of her own reactions to them.

                        Approaches
                        Trust a Stranger-Friend- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when he or his allies convince a courtier to trust them.
                        Look at the Surface- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when his trick a courtier in accepting the face-value.



                        ???, The Red Rose, the Court of Joy, the Florists and Freudians of the Ember Garden
                        Flowers don't bloom without care and fires don't grow without fuel, and so the Ember Garden makes men and beasts out of each other by seeding the Paris area with the dual lessons of love and lust from the freehold's stories-Beauty's love made a prince of the beast and an aggressor of her suitor, while Little Red Riding Hood's chosen path of the prostitute's needles tore her apart with wet teeth and the harsh lessons of messy maturity. Courtiers of the Red Rose didn't miss out on the passionate psychology in their stories, and so serve as psychologists and resource managers for the freehold so that everyone has what they need to live as well as the reasons to do so, pricking and burning themselves with others' wants to keep Paris a joyful place worth returning to.

                        The Ideal of the Roles
                        The Maiden is marked by her charity, her willingness to give what she has to others, whether it be time and attention to a Beast, answers to a Wolf, bread to a grandmother, or fairness to a Woodsman. Red Maiden Duels tend to push an element of sacrifice for others to see how far she'll go to bring joy to a person.
                        The Wild One is marked by her isolation, her seclusion from others as a result of her passions both fair and foul, her hunger for flesh either out of a need to connect intensely or vain indulgence. Red Wild One Duels tend to include elements that provide insight into whether a freeholder is strong enough to keep their problems to themselves.
                        The Woodsman is marked by her acceptance, her ability to be welcomed into any group and to be seen as a desirable creature to be around. Red Woodsman Duels often challenge the participants to get into increasingly closed-off circles as part of the resolution of their conflict.

                        Approaches
                        Dress in the Fire- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when he gets a courtier to hurt themselves in order to get what they want.
                        Beast Out of a Man- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when he drives someone to hurt the courtier out of love.



                        ???, The Blue Rose, the Court of Anticipation, the Steersmen and Punctual of the Clear River
                        To grasp time is to grasp a pure vision of Arcadia and her threats, and the Clear River master the hidden lessons of patience and haste from their stories to see situations with Clarity and act accordingly-Belle's patience allowed her to see the Beast for what he was, but was pushed too far by her sisters and nearly caused the death of the Beast, while Little Red Riding Hood was too hasty in both judging the wolf and her 'grandmother' in the dark, and too eager to dawdle on the way to the cottage. Keen analysts and planners, Courtiers of the Blue Rose tend to keep the freehold moving as it needs and uncover the truths to the mysteries of Paris, struggling against the flowing rapids of the clock which seeks to make them overspend or speed through their minutes

                        The Ideal of the Roles
                        The Maiden is marked by her opportunism, her willingness to work with the time given her by others for her own intended purposes, whether it be time with the Beast or family or enjoying the walk through the woods. Blue Maiden Duels often have a strict time limit, forcing the duelists to make as much as they can out of a little thing.
                        The Wild One is marked by her timeliness, her keen sense of how much time to make for herself and others, such as how many days a father has with his family before accepting death or distracting a young girl to have more time to setup a trap. Blue Wild One Duels often involve participants finding a way to get more time to do what they need to do.
                        The Woodsman is marked by her finality, her ability to solidly and conclusively resolve conflicts and keep them from going on beyond her own actions. Blue Woodsman Duels demand that whatever a freeholder does, it ends the conflict in a single act.

                        Approaches
                        Come and Sleep- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when he tricks a courtier to miss something important in haste.
                        Don't Abandon Us Tomorrow- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when he gets a courtier to delay fulfilling a promise.



                        ???, The Black Rose, the Court of Contempt, the Blackguards and Biters of the Grave Hollow
                        In the end, all problems are solved with hurting one another, and the Grave Hollow sees it as their mission to prepare the freehold while standing at the gate, serving as bleak living lessons of protection and violence from their stories-the Woodsmen and Beasts of each story solve their problems and guard the interests of others and themselves by tearing others apart, and the Maidens learn from the harsh experience. The Black Rose Courtiers don't have the patience for an ill-prepared city, and so teach all members of the freehold to fend and stand up for themselves while they seek out the freehold's enemies, honing a careful and vicious disdain for everyone and everything to build a tough community and rend opposition without wasting sentiment.

                        The Ideal of the Roles
                        The Maiden is marked by her potential, untested though she may be-The Black Rose strain to dig up the blessing of Beauty as a growing actor in her world, and scorn Little Red Riding Hood for her wastefulness. Black Maiden Duels should show that the duelists can pick up new skills quickly, proving they might stand some chance.
                        The Wild One is marked by her being untouchable, demonstrating prowess in putting walls between herself and her enemies, being unseen by them, and slipping from their knives and bullets. Black Wild One Duels are often arranged to cast a scornful eye on those who get affected by their opponents, skewed to build momentum for the one who hits his opponent where it hurts.
                        The Woodsman is marked by her preparation, her having the skills and tools on handed needed to deal with anything and anyone at any time. Black Woodsman Duels endeavor to include unexpected curveballs to the fulfillment of the duel, seeing if the freeholders can protect themselves and others from the random whims of the fae and the world.

                        Approaches
                        As It Used to Be- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when he catches a courtier unprepared for them.
                        If You Run Away- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when he forces a courtier to rely on another for protection.



                        ???, The Golden Rose, the Court of Awe, the Pathfinders and Strays of the Sundown Spire
                        The riddle of the fae is the struggle between primordial instinct and advanced self-control, and the Sundown Spire explores all frontiers to bring forth fruits of understanding to the freehold as they reconcile the balance of wilderness and civilization within the freehold's stories-Beauty, Beast, and Avenant are civil creatures struggling with the rule of their primal inclinations, while Little Red Riding Hood dives into the natural world that wears the veneer of polite modernity. Both rangers and hortculturalists of the Hedge and avid scientists and sociologists of the real world, Courtiers of the Golden Rose bring in the cutting edge and most mysterious of resources to the freehold, alongside their knowledge and instincts and extreme contrarian obsessions.

                        The Ideal of the Roles
                        The Maiden is marked by her knowledge, her great capacity for memorizing facts and eagerness to know more things, being possessed of an education whether it's used to good effect or not. Gold Maiden Duels often demand that the participants know or learn something to resolve their issues with one another, some hidden element missing from the equation.
                        The Wild One is marked by her carnality, potent hungers for fundamental desires that shape their lives, whether they struggle with it or use it as a lodestar. Gold Wild One Duels usually involve an element that displays a willingness to show temptation, if not to indulge in it, and having the stakes measured in part by the proof of their connection to the simple world.
                        The Woodsman is marked by her dominance, her prowess at bringing her environment under heel, shaping cities, forests, and dreams to her desire. Gold Woodsman Duels include an element of forcing their opponent to do what they desire, forcing them onto their territory, either intellectually, socially, or physically.

                        Approaches
                        Listen to a Wolf- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when they trick a courtier into engaging a clearly dangerous situation.
                        Your Look is Burning- the Huntsman gains a Yearning when they manipulate someone into judging the courtier for indulging their wilder instincts.



                        Aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaand that's the nutshell.

                            A bit of an update or expansion to my Gold Mountain Freehold, now that I've spent a while thinking (some might say obsessing) about it:


                            At the core of the story of Gold Mountain is a simple idea: that wealth leads to a better life. Wealth, in this case, can mean literal money, but doesn't have to. It basically refers to everything that a person owns which could, at least theoretically, be bought, sold, traded, or stolen. During a Changeling's durance, they owned nothing - not even themselves. In escaping from Arcadia, they won back that much, and now they want more.

                            Each of the Courts of Gold Mountain defines its place in the Freehold's story by answering two questions: how do they acquire wealth, and what do they do with it once they've got it?

                            For the Court of the Mint, aka. the Court of Greed, the answer to both questions is the same: they invest. Whether they're merchants buying low and selling high or venture capitalists carefully choosing which start-ups to finance, they believe that the only correct thing to do with wealth is to use it to generate more wealth.

                            The ideology of the Court of the Bridge, aka. the Court of Affection, dictates that wealth should be earned through hard work and dedication. It stresses, however, that true success requires cooperation, and so they share their wealth with those they trust and rely on, rather than squandering it on themselves.

                            The Changelings of the Court of the Dome, aka. the Court of Anger, take wealth from those whom they feel are undeserving, particularly those who have used it to abuse those less wealthy than they. While they certainly keep some of what they take, they justify their actions by redistributing the wealth they appropriate to the poor and downtrodden.

                            The Court of the Tower, aka. the Court of Pride, are socially-conscious visionaries who fit uneasily into the larger story of the Freehold, considering wealth a goal unworthy of pursuit. Nevertheless, they are often expert fund-raisers, or rely on the support of wealthy patrons to make their visions reality. They channel what wealth comes their way into making the world a better place, either through charitable works or by creating things of beauty in the pubic sphere.


                            The Bulwark that guards the Gold Mountain Freehold relies on the principal that symbols and displays of prosperity and good fortune can ward off evil, including the Keepers themselves. It takes the form of an extravagant celebration hosted annually by the King on the first day of the Chinese New Year. Although the customs and symbols of that holiday form the backbone of the ritual, the four Courts put on all manner of grand entertainments and spectacles, both traditional and otherwise. The whole affair is an orgy of conspicuous consumption that celebrates all that is good in the new lives the Changelings have built since leaving Arcadia.

                            Changelings from other Freeholds sometimes see the Gold Mountain Bulwark as trivial compared to their own, more onerous traditions, but they miss the dangers and costs concealed under the merriment. Firstly, the cost of hosting the celebration is always enormous, and all but the most wealthy Kings must rely on generous donations from his Court to cover the expense. More than one King has been forced to abdicate when it became apparent that he wasn’t going to be able to provide a sufficiently extravagant party.

                            Furthermore, the Gold Mountain Bulwark ritual is always held in a clearing of the Hedge. While this keeps the festivities away from the prying eyes of mortal authorities, it renders the event particularly susceptible to disruption by agents of the Keepers or other dangers of the Hedge. One might imagine that the attendees, anticipating the possibility of a raid, would refrain from excessive drink or other activities that would render them less able to defend themselves, but such restraint would defeat the purpose of the ritual.

                            Finally, the Bulwark demands that the Changelings in attendance give themselves over fully to the revelry, which usually requires them to act counter to their nature. Courtiers of the Mint spend frivolously to bedeck themselves with fine clothes and jewelry, and distribute extravagant gifts without expecting anything in return. Courtiers of the Bridge leave their mortal friends and loved ones behind, and must selfishly accept whatever is offered to them. Courtiers of the Dome set aside old grudges (at least for a while), and cavort with misers and idle dilettantes. Courtiers of the Tower engage in debauchery and excess, and cast aside all shame and restraint. Although they may enjoy themselves at the time, many feel that they have sacrificed something of themselves when they wake up in the morning.
