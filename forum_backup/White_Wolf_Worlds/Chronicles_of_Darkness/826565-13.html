                                            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                            What I'm coming from is that the stuff you're describing here? That's not mental illness.
                                            Putting aside that I feel that your definitions are not exactly right, you have moved far and away from what I was actually talking about. I said I didn't want to talk about mental illnesses. Frankly, I think you are all right and wrong at the same time. The definition of mental illness is fluid that way. And, if you want to get away from acusations of mental illness and "insanity," then having Delusional and Warped for a Condition is a very bad move. People will see those words, and think, "oh, changelings are crazy." I feel that the average reader is going to walk away with that impression from reading the Clarity section as is, and for most people, "crazy," "insane" and "mentally ill" are synomous. They're not, but I don't think that the average person will realize (or care) about the difference.

                                            If the intent of the writers is to suggest that this isn't any kind of mental disorder, I'm going to call that a failure. Hell, Clarity 0 broken perceptions literally fits the definition of insanity, and they join the "mad dance of the True Fae." I don't see any way out of the writing suggesting that Low Clarity means the changeling is falling to madness.

                                            Anyways, I wanted to specifically address the "Purely supernatural" comment. We're still talking about something going on with a changeling from a mental / subjective standpoint, which the Wyrd makes have physical weight. The physical and "psycho-spiritual" to use Arcane's term, are interrelated. A changeling could have misperceptions, and the Wyrd will make those misperceptions into truth. I don't give a frak what you call it. I just care that there is apparently a subjective component from the changeling. And that means that the changeling's mental state is culpable in some manner.

                                            So, I'm sorry, but using the idea of "purely supernatural" is innately flawed. Because there's no such thing as "purely supernatural." That creates an artificial divide between magic and the "mundane" that, given the writing of the CofD, doesn't actually exist. Sure, magic is hidden from the world. But it is no less ever present in everyday life. Every child's imaginary friend is literally real inside of the Hedge and Faerie, even as its entirely a construct of the child's mind.

                                            EDIT - In short, CtL's fae blur the line between fantasy and reality. A binary system of "dream" and "real" is innappriopriate, since anything can be both in this game.
