                                    What you have to think is first the effect you want in your game, and second the time frame you want for this.

                                    Most of what was said is about how the supernaturals will fare at the last stages of humanity. Now to the game, proper:

                                    At the moment the youngest humans are 5 years old, its certain that everyone, absolutely everyone will see the danger and think of means to counteract this. Actualy, at this stage we have a fairly numerous and stable society, but already in total red allert. Each splat, and humanity proper, are going to search for a solution.

                                    Here is your major decision, if there is a solution. Its your call entirely. Maybe there is one that humanity can find for itself. Maybe one or more splats have the secret to recover the world. Maybe there is an answer, but it is more complex than what a single splat can achieve by itself. And maybe humanity is simply doomed, so to speak.

                                    The first decade or two is going to be relatively stable, but concerned. In one way or another, every splat so far are likely to worry about humanity's destiny. Most will put more strain on rules about not killing humans, but such humanitarian deeds are going to be eventualy compensed by the horror of human experimentation, as the less humane of monsters try to find an answer.

                                    Passing this, humanity start to dwindle slowly. That will probably bring the fall of the Secret, as supernaturals become more numerous in proportion, even if some of they somehow try to keep their own numbers in check (probable for vampires). It means both less ways to hide and less reason to do so. Chaos will probably start here at some point, at least in human society.

                                    In the last decades, if no way out is found, are the moments of final decline. Without humanity, supernatural societies are likely to decline themselves, in a similar but delayed manner. It is probable that abundant chaos from the previous period start to revert as there is less to do. There is nothing to do about the coming end, and everyone alive is old and tired. The world does have much more resources than needed for most to just survive, living to the natural end isn't a challenge by itself. It is humanity as a species that is going. This is a time os despair, but also contemplation, as the last people just prepare to live their retirement.

                                    Now we have the question of the solution. A lot of interesting things can be used. Maybe the answer is in the PCs hands, and this is a Philosophical Epic about those heroes who can save humanity or fail in such a way that it won't even matter anymore, since there will be no one to care afterwards. Or the answer will be found by someone else and this is more of a personal chronicle about the PCs living throughout humanity's greatest hardship, similar to War of Worlds of H. G. Wells.

                                    Or maybe there are anwers that are so questionable that people have to choose between a heinous escape or letting humanity die out for the better. Like if the way to restore fertility is an Ordo riual that needs broken and tortured human slaves. This can make humanity live on to feed the vampires, but without anything but suffering for the living. Even if the answer isn't so awful, the people that find it and its nature will impact the future of the planet. This includes both the possibility of the God-Machine building a stopper in the process for some mysterious reason, or the possibility of the God-Machine not having complete control of the process. Maybe it is self-sabotage, or maybe supernaturals are, after all, beyond its grasp.

                                    Finaly, there's the outcome. An epilogue is likely a good end for such a game. If the world is doomed (by design or failure of the PCs), and no one will survive, think about what it is like for them to pass out. If supernatural creatures are tough enough, maybe the PCs, or some of them, live to see what is coming next. If the menace is stopped at any point, then the experience of the world with such disaster and the nature of the solution are going to shape the world forever, and any surviving PC should get the opportunity to watch this new world.

                                    You can find some inspirations in the 'last' books of the Classic World of Darkness, the Time of Judgement line. Also in some apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic stories, from Mad Max to the book I Am Legend.

                                                            Well, vampires would go extinct without humanity, except maybe a small group of Dragons and a few who have supernatural patrons to feed them. Not sure Mages could keep going, either. As Manus mentioned, sterile humanity = humanity becoming metaphysically associated with sterility, meaning that mages would have to swim against the tide even more than they usually do to fight it. No humans quite possibly means no Arcadia and no Gentry, since Changeling heavily implies if not outright states that the Wyrd depends on humanity. It's kind of what differentiates it from the Umbra and Spirits- the Wyrd springs from narrative, which is a human concept. Prometheans...some of them might derive some pleasure from the world becoming as barren as they are. No humans would probably make becoming human massively harder, so they would have to pursue other avenues, even working with alchemists to try to use Pyros to save humanity. Mummies...wouldn't really like it, but in an apocalyptic scenario they'd be a natural fit to seize power. You might have a few Sin-Eaters making themselves deathlords of the wasteland, but most of them are connected enough to humanity that they'd take human extinction the way most humans would. Humanity going extinct means that the machine massively fucked up, because the books make it clear that for all its faults it is over all concerned with humanity surviving. It's possible that the sterility effect was the result of someone fucking it up, in fact. It would probably be frantically trying to revive humanity. A few Demons might consider a humanless world to be their Hell, but many more would probably want humanity back. Some Beasts might be onboard with the "now the monsters are in charge" thing, until they realize that no humans means it's massively hard for them to feed, and no human fears or human stories means that monsters have no place in the world. I'm not sure how you see the "age of the supernatural" happening when the supernatural is so dependent on humanity. You could say that the world becomes much more reliant on the supernatural, with much smaller human population that requires supernatural intervention to sustain it.

                                                            The Pure would probably love it, though.
