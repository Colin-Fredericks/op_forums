    I tend to think about antagonists through ideological lenses; everyone covered a lot of the 'role in a story' and vibe kind of material well, but I want to revisit ideology and emphasize what Bale Hounds represent to me: surrender.

    Werewolf has moved more and more to emphasizing the first purpose of a werewolf as hunting and not fighting, but the underlying ideological tension is the same: how you approach your impossible duty and try to live up to it. Pure might twist that duty to serve the interests of the Shadow and their own rabid ideology, but they care about duty - depending on how you interpret them they might be more relaxed about their non-genocidal duties or make poor choices of priority, but they're still living up to their duty in their own way.

    Bale Hounds say fuck that, I hate losing, I'm joining the side that wins in the end.

    They give up their duty, surrender to the opposition, and make a deal. There's a lot of resonance to that being a surrender to old-school vices, and I think it fits better with 2E's take on vice than people give it credit for, but I don't think it really needs to be anything other than what you're supposed to not do. To paraphrase, and forgive me for this - werewolves either die fighting or live long enough to find themselves becoming a Bale Hound. They're incredibly understandable, because they're what every werewolf could be if they stopped fighting the world and gave in and joined up.

    To project forward into the 2E paradigm, though, I think it's also important to think about what they hunt. Whether they're a tribe or not, they should have a specific kind of prey - maybe it's what their masters point them at, maybe it varies interestingly by Maeljin/Patron and trying to act out a vice on the hunt - and I think that however it works or varies, on some level their hunts are fundamentally selfish.

    Final point here: ideologically speaking, I think you want to compare them as much to the Seers as to the Scelesti. They're sellouts for power and respite and their own desires. You should always be able to place a Bale Hound in the context of who they're working for. You don't have a rat without the cops, you don't have Cypher without Agent Smith and the machines. Etc.

    ...that rambled, and I don't have time to concise-ify it, but I have feelings about the Bale Hounds.

        My one and only Bale Hound, while there was something selfish he hoped to gain, surrendered to the inevitable. The pitch he threw at the players was that the world was going to Hell in a handbasket, the Maejlin are going to win but we can help decide which one comes out on top. He had thrown his lot in with Gluttony, arguing that it would only eat 1/3rd of the population (I was basing the Maejlin on Persian myth) where as Wrath would lay waste to everything.
        ​I used my Bale Hound as a mentor because that is how I see them working, they are wolves in sheep's clothing (pun intended), the enemy hiding within, undermining and poisoning werewolf society. They are enemies with similar strengths that don't automatically get a 'kill' response like orcs meeting elves in Tolkien. This allows them to interact with the players before the killing starts.

                        Working for the Maeljin would require fulfilling the needs of that particular entity. If I was redesigning the Maeljin to work with Forsaken I'd have each Maeljin favor corrupting a particular tribe (9 Maeljin, one for each of the 8 Tribes plus Ghost Wolves). Some associations are easier to make than others, some need a little more mental effort to make the association. Here's an example list off the top of my head. Pride, Sloth, Wrath, and Violence seem pretty solid to me, the others might need to be shifted around a bit.

                        Wrath=Blood Talons
                        Lust=Iron Masters
                        Pride=Storm Lords
                        Envy=Ghost Wolves
                        Greed=Ivory Claws
                        Gluttony=Bone Shadows
                        Sloth=Hunters In Darkness
                        Deceit=Fire Touched
                        Violence=Predator Kings

                        So a Bale Hound from a particular Tribe would probably, but not always, serve the interests of the Maeljin that favors corrupting their particular Tribe.

                                                Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post


                                                This is the most biggest problem I have with this interpretation - What is real difference between those two? In the end you have both bloody soaked wolves...
                                                I'm not Darksider, but we're talking about Thurifuge, so I'll emerge from the shadows and throw my two cents

                                                The connection between them works not because of the end result but because of the reason those wolves went out looking for blood in the first place. While both tribes are the most "warrior badasses" of their respective side, there's a slight difference in attitude that makes the more vulnerable to a Maeljin rather than the other (we're talking in broad strokes, naturally)

                                                For Asmodai, blood is a natural consequence of wrath, but the point is not blood. It's reacting destructively to every bump on your road and take every perceived slight as a dire insult. Under Wrath, you hurt people because your blood runs hot-red and you don't even consider another approach to your problems. Wrath burns, it takes your thoughts away. In the warrior culture of the Blood Talons, it's far too easy to fall for that.

                                                Violence is different. Yes, when blood is spilled and people are hurt it empowers Thurifuge no matter what (it's part of the reason because he's so strong), but violence can be cold. Wrath might lead to violence, but Thurifuge also feeds on violence made for the sake of violence. The reasoned, planned, "now we go out and hurt them not because they wronged us but because it feels good" kind of thing. It's violence pursued in a exercise of power and superiority, done for the simple reason that you can. That brings us to the Predator Kings. They're warriors like the Blood Talons but the specific identity of their warrior ideal has a different focus, wherein violence is as much as glorified as strength. You're a predator, after all. Hurting prey is what you do. It's when this sort of grotesque sacred duty becomes more a personal pleasure than a holy crusade, when, pardon the pun, it loses its purity, that Thurifuge comes knocking

                                                Or at least that's how I see it. Darksider's reasoning might have been different.

                                                    I think second edition has changed the '9 Maeljin' idea.

                                                    But in first edition, Blasphemies covers the difference pretty well.

                                                    On Wraith, 'Rather than deny their primal anger as a separate part of themselves with no ties to their reasoning minds, the Hounds of Wrath accept the fury as sacred and valuable in all aspects of thought. They see it as part of the human as well as the beast; they gorge on anger and indulge in the rush. The Forsaken fear their anger and release it when the time is right. The Bale Hounds feed their anger and unleash it when the opportunity is ripe. Release is one thing, and unleashing quite another.'

                                                    And on Violence, 'To the Hounds of Violence, the very act of inflicting pain and suffering is sacred. Rather than hurt their enemies in the heated rage of true wrath, Thurifuge’s Hounds commit their sins in cold blood, and with obsessive, psychotic forethought. The act of violence itself feeds their Maeljin totem, and any fury or passion before the deed is just wasted energy.'

                                                    Though in regards with linking them to a specific tribe, I'd like to point this out, about Dire Wolf of the Predator Kings. 'Oldest and most ruthless of the spirit wolves, Dire Wolf was Urfarah’s savage nobility given form. But what appeared as wisdom and temperance was often tainted with spite, for Huzuruth — utmost predator among predators — is one of the few Firstborn touched with true malice. It is the malice of a creature that knows its own power and fears nothing. The malice eats into his very being, souring his great wisdom and tainting all who bond with him.'

                                                    My preference is that Bale Hounds are on a hunt for evil. To draw it out in others, in the land around them. To make spirits Maeljin-corrupt, to have other Uratha and vampires and supernaturals and humans 'give in.' The lazier can go after the weak and wounded, while the more fierce go after the pious and willful. Outside of the Hounds of Sloth, the Maeljin need life, so the hunt not requiring a kill to end it is good for them.
