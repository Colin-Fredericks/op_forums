    Basic Splat Interactions

    07-14-2025, 12:43 AM
    I have a few questions I would like to ask, but they're not so simple and I need eleven answers: one for each splat and every splat, even if the answer is obvious. Preferably with reference to books and pages where the answers can be found.

    1- Do they have normal blood a Vampire can drink? If not, what do they have in its place?
    2- Do they have a human Soul? In which state is this Soul?
    3- Can their Soul, Soul-like or substitute be removed? Which rules do they follow if Soulless?
    4- Do they dream as normal? What difference is there?
    5- Can they act as Demiurges? Can they leave Ghosts behind?
    6- What would normally be the biggest challenge for a Demon using one of them as Cover?
    7- Would it be possible for their regular powers to sometimes produce a Divergence? How?
    8 - How does the splat recovers Willpower (Anchors, sleep, everything)? How frequent and hard should recovery be?

    My plan is to compile the answers in this OP for anyone's future reference:

    Vampire
    - Blood: it causes addiction, Bond and transformation. It naturally satisfies a High Blood Potency Vampire;
    Obs.: The Unnatural Affinity Merit effectively makes the fluids of the target non-normal creature type to be drinkable and satisfy High BP.
    - Soul: it's harder to affect with magic than a mortal soul, but otherwise unremarkable (pending investigation on nature). Loosing it causes the Soulless Condition;
    - Dream: normal. They have an Oneiroi and connect to the Temenos and Anima Mundi as normal;
    - Ghost: it's unclear if the frequency is different from a mortal (either lower or higher), but happens;
    Obs.: While unlikely, the Embrace may produce a ghost. Producing a ghost doesn't affect the chances of the Embrace or other ways of becoming a Vampire.
    - Cover: a Demon passing as a Vampire will have to deal with the preternatural senses and Predatory Aura of Vampires, and is unlikely to fool them.

    Werewolf
    - Blood: normal human blood, although the ST may decide the spiritual essence within causes a minor effect;
    - Soul: it's harder to affect with magic than a mortal soul, but otherwise unremarkable (pending investigation on nature). Loosing it causes the Soulless Condition;
    - Dream: weird. Their Oneiroi is inhuman and does not connect to the Temenos, although it does connect to the Anima Mundi;
    - Ghost: it's unclear if the frequency is different from a mortal (probably lower), but happens;
    - Cover: if fooling their senses isn't hard enough, a Demon passing as a Werewolf will struggle with traversing to the Shadow.

    ​Mage
    - Blood: normal human blood;
    - Soul: it's harder to affect with magic than a mortal soul, but otherwise unremarkable (pending investigation on nature). Loosing it causes the Soulless Condition;
    - Dream: normal. They have an Oneiroi and connect to the Temenos and Anima Mundi as normal;
    - Ghost: it's unclear if the frequency is different from a mortal (either lower or higher), but happens;
    - Cover: a Demon can pass for a Mage as long as they avoid heavy scrutiny, but that's a loosing game in the long term.

    Changeling
    - Blood: normal human blood, although the ST may decide the Arcadian influence causes a minor effect;
    - Soul: it's harder to affect with magic than a mortal soul, but otherwise unremarkable (pending investigation on nature). Loosing it causes the Soulless Condition;
    Obs.: it's possible that removing a Changeling's soul will turn them into a Soulless Changeling instead of inflicting the normal Soulless Condition.
    - Dream: normal. They have an Oneiroi and connect to the Temenos and Anima Mundi as normal;
    - Ghost: it's unclear if the frequency is different from a mortal (probably lower), but happens;
    - Cover: on most cases it simply won't imitate their mien, being obvious to other Changelings.
    Obs.: If the mien is sufficiently similar to a human being, it may happen that the Cover imitates it, but not the Mask. Regardless, it never has both.

    Promethean
    - Blood: have Humours instead of human blood, although a Vampire may not notice nor be inconvenienced by this;
    Obs.: at the ST discretion, their blood may leak some Pyros. In this case, it is explosively dangerous.
    - Soul: it has Azoth in it's place, which can't be affected by effects that deal with souls, being immune to the Soulless Condition;
    Obs.: A once-Promethean human has a normal, unremarkable soul (pending investigation on nature). Loosing it causes the Soulless Condition.
    - Dream: weird. It doesn't have an Oneiros and doesn't connect to either the Temenos or Anima Mundi, but has a connection to the Divine Fire and has the potential for Elpis Visions;
    - Ghost: extremely unlikely to leave a Ghost due to their overall nature, but not impossible;
    - Cover: as they lack souls, a Demon can't acquire this Cover;
    - Demiurges: any intelligent creature may act as a Demiurge and create a Promethean, as long as they do so through Alchemy. The result will probably be Extempore.

    Hunter
    - Blood: normal human blood;
    Obs.: some Endowments may change their blood, with varying effects.
    - Soul: normal human soul, regardless of powers, Merits and Endowments;
    - Dream: normal. They have an Oneiroi and connect to the Temenos and Anima Mundi as normal;
    - Ghost: it's unclear if the frequency is much higher than normal, but it is at least the same as normal people;
    - Cover: it's unclear if a Cover can have some Endowments. Otherwise this is a feasible Cover.

    ​​Geist
    - Blood: has unnatural blood, still fresh, but dead. It's unclear if it satisfies a normal Vampire;
    - Soul: it has the Geist in it's place, which can't be affected by effects that deal with souls, being immune to the Soulless Condition;
    - Dream: weird. It doesn't have an Oneiros and doesn't connect to either the Temenos or Anima Mundi;
    - Ghost: it's extremely unlikely that one will form during a "normal" death, but not impossible. It's somewhat more likely upon definitive death, but still rare;
    - Cover: as they lack souls, a Demon can't acquire this Cover.

    ​​Mummy
    - Blood: has unnatural blood, still fresh, but dead. It's unclear if it satisfies a normal Vampire;
    - Soul: while human, their souls are the property of the Judges (or a possessing Shan'iatu) and can't be tampered with, being immune to the Soulless Condition;
    - Dream: normal. They have an Oneiroi and connect to the Temenos and Anima Mundi as normal;
    - Ghost: extremely unlikely to leave a Ghost due to their overall nature, but not impossible;
    - Cover: a Demon can't acquire their souls, and as such can't access this Cover.

    Demon
    - Blood: normal human blood while under a Cover, even if the Cover is a supernatural creature;
    Obs.: it's unclear if the Demonic Form has blood or only Aether, which can't sustain a normal Vampire.
    Obs.2: a Vampire with adequate Unnatural Affinity can feed from a Demon in any form or Cover and satisfy High BP.
    - Soul: they don't have a soul or anything in its place, being immune to the Soulless Condition;
    Obs.: it's unclear if the Cover of a Soul Pact has the original soul. If it has, then it can be manipulated as a normal human soul;
    Obs.2: Spoofing soul magic will return the impression that the Demon has a normal, unremarkable soul, but that successfully resisted any attempt to tamper with;
    Obs.3: under some, very unusual circumstances, a Demon may have the Soulless Condition applied to a Cover.
    - Dream: weird. It doesn't have an Oneiros and doesn't connect to either the Temenos or Anima Mundi;
    - Ghost: extremely unlikely to leave a Ghost due to their overall nature, but not impossible;
    Obs.: it's unclear if such a ghost would be in Demonic Form or under the Cover of death.
    - Cover: Demons trade in Soul Contracts, but once one assumes a Cover, it can't be taken by another, and as they lack souls, a Demon can't acquire this Cover.

    Beast
    - Blood: normal human blood, although the ST may decide the Horror within causes a minor effect;
    - Soul: it has the Horror in it's place, which can't be affected by effects that deal with souls, being immune to the Soulless Condition;
    - Dream: weird. The Beast fuses with the Horror to their Lair (or just goes to the Lair at Satiety 10). It doesn't have an Oneiros, but the Lair connects to the Temenos, other Lairs and Oneiroi in the region;
    Obs.: while the connection to the Anima Mundi is faint, the Beast is capable of traveling there through other innate powers, regardless.
    - Ghost: extremely unlikely to leave a Ghost due to their overall nature, but not impossible;
    Obs.: more common than leaving a ghost, Beasts can leave an unfettered Horror under some circumstances.
    - Cover: as they lack souls, a Demon can't acquire this Cover.

    ​​Deviant
    - Blood: normal human blood, although the ST may decide the specific Scars and Variations cause a minor effect;
    Obs.: some Scars and Variations affect their blood directly, usually making it dangerous.
    - Soul: while human, their souls are too broken and can't be tampered with, being immune to the Soulless Condition;
    - Dream: normal. They have an Oneiroi and connect to the Temenos and Anima Mundi as normal;
    - Ghost: it's unclear if the frequency is different from a mortal (probably lower), but happens;
    - Cover: a Demon can't acquire their souls, and as such can't access this Cover;
    ​​- Divergence: through either accident or experimentation, any supernatural creature may cause a Deviation in a normal human (and possibly even in a Hunter).
    Obs.: this process may result in any Origin or Clade, as normal. That said, Coactives and Mutants are more likely, and while it may be an accident, the obvious presence of a Progenitor makes Pathologicals less common in those cases.

      2 and 3 can be found in Night Horrors: Nameless and Accursed, in the "Souls of the Sixth Watchtower" sidebar on page 134; slightly more detailed information can be found in each splat's writeup in the "Crossover Considerations" section of the Player's Guide to the Contagion Chronicle, but in short:

      I) Vampires, werewolves, mages, and changelings have souls that can be interacted with through Awakened magic with greater difficulty than mortal/Sleeper souls; hunters have Sleeper souls for the purposes of Awakened magic regardless of what powers they may have developed.

      II) Prometheans, Sin-Eaters/Bound, demons, and Beasts do not have souls that can be interacted with through Awakened magic; the text is contradictory between Beast Core and the PGCC about whether there is any degree of exception allowing powerful Death magic to treat the Horror like a particularly difficult to reach soul, but considering that the Bargain holding a geist in the same metaphysical space for a Sin-Eater is immune and so is a Promethean's proto-soul I defer to the newer material; demons don't have their own souls, but calling in a soul pact makes the "destroyed" soul belong to the demon via the supernatural document, whereupon it can be interacted with through Awakened magic as though it were an Awakened soul.

      III) Mummies and deviants have souls but they cannot be tampered with due to their ownership by the Judges and their tremendous instability, respectively; a deviant with the Dedicated Form from Shallow Graves, which allows the character to have/retain an Integrity rating, loses its benefits in response to attempts to manipulate, destroy, or remove their soul.

      I'm not sure why 5 has those two questions in the same line, but Saturnine Night has a whole section from the 1e perspective detailing how any supernatural being might become a demiurge if they avoid using their supernatural powers for the aim, and 2e includes a line in its corebook introduction emphasizing that the strangeness of the Created is such that the printed systems are ultimately no more than tendencies in the long run.

      As far as ghosts go, historically the lines have been very loose about whether or not any sapient creature that isn't a mage or a mortal human can leave a ghost behind, but the 2e mechanics privilege humans with Integrity and objects and animals interacting with such humans when it comes to what does and doesn't create ghosts — you can magically create a ghost, but the majority of the text doesn't seem to assume ghosts of nonhuman entities to be less than atypical (you might take cues from Geist's observation that necromancers tend to leave geists behind when they die due to the deathly power they held in life, but as brutal as some monsters can be I can't say I'd view blood-soaked vampires and werewolves as equivalent to liches and the lingering obsessions of mortal ritual magicians).

      The answer to 7 is "Yes" for basically all of them due to the fact that even without accounting for sideways applications of their normal energies to produce a Coactive, Cephalists and Mutants are a perpetual source of odd Divergences as traumatic or allergic responses to the supernatural — much as the Begotten cannot guarantee the nighttime depredations of their Horrors will make more Beasts, the determining factor is in the "material" being acted upon.

              Number 5 (part 2) Do they leave ghosts: is unclear - however from Geist 2e - objects and animals leave ghosts behind by default, the rank of these (castoffs and Barghests) defaults to 0. If a human had an integrity break due to the loss/death/destruction of the entity, it starts at rank 1 instead. If the breaking point fails and the human loses integrity, then it starts at rank 2 (there is no rule for loss which creates multiple breaking points and hurts many humans, but also nó rules saying ghosts/Barghest/castoffs can't start at a higher rank).

              So there is an argument for all things leaving behind a ghost. Prometheans/Demons might use the same rules for starting rank as castoffs.

              Geist are already dead, so don't leave another ghost, but are a ghost riding around in their own body (with a Geist for a soul).

              Werewolves, Mages, Hunters, Deviants, and Changelings almost certainly leave ghosts. Probably vampires too. Infact a vampire might leave one ghost when embraced and a second ghost upon final death.

              Mages explicitly leave ghosts, ghost Mage rules have been a thing since 1e.

              As far as I know Mummys can not leave a ghost, can not be killed and will always returns to the service of the Judges. No way out for Mummys they are cursed, I might be wrong about that one.

              Generally, given that lots of things leave ghosts, I think just about everything does. The already dead things (Geist, Mummy, and potentially Vampires) may be exceptions.

                  Blood: Werewolves, mages, changelings, demons, beasts, most hunters, and most deviants have physiologically normal blood. The Unnatural Affinity Merit can make these count as satisfying a need from high Blood Potency to drink Kindred blood, excepting most hunters. Promethean blood, Bound blood and mummy blood is likely slightly anomalous, but in a way a vampire feeding wouldn't notice, and the Unnatural Affinity Merit works on them. Vampires have Vitae, which is distinct from ordinary human blood, and forms vitae addiction and the Vinculum when consumed.

                  Soul: Most hunters have ordinary human souls. Prometheans, Bound, demons, and beasts do not have souls. Mages have human souls that have linked to the supernal. Mummies have human souls that have been supercharged but chained to greater powers, preventing the soul from being stolen. Deviants have souls that have broken and fissured, and are too unstable to steal. Vampires, werewolves, and changelings have souls, but probably in a state one would not describe as an ordinary human soul. Soullessness has its normal consequences for anyone who normally has a soul and who loses that soul.

                  Dream: They all dream. Soulless beings do not have Oneiroi, and their dreams do not connect to the Temenos or Anima Mundi, with the exception of beasts, who fuse with their Horror in their astral Lair when they sleep. Werewolves have inhuman Oneiroi that do not connect to the Temenos.

                  Demiurges and ghosts: Everything can act as a demiurge, and everything can leave a ghost, but in the case of Bound and mummies, it would take an extraordinary situation to do so, since they normally survive death.

Diegetically, a ghost appears to be a sort of loss-imprint left behind by a traumatic absence (as per the suggestions that wild animals usually don't leave ghosts, but that beloved pets and otherwise famous or infamous animals are more likely to, similarly with buildings), and one might suppose that if you die and are assured resurrection, your death is less likely to leave a ghost because the trauma is less wrenching and permanent, and thus leaves a shallower imprint.

                          I don't leave it out entirely a) because weird shit like doppelgangers happens and it's nice to have room for rare edge cases, and b) a Bound who hits Synergy 0 can die permanently, and the rules section on p. 95 instructs you to discuss with your Storyteller whether your Bound lingers as a ghost. I take this phrasing to suggest that usually the default is no, but the option is left deliberately open for continued play.
