            Originally posted by Hermeticus View Post
            How does one determine what supernatural creatures and sorcerers a Mummy PC has in her Cult?
            Declaratively. Mummy's mechanics for being a sorcerer or a supernatural creature exist largely independent of the cult systems apart from the systems for Investment and creating a Sadikh; the cult's mechanics largely serve as a measure of its ability to act as a force multiplier for the mummy's activities, while sorcerers and supernatural creatures are modeled as individual characters who have the relevant Scorpion Cult Initiation.

            Can Arisen serving different Judges pool Cult dots for one larger Cult? How does this work with the Judge's Doctrine?
            For simplicity's sake I would assume the answer is yes if everyone is okay with one of those Judges taking the Doctrine slot and potentially poaching the mummies who don't already serve them. Anything more complex should be sorted out by the troupe.

                    At page 122 it is written "Once per scene, spending Pillar points raises a single Physical Attribute by a number of dots determined by a mummy’s current Sekhem rating"

                    It says points, not a single point or one point.
                    I think that the possible cases are:
                    -you can spend multiple points on the same attribute cumulating the boost, but you must spend all the points in one turn
                    -you can spend multiple points on the different physical attributes giving +4 to each, but you must spend all the points in one turn
                    -points is a typo, it should be written point

                    What do you think?
                    My understanding is similar to the first option you mentioned, without the second. Having a physical attribute as favourite for one's decree gives another option of boosting that attribute for the scene. So, if Dexterity is one's favourite attribute, with Sekhem 10 one can spend 5 pillar points on turn 1 to increase Dexterity by 20, and 5 more (from one's defining pillar) on turn 2 to increase it further by 5, but no more. If base Dexterity was 10, that's a total maximum of 35 for the scene.

                            Originally posted by Hermeticus View Post
                            Does anyone see a possible link between the Urfader in Denmark story (p.290, MtC2e) and the third origin story for the Gangrel (p.18, VtR2e)? Has either story been expanded upon anywhere? Has any connection between them been published?
                            None of Requiem's 2e's allegorical "adjectivally ever after" origin stories for the clans are expanded upon, and apart from Olivia Hill's Tokyo writeups there's little in the way of additional detail provided for any gameline's 2e sample settings outside the corebooks they're introduced in.

                            The Urfader is a historic figure that is unambiguously one of the Arisen as far as the book is concerned, being notable for having been found in a bog with his remains in a state where the usual reactive awakening from disturbance has not occurred, possibly because of the binding ritual from his last observed Descent. The Gangrel's fairy tale origin concerns a man who was eaten by beasts and swallowed, then ejected, by the earth, in direct allusion to the clan's power over animals and ability to merge with soil; the general ambiguity of the story means it could concern a man becoming a vampire or a vampire becoming Gangrel, but Mummy 1e was specific that natural animals will not eat the corpse of an Arisen and 2e doesn't describe any abilities of the Arisen that remotely sound like "control animals by feeding bits of yourself to them" either generally or as things attributed to the Urfader.

                            You could make something up to connect the two, but with the current text available it'd be quite a reach.

                                        Originally posted by Stupid Loserman View Post

                                        The Gangrel story is about something prehistoric from the ice ages settling underneath a bog, demanding sacrifices, and then disgorging one as a thing that feeds on death with bog water in its veins.
                                        The Urfader story is about a mummy who was conveyed to Denmark, and who seemingly ended one Descent through an attempted hanging and being left in a muddy tomb that was found in a bog.

                                        You could certainly decide to thread these together, but taken on their own merits, they don't share much beyond ancient unliving things being dredged from bogs. The "ice sarcophagus" in the Gangrel story is a figure of speech, not a literal sarcophagus—and besides, Urfader wasn't preserved in ice anyway.
                                        The Urfader story also involves sacrifice, to an unknown end, maybe to help end Urfader's Descent.

                                        Taken on their own merit, they are indeed two stories of two unliving beings in bogs. There are plenty of bogs and bog bodies in Northern Europe, so they could be unrelated. But it may he interesting to speculate that they are related.

                                        Bogs may form from drained lakes created by glaciers, so the fact that Urfader wasn't preserved in ice isn't necessarily the case: he may have been and then the place became a bog over time. More importantly, it's not known how Urfader got to Denmark, only that three North African men (who may or may not have brought him there) woke him up, were killed, villages were attacked, and Urfader's Descent ended with him having a noose around his neck and 30 people died in a ritual fashion, presumably at that time (the book doesn't specify that the scientific dating of Urfader's remains corresponds to that of the 30 sacrifices, but it's implied since it's hypothesized they died in an attempt to end the mummy's Descent).
                                        There's also the mystery of why Urfader has slept through the Sothic Turns.

                                        I think it's unlikely that the Su-Menent, given their powers, would misidentify a mummy, so I keep the idea that Urfader is indeed an Arisen. It makes sense also with the Sekhem and cultist manipulation that are part of his story. But maybe the Sekhem-rich bog has not only produced human cultists over the ages. Maybe at some point it produced something else. Maybe, given the non-uniform origins of the vampire clans, it produced that "first bog body" that only death can nourish, and "preserves some of the dead it swallows, pickling the corpses with the black bog water in its veins."

                                        Maybe the Sekhem attracted Shades on the Neter-Khertet side of the bog. Maybe something happened there, perhaps with a complicit Urfader, to produce something new, so linked to the ground that it can melt into it and grant this ability to others by sharing its blood - preserving them forever (which makes me also think of Resilience).

                                        Maybe Urfader never awoke in the hanged body during the Sothic Turns because he found another way to return, perhaps having invested the first bog body with the Rite of Investment before that body's death, and corrupting it with the influence of the Shades. Basically an invested new vampire then turned into Urfader's body and the first Gangrel. A terrifying vampire-mummy hybrid produced by interaction with the mysterious Shades.

                                        Or maybe Urfader didn't awake because he traded Sekhem to the Shades for whatever allowed the creation of an Invested new vampire (this time not housing Urfader's soul), so the Shades have been suckling Sekhem on the Neter-Khertet side of the bog, slowing Urfader's resurrection compared to other mummies.

                                        Last but not least, considering the possibility that a mummy might descend even before the foundation of Irem due to their weird relationship to Time, Urfader might have gotten into the ice that then turned into a lake that then turned into a bog even during or at the beginning of the last Ice Age. But I don't see much point in this from a narrative point of view, it's just weird and quirky, while positing an Arisen-related Gangrel clan origin allows for a meaningful interaction between some Scandinavian Gangrel and Urfader's cultists when he wakes up, bringing an unexpected vampire twist to the stories of the Scandinavian Arisen.

                                        None of this is necessary - they can be two unrelated story. But the fun thing about CoD is having all these story hooks and unclear origins of supernatural creatures to be able to play around with it all.

                                                Originally posted by Yumlum View Post
                                                When was Irem founded? And when did it fall?
                                                "Sothic Turn Zero," when the Arisen were made, is approximately 3832 BCE, four 1461-year Sothic Turns before Mummy's original release year of 2012 (itself core to a lot of apocalyptic beliefs at the time).

                                                1e notes that most of the Arisen were created on or within a century of Sothis (aka the Dog Star Sirius) naturally appearing with the first dawn of the Ancient Egyptian year, and obviously no new Arisen were being created after the fall of the Nameless Empire, but we do not otherwise have a concrete timeline for one of the central mysteries of the game.
