    Help on a Abyssal cult idea.

    04-16-2017, 09:43 PM
    I thought up a cult to use in a mage chronicle. They are a agricultural firm named Demeter Inc. They look for ways to artificially grow crops and other agricultural products. But, they have a secret they wish to keep buried. They serve the Abyss and it's eldritch inhabitants. They are led by a scalesti by the name of Alfred Oglethrop,a Englishman who wants to use the company to spread abyssal taint through it's produce.

    I need help detailing how they spread taint through crops and detailing their Mystery Cult merits.

      I mean, the obvious thing with spreading taint through crops is "if you eat the crops, you're infected". Maybe eating the crops slowly mutates you into an abyssal monster, or opens you up to possession. More subtly, perhaps it increases the amount of "abyssal energy" in the person, making them count more towards helping paradoxes (Or more vulnerable to paradoxes if they're mages). This leads to an increase in abyssal activity in the area but it would require you spending some effort to figure out it's the new cheap food behind it.

      Alternately, it could be the plants themselves that links to the abyss. Maybe they naturally grow in glyphs, and every new moon another terrible thing crawls out of the cornyards. Or maybe they corrupt the ecosystem, replacing earthly animals and plants with abyssal versions. It can start out subtle near the edges, just weirdly-mutated plants and animals, and as you get closer to the centre of the artificial habitat it turns into an abyssal nightmare jungle.

              *considers* The wheat is sentient! By eating the wheat, you slowly become smarter, stronger, faster etc - not because of any nutritional value but because of the sentience that lays inside all living things. Taking this antitruth is easy to take one step further when you realize how many other things are sentient as well - perhaps you could gather power from other sources?

              No seriously though it would be easier to help if you defined what the Abyssal Cult believed and what they follow. I have one cult for instance which follows an entity known as the Silence, and they are big on the idea that there is too much noise, so no one can hear anything that is being said - by god, by the people around them, etc - and if the world was engulfed in pure Silence then there would be a world where everyone truly understood one another.

              Having an idea of what kind of direction this Abyssal cult was going would definitely help determine what benefits the cult gave

                  Another thing to remember is that the Abyss is the home of the "impossible", not merely corruption and decay and evil. They could be leveraging some beneficial Abyssal property like 2ptTakrill's grow-anywhere wheat, some heals-anything lettuce, or makes-you-stronger fruit, etc. Just as long as what it does does not work that way. The side-effects, of course, are completely unintentional, and will certainly be eliminated given enough time and research into the effects... as they assure you.

                  Demeter Inc, eh? What about plants that can grow in impossibly nutrient poor soil, low temperatures, and with little to no light - ideal for winter conditions. Because that is where they come from, an Abyssal reality where the world is covered in eternal winter. The only problem is enough concentrations of these plants (and their material - it persists for some time, even after being digested) brings that "reality" closer - temperatures start to fall, light grows fainter, etc.

                  Hey, maybe the side-effect are a fringe benefit, Demeter Inc reasons - combat hunger and global warming in one go!

                  If only it didn't frequently spawn the Abyssal version of Ghosts (as opposed to Acamoth and Gulmoth) so often...

                          So, here's an interesting feature of plants: Totipotency. Every plant cell, bar a few specialized, non-nucleated ones, can dedifferentiate into a totipotent stem cell in response to environmental signaling. This is why you can, say, take a cutting of a plant and grow a new one from it.

                          What if their goal really is benevolent and they want to use plants for good? Wheat that can grow on concrete, or completely without water. You can grind it up into flour and eat it with no ill effects.

                          And it's true. You can.

                          Until someone on a health kick decides they want whole grain.

                          Then, suddenly, you have people dying in agony as their flesh becomes fertilizer for the plant. Worse, turns out that this isn't something the plants will pass up. Suddenly you have vegetable zombies stalking the land, force-feeding people lettuce.

                          And out in the fields, there the corpses gather to bow in worship of He Who Walks Between the Rows.
