    The perception roll question is a good one. I really don't like how we made perception work in Storytelling, and trying to bundle other rules onto it is really hard. As an ST, I usually just don't ask for those rolls.

    The system as a whole really needs something like D&D's Passive Perception, where the thing that's concealing itself is rolling against you statically in the same way as attack versus defense. But that introduces other problems, if your goal as ST is to provide the experience of simply not finding anything.

    I think I'd try adding a sinister layer to the information gathered. It makes you hungry, or afraid, or angry -- things the Beast responds to.

                    Personally I'm not a big fan of rpgs that enforce standards of morality. I always liked the idea that Humanity is a measure of how much the Beast has taken control of the vampire. Doing things that tap into the Beast, or give the Beast more control over the body, or prove that the character is no longer human, should be what lower Humanity. Using Disciplines, cutting oneself off from human society, particularly aggressive forms of feeding, surviving situations that would have killed a normal human, etc. Low Humanity doesn't necessarily mean low morality, it just means the vampire sees itself as no longer being part of the human race any more and starts seeing humans as nothing more than toys and a food source.

Using Disciplines, cutting oneself off from human society, particularly aggressive forms of feeding, surviving situations that would have killed a normal human, etc. Low Humanity doesn't necessarily mean low morality, it just means the vampire sees itself as no longer being part of the human race any more and starts seeing humans as nothing more than toys and a food source.
                        I don't see Humanity as morality, quite. Starting from here...

                        Originally posted by Vampire: The Requiem, p.106
                        However, to blend in with mortals, to walk among the flock, Kindred must maintain perspective and understanding of their former lives.
                        But that begs the question of what the Beast is. Could we replace the falling scale of Humanity with a rising scale of Beastlyness?

                        I actually asked Rich that, once, and he got really annoyed with me. His argument was that the Beast is a scary philosophical idea that shouldn't have stats. So going from that, Humanity 7 wouldn't be Beast 3.

                        Developer emeritus Will Hindmarch proposed that the Beast doesn't oppose the Man -- the former actually uses the latter. A high Humanity vampire in frenzy would do horrible things... but with great intelligence. This would have made it more like the Other, from Sonja Blue and the Requiem Chronicler's Guide. And I rather liked that.

                        The Savage and the Macabre says that the Beast isn't an animal, that it's something worse, something evil in a way that can't be defined materialistically. The 2e core implies that the Beast is a way of talking about a vampire's survival instincts and their expression through the predatory aura, but that doesn't rule out the idea of supernatural evil.

                        And then there's a threnody in Sacraments and Blasphemies that turns your Beast into a physical being...

                        ...so there are a lot of possibilities.

                        That said, I have no problem with Vampire judging characters. The Chronicles of Darkness have a running theme that exploitation and the misuses of power are deplorable things, and Vampire is where all that started.
