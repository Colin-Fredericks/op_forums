    [Idea] Mortal Communities In The Hedge?

    06-23-2014, 12:44 PM
    Consider the following:
    A mortal human is backpacking somewhere not too close to any large community. He's got all his gear with him, and the terrain he's exploring is beautiful-so breathtakingly beautiful, in fact, that it doesn't quite register to him when he walks through a hollowed-out old tree or disturbs the wrong fairy ring and winds up in the Hedge.

    Now, there are a lot of directions this could go-and the most likely ones in a story are probably "the hiker is taken by an Other," "the hiker eats a poisonous goblin fruit/meets an unfriendly hobgoblin and kicks the bucket," or "the hiker runs across a group of player characters who rescue him." But what if none of those things happen? At some point, he'll have to realize he's lost, but maybe he's been going backpack-camping since he was 11 and decides that he's got nothing to worry about, he's been in this situation before and everything sorted itself out fine, and he'll just roll with it and set up camp somewhere around here (wherever here is...) The Hedge, responding to his quiet confidence, leads him to a relatively safe space, an uninhabited cave or a copse in the thorns with enough space to comfortably set up a tent and a fire for the night. Against all odds, he lasts the night, and the day after it too-maybe he eats the poisonous hedge-berries but yacks them an hour later and learns from his mistake, maybe a briarwolf does come after him but he brought a hatchet along and buries it right in the thing's snout, scaring it off.

    Point is, he makes it a lot longer than any of us would expect him to. Learns the lay of the land, to a certain extent. And at some point, he runs into a woman-not antlered or ablaze or with luminous skin like the only other things resembling people he's seen here (always from hiding) but a normal woman, one who looks normal and talks normal and has a normal reaction to all the abnormality of this place they've ended up in. She thinks she's been here for two days, and she's hungry, but he can show her at least a few edible plants, and she has a lighter which makes building a fire so much easier, and they band together out of both loneliness and necessity. And after that, maybe they find a third.

    It'll never be easy. The Hedge's effect on Morality means that such a group could easily descend into infighting and backstabbing. The Hedge mostly lacks electricity, limiting the technological scope of any settlement there. But for some, it's a worthwhile trade. Anarcho-primitivist idealists might rejoice seeing a chance to become entirely self-sufficient, removing themselves from the capitalist equation that is ubiquitous in the mortal world. Survivalists and paranoids see a possible new habitat for the remainder of humanity once the bombs have dropped or the sea levels have risen or whatever doomsday scenario they favor has come to pass. Creatives, the young, and the clinically depressed all might find relief in even the dangerously surreal aspect of the Hedge, liberated from the comforting boredom of a world that makes logical sense and placed in one of the fantastic ones they always dreamed of. Maybe they even learn how to work one or two gateways, and are able to send some members back to the mortal world to buy necessities otherwise unavailable to them (gas for a stove, batteries for flashlights, cooking supplies) and let them find their way back. Maybe they plant things and either learn to cultivate beneficial goblin fruits or unwittingly create a new breed when they attempt to transplant strawberries from someone's mortal garden. Maybe they bring in animals-some of those will die, and the ones that don't will of course change, but those will tend to remain friendly to the human settlement as long as none of its inhabitants freak out over the dog's coat turning the mottled green of the thorns and an extra tail dividing itself from the first one. Until an Other or a truly powerful Hobgoblin discovered it, a small community could potentially survive for a very long time in the Hedge.


    ...Now, at this point all I have is this fluffy stuff. How do you think you would incorporate such a community into a game of Changeling?

      Well ... if my memory serves me correctly, a normal dude who gets trapped on the other side of the Thorns is not in for a fun time. The psychoactive, soul shredding nature of the Hedge mutates and twists those creatures that are not properly abjured by the Wyrd. If the dude somehow manages to not get eaten or twisted by Arcadian mutations, I can imagine them becoming a changeling of a kind (maybe with the Fae touched merit, so they still look normal) or deteriorating into a hobgoblin! The Hedge is a horrifying place and it is governed by rules of cause and effect that work contrary to human perspectives, it isn't a place that normal peeps (even if they are creative) can find any kind of solace.

      But that being said ... let's say that due to some handwavium a human community somehow survives, untainted, in the depths of the Hedge ... I can imagine the community sorta devolving into a claustrophobic society ruled by bizarre superstition (which is the science of the Hedge's physics) and unyielding law. Like the Puritans. They would remain ever vigilant against any signs of witch's madness or fairy enchantments, and accusations of associating with goblins, changelings or fairies all bear the punishment of death by iron if they are found to have merit. They will undoubtedly be inbred. Have you seen that movie, The Village? Not that great of a movie, but good inspiration for this line of thought.

      Their extreme faephobia and unyielding conviction would make them a natural enemy of the freehold, but their isolation would make them a distant threat. Maybe, like the Gentry that they hate so much, the citizens of this Hedge town sneak out to the mortal world to kidnap an unsuspecting person every once in awhile in order to keep the "iron in the blood." The younger the victim, the easier to indoctrinate into the town's bizarre culture ... ideally, they prefer infants. How do the Courts react when they learn that these "Hedge-Men," not fairy lords and ladies, are responsible for the recent uptick in child abductions in the city?

                                      I remember a section in Mortal Remains that states that while extremely unlikely it is possible, given enough time, research, and willpower, for a mortal to enter Arcadia. If that's possible I see no reason for your hypothetical Hedge Village to be any less possible. Although there's also a moniker to the aforementioned section saying that while possible the mortals in question would still have to deal with the consequences of going to Arcadia, and so too would your "Hedge People" have to deal with the consequences of living in the Hedge for years on end.

                                      If I were to use your Hedge Village in a Chronicle I'd have the player's Motley stubble upon it purely by the grace of the Wyrd after a particularly violent confrontation with a rival Motley or Privateers. The settlement would be styled after the one from The Beach, another terrible movie yet a surprisingly accurate example of what you're essentially describing, wherein the humans inhabit a pristine Hollow where they've created their own version of paradise but are driven to drastic means of keeping their "Paradise" a secret for their own protection. The players would be wounded from their previous fight and now stuck in the middle of hostile territory, surrounded by normal humans that are convinced they're Hedge Beasts or the Others. To make matters worse the players would be the unwitting catalyst for the Village, after years of having their Morality slowly degrade as they ignorantly indulged in their Vices, to finally erupt into chaos as long building tension is finally given a means of release. Add a dash of panic as the ensuing chaos alerts a nearby True Fae to the Villages presence and the Motley will have one heck of a challenge, both physically and morally, to overcome. After all, anything is better than going back to Arcadia...right?
