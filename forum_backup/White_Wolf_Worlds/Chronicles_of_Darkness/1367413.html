    What makes a Bloodline a Bloodline?

    02-13-2020, 03:38 AM
    I recently got my hands on a copy of the new Spilled Blood book, as well as copies of Bloodlines: The Resurrected and The Devoted. I always thought Bloodlines were meant to be small groups of Kindred who possessed a special quirk to their Vitae that gave them access to unique powers, typically in the form of Disciplines and Devotions available only to members of the particular Bloodline, but I'm seeing a lot of Bloodlines in the three books that don't match this description. In fact they tend to be little more than small groups of Kindred that share nothing more than an ideology. Take the Ankou for example. What makes them a Bloodline? They're basically just a group of vampires who wish to maintain the balance between humans and vampires. There isn't really anything that makes them stand out from their parent clan. If a shared ideology is enough to make a Bloodline then why aren't the Brood a Bloodline. They're basically the same thing. The brood even get some unique toys to play with while the Ankou get nothing. In Resurrected and Devoted there are a handful of Bloodlines that are basically all just different kinds of vampire necromancers. Isn't that like saying the Lancea or Circle should just be a collection of Bloodlines that all have access to their forms of blood magic? Am I missing something? Are Bloodlines not what I'm thinking they are? Are the people who are writing these Bloodlines getting them confused with Mystery Cults or something?

      Bloodlines have never been a singular concept. They apply to certain dynasties of Kindred, groupings based around ideologies and philosophies, and anything in-between. If you look at the 1e Covenant books you'll indeed see that each Covenant does contain multiple bloodlines almost exclusively found within that specific Covenant.

          I think the idea is that bloodlines could be any of those things and you as a storyteller can choose what interpretation is appropriate for your chronicle. At the base level a bloodline is a vampire who has refined his blood to confer *some* special advantage or disadvantage, so by that token, the Ankou qualify with their added Vigor discipline, which is all they really need to do their job.

          It feels like the clans or bloodlines were written explicitly to be easy convertible from one type to the other. Like, very little is stopping you from making the Amari or Nhand a bloodline instead of a clan, or demoting the Ankou to just a wing of the Invictus. It feels like a toolkit showcase rather than supplemental canon, which I rather like.

          My ideas are bad and I should feel bad.

              Bloodlines can have their own little cultures; they've always had a bit of a cultish quality. Remember that, aside from Kindred who are brought into a bloodline by their sires after the Embrace, most vampires join their bloodlines later in their Requiems. You choose to change your blood if you believe in the cause enough. The clans are large enough that you can't really say "Oh, the Mekhet are a Carthian clan", but bloodlines are by their nature fairly small, and are often focused around their founders' and their political machinations. If grandsire says be in the Invictus, you better damn well be in the Invictus.

              From an out of character perspective, bloodlines are kind of the way Requiem does sub-factions of both clans and covenants (though there are covenant groups that aren't bloodlines). It's a little less elegant than some of the other games and their "z-splats", but vampires are also more tied by blood relationships than the other monsters; politics, religion, and family always end up in the mix.

              Edit: Also, as much as I appreciate how much people enjoy Bloodlines: The Devoted and Resurrected, they're basically glorified homebrew. We put a lot of work into them, and they definitely fit into my aesthetic as a Requiem fan/writer, but they don't necessarily reflect how bloodlines work in official supplements (although we certainly try not to take them too far from the baseline).

                                      I don't mind the covenant-style bloodlines or the ones without specific enumerated powers. My conception of a bloodline is that a Vampire (at high enough BP) made a change to their blood for a purpose, and that their bloodline should thematically embody that purpose through their bane, favored attributes, and Disciplines/Merits/Devotions. Ankou seem perfectly appropriate by that definition. Not all Vampires are going to go out of their way to craft super unique special powers. VtR isn't Mage

                                      Like with the Parliamentarians, could I imagine a Carthian so absolutely committed and dedicated to his obsessive pestering and argumentation that he took that change to his blood? I sure could. I like that the document specifies that even though they're exclusively created as part of the Carthian movement, the other covenants try to recruit them out of it.

                                      And if anything, gating a power through a Covenant actually still keeps the possibility for spill-out. Gating a power through a Clan actually makes it virtually inaccessible to 4/5 of all Vampires (.. or 19/20 if you're counting all the rare Clans :P).

                                              Originally posted by Kvark View Post
                                              I'm enjoying reading your thoughts about this, Tessie. Am I understanding your preference correctly that you hoped every Bloodline (or almost every) would come with a unique fourth Discipline or be locked only to a single clan?
                                              No. I merely refuted that we had reason to believe that the design philosophy for Bloodlines would steer away from unique Disciplines when those were the only non-experimental examples we had prior to Spilled Blood.

                                              It's not that I don't like the wildly different places a Bloodline can take you. What I dislike is how Bloodlines represents a wide swath of concepts that all have to fight for the same space on the character sheet. Embraced to a particular dynasty? Well now you can never join this Covenant subgroup, or suffer from this horrible curse, or make a deal with this demon, or develop a Bloodline of your own, etc.
                                              I'm also not a fan of most Bloodlines being Clan exclusive. I guess it's thematic, but I don't think it being thematic makes up for how restrictive it is for both players and ST. (I hear that Spilled Blood has opened up that a bit, but back in 1e it was so bad that even the Moroi had a text explaining that it was actually just two identical Bloodlines; one for Gangrels and one for Nosteratu.)

                                              What I would've preferred is a division of "dynastic" Bloodlines and "social" Bloodlines, as well as other "quirks of the Blood". Let Kindred dynasties have their own little quirks (like a fourth non-unique Discipline or affinity with certain Skills) that reinforces the themes of blood ties. Open up Covenant subgroups (or other social/ideological Bloodlines) for membership from all Clans by leveraging access to their advantages (whether they be uncommon Disciplines, Devotions or even Merits) with responsibilities and social bonds. And let the really weird shit stay weird and not force it into the same old Bloodline model as everything else.

                                                      The bloodlines in Spilled Blood almost exactly follow Rose's guide. That can't be a coincidence.

                                                      Originally posted by Rose Bailey
                                                      In first edition, bloodlines were meant to be a make-your-own- clan system. But we were somewhat confused on whether they were “prestige classes” you advanced into, societies you joined, or things you inherited.
                                                      They should have the potential to be all three. But that means that adding a five-dot Discipline for every one doesn’t make sense.

                                                      Take, for example, the Macellarius. This gruesome and very fun Ventrue (or are they Julii?) bloodline is distinguished by eating flesh. This isn’t a trick you should have to buy dot by dot, it should be a big jump in how your vampirism works.
                                                      Or the Galloi, blood-bathers whose strange beauty should really be a base power and then twists on Majesty.
                                                      The luck-manipulation of the Bogahande fits into a Discipline ladder, but why does someone cursed/blessed with it have to buy in? The solution is simply to not have all bloodlines work the same. But there are still best practices.

                                                      Most bloodlines should have one to two of the following:
                                                      • Modifications to their core vampiric abilities
                                                      • Special Devotions
                                                      • A new Discipline
                                                      • Merits relating to the social benefits/effects of bloodline membership

                                                      My ideas are bad and I should feel bad.
