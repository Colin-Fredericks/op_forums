            Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
            Will Hoards, Provinces and Maidens offer different abilities like Pillars?
            I hadn't considered it, though it'd be very easy to attach a 'resonance'-esque feature to Breath in regards to proclamation of Edicts. Did you have any ideas?

            EDIT: By resonance-esque, I mean perhaps Edicts that influence people would require Breath from a Province, while Edicts that manipulate objects would require Breath from a Hoard. I'd give specific examples but I don't have any good references on hand at the moment.


            Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
            Also, has any comments come of what I said in the last thread? That Honor should be safe if you do bad things in the name of getting stuff done, but not if you do it for selfish reasons or incompetently. For example, a public execution is no risk if it reduces dissent, but it is a problem if you just disliked the guy or if you're trying to reduce dissent but you're incompetent and end up fostering more unrest.
            I'm thinking the only sins against Honor will be:
            • failure to protect a Hoard
            • failure to protect a Province
            • personally damaging the Province (including scandals)
            • acting against an Ideology (forgot to list it as a type of source in the earlier post)
            • poor etiquette (break a promise, do not respond to an invitation, refuse a challenge, flee from a fight, and other actions unfitting for a proper citizen of high culture)
            • consuming another Oroboroi Heart (in addition to new Devourer rules)
            • and dying (I'll explain later)

            Note there's nothing specific about murder. If a Dragon can commit murder--even a public execution--without violating good etiquette or her possessions then she gets away without a single scratch to her Honor.


            As for ideologies, well... Let's pretend Cameron has been at it for a while and has subjected herself to a new code of ethics.

            Vegetarian <-- Ideology
            O
            []

            Ideologies fill the gap that has been left behind by the absence of Morality. A dragon can refresh her breath within an Ideology by deciding not to stray from her beliefs when confronted with a situation. For example, a dragon who possesses the Ideology "Do Not Kill" would refresh her breath in said ideology when she chooses to spare the man that was to be publicly executed.

            I'm sort of on the fence on this one. By including Ideologies we open up the option to use ANY NOUN to describe a source. But it doesn't seem as functional as the others. Are there any thoughts on this?

                        @ReeaperFrost
                        Unfortunately as Greenhorn and True Heart are Philosophies they are being discarded in the philosophy rewrite, as the new Y-Splats will emphasize styles of government. I could see perhaps writing a "hapless" pseudo-philosophy that represents characters that haven't settled into their style yet.
                        True Heart is currently on the fence. I can make it work, but it will take some doing. Any which way it will be independent of Philosophy.

                        @TheKingsRaven
                        On personally damaging the Province:
                        Sounds good. I'll make sure to present specific cases so that it's clear that the sin means "Actions that impede The Greater Good" not "Any Petty Infraction"

                        On Ideologies:
                        Well what I wanted to emphasize was that--in addition to her obligations as an Oroboros--a dragon is still responsible for herself and her beliefs. As I think on it, though, the Virtue mechanic handles that idea sufficiently and it doesn't need the support.
                        "What I was going to do anyway" is dangerous not only because it's likely to happen, but because THAT WOULD BE A VALID IDEOLOGY! So let's just toss the idea and stick to the School House Rock definition of nouns.

                        On Demanding Sources:
                        While I can see the logic of making a Province's scope more demanding as Furnace rises, and while I can predict how we would similarly impose such Furnace penalties on a Hoard, I'm just not sure how we'd apply the idea to Maidens. Your thoughts?

                                Originally posted by CJC View Post
                                While I can see the logic of making a Province's scope more demanding as Furnace rises, and while I can predict how we would similarly impose such Furnace penalties on a Hoard, I'm just not sure how we'd apply the idea to Maidens. Your thoughts?
                                Oh that's pretty easy. In fact there's two different ways but my prefered solution is to do both of them.

                                1) All Maidens have a "qualification", why it's worth singling them out.

                                At low furnace "eh, I kinda like them" is qualification enough.

                                At medium furnace they need to have two+specality or three dots in a skill that catches your eye. An appropriate Merit (Striking Looks). Or maybe they have to be a literal virgin.

                                At high furnace they need 4/5 dots. A rarer or more potent merit. Or have stats that reinforces their intangible qualification, such as a virtue or striking looks that plays to medieval stereotypes of virginal purity.



                                2) It's how much control you have over them.

                                At low levels you just need to be part of their social circle.

                                At medium levels they must depend on you. You pay their bills, are their manager or something, and they know this.

                                At high levels they basically live in your giant-dragon-palace and can't step outside without your permission. Total control, though gilding cages is recommended. If your Maidens don't look happy and well cared for that reflects badly on your Honor as a dragon. Who'd trust someone who can't keep his house in order to run a city?


                                This could mean that at high levels you're mechanically distanced from your Maidens; so to prevent that a possiblity is for Maidens to have a trait (probably their maximum Breath) that directly scales with how long you can keep them for and how desirable they might be to other Dragons. Though I must say I like the idea that as you get older you loose interest in your old Maidens but still won't give them up.

Deep Ones and Hunters who are attempting to destroy a heart would not have the same respect.

                                        In short: If something or someone is attempting to hasten the race's extinction, then it would be called a "Barbarian".

                                            On a different note, I had a cool idea for Edicts in the new edition.
                                            One of the major criticisms for Edicts was the empty dots between laws, which only serve to increase the effect rating of a proclamation (essentially giving it one more use within the scene). My hotfix for this issue was an idea I called Ordinances, wherein a character could specify on purchasing an even dot a 'specialty' target for her Edict, which would further increase the effect rating if subjected to a proclamation.

                                            I had a better idea just now, though, and I wanted to pitch it.

                                            Instead of the Law-Empty-Law pattern, dots purchased in an Edict would serve a specific purpose. From 1 to 10, that purpose is:

                                            Veto-Law1-Law2-2Combo-Law3-BreathGate-Law4-3Combo-Philibuster-Freeform

                                            I'll walk through each below:

                                            Veto
                                            When an Oroboros learns the first dot of an Edict, she acquires the universal law of Veto for that Edict. She may now detect whenever someone or something is under the effect of a proclamation in the Edict, and can make a proclamation herself to reverse said effect. For example, someone enthralled by one of the laws of Assembly might be freed from the supernatural compulsion by another Oroboros proclaiming Veto with Assembly 1.
                                            Veto starts off very limited in its application as effect rating is likely to be quite low. However, as knowledge in the Edict grows it becomes a very potent shield for constituents against an enemy Oroboros.

                                            Law 1
                                            The Oroboros may choose one of the laws available within the Edict (I'm thinking I'll set up a spread of 5 for each, reducing the total number of Edicts but making each more versatile). Each law is roughly equivalent in potency and--compared to other templates--stands as a level 2 power. Knowing the law, the Oroboros may make proclamations using said law.

                                            Law 2
                                            With the third dot, the Oroboros obtains a second law from the remaining ones available. At this point, the system is identical to the one in the Second Stage PDF except for the addition of Veto.

                                            2Combo
                                            The Oroboros may now proclaim both laws in a single action by spending two Breath, rather than breaking the proclamations into separate actions. This has the advantage of proclaiming two laws at the same Effect Rating.
                                            Any combination of Two laws is moderately useful and equivalent to another system's level 4 power.

                                            Law 3
                                            Just like Law 2.

                                            BreathGate
                                            Each Edict requires a particular kind of Breath to be utilized, such as Breath from a Province in the case of Assembly. But once an Oroboros reaches dot 6 within her Edict, that restriction is lifted and she may use Breath of any type to make proclamations in said Edict.

                                            Law 4
                                            The Oroboros learns one more law within the Edict. Note that it is impossible to learn all the laws within a single Edict. This shortcoming can be circumvented with a Graft, but that's a topic for another post

                                            3Combo
                                            Just like 2Combo, only now the Oroboros may choose to proclaim three of her laws in a single action by spending three Breath. Again, this has the advantage of proclaiming three laws at the same Effect Rating (which by the way is quite high by dot 8).
                                            Any combination of Three laws is extremely useful and equivalent to another system's level 8 power.

                                            Philibuster
                                            For 1 Breath per turn, the Oroboros may prevent any other dragon from making proclamations in the Edict, including Veto proclamations.

                                            Freeform
                                            Ever read Scion: God? You know how the final dot of epic attribute allows the character to take narrative control of the scene, within the purview of the attribute described? Well Freeform law proclamation is like that. For 10 Breath, the player may describe--within the general theme of the Edict in question--exactly how she completely deconstructs a conflict (or creates one). Have fun with this, it's a level 10 power.


                                            Thoughts?
