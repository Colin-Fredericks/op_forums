    Scrolls of Djhuty (Fate •••••)
    Practice: Making
    Primary Factor: Duration
    Withstand: N/A
    Recommended Rote Skills: Academics, Occult, Expression

    Supernal magic is wondrous and incredibly adaptable, however, only a foolish mage would dismiss it as the only type of willworking. Occultists and religious figures have developed rituals to harness the inherent supernatural powers of existence, refining them over the course of millennia into a form accessible to anyone. Some of the most potent ones leverage the symbolism of death to create powerful effects. This spell was created to furnish a mage with knowledge of these practices without spending extensive amounts of time learning them.

    This grants a target [Potency] dots of Ceremonies for the duration. Trespassing cabals that were confident in their dispellers were turned away as the Black Cat's Crossing sapped the will of those uninvited to the Sanctum. The Lover's Telephone allows for reliable communication even in areas where sympathetic magic is warded and Death Watch has proven invaluable in keeping injured cabalmates alive until a healer can be found.

        Supernal magic is wondrous and incredibly adaptable, however, only a foolish mage would dismiss it as the only type of willworking. Occultists and religious figures have developed rituals to harness the inherent supernatural powers of existence, refining them over the course of millennia into a form accessible to anyone. Some of the most potent ones leverage the energies of death to create powerful effects. This spell was created to furnish a mage with knowledge of these practices without spending extensive amounts of time learning them.

        This grants a target [Potency] dots of Ceremonies for the duration. Trespassing cabals that were confident in their dispellers were turned away as the Black Cat's Crossing sapped the will of those uninvited to the Sanctum. The Lover's Telephone allows for reliable communication even in areas where sympathetic magic is warded and Death Watch has proven invaluable in keeping injured cabalmates alive until a healer can be found.

        When cast with Mind 4, the Ceremonies can be modified to work on goetia. They can also be adapted to better fit the Astral's nature. The Diviner's Jawbone can be modified to create worry dolls that bestow the Dream merit and Persephone's Return can be reworked to create Synesnes. One of the most unusual ones summons the conductor of the Midnight Train, allowing one to barter for a ticket.

        With conjunctional Spirit 4, the Ceremonies can be altered to affect spirits or to resonate more with the Shadow's power. The Bloody Codex can be made to write down the target's emotions instead, even when they have a hard time expressing them themselves and Forge Anchor can be used as a basis to forcefully Fetter a spirit to a target until the binding is destroyed. There is even a variation of the rite that sets where the spirit will recorporate.

        A few hushed whispers say that the Mysterium is working on ways to have them affect Supernal beings by adapting the rituals with Prime, but the details are very sparse. Some even say that a Fate variation is being researched to attempt to create Ceremonies to influence the Hedge. Whether these are in the early stages or the researchers are attempting to work out some glitches or information was censored due to startling discoveries remains uncertain.
        *rubs the bridge of her nose*

        So, when we were writing the Chapter on Ceremonies and Regalia, we had to do some of the figuring out how and what, exactly, Ceremonies are in the world. You get the gist of it in the main book, but to go a little more into it, Ceremonies are the result of rituals repeatedly practiced around "death" in all of it's forms, so much that it picks up deathly resonance-variants performed by Krewes or those who explicitly practice around Avernian Gates accumulate that resonance faster but also weirder, which is why some ceremonies look like off-kilter versions of the familiar rituals. But, that said, it takes time for the living world's death aspect to get scarred that way, to develop that trauma response to a Ceremony.

        Between that and the much more straightforward rules in Mage, THe Contagion Player Guide, and (most importantly) Imperial Mysteries regarding the affectation and creation of supernatural powers, I'd say something this sort of schtick is absolutely the purview of Archmasters Only, at best as a 7 Dot Practice of Entities.

                        I think simply granting knowledge of how to perform existing Ceremonies should indeed be comparable to the Mind Patterning spell Gain Skill, since theoretically all you need is the right knowledge to perform them. (As opposed to creating entirely new Ceremonies, which presumably would involve archmaster Practices).

                        While Ceremonies as presented in Geist are very death-themed, the actual metaphysics involved (especially considering the Contagion Player’s Guide) don’t seem to strictly tied to Death at all. Rather, they’re a confluence of occult laws and symbolism, and thus fit more under the purview of Prime. However, since Supernal magic often cares more about the end result than the underlaying technobabble of other magic systems, it could be that, rather than Ceremonies as a whole falling under this or that Arcanum, an individual Ceremony is under the purview of whatever effect it actually has. Theoretically, “Ceremonies” aren’t limited to being death-themed, it’s just that the ones Sin-Eaters and their Krews learn and use tend to be so because death and ghosts are what they spend most of their time dealing with. Hence, a lot of the Ceremonies in Sin-Eater will involve Death from a mage’s perspective (since they have effects on ghosts, which fall under Death’s purview), but aren’t actually “powered” by Death, and Death doesn’t cover Ceremonies as a whole. (And, for what it’s worth, the Player’s Guide only says that Prime “is the best Arcanum for studying Ceremonies”, not manipulating their effects.)

                        As an aside, I think there is a solid argument to be made for a Mind+Spirit Patterning spell granting knowledge of, and capacity to perform, Pack Rites from Werewolf, since all those seem to require is a combination of a connection with the Shadow (usually through a pack totem) and the necessary knowledge to invoke the laws and agreements already in place.

                            So here's the thing: In the mechanical context of Mage, a Ceremony is a Mystery. People can do them by accident or without realizing that's what they're doing, but what they're doing is making use of a particular weird thing about the way the world works. The Awakened have Mage Sight for the purposes of figuring out stuff like this normally.

                            What this means for using spells to provide them is that either you shouldn't be able to provide Ceremonies to a character as a mechanical element on their character sheet (What happens when the Duration runs out? Why can't Mind be used in this fashion to fish for any other type of specific true occult knowledge?) or there's no point in providing it as a thing that's part of the character instead of conveying that information prosaically because performing the Ceremony is as simple as doing the steps.
