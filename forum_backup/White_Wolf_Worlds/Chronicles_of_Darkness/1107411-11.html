They're closer in nature to mortal psychics (who genre literature happens to also associate with them) and occult-junkie Awakened than the mutative existences of the Kindred, the Created, or the Lost, or the territorial fixations of the Arisen, the Begotten, or the Forsaken.

        Right. But that -doesn't- in any way suggest that the sad ghost of Isaac Donahue was not in fact Isaac Donahue at one point. If it wasn't, it was someone - otherwise it would have never been human. If a ghost was just a psuedo-copy of a person rather than the actual person, it'd fall more closely in line with a fetch - which is a pseudo-copy of a person, and qualifies as 'Descended from the dark mother' because of the fact that it was -never human to begin with-, no matter how human-like it may seem.

        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
        Additionally, that a ghost is "fundamentally human" does not mean it's the same human as the one who spawned it.
        This is possible. The only problem of course is this still implies that the ghost was a human at one point, and if it was -not- the same human that spawned it, you then have to figure what connection this 'other person' has with the human, and what process it is that one changes from a human to a ghost that also makes it so that you are not the ghost of yourself. There is really nothing to suggest that another person would become someone else's ghost, rather than their own.

        Edit: Don't get me wrong though - the idea that ghosts might not be a part of the people who they represent is an interesting one - and certainly a horrifying notion to those who put faith in that sort of thing. I just don't see the alleged 'implications' that they aren't - and in fact, can see multiple signs in the books that I would use that seem to suggest the exact opposite.

It's an animate construction made from faerie dross and a piece of that person (not necessarily their shadow, but sometimes). It's made -from- a human and from faerie, sure - but never was one. A ghost on the other hand -was- human. So far, no one has bothered to point out where it suggests anything besides the fact that a ghost is not what remained of the human after they died. It's not their soul (proven in mage) but their soul doesn't make them 'them'. But it -is- a part of them. Some mages suggest that it's a piece of their soul. Others suggest that it is their mind and personality. But not once have I seen anything suggesting it's not them at all.

                        Furthermore, exactly how does 'derived humanity' equate to 'Mostly human, but...'? If one made a robot and gave it a conscious, it would not be mostly human, no matter how much of their heart and soul placed it, nor how much of what we call "humanity" you managed to give it. The book we're referencing here defines the categories as "Born of the Dark Mother", "Fundamentally Human" or "Kinship does not apply". It's a very simple metric: Born of the Dark Mother states they either "Were never human" or "Were human but have since been totally transformed or merged with a supernatural being." Then it has the other, which is "Mostly Human, but..."

                        If anything, the fact that they don't fall into the category of "Was never human" or "has been totally transformed" is more suggestive that the ghostly state is a more natural transitionary and supernatural part of being human.

                                                Think of your soul as another body you have. Sometimes you treat the body as a whole. Sometimes you get a bunch of organs that have to be accounted for separately.



                                                The "essence of what you are" is what mummies refer to as the Ka (Essence), which is also what mages refer to as the Prime-aspect of the soul.

                                                Your consciousness and your personality, the lack of which would make you a "mindless shell", is what mummies refer to as the Ba (Spirit), which is also what mages refer to as the Mind-aspect of the soul.

                                                And then you have your emotions and passions and feelings and the Libido. That is what mummies refer to as the Ab (Heart), which is also what mages refer to as the Spirit-aspect of the soul. (I disagree with malonkey here, in that if the Embrace damages a part of the soul, then this is probably what gets damaged. And that this part is what probably creates Resonance, which in turn creates Essence. The Hollow Mekhet simply have even more of their soul damaged.)

                                                Then you have... your "karma," for lack of a better word. Your actions and intentions, and your identity that springs out from those things. That is what mummies refer to as the Ren (Name), which is also what mages refer to as the Fate-aspect of the soul.

                                                Finally, in your heart you have your darkness. The icky stuff that you'd rather not face, but nevertheless is a part of you and what anchors you to this world. Think of the Shadow Archetype in Jungian psychology, and the Thanatos in Freudian psychology. That is what mummies refer to as the Sheut (Shadow), which is also what mages refer to as the Death-aspect of the soul.



                                                Now, I'm not clear on Mummy lore outside the corebook... but it seems that we also have the True Name (which I think is the objective definition of who you are, rather than the subjective definition of who you are that is the Ka?), and the Will (which I think is that "free will" stuff we often see in fiction?) I'll have to get the Mummy pdfs to know for sure, so don't count me on the last two!
