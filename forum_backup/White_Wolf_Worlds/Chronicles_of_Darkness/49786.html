    Dark Steampunk

    01-15-2014, 10:51 AM
    Another concept I knock around is the possibility of a Dark Steampunk setting. A world much like our own, but with supernatural elements pushing things along. Industrialization spurred on by magic and monsters. Perhaps a race to unlock ancient Atlantean artifacts and to control ancient sites. Steampunk elements can get pretty crazy if left unchecked, but keeping it to real world Victorian tech bolstered by magic is more than enough.

    Using WOD: Mirrors as a guide, this setting would be between Sword and Sorcery and Modern Fantasy. The supernatural exists, but most people ignore it if possible. Most folks will encounter certain trickle-down elements of the supernatural world, mostly the nasty stuff like restless dead churned up by the poverty and workhouses, mutant creatures made from magical spillage etc.

    Some major players:

    Albion Empire (Britain and Her colonies)
    The Albion Empire lays claim to the spiritual and material legacy of the ancient continent of Atlantis, claiming to have been founded as a colony of Atlantis and thus holds the lgacy of that great civilization. The nation is ruled by a powerful bloodline of Atlaneans, whose command of several artifacts has aided the Albion Empire to maintain its unique power base. The Albion Empire has established colonies all over the globe and beyond, bringing the light of reason, industry and civilization (as Albion sees it anyways) to a large and savage world.
    Despite this claim, no other government is as despised as Albion. They are conquerors, using advanced weaponry, guile, with and polite brutality across the globe. Though widely seen as dangerous, the Albion Empire has put a stop to a number of atrocious activities across the globe, and stands squarely against the efforts of non-human elements at work in the world.
    Albion technology uses anything and everything it can. Alchemy is widely used as are more esoteric advances. Industrialization is energetically utilized, transforming once beautiful regions into industrialized wastelend in short order. However, thanks to Atlantean technology (ie Magic) the rich and powerful need not be concerned, as idyllic palaces and clean-air zones are maintained. The poor however, are subject to all manner of horrors. The polluted slums are teeming with disease and haunted by human and inhuman predators.

    Draconian Imperium (Austria/Slavic Nations)
    A mountainous and powerful Empire ruled by an ancient bloodline of Dhampir who are rumored to be descended from Atlantean experiments in immortality by a powerful group of sorcerers called the Sons of Belial. Because of this connection to Atlantis, the Dhampir lay claim to the traditions of that ancient civilization, and seek out the strange magics that so permeated its culture. The Draconian Empire is extremely old fashioned, and holds to many medieval concepts of honor, justice, and the absolute rule of the nobility. Art, music and dance are all extremely important to the Draconian Nobility, as-is the art of politics. Political alliances, seductions and betrayals are all part of an elaborate game of the Draconian nobles, one that they enjoy playing with each other and the outside world.
    Though not as advanced as Thule, the Imperium boasts robust industry and promotes technological advancement and education. Mortals within the empire are largely productive, healthy and intensely loyal to their inhuman masters. Industry here is quite advanced, fueled in part by undead and necromancy. Zombie slaves, captured spirits and necromantically fueled power sources are all in use.

    Thule Confederation (Germany and Prussia)
    The Thule Confederacy is a loose association of Germanic states in Central Europe, created by the Congress of Vienna in 1815 to coordinate the economies of separate German-speaking countries and to replace the former Holy Roman Empire. It acted as a buffer between the powerful Draconian Imperium and the sea. The independant states are ruled by a mix of Mortal and Warghaz noblity, all of whom have at least some connection to supernatural lines.
    Thule innovation and industrial precision is outstripped only be their martial skill. Thule soldiers are widely regarded as the most ferocious in the world. That their armed forces are enhanced with Warghaz troops and other supernaturals has more than a little to do with this fact. Hadge magicians (ala Second Sight) are well established in Thule industry and technological advancement, though a greater effort is attempted to avoid pollution thanks to the nature-loving Warghaz. Christian and Norse-themed Ceremonial Magicians are widespread with Alchemy of particular interest.


    Mortal+ Characters
    Rather than using the standard supernatural characters to be found across the WOD books, I think the lower tier or even mortal characters are better suited to a Dark Steampunk setting. So Atlanteans, Dhampir, and Warghaz are the majority of supernaturals, with the big boys being rare. Mortal Hadge-Magicians ala Second Sight are another excellent source of PC's, especially Ceremonial Magicians. Dark Heroes is another option, with an all Dark-Heroes campaign set amidst grimy steam-driven machines, smog-choked cities, and colonial conflicts.

      Atlanteans

      Atlanteans are human descendants of the Lost Continent of Atlantis. They are larger than life persons who command attention and are always a mighty force in the world. Atlanteans are born from mortal bloodlines, but can and do have otherwise mortal siblings and parents, with the "true" Atlantean blood arising spontaneously along certain families. Albion boasts a large number of Atlanteans in their noble family, with the sovereign always being an Atlantean. Albion nobility is liberally peppered with Atlaneans, forming an extraordinarily effective noble caste.
      Advantages
      Excellence: Atlanteans don’t suffer penalties for being untrained in a Skill, and achieve an exceptional success for one success less than normal (for most rolls, this is a score of four successes, not five).
      Intense Passions: Record any Willpower points an Atlantean gains from satisfying a Vice separately. When a player spends one of these Willpower points, add four dice to a roll, not three. If the Willpower augments a passive resistant trait such as Defense, add 3 to it instead of the standard 2.
      Unseen Senses: All Atlanteans possess an expanded version of the Unseen Sense Merit.
      Willful: Add one free dot to an Atlantean’s Resolve or Composure and increase that trait’s maximum score by 1.
      Disadvantages
      The Atlantean Mark: Atlanteans are larger than life figures. Traveling incognito without using a disguise or convincing acting (represented by a Skill roll such as Manipulation + Subterfuge) is useless; she’ll either be recognized or seen as exceptional among her peers.
      The Atlantean Doom: When an Atlantean fails a roll that she’s augmented with Willpower points drawn from her Vice, or is still affected by an incoming action she spent that Willpower to resist (as per “Intense Pas*sions”) one other future failure (or failure to avoid an unwanted influence or attack) of the Storyteller’s choice either will be treated as a dramatic failure, or as if the opposing force had scored two more successes. This usu*ally happens in situations where the Atlantean is once again motivated by her Vice.


      Dhampir

      The Dhampir are the noble bloodlines of the Draconian Empire, being the vampire-like descendants of Atlantean experimentation. Dhampir look much like traditional vampires in one form or another, often adopting some variant of the blood-hungry revenant. Though they appear undead, Dhampire are alive, but are closer to the realms of the dead than other creatures. All Draconian nobles are Dhampir, but as they are renowned seducers, they can turn up in any family line. Dhampir can be found throughout Europe and the colonies, playing the part of politician and businessman. Most people are at least a little disturbed by the inhuman ancestry of the Dhampir, nevertheless they are widely sought out as spiritists, lovers and allies due to their inhuman allure and supernatural senses.
      Advantages
      Dhampir characters have the following in-game advantages
      Death Against Death: Dhampirs receive a +2 bonus to resist the powers of an undead creature. In addition, Dhampirs are immune to becoming undead.
      Death’s Shadow: Dhampir characters have the Unseen Sense Merit, attuned to ghosts and the undead.
      Half-Dead Flesh: Dhampirs only take half the amount of damage from attacks that inflict Bashing damage.
      Resistance of the Grave: Add one free dot to Resolve, Stamina, or Composure, and increase that trait’s maximum score by one.
      Disadvantages
      Dhampir characters suffer the following in-game disadvantages
      Unnatural Flesh: All Dhampir characters bear some form of unnatural deformity, which marks them as one of the half-dead.
      Unnatural Vices: When presented with an opportunity to indulge in their vice, a Dhampir must gain three successes on an extended Morality roll to resist the temptation.


      Warghaz

      The Wolf-kin have ruled the lands of Thule since prehistoric times, and have repelled all invaders. Even the Roman Empire learned to fear the savage wolfmen and their human vassals and allies. Warghaz are the wolf-men of legend, resembling large hairy men and women who have a decidedly wolf-like appearance. Hypertrichosis is common amongst Warghaz and their kin, however they are also very large and have other wolf-like traits that vary between individuals.
      Though in the past the Warghaz were kings and warlords in Thule, these days the Empire of Thule is governed by the Thule Confederation (see above) which is largely composed of Mortals with a few Warghaz and ivery ordered and civilized. Still, the Warghaz are a mighty force in Thule, particularly in the military and some esoteric circles. Warghaz can be found primarily in Thule and the Russian forests, but a number of them also seek adventure throughout Europe and the colonies, finding ample opportunities as mercenaries, big game hunters, treasure-seekers and commanders.
      Advantages
      Wargaz characters have the following in-game advantages
      Enhanced Senses: Wargaz characters gain a +2 bonus to perception rolls based on hearing or smell.
      Giant: All Wargaz are +1 Size, and thus, +1 Health.
      Power of the Wolf: Add one free dot to Intelligence, Strength, or Presence, and increase that trait’s maximum score by one.
      Will of the Pack: A Wargaz character can spend a point of Willpower to augment any role made by an ally, adding an additional +2 dice.
      Disadvantages
      Wargaz characters suffer the following in-game disadvantages
      Killing Rage: When someone purposefully injures a Wagaz, the Wargaz character must gain three successes on an extended Morality roll to stop themselves from killing their attacker.
      Predator’s Scent: Animals react to a Wargaz as they would to any other dangerous predator, either by fleeing, showing extreme submissiveness, or attacking.
