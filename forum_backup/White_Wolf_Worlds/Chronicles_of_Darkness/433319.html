    Elevator Pitch a Dark Era

    03-08-2015, 08:35 PM
    Keep the pitch brief, just a game line plus historical era/geopolitical region and a few notes on influences and possible themes.

    If you like another poster's pitch, expand on it.

    My first pitch:

    Wheels within Wheels (Mesopotamia, 586-539 BC - Mage/Demon)

    "The Babylonian Captivity as seen through the eyes of two clashing factions of Mages and their Demon allies."

    Israel: A nation deceived. Their patron god, YHVH, is not the omnipotent Lord of the heavens, but a being cast out of the Supernal by an alien invader known as the God-Machine. An elite cabal of Mages within the Priesthood manipulates the kingdom on behalf of the Alienated Archmasters Enoch, Moses, and Elijah in order to restore Him to His rightful place.

    Their plans suffered a setback with the Samaritan Schism, but Judah remained loyal to her purpose. The loss of the Northern Kingdom to the Assyrians was regrettable, but an acceptable loss given that they had abandoned their purpose.

    Then Nebuchadnezzar came and carried away the Judean nobility and Priesthood, just as Sargon had done to Samaria decades before. The Mages of the Southern Kingdom found themselves drafted into a war that has been raging for nearly two millennia, between the severed halves of the very being who usurped their God’s place in the Realms Above. Assyria serves the God-Machine’s mummified husk, exsanguinated and hollowed out by Nimrod’s army when they stormed Heaven, while Babylon follows the mysterious Principle of divine fire which spilled from its Infrastructure.

    Caught within the machinations of these rival empires and the insane god-monsters they serve, the mages of Israel and Judah communicate in dream-visions and coded communiqués inserted into official state correspondence between their host nations. Aided by the fallen servants of both God-Machine and Principle, they play a deadly game … with the survival of their nation, their way of life, and perhaps humanity itself at stake.

      Meiji Werewolf (1890s Japan - W:tF)

      "Picture Seven Samurai, but with werewolves."

      Meiji Restoration, Japan (1890s Japan): The extinction of the Japanese Wolf coincides with the end of the samurai as massive reforms in the government and military forcibly bring Japan into the modern era. Western ideas and technology bring new spirits that clash with those old kami used to generations of appeasement, and the Forsaken find themselves struggling to balance tradition with the inevitable march of progress. It is clear that Japan cannot exist as it once did in this new age, and that sentiment hits hard for the heirs of Father Wolf's murderers. Can tooth and claw stand before ranks of foreign rifles?

      (The clash of two worlds that, ultimately, cannot coexist; the death of traditions that can’t survive as a metaphor for Father Wolf’s murder).

      tl;dr Forsaken as ronin in a time of profound spiritual upheavel as Japan is dragged into the modern era by foreigners.

      Remi. she/her. game designer.

                          Game of Chess- Scramble for Africa (Mage)

                          The great race has began. Europe has divided the "Black Continent" among itself, invading and occupying the land. While the locals has tried to defend themselves, the might of the Great Powers of Europe have the advantage. Belgium, France, Brittan, Spain, Italy, Germany and Portugal are the main players in the game, united at a single side for one cause- the richness and slaves of Africa. While mortals are the ones to play the game, the supernatural also finds itself lured into the vortex- many mysteries are hidden in those lands, from unknown monsters, powerful gods, hidden magics and buried treasures. Like the great nations, mage Orders also make uneasy alliances for the greater goal. Both the Diamond and the Pyramid are working together- one to find the secrets of the ancient world, another to conquer and destroy them- but in order to achieve any of that, they are better to submit the local Nameless, who fight to protect those secrets from the European strangers. Who will win that game? Who is playing against whom? What is the prize? And what would happen if the secrets of Africa have their own agenda in that game?

                              Mine are elevator pitches for very short buildings. Both are for Requiem.

                              Requiem at Home: London, 1942. As the blitz rages through the nights kindred huddle desperately together, hunting in air raid shelters and subsisting off a malnourished herd.                                           Yeah, okay. I'll throw out a bunch.

                                          How about some future/alternate Earth eras to start?
                                          ______________

                                          Dreams of Mars (Geist/Changeling Mars 2150)
                                          The first Mars colonies were established in 2026, and others soon followed. Mining operations on asteroids and Mars became big business and the corporate concerns that oversaw them bloated under their weight. Earth started to run out of clean water and food about ten years later. Decades of political squabbling over the truth of any potential problems left a world devoid of solutions to those problems, and riots began. The same story of ages past played out before – war burst out, colonies stopped imports and exports, and people stranded in the reaches of space began to die.

                                          The Martians didn't.

                                          They buckled down and weathered the vanishing support with fortitude and perseverance born out of their frontier ancestors and they lived – thrived, you might even say – in the cold, red wastes. Gangs became tribes, and tribes warred amongst each other over dwindling resources, renewable energy materiel, and found ways to integrate ever stranger things into their bodies, to better stave off the terrible climate. A century passed, and signals from Earth came again. The companies that put the ancestors on the red dust came back and claimed ownership over the Martians and their tools, and the Martian propensity for war turned up.

                                          That didn't work out too well; the invaders held the ultimate high ground, and even with all their cybernetics and emancipated robots and reborn machinery, the Martians did not win the day. The companies came down and reprogrammed what they could, fixed what they couldn't, extracted hefty fees from the rebels, and locked down the colonies. Now mines and work camps are little more than prisons, and they make them more like that every day, shipping in new meat from Earth to grind up in the mines looking for gold and copper, ignoring the tribes living squalid existences in the dust.

                                          These corporate concerns sit in orbit in their glittering homes with terraforming implements and gases all sitting and waiting for the button to be pressed to turn Mars into a new Earth, but they don't because the bottom line says that would lose them more money than it would save. They set lights out and exercise periods and uniform requirements for employees on the ground and implement their decisions through ruthless robotic stand-ins. It's 2150 and it's same-shit, different-day.

                                          Maybe you died. You suffocated in the thin air, you got pulped by a mining machine, you were the last survivor of a Martian raid or a casualty of war. Whatever happened, you got back up, you just had something else in your brain that tells you to kill, to fight back, to let the Martian dead find their rest and their exaltation in war.

                                          Maybe you went somewhere you shouldn't have. You found yourself in a winding canyon during exploratory EVA, your ship crashed someplace it couldn't have, your adulthood ritual among the tribe went wrong, and you found yourself in some other Mars, someplace with canals and angry red weeds and Martian cabbage and you took water with strange things in the depths of the caverns or hiding amongst the sharp-edged ruins and you know that dream of Mars is still out there, and it wants to be let out.

                                              Name: God-Kings of the Crescent

                                              Gameline(s): Immortals/Mummy

                                              Era/Region: Ancient Mesopotamia

                                              Major Influences:

                                              - The Epic of Gilgamesh

                                              - The story of the Seven Sages

                                              - The Tower of Babel

                                              Themes: Corruption, the ethics of rulership, divine and mortal hubris

                                              Synopsis: After the collapse of the Nameless Empire, the people of Irem spread across the Middle, many settled in the land of Sumer, and began to form an empire. The Sumerian Empire was ruled by mighty God-Kings, seemingly immortal men who carried the powers of gods. In truth, these so-called Gods were shams, mortal men who had siezed immortality for themselves. Some are benevolent, some are cruel, but all must be wary, as the Arisen and their cults still hide in the shadows, plotting to seize the God-King's thrones for the glory of Irem. And what of the mortals caught between them?

                                              Genius: the Transgression 2E is a thing that's being worked on.
