    "Vampire Lite"

    12-06-2016, 02:05 PM
    So someone referred to Vampire: The Requiem as "Vampire Lite."

    I found this odd given that I have read both V:TR editions and supplements, and I didn't find this "lite" at all. If I did, I wouldn't want to play it more if it were truly "lite" but is there anything in the game that could possibly be interpreted this way?

                  Sounds like something a salty Masquerade fan might say about Requiem. The Requiem 1e core probably looked like Masquerade Lite at the time it came out. Masquerade had 13 main Clans and countless Bloodlines compared to Requiem's 5 of each at the time of its initial releasee. Masquerade had 10 years of metaplot while Requiem was brand new and intentionally metaplot-free, which probably made it look like it had no plot depth to people who were used to the extensive plot of Masquerade. In 1st Edition, White Wolf was very conservative with vampires' powers, making Requiem vampires seem weak in comparison to Masquerade vampires.

                  And it didn't help that Requiem kept flirting with concepts from Masquerade, but changing them. Three of the Clans shared names with Masquerade Clans, but with different weaknesses and Discipline spreads. Three of the Bloodlines were named after Masquerade Clans but were much narrower in focus. The city power structure that was presented as the default was almost identical to Camarilla-run cities, and the book actually said that most cities are run basically the same, but different Covenants might use different titles for effectively the same positions, which made it look like despite there being five Covenants compared to Masquerade's three Sects (give or take a Sect, depending on how you count), they were all basically just different palette swaps of the Camarilla.

                  All this on top of the fact that, at the time, Masquerade was over. Gehenna had happened, the world had ended, and White Wolf was moving on. No more new books were going to be made for the "old" World of Darkness, in favor of supporting this "new" one. the Camarilla Fan Club was switching its LARP over to Requiem. If you loved Masquerade and were hesitant about this new Requiem, you'd understandably be bitter that this seemingly watered-down, less nuanced vampire game was replacing the game you'd spent the last decade enjoying. And so, you might try to convince your friends not to play this new "Vampire Lite", and to stick with Masquerade instead. Don't support this new direction White Wolf is taking and maybe they'll see the error of their ways.

                  Of course, as we've seen, they did eventually bring the classic World of Darkness back, and at present it's got more supplement support than Chronicles of Darkness. Meanwhile, Chronicles has had 10+ years to develop, and has really come into its own identity as a setting completely independent of World of Darkness, despite a few artifacts like Clan names that are too ingrained change at this point. Requiem has made a lot of changes that address many of the concerns people had about the 1st edition core book. To call Requiem "Masquerade Lite" today just shows that a person is still letting that initial perception of Requiem from reading that first core book (or worse, maybe even from second or third hand information about that first core book) shape their concept of Requiem today.

                          RE: Vampire Lite -

                          Maybe this is completely anecdotal, but I have been playing in the World of Darkness since Vampire the Masquerade 1st edition came out and I picked up a copy from the local gaming store. I still have a collection of all the editions and a good chunk of the supplements going back over 20 years (yes, I'm old) . And I can say with all honesty that I didn't get to run the Vampire game that the first edition book promised until Requiem. Vampire games in Masquerade often felt like action movies (trenchcoats and silver katanas galore!) and many players who crossed my table approached it like a darker super hero setting. I love so much of what Masquerade offers - but I honestly think Requiem does many of the core themes better. The exploration of the descent into monstrosity seems more focused and more central in Requiem, and much deeper than "How many mortals did your antitribu brutalize this week".
                          IMHO, those that dismissed Requiem early on did themselves a disservice.

                          The Horror Lab - A collection of Beasts, Monsters and less definable things.
                          Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
                          Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities

To me, these games are about horrors both otherworldly and human.
