                Charlaquin - Don't get me wrong; I understand why people would want that. If you like that, you do you. I just like a more broad toolbox to work with that allows for that, while also allowing for other stories too.

                A little while ago, I was talking with a friend about the changes - he doesn't check up on this stuff as religiously as I do - and he mentioned really liking the note about how, now, items lost in the Hedge may take on a life of their own; the sentiments attached to them growing (perhaps literal) roots. A Changeling taken as a child losing their teddy bear and it becoming a goblin.

                Off the top of my head, I just started stream-of-consciousnessing a story that might come from that. The Changeling character having this worrying propensity, when in the Hedge, of attracting nightmares. Not just nasty things but actual, literal nightmare-things; crept out of dreams and hunting for them. One day, when escaping one of these things, the Changeling's player fails a roll and stumbles. The nightmare chasing them is about to set upon the character when, out of the briars, comes launching an enormous beast. Chestnut brown fur periodically marked with stitches and scars and a terrible ursine growl follow as it savages the nightmare with all of its animal fury. The bear cannot kill the nightmare, but it drives it off; sending it scurrying back into the briars, tail between its hundreds of legs.

                The bear turns and looks at the Changeling. One eye missing, the wound sealed shut. The other dark and reflective, like a button. The bear turns and flees back into the briars after exchanging a long, silent, sad stare with the Lost one, who is left with the most uncanny sensation of just who that bear was.

                Cue adventures; sojourns into the Goblin Market or asking around at the freehold for who, if anyone, might know something about the bear. The Changeling wants the bear back by their side; to help keep away the terror. Eventually, after much bartering and trading, the Changeling sets out to a fabled Hollow in the Hedge; a place surrounded by thick briars, whose trods are stalked by nightmares, at the heart of which is a ruined (but still secure) castle. The Changeling - through whatever means - gets into the castle and finds its courtyard is full of doors that lead to nowhere. But as night falls, pathways appear within those doors; to bright, colourful, beautiful places. And that's when the bear returns.

                The Changeling explains why they're there but there isn't much time to talk. Nightmare beasts swarm the castle. Together, the Changeling and bear fend them off until morning.

                In the aftermath, the bear and the Changeling settle in. The bear can't speak, but it can nod and shake its head and the two can generally understand each other. The understandings come to the fore. The Changeling didn't mean to leave the bear behind; it was an accident. The bear can leave with them now. But the bear shakes its head sadly. It isn't angry. It's glad its former child is now all grown up and free from Arcadia. But its place is here; protecting the doorways to the dreams of children from nightmares. The Changeling understands. They let their precious childhood companion move on; passing it to the younger generation.

                They leave in the sunshine but, now and then, they return; having tea parties with the bear (with real tea this time), laughing, playing, and, come nightfall, donning armour and joining it in battle. Facing the nightmares, at last, rather than fleeing them. And pretty soon, the nightmare beasts no longer hunt the Changeling in the Hedge; afraid of the heroic figure who sends them fleeing into the dark.

                ---

                I know this might feel a bit cutesy, but it's a fun little fairy tale to me. It's a story about growing up, and you could easily frame it against the act in the mundane world of, say, the Changeling suffering nightmares and going through their old things to look for a teddy bear, or going through things to pass off to a younger sibling, etc.

                It's a story with horror and fear and terror; of being chased and pursued by nightmares. But it's also a story of courage and bravery and learning to face the dark; of a character ultimately growing up and learning to put away childish things, yet at the same time achieving real maturity and being able to indulge them without needing them.

                It's a story that could have a lot of powerful emotions tied to the often disappointing sensation of growing up, and the pain of realizing that you can never really go back to carefree innocence; happiness stolen from you is gone forever. But it's also about maturing and coming to realize that so long as you're alive, you can be happy for what you had and find new happiness; new ways to grow and become better than before.

                If this were the other Hedge, the agony wall, the bear and the castle would cease to be as soon as the Changeling came to terms with the fact that they can't have what they used to have anymore. Once they made peace, it'd cease to be; an act which would lead to the whole thing being tinged with loss, rather than a contented acceptance.

                I like hope in my stories. Darkness and terror are great, but if that's all it is, it's just exhausting. I like happy endings. I like the hero's efforts to be worth it.

                I don't figure this is for everyone, but I like beauty in my beautiful madness, and I like fairy tales. That's why I play Changeling. If it's not for you... Fair enough.

To take the most absurd example, if you get a paper cut and it stings and that manifests in the Hedge as a paper mosquito flitting around, it doesn't just... vanish when the cut heals. I'd expect it to stick around and join the Hedge ecology being a paper mosquito doing paper mosquito things. So why would the Bear and the castle vanish? What requires that?

                                                    To make an analogy, in the Shadow, spirits don't die when the essence that awoke them stops showing up. That's the entire reason there's a bunch of spirits who flit around doing stuff unrelated to how they were originally manifested. I don't see any reason why the same kind of thing isn't true of the Hedge. The difference is mainly that, in the Hedge, it's much more obvious and in-your-face where a goblin came from... if it's part of your story. But something like a goblin market doesn't make sense as something that'd exist if Hedge denizens only existed at the sufferance of some specific pain that someone is presently experiencing. Hell, nothing in the Hedge could possibly exist for longer than particular lifespans.

                                                    Ironically, the only reason the Bear and castle should vanish is precisely to signify that the Changeling has put away childish things, as you put it, and grown up and is no longer indulging. You could get very Susan Pevensie about it and say that Arcadia doesn't really exist and it was very fun to play king and queen but now I have lipstick and dresses. But, in your telling, the Bear and the castle are not childish things. And acknowledging that things that hurt you don't magically disappear once you've moved on--that just because you know kung fu doesn't actually mean Smith is going to stop coming for you--is also part of growing up. In seeking a happy ending, I honestly feel like you've made a fairly bittersweet one.

                                                    Anyways. It's not like this is a relevant thing to be arguing about anymore.
