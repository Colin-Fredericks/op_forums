                                      08-05-2015, 10:57 AM
                                      Originally posted by Chattnos View Post
                                      Several of the new rituals in an upcoming book require the vampire to dance till exhaustion. How long does that take?
                                      It takes however many turns the player requires to roll the necessary successes for the extended action of performing the ritual.

                                      Originally posted by Chattnos View Post
                                      What are the game effects of a vamp being exhausted?
                                      Loss of a willpower point or vitae point, depending on the ritual.

                                      This is one of those mechanic vs. narrative semantic issues. Players track their character's mental and mystical energy as willpower and vitae points respectively, but the characters themselves don't track their energy that way. Instead, a character would intuitively sense that they had expended some amount of personal energy from a finite reserve. They would describe this as feeling "drained" to some degree, and this is what the term "exhaustion" means in this context. So, the mechanic of "roll successes and spend willpower/vitae" becomes the narrative of "dance until exhaustion".

                                          I was actually thinking about this earlier.

                                          I figure it's a physical limitations thing vs a conscious/mental thing (of course, I project my belief that there isn't a mind/body distinction into the game about vampires with primeval hunger monsters living in their souls, so...). Basically, a vampiric body has specific physical limitations just like any other body, and whether or not the vampire is tired is irrelevant: their muscles still experience microtears and Vitae still has to circulate the magical animus through their body at a finite rate.

                                          After getting to that point in Order of the Stick where Roy climbs a mountain in the afterlife for 3 months and doesn't realize it, I really enjoy using mundane, simple explanations for what people might think are odd or exceptional.

                                          "Nihhina kalekal-zidu kal masun, kal manudanadu. Nihhina kalekal-zidu nukal shaghu-desasudu — nihhina kalekal-zidu kal innu-desasudu udhkal samm." Arthur Ashe
