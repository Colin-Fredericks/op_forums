            Context:
            "You? Hunt us? Arrogant mongrels! You are too few; we are legions. You must be born to the flesh; we take the flesh at our whim. O you wolves howl your fear to the uncaring moon, for she has given you up to us!"

            The world is in shadow. To one side stretches the forest, to the other the city. Your claws are stained with blood. Your senses whisper of prey that runs before you, and of predators who stalk even the likes of you. You hear the howls of your brothers and sisters. Luna rises. Your blood boils. It is time to hunt.

            The world hates you. This isn't a figurative thing - the world doesn't acknowledge your innate right to exist. You're a half-flesh half-spirit abomination that shouldn't be. Shadowy entities hate and hunt you because you're what keeps them from building their own little hell-kingdoms in the world of man. Spiders and rats dig into people's brain and hearts, controlling them the way people drive cars. Wolves stalk your children and friends because of some sin your ancestors committed thousands of years and at least one apocalypse ago. People fear you, and would hate you if they knew what you are, if they knew you sometimes look at them and can't help thinking "food".

            The world hates you, and does everything it can to kill you.

            Doesn't that make you angry?

            Show them how strong the Forsaken are. It is time to hunt.




            This is really awesome, but I do have one quibble. The whole "the world hates you because you were not meant to be" is pretty much the Promethan's thing. They are so unnatural the very land rebels at their presence. Werewolves are more about trying to keep balance and live in harmony as much as possible, their "morality" stat is even called "Harmony." That's what makes their dealings with the spirit world so tricky. . . spirits ARE a part of the natural order of things, and certain amount of certain types of spirits active in an area is healthy. It's when there's too many of one or a few kinds of spirits, or not enough spirits at all, that werewolves should intervene to try and keep things in balance.

            One complaint I hear frequently about WtF is that it's just a "monster of the week" game, with werewolves rushing out to shred whatever big nasty is mucking things up this time. While this is a perfectly valid game style, there can be so much more to the role of "spirit police" than just playing whack-a-mole with uglies. There was a passage somewhere in the Core rulebook about managing the spiritual health of a particular area, for instance a part of the city the pack claims as their own. A strip club might attract lust spirits to the area, which is not necessarily a bad thing. But as more lust spirits move in, they need more lust to feed on, so they start encouraging it in the people, which attracts more spirits, and so on. Fighting the individual lust spirits is a loosing proposition, because as long as the thing that attracts them exists, they'll keep coming. So the werewolves have to find a way to shut the club down, or decrease its patronage (decreasing the overall lust in the area so the spirits will start moving on), or establish something else in the area that will attract spirits to counter the activities of the lust spirits. None of which (should) involve turning furry and shredding something.

            Now, sure, "Sim City With Claws" may not be a chronicle idea that appeals to everyone, but it's an example of how there's a lot more depth to the werewolves' interactions with the spirit, and to the game itself, than just "find nasty, shred nasty, move on."

                                        Werewolves are hunters. They can't be anything else.

                                        Note that "hunter" implies proactivity. They choose their goals. They seek a goal out, figure out how to get it, and run it to ground. For werewolves, this doesn't always mean fleshy or spirit prey. It also means goals, concepts (some of which they literally hunt down in the form of spirits), territories, feelings. For werewolves, nothing comes easily. Nothing comes without a struggle. A werewolf wants a lover? She goes out and takes him in a torrent of passionate heat. She wants peace? She rips it from the throats of her enemies. She wants something, she goes out and earns it. Money can't buy a werewolf the things she needs, and rarely the things she wants most. Money can't replace her drive, her will to hunt, to be the best. An Uratha hunts because she wants to. She hunts because she knows no other way. She hunts because she must.

                                        For a werewolf, the world gives her little choice. It is full of threats and desires, and she cannot help but hunt or be hunted by them in turn (sometimes both). The world gives her little choice, but the werewolf gives herself no choice.

                                        Which would you rather be: predator or prey?

                                                            Again, my werewolf knowledge is rather spotty, though I do seem to remember that the main emphasis from the corebook, when it came to generic weirdness, was "shred first, shred again, shred some more. . . were we supposed to ask questions at some point?"

                                                            Now, yeah, I'd agree that engaging in physical confrontation with incorporeal entities is, well, less than productive, the mechanics of the game seemed to revolve more around "wolf out and lay the furry smackdown." Which is where I think the "monster of the week" misconception comes from. More space was given in the book to how to solve problems violently than how to solve problems in other ways. Which has always been the MORE interesting concept for WtF, in my mind. Werewolves are arguably the most physically formidable splat in the entire nWoD (Prometheans might give them a run for their money), so physical conflicts are an easy thing for them. To really solve problems in the Spirit and Physical worlds, they have to do a LOT more than just find something punchable and punch it. Werewolf Grassroots Campaigns members canvassing an area for donations to fund education to reduce hate crimes, werewolf political activists cleaning up political corruption, werewolf citizen groups cleaning up corporate corruption, werewolf corporate board members demonstrating that you run a company both ethically AND profitably. . . these are the stories that interest me for Werewolves in the nWoD. Much as I do like seeing me some clawed furry smackdown.

                                                            Originally posted by Christian A View Post
                                                            Spirits are fought by changing the Flesh so that they lack appropriate sources of essence. Solve the murder case and bring the murder to justice. Stop the pollution. Conquer your fear. Anything else is just a temporary solution.
                                                            I wholeheartedly agree with you on that. The misunderstanding, I think, comes in that most people have a hard time reconciling that idea with "The Wolf Must Hunt." I imagine most people read that and start thinking of the Predator, who, cloaking technology aside, is about as subtle as a brick wrapped in guts. "Hunt" doesn't mean tearing through the wilderness chasing your prey down. . in fact, in the real world, a predator who gets to that point has already lost. A true hunt is about stalking your prey, making sure that when you pounce, you WILL take it down. Chasing prey thither and yon is a waste of calories. Watching, waiting, finding the perfect moment to strike and deliver one, solid, killing blow. . . that's the difference between a predator who eats well and one that starves. Expanding that idea to things we don't consider normally "huntable," like political and corporate corruption, and you wind up very much with something not unlike a police procedural crossed with a spy show: finding the patterns and weak points, formulating the plan, then taking your enemy down in one swift stroke.

                                                            Burn Notice should ABSOLUTELY be in the inspirations for Werewolf 2E. Michael Westin's Miami Pack is a perfect illustration of how the Hunt works.
