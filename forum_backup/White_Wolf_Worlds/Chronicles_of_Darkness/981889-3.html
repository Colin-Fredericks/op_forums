            Edit: 'aight, I'll explain my questions as best as I can

            With Deviant, the reason I said you seem to be building yourself towards a fall is you were using the capitalised Deviant, while talking about supernaturals within the setting. At the moment, we really don't know how well Deviants will map to the other splats. Since your main issue with Mage is that it doesn't map well to how you envision horror, I'd say, at a guess, that it won't map to exactly what you want since you aren't the one writing it. This, like in the case of Mage, would most likely create a tonal dissonance with what the setting will actually provide when compared to the canon you are already creating.

            With the comparison of forms of horror, your writing seems to indicate you want there to be massively negative impacts on the characters, in this case, Mages being soulless monstrosities who can only see the world viewed through a lens provided by their Arcana. This seemed to imply that you view horror as requiring some sort of physical or mental deformity for their to actually be horror within a setting. To me this seems rather limiting in that it denies mundane horror, the horror of humanity, which limits your setting quite a bit.

            When I questioned if you'd read the Mage books, I guess I more meant, "the majority of mage books" because reading through material you've written, I'm not the only one who has pointed out that some of the material you've highlighted as wanting to exist within your version of the setting, already exists. For example, you mentioned Archmastery and then described what you'd like it to be. Your example of what it should be like pretty much directly used the material as its already written. There's no shame in not having read every Mage book, or even just forgetting a segment, but as someone reading what you write, it makes your writing seem confused when I read a statement that says an aspect of Mage should be changed to what it already is.

            With my final point, I was mainly highlighting it because thematically super villains and heroes don't map out differently enough for me to see where you divide them. With the complaint about Mages being magically powered heroes, I can't personally see where the idea of magically powered super villains is distinct enough from the former to become a cornerstone for a complete rewriting of the Mage setting. Rather than changing canon here in a rewritten setting, this seems a minor element of the game that would be reinforced merely by the tone used by the ST when describing scenes within his game.

            Oh, and finally, a new note. In general it makes it difficult to talk about what you've said if you do things like edit your own posts to remove what was previously said. By removing what you'd written about Archmastery and how you envisioned it, when someone like myself attempts to use it within their own posting, it can seem like an attempt to modify your own statements to win over an audience in an argument. I'm not trying to imply this was your intent in this case, but since the statement wasn't contentious, nor asked to be removed by a moderator, it can seem so. (I'm sure we've all seen examples online where someone has written something like "Children Soldiers are cool", waited for an outcry against it and then modified the initial statement to something like "Women's rights are something we should all get behind".)

                        The Venture Brothers (a beautiful depiction of obsession, futility, and shadow societies of broken people lost in their own personas)

                        Breaking Bad (even though it's known primarily as a gritty crime drama, Breaking Bad is probably one of the best explorations of the psychology of the mad scientist ever made)

                        Worm (one of the countless dark superhero deconstructions, any of which could provide material. Worm, however, has a lot of twisty cosmic horror material and mysteries that make it one of the best fits)

                        Genius: The Transgression (if you don't like Genius, you probably won't like The Hollow Chronicle. Mad scientists, futility, and broken people frantically struggling for an impossible utopia abound in both.)

                        Re-Animator (one of the classic mad scientist films)

                        The Prestige (two stage magicians and weird science-dabblers are caught up in an obsessive rivalry, which fits THC pretty well)

                        Jojo's Bizarre Adventure (what, you're playing ChroD and you haven't read JJBA?)

                        Dorohedoro (a weird fantasy series with pitch-black humor about two parallel worlds and the freaks and wizards who inhabit them. The magic here is primarily a device used to explore the characters and their world, but there's a complicated occult mystery at the heart of it, surrounding an amnesiac assassin with the head of a lizard and a man living inside his mouth)

                        Dr. Faustus (one of the classic stories of sorcerous hubris)

                        The Tempest (another classic tale of sorcery)

                        Unknown Armies (probably the greatest occult-themed rpg ever made)
