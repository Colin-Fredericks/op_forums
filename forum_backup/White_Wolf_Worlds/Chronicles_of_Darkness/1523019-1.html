    Originally posted by Huitzil37 View Post
    CofD is meant to be a system with a lot of flexibility, I present a lot of things that CAN be small or large in scope, and you've reacted as though they can only be small in scope.
    Because the presentation of the information only focuses on them at the small scope or the abstract level.

    I don't explicitly state that an Enthroned can be as big as Walpurgisnacht because maybe you want a smaller one. I say how they work and what they do so you can use them. This is a setting that's supposed to be completely modular and everyone's table is going to be different.
    You say that they're the biggest threat to the biggest goal the Noble have short of eradicating the Darkness and explicitly put their global numbers in the low double-digits. You model them as a self-directed, mobile, and harder-to-kill version of Nightscapes, which themselves have a substantial amount of wordcount dedicated to them without specifying how commonplace they are. Modularity goes out the window as an excuse when these things are part of the setting's history and you have shown no problem including suggestions for adjusting Nightscape mechanics for more difficult resolution than the baseline.

    I say that the Dreamlands has, among other uses, the ability to do a big world-changing story if you the Storyteller want to subsequently have an adventure where the world isn't changed, and explicitly frame it in terms of "if you want to later tell a story that involves this change not happening." You take this to mean that the only reason to do the Dreamlands is to remove the ability to affect the world, and that the game is saying the best way to resolve things that affect the world is to do them in the Dreamlands where they don't matter, even after saying in multiple places that their use is optional.
    I say the book as presented should probably not put tips for using the Dreamlands to pull your narrative punches re: world-changing stories in the main section on the Dreamlands, just as I say the place to put advice for using Shikigami as a dial for chronicle levity is probably not the Shikigami writeup itself in a fangame for a franchise whose corebooks usually have a dedicated chapter for advice on running the game.

    If a part of the cosmology as-written prompts in-text questions about why it matters, the text should provide meaningful answers to those questions — and on this front the "why do the characters go here?" section serves this function adequately, albeit the things it gestures at would do well to be supported elsewhere in the text — and it should definitely not have an entire extra section/sidebar questioning the point of including that part of the cosmology at all with an appeal to Comic Book Stasis and a recommendation that you use it for when you don't want to commit to the story your group is telling with the game they're playing.

    The text should not end a chapter about the world your characters' main mentors are trapped in and what can be done there by undermining the importance of that place in the game as a storytelling tool. If using the Dreamlands for a diversion is worth mentioning it is your job as a writer/developer to convey what is worthwhile about it for a game of Princess, not shrug and say "use this place to delete consequences, run an AU, or play a completely different genre of game with the same system, I'm not a cop."

    You say that the sample Defilers are so weak that it gives the impression that they don't want anything and can't scale up, and I went back to double-check, and... no? The Violent Enforcer is explicitly a starter-level link between two types of evil.
    They're explicitly not involved in indirect threats unless they're being manipulated by a Nameless Cult masquerading as a crime syndicate. Nameless Cults are presently just described as Mystery Cults in the same paragraph as one of the text's existing "people who aren't Princesses can't identify the Darkness as anything unusual" bits, which means the reader has functionally nothing to go off of for that second tier beyond the Secretive Warlock; the term is presently interchangeable with any esoteric group with a Purpose, Relic, and Doctrine, and we here run into that "the Darkness is and is not connected to all the rest of the supernatural" problem again.

    The Secretive Warlock is one of the monsters who didn't have a stat block yet, and in its description, has... the things you're saying it lacks. They're driven by fear and desperation explicitly, having always lived in fear of what others would do too them. How they change their behavior and justifications based on accumulating more power is laid out explicitly: they sought out power from an occult source or rose through the ranks of a Mystery Cult, they grow bolder and more callous by going from "take only what they can justify" to "justify whatever you want to take."
    In a one-page writeup you are telling me you expect "At first, they only use their powers to take what they can justify" and the converse of "now they know the punishment they get will be deserved" to do a lot more of the heavy lifting re: sympathy for this example character than it actually does, and are apparently also expecting me to interpret a character's background information as reassurance that they will be a growing threat later in the chronicle instead of someone the game expects the PCs to put away before the ST has to update their character sheet.

    They have large amounts of minions instead of working personally through things.
    They have a background in a Mystery Cult and work through minions, which the generalized writeup (in a paragraph that also undercuts them by emphasizing their lack of knowledge) suggests are normal people instead of either of the supernatural beings the game literally uses to model cannon-fodder minions. A Mystery Cult is not intrinsically assumed to be a large organization and neither the generalized Defiler section nor the Secretive Warlock writeup say anything about large numbers of minions. In the absence of a statblock the only clue about how many people answer to them is that they "[rose] through a Mystery Cult," which could mean anything from "leads a regional conspiracy" to "is a senior member of a crew of fifteen people." They could have two people and a cat doing their dirty work for all I know at this point.

    There's... there's nothing there to suggest they're weak and dismissible. You wrote them off as "monologuing warlocks" as if that meant they were not dangerous or interested in growing in power.
    You end the Secretive Warlock writeup literally saying that they're going to start chewing the scenery once their identity is revealed and provide extremely abstracted goals. The genericized format of the Defiler writeups is doing them no favors for looking at them as characters to use as-is or as reference.

    That's a completely indefensible read. I talk directly about those specific characters seeking out and growing in power, and you cite those specific characters are giving you no reason to believe they are interested in growing in power.
    Let me ask you: when you watch magical girl anime where a villain does not recur once dealt with, do they not generally have clear goals and things that they are doing that would make them a bigger threat to the protagonists once accomplished? Because right now the text has elements that could link up to present this kind of driving pressure and rather sort of doesn't do anything with them.

    The Nightscapes writeup says that certain antagonists (chiefly Defilers and Princesses of Tears) will subvert a Nightscape for their own ends, including Defilers; from the way the rest of the text focuses, apparently this doesn't happen often enough with Defilers to be worth mentioning anywhere else, whereas Lacrima interacts with the process specifically in ways that link up with a defined goal. If organized Defilers weren't stated to not know enough to recognize the common source of their power, it would be really easy to say "oh, of course Dark-based Mystery Cults would try to take control of a Nightscape for [example reason to do so based on desired material outcomes or philosophical beliefs]," but we also don't have much of either of the bracketed things to go on beyond the abstractions of "for power" and "because this is the way the world works." There is presently nothing in the book pointing toward the idea of getting Defilers to give up the power of the Darkness as a practical and achievable goal for Princesses within the scope of a story, which makes the easiest way for troupes to deal with them in games where they're used to deal with them in a way that takes them out of play for the foreseeable future (whether by murder or the justice system or some other fashion); since they're NPCs, they do not gain or spend Experiences, and there is no currently published system in Princess or baseline ChroD to model the progression of evil schemes or other methods of advancement, which means they use their character sheets as-written along with whatever the Storyteller fiats onto them as the game proceeds.

    Deviant's conspiracies have Projects they work toward that can be hampered by players making moves against their Nodes in the Web of Pain. Mummy has sorcerers and other Arisen incentivized to pursue relics for the powers they possess or the energies that can be harvested from them. Vampire's basic kit includes an entire extended set of tools for proxifying people whose root purpose is functionally "I need blood to move around and diverse investments can bring big returns on that front," and magical R&D is one of those investments that can feed back into that process. The Exarchs of Mage send Mystery Commands to their Awakened servants and those humanlike monsters that have been taught to read their messages to spread the reach of the Tyrants. The Fae send the Wild Hunt in its myriad forms to drag the Lost back to Arcadia, most famously the relentless hunters with powers built to undermine their confidence in themselves and their worldview. Beasts are literally incentivized to tilt their feeding to align with their Aspirations and have access to more nuanced supernatural tools when they're well-fed enough to need at least a chapter's worth of setup for a direct meal.

    The list goes on. Defilers, meanwhile, are presently people with abilities modeled as Merits, drawing power from the Dark through nonspecific channels that apparently don't require an occult idiom at all, and their goals are abstractly prosaic but apparently achievable through overwhelming intimidation. Are they trying to goad local police into wandering into the Dark World with military surplus gear that they can then steal to effect a violent insurrection against the city mayor? Running an occult smear campaign against a rival business by associating its mascot with unrelenting terror? Frightening the neighbor kid into being their prom date? I could not tell you, because all the text is giving me as examples are generalities and abstractions along with the notes that the occasional idiot who volunteers for the maximum capacity of Dark power a personlike actor can have gets body-snatched by a much less functional clone. There is nothing for which pursuit of Dark magical power is necessary or even especially expedient compared to other occult methods, which means Defilers' motivations for going beyond dabbling and their means of getting there need attention and/or the Darkness needs the insidious slow-roll pointed to beyond basic gestures toward the slippery slope.

    You say that a problem is that the explanation of the forces of Darkness only focus on the bottom rungs and the top of the ladder, and elsewhere, you essentially say there's no top of the ladder. There's the monsters of the week that're okay to kill because they don't have interiority and are used to express something about someone else's experience or personality, and there's the people who use them as tools to do what they want who do have personality and interiority. I say all over that they use the Darkness and its Darkspawn as tools to get what they want. They're the intermediate stages of the ladder.
    The Cataphractoi writeup uses the word "Darkspawn" twice in its entire contents, once to say that they intrinsically have the Command Darkspawn Dread Power and once to say that they release minor Darkspawn when they finally fall apart. There is currently no example Cataphract to exemplify their use as anything resembling a Dark General instead of a Dark-tainted revenant with the nominal capacity to be more insidiously disturbing than typical Darkspawn. We are told they have the capacity to plan for the future, and then are given no real examples of what they would do with that besides "defeating and devouring Princesses" as a grossly inefficient means of staving off their inevitable degeneration.

    The general Defiler writeup, again, suggests that organized Defilers use human minions and lieutenants despite Darkspawn notionally being a better fit, and puts the only other reference to Darkspawn in the Violent Enforcer writeup, where it states that that archetype treats them like ordinary goons. The Command Darkspawn Dread Power is one of two options listed as the defining power for Defilers, and the paragraphs immediately preceding this information combined with the order of presentation tilt the priority toward Siphon Energy despite Command having direct utility for handling Darkspawn. Dark Gate is described as practically de rigueur for Defilers who don't have a reliable entrance despite a bunch of them supposedly not understanding what they're dealing with.

    The intermediate stages of the ladder are more than just "model these things as Horrors with variable Potency, model these people as characters with extra XP options that suggest a generic organizational chain of command, and model organized groups of the latter as a Mystery Cult." The text as it currently stands is a loose assortment of tools with relatively little actionable suggestions for how to use them together in a coherent fashion beyond "some of the things that are better than Darkspawn can command Darkspawn, and some of those are part of groups modeled using the generic Merit-based system for belonging to a cult."

    Crystal Edition has two and a half otherworlds relating to the Light and the Dark. It's a game about magical girls, specifically of the kind that fight The Darkness. In such a game, your antagonist mechanics and examples for things that are not modeled the same as PCs need to be more detailed than "reference the generic mechanics from Chronicles of Darkness Core," and if they're interfacing with those otherworlds in a way that is supposed to threaten to escalate in consequences then it would be prudent to… y'know, actually have the ways they and those things interact with themselves and each other in concrete terms.

    Recurring bit of impossible bookkeeping notwithstanding, I know how a locus forms in Werewolf and what effect its attracting spirits of a certain type to the area implies for the neighborhood if it's not gotten under control. The lack of hard mechanics for arcane physics doesn't change the fact that I know the basic idea behind how Infrastructure works in Demon, including that it is built for a purpose whose end goal may be mysterious but whose effects can be inferred through investigation. I do not need to ask how a conspiracy in Deviant is going to advance its evil plans, because building the resources at its disposal and its operating Principles are part of the arc setup process for that game. I have almost nothing of the sort for the forces of Darkness and the war thereupon in Crystal Edition so far, because all the information given to me is in the form of loose tidbits that work serviceably enough as isolated drop-in systems but do not cohere to form a bigger picture.

    Nightscapes are formed at a rate that's barely gestured at from a mysterious subset of fear-based breaking points influenced in an unspecified way by the effects of the Darkness's actions in the Astral Pools of an area, and have an equally undetailed but frequent chance to form Bleeds into the Dark World around them when they shrink or expand based on yet-unstated factors. The Dark Queens supposedly dwell deep in the Dark World, and they found and enlisted the Wardens to maintain the Trap; despite the mirrored nomenclature, the Wardens have nothing to do with the Prisoners kept in Nightscapes by Nightmares, and in fact there's no statement of what the forces of Darkness that act on the Pools might be — the Dark World explicitly doesn't link up to any other part of the cosmology besides Nightscapes and the real world, so Darkspawn are out of the easy runnings, and Nightmares would seem to be the most appropriate tools if not for the lack of described interface between Nightscapes and the Dreamlands. The Enthroned are functionally mobile Nightscapes, but they number dramatically less than a hundred worldwide and their influence on things outside their confines is limited to sending out Nightmares, including the Caricature of their former Transformed self; their writeup reiterates the only semi-consistent form of influence Nightscapes have on the Dark World, which is that when they're gone the hole where the Nightscape was dumps any intruders who couldn't hold on in the Dark World.

    Darkspawn form in the Dark World in response to the presence of the Light, and can be commanded by Cataphractoi and some Defilers using the right Dread Power; the Dark World can be accessed by the undefined ill fortune of wandering through a Bleed or by using the Dark Gate Dread Power to turn a Bleed into a Dark Gate by taking at least three hours (Dark Gates are explicitly not naturally occurring and are "incredibly hard to seal" in a fashion that is not elaborated upon anywhere in the book despite Dark Gates being one of the specific example causes of Chronic Sensitivity). Defilers can and often will have the Command Darkspawn Dread Power, but apparently organized ones use the generalized garden-variety Mystery Cults they form or join as sources of minions instead; also, Defilers are one of the main culprits for Subverting a Nightscape to their own ends — what specific sort of ends are not specified, but Nightscapes feed themselves by sending out Nightmares, which collect Essence instead of Willpower and are also not Darkspawn, which leaves the reader to determine, sans examples, what kind of plans are being advanced specifically by scaring people out of their minds with specific traumatic fears.

    This is not a warped ecosystem or a hellish economy or a hideous machine of ugly impulses for my team of magical girls to work at cleaning out the nasty parts of (or even smash to bits). This is a disconnected skeleton that I'm not convinced has all the bones it needs to make whatever creature I am supposed to be being shown.

    It is a problem of presentation. It is a problem of the words that I have been given to read on this topic, both narrative and mechanical, and the relative quantities thereof, and the ways the parts of that text connect with each other or fail to do so. This is not asking for perfection. I do not consider "have a compelling system of antagonists in a game focused on dealing with antagonists" to be an unreasonable expectation, and I do not think it is unfair to say that this expectation is not being met by having a significant portion of the game's antagonist-focused wordcount dedicated to castigating the unthinking abstract power-source that makes Made-Of-Evil minions for being evil before it gives a single detailed Dark General or puts a real name to an example Defiler.

    I think that's plenty defensible as a read for a book you've described as currently unfinished but feature-complete.​
