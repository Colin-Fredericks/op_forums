                Originally posted by LostLight View Post
                makes sense. I do remember many references, but I've thought it was a fan term, not official one. I probably saw it once or twice- but I probably saw it as a metaphorical thing instead of some entity which is the manifestation of vampirehood. That the outcome of having some problems at focusing while reading long texts, especially when they lack some story structure. That's why I prefer my game books more as manuals instead of masses of words, so I can jump between sections without feel like reading the book drains the life out of me (hence why I just can't bring myself to read the Clan books or Secrets of the Covenants).
                I actually have a theory about that, with the Curse infecting blood like a metaphysical virus that then causes the supernatural alterations and abilities we see in vampires and their related things like ghouls and dhampir. It's not so much that the Blood is a manifestation of vampirehood or a conscious entity that's exerting will on the setting as it's a meta origin for all vampire-like beings in the Chronicles of Darkness. It's a separate layer of supernatural biology that follows, loosely, the principles of evolution and speciation for vampires. It also serves as the binding glue between all of Vampire: The Requiem's concepts in the same way that the existence of Spirits is for werewolves, or ghosts for Sin-Eaters.

                I think the Curse is always the same; it's a single magical...something from somewhere that was created or entered the world in the distant past. However, once it becomes the Blood, it can have a wide variety of effects and mutations. The Curse causes a few "universal" traits, like the intake of blood and the creation of Vitae and the animation of dead tissue. But it also reacts differently in different hosts, creating different "species" of vampire. Kindred vs. Jiang Shi vs. Strix (who could be the equivalent of patient zero for the Curse, having spread it into the mortal world, or are just another strain that found purchase in the ephemeral instead of the blood and tissue).

                The Blood passes down between sire and childe, creating bloodlines and clans as related groups of vampire that are all part of the same "species." However, since all vampires spring from the same Curse, it's possible for vampires to share Blood and, thus, their unique powers and weaknesses. Do that enough between a large enough population, and you can actually blend "species" together until they become more similar than distinct. (Thus, the Kindred, five clans that have grown together enough to be considered a single(ish) body) The Jiang Shi in the Triangle are, in my opinion, a perfect (and deliberate...) example of a non-Kindred strain of vampire that's slowly being blended into the wider Kindred population. Give it enough time, and the Phantoms likely will be as similar to the Daeva or Ventrue as those two clans are to each other.

                So then you have things like ghouls, which are still-living organisms that are exposed to just enough of the Curse (via the Blood) to begin exhibiting its traits, while not being so overcome that they are totally transformed. A revenant is a vampire whose Blood doesn't contain enough of the Curse to stabilize itself in its new existence. Dhampir are created when magic allows a little bit of the Curse to pass to a child without the Blood, creating weird and unnatural (from the perspective of both normal life and the vampires' supernatural approximation of it)

                Anyway. The bottom line is that the Curse creates the Blood, and the Blood creates vampires. And vampires spread the Curse.. Everything else spins off from that. If that all makes sense...
