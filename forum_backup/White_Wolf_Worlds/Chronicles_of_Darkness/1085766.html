    Mapping the Black Sun [Cosmology Making Thread]

    04-30-2017, 08:57 AM
    So ever since I've posted my cosmology interpretation to the Grand Unified Theories of the World of Darkness thread, I've found myself constantly returning to the original post, making slight adjustments or adding new details to it. I must admit, world building in cosmological scale is an hobby of mine, and I have made a number of iterations to my cosmology- but while I am pretty sure about the basic structure, I was never quit happy with the exact details. Sometimes, it becomes too fractured, a collection of many small details who don't really have any solid connection between them. On others, some overall myth starts to take over, from which I wasn't really happy. Anyway, I've decided to make yet another attempt at building a cosmology for the CofD- but this time, I've thought about detailing not only how I view the setting's multiverse and Powers that Be, but also the thought process which leads me to make certain decisions about the said cosmology- both in order to make sure I won't get lost in the process, and perhaps so it would be a bit more useful for people who want to make their own cosmologies.

    And without any further notice, here we go-

    The Big Picture

    This is the most basic step at planned world building, IMO. You need to ask yourself what do you need for your cosmology, why do you need it and how the world you make fulfill those requirements. Some cosmological ideals are very good and interesting- but they are not useful for you, as either a ST, player or even just a plain reader, except as heretic rumors or in-game creation myths. While sometimes good things can be "spontaneously assembled" from tinkering with different setting parts and try to find which works with which, usually it's better to take a step back, think about your needs and wants first, and then start putting the different pieces in place. Examining and criticizing your work during the process would make sure you won't get lost with your ideals, and that the end product itself is what you really need for your games, and not just a nice waste of time.

    So, like I've mentioned, the first thing you need to ask yourself is the goal. Are you a ST or a player? Is the cosmological map going to take an active part of your Chronicles, or just something which sits in the background? Is it going to work only for a certain gameline, or is it meant to be a crossover friendly? After all, if your game is based mostly about changelings and you decide to add vampires just to "spice things up", you don't really need to invest too much of effort at thinking about the origin of vamprisim- just blame the Gentry or connect them to certain Goblin Fruit which turns those who eat it into undead- all of the theories vampires has about their nature is simply irrelevant, and in your game, also untrue. The same for Mages and Mummies- Aaru could be the Supernal or the Supernal could be Aaru, the Devourer may be an Abyssal invader (if not the Abyss itself) and the Exarch and the Judges may be the same thing. The question "who is right" depends on the gameline you currently playing.

    However, some cosmologies (like this one) are meant to be wider- meant to exist as full crossover material, either for practical reasons (making a crossover game) or just for fun (like this one). At those cases, you may still prefer to give special preference for one of the existing cosmologies (such as Mage's or Mummy's), and claim it to be a little more "correct" than the others- the other versions of reality aren't wrong, but are flawed, and are actually twisted reflections of the true map of the cosmos. Such interpretation could work, but it may feel like you are giving a special attention to one gameline over the others, making its protagonist into more important than the rest of the setting's supernaturals and undermine their themes (not to mention start an internet war or two about how "mages know EVERYTHING"). Another option is making a very fluid, very simplistic version of reality, perhaps based around a certain real life mythology. Maybe there are nine worlds floating around Yggdrasil. Perhaps there is only Heaven and Hell, with Earth in the Middle. Perhaps there are fives echoes of the world, with each reflect a different aspect of a single world. Something which is both simple, yet fluid.

    And then, there are kitchen sink cosmologies. Like this one.

    A kitchen sink cosmology is the closest thing for "canon" cosmology you may have for the CofD- all stories are true, all mythologies are false. There isn't any single, unified "world of darkness", but many varied small worlds, cloistered together like a bag of marbles. Who said you can have only one Underworld? What stops you from having as many "hells" as possible in the same time? Why does the existence of God stops the Crone from walking with Him side by side, sharing a glass of Bloody Mary from time to time? While it is the easiest solution, it must be managed carefully- while chaos is a feature of that cosmology, it could easily backfire, as suspension of disbelief collapse under its own weight and Occam wildly wave with his Razor, cutting unnecessary information and complications out of pure despair so things may make sense again. Because of that, when making a kitchen sink cosmology, deciding about your goal is the most important thing you need to do.

    Now, let's try to phrase the exact goal for this cosmology (other than wasting my time instead of reading about photolithography):

    The major goal of this cosmology is giving each splat the respect it deserves in the CofD. That means treating every sub-cosmology as true, yet different and independent from each other. All realms of existence and all gods are all true at the same time, making the multiverse into a loose coalition of different Powers That Be, with each having its own agenda and endgame it has in mind. Some of those powers are neighbors, some are friends, some are enemies, some may even be lovers (who knows? May the Principle is the God Machine's axe?). The multiverse is wide and broad, big enough to contain as many worlds as needed. Some may be closer to the top, others closer to the bottom, but each has a place, agenda and plans of its own to the whole world. You may say that the material world is some sort of compromise between the many gods of the setting, with the Crapsack World the characters live in is the best the "good guys" of the universe could achieve under the terms and conditions forced by the "bad guys". It is a wide world of darkness, which is meant to swallow everything which we could throw at it.. to a certain limit, of course.

    And we are going to throw a lot at it, actually.

    First, this isn't going to include only canon material. It is going to include a lot of homebrew as well, written both by myself and by others- yet it should be flexible enough that other people should be able to take the parts they don't like out while the structure itself won't break down. Worse, I'm going to add certain features from the WoD setting (yes, the Classic one), and try to adapt certain aspects of their cosmology to this setting. Cain is going to play chess with the Strix, the Wyrm and Ammut can start fighting on scraps of meat, Dreaming's Changelings live in grand castles near the forests of the Primordial Dream and the Fallen try to escape the Hell the Unchained yearn for so much. Yes, it is not going to be easy- but I believe it would be possible. In order to make sure we won't lose our goal in the process, however, here are some ground rules we are going to follow-

    1) each major splat, playable or not, should be represented among the Powers The Be. Some minor splats or sub-splats may also have their own patrons, but at least one such patron exist for a supernatural breed.

    2) each realm of existence belong to one major god. Gaea may share Hisil with Luna and Helios, but she is the big boss of the Shadow. The Watchtowers shine brightly upon the Supernal, but everyone know who are the Kings of the Lie. The Machine constantly work to maintain the world, but it doesn't mean it belongs to it. There is no realm with a god to patron it, and at many cases the difference between the realm and its patron are not so distinct- after all, Duar may well be the Maw of the Devourer herself.

    3) first among equals- while this cosmology is going to include many small features and treat everyone as best as possible, sometimes harsh choice would be made, and we also have to decide from where to start- after all, the decision of from which gameline we start to work on may well define the cosmology's finale structure. Because of them, we need to decide beforehand who is more important than whom. Because the monsters of the setting usually take all the spotlight, we should start with them as the base. Afterward, we'll start working on the Blue Books and their protagonists, and see what they have to offer. The next step would be my personal homebrew. The next should be other fansplats and homebrew which I've collected through the ages (I'll do my best to credit everyone, of course). Then, finally, we should peek before the apocalypse and scavenge among the many horrors the WoD has to offer- including the famous Zombie: the Coil. And if we'll manage to survive it all, I may even add some thoughts about Exalted's great heroes, being adapted to the ravening Darkness in which all light fails.

    And that's it for now! The next post would start from where every CofD should start- the Core! Stay tuned :P

      The Core of Darkness

      so, let's take a look at the Core book, shall we?

      The book itself is focused around regular people as their protagonists- poor souls which has came in contact with powers they don't completely understand. It is the classical Horror Story, in which you open the door you shouldn't have, you look at the wrong alley, you wondered about the screams which come from your neighbor's place and peeked through the window. It is a game about people who were just in the wrong place in the wrong time and made the wrong choice and now they are screwed. Most of them would die, horribly, we can imagine. Some would survive- but they would never be the same people again, always watching the shadows to see if something is going to come out and drug them back in. They are not hunters, who chase the darkness with zeal and are willing to sacrifice their life just to light another candle- they are people who just want to close their curtains, lock their doors and go to sleep- telling themselves that there are no monsters under their bed, there isn't a not-so-dead body in their closet and that the screams which come from the street is just the wind blowing on an opened gate. They know that there is evil out there, but they can't (or don't want to) do anything to stop it.

      The cosmology should reflect that.

      As I've said before, the main idea behind this cosmology is that the world is as is, because of a compromise between the different powers of the universe- powers which care less about the people who live in it and more about their own agendas. Well, at least, most of them. Some care deeply about mankind- they love it, in a way. They need it. They don't see them as tools or pawns, but as something their must take care for, tend them and groom them and make sure that they would be as beautiful and perfect as possible. The problem- their ideals for "beauty" or "perfection" are not what usually come to mind- for them, beauty is blood splattered on the walls. For them, perfection is a person's last breath. People who cower in fear and scream with horror or driven into the depths of madness are just a beautiful sight for those powers, while things like self sacrifice, love and altruism are disgusting corruption of the natural state. Humanity is at its finest when it fears, all else is just an ugly, ugly mockery of mankind's true nature.

      And they would do anything it takes to stop that from happening.

      So, when looking at the Core, we can see 5 "major splats" (mortals, spirits, angels, ghosts and horrors) and one realm of existence (the material). The Twilight is also mentioned, although most books treat it as a "state of matter" instead of a different plane (except in Mummy, in which the Neter Khertet is the realm of Anpu, but we'll talk about it in depth when we'll reach that gameline). Anyway, the three Ephemerals are pretty easy to map into the cosmology- spirits belong to the Shadow, Ghosts to the Underworld and Angels to the God Machine. Horrors and regular (or even Supernatural) mortals belong to the material realm. Horrors present a challenge in that that each of them is usually a unique creation made through unique conditions- horrors don't share a single origin, but are usually creations of things that happens just because the world is so fucked up. Mortals give us another problem, as thinking about what or who could be humanity's patron could prove itself quit problematic.

      So, let's start with the easiest parts of the Core- first, where the material plane exist in out cosmology. There are many options for that- the original 1E Mage cosmology put it in the bottom, although the existence of the Lower Depths pushed it to the middle. We could also put it in the top, just to shake some things up, but I can't see the real benefit of doing so. It may exist side by side with many other, alternative "material planes", something like a cloister of alternative dimensions. However, as we decided on making the material plane into a compromise, I think that putting it in the middle and building the rest of the cosmology around it makes the most sense. The next question is "who is the material realm's patron?", but it isn't something that easy to answer, so I'll ignore it for now.

      Now, the Twilight could be easily resolved in our paradigm as an "high energy" state of matter. The future Mummy review may change it, but for now, as it is not a different realm, we do not need to bother ourselves and forcing some high level "Twilight God" to rule it (Anpu would probably feel that niche later, but again, we have some time before dealing with it).

      Now, we have the most common of all Ephemerals- ghosts. Ghosts all linger in the material (which makes sense, as they are all born from it), but eventually fall down to the depths of the Underworld to rise with Rank and power. We also know that ghosts, while may still be people, are not souls and don't have souls. That should make you ask what ghosts exactly are- the basic answer is "ghosts are ghosts", that is, they are a category of their own, but I prefer a more in-depth explanation. You may also say that they are simply an agglomeration of death energy which mimics a person's death, but I find that it takes something from being a ghost- it turns them into a lifeless automaton, which is ok to torment and use for your own ends. In that case, I think we should say that ghosts are exactly what the book says they are- echoes of the dead. Ghosts are the imprint of human soul and life, something which preserves the image of things which people truly cared about. It may be people, it may be animals or plants, it may be whole cities. Ghosts may form for still living people (even if it is not common), as they have a sudden change in personality or leave their past behind. Ghosts are out memories, our unfulfilled hopes and fears, dreams and nightmares, passions which weren't resolved and regrets which still haunts us. Ghosts are us, the part of us that still fight against all chances, even against Death itself.

      The question is- what that makes the Underworld into? I think that the best way to describe the Underworld would be as a huge storage room, where the world throws in all the junk it ever had. It is every regret we ever had, every failed hope or broken dream. As long as a ghost clings to the material, it can still try and win, can still have its last regret fulfilled. Once in the Underworld, however, all hope is lost- this is the Boulevard of Broken Dreams, where only the shadows walk beside of you. As ghosts fall into the Underworld, they no longer care- not anymore. They lose themselves, allow themselves to be enslaved to rules which make no sense just in order to feel something, to give themselves some sort of purpose. Those who survive long enough in that state, lose more and more of their humanity until they become things which are no longer human- the chtonians, terrible god-ghosts of unfulfilled desire, who cling to anything which could remind them they were once alive. If we want to give a patron to those death-gods, it should be the first ghost which ever existed, the first regret mankind ever had, the first hope which was broken and shattered yet still tries to hold on- even if it forgot why it even tries. If I were to give it a name, I think I would call it Proserpina. Proserpina, however, is not the ruler of the Underworld- that mystery being would be discovered later, during Geist's section. Anyway, true to its name, the Underworld would be under the material world. Simple.

      Now, let's talk about spirits. Unlike ghosts, spirits were never human or owe their origin to mankind. While humanity shapes and transforms the Essence and the Shadow, Hisil does not owe it anything. Of all invisible realms, I imagine Hisil would be the closest of all to the material, separated only by a thin, yet powerful, veil called the Gauntlet. Spirits are beings birthed from wild and primal Essence, created together with the world. Following the stories of the Forsaken, once the Spirit and the Flesh were one- although now we all know it's not true, at least as long as humanity birthed. However, maybe at its very beginning, when the world was still young and hot, there was some truth to the claim- maybe both the Hisil and the material were made as one, and as time passed and Earth cooled, the two levels of reality has started to separate from one another. Eventually, a transition zone was created, were the pillars of creation stood to support both he Flesh and the Spirit- Pangaea- and the greatest among those pillars became its god- Father Wolf. However, those things would be explored later, during Werewolf or Dark Eras. In the meanwhile, we can safely say that the Hisil is the world's natural Essence, and that spirits are spawned naturally from that Essence, symbols of powerful events which took place around them. Unlike ghosts, spirits don't represent the past, but the present. They are a reflection of the world as is, of everything which is important and relevant. When you think about it, it's funny- after all, humans don't have spirits, what does it say about us?

      Anyway, according to our paradigm, every realm of existence have its "core god", and the question is whose the Shadow's? Luna and Helios are both powerful beings, but they are too distant from the world to be its primal gods. Father Wolf could have been a decent candidate, but he is dead- and Pangaea collapsed together with him, meaning he was the god of that kingdom. Mage's 1E claimed it to be "Ain Soph", but Ain Sop usually means something divine and distant, so let's ignore it for now until reaching Mage's section. Because of that, I think that the best candidate for Hisil's great patron would be Gaea- the spirit of the world itself. It makes sense, as the first spirit to ever exist should be the world's- the Spirit of the Flesh, so to speak. Of course, that brings to question where Gaea may be- and may answers is *everywhere*. Gaea is the Shadow- when you walk on Hisil, you walk on her Flesh. When you breath its air, you breath her blood. Luna guards her from the darkness with silvery light, Helios burns her surface and scourge those who exploit her. Gaea is everywhere- and no one knows it. And that's the way she liked it.

      The third of the Ephemerals are the angels- mechanisms created by a divine machinery which works toward an unknown goal. Unlike other Ephemerals, angels don't come from some unknown realm of existence- they belong to our world. This is their heaven, this is their god. They all are built for certain missions, all meant to maintain the status que- to maintain their heaven- and increase the influence of their patron to make sure it won't turn into Hell. Their work isn't yet complete- for as long as the status que may change, the Heaven is yet to triumph. Preforming changes in order to maintain the status que- sounds ironic, don't you think? At any case, we all know who is the god of angels- the God Machine, the grand mechanism created long ago for some unknown purpose- although that as a machine, it must have one. While its goals may vary, I've always liked the idea that the God Machine was made years ago by mankind in order to make the world into a better place- into a true heaven. However, some disaster has happened (see the Pain Prophet's Testament), and all things went to hell. In order to stop the collapse, its last operators (before losing control over it), gave it one last command- keep the world as is. That was a mistake- as by doing so, the God Machine also works to stop any attempt to make the world into a better place, but in their eyes, it was better than the alternative. Maybe one day, mankind would discover the secrets to operate it (by completing the Black Sun Map, the God Machine's operation instructions which is described by the true cosmology of the CofD), but until then, it would do what it has always done- maintaining the status que.

      So, after finishing with the Ephemerals, let's go to the main problem- horrors. As I've mentioned before, horrors lack any common origin, each being a unique creation. However, in 2E, vampires are in the same situation- a collection of unrelated beings which all share the same characteristics. While horrors are much more diverse than vampires, if we could unite vamprisim as the same phenomena expressed through different mediums, maybe we could do the same for horrors. They have, after all, one thing in common- they are all meant to scare the shit out of you! Now, let's assume that all horrors, while created under different conditions, ultimately are expression of that same phenomena, the same dark power we discussed earlier which loves humanity so much, but express that love in a way abhorrent to any sane being. Something like Ravenloft's Dark Powers- unknown force which grants you gifts for being evil, and in the same time use those gifts to corrupt you even further, trapping you in your own story. You can defeat those forces- but it may require sacrificing your life to do so. That unknown power, which reward wickedness and adores fear and hate, that power is the true master of the material world, perhaps the true maker of all existence. It is the core of the cosmology, a being which lurks behind the scenes and plays a long game with the other forces of creation to make sure it would always stay on the top. It creates horrors. It grants Supernatural Merits. It manipulates and corrupts, lies and cheats. It has whispered to the first people to rebel against their masters, and to their masters to shut the great Machine down with one last command. Those are the Chronicles of Darkness, and that power, is that Darkness.

      From where the Darkness came from no one knows. Most likely it always was, and always will be- the creator of the world, which made it in its own image. It may have even made the first humans to suffer under its reign- but while it loves humans, it is not the patron of regular mortals. Leave it to the Darkness, and we'll all turn corrupted like horrors, fearful from our own shadow as every action we take would lead to the worst case scenario. Something stops it, something balance it- not the delicate pacts made with distant powers from beyond existence. No, there is a force which may not love humans as much as the Darkness does, but it hates the Darkness ten times more. A power which has great interests in regular, plain mortals, which wants to protect them and gives them the tools to fight the Darkness. A power which sent its first messengers (Pain Prophet's Angels) to teach mankind to build the great Machine in order to fight its enemy. A power which came to a world of chaos and darkness which covered the deep.

      God. The Biblical God.

      Under that paradigm, God is not the maker of the world- but under certain interpretations, he also didn't done so in the bible. The world was already exist when God came in and spoke "light", giving order to chaos and banishing the darkness from the world. Our cosmology would use that interpretation- the Darkness made the world, and tormented the people around it. God, a foreign being from perhaps another universe, discovered the world of the Darkness and was disgusted. It intervene, giving its help in order to fight the Darkness and support mankind against the dark powers. The Darkness didn't liked it, and created thousands of horrors to battles. The other Powers That Be took a stand, making alliances and breaking them. Some took the side of the Darkness, their parent. Others choose God's. Some just kept to themselves, supporting both sides or neither of them. The result is the world as we know it- the compromise, few points of light in a vast ocean of darkness. God, having his interests in the material world, exist in it to battle the Darkness- but he also leads somewhere else, far above the world, in a place you would only know as Eden, and from which all souls who were not devoured by the Darkness would eventually return. Eden is both in the material and faraway from it, the one place of purity which the Darkness could never corrupt, unless all rest would fall beforehand.

      so- let's sum up the cosmology we have until now, from the highest to the lowest-

      Eden- God- Mortals

      Material- Horrors/Supernatural Mortals- Darkness; Hisil- Spirits- Gaea; Infrastructures- Angels- God Machine

      Twilight?

      Underworld- Ghosts- Proserpina

      Well, that's all for today- next time, Vampires!

                          Vampire: the Requiem

                          So I've tried to make this post a number of times, and I didn't manage to finish it. Sure, some times is was just a stroke of bas luck (the post was destroyed by some accidental click of the mouse), while on others it happened because of Vampire's greatest weakness for cosmology writing, which is also its greatest strength as an interesting game- it is way too rich game.

                          Seriously, trying to analyze all of its lore would take me a lifetime, not to mention that I lose interest while writing the post. Any attempt to squish all of its mythology into a single post is doomed to fail, but that doesn't mean a cosmological post for Vampire is impossible- it only means that instead of dealing with the problem, we need to find a way to go around it, and thankfully, Vampire has gave us the perfect way to do so- that is, vampirism as a phenomenon.

                          Instead of trying to give a "correct" explanation for each Clan and Covenant origin myth, we would use our cosmology's "flexibility" feature and the games own themes in order to create a fluid cosmology which would allow us avoid much of the mess. Just as Rose once said, it doesn't matter if you are stabbed, shot or hit by a car- a wound is a wound, and the wound is vampirism. We don't need to explain why and how the wound was formed- people suffer from countless small cuts through their life, most of them not leaving even a scar behind. Instead, we need to explain the wound itself- why does the blood is spilled, how painful or dangerous is it, what colour is the flesh and more. Our interest is the wound, not the knife or gun or car, not to mention the one who actually performed the act. The story may be interesting, but it is not important for out cosmology. What we want is an explanation for why vampirism, as a phenomenon, exists.

                          And for that, we are going to use the Crone.

                          Now, there may be some problems at using the Crone as Vampirism incarnated- after all, it may feel like it gives an approval for the Circle's ideology over the other covenants, not to mention that the Gangrel's Echidna may well be the Crone herself. However, when looking at the said splats, I must say it does make sense in the game's context- first; it is a being from inside of Vampire's own mythology. Second, the Circle sees being a vampire is some sort of "divine state", with them "releasing their inner divinity". The Crone is already the best symbol for vampirism the game has ever gave us. And finally, we all know that the Gangrel are considered to be the closest to the Beast of all other Clans, while other Clans have their own unique reasons for their existence- some of them, like the Julii (and maybe the Daeva), are closer to the strix than the rest of the Clans. The Mekhet may be connected to Sutekh, the Nameless Serpent. The Nosferatu may come from a lost Pangaean, and the Ventrue could track their origin to the Empyrean itself. The Gangrel as the "purest" form of vampirism, as the first Clan to raise from the mess it was during the Neolithic era does make sense.

                          In sort, we are going to go with it.

                          Now, after establishing the Crone as the "Power that Be", we need to find where is her realm in the great multiverse. It is quit clear that the "realm of vampirism" is found somewhere in the Lower Depths- standing side by side with countless other hungry, small hells. Each of them lacks something- morality, creativity, memory, existence… life. Everyone can agree the "life" is what the vampiric realm lacks- vampires live on pure life force, consuming it to survive. The Lower Depths define by what they eat, and they eat what they lack. We are talking about a lifeless realm, a place where nothing lives, nothing grows nor rots. A realm, were even the concept of immortality lacks meaning- as does who were never alive, can not live forever. A parasitic, undead realm which feeds upon the whole universe like a giant tick.

                          Using Vampire's terms once more, we are going to call this terrible place Dis.

                          Now, we could leave Dis as simply being a mystery, a dot in the map which simply says "this is where vampires come from", but I personally believe that if you take the effort and make a great map of the cosmos, the least you could do is making it cool- saying "there is a realm which is the source of vampirism, yet no one knows where it is or what it is and they can't ever find their way into it" is pretty boring. Instead, I think it is much more interesting to detail it a bit, to give people an interest to investigate that dread realm and discover new things about it.

                          Thinking about Dis as a realm without life, made me think that while it may be a terrible place to live in, it could be a great place to be dead in- or, at least, be buried in there. That's true especially for beings which can't usually die- immortal beings, gods, strange divinities and other beings which simply don't know "death". They could be broken and shattered, but their mind would still live, suffering from endless torments, unable to ever heal. This is why they go to Dis- a place in which their immortality is no longer in effect, and as such they could finally rest. That would make Dis into a graveyard, a vast mausoleum where the dead gods sleep their deathless sleep, no longer tormented by being unable to die. And the great undertaker of that place is a being which feasts upon the lost immortality of each of those dead gods- the Crone, a being which was never alive, and as such could never die.

                          Now, on the surface, that should be enough for Vampire- here! We have a Patron deity and a realm! Finished! However, I must say that it is not enough. First, vampires are not the only template in the game- we also have ghouls, revenants, strixs and the many other weird things we got in Wicked Dead and Ancient Mysteries. And even if we manage to squeeze it all to a single Power that Be, we can't ignore the fact that the Crone can't explain everything which goes in Vampire- Theban Sorcery, for example.

                          So, before deciding what does not "belong" to the Crone, let's decide what is- which means, everything which comes directly from being a Vampire. That includes most Disciplines, Threnodies, and things like Blood Oaths and Carthian Laws- sure, the last two are Covenant specific, but they are just specific manifestations of vampire nature. It does not come from an "outer" power which manipulates the Curse. That also includes Curac- or, at the very least, certain Styles of it. That means that the Coils of the Dragon/ Treasures of the Sakadagami, Theban Sorcery and other forms of Blood Sorcery are not under the Crone's domain, and need to be explained- which means we are going to investigate at least some of the more mystical Covenants.

                          Let's start from the (seemingly) simplest of all such "strange" phenomena- Theban Sorcery. On the surface, all we need to do is connect it to God (which already had his existence established in the previous post) and be done with it. However, while Theban Sorcery at its modern form may be connected to God (or at least, one of his angels), it was already established that Theban Sorcery has much older roots than Christianity, being connected to the magics of Osiris and Ra. While we can just claim that the "Egyptian" Theban Sorcery was just another Style of the modern one, we can't ignore the fact that Theban Sorcery is powered by a certain aspect of the vampire's soul- his Man. And just as the Man stands against the Beast, so does the Crone requires someone to stand against her, top balance her, fighting her in her own realm. And just out of symmetry's sake, just as the Gangrel are the most bestial of Clans, the Ventrue are the most human- and so, let's call that "Man incarnated" Cronus.

                          Do not be confused- the fact that Cronus represents the Humanity of vampire-kind, it does not mean he is kind. Like the Titan which his name he caries, he would eat his own sons in order to ensure his own rule. Unlike the Crone, Cronus is a foreign to Dis- he is the first king humanity has ever had, which in search for becoming a god searched for the place were the gods themselves die and drank their blood- only to become trapped in the kingdom he wished to rule. He fights the Crone be maintaining the humanity of vampires- his descent to Dis may have been the time when the first vampires rose above bestial mindsets and detached themselves from the Best for the first time. Cronus is also as mad as the Clan he helped to spawn- believing himself to be the king of the world, he constantly fears being dethroned by his "children". Maybe he was already dethroned in the past, only for the new "king" becoming as paranoid and mad as its predecessor, and eventually believing himself to be the old, mad king.

                          The next thing we are going to explain is going to be the Coils of the Dragon. Those powers are based around the vampire becoming something "more" than the regular, blood sucking monster- evolving beyond the vampire state into a greater form. The 3rd tier form of the Ordo has claimed that the being to represent that concept is the Devil- yet we already going to have our own fair share of demon kings in this cosmology. Because of that, we are going to call the power behind the Coils the Dragon- both a nickname to the Devil and Dracula. No one knows who or what the Dragon truly is- perhaps it was once a vampire. Perhaps it is Dracula himself. Perhaps Dracula ate him to become him and reach new heights. All that is known is that the Dragon is locked in Dis, bound by powerful seals forged by Cronus himself and blessed by the Crone. The Dragon constantly evolves, trying to escape his chains, and so the two constantly add new seals. One day, it may not be enough- and the Dragon would escape, bringing chaos to all of Dis.. and perhaps, the whole world.

                          Ok, now I've came to the unfortunate conclusion that no matter how much I'll try, I'll never finish all of Vampire in a single post. So, we are going to break it to 2-3 posts! Next time, we are going to cover the strix and other supernatural creatures detailed in Vampire, and the secret powers which work behind the small covenants of the world. Stay tuned!
