                                    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                    Those benefits come to Forsaken only when they start to agree with ideology, And even then, converts from Forsaken to Pure are treated badly, at most as Omega in Pure packs. Why then join your enemy that is not have compelling ideology, have clashing mythology with what you fight for and all benefits are last to you as you will be treated badly in their group?
                                    Because even if you're treated as the lowest of the low of the Pure Pack you joined, treated as the "Omega", you're still a member of the Pack. And because of that, you're still entitled to the protection of the Pack. The Pack's alpha is still responsible for your actions, your safety is still their concern and the concern of the Pack's Totem. Even if that concern is only so much that no one outside of the Pack can treat you like shit. Because if someone from outside the Pack disses you? They've insulted your Pack's Alpha and now need to deal with THEM.

                                    Forget ideology. The compelling argument here is, if aimed at a Ghost Wolf, "You are alone. You have no one to watch out for you. Spirits hate you and other werewolves treat you with paranoia. Cast that aside, renounce Luna and join us. I and our god will protect you. If someone outside of us wants to hurt or kill you, they have to fight through all of us, because you would be of our Pack and you would be mine and our god's and we protect what is ours."

                                    Yes, you'd be the lowest member of the Pack. But that just means you only have a lot to prove, right? It's your responsibility to show that you're not the weakest of the Pack and if you can show your strength, if you can show how much of a help you can be to your Pack and your Alpha, that means they'd respect you, right?

                                    ...And that's the thinking that traps people. The feeling that their situation is their fault, that things would be better if they only DID things better.

                                    This is why when you think of Pure converting people? The Pure aren't really targeting Forsaken, or at least not Forsaken who are set in their ways. They're targeting the fresh blood. Ghost Wolves who don't have allegiances set. Young Uratha who don't know whats going on and crave simple answers for why they feel the urges they have. Forsaken who have just started to walk their moonlit path and still have questions and fears about a world they're desperately trying to understand.

                                    And that's how the Pure convert. By playing on FEAR. Fear of themselves ("We can teach you what you are."), fear of the unknown ("We can tell you why the world is as it is.", it doesn't have to be TRUE, just answers, even if lies), fear of outsiders ("Obey and we will protect you.")

                                    The Pure could be preaching worship of the Great Moon Cheese Spirit, usurped by the Luna Witch Spirit, and that wouldn't change the fundamental benefits that the Pure provide their members.

                                                    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                                    In other way - I want my eventual Forsaken players to consider Pure offers. Not to become next slaves to them, but in the way "those Pure could have right, Forsaken could be wrong". Because when you portray them as them sound in first chapter of Pure book, they can convert Forsaken only on basis "join us or die".

                                                    So yes, I look for compelling parts in Pure ideology and mythology to tempt possible Forsaken players with that. Benefits coming last on this list, as players do not look on them in "dark, tragic, heroic game" that the Werewolf is. When you play Forsaken, it's because you believe that Flesh and Spirit relations should be better, that werewolves should goes in the footsteps of Father Wolf in Pangaea times, and show "human" side to the Pure.
                                                    I've played around with this a good deal in my chronicle - although I'll say first up that I make a few changes and assumptions that other Storytellers might not go for; I downplay horror, and make the Pure less rabidly threatening, trying instead to present them as sympathetic antagonists with a competing ideology.
                                                    I also play around with myth and blasphemy, which is why I think you might be interested in my approach.

                                                    The Pure, as I present them (in this chronicle, at least), want all those same things you said Forsaken want: better relations between Flesh and Spirit, to follow Father Wolf's example. They claim, though, that the Forsaken's desires are being manipulated by an envious, irrational alien tyrant; whereas the Pure approach will heal the world. And they try to only let outsiders see evidence which supports they're right.

                                                    They key ideology I've given to the local Pure (especially Fire-Touched), is that everything wrong in the Shadow is caused by Luna - but proper things do exist, and they serve those. Luna's title of "Queen of the Shadow" might have some literal truth to it in my game: there's been implications (in my setting; not published) that she controls how and when material resonance crosses the Gauntlet to become Essence. The Pure claim this drives otherwise decent spirits to abuse the material out of hunger, while any who submit to Luna get tainted by her madness, and turn cruel, alien, and warped in their purpose.

                                                    In contrast, my Fire-Touched serve the incarnae natural to earth: emotions, nature and element spirits, ancient conceptuals. These are spirits born of Earth; these are the spirits meant to rule it - the influence of celestials can only corrupt what exists here. They claim some spirits are "honest", that they know their place in the world's ecosystem, and can be reasoned with, befriended, and beneficial to both realms (and that Gurim Ur reveals such spirits to them); if these spirits are given power, and allowed to play in the material, they'll improve things - is one of the philosophies. As a result, they're well liked (and feared) throughout the Shadow.

                                                    One scene I used to emphasize this was I had the players led through a Pure territory by a Predator King shaman. Bunches of spirits were hanging out in Twilight. As they passed a clump of flowers, a gaffling meekly approached and offered chimiage of its own essence to the Predator King, who then casually used "plant growth" on the flowers to return to the gift. --An utter reversal of how things are for the Forsaken. This is the Pangaea they try to restore.


                                                    [I should note: my PCs now have better relations with the Pure than with the local Forsaken - brokering peace with them has been the longest-running goal of this chronicle. One of the players actually converted to the Fire-Touched, although he did so for the more personal, human reason of regaining the last living connection to his former pack (a dead packmate's nephew, which he got to meet and help out, is Fire-Touched) and only on the caveat that he could remain part of the players' pack (sparing him from the terrible things that happen behind closed doors).]
