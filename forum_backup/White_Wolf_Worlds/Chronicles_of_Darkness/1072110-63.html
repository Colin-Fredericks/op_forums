                                        Okay, lemme start with the beginnings of backstory:

                                        The Cold Guard (The Tenebrous Gaol)

                                        Through shadow and day, through pain and empty promises, I will not falter. I am the line, and I will hold.

                                        Once there was a Kingdom. More accurately, there were several Kingdoms, each who loved the Light. One saw Light in searing brightness of knowledge, another in the warm glow of a hearth, a third in the clarion call of destiny, among others. Together, they sealed away the Dark of the Void, a hungry, empty nothing that wished to devour all that was, or ruin it so that all others may share its pain.

                                        This is not their story. This is the story of what lay between the borders of the Kingdom, the wild lands where Light did not shine. There, away from the insular civilizations that denied them, lay the Dark, eternally mustering for the moment the borders would falter and they would destroy the hideous brightness that drove it away in shame of what it was. And it was there that the Cold Guard lived, watching the Dark to ensure it never had the chance-not because the borders could not falter, but because the Dark would not be free to do so.

                                        The Cold Guard does not keep a long history of its founding-part of that is due to their incredible age, but mostly it's because the space their dedicate to their records are mostly rolls of various prisoners and the locations of the Bolgia curse-pits they keep them in over the eons, and the security measures they must take to prevent escape or release, with a significant portion of the remainder dedicated to medical procedures and meditative techniques they use to ensure their own Endowment does not devour them. But they do have a myth; the Cold Guard was founded by an alliance of the subordinate nations of the Kingdom as an exiled force of wardens and prison keepers, meant to contain those aspects and children of the Dark too powerful to defeat without great loss of life. Ancient monster-gods of sorrow and rage, powerful demons born from the unholy union of black magic and corrupt human flesh, but above all else was the Hollow Court, a reflection of the very rulers of the Kingdom itself.

                                        Modern Guards like to use the analogy of a vacuum-whenever a Noble Blossoms, the Light that gives them power is taken out of the world, and the gap tries to fill itself with existence. Because the Light is at least partially intelligent, so is the vacuum, and it seeks its own embodiments to give power to, equal and opposite; whereas the Nobility seems concerned with bringing hope, the unfortunates possessed by the Hollow become embodiments of existential despair, semi-living monsters brimming with hunger for what they were and seeking to spread the Dark as far as possible to strip the world of the Light they need to feed their insatiable emptiness. Only the Light being returned to them, however, can sate them, and until then they would rampage eternally until the host was given a merciful end and the Hollow returned to the Dark World. Except then, of course, the Hollow is still free to seek another host and become an even greater menace than before. Thus, the Cold Guard, exiled from the Kingdom to not only track the Court, but to imprison them within their own stolen bodies within great prison complexes so that they would trouble the world no more, until it was finally time for their corresponding Noble to pass on to the next life and fill the void.

                                        Unfortunately, the Nobility, however heroic they might seem, are human. And humans fear death, to the point where comparing their own life to that of the world, some will chose their life.

                                        The Cold Guard has many theories, each more vehement then the last, about who betrayed the mission of the Guard and humanity itself to save themselves. Generally, however, the gist is that some individuals chose to invert the relationship between Light and Noble, choosing to become magic rather than filtering it into the world. How varies on the particular (from enclosing themselves in great cocoons, fleeing to the "world beyond sleep", or to consume their own bodies to power a self-sustaining spell with their minds for hearts), but what is known is that this stole even more Light than their allotted share, and their Hollows became as gods from the newfound emptiness. Even worse, lesser Hollows could be sustained by the all-consuming emptiness of the Hunger Kings, even beyond the deaths of their Nobles. Soon, all that was left of the Kingdom were its exiles-the Guard included.

                                        In the confusion, hundreds of prisoners escaped, some of which menace the world to this day. Worse, the Traitors had learned to donate their bloated Light to fools and madwomen who stole more Light to support their corrupt lieges for eternity, to haphazardly burn out the Dark in some parody of controlling the consequences of their selfishness, or indulge in childish, parodic passion plays of the ancient nations. None accepted the Cold Guard the few times they presented themselves and asked for help to resume their duties, and in many cases the Guard was even attacked due to suspicion of contamination by their own prisoners.

                                        But the Cold Guard did not learn how to contain evil by being unimaginative. In this new world, they noticed, there was no small amount of magic that did not draw from Light or Dark-not directly. This was in complete contradiction to how they were taught in the days of the Kingdom, and so, the Guard developed a theory that the rift torn by the Hunter Kings had corrupted the Dark itself; no longer was it simply a disincarnate force of consumption, but it had turned to the primal, fertile proto-energy of the dark Void that existed before time, when Light and Darkness had not become disparate. These new powers were atavisms to that time, neither good nor evil, inherently creative or destructive. The most easily available magic, however, was still the Dark, seeing as how most of it was still pure-and thus, pure evil. But, just as the Empty Kings corrupted it from pure malevolence to amoral power, could not the Cold Guard do the same by understanding-and controlling-the parts of them most like the Kings?

                                        And so the Tenebrous Gaol learned to turn the Dark upon itself, and transform its own Shadows into its bars, a hell of its own making.

                                                    If the Guard had a function in the ancient Kingdom(s), how does Alhambra (which dates back to then) treat them?
                                                    Well, the Guard hates Alhambra, because to them, Tears is replicating their mythical Original Sin, for the same motive, every time they drill for lamp oil. They have a shoot-on-sight order for Princesses of Tears, and that has led to them being referred to in Alhambra as having been corrupted by the Dark, driven mad by the Queen "needing" to abandon them. To Alhambra, they are rebels from the Provinces who are aware they are rebels, and willingly spurn the Light out of hatred for its perfect user.

                                                    Naturally, this view would make them even angrier if they knew it, because they don't hate the Light at all. They regard themselves as martyrs trying to stabilize the balance between it and the Dark, while Tears is a parasite who ruins it not because it is her nature, but because she wants to. In many ways, they're what Alhambra could have been if it wasn't so self-centered.
