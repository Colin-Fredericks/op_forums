    Originally posted by atamajakki View Post
    I'm honestly just adding the new direction for Seemings to the list of reasons why I'm really not excited for 2e. The game as a whole appears to be headed in a direction that I really don't care for, and has lost the magic of the original.
    Originally posted by Eleusis View Post
    I don't mind the method of escape giving you the Seeming, but in that case tying them to physical appearances seems odd to me.

    I also really do not like the new direction of the Fairest, which were my favorite 1E splat because of their fey callousness, whereas now their whole thing is feeling responsibility more than anyone else.
    Originally posted by Khalvin8 View Post
    I have one friend who plays a Trickster Fox Changeling. A Beast seeming. For this player the novelty of the character includes the thematic of the Beast Seeming, which for her has been the largest descriptor of her character for three years now.
    The Character is highly social, as befitting a sly trickster and suave manipulator. But 2e Beast overtly contextualizes the seeming as anti-social, even anti-culture. She is presently frustrated with how to re-frame her own character to this new design. The Cruse and Blessing mechanics directly conflict with the character's design. I should further state she is very open to changing this character's Seeming. But for her the new Beast design/sample/example rubs her raw.

    I'll be honest here. If I can't get the players that sit at my table excited about 2e Changeling. I have no use for it. This thread is my attempt to make my concerns known. I hope it is coming across respectfully. I love a lot of the rest that is coming with this new edition of Changeling. But for five out of seven of my friends, these samples alone have turned them cold to even broaching the topic of a 2e game.
    I must echo all of these posts in the dissatisfaction those I play with and I are feeling with the previewed information we've read regarding Changeling 2e. While I like the idea of switching Kiths and Seemings in theory, since many players just took Dual Kith anyway, much of the additional changes to the fluff surrounding this innocuous alteration seem entirely unasked for and do absolutely nothing but disrupt the original flavor of the game and limit choices we previously had. In presenting Kiths and Seemings in this new way, a specific sort of narrative is being forced on the player, one that goes against the very broad approach that the original game had.

    When you say you're going to switch Kiths and Seemings to just do what everyone did anyway, that's really a savvy observation of the fanbase's behavior and all you need to do. Otherwise, Changeling was already good and Changeling's players were already satisfied with how it was. You don't need to fix what isn't broken, and the more you alter something from its original portrayal, even in fine details, the more it stops being what it used to be. Such fine details are things like privateers and Loyalists no longer getting to be actual Changelings for no good reason, which damages their significance as antagonists in subtle but absolutely significant ways.

    Ideally, Changeling's fluff should have been largely preserved, taking the original corebook as a foundation and drawing from its proceeding sourcebooks with regards to how they expounded upon various details and further unified the gameline as a whole. When I read that things were being altered to fit "in line" with nWoD 2e stylistically, I could only look from side to side and then pensively ask, "Why?" Changeling was already pretty grimdark in and of itself and, as is par for the course, took the myths and legends of old and applied them to a modern context. Given that, in what way does it not already stylistically fit what we've come to know as the World of Darkness? Bringing it further "in line" with any sort of unified style only serves to make it lose its unique fairytale aesthetic, something that I definitely felt was missing from every single preview.

    Indeed, that's something of a theme a friend of mine has been seeing with nWoD 2e products: too much focus on style and flash. Everyone is dangerous and sexy and primal; the vampires are dangerous and sexy and primal; the werewolves are dangerous and sexy and primal; and every sort of Changeling Seeming is their own brand of dangerous and sexy and primal. In approaching the fluff this way, much of the substance and meaning behind each splat has slowly begun to slip away and they're all beginning to read the same.

        I never get why people take the, "if it isn't broken, don't fix it," mentality to shut down the possibility of making something better. If you end up liking CtL 1e better, it exists and is fully playable. That is no reason to reject the idea that the people working on the games shouldn't try to make a better game; even if that better game ends up not appealing to everyone.

                                    As has been stated already, most of these complaints are for things that do not seem to be present in the teasers.
