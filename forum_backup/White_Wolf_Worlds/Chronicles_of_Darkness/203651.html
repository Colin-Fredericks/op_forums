    Show me your Hunter characters!

    07-21-2014, 05:17 AM
    I'll start with two I'm rather proud of.
    • Ridley Duquette, ghoul in the service of the Cheiron Group’s FPD. Duquette was the reluctant blood slave of a vampire, but was too thoroughly addicted to the monster’s blood to dream of escaping. When an FPD team kicked in the door and staked his master, Ridley demanded to join up with them; now, he’s one of their top agents. Cheiron keeps the ghoul on a short leash - they supply him with the monthly dose of vampire blood that keeps him alive, and will cut it off if he gets uppity - and treats him like a glorified attack dog. Most agents find him incredibly creepy.
    • Sidney, a Les Mysteres (Path of the Soul) forced into the tradition by his crazed father. The man was (depending on the day) either a New Age spiritualist who did a lot of LSD in the 60s or the subject of top secret CIA experiments; either way, he isolated and indoctrinated his son. Sidney has escaped and adjusted surprisingly well, but can’t shake the ‘gifts’ that resulted from his abuse. With no other road ahead of him, he reluctantly takes to the life of a wandering spiritual troubleshooter, all while looking for some peace.
    I've been trying to come up with a Loyalist of Thule that I like, but I keep getting distracted by Delta Green.

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

      James Sutton [Ex Bear Lodge]: James was (by the end of the chronicle) a twenty two year old man whom grew up in a town that was under the open protection of a pack of Forsaken. The Forsaken treated their Kinfolk well enough, and even taught them a little bit about their heritage and what they could potentially become, if they where blessed enough - but James didn't want that. He wanted a normal life - a wife, children, a job. I mean sure it sounds cool in principle, and that they where chosen for this and whatever, but what if you didn't want it?. What made it worse was that he had to watch his closest childhood friend, Amy, go through her First Change - at which point she stopped being the girl she had been before. She became increasingly violent and distant as the pack adopted her and taught her their ways. A few weeks later, she ended up dead in an encounter with a powerful spirit.

      Now, this could have been the end of it - while bad, it certainly doesn't push one into the life of a hunter, does it? Except James is unknowingly being Urged by a Spirit. Taking a leaf from Hunter: The Reckoning, what James sees an Uratha as - even for a few moments at a glance - is something inhuman and wrong. Something angry. Something that wants to kill everything around it. Which, well, isn't exactly wrong - but that was what he had to suffer through seeing his closest friend become. And so, really, that was that. So he decided he'd hunt down and kill any Uratha he could to protect people from these angry, inhuman super predators. James was only seventeen when he fled the town - but he was a smart kid. He took to the internet and managed to get in contact with The Bear Lodge. James knows quite a lot more than most about the Uratha - which helps him hunt them down. He also acknowledged that if he ever changed, he'd shoot himself in the head with a silver bullet. He'd rather die than become that.

      Over the course of the chronicle I played him in, James proved himself good at tracking and killing Uratha - as is expected of The Bear Lodge. Unfortunately over that same period of time he met a few Wolfblooded whom laid down their lives to protect the Uratha he hunted - some he'd had to kill himself. Maybe it was this or maybe it was the constant Urging of his 'inner voice' (actually the Spirit) but it eventually hit him that every Kinfolk could become an Uratha. An inhuman monster. And slowly, but surely, the Spirit began to poison his view of others like him as well. He'd seen a flash of the Beast that lurked in their blood. A glint of the wolf in their eyes.

      So then he began to hunt Kinfolk and Uratha alike. By the time he was twenty years old, he'd become a Slasher (I believe a brute? An unusual one; he used guns rather than melee weapons) - still hunting Uratha, but also hunting down and slaying Kinfolk to save them from what they could become and the danger they could cause others. The Cell he'd been hunting with eventually had to hunt him down, but they didn't manage to kill him.

      James was - is - definitely my favourite Hunter I've ever had the pleasure of playing.

                  My favorite hunter, who got killed by his own cell, was a man named Eli, and his friend, the Strix.

                  Eli came from a long line of hunters that stopped with his mother. She refused to indulge in the family lunacy and instead settled down outside of Boston. She got married had three kids, Anna who got into the antique business, Eli who joined the Marines and lost a leg, and Drake who only recently moved out and is the lead vocalist for a death metal band. The whole family got together one Sunday for dinner and when they got home, Edgar, their grandfather, was laying on the couch, bleeding slightly and looking haggard. He refused to go to the hospital, instead he had Eli help patch him up, asked Anna for the some information about something, and slept on the couch. The wound looked suspiciously like teeth marks, but Eli never got the chance to ask, because Edgar was gone in the morning. Anna, Eli and Drake decided to try and find the grandfather they never knew.

                  They found him on Tuesday night, slipping into a Southie Warehouse. Leaving all sense behind them, they followed after their elder. What they saw in the building was obscene; cannibalism, blood, occult symbols and lots of shadowy owls in the rafters. They became even more worried for the old man and hurried along faster. As he had the most experience, Eli lead the group, his pistol out. They arrived just in time to see their grandfather hew through someone's skull with an axe. A tablet stood on an altar, surrounded by fire, blood and empty-eyed people with gore stained hands. The People were attacking Edgar, and without pause, Eli began firing into the group of 8 people.

                  The firefight was intense, but Anna couldn't take her eyes off of the tablet, and picked her way over to it. Eli's gun clicked empty, just as his sister grasped the tablet, and Edgar shouted something. Without thinking, without hesitation, Marine and Brother clicked into place, and Eli sprinted to his sister, the prosthetic ripping into his leg. He slipped on the gore, the leg snapped and he fell head long into his sister, dislodging her grip and spattering his blood across the tablet moments before it shattered on the concrete. The Spirit in the tablet, the same beast who drove these humans into a frenzy of blood, was the Strix. With its vessels dead, Strix had only one place to go, other than pieces. It used the blood sympathy with Eli and climbed into his body.

                  What followed was a wholly spiritual battle that ended in a stalemate. Strix was too well fed to be dislodged, and Eli was too willful to be overcome; the two were stuck together, constantly fighting for dominance. Each personality bled into the other, Eli slowly became more and more merciless and vengeful even without Strix taking control. In game he: firebombed an apartment complex because it also had vampires; shot a werewolf in the head with a .45 at pointblank range (and said "Bad Doggie!) because it was putting the moves on his sister; shot a cell mate in the head while eating at Denny's because the cell mate wanted to "borrow" someone else's soul, permanently. About five seconds after killing the other cell mate, he got hit with an epically successful cryokenesis attack from another cell mate, and died.

                      Eric Mattick-Former detective for the Seattle PD.
                      Mattick had just been declared the youngest detective to make homicide. Bright, determined, good instincts. Double major in criminal justice, and biology. He was a CSI character almost made flesh. Outside the job, Mattick was a conspiracy theorist who whole heartedly believed in alien abduction and ancient astronaut theories. If not woeking on cases, he was either researching alien sightings or blogging about aliens. His partner, senior detective -Seargent O'houlihan thought he was a niave kid with a brifht future if he would just shut up. Then the weird cases came...and Mattick ate it up. The due were deemed the "mulder/scully" duo and ridiculed over cases involving suspects with glowing eyes, suspects who ate all the neighborhood pets, or practiced strange rituals. After getting a case that involved a high ranking employee of a major pharmecutical corp, Mattick fell into the deep end of supernaturals pulling strings in politics and business. He was framed for murder, terrorism, and corruption. It was then that he left Seattle and teamed up wirh several other victims of this pharma company.
                      Mattick now travels with his fellow "burned and blacklisted" cellmates trying to gather evidence to clear their names while trying to find a way to hit back at the supernatural baddies who forced them into the vigil. He still blogs about aliens, even though he has yet to see one among the scores of shapechangers, vampires, ghosts, possesed dolls, and the case that dealt with pod people...turns out it was a fungi like thing living in the local water resevoir that had a hive mind link. His blog is linked to Network zero, though he has no actual status with them. He has recently picked up a mentor while in Boston, a catholic preist who helped exercise a demon from an innocent girl.

                                      I've got a Hunter idea that's been in the wings ever since Mortal Remains came out. Haven't had the chance to use it yet but I'm very proud of it.

                                      Edgar L. Noran, author of a popular series of fantasy novels and one of the Faithful of Shulpae. Edgar grew up caught between two worlds. His mother was an honest, god fearing christian who taught her son the importance of virtue and standing up for what's right. His father on the other hand was one of the Faithful of Shulpae who taught his son that the Gods were amoral beings and that the only way to give them true worship and respect was through the amoral act of the Feast. Having to keep his father's lessons a secret was tough enough on a child, but nothing could have prepared him for his rite of passage and first Feast. The nightmares he endured and the sickness that followed as his body reacted to the "God's Blessings" nearly drove him over the edge. Thankfully both of his parents encouraged his love of writing stories from a young age, and it was through examining his condition through stories that let him get past those difficult times. It's also what made him a household name in the current day.

                                      Now thirty-two years old and having started publishing his second series of novels Edgar balances his career as an writer with his duties as a Guide for the local Temple of the Faithful. He uses his novels to subtly warn the public about the dangers of the World of Darkness whilst simultaneously scouting for potential additions to the Faithful's ranks. Unfortunately he's had very little success in this regard thanks to the current market being flooded with fantasy novels. However, his connections to the art community and network of fans has proven invaluable when it comes to scouting for hidden Gods to hunt for the Feast. He rarely takes part in the actual hunt, preferring to leave the capture of the God to more skilled warriors, yet continually surprises his fellow hunters with his skill at coordinating ambushes and traps.

                                      Edgar has come to an amicable peace with his faith and morals, seeing that the Faithful's hunt for the divine has brought more good to humanity than harm, but deep down he knows that the Faithful walk a very fine line between apotheosis and damnation. One that gets harder to see as time rolls on.

                                          Sir Larcen Cruise, The Fortune Hunter
                                          Apparently a very fit young British gentleman, Larcen is famed as a writer of 'Historical Fantasy', particularly the "Fortune Hunter's Guide" series which details actual supernatural events and goings-on in the world, spanning back for over a century. Larcen himself was born in 1873, and first encountered the supernatural at the age of 17, when he visited a newly-unearthed pyramid and met its owner face-to-face. After surviving that encounter and putting the mummy 'back to sleep' long enough to get away, he travelled to America and learned from Old West outlaws how to ride and shoot and survive in the wild.
                                          That is also when he started to write his books, starting of course with "The Fortune Hunter's Guide to Egypt". At the turn of the century, Larcen and his cell of American 'Gentlemen of Violence' prevented an extradimensional entity from engulfing New York City. During that ordeal, Larcen was exposed to some unearthly energies that seem to have been what has endowed him with unnaturally long life. Indeed, he has not aged a day since.
                                          In modern times, he is fabulously wealthy and his moniker 'The Fortune Hunter' is known through the supernatural world, a sort of bogeyman for the bogeymen. He spends most of his time looking for magical artifacts from long ago or hunting the very rarest and deadliest of monsters. To stave off boredom in-between these pursuits, each generation he takes on one protege or a handful of students at the most, and teaches them what he knows. And he knows a lot.

                                          This was a favorite character of mine for a game set in the 1890's, that ended up a powerful Mentor for PC's in a modern-era game.
