    Favorite nWod/CofD books, and why?

    06-12-2017, 09:58 PM
    I've got a good bit of core nWod books (about ten or so) but I'm looking to expand my collection. Instead of just asking "so, what book should I get next?" I figured I would ask what your favorite 3-5 books of the core line are and why.

    My favorite supplements are biased on the type of game I like to run. My game world is more or less "normal" and so are the characters. While they might be really good investigators, fighters, or con men, they fall within the realistic spectrum of "normal humans." That might change, though, as I'm considering expanding into some more "superhuman" ideas (through the use of supernatural merits and such). This circles back to my intent of this thread - to see what supplements others lIke and why.

    For me:

    1) Hurt Locker - really expands normal humans with updated merits. I also like the new conditions.

    2) Tales of the 13th Precent - as my games are centered around normal people, law enforcement plays a major role. This book fleshes out LE pretty good.

    3) Slasher - awesome book for serial killing, deranged "normal" foes.

    4) Second Sight - I haven't used this one a lot, yet, but as my games might begin to push into the "extraordinary" realm, this book is nice. I really like the low level psychics and mages that this supplement introduces.

      Core. Lets see. I generally like people to have options that can supplement their player abilities. I don't force them to pick it but if someone is the descendant of a family of hyena cultists, it's nice to have something mechanical I can give them to represent it. These things tend to work on the other side of things, as well, where the players might look at ravaged corpses and expect to deal with a werewolf only to find silver doesn't affect the hyena-woman. An incredibly unsubtle example, my players are in the Neolithic right now and there's a 'tribe' of people called the Blood Drinkers who are eschewing the whole agricultural fad in favor of something much darker. Between the name and the rumors that they only hunt at night, they're thinking vampires, but they just stumbled upon some of their traps and are finding them to be something else.

      Inferno, because it has a nice mix of storyteller and player options, as well as assorted bad guys and regular NPCs to throw into things.

      The Book of Spirits, which is incredibly useful as a werewolf fan and someone generally interested in the spirit side of things. It has some info not available across the werewolf books and shows the Hisil from the perspective of the mortals that occasionally stumble into it and are all-too-often victims of its inhabitants.

      Asylum. I'd probably throw in 13th Precinct if you hadn't already. There's a few of these books that show the Chronicles of Darkness from the perspective of some of its major inhabitants. Precinct gives you law enforcement, Dogs of War the military, and Asylum the medical health field. In addition to helping players and Storytellers both flesh out their characters, they often show how to put these characters into scary situations. Asylum and Precinct also offer a specific setting, with the 13th Precinct and Asylum's Bishopsgate Hospice and Asylum, complete with inmates. I was hoping there would be more in this vein. One perhaps for politics and business, another for organized and disorganized criminals.

      Skinchangers, I think, really helps when it comes to throwing the weird into games. It seems like a simple concept, people who change into animals, but it offers a lot to go beyond that. A man who can turn into a swarm of ants, a lab rat with malign human intelligence, evil birds that watch over a wealthy and decadent family. Any game would easily be able to take one creature from it, but even if they didn't want to there's a system and advice inside for designing new monsters. In the same vein I like Immortals but I think it doesn't offer as many player-friendly options as Skinchangers does.

      I'm not sure if it counts for core, but Dark Eras, too. Great settings for a variety of games.
