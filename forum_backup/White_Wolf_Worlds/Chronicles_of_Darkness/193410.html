    Help designing nWoD Digital Web

    07-10-2014, 08:28 AM
    I did this homebrew project to help convert the Digital Web from the classic World of Darkness to the new--but I need help making it feel authentic, especially in regards to other supernaturals. Please, if anyone can look over this and tell me where and how I can improve the project, it'd help out tremendously.
    ----
    The Digital Web is the convergence realm of both the Aether and the Primal Wilds, being reflected in the Shadow due to the massive emotional resonance given off by users every day. Meanwhile, the use of code and immense amounts of energy to power the network is what gives its ties to the Aether, which focuses on power and order.

    Contrary to popular belief, the Free Council did not create the Network. They are, however, the most capable at navigating and studying it due to innate fascination with technology and mundane sciences.

    Access
    There are 3 different ways to enter the Web, each with its pros and cons:

    Sensory Visitation -- aka "telepresence", SV is safe and easy to use since all that's necessary is a computer and a pair of imbued goggles the Free Council readily sell. Either that, or Forces 2 and Space 2. Unfortunately, actions outside basic movement require magic to affect anything on the Network.

    Astral Immersion -- requiring Forces 2 and Mind 4, astral travel through the Web allows for greater sensory input as well as physical interaction with objects and icons. This is represented by having said-users double their Initiative modifier. The downside is that any damage done to your icon affects your body in the form of bashing damage. Said-body is also vulnerable to attack while your mind is in the network. This is generally why libertines ensure some form of alarm system is active once they jack in. That, or a friend is there to keep watch.

    Holistic Immersion -- arguably the most exhilarating way to access the net, this type of immersion completely digitizes the user's corporeal essence, requiring Space 2, Life 4, and Forces 4. This means that the user's body is not left behind in meatspace while logged in, and theoretically means the mage can exit the Web from another computer entirely. Furthermore, being fully immersed in the Network grants a user triple their normal Initiative modifier. Unfortunately, it also means that any damage taken while in the Net becomes all too real. Aggravated damage is not converted into bashing. It stays aggravated. Even should you exit the Web.

    Icons
    Upon entering the Web, the User can design a virtual body, or "Icon", for her personal use. Ranging from basic 2D images, to elaborate models based on mythological beasts, the icon is there. By default, all icons begin as mere replicas of their users. However, a user may change their icon by either rolling an extended Intelligence + Computer check, or using the appropriate arcana (storyteller's discretion).

    A mage can also alter their physical and social Attributes while on the Net, and can even exceed the 5 dot limit regardless of Gnosis rank. First, the mage takes their highest mental attribute. This determines the number of free dots he can add to any of his other attributes. Eg, if the user's highest mental attribute is 4, he gains 4 dots to distribute among social and/or physical traits. In addition to this, a user can "subtract" dots in non-mental attributes to place elsewhere, meaning a user with Presence 3 can move any or all his dots in Presence to, say, Strength. These dots cannot, however, be placed in any mental attributes.

    Changing an icon's attributes generally requires an extended Intelligence + Computer check, with each dice pool equaling 5 minutes in game time (unless altered with magic).

    Keep in mind that there are limitations to what icons can do. Even if a user's icon appears to have wings, it cannot fly unless the user has the arcana to do so (or within a Constraint Realm where flight is part of the theme). Clever users, however, will notice that its easier to cast certain spells depending on their icon's appearance. If a willworker's icon looks like a dragon, she finds it easier to cast spells relating to her breathing fire! Storytellers should consider adding +1 to +3 to player dice pools when characters utilize this concept.

    Note: once developed, a user's icon will remain the same anytime he or she accesses the net, or until she decides to change it.

    Sector Formatting
    Sector Formatting requires either the area in question to be Virgin Web, or for the mage to enact the "Overwrite Program" rote (see below). The latter choice has the risk of causing glitches to anything built upon it. After deciding between the two, the mage(s) must choose a "concept" or "theme" for the sector. From a backalley dive bar to a Victorian manor, it's all good. The mage must also have at least 4 dots in Computer skill (3 dots if he has an appropriate specialty). Finally, 1 willpower point must be spent to begin the procedure.

    Formatting is an extended Intelligence + Computer + Gnosis check. Depending on what the willworker wishes to build, the needed amount of successes can range from a minimum of 1 and beyond. Note that once it's written, it can't be erased. Even with the Overwrite Program, ghosts of the original formatting will twist and turn the new one in unpredictable ways (sometimes fun, sometimes not).

    Concepts -- # Successes needed

    Simple Room -- 4

    Elaborate Room -- 5

    Multiple Room Area -- 7

    Large Area with Basic Countermeasures -- 10

    Huge Area with Elaborate Countermeasures -- 15+

    Constraint Realm -- Add +1 to roll

    Restricted Sector -- Add +1 to roll

    Twilight -- Add +1 to roll

    New Rote: Overwrite Program (Fate •••••, Forces ••••, Mind ••••, and Prime ••••)
    The willworker can attempt to erase a formatted sector in the Web of its original format, allowing it to be later reshaped by his will or that of other parties. This does not affect others within the targeted sector, it simply trashes the place and allows the mage a "clean slate" to work with.

    Practice: Unmaking
    Action: Extended; caster must roll a minimum of 10 successes.
    Duration: Lasting
    Aspect: Vulgar
    Cost: 1 Mana
    Free Council Rote: Tabula Rasa
    Dice Pool: Intelligence + Computers + Fate

    Arcana in the Digital Web

    Death
    Data can decay over time, even destroyed. In sectors where corpses, shadows, and e-ghosts are prevalent concepts, the Death Arcanum retains much of its power.
    Spells that tear Mana from a living pattern, or manipulate the soul (as with “Soul Jar”) do not function in the Network unless the target is Holistically Immersed. This is due to the life force of the target being elsewhere rather than fully in the Web.
    Twilight does exist in the Web, often used by e-ghosts, sector security, and certain sprites (though not always).

    Fate
    Probability and chance still exist within the Web. However, oaths magically sworn in the Network are considered limited. This is due to the oath being made in the universe the willworker currently occupies—which ever sector he is currently in. Within any other dimension, be it material or another sector, the oath is considered void. Note that oaths sworn on the material plane are still considered valid in all the Inner Planes (that is, anything that’s not the Supernal or Abyss).

    Forces
    Arguably the most useful of the arcana in Webspace, the Forces Arcanum allows for immense control over the terrain. This translates to all spells utilizing Forces as gaining a +1 to dicepools when cast in the Network. Access to the plane is considered impossible without at least some knowledge of Forces (or the proper imbued item).
    However, certain areas of the Web, particularly Constraint Realms, can stymy spells that don’t work with the sector’s theme. Casting fire in a sector completely submerged underwater risks creating paradox.

    Life
    Sectors and sprites that appear to be organic or alive can still be affected by this Arcanum. Healing magic works differently in the Network in that it can only affect holistically immersed characters, or natives. Programs that appear to cause “disease” or “sickness” can be cured with Life as per usual. It can also be used to improve or degrade the physical attributes of icons, as well as alter its appearance (if the icon appears organic).
    Life created in the Web essentially becomes a native of its sector, aka a sprite. Designing what said-sprite can specifically do, however, requires the Spirit Arcanum. Any life created with a duration of days or longer (especially indefinite) is sure to either be absorbed into the sector’s everyday function—or eliminated.

    Matter
    Following the logic of perspective, spells utilizing the Arcanum of Matter still work in sectors where such magic seems plausible. If the theme of said-sector contains what appears to be earth, Matter retains power and influence over it. In addition, icons, sprites, and objects within the Web that appear to be made of mechanical parts can be affected as if regular machines (though if sentient, such spells usually become contested rolls).
    No amount of magic can determine the composition of elements found in Webspace beyond basic concepts. If Matter is used to determine the density of what appears to be water, all they will learn is that it is water. Furthermore, attempts to replicate impossible machinery from the Network while on the material realm (without magic) immediately end in failure. A willworker can figure out the function of such objects, but not how to replicate them in Realspace.

    Mind
    Anything that appears to have sentience can be affected with the Mind Arcanum as per normal. Users that are not holistically immersed can possess the icons of others (with Mind 5)—though this generally appears as the target’s computer being “hacked.” Icons hijacked in this way retain their physical traits; though can be altered by the hacker using the rules under Icon Creation.
    Other users can be affected by Mind spells if they are cast on their icons; though any bonuses granted against supernatural compulsion still apply.

    Prime
    With the Arcanum of Prime, a user can analyze auras to determine the nature of other entities, whether sleeper icons, sprites, or fellow awakened. Phantasms work equally well in the Net as they do in Realspace.
    A mage capable of creating a Hallow can do so on the Web, though any mana procured from these “virtual Hallows” will not translate back into Realspace unless the willworker uses the appropriate ranks in both Prime and Forces, or was holistically immersed when she obtained it. Hallows that were not created naturally within a sector risk causing a form of paradox called "Whiteout", adding a number of bonus dice to said-paradox' equal to the hallow's dot value.

    Space
    Just as necessary as the Forces Arcanum in getting into the Web without help, Space grants many unusual advantages to users who’ve trained with it. On its own, it can be used as per normal within the confines of a sector. Creating a portal (aka a “hyperlink”) or teleporting to other sectors requires an equivalent rank in Forces. The same is true of attempts at scrying.
    Users cannot use Space to physically teleport from a sector to a place in the material unless holistically immersed—and even then, the user must have the appropriate arcana (Space 2, Life 4, and Forces 4) to convert their body from data back into flesh and blood. The same is true vice versa.
    All sectors generally have sympathetic ties to their counterparts on the Internet, as well as connection to said-sites’ creators. People who frequent such sites also gain sympathetic links, the strength of which depending on their familiarity with it. Space can allow a user to see the sympathetic ties of users between each other (Users who have only met via the internet generally growing more sympathy towards the icons than their actual operators, but not always). A user always gains a temporary sympathy of “intimate” strength to the sector she entered upon accessing the Network.

    Spirit
    Use of this Arcanum in the Network often has mixed results. Virtual spirits, or “sprites” are not entirely like those found in the Shadow. Attempts to summon them outside the Web without Space 2, Spirit 4, and Forces 4 always fail. This is also true of summoning regular spirits into the Network, unless they have or gain the “digitize” numen.
    Creating a spirit in Webspace ends with it becoming a sprite native to the sector it was born in. If Twilight exists in said-sector, the spirit manifests in it by default.
    Users attempting to enter the Shadow via Webspace need not worry about the Gauntlet, since both the Network and Shadow are considered “on the same side of it.” They can only enter the Shadow from the Web if they are astrally or holistically immersed, the latter of which requiring either a spirit cast the digitize numen on them, or a mage use the appropriate arcana (Space 2, Life 4, and Forces 4) to convert the user’s body from data back into flesh and blood.

    Time
    Practitioners of this Arcanum will quickly notice that every icon, sprite, and sector on the Net is affected somewhat differently by time. Depending on the hardware of computers in Realspace, the icon of a sleeper could lag considerably faster than anything else in the vicinity. This problem usually doesn’t happen to mages astrally or holistically immersed in Webspace, since the processing power of their computer is literally their brain.
    Relating to the passage of time between Realspace and the Network, both realms are considered synchronized. Spells that freeze the passage of time (like with Stop Time, or Faery Glade), affect both worlds equally. The process of naturally aging is nonexistent in Netspace. Icons don’t appear old unless deliberately made to look so. However, holistically immersed users that return to Realspace will quickly age by the amount of time they were gone for. This is believed to be the Fallen World’s attempt at retaining the Lie, and also why most users don’t remain too long in the Net if fully immersed (unless they plan to remain indefinitely online).

    Paradox and Disbelief

    Even though considerably more dynamic and accepting of magic than Realspace, the Abyss has its own strange connection to the Digital Web, most likely due to the constant presence of sleepers. Though they are less skeptical of strange anomaly occurring on the Net, disbelief is still possible, as are paradoxes. The following is a list of scenarios where magic is considered vulgar and/or dangerous.

    • Magic that comes from "nowhere"
    Even though supernatural in nature, the Digital Web is based on the concepts of technological science. Any magic performed inside it requires an appropriate tool, imago, rote, and/or medium (such as an icon which appears to have a built-in blaster). Even if sleepers are not present, or covert in nature, such spells are considered vulgar for the sake of determining paradox.
    This rule is negated if a given sector “accepts” certain spells as part of its theme. Many magi that build sectors will incorporate a “mystic” theme, allowing for most cases of spellcasting to ignore this rule (though only by making the sector into a virtual demesne can willworkers fully remove the chance of paradox).
    Awakened users accessing the Net via Sensory Visitation are not subject to this rule, as they are casting their spells from Realspace and into the Digital Web through their icon. That said, they still remain vulnerable to causing paradox in the Fallen World if without a demesne.

    • Spells cast within a Restricted Sector that do not come from one of the area's approved residents
    If an awakened user enters a Restricted Sector without authorization by one or all of its creators, her magic is considered vulgar even if otherwise it would be covert. In most cases, this feature is frowned upon by Awakened Society, as it risks paradox despite being against an interloper. Many Seer controlled RS’ maintain this feature regardless.

    • Magic that contradicts the setting of a Constraint Realm
    Casting spells not in line with a given Constraint Realm’s theme, such as making trees grow in what appears to be the vacuum of space.

    • Forces and/or Prime spells that achieve more than 5 successes
    In this case, paradox does not occur normally. Rather, due to the nature of the Network, such spells risk fluctuating out of control should the mage roll higher than 5 successes. Anything and anyone within close proximity to the willworker (or targeted by his spell) suffers damage equal to the caster's number of successes minus 5. For every 5 points of damage done this way (regardless of severity) the willworker suffers 1 point of bashing as if from backlash. This rule does not apply in Warzones.

    • Spells of tremendous size
    Large scale spells (explosions, great conjurations, stopping time, gigantic icons, morphing or co-location of whole areas, dimensional rifts, and multi-icon morphs) shift power around so radically that paradox becomes inevitable. In such cases, the spell becomes vulgar and suffers from disbelief (that disbelief coming from the Network itself), and generates paradox accordingly.

    • Effects that jump from one sector to another
    Magic cast on targets in a different sector, or that allows the user to jump from one to the other, is always considered vulgar and capable of causing disbelief.

    • Gates between the Web and other Realms
    Any and all spells that leap from Webspace to different realms (eg the Hedge, Underworld, Astral Realms etc.) is considered vulgar. The exceptions are projecting from the Fallen World, and/or entering the Shadow.

    • Anything cast in Corrupted Web
    Any and all awakened magic turns itself inside-out within these sectors. Paradox will happen.

      Digital Web Inhabitants


      Within the vastness of the Digital Web, literally anything can be found wandering the digital landscape. From the icons of fellow users, to virtualized spirits, to even electronic ghosts of the departed, one can never be too sure what they’ll find after jacking in.

      New Numen: Digitize
      A sprite with this Numen can pull an object or living creature into the Digital Web. Such targets are considered holistically immersed when determining advantages. Supernatural targets cannot be affected by this power without their consent (this does not apply to "lesser" supernaturals such as sleepwalkers, wolf-blooded, etc). The sprite rolls Power + Finesse after spending a number of Essence equal to the target's Stamina. A mortal targeted like this reflexively rolls Resolve + Composure, though if willing, the sprite gains a +3 to its dicepool. Success means the target is digitized into the Network, and will generally remain there until the sprite deems otherwise, or they find an alternative way out.
      Sprites who have this Numen can bring holistically immersed users back into Realspace, but otherwise cannot do the same for Web natives. Spirits of the Shadow that are connected to computers, information, and technology have a strong chance of having this Numen, and may use it on themselves if properly connected to the Network somehow.

      Users
      Second only to sprites in sheer number, the Web hosts billions of Users every day. Whenever someone accesses the material plane Internet (whether mortal or supernatural), they’re represented in the Network by a virtual avatar, or Icon. The vast majority of users, besides mages with the proper gear and talents, are unaware of this, their icons being nothing more than blank white-gray mannequins (unless they use an avatar pic for the site they’re on).
      Regardless, the actions of users are reflected in Webspace, with symbolism being prominent. If two users are arguing on a site, their reflection in that site’s sector of the Web will show their icons doing so. A flame war can literally become a battle where the icons are spewing fire at each other.
      With that said, these types of users are seldom in any real danger (at least directly). A user that leaves the aforementioned flame war will either appear to be fleeing, vanish into thin air, or turn to ash by the last scathing remark thrown at him. However, supernatural entities in the Network (especially sprites and immersed magi) can influence these users and their computers via these icons. Casting a mind control spell on an icon can allow the mage to either “hack” said-icon, or, in conjunction with Forces, take direct control of the user.

      Supernatural Users
      Rarely anyone else besides Awakened users are found immersed inside the Web, mostly due to the realm being (theoretically considered) a recent creation of the modern world. Tech-savvy supernaturals, however, can hold a great advantage on their peers should they discover its existence.

      • Vampires
      Creatures ranging from ancient predators to stalkers of the night, few vampires save those born in the digital age would likely know about the Network. Neither sprites nor users procure vitae (even if holistically immersed) if fed upon, nor is it easily accessible save for those with the proper connections and/or disciplines. Among the covenants, the Carthian Movement and Ordo Dracul would have the most interest in such a place, though how any of them were to gain access remains up for debate. Perhaps a rare form of discipline, or a compact with the appropriate supernaturals? Many talk about a Nosferatu bloodline dedicated to an information network of sorts. Or was it Mekhet?

      • Werewolves
      The Uratha can enter via the Shadow, or if they have computer spirits as allies. Most, if not all such werewolves are generally Iron Masters, whose fascination with modern technology can easily drive them into exploring the Net—although a few Bone Shadows also find Webspace just as interesting, seeing its connections to the Shadow as a sign of things to come.

      Icons owned by werewolves generally have a feral aura about them, regardless of shape or design. Aspects of their lupine heritage seem to leak through, eyes that flash yellow, the smell of dog, or teeth that seem all too jagged and sharp. The icons of wolf-blooded users seem to experience a similar albeit weaker version to this.
      Renown also translates into the Web, appearing in a symbolic manner over the Forsaken’s icon. Depending on the werewolf in question, the marks could appear as tribal tattoos, clothes, even special effects like back-lighting or being wreathed harmlessly in colored flames. These marks will generally change to better match the theme of a given sector, but are mostly cosmetic in nature.

      Rumors persist that the Azlu have found ways into the Network, the Iron Masters joking that it being because of the symbolism behind the "Web". If true, the spider-host could potentially locate the remaining shards of their patron spirit at an alarming rate, spanning the globe quickly as they trap both user and sprite in a web of light and sound. As for the beshilu, the rats may prove at first interested in the Network, but find it too indirect with their interest. Why create a hole to the Shadow via the Web when you can do so directly with the material? Confronted by both their Azlu counterparts and the Forsaken, the rat-host would be hard pressed to find reasons to venture inside (though just like the Azlu, the concept of locating their patron's spirit-shards could prove interesting enough that they would try).

      • Prometheans
      New Athanor: Firefly – Charm
      The Firefly teaches that the brightness of one’s personality can cast away the shadows of ignorance and despair, drawing close those of similar mindset no matter their distance apart. Prometheans set on developing this athanor tend to be shy in person, but are social butterflies when using a medium like telephones, computers, or even the written word.

          Shapes and Forms of the Web

          Forming the basic structure of the Digital Web are the following:

          Virgin Web
          Formatted Web
          Corrupted Web

          Grid Sector
          Stacked Files -- virtual equivalent to Glades in the Shadow
          Junklands -- virtual equivalent to Wounds in the Shadow
          Constraint Realms
          Servers(?)
          Warzones
          Haunts
          Trash Sector, the -- Fabled Dead Dominion tied to the Digital Web.
          Hung Sectors
          Sex Sites (shut up, it's in the book)
          Conduits

          Virgin Web
          Also known as Unformatted Web, Virgin Web is characterized as being pure digital ephemera, untouched by the rest of the Network. Rare and highly sought after by awakened users, Virgin Web is different from Formatted Web in that it can be shaped into anything without fear of resistance. Designing the shape and form of a sector made from Virgin Web grants a bonus of +3 to the programmer's dicepool, and halves the time needed to complete. Once formatted, however, this bonus is gone for good, and the sector can never be changed back into Virgin Web (except maybe with Imperial-level magics).
          Reasons for why Virgin Web exists remain debatable, as are the theories on how to predict where and when it manifests. Focusing on the similarities between the Digital Web and the Shadow, awakened users speculate Virgin Web to be the equivalent of the Shadow's Places-that-Aren't. This means that sectors of Virgin Web have no reflection on the internet in Realspace. Even if later formatted, these sectors never appear on mundane network channels or search engines of any sort, making them invaluable to covert operations done by various supernaturals. Such oases generally have all direct conduits turned into Grid Sectors to prevent unwanted visitors.
          Formatted Web
          Being the most common form of Web, the vast majority of the Network is comprised of sectors already formatted. When a website is created on the internet, it formats a sector within the Network that is essentially its symbolic reflection. From there, the sector gains sub-types related to its metaphorical nature and purpose. A site that is private and dedicated to trading secrets will naturally become a restricted area in Webspace, requiring either a password, invitation, or clever maneuvering to enter.
          Formatted Web is known for being difficult to permanently alter, requiring its mundane counterpart in Realspace be dramatically changed somehow, or the use of powerful spells like the Free Council's "Overwrite Program." This can cause dramatic changes to both it and its material cousin, which appears either to be under new management, or have its fan-base suddenly altered over night.

          Corrupted Web
          An anomaly that occurs when a sector is heavily damaged by paradox, or rewritten too many times, Corrupted Web causes countless problems for anyone traveling through it. They are dangerous for anyone to enter, especially if holistically immersed, as the sector is without logic or any form of reliable stability. What was once a site dedicated to a scenic local park becomes a nightmare. The sky may turn into murky static, with sections missing to reveal black segments and computer code. Grass turns razor sharp as caustic streams of ooze fall from out of nowhere. Even the roads and pathways flicker in and out, potentially having users fall right through them and into the void. And all of this could last for a matter of moments before changing to something else entirely.
          Awakened-users attempting to cast spells within the confines of Corrupted Web immediately risk causing Paradox, regardless if it's covert or not. Every round a mage stays within such areas, paradoxes gain a +1 to their dicepools when spells are cast, to a max of +5.
          Sprites found in Corrupted Web are often hostile towards anything and everything they find. Driven completely mad, they are noted to even attack themselves on occasion. Because of this, sectors found with Corrupted Web generally do not last long before being locked down--as if the Network itself were acting to contain the problem. This only happens, however, when there is no risk of living users being trapped inside. Few know what happens to the ghosts of those who die within a sector with Corrupted Web. Even fewer still are those wish to find out first hand.

          -----

          Conduits
          Conduits are what connect one sector of Webspace to another. Every website on the Internet, whether public or private, has at least one conduit in its Network counterpart, leading from it to another site. Private sites generally have restrictions on who can use these conduits, which can range from passwords to elaborate prerequisites. In the case of Constraint Realms, a user cannot access its conduit until they've accepted the terms of agreement and/or dresscode.

          It should be noted that Conduits are not sectors in and of themselves. Rather than long tunnels of space between areas, they are more akin to doorways (though certain sectors can cleverly disguise conduits as vehicles, manholes, or even traps). There is no limit to how many conduits a given sector may have, though the more private the area is, the less likely there are to be more than a handful. Curiously, most conduits are not initially created by users. Rather, they often develop when two sectors hold similarities with one another, meaning a site dedicated to healthcare may acquire conduits leading to sites on medicine, biology, and other related subjects. This can sometimes prove annoying to users wishing to filter who can enter what areas of the net. But it also proves fascinating in how the Digital Web connects sectors via laws similar to that of spatial sympathy.

          Certain sectors, called Nodes, are built solely as a collection of various conduits, acting as the middle ground between various locales on the Web.

          Constraint Realms
          Constraint Realms are sectors designed under a specific theme. This generally means user icons must change to reflect the given theme before entering. It also limits what programs (spells) you can get away with casting. If you create fire in a place where the theme is a fish tank, you'll either have a large penalty against paradox, or it just won't happen. Most sectors made from Formatted Web fall under this category.

          Grid Sectors
          A mix between a security checkpoint and a false-front, a Grid Sector (GS) is designed to look on the outside like a public area, but in truth guards access to the real site. If the sector is a club, the GS might be a club's website, with the real sector being disguised as the VIP Lounge. Now, like a VIP lounge, the GS is usually guarded, with security sprites taking the shape of an appropriate avatar (eg a bouncer). Getting past a Grid Sector usually requires permission from the GS' administrator, or for someone to "hack" through. Unlike those sectors with password-locked conduits, a GS is entirely dedicated to keeping undesirables out and away from whatever location its guarding. Hacking often requires figuring out not just where the conduit is, but also getting past all its security measures without raising the alarm. If caught, the conduit can be temporarily moved to an alternate location (predetermined by administration). Some will just redirect you to one of the sectors publicly accessible to the GS. Other, less scrupulous sites will dump your ass in hostile territory, or even corrupted web. Another option is that the conduit simply disappears for a time, blocking users from getting in or out via said-conduit. Finally, a conduit can easily be booby-trapped with various nasty software that activates upon contact (unless the hacker disables it or has the appropriate key).

          Haunts
          Sites on the internet relating to the death of living creatures often become fetters for their ghosts. Such sectors, known as Haunts, will often have subtle hints that warn of their nature. Electronic ghosts--or "E-ghosts"--do not necessarily have visible avatars, or even be aware of where and what they are. Since all Haunts have a form of Twilight, most of these beings will be invisible until they choose to manifest. It is said that all haunts are tied to a place called "Trash Space", a dead dominion found within the Underworld.

                                              Originally posted by MrParaduo View Post
                                              I did this homebrew project to help convert the Digital Web from the classic World of Darkness to the new--but I need help making it feel authentic, especially in regards to other supernaturals. Please, if anyone can look over this and tell me where and how I can improve the project, it'd help out tremendously.
                                              ----
                                              The Digital Web is the convergence realm of both the Aether and the Primal Wilds, being reflected in the Shadow due to the massive emotional resonance given off by users every day. Meanwhile, the use of code and immense amounts of energy to power the network is what gives its ties to the Aether, which focuses on power and order.

                                              Contrary to popular belief, the Free Council did not create the Network. They are, however, the most capable at navigating and studying it due to innate fascination with technology and mundane sciences.

                                              Access
                                              There are 3 different ways to enter the Web, each with its pros and cons:

                                              Sensory Visitation -- aka "telepresence", SV is safe and easy to use since all that's necessary is a computer and a pair of imbued goggles the Free Council readily sell. Either that, or Forces 2 and Space 2. Unfortunately, actions outside basic movement require magic to affect anything on the Network.

                                              Astral Immersion -- requiring Forces 2 and Mind 4, astral travel through the Web allows for greater sensory input as well as physical interaction with objects and icons. This is represented by having said-users double their Initiative modifier. The downside is that any damage done to your icon affects your body in the form of bashing damage. Said-body is also vulnerable to attack while your mind is in the network. This is generally why libertines ensure some form of alarm system is active once they jack in. That, or a friend is there to keep watch.

                                              Holistic Immersion -- arguably the most exhilarating way to access the net, this type of immersion completely digitizes the user's corporeal essence, requiring Space 2, Life 4, and Forces 4. This means that the user's body is not left behind in meatspace while logged in, and theoretically means the mage can exit the Web from another computer entirely. Furthermore, being fully immersed in the Network grants a user triple their normal Initiative modifier. Unfortunately, it also means that any damage taken while in the Net becomes all too real. Aggravated damage is not converted into bashing. It stays aggravated. Even should you exit the Web.

                                              Icons
                                              Upon entering the Web, the User can design a virtual body, or "Icon", for her personal use. Ranging from basic 2D images, to elaborate models based on mythological beasts, the icon is there. By default, all icons begin as mere replicas of their users. However, a user may change their icon by either rolling an extended Intelligence + Computer check, or using the appropriate arcana (storyteller's discretion).

                                              A mage can also alter their physical and social Attributes while on the Net, and can even exceed the 5 dot limit regardless of Gnosis rank. First, the mage takes their highest mental attribute. This determines the number of free dots he can add to any of his other attributes. Eg, if the user's highest mental attribute is 4, he gains 4 dots to distribute among social and/or physical traits. In addition to this, a user can "subtract" dots in non-mental attributes to place elsewhere, meaning a user with Presence 3 can move any or all his dots in Presence to, say, Strength. These dots cannot, however, be placed in any mental attributes.

                                              Changing an icon's attributes generally requires an extended Intelligence + Computer check, with each dice pool equaling 5 minutes in game time (unless altered with magic).

                                              Keep in mind that there are limitations to what icons can do. Even if a user's icon appears to have wings, it cannot fly unless the user has the arcana to do so (or within a Constraint Realm where flight is part of the theme). Clever users, however, will notice that its easier to cast certain spells depending on their icon's appearance. If a willworker's icon looks like a dragon, she finds it easier to cast spells relating to her breathing fire! Storytellers should consider adding +1 to +3 to player dice pools when characters utilize this concept.

                                              Note: once developed, a user's icon will remain the same anytime he or she accesses the net, or until she decides to change it.

                                              Sector Formatting
                                              Sector Formatting requires either the area in question to be Virgin Web, or for the mage to enact the "Overwrite Program" rote (see below). The latter choice has the risk of causing glitches to anything built upon it. After deciding between the two, the mage(s) must choose a "concept" or "theme" for the sector. From a backalley dive bar to a Victorian manor, it's all good. The mage must also have at least 4 dots in Computer skill (3 dots if he has an appropriate specialty). Finally, 1 willpower point must be spent to begin the procedure.

                                              Formatting is an extended Intelligence + Computer + Gnosis check. Depending on what the willworker wishes to build, the needed amount of successes can range from a minimum of 1 and beyond. Note that once it's written, it can't be erased. Even with the Overwrite Program, ghosts of the original formatting will twist and turn the new one in unpredictable ways (sometimes fun, sometimes not).

                                              Concepts -- # Successes needed

                                              Simple Room -- 4

                                              Elaborate Room -- 5

                                              Multiple Room Area -- 7

                                              Large Area with Basic Countermeasures -- 10

                                              Huge Area with Elaborate Countermeasures -- 15+

                                              Constraint Realm -- Add +1 to roll

                                              Restricted Sector -- Add +1 to roll

                                              Twilight -- Add +1 to roll

                                              New Rote: Overwrite Program (Fate •••••, Forces ••••, Mind ••••, and Prime ••••)
                                              The willworker can attempt to erase a formatted sector in the Web of its original format, allowing it to be later reshaped by his will or that of other parties. This does not affect others within the targeted sector, it simply trashes the place and allows the mage a "clean slate" to work with.

                                              Practice: Unmaking
                                              Action: Extended; caster must roll a minimum of 10 successes.
                                              Duration: Lasting
                                              Aspect: Vulgar
                                              Cost: 1 Mana
                                              Free Council Rote: Tabula Rasa
                                              Dice Pool: Intelligence + Computers + Fate

                                              Arcana in the Digital Web

                                              Death
                                              Data can decay over time, even destroyed. In sectors where corpses, shadows, and e-ghosts are prevalent concepts, the Death Arcanum retains much of its power.
                                              Spells that tear Mana from a living pattern, or manipulate the soul (as with “Soul Jar”) do not function in the Network unless the target is Holistically Immersed. This is due to the life force of the target being elsewhere rather than fully in the Web.
                                              Twilight does exist in the Web, often used by e-ghosts, sector security, and certain sprites (though not always).

                                              Fate
                                              Probability and chance still exist within the Web. However, oaths magically sworn in the Network are considered limited. This is due to the oath being made in the universe the willworker currently occupies—which ever sector he is currently in. Within any other dimension, be it material or another sector, the oath is considered void. Note that oaths sworn on the material plane are still considered valid in all the Inner Planes (that is, anything that’s not the Supernal or Abyss).

                                              Forces
                                              Arguably the most useful of the arcana in Webspace, the Forces Arcanum allows for immense control over the terrain. This translates to all spells utilizing Forces as gaining a +1 to dicepools when cast in the Network. Access to the plane is considered impossible without at least some knowledge of Forces (or the proper imbued item).
                                              However, certain areas of the Web, particularly Constraint Realms, can stymy spells that don’t work with the sector’s theme. Casting fire in a sector completely submerged underwater risks creating paradox.

                                              Life
                                              Sectors and sprites that appear to be organic or alive can still be affected by this Arcanum. Healing magic works differently in the Network in that it can only affect holistically immersed characters, or natives. Programs that appear to cause “disease” or “sickness” can be cured with Life as per usual. It can also be used to improve or degrade the physical attributes of icons, as well as alter its appearance (if the icon appears organic).
                                              Life created in the Web essentially becomes a native of its sector, aka a sprite. Designing what said-sprite can specifically do, however, requires the Spirit Arcanum. Any life created with a duration of days or longer (especially indefinite) is sure to either be absorbed into the sector’s everyday function—or eliminated.

                                              Matter
                                              Following the logic of perspective, spells utilizing the Arcanum of Matter still work in sectors where such magic seems plausible. If the theme of said-sector contains what appears to be earth, Matter retains power and influence over it. In addition, icons, sprites, and objects within the Web that appear to be made of mechanical parts can be affected as if regular machines (though if sentient, such spells usually become contested rolls).
                                              No amount of magic can determine the composition of elements found in Webspace beyond basic concepts. If Matter is used to determine the density of what appears to be water, all they will learn is that it is water. Furthermore, attempts to replicate impossible machinery from the Network while on the material realm (without magic) immediately end in failure. A willworker can figure out the function of such objects, but not how to replicate them in Realspace.

                                              Mind
                                              Anything that appears to have sentience can be affected with the Mind Arcanum as per normal. Users that are not holistically immersed can possess the icons of others (with Mind 5)—though this generally appears as the target’s computer being “hacked.” Icons hijacked in this way retain their physical traits; though can be altered by the hacker using the rules under Icon Creation.
                                              Other users can be affected by Mind spells if they are cast on their icons; though any bonuses granted against supernatural compulsion still apply.

                                              Prime
                                              With the Arcanum of Prime, a user can analyze auras to determine the nature of other entities, whether sleeper icons, sprites, or fellow awakened. Phantasms work equally well in the Net as they do in Realspace.
                                              A mage capable of creating a Hallow can do so on the Web, though any mana procured from these “virtual Hallows” will not translate back into Realspace unless the willworker uses the appropriate ranks in both Prime and Forces, or was holistically immersed when she obtained it. Hallows that were not created naturally within a sector risk causing a form of paradox called "Whiteout", adding a number of bonus dice to said-paradox' equal to the hallow's dot value.

                                              Space
                                              Just as necessary as the Forces Arcanum in getting into the Web without help, Space grants many unusual advantages to users who’ve trained with it. On its own, it can be used as per normal within the confines of a sector. Creating a portal (aka a “hyperlink”) or teleporting to other sectors requires an equivalent rank in Forces. The same is true of attempts at scrying.
                                              Users cannot use Space to physically teleport from a sector to a place in the material unless holistically immersed—and even then, the user must have the appropriate arcana (Space 2, Life 4, and Forces 4) to convert their body from data back into flesh and blood. The same is true vice versa.
                                              All sectors generally have sympathetic ties to their counterparts on the Internet, as well as connection to said-sites’ creators. People who frequent such sites also gain sympathetic links, the strength of which depending on their familiarity with it. Space can allow a user to see the sympathetic ties of users between each other (Users who have only met via the internet generally growing more sympathy towards the icons than their actual operators, but not always). A user always gains a temporary sympathy of “intimate” strength to the sector she entered upon accessing the Network.

                                              Spirit
                                              Use of this Arcanum in the Network often has mixed results. Virtual spirits, or “sprites” are not entirely like those found in the Shadow. Attempts to summon them outside the Web without Space 2, Spirit 4, and Forces 4 always fail. This is also true of summoning regular spirits into the Network, unless they have or gain the “digitize” numen.
                                              Creating a spirit in Webspace ends with it becoming a sprite native to the sector it was born in. If Twilight exists in said-sector, the spirit manifests in it by default.
                                              Users attempting to enter the Shadow via Webspace need not worry about the Gauntlet, since both the Network and Shadow are considered “on the same side of it.” They can only enter the Shadow from the Web if they are astrally or holistically immersed, the latter of which requiring either a spirit cast the digitize numen on them, or a mage use the appropriate arcana (Space 2, Life 4, and Forces 4) to convert the user’s body from data back into flesh and blood.

                                              Time
                                              Practitioners of this Arcanum will quickly notice that every icon, sprite, and sector on the Net is affected somewhat differently by time. Depending on the hardware of computers in Realspace, the icon of a sleeper could lag considerably faster than anything else in the vicinity. This problem usually doesn’t happen to mages astrally or holistically immersed in Webspace, since the processing power of their computer is literally their brain.
                                              Relating to the passage of time between Realspace and the Network, both realms are considered synchronized. Spells that freeze the passage of time (like with Stop Time, or Faery Glade), affect both worlds equally. The process of naturally aging is nonexistent in Netspace. Icons don’t appear old unless deliberately made to look so. However, holistically immersed users that return to Realspace will quickly age by the amount of time they were gone for. This is believed to be the Fallen World’s attempt at retaining the Lie, and also why most users don’t remain too long in the Net if fully immersed (unless they plan to remain indefinitely online).

                                              Paradox and Disbelief

                                              Even though considerably more dynamic and accepting of magic than Realspace, the Abyss has its own strange connection to the Digital Web, most likely due to the constant presence of sleepers. Though they are less skeptical of strange anomaly occurring on the Net, disbelief is still possible, as are paradoxes. The following is a list of scenarios where magic is considered vulgar and/or dangerous.

                                              • Magic that comes from "nowhere"
                                              Even though supernatural in nature, the Digital Web is based on the concepts of technological science. Any magic performed inside it requires an appropriate tool, imago, rote, and/or medium (such as an icon which appears to have a built-in blaster). Even if sleepers are not present, or covert in nature, such spells are considered vulgar for the sake of determining paradox.
                                              This rule is negated if a given sector “accepts” certain spells as part of its theme. Many magi that build sectors will incorporate a “mystic” theme, allowing for most cases of spellcasting to ignore this rule (though only by making the sector into a virtual demesne can willworkers fully remove the chance of paradox).
                                              Awakened users accessing the Net via Sensory Visitation are not subject to this rule, as they are casting their spells from Realspace and into the Digital Web through their icon. That said, they still remain vulnerable to causing paradox in the Fallen World if without a demesne.

                                              • Spells cast within a Restricted Sector that do not come from one of the area's approved residents
                                              If an awakened user enters a Restricted Sector without authorization by one or all of its creators, her magic is considered vulgar even if otherwise it would be covert. In most cases, this feature is frowned upon by Awakened Society, as it risks paradox despite being against an interloper. Many Seer controlled RS’ maintain this feature regardless.

                                              • Magic that contradicts the setting of a Constraint Realm
                                              Casting spells not in line with a given Constraint Realm’s theme, such as making trees grow in what appears to be the vacuum of space.

                                              • Forces and/or Prime spells that achieve more than 5 successes
                                              In this case, paradox does not occur normally. Rather, due to the nature of the Network, such spells risk fluctuating out of control should the mage roll higher than 5 successes. Anything and anyone within close proximity to the willworker (or targeted by his spell) suffers damage equal to the caster's number of successes minus 5. For every 5 points of damage done this way (regardless of severity) the willworker suffers 1 point of bashing as if from backlash. This rule does not apply in Warzones.

                                              • Spells of tremendous size
                                              Large scale spells (explosions, great conjurations, stopping time, gigantic icons, morphing or co-location of whole areas, dimensional rifts, and multi-icon morphs) shift power around so radically that paradox becomes inevitable. In such cases, the spell becomes vulgar and suffers from disbelief (that disbelief coming from the Network itself), and generates paradox accordingly.

                                              • Effects that jump from one sector to another
                                              Magic cast on targets in a different sector, or that allows the user to jump from one to the other, is always considered vulgar and capable of causing disbelief.

                                              • Gates between the Web and other Realms
                                              Any and all spells that leap from Webspace to different realms (eg the Hedge, Underworld, Astral Realms etc.) is considered vulgar. The exceptions are projecting from the Fallen World, and/or entering the Shadow.I'm delighted I came upon your advice since I've only recently begun dealing with SVG files.
