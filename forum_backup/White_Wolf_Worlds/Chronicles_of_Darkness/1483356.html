    [Haunt] The Eulogy

    06-07-2022, 11:14 PM
    “Dead men tell no tales” may not be technically true, but the dead still need the living to remember them if they wish to avoid the downward spiral of the Great Below. As liminal beings, the Bound cannot replenish ghostly comrades simply through burning incense and speaking fondly of the departed, but their ties to the Underworld grant them the potential for empowerment through similar channels. By means of the Eulogy, a Sin-Eater can not only perpetuate the memory of a ghost, but give her the chance to remember how she once was when the exigencies of undeath do their best to erase her sense of self—or, perhaps, to be remembered how she wishes she had been. More insidiously, Bound can use the Eulogy to manipulate how ghosts remember themselves, spinning false identities and fabricated pasts to turn them into pawns for long cons or Plasm-charged shock troops in ephemeral attacks against rival krewes. After all, mortals lie in eulogies all the time, smudging out the messier details of a person’s life for the sake of social niceties or cognitive dissonance.

    Though similar in some ways to the Dirge, where that Haunt can enthrall an entire concert hall in plasmic music, the Eulogy is always singular and personalized in its effects. In exchange, the Bound has a much greater degree of control over the target, and with the proper word can fine-tune nearly any aspect of a ghost’s ephemeral existence.

    Although every Eulogy requires an act of remembrance, the particulars vary according to the tastes of the Sin-Eater and preferences of the target. Many Western Bound recite the Eulogy as if at the ghost’s funeral, though another might write out an obituary on a Memento typewriter, while another paints a portrait of how deceased wish they had appeared in life. The Key employed to unlock the Haunt alters this further: A Eulogy recited with the Stillness Key is whispered only in the target’s ear, a secret to which only they may be privy, while a Sin-Eater wielding the Pyre-Flame Key lights a succession of candles as she intones each stage in the ghost’s life and desires for a better future. The Key most closely related to the ghost’s own death is often the most common unlocking choice, for it is always considered resonant.

    At character creation, an Abiding or Bereaved may swap out one of their favored Haunts for the Eulogy.

    Recite the Eulogy (1)

    Requirement: You must speak aloud, write down, or create some image of the memory you wish to evoke. Each alteration takes about one minute of work (one turn in action scenes), though the Haunt may be unlocked after the first evocation, with additional alterations applied as they are spoken into existence.

    Cost: 1 - 5 Plasm

    Action: Instant and contested

    Subject: One ghost you can perceive, and about whom you know at least three pieces of information (such as name, cause of death, and prized possession). Geists cannot be subject to this Haunt.

    Dice Pool: Synergy + Eulogy vs Resistance

    Success: The subject gains the Eulogy Condition with a number of charges equal to Plasm spent.

    Exceptional: When the Haunt ends without resolving, the Sin-Eater regains half the Plasm spent on Recite the Eulogy.

    Failure: The Bound’s remembrance sparks no change in the ghost.

    Dramatic: The Sin-Eater’s recitation is muddled and lacks critical insight. He cannot use the Eulogy on the same target again in the story until he uncovers a new piece of information about the ghost.

    Condition: Eulogy

    A Sin-Eater has evoked your memory with the Eulogy.

    Effects
    • The Eulogy has a number of charges determined when it is placed upon you. Each charge can cause one of the following effects, after a minute of evocation on the part of the Sin-Eater. All changes last until this Condition ends unless otherwise noted. (Other Haunts may add additional options to this list.)
    • Make a minor alteration to a Ban or Bane.
    • Remember one true fact the Sin-Eater states about your life, or remember one altered, embellished, or downplayed fact the Sin-Eater states as true.
    • Make a minor cosmetic alteration (hairstyle, eye color, clothing pattern, etc).
    • Whenever the imposing Sin-Eater spends a charge to effect a change, mortals who witness the Eulogy (hearing the sermon, reading the obituary, etc) remember the ghost as changed unless they succeed on a Resolve + Composure - Synergy roll for the Condition’s duration. A mortal may make this roll again if presented with evidence contrary to the implanted story.
    • This Condition ends (without resolving) at the end of the next scene.
    Possible Sources: The Eulogy Haunt

    Resolution:
    • You are confronted with the Ban or Bane of the inflicting Sin-Eater’s geist.
    • The inflicting Sin-Eater comes into contact with their geist’s Ban or Bane.
    • When this Condition resolves, the Sin-Eater who inflicted it also gains a Beat.
    A Change In Perspective (2)

    With the right words, a Sin-Eater can change the way the ghost thinks about himself. In the land of the dead, the difference between perception and being is as brittle and porous as sun-bleached coral, and a ghost who becomes convinced he was shot rather than mauled by a pitbull manifests oozing bullet holes across his Corpus, while his haunting power over vicious dogs transforms into an affinity for cursing handguns.

    At this level, the Bound begins to exert greater control over the ghost’s sense of identity. This can be a powerful tool for helping a forgetful wraith to remember events forgotten or suppressed, or as a means of physical or mental disguise for espionage or to keep the ghost safe from Reapers or necromancers. There are more unsavory uses for this power as well that unscrupulous Bound may exploit; their particulars will be left in the realm of subtext.

    Enhancement
    2 Plasm: Add one of the following effects to the options granted by the Eulogy Condition. This Enhancement may be applied more than once:
    • Alter one general physical characteristic (height, build, sex, ethnicity, deathmarks, etc). This may increase or decrease the target’s Size by one. You may also choose for the ghost to believe they always looked this way.
    • Impose or suppress the Addled Condition for the Haunt’s duration.
    • Replace one dot of the ghost’s Influence with another.
    Madeleine (3)

    What good is recollection when it is so fleeting? What good does a memory serve if it has no purpose to anchor it and give it direction? With this level of the Eulogy, the Sin-Eater’s words last longer than before, echoing beyond immediate concerns into projections of the future and newfound remembrances of things past.

    Enhancements
    Free: The Eulogy Condition now lasts until the end of the next chapter.
    1 Plasm: Add an additional charge to the Eulogy Condition after it has already been imposed. This Enhancement may be applied more than once, up to (Synergy + Eulogy) per use.
    2 Plasm: Add one of the following effects to the options granted by the Eulogy Condition. This Enhancement may be applied more than once:
    • Replace one of the ghost’s Aspirations with another of your choosing. This costs two charges unless the Aspiration was one (or resembles one) the ghost possessed in life.
    • Replace one of the ghost’s Anchors with another of your choosing. The Eulogy Condition immediately resolves if this new Anchor is destroyed (and the ghost gets their old Anchor back before they can be pulled into the Underworld).
    • Grant, suppress, or switch out an Iconography Merit. Ghosts can still only have one of such at a time.
    Multiple-Choice Past (4)

    We really do live on in the memories of others. Tell the world that someone long gone was a genius, a casanova, or a monster enough times, and it might start to become true. Forgetful minds fill in the gaps to justify the new reality, and a ghost’s very Essence follows suit.

    Enhancement
    3 Plasm: Add one of the following effects to the options granted by the Eulogy Condition. This Enhancement may be applied more than once:
    • Change the appearance of the targeted ghost wholesale, including mimicking another character, provided that you know what they look like and according to the detail of your description. You may also choose for the ghost to believe they always looked this way.
    • Replace a Ban or Bane with a new one of equivalent severity.
    • Grant the ghost a dot in an Influence. Up to a total of (Synergy + Eulogy) Influence dots may be granted this way.
    • Grant the ghost a new Aspiration.
    The Hallowed Dead (5)

    The zenith of the Eulogy’s power is frightening in its ability to weave an entire mythos from the tale of a single corpse. The legends and mysteries of the Great Below are myriad, reflected and scattered among the ever-changing memories of humankind toward their gods and heroes and monsters. Some Sin-Eaters use the Hallowed Dead to elevate their ghostly coreligionists to the level of the Regalia of their krewe as part of important ceremonies or to establish a sense of awe, while others make up tales on the spot for targets to inhabit, tailored to the function they’re made to serve. What the highest bards among the Bound can accomplish is formidable, but there are plenty who look askance at imposing abstracted raiments of death and glory on ghosts just trying to get by. On top of stripping vulnerable individuals of their autonomy, it’s too much like what the Rivers and Chthonic Gods do. Very suspicious.

    The other power of this Enhancement is less understood and often overlooked: It can make a geist a little less inhuman, at least for a brief period. The intricacies of the Bargain prevent Sin-Eaters from enacting this power on geists already bound (and especially frustrating, even on their own death-partner), but for those not yet cohabitating, it can be a useful talent for unraveling the mysteries behind these little gods of death, very useful indeed.

    Enhancements
    5 Plasm: The Eulogy may now be used against unbound geists. This Enhancement must be applied at the same time as Recite the Eulogy. In addition, add the following effect to the options granted by the Eulogy:
    • Remove either the Iconic or Broken effect from the geist, or alter its particulars, for the rest of the scene. This has no effect on the geist’s own memory.
    5 Plasm: Add one of the following effects to the options granted by the Eulogy Condition. This Enhancement may be applied more than once:
    • Inflict either the Iconic or Broken effect on the ghost.
    • Create a geist-like mantle for the ghost to inhabit, such as The Rime-Rimmed Priest or Mistress of Rot. The ghost becomes Rank 3, with additional Traits as appropriate determined by you when this Enhancement is applied. The ghost’s appearance, memories, and personality alter to fit the legend of the mantle, not unlike donning a Deathmask. Each application of this Enhancement creates a single mantle, designed by the player at that time.

              So, will you do it please?
              My first impression is that a supernatural power that messes around with Manifestations, Anchors, and the Liminal Aura like that should be the remit of Ceremonies, in a sort of parallel to certain Werewolf Rites that work to comparable effect. But then I thought, "But what if it is a Haunt?" And there is some precedent, what with the Dirge's Visitation Enhancement. I'd need to consider this a bit more, but "[regulating] ghostly access to the world" is almost like an anti-Haunt, yeah? It also smacks of the sort of unsavoryness that would interest Gatekeepers, Reapers, and everyone else invested in keeping the dead where (they think) they belong. The Liminal Aura is the most interesting part of this, and synergizes nicely with the parallels drawn with the aura-powers of other monsters, smacking rivals down with your own innate badassitude. There might be something there.

              That said, it's like the thing David Lynch says about fishing for ideas, in that these things only really come to me at their own pace. When I'm out riding my bike, or drinking coffee and dissociating while staring at the rain outside my living room window, or when strange men approach me deep in the woods. A seed can be planted, but the conditions for its growth are more occult than just water, fertilizer, and sunlight.

              Hmm. Let's see: Nullify/counter Influences and Numina. Shut down Manifestations. Maybe force ghosts to use Manifestations based on your Liminal Aura, and thus on your own terms. Overwhelm other Bound Auras. At highest echelons force other Bound to act more like ghosts in your Liminal Aura? The living too, even? Death is a lack of power to affect the world. You fade away, and only echoes remain. New life sprouts from the detritus of rot. Places of reverence, sacred ground, Towers of Silence. The Shroud in inverse. Peace is the threat of violence. The state has a monopoly on violence through the police and military. Laying the dead to rest is hard work, easier to just shut them up for a while. Too many people need help and you can only do so much. And they get so unpleasant when they realize you're not going to help them. Anxiety does not breed diplomacy. Bad cycles of lopsided interactions, charity models of economic redistribution. Okay, something might be coalescing.

                      Originally posted by Excess View Post
                      So many decisions to make. Is it still the Exorcist or the Gatekeeper now? Is it like the Well and Void or a thing of healthful boundaries or encompassing both?
                      If I may weigh in on the latter:

                      The Void represents the Underworld in its function of devourer (the Reaper's work).

                      The Well represents the Underworld as the place where all information winds up, forgotten (the Ocean of Fragments).

                      Clearly, a power that regulate and constrains the behavior of the dead stems from the Underworld as a place of Laws. Which can be good or bad, but probably requires one to interact with several Dominions and Kerberoi before perfecting such a craft.

                      In that spirit, and maybe to distance it from Exorcism (which I indeed prefer to keep ceremony-tied, for the intertextuality with Mummy and Werewolf), I'd have two contenders for flavor-orienting names:

                      The Epitaph if we want to emphasize the idea of command. You can engrave words upon your heart, like those on a gravestone. And those words of the living imprint their shape to the remembrance of the dead. They quell grief and mark the limit between them and those whose stories finished forever. Or maybe they falsify what has been, for the peace of mind of those who will be. Or maybe both are the same thing.

                      Alternatively, the Cortege (or the Procession) adds the tinge of a cohort of beings (dead, sometimes living) who have to move like you if they want to stay by your side. Who must behave, constrain their behavior to be part of the eternal funerary rite that you embody.

                      Edit: If we want to make it more akin to the two dangerous Underworld Haunts (the Well and the Void)... mmmh, monosyllabic, ominous, dark... the Seal, maybe?
