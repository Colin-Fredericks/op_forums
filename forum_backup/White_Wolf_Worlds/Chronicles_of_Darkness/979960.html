    [2e] Help with focusing on Mysteries

    10-01-2016, 08:43 PM
    I could use a hand. I understand the primary thrust behind Mage 2e is that the PCs are meant to be seekers of occult knowledge, obsessed with ferreting out all the secrets of the Fallen World and becoming masters of the unknown. I also understand the concept behind Mysteries (which, at the end of the day, is a bit of a vague term you'll admit), to be basically a catch all for "supernatural effect of unknown provenance or operation", and that the rules are designed such that the PCs are meant to be actively studying them and solving them.

    But, I have to admit, at the end of the day, I'm not entirely sure what that should look like.

    To take a practical example, the idea that founding a Legacy is about studying an underlying Mystery through action and contemplation. Now, my players are all new to nWoD and Mage, so they have no knowledge of any of the Legacies from 1e. To that end a couple of them are already thinking they'd rather found their own Legacies. One PC is a Free Council social activist who wants to base a Legacy around Imbuing items to give to Sleepers to help them detect and resist magic (which I adore because of how suicidal yet awesome that will be), but what Mystery is explored in doing that? Like, what thing would be out there that they can find and study that would be appropriate for this? What can they Scrutinize? So far all I've got is "they go study the construction of imbued items", but what would that entail, how can I make it appropriate for the Legacy, and above all, how can I make it entertaining?

    In general, I'm also curious how often you actually find your players Scrutinizing and Revealing magic, mine almost never do or even think to do it (in fact Active Mage Sight tends to slip their minds), and I'm not sure how to get them to start doing it. I've had to go "you know, you could do this" far too often for my liking. Additionally, how do you account for Mysteries often being threatening (like really, why would you Scrutinize the the Seers casting Fraying spells at you instead of returning fire?), and how do you build them into the story without it just turning into a drip feed of exposition?

                  I was mostly answering the question of what a Matter Mystery could be, but lets see what I can cook up here.

                  You'll need to figure out where the manufacturers are, go there, inspect their methods of production, and then determine what factors are actually contributing to the magical effect the knives possess. Yes, some of these things can be solved mundanely, but some things you're going to have to use magic and Mage Sight to figure out. Whether the makers are a threat or not is pretty much up to you but I was generally thinking that the people making the knives are just normal people making knives. Whatever it is that's giving the knives this property is completely unknown to them, they don't even know it's a thing. Which means something, be that something an aspect of where the knives are made, a specific set of circumstances around how they're made, or something else of the like.

                  Also, it doesn't need to cause conflict. The simple need to know is all that a lot of mages would need. Now making your players care is kinda on the ST. Specifics are going to almost completely depend on the characters and what they care about/how interested they are in exploring the unknown.

                      I think it should be pointed out that the game doesn't require that Mysteries be solved via Scrutiny. In the end Mage Sight won't reveal more than the proper Knowing or Unveiling spell will, what Scrutiny can do is provide hints on what kinds of spells might be best designed for figuring a Mystery out, and/or brute force its way to the heart of the Mystery. Something Scrutiny does that Knowing and Unveiling spells don't do is tell you, "That's it, there's nothing else to find here."

                          I guess, yeah. So Mysteries themselves aren't really the things that are going to be getting players after them. But Mysteries tied to Obsessions and Aspirations have some incentive built in. And I think you get some special Beats for understanding a worthwhile encounter with the supernatural.

                          So to go with Ashenrogue's example, it might not be something that really leads the players anywhere. But if it somehow tied into the Obsession of the characters, for instance their mentor was found killed, their body entirely converted to silver, finding the knife and locating the manufacturer and other people who have that knife might work towards the hows and whys.

                          For what it's worth I think Signs of Sorcery is really going to expand on Mysteries, and the Mage Sight expansion really offers some clarifications of mechanics involved, as well as things that could detract from a character's ability to identify things through it, including the linking of smaller Mysteries into larger ones.

                          It sounds as if Mysteries can really be anything, from a mage's lingering spell to the bits of a corpse left behind from a werewolf's hunt.

                              Totally reasonable question in the OP. I've been running a chronicle for about 10 sessions and finished 2 stories, let me see what I can offer.

                              My PCs are probably using Scrutiny/Revelation...every/every other session? It almost always happens when I prompt them "this is something your Scrutiny could help with," or "do you want to turn Active Sight on? What Arcana?" I imagine it like an adventure game where I'm telling them "your cursor turns blue when you highlight this object!" It seems like my players are a little different than I am when I get to play Mage, I'm usually very curious and active whereas they tend to lean more "enjoying it, but more along for the ride." When they do latch onto something as especially interesting I lean on that hard and make it more important.

                              Just as an example because I was thinking about it too: my players investigated a Matter Mystery for a lot of the first story. In the first session, a situation arose where they wanted to buy some coke to use as Soft Leverage in Social Maneuvering. I had the dealer mention a new hallucinogenic drug, and offer them a free sample to get them interested- making sure to heavily imply that there was something unusual or supernatural about it. One of my PCs is an Obrimos scientist who used Zoom In to examine the pill's chemical structure, and found the High Speech glyph for Matter fractally repeating at the molecular level, which got them curious. So I'm going out of my way as ST to tell them "THIS IS SUPERNATURAL, CHECK IT OUT."

                              Eventually one of the PCs took the drug (because a demon of Vice used its Influence), and started having an incredible powerful hallucination. Another PC found this weird and, after subduing the Vice demon, I reminded him that he could Scrutinize her with Mind Sight to see what was happening. So we did, and he didn't get enough successes and decided not to roll further to avoid contaminating the Mystery. The first PC is still unconscious and drooling. So they decide to go visit their more powerful Mentor to get help. Somewhere in there was some Active Matter Sight that got some confusing results (the pills are Availability 6+, or as I put it, "the most valuable substance in the Fallen World"), so they did Scrutiny and found that it was made of Apeiron, the Lead of Stygia. Which just raises further questions.

                              It was great! They were legitimately interested and wanted to find out what was going on, especially because there were concrete stakes: "you need to figure out what's happening because one of the PCs is gonna die if you don't fix this." It helps that my players uh, don't really care that much about their stated Aspirations and Obsessions, so they're very willing to listen to my suggestions of stuff to do.

                              Some other things my PCs have used Scrutiny on:
                              • They were in a labyrinth and found a section of wall that they could walk through and didn't know why/didn't want to die in a trap. Another Matter Mystery! (It had Ghostwall cast on it.)
                              • An important Sleepwalker NPC had a couple of spells cast on their Pattern (seen in Prime Sight), one PC was curious and wanted to know what they were.
                              • Their Sanctum has a haunting, and it's not a ghost. They tried Active Sight in a few different permutations and eventually found something in Time Sight- it's a Paradox effect that eludes easy explanation with a Knowing spell.
                              • Their Mentor was acting strangely during a conversation, someone's Peripheral Sight went off, a PC turned on Time Sight and saw temporal warping around her- they quickly used Revelation and found that she had used Shifting Sands to relive the current scene.
                              • Their Mentor's Sanctum has completely disappeared from Space, the lot is empty, and no one remembers her. Once they actually found what to look for with standard Knowing spells (Pierce Deception helped), they did Space Scrutiny and found that it was the target of a "Quarantine" spell, and discovered one of the Keys as Deep Information.


                              I believe every single one of those was prompted by me saying "do you want to Scrutinize this?" My PCs have never turned on non-Ruling Arcana in Active Sight if I didn't bring up the possibility, and even then, sometimes they decide not to.

Get your PC invested in the completely nonmagical, but still uniquely interesting concerns of this person's life, and see whether they use magic to try to fix it. Have them study what happens when the Sleeper inevitably causes Dissonance by being around magic. Is their Pattern affected by being around an Imbued Item all the time? (Answer: yes, it is.)

                                              Scrutiny mechanics are just fancy way to get info about new problem. It is handy, from time to time, but don't sweat when players are not using it. Mine players use all the time Active Mage Sight to check 'if there are weird things under X Arcanum' thing, as it is much quicker. It's not a heart of Mysteries seeking in game. I don't bother in writing proper Mystery Sheet if the thing researched is not starting point of whole story, at least.

                                              The heart of Mysteries is in characters Obsessions - Arcane Beats as reward are more than enough for players to plunge in the Mysteries all around them. There is great topics on that very idea - I just have simple advice to all on this: Give one extra Obsession slot for all characters ( above normal Gnosis limit ) and commit it to cabal's / chronicle main Obsession.

                                              In my London's 'You can be possible next Magic Jesus' game this slot is occupied on 'Are we Hieromagus?' Obsession and in Boston Alchemical play it's for simple 'Philosophers Stone' that is main Mystery of chronicle. If you get this common Obsession of characters, play is much more coherent and players like to cooperate on main story to generate Arcane Beats for themselves.

                                              Then just tell from time to time your players 'You can get Arcane Beat from this Obsession by doing this' and they will be more than happy to sell their soul to the devil... eee, engage in story about those Mysteries.

                                                  Originally posted by Caladriu View Post
                                                  Totally reasonable question in the OP. I've been running a chronicle for about 10 sessions and finished 2 stories, let me see what I can offer.
                                                  This whole reply has been quite helpful. So the way I'm thinking of structuring it now is:

                                                  -Peripheral Mage Sight = Something is up
                                                  -Active Mage Sight = Is it something obvious/directly in my wheelhouse?
                                                  -Scrutiny = This might be a problem, but I don't know enough to make a call and act

                                                  Whereas the general concept of personal Mysteries is "aspects of the supernatural my PCs pursue on their own time"
                                                  While more location/Consilium based Mysteries are "things that let my villains have a grand plot or my Chronicle have a unique terrain"
                                                  And Story-based ones are "shady stuff having an effect"

                                                  This structure lets me think about personal Mysteries as being where we bring in character development, Story as bringing in plots, and location as bringing in overarching narrative. That would make it easier to parse and distribute information, for sure. And it does save me from the fear that they need to be Scrutinizing the whole time or I'm doing it wrong. Granted, it does highlight how much system and lore knowledge you need before writing anything, but that's not new to Mage.
