                            Perspective bias can go do one, or stick to Ascension where it belongs.

                            In the gameline we actually have we explain why the Pentacle believe the things they do while in the next para cheerfully point out why those things make them fail.

                            Seers have to accept that by any routine standard of morality they're doing evil by serving the Exarchs. They do it anyway, in the same spirit as someone working for a company they know is up to no good, or the way we all know our society is built on the backs of the developing world's slave labour but doing something about it is too hard.

                            A Seer could serve because he wants to be on the winning side, or believes himself an exception, or feels like the world pushed him around so now it's his turn to get some, or is afraid of the Exarchs. Unless he's completely delusional, the things his Ministry (and once a prelate, his dreams) tell him to do in their service are obviously Not Good. But magic is payment, and that's the price.

                                    Sorry. To elaborate;

                                    Seers was my first Mage book as a writer. The team still included many cwod veterans, who had had years of people arguing about the Technocracy in Ascension and whether they were justified. The Guide to the Technocracy was written as satire, and taken at face value. In the nWoD, when the time came to write the Pure and Seer books, we were determined to not have that happen. My induction into freelancing was discussions about how we could communicate what the Seers believed while being absolutely clear that they were villains.

                                    It is an experience that has served me well over the years, which is why - coming around to the thrust of this thread - Mage now has sections in the Orders about their hubris, sample Awakenings where you see how obsessed mages have to be to Awaken, and the intro of the book clearly states the horror themes of the game in defiance of certain posters ignoring it. Mage is about obsession, and how chasing it harms the people around you. Its protagonists in the Pentacle are dangerous assholes. The Seers are worse.

                                    Come into the game half-reading it because you read Genius and internalised its creator's tantrum about the sons of ether, and thinking it's cheerleading for mages the way Ascension does, and you get... Threads like this, really.

                                                Spirits of sickness and healing are likely to be attracted or attached to her Sympathetic connections, oils and powders start taking on strange properties, unlikely hybrid organisms crop up, etc. The best way to have the LTN act though is for the actions of the character to indirectly influence how it manifests which requires game time (And can explain why at low Gnosis it's subtle, but the higher your Gnosis rises the more pronounced the effects are). You might get to best bang for your buck by having the Nimbus confront the Mage with positive confirmation bias. They see things that seem to prove them right and this confirms their course of action and outlook to themselves. It's a fairly organic way to run it and represents their magic leaking out of them, and occasionally you can drive that point home by taking a Nimbus inspired set of circumstances to a horrifying logical conclusion, in her case possibly an organic, grey goo that devours people and spirits alike.

                                                Edit: Need an obvious connection between the two? Slap her Signature Nimbus on the effect. Though honestly that might be too on the nose. YMMV.

                                                    The allure of the Seers, particularly for newly Awakened mages who don't yet have a full picture of their new, vastly expanded world, is easy to understand.

                                                    As Dave indicated, just like in the real world, one need not serve because of altruism or some higher purpose. The Seers being "right" has absolutely nothing to do with it.

                                                    The Exarchs have already won much of the fight for reality, the Seers are their winning team, service grants fantastic mundane and magical rewards, and it only requires occasional bad acts while the rest of the time you're free to pursue your own Obsessions. It's a simple cost / benefit analysis.

                                                    When inquiring why any mage would join the Seers, contemplate why any regular person would work for tobacco companies or why so many attorneys would represent known criminals and mobsters. The vast majority of these people sleep well at knight, comfortable that they and their families are safe and secure in a cruel world. Given how the CofD is worse than the real world, the benefits of such service are even more understandable.
