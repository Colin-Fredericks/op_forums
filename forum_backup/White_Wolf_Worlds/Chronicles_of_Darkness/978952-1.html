    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    Honestly, I think White Wolf is just trying to score points with cWoD fans by down-talking ChroD. They kind of have been since the beginning. The very "One World of Darkness" pitch reads like something trying to win over people who resented the split between classic and new. Re-naming nWoD made sense, but it's also a gesture that says "this isn't the real World of Darkness. Martin's comments in that one interview about how "the fans hated" nWoD were very telling as to what fans they are interested in catering to. And this "WoD is a world first and a game second, ChroD is a game first and a world second" sounds like something only a person who knows nothing about ChroD and is just going by what they've heard from people who skimmed the original Requiem, Forsaken, and Awakening core books. Which I'm pretty sure is not true of Shane, which leads me to suspect he's just saying it to win over cWoD fans who that is true of.
    I think a more apt (and less aggressive) way of framing it would be to say that CoD is more about externalized struggle (hence the "punk" element), and ChroD is more about internalized struggle. CoD tends to be more players against the machine (whether that's the Camarilla, the Wyrm/Weaver, or the Technocracy). ChroD is more about how your characters hold onto their integrity in the face of challenges (keeping your sanity in the face of darkness as a mortal, using the people and things you love as an anchor for your humanity in Requiem, balancing your primal urges in Forsaken, learning what it is to be human in Prometheus).

    As long as we keep getting ChroD books, I'm entirely fine with the split and with Paradox hyping the core fanbase however they want. That said, I would have loved to see them say some positive things about lessons learned. ChroD does a very good job of marrying themes to mechanics and making the day-to-day lives of characters more important, and I'd like to see them reinterpret some of those strengths for the cWoD. Hell, the game lines have been around a decade now. You'd think there'd be some positive lessons there. But then again, I'm not the audience they're appealing to.
