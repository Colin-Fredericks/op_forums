    Attributes and Skill Dots Mastery Level : How do you handle it?

    08-15-2024, 12:30 PM
    I love chronicles attributes and skills system. I think since they revised all the attributes in nWoD that they really managed to create a very solid structure. The way Power/Finesse/Resistance are crossed with Mental/Physical/Social is so elegant that I reused it in my homebrew system. I find it strikes the perfect balance of complex and simple in a neat and organized fashion.

    Something I’ve always had trouble with, in CoD, however, is the way each dot implies a level of mastery that’s almost exponential.
    • One dot in an attribute means it’s a weakness, in a skill it means entry-level mastery.
    • Two dots is average for an attribute, or professional level for a skill.
    • Three dots is above average for attributes, or experienced for a skill.
    • Four dots is extremely gifted or an expert in a skill.
    • Five dots is human peak in both attributes and skills.
    And it’s not super hard to increase a character’s attribute and skill to 5.
    This often means that, unless the storyteller restricts character creation and advancement, that a party will be composed of a weird setup of overly exceptional characters. I’ve had parties with the equivalent of a world class surgeon, a spec Ops sniper, Bruce Lee, Einstein and a billionaire within the span of a few games.

    And while it is certainly fun to play such characters and I appreciate that the system lets you create them, sometimes it makes very little sense to have all these exceptionally gifted characters in the same chronicles.

    I can’t blame the players, though. If you want to excel at something you put points into the skill you want to use that make sense. I also find that compared to other Chronicles lines, the basic CoD doesn’t have a lot of things you can spend XP one. Sure there are a lot of interesting merits, but once you got what you need, you don’t have disciplines, power stats and covenants status to increase.

    So how do you usually handle it? Do you restrict attributes and skills to a range that makes more sense? Do you redefine what dots means in the context of your chronicles? Or you just enjoy the campiness of it all?

      I don't put too much stock into the fluff descriptions of what each dot level means. In actual gameplay the mechanics do not bear this out. In fact, mechanically dots work the opposite way.           I'll get the big point out of the way and just note that the limits on Beat earnings means it's unlikely one will earn more than one Experience per session, which puts a bit of a throttle on how quickly one can raise Attributes as the second most expensive Trait available to a bluebook mortal.

          That said, considering Merits are where the entire extended social circle mechanics live (organization rules from Geist, Mummy, and Deviant notwithstanding), and considering that Area of Expertise and Interdisciplinary Specialty are deliberately priced to make Specialties more efficient than Skill dots, while it's theoretically possible for players to decide they want to go a month at a time without getting anything new for their character there's a wealth of opportunities to apply pressure from fresh directions given the breadth of options for Social Merits alone.

          "Once you've got what you need" is a tricky phrase to start bandying about in a setting where the supernatural is in play even if you don't break into things like Dyads or Supernatural Merits, because even at its least fantastical Chronicles is operating in a mode adjacent to a Stephen King story. Mere inertia isn't likely to justify a character becoming an Olympic athlete or a Nobel Prize winning scientist, but if the character is putting the establishing effort in onscreen to explain where those purchases come from I don't see much issue with PCs reaching the peak of human potential after years of dodging monsters and running the rat race.
