      Drawing a bit from my collation of incidental notes on spirit perspective in 1e Werewolf…

      Hunting spirits is difficult. Irraka among the Bone Shadows are taught how to track beings that don’t leave trails, how to mark beings without bodies and how to sneak up on creatures that embody the concepts of perception. In many ways, Irraka of the Bone Shadows are taught to alter what they are in order to best beings that cannot alter what they are (i.e., spirits). That — change to accommodate a goal — is the essence of Cunning.

      […]

      Vindictiveness: A werewolf who just lets things go isn’t a good fit for the tribe. The tribe’s very motto — Pay Each Spirit in Kind — is a cry for justice and vengeance as much as payment. Bone Shadows need to have long memories, and forgiveness is always conditional. If someone wrongs a Bone Shadow, the one who wronged should be prepared for retribution at some point. This refusal to let matters drop is something the tribe sees as critical, because spirits are not generally capable of compromise or forgiveness unless it is explicitly in their nature to be so.

      Ruthless: There is a difference between being ruthless and being completely amoral. […] That said, the Bone Shadows aren’t unduly concerned with honor, and spirits lie, cheat and break their word unless it is in their nature not to. A Bone Shadow isn’t concerned with rising above such behavior, on the whole, but with coming out of the situation intact.
      The Witness is a spirit that listens to vows, oaths, declarations, that kind of thing. But once you’ve sworn to the Witness, you’d better be prepared to follow through. The Witness is a spirit, so it doesn’t understand “I changed my mind” or “My quarry is dead” or “Irreconcilable differences” or anything like that. All it understands is: You made a promise. If you don’t keep it, you’re dead.
      Spirits think in terms of resonance and imperatives, the commands of their bans and the fields of their Influences. What a totem-spirit suggests when its pack asks for advice may be illogical, ill-advised or just plain impossible. This fact is a part of what makes totems more than just reservoirs of power or summoned creatures that can blast spiritual foes. A totem may not attack another spirit because of its ban — though the totem won’t necessarily tell the pack that beforehand. The bond between totem and pack tries to inform the werewolves of how different the totem’s worldview is by requiring the packmembers to hold to a ban, but many packs ignore the underlying lesson.
      And borrowing a bit from Promethean:

      Likewise, spirits maintain very myopic views of the world as well. Everything in a spirit’s surroundings is viewed through the lens of its conceptual attributes: spirits of hate try and clumsily fit even acts of charity into their spiteful world-view, and the concept of a desert is utterly foreign to a spirit of the forests.
      Spirits understand the world only in terms of their concepts. Normally, spirits only see the manifest world clearly and distinctly when it has to do with their nature: a highway is indistinct, a streak of a blur, to a spirit of the hills, but to the travel-spirit that walks the highway, it is the only genuine thing in its sight.

      Resident Lore-Hound

          You're fire. You burn. There is nothing else to your existence.

          Some half-flesh half-spirit abominations show up. They trap you in a circle of cold, beat you to submission. You hate them for being abominations against the nature of things, but you hate them even more for stopping you from burning - from being true to your nature. And yet - you cannot help but respect their power over you.

          But then they do something entirely unexpected - they set you free. They offer you their worship and service. The half-man half-wolves even hunt down and burn one of your enemies, or grant you gifts of essence and access to the Flesh. They work hard, and earn your respect some more.

          So you give your blessing - one of flames and death, as is your nature. The impure nature of the moon-wolves still disgusts you, but the pack has shown loyality and honesty in its deals and agreements. They get to hunt as they please, but still burn their quarry alive, as an offering to you. You get to spread the flames - over victims of their choosing.


          -

          Picture a spider the size of a bus. Picture its face — eyes that don't blink, no facial expressions. The only things that move on that spider's face are its mouthparts, and those for the explicit purpose of feeding itself. On living prey. The spider might be intelligent as you are, but it doesn't care about you, or even other spiders — it's not a social animal, it has no need for society, and it doesn't understand compassion as an abstract concept.

          Spirits are that spider. They don't care about you. They feed on their own kind. They try to promote the things that make them strong, but they do so at the expense of other things — a spirit of healing will promote healing, but it doesn't understand mercy. Without wounds, there can be no healing, so it is happy to let other entities savage and maul their prey so that there will be wounds to heal. It doesn't care if the creatures it heals become overpopulated and begin to starve. It doesn't even care about the people or animals it heals except as a means to its end.

          It cares about werewolves, though — it doesn't like them. It doesn't like the way they smell, or the revolting blend of flesh and spirit with flesh the ruler. You can maybe appease it if you work hard enough and sacrifice enough, but it won't love you in the way that a human being or even an animal can; it isn't equipped to understand *love* as a concept; only the wary sort of respect that a predator can give to a fellow predator it knows it can't take down.

                      Now how would a spirit of loneliness view the world? How would it hunt? A lone stalking monster comes across a loving family, how does it react?
                      You cannot conceive existence on your own; your need others to survive. It hurts - quite literally - your being and essence to not have someone else. So you hunt for someone, anyone, to be with you.

                      You're a good hunter, because you've been doing this for a while, so it doesn't take you long to find someone to share your burden with you.

                      Except that it doesn't last. It can never last. Eventually, all your special someone's energy is gone, left, drained, consumed.

                      And so it's time to start the cycle again.

                                      The comparisons I've used trying to explain spirit nature lately are arousal and addiction. If you're trying to seduce a spirit, it can work, because the whole thing is one giant ball of nerves all excited by one input, but that input probably isn't you or your body. It's water, or death, or sadness, or greed. You have to find a technique that fits what they're made for because they won't change.

                                      Addiction is more obvious, I think. Spirits are consumed by one thing except they never even had other drives to begin with. If they're getting their fix they're somewhat easier to deal with, especially if you're the one providing the fix, but they're still under the effects of some drug and care first and foremost about getting more. If they're worried about getting their fix, they will do all kinds of crazy shit to secure the next one.
