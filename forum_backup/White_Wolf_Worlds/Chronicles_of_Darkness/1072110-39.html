            Design Process Notes: Done with work but have to head home so more thoughts.


            To sum up the new core premise of the Cold Guard: Yes. They oppose extremists who use their supernatural powers and otherworldly magic to amass followers and forcibly control belief. The reason for this will become apparent with this next focus for the new write up: The Cold Guard's history and the extent of their reach.

            Before the one thing I had trouble seeing in regards to my own contributions to the doc, the Cold Guard and Magisters respectively, is how the Cold Guard IS a Conspiracy compared to the Crown Jewels and the Magisters of the Economy...ok quick quick tangent. Is it still possible to change their name to something like The Magisters of Dreams or something? The Economy just doesn't roll off the tongue that well...right, back on topic. For this new write up I'd like to spend a but more time going into their background and how they became a full fledged Conspiracy compared to just a Compact. And for that, I'd like to purpose that the Cold Guard came to be during the height of the Terror after the French Revolution. It was a time where belief in liberty, freedom, and equality for all strata of society could be achieved before the reality set in and the revolution's lack of foresight led to the entire dream collapsing in on itself and into a hungry monster. Never has there been such a clear metaphor for a Noble becoming Dethroned than what happened to the ideals of the French Revolution after the dust settled and it became clear that dreams alone weren't enough to make a stable system of government. It's during this terrifying era that a group of revolutionaries watched their leader, the one who rallied them to the cause and gave them such hope, turn into a literal monster and rampaged before he was put down with sword and gunfire. After his death the eyes of his compatriots slowly began to clear and they realized just how powerful an influence he had over them. Supernaturally so, and from there they began a journey of discovery that led to the revelations of the Nobility who stoked the fires of both the revolution and aristocracy and how, while not directly their fault, their ability to effortlessly sway the hearts and minds of others made the situation worse for everyone in the end. Thus did they swear an oath to do what is necessary to prevent these Enchanters from ensnaring the beliefs of society as heavily as they had done to their own nation. Thus did the seeds of a Conspiracy get sown.

            Yes, I realized while writing this that the whole escape from Dreamland didn't happen until the Apolo 11 moon landing. But I'm pretty sure that it was said that there were basic Princesses throughout history, just not nearly as many as after the whole escape.

            Yes, this does fundamentally change some of the other themes of the Cold Guard. But it's still an interesting theme to explore in my opinion, and it doesn't change the fact that they still Hunt the Nobles. Only that they don't always hunt them to the death now. Everybody wins.

            Please feel free to leave thoughts & comments.

                Okay, got a rough idea for the mob war.

                The Philly Mob War

                A decade before the princesses returned to Earth, Philadelphia’s criminal underworld experienced a massive upheaval. For years, the local family of La Cosa Nostra had been relatively quiet. Angelo Bruno, “The Gentle Don”, preferred the business-like approach to crime that involved quiet rackets with minimal violence to make money. This didn’t sit well with his underlings however, and on March 21st, 1980, the Gentle Don was murdered in front of his own home on 10th and Snyder. The murderer was brought to answer to the Commission, and suffice to say never made any trouble again.

                Bruno’s assassination paved the way for younger mobsters to try and take the city themselves, but they made themselves too big a target for the Feds to ignore. A short mob war was fought, and despite angling by others the New York families organized John Stanfa to take over Philadelphia. This didn’t sit well with many of Philly’s native mobsters, and these “Young Turks” began their own war to seize control of Philadelphia, especially the drug trade. For over a decade Philadelphia would experience a mob war right out of the worst Depression Era. Street hoods with chips on their shoulders squared off against the experienced men who made their bones for decades. The fighting dragged in other crime outfits, from outlaw bikers to black gangs who wanted a cut of what the Mafia could offer. Despite the lucrative hopes, the volatility of the family pushed many criminals away, and even into the arms of the up-and-coming Slavic mobsters streaming out from the Iron Curtain.

                By all rights, the newly blossoming princesses would have become part of the mob war if not for the fact that the various hunter organizations were already making their plays. TFV made sure that the FBI had a close finger on the pulse of the problem, as the Malleus listened in on some dark and desperate confessions. The embryotic Union took to the streets, as the Ascending Ones cut off drugs to anyone getting too violent towards civilians (and out of their control). The new princesses, most of them teenagers, were still trying to figure out what, exactly, they were. The idea of facing down armed and dangerous groups in such an environment was a little too much even for such proactive individuals, especially when the stories started to spread about hunters calling them dangerous witches.

                Realizing that they needed to safeguard their lives, the oldest and most experienced princesses in Philadelphia steered their younger charges away from the mob war, focusing them on anything else. Urban decay, street gangs, decaying schools, anything to keep them safe from the mob war, and the hunters who claimed it as their problem. Two princesses who decided to buck the warning were later reported missing by their families, and have never been found.

                The aftermath is still felt through the city, as the Philly Mob is weakened and left adrift by the bad blood felt by other criminals thanks to the Young Turks actions. Despite being reasonably active for such a weakened family, the Philadelphia Mafia is seen by many to be on the decline, and some have posited that what was once their territory will be up for grabs by the New York families very soon.

                Story Hooks

                An older princess who lived through the war sees the lack of attention on the Philly Mob as a chance to right the wrongs she never had a chance to. The police are spreading rumors that a woman is attacking gambling rackets, money laundering fronts, and threatening the lives of several soldiers of the mob. Unfortunately, the mob has also become semi-reliable for getting weapons and information for several hunter groups, to the point of being nearly indispensable. Either the princess stops, or someone needs to fill a sizable power vacuum.

                    Design Process Notes: Urrrghh...so...tired...


                    Well I'm quite sure I'm not getting to the write up tonight after all. In my defense, I dare anyone to walk 30 min home with the last leg being up a particularly steep hill and then see just how much into writing you are. That being said, I did want to talk about one last point I'd like to hone...*hehe*...when I do get some time to write without feeling exhausted. Particularly something that's also been a point of pushback from the community: the fact that the Cold Guard does hunt the Hopeful.

                    Because there's no denying it is a brutal aspect of their character. The Hopeful are often young, genuinely trying to help, and have put themselves in danger for others many times. What would drive a person to look at someone like that and see a monster? Especially when it could really be just a child underneath the magic and illusions. Well, the fact that they can and do gives the Conspiracy Teeth. And every Compact and Conspiracy needs Teeth in Hunter the Vigil because the game emphasizes both the potential for great heroism and great atrocity. Without Teeth, they're not really Hunters with a capital H are they. They're just regular mortals. With too much Teeth they become unsympathetic. And to the credit of some of the pushback, the way they are written now does lean a little to much on the Teeth aspect. I don't think this was done intentionally though, and from many of the supporters of the original concept we see instances where the balance works out just right. To give some examples of my thought process for why the Cold Guard hunts the Hopeful, here is a list I made while on a 10 today:

                    ~ Q: Why do Hunters hunt Princesses?
                    ~ A: Because despite their intentions, all Princesses are walking time bombs that will naturally cause many casualties when they do finally go off.
                    ~ A: Because the face of the Dethroned is the true face of the Nobility. For all their ideology they are still nothing more than hungry monsters.
                    ~ A: Because Princesses foster dependency in those they help, forcing them to rely upon the Nobles to solve all their worldly problems.
                    ~ A: Because Princesses straight up manipulate people, and because they believe they're right that makes them extremely dangerous.

                    That last point has stuck with me the entire day, and has informed a lot of the design process I've been formulating. Because of all the answers given that one is the most human and relatable reason for why a person would look at a Princess and see a monster. Everything else is an added incentive while this could very well be what make a mortal a Hunter. And that angle is what I'd like to explore with this iteration of the Cold Guard. So, if this is why they hunt Princesses then the next logical step is: What are they willing to do to stop them?

                    Yes, this has been gone over before in the doc. That's entirely to the community's credit though. It's what gives me confidence in the heart of this project.

                    Yes, writing is not my strong suit. But progress is being made. I'll get a write up for it out, it just might take a couple of days.

                    Thoughts and opinions?

                        I feel like I should correct some factual inacuracies.

                        Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                        Because Princesses straight up manipulate people
                        The leadership splat of Princesses, Graces, have an automatic Belief Compromise for manipulating people.

                        Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                        Because Princesses foster dependency in those they help
                        Hearts and Diamonds go out of this way to avoid this by investing in mortals. Other courts might foster dependency by accident; but intentionally encouraging mortals to depend on you is considered Mirrors behaviour by the Radiant.

                        Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                        with how they technically "feed" off of the hope of others
                        They do not feed on hope. Princesses recharge by generating new Wisps internally when feeling positive emotions.

                        Only the Court of Tears feeds from Hope, and that's why they're considered to be bad guys by other Princesses.

                        Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                        how fucked up Belief as a Integrity system actually is when it punishes the players for "giving up on their beliefs"
                        While giving up on your beliefs does trigger a breaking point roll; there is a positive dice modifier when a Princess abandoned a belief because she realised it wasn't a good idea. I designed the Court of Mirrors as a metaphor to highlight that:

                        1) Clinging too hard to impractical Beliefs is a flaw Nobles are prone too.
                        2) Nobles know they have this flaw and work to overcome it.

                        There is nothing fucked up about saying that strong belief can be a source of strength. Which is what the Princess' core book actually says. And there is nothing in the Princess text that says Nobles should dogmatically hold onto their Beliefs; quite the opposite. By villifing the Court of Mirrors the book says that if you draw strength from Beliefs you should make sure your Beliefs are sensible ones.

                        Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                        and of course with how fucking comfortable this game is with all of it's focus of dreams and hope with turning the player characters into monster butchering machines.
                        Given that a theme is child soldiers; that Sensitivity explicitly says that a Princess will never become immune to the pain of seeing friends and comrades fall in battle; that Shadows are inspired by PTSD. I don't think you can call the text too comfortable with anything.

It's simply a natural force like gravity that's drawn to some people and turns them into Princesses. So nobody's morally culpable for making Princesses fight, and nobody is preventing her from stopping (Princesses encourage each other not to push themselves too far).

                        (It's also possible for a Princess to have a long and successful career without ever fighting monsters. Champions are one X splat; there's four others)

                        I think that the reason you're getting pushback is that you are not exploring what being a Noble entails; but exploring a misuderstanding of what being a Noble entails; criticisng Princesses for things that the Princess book takes the time to explicitly address without taking that addressing into account.

                        Tony is a Prince of Hearts, and Hearts believe that Princesses must be held accountable to the communities they serve. Steve is a Prince of Swords believe that Princesses should be independent heroes who're free to follow their conscience.

Since Princesses must be Paragons due to that aforementioned fucked up Belief system the material in the vid can easily translate to Princess on several levels.

                        Originally posted by Dusksage View Post
                        ~ Q: Why do Hunters hunt Princesses?
                        It is a a tough question. While it's easy to say why do Hunters hunt this specific Princess; asking why Hunters hunt Princesses in general is a very hard question.

                        Because for most reasons you can think of, either they're factually wrong (e.g. all Princesses are walking time bombs) which means the Conspiracy will not have the power to make a Princess player stop and think. Or they apply to some Princesses but are directly contradicted by other Princesses. If you say that Princesses are too emotional to be trusted with so much power; the Court of Diamonds believes in logic and reason. If you say that Princesses are too stubborn and always doing what they think is right the Court of Clubs believes in understanding and compromise. etc

The Magisters of the Ecconomy work because even though their criticisms of the Nobility don't work for all Princesses, the fact that they are also in it for personal gain means that it dosn't need to work for all Princesses.

That evil is something which must be fought and overcome. Come up with another definition of good and evil; and another way of doing good that fits the new definition.
