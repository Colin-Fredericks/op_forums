    Princess: the Hopeful, Crystal Edition

    09-23-2023, 09:36 AM
    Princess: the Hopeful is a fanmade game line about magical girls in the Chronicles of Darkness, struggling against the overwhelming evil with the light of Hope. A lot of people loved the premise but found the rules clunky and the execution profoundly unfocused. I was one of them.

    Princess: The Hopeful, Crystal Edition

    It's not DONE done -- it's in beta, which means it's feature-complete. A few options need to be filled in (a couple stat blocks, some Signets only have one option at a given level, Embassies don't have all their benefits, and not all the interstitial fictions are in), but all of the systems are in and you can play it perfectly fine. I mean, it's probably imbalanced as all hell, but that's what a beta is for. It's also probably formatted badly, and there's places I didn't catch where I use the wrong term for something because I changed it later. That's why it's only 0.8.

    Crystal Edition is not a page-one rewrite of P:tH, but it's at least a page 2 or 3 rewrite. The structure and Queens are still there but everything has been revamped and refocused to give it clear through-lines both emotionally and physically. And the magic system is totally new and, though I do say so myself, it's really cool.

    There's no more specific spells to buy one at a time. Each Charm has a basic action to it, and a bunch of Upgrades that expand its capabilities multiplicatively, so you stack together your Upgrades to get a spell that can do something neither Upgrade alone could do, and in doing so make an anime word salad spell name! Do you want to hit a ghost with a truck? Give it a Redline Phantom Step! Fling someone's bullets back at them? Rewinding Restoration, Stat! You wanna do sick air combos with multiple weapons? Weapon Master into Aerial Rave! Play three-card monte with your own presence? Blunt Tricky Cunning Illusion! How about a devastating magical finisher? BOTTOMLESS INFINITY PETADRIVE ANNIHILATION HYPER-EXPLOSIVE BUSTER!

    Story and theme wise, The All-Consuming Darkness is not some new and distinct entity that inflicts supernatural taint on people to make them Darkened when they spend too long near a tainted place. The taint of the Darkness is fear. The supernatural corruptive force that drives others to spread misery in its name is literally the emotion of fear. The game's central thesis is about how much of the suffering we inflict on others has fear at its heart, how fear offers an ultimatum of "spread me to others and I will stop tormenting you for a moment." The world is drenched in the Darkness and thus drenched in fear down to its bones, and Princesses believe that all of the weird-ass inexplicable nonsensical spooky horrors of the CofD are all manifestations of the Darkness, though of course whether or not they are correct about that is another matter.

    The basic "okay so what do I do" problem is solved by presenting a basic ground-level threat: Nightscapes, which are like Madoka labyrinths or Persona dungeons created by someone breaking under the weight of fear, and send out monsters to re-enact it. You don't just need to blow up the monsters, you need to figure out the source of the pain and overcome the psychodrama the prisoner has created in order to solve it, so there's multiple types of gameplay involved.

    Instead of having a focus on virtue and public service that got frankly weird in the old versions, a magical girl character is someone who has a vividly defined personality and ideals, such that when they overcome obstacles, it's validation of their ideals. Princesses now have a Drive and Dread instead of Virtue and Vice; a guiding principle that they're About and something whose fear most motivates their behavior. Wisps are regained from spending Willpower, your human spirit purifying and refining. You can emotionally invest yourself into whatever you do by Flaring your power, granting you a free dot of your power stat, Inner Light, and you can do it multiple times... but if you emotionally commit like that, and you still fail, or you still have to compromise your Belief, the consequences will be devastating. The stakes are high and the tension is overwhelming and you will need to devote everything you are to succeeding and that might still not be enough. Rather than uncertainty of if you're a human any more, the driving uncertainty is if you are good enough, if anyone could possibly be good enough, and what happens when you fail. Camaraderies are gone, coordinating information through the Dreamlands is next to impossible, there's no extant support structure for Princesses -- it's all on your own shoulders.

    I've spent the better part of a year on this, with help from the Princess: the Hopeful Discord, and I think the result is really, really good. Check it out if the premise even vaguely interests you, I'd love to get as much feedback as I can, I want this to be the best book it can possibly be.​

                  Caveat that I'm still in the process of reading, having just reached the start of the Signet Charms section today, but so far…

                  The degree to which Belief is easier to retain and regain than other purchased Integrity analogues makes me wonder why it isn't running on a costless model instead.

                  The general tone of the text seems more interested in being nominally a magical girl game that tells you it's set in the Chronicles of Darkness than actually engaging with what "magical girls in the Chronicles of Darkness" could mean. An argument could be made that the Noble are coming at the supernatural from the same prioritized angle as werewolves, deviants, and hunters such that even among the court of the Queen of Currents there aren't many occultist-adjacent Princesses who're curious about what the Inner Light is in the greater scheme of things and the metaphysical nitty-gritty of their condition, but when Princesses have been around for at least long enough to have reached middle age it feels off for the setting presentation to include discussion of the rough "timeline" of the fall of the Fearless Kingdoms not any discussion of how they've moved beyond the most simplistic declarative statements about how the world works.

                  Speaking of which, I don't feel that the presented history and metaphysics of The All-Consuming Darkness as the actual emotion of fear (somehow distinct from the heuristic of caution in a world where monsters still existed and things still went wrong and people died and all manner of normal things to be afraid of) hangs together at all. Survival is hard; growth and development and community require directed work; utopia is a process and it feels cheap to claim that "the best of all possible worlds" was achieved without anyone ever fearing for their life along the way and simultaneously that the Noble being reincarnated from such a world are not meaningfully psychologically different from the people they grew up with.

                  The overall flat structure of Charms and Upgrades feels a little fiddly — there's a lot of Charms that are basically modeling minor subsystems of their own even before bringing Upgrades into it, and the language model for some of those subsystems feels like it's eating more wordcount than necessary to communicate the mechanical effect and its intended feel, which, combined with Upgrades themselves being explicitly designed to be able to be stacked onto each other for complex and partially freeform effects, gives the impression that there must surely be more mechanically and stylistically elegant ways to accomplish the intended outcomes; Mage has its spell factors and Practices and Arcana (and Down and Dirty Spellcasting mechanics for if "do I pull off this narrative effect" is the most important mechanical question), Deviant has Magnitude and Deviations and keywords affecting Variations at a level that nevertheless changes slowly enough for your powers to be largely consistent from chapter to chapter, Geist 2e makes Keys largely optional motific power-boosts that don't act as gates for new Enhancements, and so on, but if I drop the three Experiences for my Grace to learn how to Detect I basically have to memorize or cross-reference a spell-grammar and a specificity-based range adjustment and a list of modifiers (applicable on-the-fly) that gets longer and more complicated if I invest more into it.

                  Almost none of the Charms except Buster have a basic dicepool that doesn't change, Plea's twist on the Social Maneuvering rules is a headache to parse out, Restoration's limits on piecemeal healing add another layer of bookkeeping that really isn't necessary, Anthem explicitly ignores the Clash of Wills system for a category of power it should logically just Clash with where appropriate, and there doesn't seem to be any rhyme or reason to how many other Upgrades are needed to be able to buy a given advanced Upgrade, nor does the book actually seem to spell out whether Charms can be used without being Transformed (there's language that suggests this to be the case, but the breadth of bonuses from Transformed Attributes and Skills and Regalia could as readily mean they're just a lot easier to use in that state). Coupled with the explicit statement that Charms and Upgrades can do some markedly nonstandard stuff (beyond multiple Upgrades outright changing or inverting the basic effect of the Charm), it all starts to look like a bit of a hodgepodge for a power system whose root differentiating structure is essentially verb-based.

                  There is a general issue of seemingly making dicepools very large that is giving me vibes of the old RPGnet discussions coming up around the game borrowing a lot of sensibilities and aesthetics from Exalted 2e; I can understand the notion of sincerity as a theme, and there's certainly something to be said for giving every Princess affinity for the "big finishing move" Charm, but I've seen enough anime to know that literally shouting translated six-kanji confections of attack names is a presentational quirk rather than a diegetic phenomenon and I think the multiple Charms with explicit exemptions to that means you do, too.

                  While I understand the notion of making a gameline where playable character types are broader than the explicit archetypal basis from media, I can't say I get why Devil May Cry gets more play than Kingdom Hearts on the inspirational media front, nor how the repeated suggestion of 3D glasses as Regalia is supposed to be taken. Even disregarding the divergence from Chronicles' "house style" in the writing, the tone feels all over the place and more than a little over-memed in a way that doesn't help me sell this book to a hypothetical friend who is interested in playing magical girls in the Chronicles of Darkness instead of, say, a very particular game of Deviant.​

                      Thank you very much for the detailed feedback! I am always looking for negative feedback, and I consider it all valuable, even if I don't end up agreeing with it. I would quote reply to each point line by line, but that would take a billion pages.

                      I don't get what you mean by Belief being easier to retain. There is a load-bearing core mechanic of "stack this buff that gives you a penalty to all Compromise rolls" and you can roll Compromises for failing, even and especially when you had no other option. Buying with XP is not the primary way you're supposed to get it back, but I figured leaving it in would be useful in a few edge cases and wouldn't hurt anything to have the option. Why do you think it's easier to retain?

                      Fear is explicitly distinct from caution or wariness in the setting chapter. I made this clearer in that section, used the phrase "fear as we know it" to help emphasize this is not the way we're used to things working, and added to the glossary section: fear is not the emotion of being concerned for one's safety, it's the thing that tortures you when there might be a concern for your safety, paralyzes you from doing something about it, and demands you inflict pain on others to make itself go away.

                      I don't understand what you mean by "a magical girl game that tells you it's set in the Chronicles of Darkness" vs "magical girls in the Chronicles of Darkness." If you mean atmosphere and tone, I talked in the introduction about how the setting doesn't seem like it should have magical girls, and that's what every magical girl setting is like, because a magical girl story is fundamentally a conflict about what genre the story is. Do you mean it's not dark enough and focuses too much on being cool and empowering? Is that something that darker interstitial fiction could help?

                      Or do you mean that it doesn't engage enough with existing canonical setting elements? Because I intentionally took most of those out, those are crossover things and the game needs to stand on its own independently. Also when you talk about the other established setting things, 90% of the time it's Mage, I noticed that and just started tearing pieces out.

                      Transformation is required to use Charms unless you have Regalia that lets you use it while not Transformed. This is one of those editing things where I moved stuff and rephrased it and didn't notice the only place I actually stated you have to be Transformed to use them was the section on Luminous Beats. Fixed that. Same deal with the 3D glasses: I just copied it from one section document to the other and didn't notice I didn't take it out.

                      The Inner Light in the grand scheme of things is not an external power source you tap into, it's a thing inside of you. Like "using black magic to suck out someone's juices" and "intimidating and browbeating someone into giving you your way" are on the same continuum of "using the Darkness for power," your magic is on the same continuum as your ability to aspire to mundane things and then make them happen. I definitely could have made that clearer, but don't know if I should. The reason there's no occultism into the Inner Light is, in addition to the notion that it is *extremely* hard for the Radiant to coordinate and communicate, it doesn't fit the metaphor of what magic is. It's part of your transformation, which is discovering your potential and growth and who you want to be. Therefore, it can't be occult, because it cannot be "hidden" from you by any external power and the information can't lie elsewhere. You don't learn Charms by rote, you develop them as your own skills -- where would be a good place to mention this, and how would be a good way to phrase it?

                      I erred on the side of wordy in the Upgrades descriptions for the sake of clarity (my thought was, like Magic cards are worded). I find a few more words that make it exactly clear when things end and suchlike are less complicated than being shorter but vaguer. I feel like here's the most intractable disagreement, because you just seem to object to the fundamental elements of a modular system, which is of course going to allow you to increase complexity with each upgrade.

                      My experience and opinion have been counter to yours on how complex the system is. I've specifically been told Detect is the clearest and easiest to grasp Charm and the place where the modular Upgrades system really shines. I don't find that all the potential Skill and Attribute combinations you have are unnecessarily complicated, because IME, you pick the ones that give you A Good Dice Pool and that's it. The reason I had the Upgrades allow you to use different Skills or Attributes is to A: feel more like it's Your Thing and not a spell off of a book, and B: since the Attributes you favor are dictated by your Y-splat but the Charms you have affinity for are dictated by your X-splat, you need a way to make every Calling's Charms use at least some of the Attributes each Queen is good at. A Grace of Spades should exist, but if they can't use Manipulation to cast Plea instead of Presence, then they aren't getting to do their thing in their way.

                      The dice pools can be a bit high -- it's not a huge number of bonuses since you're picking your best Attributes and Skills instead of stacking them all, but you are getting equipment bonuses and buffs along with it. That's why none of the Charms are pass/fail, and everything scales with Intensity, so it's not a matter of "well I have all the dice ever so I never fail."

                      But ultimately, whether the system is too complicated and fiddly, too wordy, or too dice-y isn't really something we can hash out. I am open to the possibility it might be, but it's the kind of thing we need to see people play in order to see how it shakes out. That's the point of releasing a beta.

                      Anthem's Clash is specifically so you can amp it up when usually you can't do that with a Clash. If you rock harder, you should be able to blast away harder magic. Plea I admit is a bit clunky, but I didn't see any other way to do what it needed to do (which is to engage with existing systems in a way that gives it several hooks for Upgrades to go instead of bypassing the system, serve as a way to deal damage to Nightscapes, and very clearly be an "ask very nicely" power and not a "mind control" power). You didn't hit Nightscapes yet, so maybe knowing that "it's the same thing as dealing Psyche damage to a Nightscape" would help. Restoration's re-cast is an artifact of when it worked differently, so I fixed that.

                      As to why I included DMC, I explained that in the "inspirations" section, I think it fits in really nicely both in terms of "power of emotions makes you stronger than evil" and "how you fight is an expression of who you are," it gives a bit of a hook for people who need help figuring out how to make a character concept that isn't the most typical magical girl, and it really opened up some needed character space. Once you say Dante is a follower of Spades and Vergil a follower of Mirrors, who are really some of the best examples to use, why not go whole hog? The reason I didn't use Kingdom Hearts is, well, I have no idea what the fuck is going on in Kingdom Hearts. "Darkness" and "Light" are super important in KH but are very poorly defined, which is not what I need when I'm about vividly defined characters whose actions are validations of their ideals; and the other concepts I know about are either already covered, or would take lots of focus and are too specific (stuff like Nobodies)

                      Again, thank you so much! You caught some things I needed to change right away, made me ask some other questions I needed to ask, even the things I didn't agree with are things I should be aware of, and just really I appreciate you taking the time to provide such detailed feedback.
