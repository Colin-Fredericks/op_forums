    The crunch side of ephemeral

    03-18-2015, 10:49 AM
    I've got a couple questions about spirits and dice pools for people who have played these sorts of things out.

    To begin with, Spirits and the like just have the three Attributes, which are supposed to stand in for the Attribute + Skill that corporeal beings have. That much seems pretty straightforward and clear. The blurb to generally call out what they roll (p. 184) says "They roll the appropriate Attribute + Rank for actions relating directly to their concept, or Attribute + Attribute for actions like surprise or perception". That seems to suggest that surprise and perception are exceptions to the just-roll-one-Attribute rule and that Rank is a bonus for things particularly in line with the concept. As such, a murder or stealth spirit might roll Finesse + Rank to tail someone.

    All the rolls that specifically say what to roll, though, have something like Power + Finesse, or generally Attribute + Attribute, as though that were the norm. Given the general paradigm of corporeal beings doing Attribute + Skill, that makes sense, but being as each spirit Attribute is supposed to be an Attribute + Skill pool all rolled up, putting two together gets to be pretty intense once you get past Rank 2.
    At the bottom edge of Rank 3, a spirit with balanced attributes would have no dice pool below 10, whereas one that stacked a particular stat or pair (and grown naturally from a Rank 2 spirit) would have a minimum of 7 dice for anything at all that they did. At the high end of 3, no pool would be less than 16 dice.
    And that's before the stat-boosting that spirits are allowed to do by spending essence.

    So the actual questions I have are these:
    Why are so many things that get called out based on two Attributes instead of one?
    How do you actually make any headway against anything stronger than a Hursah?
    Is the intention that, while Rank 6+ are explicitly untouchable, Rank 4 is roughly the threshold for grand-story-arc-to-overcome?
    My best guess as to how to deal with an Ensah that needs to go down is to use Bottle Spirit and then make it into a Fettish, thereby skipping over its defenses entirely. (Sure, the bottling half is risky as the thing gets four turns to kill me or the bottle, but still...)

    What are other ways to deal with actively antagonistic ephemeral beings of higher but approachable ranks?

      If at Rank 6+ they become veritable stat-less gods, then it follows that the average spirit at Rank 3 is "dangerous", at Rank 4 is "very dangerous", and at Rank 5 is "fucking terrifying". This is, I'm pretty sure, completely on purpose. It's not a bug, it's a feature. Rank 4-5 Spirits should be scary even to a semi-experienced pack of werewolves. It's the highest tier of "statted" spirits you can get.

      The best way to deal with any spirit, of any Rank, is to decipher its Ban and Bane, and then exploit the ever living fuck out of them.

                                  I went though the book with a search. Here's what I found for multiple-attribute rolls:

                                  Seeing through an Ithaeur's 4-dot moon gift takes Finesse + Resistance penalized by a Rank, though not the viewers (117)
                                  Attempts to steal Essence are Power + Finesse contested by Power + Resistance (184)
                                  Gorging on Essence at a point of suitable Condition is Power + Finesse (184)
                                  Voluntarily coming into contact with bane takes Resolve + Composure (185)
                                  Attacking is listed as Power + Finesse (185 & 186)
                                  Using and influence requires Power + Finesse (186)
                                  Power + Finesse to read an Urged beings thoughts or urge it to act (190)
                                  Detaching from a Claimed host is Power + Finesse - Rank (191)
                                  Each Numina is activated by a Power + Finesse roll (191)
                                  The Implant Mission Numina calls for a special Power + Finesse roll, possibly redundant with the previous line (192)
                                  The idigam Essence Trap power uses a Power + Finesse roll, opposed by just Resistance (217)
                                  Idigam powers of Gauntlet, Ghost, and Spirit Manipulation all call for Power + Finesse rolls, with the latter two being opposed by Finesse + Resistance (218)
                                  Several other idigam powers, including all of the Forge Servant ones...
                                  Also, Totems oppose the Totem's Wrath (Honor Facet of Gift of the Pack) with all three attributes combined.

                                  So most of those do seem like the use of powers, other than the attacking one and the seeing through the Spirit Masquerade one. The seeing through the Ithaeur's mask roll seems like you'd almost never be able to fool even a beefy Rank 2 spirit if you slipped up.
                                  That said, what's a spirit ever going to roll for other than to use its powers or to attack?
