                            Originally posted by Axelgear View Post
                            Having the capacity to cause pain is not the same thing as existing to cause pain. There is a big difference. Stuff in the Hedge sounds like the latter.

                            If you want the capacity to cause pain, any sort of danger suffices. The Hedge is the place where dreams live; a place of extremes and unreality. If you want it to be psychoactive, just let emotional extremes take hold.

                            You get the same effect without specifically laser-sighting it on something that will drive players away.
                            This is a sliding scale, not a binary state. Some players want no danger. Some want all danger. This is a moderately high amount of danger, with remarkable rewards that can save you from captivity. Braving the things that hurt you in order to save yourself from experiencing the worst again? To some people, that's worth it. If you're being hunted, getting your Icon could mean the difference between being caught and being free. Some will go for the goblin fruits. Some will go because Hollows are not only keen gear, but they're also potentially life-saving. Some will go because when you jump in the Hedge, the Huntsmen lose your scent. Some will go because the real world is ALSO very dangerous, and sometimes it's better to travel in the Hedge you know, rather than the jungle you don't.

                            As a sliding scale, some players will always be driven away. From everything. It's why there aren't any games that appeal to everyone. Some players want the light-hearted approach of Toon. Some want the visceral intensity of 3:16. I cannot please every player, and trying to only leads to frustration and watered-down games. And there's a certain segment who will latch onto a specific idea, to the stark exclusion of explicitly stated content that contradicts their preconceived notions.

                            Pretty things can hurt you. While poetic, that's true in the real world. Making that more literal in a place where abstract concepts are a little more literal? I don't think that's a big stretch.

                            Ultimately, the Hedge isn't that much different. The rewards are higher. The idea of "thorns" is getting an intentionally more abstract treatment. So it sounds to me (and correct me if I'm wrong) like you just don't like the idea of the Hedge, and you want it to be something different entirely. Which is valid. But, I rather enjoy the Hedge as it is. I just wanted to make the rewards for going there a bit higher, and make the decor less assumedly uniform.

                            And let me level with you: If I did what you were saying, I'd have dozens of people saying I was "changing it for the sake of changing it" and insisting that they won't touch this edition on account of that change.

Now, why pain and not fear or anger or some other negative; that's a different question. One that has it's own answers likely to do with making it different and unique from other Otherworlds already present and covered in other ways.

                                            The Hedge was made of Thorns in 1ed too, this is not new. Just a refocusing and refining. You've already said you didnt like it then either. I think you just don't like what the Hedge is, and want it to be something else. Edit: It sounds like what you want is Arcadia without the True Fae, and that is not, nor has it ever been what the Hedge is.
                                            The Hedge, the place where Changelings go when they dream, and where there are literal doors into the dreams of people scattered throughout it, is not the place dreams live?

                                            As for your reasoning as to why pain, I would say that my counter-point that a far better way to emphasize the horror related to Changeling is to go for extremes and not just to aim for pain innately. Aiming for pain straight and true is simplistic; it gets the job done but it only gets it done one way. Emphasizing pain first, foremost, and always removes other possibilities and dampens some of the other themes of Changeling.

                                            Consider, for example, the Seasonal Courts. The Seasonal Courts are either inspired by the Stages of Grief or they're channeling them subconsciously. All the emotions involved are in some way natural and potentially healthy but the blunt truth is that the Courts aren't about healthy expression; they're about compensation without moving on. Even the seemingly "good" Court of Spring, which supposedly spreads joy wherever it goes, is a walking pile of denial and decadence. Spring Courtiers could, theoretically, live good and noble lives in moderation and temperance but the game doesn't reward that. Instead, they're incentivized to be licentious and Bacchanalian revelers; drowning everyone around them in drink and drugs and decadence.

                                            That is a horror story; trying desperately to run from your past and failing; turning other people into vessels for your excess. You don't have to ruin lives, but you will, because you're an addict and the Gentry made you that way. It's a horror story you make because no-one forced your hand, and so you don't have the opportunity to pretend it wasn't you every step of the way when the curtain falls and the audience is applauding at the tragedy you've made of your life.

                                            The Hedge is at its best when it's the place of fairy tales; when it's the path to Grandmother's House; when it's got a dragon's lair or a cannibal's larder. A place for Changelings to indulge their fae side. The Thorns existed in 1e, but they weren't out to cause you pain; they were hungry. Hungry for life; for emotion; for warm blood. The Thorns were psychoactive, but the Hedge itself wasn't out innately to hurt you. It just never really had enough in it that justified the risks taken.

So it sounds to me (and correct me if I'm wrong) like you just don't like the idea of the Hedge, and you want it to be something different entirely. Which is valid. But, I rather enjoy the Hedge as it is. I just wanted to make the rewards for going there a bit higher, and make the decor less assumedly uniform.
                                            To be perfectly honest, none of those things hold that much appeal to me.

                                            Icons are a purely reactive thing, and I've never been a huge fan of purely reactive gameplay. I've run entire games of Changeling where goblin fruit has not come up once because none of the examples from the game have been all that compelling; they seemed like the mandatory healing item, something that the Spring contracts made moot in most motleys. Hollows are neat but, again, all the benefits you're talking about are reactive; preparations the players make for a hammer coming down that, honestly, might never come.

                                            If the Hedge is all about avoiding going back to Arcadia, I'm... Honestly kind of bored with that notion. The persistent threat of the True Fae coming to get you is fun, to be sure, but it can't and shouldn't be every arc a Changeling has. The "I am being hunted" thing is a grand and epic peak to a story, not the main body of it; a thrilling and terrifying conclusion to an arc or even a game. It's far and away not enough meat to fuel a long-running game.

                                            I like the Hedge, but I like it as a place that lets fairytale stories play out, and that doesn't sound like where the focus is at the moment. Right now, it sounds like something that purely exists as a complication for other stories. When it's just contributing to yet more of the "I want to run from everything fae" side of Changeling, I feel like that's getting ignored.

                                            If no-one else agrees with me, that's fine. I'll just... Do what I did before.
