    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    So we're dealing with these Chronicles of Darkness where a diverse population of monsters are hiding in the shadows, conducting their affairs away from mortal eyes…

    …and said supernatural entities cluster together, each into its own shadow society that exists in parallel to the others and largely ignorant of each others.

    Right. That seems rather unlikely.
    If your assumption is that the distribution of vectors for gregarious supernatural beings is both uniform and large enough for all of them to exist in every population center, sure.

    That's never actually been presented as the case, even before getting into how a variety of factors tend to prompt monsters who weren't made in a town to move there or conduct the bulk of their supernatural activity there (as with mages and vampires most commonly), or how some of those monsters interface with other dimensions in a fashion specifically designed to limit their influence (werewolves and Sin-Eaters, naturally), or how most of these monsters have a practical and/or metaphysical inclination towards secrecy as regards their magical nature (demons and changelings), or the matter of simple low numbers (Prometheans and Beasts and possibly mummies).

    The base state of a monster is solitude, and that doesn't shift readily to diving headfirst into the politics between four different societies messing with five different dimensions without the kind of specialized focus you go on to allude to, which itself constitutes both a dedication of precious inhuman resources and a potential liability with regards to secrecy and subversion.

    It doesn't take an egregious amount of effort for one group of monsters to discover a different group of monsters exists, yes. It also doesn't take even a significant amount of contemplation for one group of monsters to determine that, absent a collaboration or conflict of interests with that other group, they want as little involvement with that group as they can manage. Two or three groups of thirty or so people can manage pretty well in a decent-sized city without running afoul of each other in the course of pursuing their own agendas.

            I've always found the arguments for each type of supernatural sticking to its own kind to be less than compelling; but normally, they're just good enough for me to accept in order to facilitate an overall setting where each gameline can operate in isolation. That is, they strike me as rationalizations for a pre-ordained status quo rather than something from which the traditional status quo naturally derives. So if your goal is to avoid crossovers, the arguments that Satchel makes rationalize the “each unto its own” conceit well enough.

            But this thread is about a recently announced book tentatively called Crossover Chronicle. The assumption here is that your goal is to embrace crossover; so the fig leaf excuses for why the city's vampires always seem to find each other but never seem to find the city's werewolves or mages? They're not needed here. Rather, the base assumption is that the city's supernatural community is aware of each other, and doesn't engage in willful blindness based on race (where the races are “vampire”, “werewolf”, “mage”, etc.). Instead, divisions are more likely to be based on interests, with a vampiric practitioner of Cruac being more likely to hang with a Thyrsus shaman than with a vampire who's pursuing the Coils of the Dragon, or a Gangrel finding as much common ground with an Uratha pack than with a Ventrue and his political intrigue. Which, on a larger scale, implies sociopolitical structures where race is at most a secondary concern rather than being the dominant organizing principle: a mystical organization inclined to facilitate messy, carnal magics, for instance; or Pack-like gangs that are as likely to have Gangrel vampires and Beast changelings as Uratha in their hunting parties.

            In particular, mages and their addiction to mysteries run directly counter to the notion of each race existing in isolation from the others. The fact that the other supernaturals don't count as Sleepers where Supernal Magic is concerned means that the single most common reason for mages to maintain secrecy (the effects of the Sleeping Curse) doesn't apply to other supernaturals; and the fact that mages can earn arcane experience through encounters with other supernaturals means that they're not inclined to ignore them.

            In fact, they arguably rival Beasts for the position of most crossover-friendly type in the Chronicles of Darkness; and Beasts were designed with crossover-friendliness in mind. So much so that I think one of the main challenges for Crossover Chronicle will be to avoid the perception that it's a Mage-centric book.

                        Agreed completely, especially on the Mage crossover point (though I think Deviant may rival them in crossover potential).

                        One of the biggest hurdles running crossover is maintaining a sense of disconnect, explaining how different night denizens can remain blind to one another. Some are easy, like Demons* or Prometheans, while others are seriously hard, namely Mages and Werewolves (both being naturally talented at sniffing out the strange). I've added things to help, like all Diamond orders having a standing rule that Mages are not to let other supernaturals become aware of their existence, but that still leaves the Free Councils, Nameless Orders and random disparates to pal about.

                        Even keeping the major supernatural splats low in numbers as I do, blurring of lines becomes inevitable. Some groups make this easy, the Ordo Dracul is a good example, being capable of embracing (no pun intended) many other splats in matched purpose. Others not so much, I found the Werewolf tribes especially difficult to manage (difficult enough to drop them entirely) what with their hunting focus and how certain supernaturals (cough Sin-Eaters cough) appear so much like prey.

                        My solution to all of it is to keep things to their own story. Don't let anything get to global. But that's not something the book can do, so I'll be interested in seeing how it's all justified. Though, frankly I think people would be happiest with a branching option approach. Maybe four or five takes on the setting each with different core assumptions. That'd probably be the most agreeable handling of the material.

                        *Demons are easy to keep hidden, what with Cover and all. And their espionage angle means its actually in theme for them to know more about other splats, and even infiltrate them.

                                    Global alliances as an option? Sure. As a default? Not in my opinion.

                                    That said, the various tribes can work with others in slightly more nuanced ways than the groups above.
                                    • Blood Talons - Willing to work with others to bring down the most powerful, troublesome werewolves.
                                    • Bone Shadows - Realise that hunting Hisil is more than just killing spirits. You need to work bigger changes on both sides of the Gauntlet. Other groups can have a big impact on this.
                                    • Hunters in Darkness - Territory is sacred. Some other creatures understand that, others can be educated. Still others need to be eradicated, and the more agreeable folks can help. Plus, supernatural residents of a territory can be considered part of that territory. The Hunters will defend them as vehemently as any other aspect.
                                      Iron Masters - Every other supernatural creature has an interest in humanity. So do the Iron Masters. Crossover ideas are plentiful.
                                    • Storm Lords - Whatever the game, the Storm Lords will have an opinion about it. With their need to hide away perceived weakness, once they're in something they'll represent the tribe to the best of their ability. And many other supernatural types will almost invariably (perhaps ignorantly) bring the Storm Lords into it.
