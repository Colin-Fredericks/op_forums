    Recruiting for an Online Beast: The Primordial Game

    09-14-2015, 08:34 PM
    Hello friends!

    My name is Jacob. I am starting a Beast: The Primordial chronicle over on roll20 and would like to see if anyone is interested. Be warned that my games tend towards being slightly surreal, not entirely serious, and more narrative than action-packed or dice heavy.

    What I have:
    • Experience Storytelling in two previous WOD Chronicles and playing in three others
    • Two players
    What I need:
    • An additional three to four people to participate in a predominately Beast game
    • A chance to explore this exciting new game
    Our first session will be devoted to the discussion of the game as a whole, decisions about the setting and crossover potential, and character creation.

    If you have played with me before (Alejandro? Patrik? I don't know how to contact you guys, but if you're still there, let me know) I'll be more than happy to have you.

    I am no stranger to teaching games so even if you've never played a game online, never actually played a WOD game, or even if you've never played any RPGs at all, I will happily accommodate you.

                      Thank you all for your interest!

                      I will now attempt to address each of your concerns.

                      Re: Timeslot
                      I will need to confer with my existing player, but as of now I don't have any real time commitments of my own, so the answer to this question is essentially: "whatever time I can get the most people together."

                      Re: Playstyle
                      My previous games tended towards investigation, exploration, and roleplaying more than combat or any other sort of action, but I intend to run a very character driven game, so action may arise in places where it makes sense. It will mostly be up to the players to seek action if you want it.

                      Re: Surrealism
                      You're right about surreal being a subjective term, I might have better said unsettling or bizarre, but those probably aren't any better. Imagine this instead:

                      You climb the creaking steps to the second floor of the derelict casino. Your shoes are damp from the constant rain outside. Your friends and loved ones sit in a loose circle at the top of the stairs. They moan quietly and rock back and forth. A robed creature with a deer's antlers, a llama's face, an old man's gnarled hands, and a second, human mouth behind its gaping ungulate lips sits in the center of the circle. His first mouth screams, and his second mouth howls words of dire prophecy. His name is Jerry.

                      That legitimately happened in my last mage game. That should give you an idea of the level I usually reach.

                      Re: Online play
                      Roll20 is very easy to use and very easy to teach. With our help, you'll get up to speed in no time.

                      Re: Setting and Character Niches
                      I believe character creation should be at least partially a communal activity. For exactly the reason you pointed out. Nobody's toes will get stepped on if everyone's on the same page to begin with. As for the setting, the Beast book's storytelling section suggests waiting to nail down details until the characters are created, so you end up in a reasonable location for everyone's character concepts. We'll discuss that in the first session.

                      Re: New to Wod
                      We had a few players who were new to Wod last time around, and with this being a new game line, I think we'll all be on fairly even footing anyway. It should be no trouble at all to get you acclimated to the game.

                      @WarDragon, Xenowrath, FallenEco, Draca: If you are still interested, knowing all this, you may post your availability here in the forum so we can start narrowing it down, or you can send me a private message.

                      Please feel free to ask any questions you still need answered!
