    It seems to me as though Duel Arcane massively favours either the person with more Doors or, if they're both equal, the one who goes first. Being on the defensive about it feels like it can only ever drag the process out; you're forgoing the opportunity to progress towards victory in exchange for the opponent wasting your action with an Arcanum unlikely to work anyway or losing their best and possibly being stuck on one Door for a while. How many steps does it take to get an insurmountable lead? Or I guess in a case such as that you can try defending to stall them down to their worst Arcanum and then batter them to catch up. But that also is just dragging things out.

    It kind of makes me wonder if the optimal strategy is to not just defend with your best dice pool until the opponent has used up all of their Arcana and then just spend the next seven or so rounds breaking them down while refusing their offers and leaving them impotent. I guess that would be a reason for Apprentices to not challenge Masters.

    Although I guess that's a strategy that only needs to slip once, bad luck on your part or good on your opponent's, and you've ceded them the advantage of being one step ahead that they can ride to the end unless you try again to make a Door impregnable. But what if that slips up again and you're two Doors behind? Although, how else can you win if they can afford to lose Doors because a back and forth will get them to the end first?

    I suppose it can help to see it as practically more of an "I know you know I know" kind of battle of wits, although I find myself wondering if this is less character decisions and more gaming the system, and even then the aforementioned options don't quite seem sophisticated.

    I suppose the rewards for victory are valuable enough to encourage wading in occasionally, but I still wish it would have been a bit more elaborate and interesting if it was going to still be there.

                            Originally posted by Epimetheus View Post
                            Duel Arcane never really made sense to me in 2e simply because in actual play an apprentice with dominate mind can completely annihilate any master who doesn't have an absurd withstand or mind/prime protection. Personally, I prefer the alternate dual arcane that was proposed in one first edition book whether the contestants had to do a series of challenges rather than straight up fight.
                            1.) You don't actually apply any spell effects in the Duel Arcane. Which Arcana you have doesn't matter beyond ratings.

                            2.) The Duel Arcane is a comparatively civil conflict resolution decided upon by both parties. Debuffing the opponent before the Duel begins is a surefire way to make sure there's no Duel Arcane for you to attend.
                            Basically, if your preparations for a duel consists of breaking your opponent's weapon arm, there's no chance in hell the duel will actually commence.

                            3.) You need to be at least a Disciple of Mind in order to affect any stats relevant to the Duel Arcane.

                                                    Originally posted by Isator Levi View Post
                                                    How are people inclined to handle improvised casting of spells that might be regarded as more esoteric or non-intuitive?
                                                    To quote myself from a Discord discussion two years back, it's probably prudent to operate under the assumption that supernatural power development needs some kernel of experiential focus rather than simple imagination and smushing concepts together.

                                                    Mage is a game where gnostic insight and initiation into the Mysteries is the basis for your magic working and the vast majority of experienced mages can be expected to have permanently altered the shape of their soul-vessels to better harmonize with one specialized view of magic or another — research and/or comprehensive Scrutiny would be the bare minimum I'd expect for a mage to reasonably start considering throwing together a spell to handle a weird phenomenon they had no exposure to a week ago.
