                                Originally posted by Ephsy View Post
                                What makes lack of higher thought more verosimil than actually noticing a vampire under the clause of it calling your attention like it would with loudly stepping on a floor behind you? Not much.
                                Because Obfuscate works by making observers ignore the sensory information they are receiving. It doesn't matter if you're receiving more detailed sensory information, Obfuscate still makes you ignore it. The vampire calling attention to itself breaks Obfuscate because if you're paying attention to the vampire you're not ignoring it any more. Also, stepping loudly is not an example of calling attention to yourself. The example is pulling a gun in a crowded place.

                                Originally posted by Ephsy View Post
                                What makes lack of higher thought somehow extrasensory, as you've put it? Nothing.
                                Right, nothing. Extrasensory perception is not the reason animals can see through Obfuscate in The Blood (which, again, was demonstrably wrong in several places, even in 1e). It says that animals (and mentally ill people) can see through it not because their senses are more powerful, but because their brains are too simple for Obfuscate to fool. Extrasensory perception allows you to pierce Obfuscate because you're perceiving the vampire with something other than the 5 senses, something that Obfuscate doesn't make you ignore. Two completely separate things.

                                And, if all this still doesn't convince you, Requiem 2e pretty directly states that Obfuscate works on werewolves.

                                Originally posted by page 137
                                Obfuscate tricks the victim's mind directly, removing traces of the vampire from the sensory information reaching her brain, rather than fooling her senses directly. Obfuscate affects smell and taste as much as sight or hearing - potentially useful if you've angered a pack of werewolves, or a vampire with sharp Kindred senses.
