    Days After the First Change Chronicle

    09-19-2014, 02:54 PM
    All of the WoD games start with a mindset of "You're established and Made, albeit newbs." At some point, your character had to have chosen a Tribe or to be Ghost Wolf, maybe even a Lodge. At some point they were probably approached by packs to join, or they did the approaching. Or they were In the Know already and they've been waiting their whole lives.

    The point is, a lot happened to our characters before the game started. And a big part of that was learning about Uratha societies, Spirits, the Oath of the Moon, Father/Mother Wolf and Father/Mother Luna, the Pure, shartha, and perhaps even caught themselves a totem. Not to mention all that jargon.

    My players are earnest, but once I start running my game after 2E comes out, this will be their first exposure to Werewolf, and only their second exposure to World of Darkness. So I feel the need to do a lot of this "Learn how to walk" stuff in-character during their first Chronicle.

    I'm planning a tightly-focused tour of Forsaken involving a grouchy Mentor, a waning Pure population (in response to the players' actions during our Vampire 2E game a decade earlier), and a bunch of open land next to the Forsaken territory. The hisil will be all kinds of screwed up because of the Pure's domination of the area for several decades, plus the aforementioned vampire problems (think of all of the Gluttony, Lust, and Murder spirits created from a vampire population explosion).

    What methods have people found work for an introductory game to werewolf? I'll be borrowing liberally from Manitou Springs already, possibly even with a cameo by Max Roman.

    "Nihhina kalekal-zidu kal masun, kal manudanadu. Nihhina kalekal-zidu nukal shaghu-desasudu — nihhina kalekal-zidu kal innu-desasudu udhkal samm." Arthur Ashe
    She/her, contributing writer for Scion: Pride 2021

      Step one...............no, wait, I'm not that organized...........try some of this stuff, see what works...........

      Ride along: They have a mentor already, mentor has a pack? They go along with the pack on a Hunt, told to stay back, maybe armed, maybe just left with the keys to the car and told to run. They get to witness, first hand, an interaction between the pack and a spirit. Make it anything you want - hunting & ripping it up, summoning & binding, bribing it for a Gift, whatever. The important part is the descriptions of the spirit - how it looks, how it moves, how is looks alien but does not feel that way to them, the way it seems to blend into the environment (or seems to completely clash with it if that's why they're hunting it down), and how it reacts to the pack. Run in fear or turn & fight? Try to strong arm them when negotiating for information? Is this the pack's Totem? Very different circumstances if so.

      NPCs: Don't stop at just a few. Have a bunch of NPCs for them to see. Show different perspectives, different combinations of Auspice and Tribe, different personalities. Yes, you are going to have to become a cast of thousands, but it gives them an idea as to what to expect and what they can decide to be. After a while, your players might show interest in just a few, maybe even wanting THEM as a mentor individually. You can drop some of the others once the players start to decide who they like.

      Random encounters: Spirits are everywhere. The Pure may be waning, but they're still out there. Vampire population explosions! Show how the rest of the WOD looks at werewolves in general, and their little group specifically.

      Lore of the Forsaken, The Rage, Predators, Blood of the Wolf. Get these books, combined they are Uratha 101. Lore of the Forsaken has a lot of good info on the spirit magic of the Uratha (Gifts & Rites). Not just lists of them, but descriptions of how they look, how they change an Uratha, what they mean to them. The Rage is great for what is means to be member of an Auspice and/or a Tribe. What it's like to have the attention of Luna and the Firstborns. For even more expanded materials along those lines, get Tribes of the Moon and Signs of the Moon, though The Rage will get you started. Predators is your "monster manual". All different Ranks and phylum of spirits, how they look, what they do, how they might interact with the Uratha. Blood of the Wolf is all the nitty gritty about physically being a werewolf. How shape-shifting feels, what happens when an Uratha is trying to NOT heal too fast and give themselves away, senses, hunting, etc.

              End running this kind of chronicle arc with Tribal Initation myself - about month after First Change - I can give you some ideas.

              1. Let them play "humans" - before first change characters are totally mortals. They may get some wierd visions of time before humanity. They could have strong anger issues. They could be entwine in the some spirits machinations. But they are basiclly humans. Or no - from story perspective they all will better work for those couple of days as more new Wolf Blooded - someone is spirits magnet. Other has life destruction anger issues. One of the have dreams about what he is wolf hunting in nearby woods in the night. They don't need a template for it ( they will get the proper Uratha one in the end, right?) but those Tells are instant pre-First Change stories, preludes to the life of werewolf.

              Only in the time of great stress under they Auspice the can go with proper First Change ( but they could also omit they phase in this run ). Each Auspice have three consequent enought twice in the moon month - this about one week priod is enought for most characters. You only need to decide with players what Auspice they want to play.

              2. They need teacher(s) - PCs need at least one Mentor figure, more could be interesting idea. In my games they got ex-Bale Hound Irraka that become lover to our pack alpha. She isn't part of the pack itself, and has her secret motivation, but is also great way to make player accustomed to setting.

              3. They need territory - Forsaken is very "grounded" game, and by running it, you make a very detailed local setting - more even than in Requiem or Awakening. Make them look for they own Locus ( or create one ), to hunt for Totem or to mark a boundary of they terrain. By those ordeals, a new pack will rise, and PCs will generate they own hierarchy, with pack leading alpha.

              4. They need a threat - games chronicle is not really engaging until you make a conflicts. And werewolves strife to physcial confrontations. Personally, I don't like Pure - they are more bestial Seers of Throne - for me, more interesting are Bale Hounds, spirts and Claimed. Choose one as main atnagonists, and make a few smaller challenges - you got yourself material for all game.

              5. They need a heard - you need to make couple of normal, mortal characters to confront them with savage nature of werewolves. Or make them people they need to defend to. With Lunacy and Death Rage they will nicely show players that they are playing monsters.

              Hopes this helps!

                  The concept of playing a prelude before adding a template has been around for a long time. A Werewolf prelude is a bit risky though since the First Change for each character has to happen during specific phases of the moon based off Auspice. Players may get bored if you sell them on a Werewolf game and then ask them to play weeks or months worth of sessions before they can do the "cool stuff". There's also a chance that wolf-blooded players might feel upstaged by or resentful of the players that have already changed. You'll have to be careful that you're not spending so much time and effort mentoring the new werewolves that the wolf-blooded are left out. Without knowing the players, I can't say if that will be an issue.

                  One possible compromise is to have the first session focused on the First Change of the last player to change. That way the Mentor character only has to give the crash course once and you don't run the risk of either spacing the Changes out so much that players get bored/resentful or spacing them so close together that it feels like watching the same pilot for a TV show five times in a row before you're allowed to watch the second episode (or perhaps, more aptly, replaying the same tutorial five times before you can play a game). Bonus points if the supernatural threat of the week is uncovered by or directed at the wolf-blooded or if their First Change somehow turns the tide at a critical moment (Rahu going berserk on his captors, Cahalith getting a vision of a spirit's bane, etc.)

                  It's also possible to start the Chronicle with the players as stray wolves if you want them to have (most of) their cool powers off the bat, but want an excuse for them to not really know about Forsaken society.

                          Yeah, I definitely wasn't going to make them do their preludes first. I like doing one character's First Change as the beginning of the Chronicle, combining it with the Ride Along scenario from Papa Bear.

                          I really appreciate all of the responses, though I have another question.

                          When I'm having my players conceptualize their characters, I'll need to make sure they understand the kind of game Forsaken is. I have something of a spiel ready, and want to run it by y'all:

                          "The werewolves of Forsaken are not Hollywood style werewolves. They are almost always in full control of their faculties, and possess five forms rather than just two. It's still horrific to be a werewolf, because there is a rage boiling under your skin at all times, much like the Beast from Vampire. You also weren't bit to become a werewolf, and you won't spread werewolfism by biting people: You were always a werewolf, though you might not have known until your First Change. The physiological changes notwithstanding, you also have enemies that hate you just for being what you are. I'm not talking about werewolf hunters, I'm talking about Spirits and The Pure.

                          "We'll get to them later though. The First Season Chronicle will be about your characters adjusting to your new lives. I want to introduce the concepts of werewolf to you a bit at a time, so you'll be able to learn in-character while you're learning out-of-character. This Chronicle's themes will be all about learning what kind of fucked up world you live in now and why you can't do anything about it. But it's okay, because you can turn into a wolf monster when things start to get hairy."

                          The last line is the most important, because it sums up why I like werewolf: Things suck, but at least you're an awesome wolf monster.
