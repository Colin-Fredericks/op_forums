                              So, ignoring a lot of the side commentary, and going at it as a broad idea, and particularly in a crossover context, I wouldn't strictly say no to allowing a werewolf to gain access to a single Arcana that rise with Primal Urge starting at 6.

                              Buuuuuuuuuuuut if we we wanna talk about why it's infeasible, we should probably address the reasons why it's "Probably not."

                              Reason 1) THe Pangaeans were just on a whole 'nother scale.

                              One of the problems of Second Edition having it's lists of scales be so prominent, and in particular having four of it's most frequent entities sharing one scale in particular, is that it can be tempting to think that a lot of things are just comparable to each other when that's strictly not the case. A Rank 5 Pangaean* makes other Rank 5 Ephemeral entities look like chumps, and Pangaeans don't go lower than 3. Werewolves are beasts in their own right, god-killing demigods all of them, but the scale between them and where Wolf was at (particularly derived from understanding their peers, Bird and Snake) is vast enough to make one wonder.

                              Reason 2) The metaphysical makeup works against them.

                              One thing about the Pangaeans that is of a side note is that, in addition to walking the entire breadth of Flesh, Border Marches, and Shadow (well, would walk if it weren't for Wolf, and often in spite of them), Pangaeans also could dive into and connect with the Astral-and while there's no direct metaphysic that says the Astral is needed for employing the Supernal, it's worth noting that when the Arcana are expressed as Arcana, it's often throguh entitites who have connection to the Astral-it's a relationship we don't really see through the Shadow, Hedge, or Underworld through all but the wibbliest (and often questionably canon, given the state of second edition) of ways. Now, the Uratha aren't divorced from the Astral, the Cahalith regularly take advantage of the Dreaming Earth and Mother's Land with their dream abilities, and certain other gifts and Lodges breach it-but it's a strained relationship that implies that it would take more than just raw might of Primal Urge to do, you'd have to deliberately dig and change to really make that happen, partly because

                              Reason 3) Werewolves are only part Pangaean.

                              So on top of that gap between werewolves and Pangaeans, werewolves aren't commonly asserted to be pure Pangaean. The origin story they have implies that they're more a hybrid of Wolf and Humanity, with design input from Luna. So that adds a layer of divide all the more. But wait, wouldn't that added bit of humanity possibly mean they'd still have some kind of ability to tap into the Supernal? No, not really, since it possibly introduces a vector for the Lie to work on werewolves ala that shard of the abyss (Not enough to not make them sleepwalkers, but enough to be a barrier) and the access to the Supernal is implied to be an aspect of nature of the Pangaeans that this world simply doesn't have, and werewolves probably didn't have even at the time.

                              There are probably other reasonable reasons to not really think of it like that.

                              I dunno, I feel like if it were a really viable option, it'd probably have been mentioned in the Era-it's a pretty short enough mechanic to introduce so long as you cheat, and while most people don't play at the end of the power pool, it certainly would be more viable there than anywhere else. My go-to is, and remains, asserting that werewolves on the upper end of the Primal Urge tend to take on the Lone Wolf Template from the Chronicler's Guide-but I could see some games where this'd be a fun way to go about it to, and I wouldn't even bat at an eye at an obscure Lodge trying to explore the idea with limited results. But on the general vibe of the thing, I'd have to lean towards Nah.

                                  I mentioned "weird and unique circumstances" earlier. I think it's far more likely (if extremely rare to the point where it might not have happened at all even if it's possible) that a werewolf would gain access to an Arcanum through their general mutability (especially Ghost Wolves) as well as their connection to the Anima Mundi. A werewolf who learns to dream lucidly and to explore Astral Space could possibly encounter the Aeons, and (potentially by forming a lodge around one) pick up part of their nature and Resonances into her own. Not to mention that the Aeons can grant use of spells from their associated Arcanum to mages. Who's to say they can't do it with non-mages?

                                  Or, as I realise right now, it's also possible to just have a lesser Supernal entity for a pack or lodge totem without having to navigate the far reaches of the Astral. Totems do affect those who are bound to them, though normally not to the point where they grant powers associated with other splats.

                                                      It's deliberately obscure.

                                                      The idea of the "Bodhisattva Predator" is not a scalable thing, particularly since "bodhisattva predator" is legitimately not a game term, but instead a very illustrative term that people have grasped onto to as an icon to ask answers for-and-from. The phrase is...............not on the same scale as the way the God-Machine was demanded prior to 2nd Edition and Demon, but it's about comparably below on that scale. It's a thing the fandom latched onto, is what I'm saying.

                                                      There have been teases that there might be more to the idea of an enlightened threshold-dwelling hunter-leader-liberator than simply references, but there is a lot of irony in the statement already, particularly since part of the reason Uratha elders aren't given the same space as vampire Elders or Mage Masters (let along archmasters) is because most Uratha tend to die long before that point, and that the business of being a werewolf hews pragmatic tends to mean they don't often get caught in the esoterica of reality. In the same way most werewolves would go "huh, neat" and then go back to business with minimal adjustmnent on learning they used to rule a city in Sumer or all the details of the Sundered World, "Enlightenment", whatever that means and looks like for werewolves, is not usually a thing of interest to most of them. As always, though, there's probably a lodge that takes it deathly serious.

                                                      Anyhow, I mentioned before that when I get into higher power werewolf characters, I tend to make them Lone Wolves from the Chronicler's Guide, and that's because that's what I mostly imagine an escalated form of werewolves would look like-able to do the work of a whole pack on their own, totem and head of a spirit court in their own right, hyper powerful Auspices, stuff like that feels right.
