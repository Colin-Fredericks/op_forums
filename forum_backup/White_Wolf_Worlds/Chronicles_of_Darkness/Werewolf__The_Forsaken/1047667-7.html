            Originally posted by Iry View Post
            Oh certainly. The incentives to take Sacred Hunt are so impressive, and so great, that it would be almost silly not to take it. Which essentially transforms the option into a nearly mandatory tax for... at least one person in the pack. Not actually mandatory, of course, but almost unheard of for nobody to take it. Somewhat similar to the way that certain feats in D&D became nearly mandatory, because of the way that they were so beneficial almost everyone took them. Or any other game where one option gives a considerably larger benefit than many other options.
            The difference, at least in Feats, is that the rite is offered freely at character creation, and can be bought at any time later. It can even be used through a mentor, though he might tire of using the rite on your pack. It's something they can seek out in-game without anyone having to write 'Sacred Hunt' in their list of rites. Rites are not something you can get every other level and max out at a certain point and can never get another again. The Sacred Hunt is always potentially available.

            I think you have created a red herring here. Having Sacred Hunt be free only takes away player choice in the same way that being able to shapeshift takes away player choice, or naturally healing bashing damage takes away player choice, or playing a werewolf or wolfblooded in general takes away player choice. Which is to say, you are choosing to play a particular splat -- with it comes a series of benefits and complications. Among those are X, and you are free to negotiate with your storyteller to have less than X if you can come up with a compelling reason for it.
            Having Sacred Hunt be free and forced eliminates any option of having the character who just doesn't know. Who has not been exposed to the greater society of werewolves and does not know what they are. Who is Forsaken. And the setting has that as a prominent option.

            Instead, it's offered with free rite dots. Take it, or don't. You can have an oblivious, lost character, a character sure in their ability to get access to it in the world, or a character who can fend for themselves.

            I mean, this is the entire point of the toolbox approach. You can adjust your games accordingly, as people in this forum have. But it offers a playstyle with more options that fit the designed setting, and lets you subtract or add from there.

                        Originally posted by Iry View Post
                        I'm just certain (IMHO, once again) that giving it to everyone adds more options, instead of less options.
                        Here's a notion: Do what Beast does with the starting Lair Chamber.

                        From one perspective, a starting Beast character can choose to begin the game without having established one of their Lair's two Chambers (the second being the universally-required Heart) and gain two free Experiences instead.

                        From another perspective, a starting Beast character begins the game with two free Experiences, which they may spend at character creation to have already established one of their Lair's Chambers outside the Heart.

                        By a similar token:

                        From one perspective, a starting Werewolf character begins the game with two free dots of Rites (in addition to any they spend their starting Merit dots on), which they may spend at character creation to already know the Sacred Hunt ritual.

                        From another perspective, a starting Werewolf character can choose to begin the game without having personally learned the underlying symbols of the Sacred Hunt ritual and gain two free dots of Rites instead.

                        It's a matter of framing.

                                            I agree with what you are saying in a general sense, but this is not one of those situations. You don't technically need storyteller permission for your character to be 'ignorant' of something that doesn't force you to use it. This is not even something particularly radical like "I do my best to avoid ever using Urhan". You could go an entire game never drawing attention to the fact that your character has no idea how to do the Sacred Hunt, the same way you could go an entire game never drawing attention to the fact that your character has no idea how to read.

                                            But we're not here to hide things from the storyteller, that was just being technical. Asking your storyteller if you can play a character who is ignorant of her heritage (and abstaining from activities like the Sacred Hunt on a player level) is no more or less likely to be rejected by your storyteller than someone who wants to play a foreigner, someone missing an eye, someone who wants to play a ghost wolf, or any other straightforward almost always approved thing (as long as you are not being disruptive at the table, as is the case with anything).

                                            Of course, there will always be a few storytellers who have very specific visions for their stories and might say no. But those storytellers could just as easily ask that nobody play a Blood Talon, that everyone be raised as a child soldier, or any number of other things that technically narrow your choices down but contribute to telling a hopefully interesting story. Not ever character we dream up fits perfectly into every game, and that's okay too (as long as everyone is having fun and knew about it up front).
