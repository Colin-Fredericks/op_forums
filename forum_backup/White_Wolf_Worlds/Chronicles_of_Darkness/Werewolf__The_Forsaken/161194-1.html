    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
    I have very obvious question, but that is crucial to this sticking Tribes with they favorite Prey – what is if I don’t want to use specific kind of threat in chronicle? For example, I now run game based on Bale Hounds and Malejin threat. I have little, secluded town in far reaches of Sweden. Using spoilers of Idigam Chronicles as written, sure, I will have Blood Talons for Bale Hounds, Storm Lords for Spirit-Ridden taken by Malinets demons, and Iron Masters for fighting with humans cult that worship Malejin. But this little town on the outskirts of civilization is also great place for Hunters in Darkness and I don’t see a place for them in chronicle structure and I don’t want to push them on force in to story. What to do in this case? Adding next threat in the overcrowded game isn’t solution for this.
    I think a lot of people are taking the favored prey thing to be a bit more significant than it actually is. Like Satchel said, it's not a fixation, it's a specialty. Just because there are no Hosts around doesn't mean the Hunters in Darkness have nothing to do but sit on their hands and wait for some Azlu to show up. A Tribe considers it's favored prey to be the most dangerous, and therefore the most worthy. But just because hamburgers are my favored lunch and I consider them to be the tastiest, and therefore most worthy does not mean I won't eat a salad from time to time. Likewise, a Hunter in Darkness would still be perfectly willing and able to help the pack fight a Bale Hound threat. In fact, they would have a unique perspective on that threat not shared by members of other Tribes who have different priorities in terms of prey.

    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
    Also, were is gender swapping on Father/Mother Wolf and Mother Luna/ Father Moon now? It’s a bit crucial in mythological part of my Sweden game, as I imagined that local Uratha think they are children of Angerboda, Mother of Wolves and Mani, Father Moon. It has sense, isn't it?
    Gender pronouns are fairly meaningless to spirits, especially ones as powerful and abstract as Urfarah and Luna. I'm not familiar with the Angerboda or Mani you're referring to, but I assume they are Sweedish mythological figures that parallel Luna and Urfarah? That's a great example of the reason for the new ambiguous approach. There are probably a great deal of mythological figures from around the world that bare similar resemblance to them, and there are undoubtedly some that are male, some that are female, and some that are both or neither. In the World of Darkness, those myths were most likely influenced by the spirits themselves, and probably influenced the spirits in turn. So sometimes you'll see Luna referred to as Mother, and other times as Father, and likewise for Urfarah.

    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
    And last but not least - with all write-up of WInter Wolf, there is a lot more sense of my little town to be in everlasting ( for time of chronicle ) grip of snow. Had Skolis-Ur appeared in the area to test young Wolf-Blooded?
    That is super cool!

    Going by Willow now, or Wil for short. She/Her/Hers.

                            If you want my opinion on Rites, I enjoyed them, but hated Rite of Dedication with a passion bordering on insanity. I felt that anytime you had a ritual that played fast and loose with the rules in addition to being almost 100% mandatory for any Werewolf regardless of playstyle, then you have an issue. The primary issue was that this ritual alone let Werewolves take items to the Shadow which was extremely powerful in addition to being the only ritual that let Werewolves not become naked every 20 minutes. However, all of that pales in comparison to the built in mechanic to bypass the Primal Urge limit through exceptional successes and the ability to dedicate an entire bag worth of items for the same cost. Why would I dedicate a gun when I could dedicate an entire bag worth of guns for the same price? That, and if I played the system long enough, I can gain an exceptional success and dedicate even more issues.

                            Long story short? I like rites, but that ritual needs to go or be heavily reworked.

                                    My favourite tribe out of the five, and I feel its better than ever. What originallt drove me to like the Storm Lords, when they at first seemed like a Silver Fang and Shadow Lord combo, was the Tribe's of the Moon description of 'defiance'. It's not just that they will not stop their hunt, but also that they do not want to give their enemy the pleasure of killing them in its own terms.

                                    I like the way they test each other, and the cultural note (always important, in my opinion) on those who live in warmer climates reaching for Skolis-Ur. The meeting to see each other's weakness as well really works.

                                    But most I like the visible cultural shift. Now, the Storm Lords aren't the tribe who lead because of Skolis-Ur's mistake. They lead, because they are often the most toughest bastards around and it impresses other uratha. That, and the fact that they themselves don't see themselves as the leader tribe, just trying to fill a role well.

                                    The replacing Father Wolf part is also nice, the spirit is dead, and he fell to Weakness. So it is up to the Storm tribe to fill his shoes and do his role. I feel this changes things in an interesting way, as the angst factor is gone and duty is more present.

                                    As for the hints of the Pure, the nickname is interesting and I wonder if it refers to 'bringing the winter' as in the story provided. But more than that, in older Forsaken stories, the Pure first-born were included(If I remember right). Now, it seems only the Forsaken are involved. Might be that the Forsaken are excising the Pure from their mythos on purpose. Or might be the role of the Pure has changed?

                                    My gallery.
