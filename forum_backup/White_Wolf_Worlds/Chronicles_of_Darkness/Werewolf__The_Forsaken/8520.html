    Werewolf: the Forsaken Instincts; the early incarnation of an overhaul.

    11-11-2013, 09:32 PM
    Some of y'all were curious what I was rambling on about with the Instincts we were working on between myself and my co-ST for the game we've been running some years. So far as the first draft this is what we've came up with and are deciding to use/playtest to see how they pan out; they're not perfect, and no doubt they'll need further refinement. This is derived from watching things play out, player input and ongoing tweaks and house rules and finding the light approach just wasn't enough for what we wanted in our game. This is the (tentative) results of that, one part at least given the numerous other areas we're tackling with some player input. Luckily they're used to ongoing tweaks; the game has been rolling for years, so we've had to dig into things as they've came up.

    It should be noted these are predicated on quite a few changes to the game.

    Renown is being reworked into a Status mechanic for Tribes and Auspices and has no impact on power caps, nor Instinct dice rolls. It doesn't impact overall honorary spirit rank; that's being dovetailed into Primal Urge. It -does- have some supernatural impacts to the character (which will be discussed later when we've come to more of a consensus) but this is a big paradigm shift; it won't be touching Gifts. Gifts right now is the term used for when a spirit part of a Totem/Spirit Pact grants the individual minor blessings. There is no cap on Instincts.

    Essence is -not- gained from Loci; they're certainly areas where resonant essence spills most freely into the Shadow, but there are no more "werewolf loci farming". They're areas that, like a body of water in the woods still attract ample prey; spirits, and in turn they're still considered prime areas to have as territory and to keep an eye out for trouble. That said, you get your essence for hunting and feasting; spirits, humans, wolves, werewolves, and rarely much else. None for your Auspice. No Essence batteries for the half-fleshed. They're hunters, so they hunt for their fuel stat.

    Spirit Numina replace gifts. They're not part of "sets" rather than individually bought, though we're thinking a Werewolf may be able to gain equivalents by incorporating/learning a portion of another's Influence. Trophies, scars and other supernaturally altering bits from Werewolves successfully hunting down spirits (and in some cases other werewolves and spiritual critters).

    It's possible to trick or barter for them but that's usually the exception for Forsaken; they're not liked by the Shadow and to have your Essence and Resonance altered in that way to make your spiritual side manifest a Numina by a spirit outside of hunting means, you guessed it, you may be changing more than you bargained for taking some of the concepts presented in the Chronicler's Guide. They're still in the game, they're just not the default power set (though most characters will have the Materialize numina save as a means to dematerialize and pass through the gauntlet). Obviously many won't translate directly, given the half-fleshed werewolves nature, and some might just be outside their ability to learn without other events in place. Still, they're not the primary nor relied about sets. We'll be presenting some ideas and systems for this later on.

    Rites are still around. However, they will no longer be a random grab-bag of powers that didn't fit within a Gift set or the absolute mess they were before. They're not going be using Harmony as the primary way to perform them mechanically. They're going to reflect more finesse based Essence and Resonance manipulation and weeded out and tweaked to reflect that. Rites are the funky spirit arts of Werewolves that can use the fundaments the Shadow is built on for some of the funky effects they had before. Once we have more functionally fleshed out we'll share these notions, but it's still a system being discussed. Instincts more readily reflect the Influence of a werewolf. Their base "Numina" to look at them at a spiritual POV is their innate powers; shapeshifting, regeneration, etc. Purely social rites will not require a purchase under this system; they become cultural traditions of the various Tribes.

    Dalu and Urshul are not accessible by default without further Shapeshifting Instincts; most will consider this a debuff. Once again, there's some reasons for this we'll explain in later documents once we start putting them together with Instincts. Dalu gets some minor changes. This is definitely something specific to our game; I doubt we'd see this alteration in Idigam Chronicles.

    The basic structure works like this; each Instinct has a passive effect that, as it is raised increases a facet of being a werewolf. Getting better at pursuing prey; more time in Gauru; easier time shapeshifting under duress and so on. Each level (1-5, consider them "Honings") starts small, subtle, and then increases in magnitude.

    Many are Reflexive and require no rolls; Instincts are primarily quick and responsive or capitalizing on already present facets of being a Werewolf. Some do require rolls, but most that bolster oneself simply cost Essence and in some instances Willpower. This is intentional; myself and my co-ST don't want to arbitrarily toss down Dice rolls for their powers without need unless the power specifically targets someone beyond the werewolf (and therefore resisted or contested), or would better served with a success basis.

    1-3 are primarily internalized unless an instinct predicated on affecting others like Dominance, usually bolstered by the passive bonus. They're the major portion of the Instincts; 4-5's are just gravy, 1-3 is your reliable tool-set and should cover all the major bases of the Instincts theme. You shouldn't be racing to get the most utility running for 4-5s.

    4 is where you start to see further refinement of the existing themes into a stronger effect or unexpected permutations of an Instinct, and 5 is generally where one sees a "What the hell, I didn't know they could do that?" sort of effect. Sometimes this simply changes what lower Honings are capable of; other times it shows one inflicting their Instinct onto their environment or other entities in frightening ways. We took quite a bit of nod from the ideas presented in Blood and Smoke regarding progression and aggregate effects.

    We assume that there may be additional Instincts out there (particularly among the Pure), and that there may be individual Honings that branch out from the standard 1-5 (think more like Promethean Transmutations, or Single Level Devotions) but right now we wanted the bread and butter covered. Right now, they don't rely on spirits being part of the game focus; they work on any viable target provided they're in the same state/plane of existence. You can use Dominance as readily upon a human as you can a Changeling as you can a Spirit. I'd say overall they are more potent than many of the prior Gifts overall, less so in a few instances, though not as broken as quite a few were (not to say they couldn't turn out to be broken in brand new ways mind you). They need cleaning up, as well as later expansion, both to address The Pure and additional diversity. They definitely need tweaking in a lot of areas and some have already been rewritten, no doubt with more Honings requiring that.

    Every werewolf will have Feral Instincts and Shapeshifting Instincts as an affinity; each Tribe has a particular Instinct they tend to focus on and learn more readily that helps bolster their practices and assumptions. The Blood Talons treat Rage as affinity. Bone Shadows treat Sensory as theirs. Hunters in Darkness treat Territoriality as their big thing. Iron Masters treat Stealth as theirs and Storm Lords emphasize Dominance. Others may learn these Instincts as non-affinity costs. Right now for the quick and dirty until their individual Instinct is done, the Ivory Claws treat Dominance as theirs, and the Predator Kings treat Territoriality as affinity and the Fire-Touched Rage (odd choices I know, but there are reasons for it we'll get into later). Odds are with these you'll see some funky ideas like Rabid Instincts, Purity Instincts and Savagery Instincts with some nods to their existing sets; more likely than not these are just complete sets of "alternate honings" from the base one that they developed an affinity over time over the "originals". We originally debated a Passive effect to each individual level; this proved far too unwieldy thoguh.

    Obviously we wouldn't mind input on these, how to clean them up, tweak them or otherwise change them with better options. We have our own player input, but the more ideas the better to getting the final form to gel. That said; don't waste our time with "you shouldn't use these at all. Go back to the old core W:tF setup." type of comments please; it's not happening, and not constructive to what we're trying to do.

    The first I'll present for you folks is Feral Instincts; it's based around the Father Wolf Gift set and primarily addresses a lot of innate physical/feral nuances of werewolves.

      Right now Feral Instincts is very internalized; unlike quite a few of the others made it's level 5 does not start to outwardly affect one's environment or others overtly, save in how readily they can be hunted and consumed for Essence. It's subtler in that fashion comparatively; that said it does make for Shadow and material world survival challenges to find food and keep supernaturally empowered much easier than others; we'd imagine quite a few very high Primal Urge werewolves go for the pinnacle of it to have easy times surviving in both worlds with less actual problems. Hunting less, eating less, fewer issues with oddball Resonance as often and so on.

      Dealing Aggravated Damage -currently- is omitted from the power sets until we see enough precedent otherwise; you can inflict Lethal on individuals who'd normally downgrade that type of damage from claws and fangs with Savage Rending now, along with some other bonuses. We're strictly leaving Aggravated Damage to 'wrap-arounds' and the actual supernatural banes that inflict such; there won't be many powers that specifically do that as a result. In most instances it will be Numina that may allow for such in appropriate instances when it comes to fire and other banes that inflict Aggravated on the appropriate supernatural; silver however, is right out. It's being treated spirit-wise as far more predisposed against werewolves than many spirits that already have an abject distaste and fear of them. It's debatable if a werewolf could even properly consume a spirit of silver to gain it's Numina or a distilled effect of an Influence of it; its entire resonance is likely entirely incompatible but there may be ways in the form of plot hooks. I just don't want it a default assumption; it's bane, their one innate one; it shouldn't have any sort of easy use or pass on a supernatural level.

          Shapeshifting Instincts currently uses much of Mother Luna Gift Listings. As mentioned in the initial post one house-rule that's in place is that Dalu and Urshul are -not- part of the default werewolf package, so keep this in mind. Primal Form got switched to Level 4; it's now more versatile (though needs some tweaking) and allows you to push your alternate forms past their usual shapeshifting limits, creating customized forms.

          We're still a bit iffy on how to reflect the bonuses of Skin Stealing; scent transferred before, but right now that's part of Stealth; there may still be bonuses towards that worth adding in, but the idea is that for wolves and werewolves scent is a ridiculously important sense to determine individuality and identity; things that play around with that should specifically fall in line with Stealth, not shapeshifting alone. This could be changed, of course.

          The level 5 is something I wanted to see back in the werewolf power sets for a long time; you had vampire powers that played around with the embrace with certain bloodlines (those suicide kings and queens I totally forget the name of right now) and we figured the pinnacle of Shapeshifting should be control over the form of -others-. Also needing some cleaning up, durations and trigger explanations, but basically, turning someone temporarily into something more like a classic werewolf? That's the one that does it. Locking others away from other forms for a time? That too. You'll notice that a lot of the level 5s are quite potent, and that's entirely intentional. Shapeshifting is where you first start to see how various levels/'honings' can influence one another into funky combinations.

              Dominance Instincts; the desire to be at the pinnacle of a pack, an alpha, to have others fall into line and work as a cohesive unit under your own influence. I absolutely along with my Co-ST did -not- want it to be a limited Dominate redux so folks will notice it doesn't quite run in the same way, be it old or new. It still can affect groups of people; that's part of the point, this is the Instinct that most certainly allows leaders of their packs to enforce their will on others.

              At 5 you can -force- others to become temporary members of an "artificial pack" to aid you. At lower levels it starts with the dominant werewolf being able to act the part and stand their ground more readily than most (how else do you become such a leader? Certainly by not being a spinless pushover or easily influenced; then you're just a puppet). A lot of it pushes the angle of making others "submit" to you rather than direct overt control. 4 amplifies this idea; you become such a dominant (and frightening) individual that others hesitate to actively threaten you.

              As always, it could use some more tweaking; there was a lot of initial redundancy that had to be heavily re-jiggered, and it may still require a bit more.

                  The current Rage Instincts resembles a mix between the Strength and old Rage set, though with some stark changes here and there. The level 5 is probably the most unexpected of the lot; being able to create infectious Rage in others that spread through violence while the werewolf source is able to designate who's safe from the furious wildfire. Divide and conquer. Lower levels also use some inspiration from a Cahalith gift from Signs of the Moon where you grow in fury the more you kill. We've debated adjusting the Initiative bonuses to something else or retweaking, but the base effects is what we want. The trouble is keeping it from turning into an overwhelming bonus; the idea however that the more pain you suffer, the more you get angry, and the angrier you get the more vicious seems like a good idea. Lower levels address that those who hone their Rage can push themselves to more uses of Gauru outside of Death Rage.

                  One thing to note; we are having that your Stamina + Primal Urge turns aren't a hard cap; any additional turns in Gauru past your grace period add a cumulative -1 Penalty on top of your initial Gauru penalties to avoid Death Rage. The further you push, the more likely you are to rage. Rage Instincts doesn't obliviate this (it seemed to be counterintuitve), but certainly lets you get more out of it. It's not a power set that generally makes you directly tougher; that's Feral Instincts right now; it does however make you much deadlier in conflict and tearing the shit out of things.

                      Next we have Sensory Instincts; tapping into ones ability to perceive and understand ones surroundings more effectively. It starts with a heightened more precise danger-sense and eventually becomes the ability to track someone or their property to an insane degree like the old Find Path (which was one extraordinarily potent Gift in my and my co-STs experience) while you're on the same plane of existence.                           Stealth Instincts; amplifying the instincts to effectively move quietly, unseen and more effectively avoid danger or ambush prey. It avoids a lot of overt things like direct invisibility or mental false images; again we didn't want this to be too much like a vampire's Obfuscate or some aspects of the Contract of Smoke. While level 4 might not seem potent (at first) it's worth keeping in mind the idea of shapeshifters as trickster figures at times; leaving the physical evidence of someone else at your choice? Talk about incrimination and falsified crimes. Got an opponent who's blinded? Change your scent to their packmates, and their packmates to yours and see how things go. It's intended to be versatile, potentially frightening. Use that in tandem with Shapeshifting Instincts and you may see where we were going with it.

                          Level 5 does have on effect that is a "soft" memory wipe; given we're not using Lunacy in the old fashion but instead using the Predatory Aura to cover making others feel like prey, acting irrationally etc. it was here it seemed to have a better place; at the pinnacle, people don't even remember the details of you being around or what you were with proper clarity without rolls among the other effects it amplifies. This one had a -lot- of tinkering as an Instinct; it still likely needs more, but the level 1 is something we've wanted for a while.

                              Territorial Instincts primarily plays upon the Predatory Aura; that being said, we're still hashing out the mechanics behind it, and we're wanting to divide it up into 3 aspects much like Blood and Smoke does; a physical one, that's all about making an individual wish to flee and feel like prey, having trouble to actions that don't emphasize that; one that impacts the mental with a break down of the rational mind, going somewhat feral; and another for the social that may play up the whole "animalistic dangerous magnetism" aspect to reflect getting mates, or perhaps to reinforce ones position as the dominant predator. If the latter there may be some tweaks to Dominance Instincts.

                              Right now it uses the ideas of a few gifts along with the notion of Warding; it's all about marking things, people and places as yours and getting increasingly more impact on that, from tracking the marked things down, to warding others off, defending them with increased fervor and efficiency, knowledge of the areas tied to you and later broader influences on those within your territory. It's still very rough, but it has some potential.

                              That's the last of the ones we have complete as is, what do you all think, and what areas would you tweak mechanically or otherwise? These are just the basics mind you; as I sad, no alternate levels (yet), no Pure specific Instincts just yet.

                                          I’ve gone with a much simpler implementation: “All Good Gifts” from Forsaken Chronicler’s Guide, with “Gifts” renamed as “Instincts” and buyable with experience points, without any need to involve spirits.                                                           Well, wolves are a type of canine, I didn't think there was much need to get too redundant in term use; I wanted the ability to affect canines as a whole (including wild dogs and similar things for werewolves in regions that host them rather than wolves) to present that a werewolf is going to be top of the canine food chain when they play around with that. And yeah, the Sources inclusion hasn't been added yet; we're going back to plunk them in, but we didn't feel they were a priority with the initial draft. It's something to plunk in when we go back over them. Honestly it was the easy part; we focused on getting past the hard part first, so it's obviously still needing completion in some areas.

                                                          With Pack Minded and a carrot; I agree there, we've just not figured out a concise way to do it. Originally it had a nod towards that but it came out too muddled in explanation so it just got deleted out some time back. If you have a suggestion there it would be greatly appreciated; I really think it'd benefit from something like that. There was originally a teamwork bonus; right not it benefits from the Pack Bond merit we've been tooling around with which does give an incentive like that (provided the user has it), but I could see an ingrained bonus working better for the power as long as it's not too much; it's a pretty potent effect; it's been down-toned from the first brainstorming sessions but may have lost something in doing so.

                                                          Auspex was heavy inspiration for Sensory; we didn't want it the same, obviously, but treating things more of a 'questions asked' system overall seemed the right direction so we didn't throw the mechanics teased about Auspex entirely out just for the sake of being different. Q&A really seems to be the ideal format for those types of powers.

                                                          After playing around with the idea (and having long since used things like the Mekhet Dreams merit in the past and present) it seemed like a good starting point to go from so there's definitely some overlap compared to say, looking at the old Dominance gifts and comparing them to the old Dominate. Dominance I think diverges heavily; Sensory has been a tougher nut to crack. Some of the questions and presentation still needs some tinkering mind you; it's definitely not polished and complete, but I -think- it's going the right direction. Initially it was a different sense per "honing" that was affected for the earlier levels, but it seemed too cludgy to break it down that way along with Stealth so you get what you now have as a result. All of them could have their descriptions written in a more concise way, and more cleanly to boot.

                                                          The game we run is one where information -really- is power and extraordinarily important; I guess you could say it has some tugs toward Call of Cthulhu with investigation given all the player handouts and the level of mystery and hidden information in our setting, so we definitely wanted werewolves to have a strong set of investigation tools for what they consistently run up against. We've got quite a few plot-lines started over 5 years ago that only now are getting the surface scratched as new and old PCs have dug up information; we wanted to make sure sensory got a lot of love as a result especially considering when we think wolves (and werewolves) we think expert hunters, trackers and supernaturally sensory acute. I'm hoping what's there will do the trick without stepping on Auspex's toes too badly.
