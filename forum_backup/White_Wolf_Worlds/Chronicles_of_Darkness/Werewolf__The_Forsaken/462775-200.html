                Originally posted by ShadowcatX View Post
                Can equipment, specifically mystical equipment or plans, assist in the activation rolls of gifts? For example can an Ithaeur with proper knowledge of the occult craft a piece of mystical equipment to help with the first level crescent moon gift?
                It's conceivable, but broadly speaking supernatural powers don't benefit from equipment bonuses because equipment tends to be for mundane Skill rolls whereas supernatural powers in the vein of Gifts tend to operate through channels that aren't by default assumed to be adjustable with readily-accessible materials and/or are themselves the tools being used to benefit a plan.

                Like, you could certainly do the legwork to justify rigging some occult nonsense to add dice to Shadow Gaze, but getting to that point in a way that justifies why other Ithaeur don't do so all the time is its own process, and no amount of planning is going to help make your werewolf eyes see into the heart of a spirit's nature.

                            Originally posted by cybirddude View Post
                            Does killing indirectly count as violating the Oath about People not Murdering the People? Some examples:
                            ​​
                            1: Hiring a hitman to kill someone for you.
                            2: Manipulating others into doing the killing for you.
                            3: Working an enslaved enemy to death.
                            4: Starving an enemy to death.
                            5: Driving someone to suicide.
                            If your intention is to make someone die and actions you take — including incitement of other people — cause them to die, you are sufficiently involved in their death at the level of means and motive to be considered a murderer. If this were a matter of legal code we were talking about, a character might wriggle their way out of punishments specific to murder by claiming mere criminal negligence, but on the level of "does this character know they're killing someone" I would imagine it takes some severe distancing from the chain of events to not have "I deliberately caused the death of one of the People" result in the spiritual consequences of killing one of the People.

                            Less finely-grained is the fact that "the High Respect the Low" means some of these are dicey methods to use in keeping with the Oath even without ending a life.
