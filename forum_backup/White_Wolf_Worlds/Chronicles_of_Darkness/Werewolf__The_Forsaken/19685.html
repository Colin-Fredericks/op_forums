    Have you ever used googles "maps engine" for your territories?

    11-28-2013, 09:14 AM
    Obligitory Hello I am new to the forums!

    I have been playing WoD for a while now over all of the splats and in an ongoing cross splat game and my GM from that introduced me to the idea of using googles Map Engine to lay out features in his world.

    I am currently GMing my second werewolf game and instead of writing my world down into a large book (full of pasted pictures of maps) I instead am using googles Map Engine.
    So far I have found it really incredible for laying out territories for my local packs, setting up landmarks for potential story hooks, placing things inside my packs territory and so fourth.

    I have on map called "The GM Map"
    and another map called "The player Map"
    Boy am I original with my titles.

    However as my players discover new things in the world I simply add a marker to the player map and write a little about it. The players can also add their own markers and regularly update many of the features in their territory. It makes it much more collaborative than the usual "GM book" system and the players often add things in between sessions.. I get a few texts a week asking if they can do research rolls in between sessions on this or that to get more information on the map.

    I guess what I am wondering is has anyone used this tech before and if so do you have any advice for me?

    Also sorry if this is in the wrong place just figured since I am doing it for territories id post it in werewolf!

    Thought I would add a link to my work in progress for a new map that I started making yesterday

                              I have not found a good free source for online fantasy maps. Google Maps Pro and ESRI both provide pay to use editors and publishers. I like ESRI for its modular map usage (and its ability to create/use ANY map fictional or real), and editors of base map data sets, Google Map Pro is slick, but is best for Google supported map sets (earth and Mars now).

                              Alternately I have tried Fantasy Cartographer. Its actually easier to fashion a map with it, and using a Free ESRI account you can publish player maps. Limitations still apply.

                              For what it's worth, I end up using GIMP and draw my own maps, I composite historic maps with modern maps. Then I upload them to ESRI and use the mapping tools. It costs $100 for the licensed version. (I also use it for my running blog)

                              I do this because I like to add layers of average Gauntlet Strength and Shadow Resonance for the city. Plus I map locus, verge, Aversion gate, Hallows, Trods, Essence flows, and significant territories. Otherwise I create PDFs, and allow my players to annotate the PDF as they need. Neither tool is perfect.
