    For Evil Wears a Pleasant Face...

    09-10-2015, 03:39 AM
    Spirits. We all know that, from a human perspective, they're pretty much universally evil. Part of what makes the World of Darkness so crapsack is that even spirits of pleasant places or positive concepts can be inhumanly cruel or destructive, since all they care about is promoting themselves and scoring more sweet, sweet Essence.

    Now, this is a wonderful bit of flavor and all, and really evocative... the thing is, it's not always easy to envision. I don't know, maybe it's just me, but has anyone else had problems envisioning how to pervert a kindergarten spirit or a joy spirit into something evil and inhuman?

    So, what I was hoping to do was to open this topic and give people a place to swap ideas, or experiences, with making villains or terrifying encounters out of "good spirits". I hope folks are willing to share.

    Also, really need advice on making an evil figure out of a Joy spirit. Need it for an upcoming project.

                  I recall an example in one of the 1st ed books about a Joy/Happiness spirit feeding off the high of heroin addicts.

                  The best way to emphasise the inhumane nature of spirits is to let them act in their animalistic logical manner while juxtaposing the very human context. A murder spirit encourages and feeds upon murder, be it out of geed, self-defense, patriotic duty (like a soldier) or mercy. It doesn't care, it just feeds on the Essence of purposeful sentient death. It's exact diet alters it's resonance and can turn it into a maggath if it varies it's feeding habits to rapidly.

                  So if a Joy spirit attaches itself to a sadist, it will encourage the sadist to do things that make him happy. If it stays with him too long, it might begin to change but if it tries to switch to a different food source, it might need to inflict pain in order to feed on Joy, or else become maggath...

                  Thoughts ripple out, birthing others

                      Emphasis mine. Spirits do care about the flavor of the essence they consume, not merely because it defines them, but because it gives them specific focus. The joy spirit that is described in the example you mention developed a taste for essence linked to the joy of the high, of, initially, the buzz of underage alcohol consumption. You can, at the end of it's lifespan, mistake it for an spirit of addiction. Which is interesting because if you look at all the spirits you could encounter, they all likely get begotten by such a simple concept, and evolve into what they are as you present them.

                      A spirit of "murder for patriotic duty" (soldiers will argue that killing for your country, in a conflict with defined rules of engagement isn't murder at all, but that's beyond the scope of this discussion) won't likely consider itself a mere spirit of murder, and it's a completely different beast from one born of revenge and sanity eclipsing passion.

                      To expand on the example? The war spirit ("murder for patriotic duty"), maybe a reflection of the adrenaline from the survival in the battlefield will likely have bans against engaging "friendlies" (Your countrymen?). Rules, in essence.

                      The Homicide-for-passion spirit? not so much. And you can already picture the war spirit hitching a ride back to the Estates with the soldier that spawned him, using it's influence to press enough stress to cause deadly fight or flight responses on the poor man, can't you?
