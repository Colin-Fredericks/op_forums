    Wasu-Im and Basu-Im are both considered part of Kuruth, so you could probably still roll for it.

    I would have you only erase the marks when you've gone Kuruth because of it, so after you've filled up. So at Primal Urge 1 you'd get 2 uses out of it. One puts the mark up, the next one 'acts' with full marks. I'd rule it that way, at least.

    Addendum: I like Elfive's 'ticking down by day' idea.

    Yes, I could see the issue with that. I've had some bad luck with aspirations and things, so I haven't gotten much in the way of Experiences, but we generally play about once a week or so. Still, Primal Urge is considerably cheaper now. Depending on your game, if you're running it or it has a limited duration, so this wouldn't be too game-breaking, you might offer a Primal Urge for every successful, but momentous, Siskur-Dah. I wouldn't use that in my own games, which tend to be sandboxes, or a more long-running story unless I had a series of 9 previously determined 'prey' that would lead to the big finale, but if you had a shorter game it might be a nice way to do it, reflecting a werewolf's progress through the Siskur-Dah.

                        Primal Beats

                        - You go on a Hunt in excess of your minimum requirement.
                        - You enter Basu-Im.
                        - You use your Hunter's Aspect on a mortal.
                        - You successfully use a form to its stated purpose.
                        - You exemplify your Auspice without flaring Renown.
                        - You exemplify your Tribe.

                        There's probably more.

That felt much less true in Werewolf 2E than I remember it being in 1E. Sure, it was super expensive, but then everything was. The fourth dot in your Renown tied to your Auspice or Tribe was 24XP, for example.

                                            While it may seem a lot more possible to get it now (a phenomenon that doesn't strike as close to home for me as it does for many), it also looks a lot less useful than it seemed to be. Hence "anymore".
