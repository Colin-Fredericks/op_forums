    A vampire in the pack

    08-24-2015, 09:34 PM
    So we are starting a game of Werewolf 2nd edition and one of our players wants to play a vampire. The GM has tentatively okayed it and we are not trying to consider all the complications that come along with a vampire member of a pack.

    Anyone done this yet? Any tips that would help make this a fun experience for all involved? We have a blessed pack and a vampire that is a close, close friend with the pack alpha.

    For the record, she will not be playing a gangrel. She'll probably be playing Daeva, Ventrue or Mekhet, and will be unaligned as far as covenants.

    Primary concerns are balance, the fact that the player is out of any daytime pack activity, the fact that a large portion of the game takes place in the Shadow (can vampires even enter the shadow except through a verge?). Is group pathfinding a thing still in 2e and if so would the vampire lose all their clothes? In general how would a vampire fit in with a pack of werewolves socially speaking? Would they necessarily be on a rung below the werewolves as far as hierarchy? Would they get totem bonuses? (I know that's a no).

    Just wondering what the general opinion and advice on this sort of thing is.

      1- I did;

      2- If the ST don't want to mix vampire's politics, there are no need for it. If she want to do, there are no harm in it, either. Both work well.

      3- Balance is a given of sorts. The vampire will be good at her stuff, but no worries about anyone there being overshadowed.

      4- Daytime activity have two possibilities. If it is frequent and the game primarily about werewolf stuff, just give the vampire an item to get rid of sunburns. If infrequent, just work through it.

      5- Vampires can enter the Shadow as much as any human can. The werewolves can find ways to do so, specially in 2nd ed.

      6- I think things are easier now.

      7- Socially each pack is a unique thing. That means that its fit depends entirely on the group, no hard rules.

      8- The same as above, hierarchy doesn't even need to be a thing in the pack. Or the vampire can have a high standing if the pack decides so. In my game the Alpha was a wolf-blooded and the Beta was his mortal wife. They got things done, so the pack didn't complained.

      9- I don't remember any rule that forbid vampires from getting the same treatment as mortals, so yes, they can. They can get all the pack benefits, and actually invest on the pack's Totem.

                                      Originally posted by Bunyip View Post
                                      Humans get the totem's advantage, but they don't have the Totem merit and don't contribute towards the totem. (Supporting-cast Wolf-Blooded are limited to a single dot in the Totem merit as well p.91.)Unless your Storyteller decides otherwise, a Vampire pack member does gain the totem advantage, but can't contribute to the Totem merit - she lacks the connection to the Hisil.
                                      My interpretation was that the humans didn't got the Merit just for ease of the rules for pack creation. The same reason for the Wolf-Blooded to only begin with one dot: not because they can't have more, but because those where NPC's creation rules for a pack.

                                      Anyway, some limitations only have to be in place if the ST wan't, they're not a balance issue. The weakness to fire is a balancing issue for vampires, it gives them a weakness ready to be used by an enemy with thorough preparation, as is silver to Uratha. But the weakness to sun light is just a thematic enforcer. If the ST want to use this theme, it stays. But if not, she doesn't have to even give a cost for ways to get rid of it. A magical ring or extra-super-special spiritual blessing is more than enough explanation for this vampire to walk in the daylight with the pack. Or simply ignore that the weakness ever existed.

                                      The same goes for any obstacle in dealing with werewolf's themes. You can even give the vampire a Devotion to cross the Gauntlet free of cost, simply because this ability isn't powerful in your game, but instead simply default.

                                      Just take care to not give for free anything that REALLY makes the game easier compared to the Uratha. Alternate forms isn't a bad idea if the player wants it, but it certainly should cost. Only get rid of limitations that don't play on balance in THIS chronicle and would only get in your way. Difficulties to do some common things, if still doable to the degree needed, should stay in place to also reinforce the theme of the difference between the splats (a natural theme in most crossovers).
