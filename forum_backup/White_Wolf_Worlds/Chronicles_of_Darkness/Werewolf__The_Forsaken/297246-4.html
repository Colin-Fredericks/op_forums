                I think you're focusing a bit much on the "power level" side of this.

                It feels to me that the primary motivating factor with how Gifts were changed is not about power level (though PU is getting buffed because it is fairly weak in 1e; I'm not sure what you think is debatable about this). It's about how Gifts and Renown interact. In 1e, Gifts were gated behind expensive Renown costs, had XP penalties for buying ones you wanted out of order, Gift lists often used a wide range or Renown categories in dice pools creating further XP taxes, and so on.

                The new Gift scheme is designed to counteract this. There is a greater positive synergy between Renown and Gifts, since you can focus on one Renown category, and the Facets associated with it, and not get penalized for not buying powers that rely on other Renown traits. The power level of Gifts is also more decoupled from an arbitrary decision about what level that power should be, and more dependent on the investment into the Renown in question. This means Gifts grow more with your character as you invest in Renown and watch their linked facets grow in power.

                The Gift system didn't actually work as well as possible for its intended purpose, which is why its changing. The problem seems to be a bit of conflating how you like things working in your games with the actual intended purpose. But you don't have to switch to 2e. The 1e WtF books have plenty of fun stuff in them to keep a group going for years if you like them more.

                                            Originally posted by Tymeaus Jalynsfein View Post

                                            Interesting exposition...
                                            However... all of the things you describe above could be done in 1st Edition, with no need to make any changes whatsoever (and the narratives you provided above even work as described). SO... WHY did the team feel the need to make the changes they made to buff up the Werewolf to the new levels we are seeing? It is the incessant need to BUFF UP werewolves (and even Vampires) that has left such a bad taste in my mouth for the new Editions. Yes, there are some really interesting things there, but in my opinion (which matters little in the grand scheme of things) you did not need to increase their powers to be the unholy terrors that they are now shaping up to be.

                                            That said, Chris, I think the work you are doing is awesome (especially some of the fluff changes), though ultimately unneeded in my opinion, at least for me and the group I play with.
                                            So, why have we made the changes we have? I can't offer a really in-depth look at this time, but I can give an overview.

                                            Firstly, I think it's worth noting that any declarations of Uratha being 'buffed' need to be looked at within this specific context: The entirety of Forsaken 2e has not yet been revealed, and it takes place mechanically within the framework of the GMC rules update.

                                            In terms of the former, for example, not every bit of capacity of the 1e Uratha has made it over to 2e – no Gifts that allow you to inflict Aggravated damage, for example – and in terms of the latter it means the core, underlying rules mechanics have already changed. The new Forsaken can't just put up the old Gifts, because even before we get to all the changes we wanted to make with 2e, there are meaningful modifications already in place to eg. the combat system.

                                            With that pre-existing context out of the way, then, there were a lot of changes we wanted to make in 2e. Gifts aren't just a free-floating part of the system but tie in with all sorts of other aspects. Again, this feeds into a situation where by the time I even get to writing the new Gifts rules, significant alterations to what exists already are definitely on the plate.

                                            Obviously we had specific goals in mind for the new Werewolf powers, much of which built of our and the community's experience with running Forsaken over the years since its inception. There were all sorts of issues I wanted to address – the problems with speedbump powers, powers that were unthematic, those with near-broken rules and those with near-useless rules. Given the changes to the underlying structure of how Gifts work (no more linear ascending dots, etc) that we implemented as part of the overhaul of how we wanted the werewolf supernatural powers and Renown to work, I was faced with a situation where large amounts of the existing Gift lists were simply impossible to port anyway, before even looking at those Gifts that were just plain uninspiring or mechanically or thematically 'misses'.

                                            My guiding goals while writing the new Gifts involved every Facet having an impact, Facets having a meaningful link to the Renown that they represent and/or to the role of the werewolf as a hunter, and no speedbump or spacefiller powers. Would I say that the new Gifts have been 'buffed'? Yes, inasmuch as that they are designed to all be useful and meaningful – the new werewolves lack several of the powerful old 1e capabilities (no turning yourself into a shadow, shouting healing at packmates or hitting people with lightning bolts) but they also lost a lot of the 'packing material' around the choice Gifts – you don't have to slog through various dots of a Gift to get the one or two powers in it you actually want.

                                            The 2e Gifts are less of a total grab-bag of powers and more strongly themed where possible; they have a specific purpose, which is to better equip the Uratha to be the hunter and packmate he or she needs to be. Quite apart from the mechanical issues with 1e Gifts (of which I believe there were many), most of them were a mix of odd tricks taught by spirits, while what a Gift is is now more firmly defined and tied to the Uratha experience.

                                            It's also my understanding (I may be incorrect) that 1e's Gifts owe a lot in terms of their design and themes to Apocalypse's Gifts. With 2e, there's been less reason to stick to any hold-overs from Apocalypse and more of a push to make Forsaken stand proud in its own right; as someone who never played Apocalypse, I've been happy to work on the new Gifts from the ground up without any particular preconceptions or preferences based off the cWoD gameline, and this may feed into some of the changes as well.

                                            At the end of the day, I feel confident that the new Gift system will be more fun and more engaging than the old one for most groups, that the Facets will feel more impactful and more appropriate for Uratha, and that the whole package will help make Renown a more enjoyable element to engage with as well. I'm not particularly fussed about overall or comparative 'power level', buffing or nerfing – rather, I want a given power to feel meaningful and have a definite impact on the game mechanically and thematically. Where Gifts stood, power-wise, in 1e is not important to me when designing them in the framework of 2e, which is the context in which they will actually have to perform.

                                            The new Gifts system may not work for every group. That's fine – great, even, if you're happy with the old system, since it means you already have something that works for you, and that's the aim of game design! However, for many people the old Gifts system was not working for them, and in 2e I have endeavoured to create something new that will.

                                            I hope that, once 2e is released, everyone will have an edition of Werewolf that they can enjoy, whether that's sticking with 1e, moving to 2e or nabbing bits of each edition for a hybrid to their preference

                                            - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

Again, this feeds into a situation where by the time I even get to writing the new Gifts rules, significant alterations to what exists already are definitely on the plate.

                                                Obviously we had specific goals in mind for the new Werewolf powers, much of which built of our and the community's experience with running Forsaken over the years since its inception. There were all sorts of issues I wanted to address – the problems with speedbump powers, powers that were unthematic, those with near-broken rules and those with near-useless rules. Given the changes to the underlying structure of how Gifts work (no more linear ascending dots, etc) that we implemented as part of the overhaul of how we wanted the werewolf supernatural powers and Renown to work, I was faced with a situation where large amounts of the existing Gift lists were simply impossible to port anyway, before even looking at those Gifts that were just plain uninspiring or mechanically or thematically 'misses'.

                                                My guiding goals while writing the new Gifts involved every Facet having an impact, Facets having a meaningful link to the Renown that they represent and/or to the role of the werewolf as a hunter, and no speedbump or spacefiller powers. Would I say that the new Gifts have been 'buffed'? Yes, inasmuch as that they are designed to all be useful and meaningful – the new werewolves lack several of the powerful old 1e capabilities (no turning yourself into a shadow, shouting healing at packmates or hitting people with lightning bolts) but they also lost a lot of the 'packing material' around the choice Gifts – you don't have to slog through various dots of a Gift to get the one or two powers in it you actually want.

                                                The 2e Gifts are less of a total grab-bag of powers and more strongly themed where possible; they have a specific purpose, which is to better equip the Uratha to be the hunter and packmate he or she needs to be. Quite apart from the mechanical issues with 1e Gifts (of which I believe there were many), most of them were a mix of odd tricks taught by spirits, while what a Gift is is now more firmly defined and tied to the Uratha experience.

                                                It's also my understanding (I may be incorrect) that 1e's Gifts owe a lot in terms of their design and themes to Apocalypse's Gifts. With 2e, there's been less reason to stick to any hold-overs from Apocalypse and more of a push to make Forsaken stand proud in its own right; as someone who never played Apocalypse, I've been happy to work on the new Gifts from the ground up without any particular preconceptions or preferences based off the cWoD gameline, and this may feed into some of the changes as well.

                                                At the end of the day, I feel confident that the new Gift system will be more fun and more engaging than the old one for most groups, that the Facets will feel more impactful and more appropriate for Uratha, and that the whole package will help make Renown a more enjoyable element to engage with as well. I'm not particularly fussed about overall or comparative 'power level', buffing or nerfing – rather, I want a given power to feel meaningful and have a definite impact on the game mechanically and thematically. Where Gifts stood, power-wise, in 1e is not important to me when designing them in the framework of 2e, which is the context in which they will actually have to perform.

                                                The new Gifts system may not work for every group. That's fine – great, even, if you're happy with the old system, since it means you already have something that works for you, and that's the aim of game design! However, for many people the old Gifts system was not working for them, and in 2e I have endeavoured to create something new that will.

                                                I hope that, once 2e is released, everyone will have an edition of Werewolf that they can enjoy, whether that's sticking with 1e, moving to 2e or nabbing bits of each edition for a hybrid to their preference

                                                While Werewolf is not my favourite foray into the NWoD, but as someone who does plan on getting the 2nd edition at some point I really appreciate posts like this. As I consider myself someone who has some apprehensions about the 2nd editions so far as far as power levels go. Really helps set up a benefit of the doubt for enjoying the materials.
