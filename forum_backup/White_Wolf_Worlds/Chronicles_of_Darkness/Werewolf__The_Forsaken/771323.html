    Not sure what the best tribe would be for this character I'm making.

    11-22-2015, 04:42 AM
    So I've written up this werewolf, and I'm not sure exactly what tribe would be best for him. Each one, save the Bone Shadows, seems like somewhere he could fit in somewhat, but no where feels like a perfect clean fit.

    "Matthew Lee was always kind of a quiet, unassuming, and generally kind of boring guy to talk to. He had a few niche topics of interest. Chemistry. Comic Books. His beloved dog Mable. On any of these topics, he would rant for hours, much to the chagrin of any bored listeners he managed to ensnare. On any other topic, however, he would clam up, stare at his shoes, and wait for the conversation to turn to something he was more interested in. People intimidated him, and he didn't really have a grasp on most social niceties. Most weren't able to tell if he was on the spectrum, or just kind of awkward.

    His wife, Virginia, had married him for the stability that he provided, what with his well-paying job as a chemical engineer. But as the years went on, his utter lack of any definable personality began to wear on her, and she began to crave excitement. He arrived home one day to find her packing her things into her car. When he asked where she was going, she revealed that she'd been having an affair, and that she was leaving him to find a life more fulfilling than that of the boring housewife of a boring man.

    Matthew fell into despair at this point, his entire world and sense of self undermined. His daily routine was nothing but waking up, going to work, going home, watching tv for an hour, and falling asleep, with no real social interaction whatsoever. To be fair, that's about what it was before anyway, but now he was aware of just how sad it was. He craved some kind of a change. He needed it. He needed to be something other than boring.

    On his way home one day, however, Matt came upon a woman being assaulted by a group of men in a back alley way. He never quite understood what came over him that day, but something compelled him to intervene. Matt leaped into the fray, a furious whirlwind of awkward punches and kicks. Against all odds, he drove the attackers away and saved the girl, who called him her hero and kissed him on the cheek before leaving. His face was turning purple from the merciless beating he had received in the process, but something inside of him felt right. He knew then that he had found his calling.

    Matt joined a local MMA gym and began training. He took a sewing class or two and he began researching urban exploration and free running. After a year of preparation, he made his debut on the streets of Baltimore as the masked vigilante, Night-Wolf

    Why a wolf? He wasn't quite sure. He just always liked dogs.

    Matt received many a beating over the next few years, but he kept at it, prowling the streets at night in search of people in need of help. It was only a matter of time, however, before he bit off more than he could chew.

    It was a cold December night, and he remembers seeing the crimson red of his blood in striking contrast to the snow around him, as a gang of 6 or 7 punks surrounded him and beat him with pipes, chains, and their own fists and feet. At the moment he thought he was going to die, however, the clouds in the sky parted, and the light of the full moon shone down upon him.

    When he regained his senses, his wounds had completely healed, and the punks who had been beating him had been turned into a fine mist covering the dark alley. Behind him were 3 mysterious figures, who told him that he had been chosen by Mother Luna.

    Matt's a bit of an odd duck in his pack. He still dons his Night Wolf costume to patrol his territory, and in group hunts it's a bit awkward having one weirdo in spandex when everyone else is wearing normal street clothes. His pack-mates have somewhat learned to accept it as one of his eccentricities, and hey, if someone sees him doing something supernatural, at least the explanation they come up with will probably be something about Night Wolf himself, rather than pondering the larger existence of the Uratha. That said, they're mostly happy to distance themselves from him somewhat and let him be weird on his own.

    Matt, for his part, is just happy to feel like he's found somewhere he belongs."



    As far as the tribes,


    Blood Talons- He is a warrior, make no mistake, but I don't think Matt would gel with the fixation on violence. He enjoys the validation he gets from fighting, but he isn't really blood-thirsty to fit in with the other Talons, I think. I also think he's a bit too much of a lone wolf to get in on the whole band of brothers angle.

    Storm Lords- A bit of the opposite problem. Matt's a lone wolf, and the book says that the Storm Lords are the ones most likely to run off on their own to hunt by themselves. But their whole ideology of being the biggest bad-ass you can be and stoically enduring whatever life throws at you doesn't seem like something that would entirely appeal to him.

    Hunters in Darkness- One of the two I'm most strongly considering. Matt would likely gel with the idea of your territory being something sacred, as that's very close to the relationship he had with his city already. He already envisioned himself as a kind of apex predator patrolling his kingdom or territory for those who were violating its laws. The only thing making me hold back is that the book is really clear: if you violate a Hunter in Darkness's territory, there's no mercy and no warning: He fucking kills you. I know that stereotypes are made to be broken, but Matt doesn't strike me as the type that could just coldly decide to kill whoever and whatever violated his territory on the spot. At worst, he'd give them a warning in the form of a few broken bones. Would that be enough for the Hunters though? I'm not really sure.

    EDIT: While it's not the most pure motivation, I do, admittedly, have another motivation for favoring Hunter in Darkness: Rahu benefit from many dots in Purity.

    Iron Masters- The other I'm most strongly considering. Matt would likely want to continue stalking his city, and he'd likely focus on human issues, so the Iron Masters would call to him as well. They also seem the most flexible on how to hunt your prey, and having your hunt end in something other than a bloody massacre. My caveat here is that while he blends with a lot of their methods and hunting goals, their philosophy isn't one I see being all that important to him. Making sure you're always adapting and changing and growing isn't quite what he's about. He's not really the inquisitive type apart from the few areas where he has a really strong interest.




    Thoughts? Suggestions?

      Best tribe is always Blood Talons, no matter the concept!

      Just kidding. Anyways, My personal preference, when deciding on a Tribe, is simply to match it to the associated Auspice. In all games I have ever played, Tribes play a smaller role - some influence with personal stories, but never an organization on par with a Covenant, Court, or Order. So, I judge the y-splat entirely by the advantages - both Gifts and merits. The interesting thing about the Tribal merits is that they were practically designed for each Auspice theme. Blood Talon's merit makes you a better Rahu. Bone Shadow makes you better Shaman. Hunter in Darkness merit is better for Irraka class sneaking. Iron Master merit grants advantages to social merit networks, like the Elodoth. Storm Lords work the Cahalith social fu angle.

      Now, I am not saying you can't mix and match - half warrior, half social works well. But if you aren't looking for the social stuff, and go with a pure Rahu hunter, Blood Talon will make you better

      You cannot take three dots in a Renown at Char Gen, so HtD Purity stacking with Rahu and free dot isn't a thing. And I don't really suggest worrying about the favored prey thing. Tribe doesn't tell you what you can or cannot do with your prey, or which you can hunt - it merely gives you the tools to hunt prey down.

      So, what is your favorite method of hunting? Stealth attacks, social manuevering, being bigger and badder...? That should be the answer to your question.

                          No, I'm talking about 2e. I have no idea what made you think I was talking about 1e. Frankly, I find that the association between Auspice and Tribe are even stronger now, and I find the implication that I don't know what I'm talking about is rather annoying.

                          Further, there are only a handful of abilities in the entire game that directly impact the five sacred prey. The first is, ironically, the Ithaeur gift line, which deals with spirits, which just pushes the whole Tribe-Auspice association thing even harder. A second could be considered Bone Gnaw, since you're eating human and were bones, though that could easily apply to anyone, since humans tend to be prey for all five sacred prey; I'm not going to count that as hunting humans or werewolves, especially since Death Gifts are affinity for Bone Shadows, not Blood Talons or Iron Masters. I suppose you could argue that Technology Gifts are for hunting humans, given that humans benefit from technology more, but that's a weak argument when werewolves, Ridden, and Hosts all benefit, as well as artifice spirits. Predator's Claim deals with specifically dealing with spirits as a sacred prey - and its affinity for Hunters in Darkness, not Bone Shadows. Then, there are the rites - Bottle Spirit (Bone Shadow only), Banish, and Expel deal with spirits, while Urfarah's Bane deals aggravated damage to werewolves. With the exception of Bottle Spirit, these abilities can be taken by anyone.

                          A grand total of one Gift and four rites deal directly with hunting the sacred prey. Only one of them is affinity for Bone Shadows, and its a Rite, not a Gift. So, no. I'm sorry, but the Gifts are not designed for hunting their sacred prey. These affinities grant a certain style of hunting - tanky, stealthy, talky, mystic, and web-of-allies-y. You can argue that being tanky is the best way to hunt werewolves, stealthy for hunting Hosts, etc. But a HitD Irraka is still highly effective taking down humans, Claimed/Ridden, fellow werewolves, and others using the same tactics as the Hosts. A tanky Blood Talon is still effective in battle against any prey. The manipulate-human-world abitlies of the Iron Masters might be ideal for hunting humans, but it is still effective when dealing with any prey. And so on.

                          Here's the thing about affinity Gifts. They're a bit misleading, because you really have to look at the cross section of Gifts by Renown. A low Purity werewolf isn't going to be using a lot of Purity-based abilities, even if he does have affinity for purchasing them. A high Puritiy wolf, meanwhile, is going to be attracted to picking up some non-affinity Gifts that work well with his Renown score. Because of that, you want to look at the Tribal-affinity merits and Rites as well as the affinity Gifts; the former two are exclusive to the Tribe, while the latter two are not.

                          As for that "I'm a role player and don't care about mechanics" bit? Onyx Path writers are very good at using mechanics to support the themes of the associated tribe/auspice. All the mechanics support the goals of the splats, and the splats defined what the mechanics should be like. The two are tightly interconnected. If you want to know how a Storm Lord hunts, you look at their affinities - merits, rites, renown-based affinity gifts together. Choosing a Tribe is choosing the tools you want to hunt with. Don't want to be accused of being a min-maxer, that's fine. I'm not advocating optimization or anything like that. However, its unavoidable that mechanics inform the story and roleplaying aspects of the characters, and choosing the right abilities that complement your concept does make the game more fun. If you go Storm Lord, and are stuck with a suite of powers that involve inspiriing allies and maneuvering when you wanted neither is a bit of an issue.

                          Plus, you're the one who was contemplating a Tribe just so you could start with Purity 3, and now you're looking at Iron Masters just for Technolgy (which could easily get at a 2exp penalty for all of the facets). Nevermind that Warding would be just as awesome for keeping tabs on your territory for another superhero-ish ability. Or that the Hunters in Darkness have exclusive powers to practically teleport across their cityscape. Or that Irraka/Hunter In Darkness vibes with the whole loner bit.

                          You wanted an opinon, I gave an informed opinion based on an analysis of the 2e material. Cheers, I'm out.
