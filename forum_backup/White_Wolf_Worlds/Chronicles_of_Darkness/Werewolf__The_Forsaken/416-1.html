                    I meant to the werewolves regarding unilateral buffing, in response to Kindred being able to (with enough investment in the right powers) being able to hand Werewolves their asses here and there. Werewolves don't need to be -more- powerful by default because a vampire might kick their ass with the right power set. This isn't to say that I don't want their own powers refined and cleaned up (and in some cases their outright crap, and in others -really fucking broken- as is). That's going to happen regardless of how things are set up when you've got individuals purchasing and investing heavily into combative powers. It turns into anything but skewed in one or the other's favour, or a given "I win" button once either party starts buying and using those kinds of powers.

                    I'm not a fan of knee-jerk reactions of "What, that splat got beefed up? Shit, we better beef up ours to be more powerful". Vampires got some heavy buffs -because they needed them- and to more readily have them jive with the material as presented syncing with the mechanics. With werewolf, it's less of an issue of them falling behind, so much as messy power sets that require cherry picking when they should otherwise be more streamlined and consistently useful. I've never found werewolf to be too weak in years and years of playing; with a proper GMC ruleset conversion they're still not doing badly if you cut the rancid fat off their mechanical bones.

                    The fact of the matter is, a physically invested -anything- splat is often going to hand a werewolf it's ass who invests nothing or very little in those areas. Their physical advantage is an out of the gate edge when comparing one of them to a similarly built supernatural of another stripe at the start; it requires merit and power support to pay off later once others start playing around in those areas and usually more investiture in the form of Experience and combative powers to catch up and exceed a werewolf. You get a quick and beefy edge at the start; it just isn't a be all end all once characters actually start growing in power across the splats, the werewolves still need to invest too, and that's how it should be if that makes sense. They're not always going to be the apex of ass-handing by nature of what they are alone.

                    On the matter of Wolf-blooded; oh boy, so many options. I definitely want to see more for them. Book of Spirits has some tidbits in there (not Wolf-Blooded related directly) in the form of Merits I could see using as a basis, along with quite a few others. I definitely want to see them have more love and options available, though with my distaste of Lunacy I suppose that could be said to be that I am wanting them buffed up in general.

                            If I was working on it, I can tell you this: the power level of other splats is not going to factor heavily into my thinking. I do want to see werewolves beefed up, but not because everything must have white room parity (which doesn't exist). It's because we've learned a lot since the nWoD early days and design intentions have changed accordingly. The system is much more refined and dynamic now. B&S, and even the later games, demonstrate the depth and flexibility possible, and also after years of experience, new themes and concepts emerge, as well as new ways to reinforce those things. Yes, there does seem to be a significant boost in power level for vampires, and I expect there will be one for werewolves — not because vampires got one, or because there's some specific level werewolves need to reach, but because of the evolution of setting expectations and concomitant game design.

                            Tell you what, though, one of the things I'd like to see with a heightening on the emphasis of "wolf" in "werewolf" is more detail for pack tactics and lupine instincts. Facing a lone werewolf should be scary enough, but facing a pack should be a death sentence unless you are very badass and very prepared.
