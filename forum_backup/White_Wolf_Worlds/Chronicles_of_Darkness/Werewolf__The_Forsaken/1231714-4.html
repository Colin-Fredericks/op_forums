    Ok, so we're back from holidays with a nice, simple session as the Harbingers followed through with one of their outstanding obligations.

    Also Orla's player is going to be absent for a while, not sure how long, for personal reasons. In-universe we've decided that she's gone off on some kind of spirit quest in the meantime, although that could just be code for following a band on tour around the country.

    Session 17

    Aaron's injuries healed slowly over the week following the New Year's events. His left eye regenerated first, slowly weaving itself whole again from the ruin the wound-bound spirit had made of his face. By the time he could see again, marvelling at how colours had returned all looking slightly different in some indefinable way, he was champing at the bit to return to the hunt. Though more and more bats seemed to be moving into Andersonstown, a worrying coincidence after their fight with the Bat Host, Aaron was reluctantly persuaded by his packmates that their first duty was to discharge an existing obligation. They had promised the Whispers as part of the peace treaty that they would lend their help in running down the Magath that had once been their pack totem. We-who-eat-in-darkness was still out there, causing trouble in Blackstaff and bringing more darkness spirits across the Gauntlet with each passing day, but with no Loci to give them essence and no Totem to allow them to perform the Siskur-Dah, the Whispers were unable to take action themselves.

    The three wolves still in Belfast headed north in Aaron's car. Aaron himself now felt comfortable going about in public, though his new eye was a little small and thoroughly bloodshot. By keeping his head bandaged he could sell to anyone that knew him that he had "nearly lost an eye" in the attack. It was evening on a Thursday, after Regan had been let out from school, and Aed took it as a point of pride that it was a Gibbous Moon that hung in the air for their first hunt of the new year.

    They reached the alleyway where they knew the Whispers had wards and waited for the other pack to arrive. The shadows in Blackstaff were as long and unnatural as ever, stretching out like it was late afternoon even during the middle of the day, and at night they seemed darker and utterly impenetrable.

    When the Whispers arrived, Jamie, Dorian, and Greyson all together, the two packs went straight down to business. Aaron tried to work out where the Gauntlet would be weakest, making it easy for We-who-eat-in-darkness to open gateways for her allies, while Jamie suggested they try to track her from where they had last seen her. That location was actually inside the Blackstaff River, running beneath their feet. The Harbingers followed the Whispers to their Den, somewhere quiet and out of the way where they could invoke the Siskur-Dah to have away to actually end the Magath permanently. The den looked like a simple, modest home from the outside, not far from the school where Regan had located Dorian previously, but its floorboards had been mostly ripped up and the places beneath filled with all kinds of traps. The living room was devoid of furniture, floor stained by past rituals, and it was here the Harbingers invoked the Rite.

    When the two packs left to track We-who-eat-in-darkness, the Whispers led them all to something that seemed remarkably similar to a sewer access point. They descended into the darkness.

    From their previous encounters with the Magath, the Harbingers were aware of that her Ban required her to follow the instructions of anyone who presented her with a great feast. Jamie added that, using her Gifts of the New Moon, she had determined that We-who-eat-in-darkness's Bane had changed as well from when she had been the Whispers' Totem. Growing stronger and warping in nature as she devoured more and more darkness essence, We-who-eat-in-darkness could no longer stand the light of the sun and moon conjoined. When the two celestials appeared in the sky together, she would burn and whither away into nothing.

    Aaron took point as they descended into the river, all of them shifting to join Greyson in Urhan, and began to track. The spirit's trail headed upstream. They were underground here, in a concrete tunnel through which waters flowed without ever seeing the light of day. The wolves had to swim and wade their way against the current, and after half an hour of this Aed was already feeling exhaustion creep in. The trail brought the two packs to a split in the tunnel, where a cavern appeared to have been bored through the concrete and into the earth beyond.

    The tunnel inside spiralled into a maze of rock formations, half-filled with water, but they had only gone a short distance before they heard voices in the distance babbling in the First Tongue. Aaron invoked his Hunter's Aspect, letting loose a shattering howl in the darkness. The hearts of the spirits ahead were filled with bravado, convincing them to Materialise and fight the wolves with tooth and claw. Moments later, the wave of darkness was upon them.

    At first, in the lightless depths, it was impossible to discern their attackers from the shadows all around. Aaron and Aed felt their flesh turn to rot, sloughing off as pulsating ulcers formed in their flesh. Regan turned towards them, and found herself with a pallid creature with bloated, white flesh. She recognised those wide, staring eyes from her encounter with them in the Hisil. Drowners. Long, pale fingers wrapped around her throat before she could react, and began to choke.

    Jamie saw the first Drowner appear, then a second move towards Aaron, and cried out, "Drink the water!" She bent down to do so and had managed to swallow a mouthful, when the water came alive. Aed recognised the leech-like Shadow spirits he had seen throughout the underground, swarming up and over the werewolves as they fought. One flung itself onto Aaron's back, another two latched onto Jamie. They could see Dorian battling something unseen in the darkness, and then, in the midst of the chaos, We-who-eat-in-darkness materialised among them.

    Greyson saw his former totem and seemed to forget the plan, which required the Harbingers to be the one to strike the killing blow. He shifted up into Urshul, gaining bulk and muscle, and threw himself at the creature. His teeth rent large holes in the Magath's corpus as he bore it to the ground, but it seemed content to ignore him as it struggled back to its feet, wounds already closing. Aaron felt the rot in his body take greater hold, and only retained consciousness by flinging himself into the Garou form. Raging, he tore at the spirits clinging to his back, but the weaklings actually put up a fight. For a moment he felt his vision going dark, knowing instinctively that the rot had taken such a strong hold that it would take days to regenerate, before he finally sank his claws into the little wisps of shadow clinging to his body and rent them asunder.

    Regan was in a similarly dire position. The hands around her neck were choking, crushing her windpipe and stifling her cries. Aed went to help of his teammate, even as Jamie won her way through of the shadow leeches. It took three Werewolves to bring the first Drowner down, and it discorporated into little more than brackish water at the last. Aed and Regan immediately followed Jamie's instructions, drinking deep from the water around them.

    By this point, only Aaron hadn't had a moment to drink, and it was clear that he was the focus of the surviving spirits. The onslaught of decay continued to ruin his body even as he turned his attention towards We-who-eat-in-darkness. He struggled to harm the creature through its rolls of fat, but at last his teeth sank deep into her neck and struck something that made her squeal. Her injuries were closing rapidly, but not enough to keep up with the onslaught as Regan and Aed joined the attack. Each Harbinger had their teeth into a different part of the Magath's body, and when Aed pulled one final time her body came apart in every direction.

    Aed feasted, absorbing the last essence from the spirit. She had been near-drained by the fight, and was left with nothing she could use to reform her body. The Magath, former totem of the Whispers, was dead. She would not be coming back.

        Session 18

        The two packs left the gently discorporating bodies of the slain spirits, and the ruined cadaver of the Magath, behind them as they ventured back out into the tunnels. Only Dorian paused, spitting on the body of his pack's former totem. They followed the current in the dark until they reached the ladder they had used to enter the underground river system. That was when a figured stepped out of the darkness, illuminated by the faintly glowing lightbulbs near the entrance. A voice boomed, accusing them all of trespass. The figure was thin, a man with a scraggly growth of hair around his chin, each bone in his trembling hands standing out as he pointed at them in accusation. He smelled like the river itself, but his words made it clear he was one of the People.

        The Whispers, for their part, seemed more bemused than concerned. This was their territory, after all. But the man insisted, he had dominion over all the underground places of Belfast. Dorian shook his head and just shoved the man out of the way. The push sent him staggering, and the newcomer made no effort to retaliate or prevent the Whispers as they passed him and left the tunnels.

        The Harbingers struck a more conciliatory tone. Aed asked the strange wolf what his name was. At first the man just grunted the word "Wade". A beat later and all the Harbingers were laughing, looking pointedly at the knee-high water they were all standing in. The stranger swore and added that his first name was Eddy. The Harbingers each introduced themselves, and Aed tried to work out where Wade's allegiances lay. His hackles went up at the suggestion, and he swore he had no connection to either the Forsaken or the Pure. He was a Ghost Wolf, one who'd been driven underground by the predations of Werewolves of all stripes. He claimed the Balelight Howl had killed his family as part of a ploy to force him into a Forsaken pack, and that near every Uratha he'd ever met had tried to beat him, bite him, or drive him into the arms of a new pack. He'd claimed Belfast's underground, all of it, because for the last twenty-seven years there had been no place for him on the surface world at all.

        Aed mentioned that they'd be in touch, and offered the Harbingers' help if Wade ever needed extra hands policing his immense territory. Regan noted that he struck a balance between arrogant and pathetic, unable to meet Aed's eyes without his hands trembling, but proud enough to dismiss the offer for help out of hand. The Harbingers bid him farewell and made their way back to the surface and, under cover of darkness, travelled south to return to their territory.

        Their victory allowed them all to head home for an early night. With We-Who-Eat-In-Darkness destroyed, the Whispers should be able to seek out and take a new totem and begin the long process of restoring balance to their territory. Aed was feeling exhausted, so he dropped Aaron off at the rectory and then headed straight home, making Regan walk the last few blocks to her own house. He headed straight to bed and collapsed face-first into it.

        Regan was at the front door of her house when the lamplight above flared out into a brilliant silver sphere of light, large enough to encompass her and the front of her house. Blinking, she witnessed a Lune of a kind she had never seen before. Brilliant silver armour, pure white wings, and a voice like chiming bells that made her ears ache and bleed, it praised her for her triumph over the twisted Magath. Regan felt the blood running down the left side of her neck begin to hiss and sizzle, branding in her first Purity Renown. Across town, Aed too woke momentarily from his slumber to receive the same gift, as the hooked blades of the visiting Lune bit into his chest and began to extend his existing brands.

        Aed woke unusually early the next morning, thanks to the early night, and decided to make his way to Regan's home. It was a Friday, so Regan was busy getting ready for school and failing badly in dodging Steph's question about the odd silver light that had come in through the windows the night before. She was saved by Aed's knock on the door, though when he and Steph started making eyes at one another she quickly wished her previous predicament would return. She made it clear to Aed that she wanted to get the pack together after school and slipped away while they were making breakfast plans. Aed took Steph out to the best cafe he could find, and they had a nice meal before she had to leave for work.

        Regan's plan was to head up to the Old Man's court, in the hopes that he would be willing to teach her a new Gift. She wanted to get a good present for him, and they ended up at the local music store shopping for some tapes he would like. Staring at the shelves, the two of them came up with a brilliant idea. Aaron was champing at the bit to go after the Scream of Slaughter, and they'd seen how noise affected the Suthilu hybrid during their last fight. Aed started buying all the amplifiers he could get his hands on, and Regan sought out the loudest and angriest music she could find. They then realised that Aed's car didn't physically have the space for everything they'd purchased, so he had to leave Regan minding the gear while he ducked inside to use the store's phone to call Lizzie and get her to bring her band and their van around to transport everything. On the drive back to Aed's house, careful negotiations between him and Lizzie lead to the Harbingers securing (probably) the use of the van when they needed it to move the equipment to go on the attack.

        With evening approaching, they gathered Aaron and slipped into the Shadow through the mirror locus. The Shadow, brighter in the weeks since the New Year's Eve party, seemed quiet and at ease as they headed up the mountain slopes. The mountain's eye stood open, allowing them to pass through the passage of chains and into the Old Man's court. There were fewer Broken around than they recalled, but the Old Man himself seemed healthier and without the fever-like heat he'd exuded in previous encounters. He greeted Regan warmly, and she presented him with a boombox playing some of the music she'd selected. He took the device and hugged it close to his chest, allowing the vibrations to channel straight into his torso. He stood there, eyes closed, until the first song stopped. Afterwards, he listened to Regan's request that he gift her with mastery of the elements. Only a token gathra was required. The tribute was paid, and he had her turn around and remove her shirt. Then, lifting her in one hand, he carved a mangled wound into her back. The new channels left Regan breathless, but it was like she could feel the entire world feeding into her through that wound. The air itself seemed to respond to her as she let the wound knit itself close.

        While they were there, Aaron asked the Old Man if he had sensed any Wounds opening up in their territory. The Old Man was curt with Aaron, clearly still remembering the smell of the Wolf in Golden Chains upon him, and told him that he had no knowledge of any such happenings.

        When they returned to Aed's home, just past dinnertime, the pack agreed to bring down the Scream of Slaughter as fast as they could. Regan sent a message, using her rite to dispatch a minor spirit, off to their new contact, Wade. The pack had the smart idea to ask him if he had noticed any particular congregations of bats underground. Then they pulled out the explosives from the attack on the New Year's party and tried to work out what they could about where the Suthilu hybrid could possibly have got them from. The device was clearly homemade, but it was Regan's keen nose that noted that there was the barest trace of something sweet undercut with something fouler, like flowers gone to rot.

        The meeting with Wade also went smoothly. In return for a dinner with Aed in Andersonstown, the sewer wolf told them that bats had been unusually nesting in the sewers beneath Belfast. They were all focussed in Andersonstown, but at their strongest near a cluster of backstreets over in the eastern part of town owned by the Silent Sons. By this point it was past ten in the evening, but Aaron put in a call to Iule to get permission to cross into the Silent Sons' territory.

        Iule answered the call sounding faintly out of breath and wanting to know what the emergency was, and was a little impatient when Aaron explained the situation to her. She felt her pack would have no problem reciprocating the access the Harbingers had already offered to them for a hunt, and promised to talk to the rest of her pack in the morning. Aaron wanted to keep her on the line to find out what she, as a Hunter in Darkness herself, knew about the Bat Hosts, but Iule fobbed him off saying she might remember something in the morning. Aaron hung up the phone. Only then, seeing the smirk Regan was wearing after overhearing the conversation, did it slowly dawn on him what had been going on down the other end of the line.

        The Harbingers decided to take the night to scout out their prey. They invoked the Siskur-Dah, and then worked with Hope and Regan to bind their totem into the Irraka. Regan would head into Andersonstown on her own, to see if she could locate the Scream's lair. She shifted into Urhan and slipped away into the darkness.

        Calling on all the Gifts at her disposal, Regan moved like a shadow. Unseen by the humans still on the street, she closed in on the blocks Wade had indicated as the best to search. The local pub, the Crossed Staves, was doing a roaring trade into the night. She was trying to find a scent amidst the rubbish out the back when she heard booted feet on the street beyond. Shifting back into Hishu, and invoking her gifts to shift her gender and obfuscate her identity a little, Regan entered the pub. He could see soldiers passing out the front of the building, an army patrol sweeping the streets. His gaze was drawn to the second level of the building though, when the faintest trace of a scent he recognised crossed his nose. Moving up, Regan could see a small man in a dirty green jacket sitting by the pub's window. He was wiping condensation away from the window, and the armpits and neckline of his jacket were soaked with sweat. He had a small clicker in his hand, and appeared to be counting the soldiers as they passed. Most importantly of all, however, Regan could smell that faintly rotten floral scent about him, stronger than what he'd detected on the explosives. Much stronger, in fact, it could only be hours old.

        Rather than confront the man, Regan waited for the soldiers to pass and then slipped out of the pub. He returned to Urhan and sought out the scent of the man in the green jacket, and tried to retrace his steps. It wasn't difficult, not with the senses at Regan's disposal. The smell led to a small backstreet called Orchardville Crescent. Only four houses actually stood on the street, with a butcher's place on the corner. The second house, with white walls and a lavender roof, was the source of the smell. Regan tried to see if he could hear anything coming from inside, but there were no sounds from the front. He circled around the side to get to the back, and listened again. This time he could hear a sound uncomfortably like a blade striking meat, and then heavy footfalls coming towards the backdoor. Regan froze, paralysed by indecision, and was still sitting there when the door opened and a grossly overweight woman came into view.

        There had to be something supernatural about this woman. She was so fat, so incredibly burdened with an excess of flesh, that there was no way her bones should have been able to support her body. Yet she moved with speed and grace, easily maneuvering her titanic form through the door as she smiled down at Regan. Uncomfortably, Regan saw that the woman's pupils had spilled and run like ink, forming a cloudy stain across her irises and whites.

        "Come on in, pet," the woman said, and returned inside.

        Regan shifted back into Hishu. He took a few careful steps forward, peering in through the door. He could see a large room, a combined kitchen and dining room. There were large mirrors dominating every wall, and small ones right down to the size of hand mirrors propped up in the corners of the room so that there was nowhere to stand without casting a reflection. He could see himself in those mirrors, looking a decade older and female with a nasty scar running from the right side of his lip and down his neck, pulling his features into a frown. The woman he'd followed was speaking beneath her breath, and he could hear that she was crooning to something unseen and saying, "Don't worry, I don't think they want to hurt you. You are not their prey."

        Regan asked the woman who she was talking to, and she asked the Voices to come forward. Spirits emerged from hiding places around the room, crawling out from behind the mirrors, from the pantry and corners of the kitchen, and from beneath the dining table. Looking at that table more closely, Regan could see that it was covered in a lace table cloth and with an odd veil set up around one end, so that someone could sit behind the veil without being seen by whoever else was seated at the table. A pile of crystals sat just in front of the veil itself.

        The spirits the woman had beckoned forth looked almost human, but thin and stretched and drawn out. The woman called them Voices, and said they were manifestations of despair that the Harbingers themselves had created by shutting down the mine. But then, rather than take responsibility for their creations, they had started to starve the poor dear by fighting back against the community's hopelessness. She'd taken them little ones in before they starved, just as she had done with lesser spirits before them.

        Regan was startled to find out that the woman knew who he was. When he asked, the woman looked at him, smiled, and said that the spirits she had reared often went on to do great things. She liked to keep an eye on those who left her side, just in case, and she was so proud of Hope to see her making something of herself. If Hope and Regan weren't sharing a body right at that instant, Regan would very much have liked to give the Totem a glare.

        Feeling both a bit creeped out and a bit more confident that the woman wasn't a threat, however, Regan explained that he was here hunting the Scream of Slaughter. The woman said she recognised the name, and by her expression and tone it was clear that she was not a fan. Regan dug a little, and was told, "He took a friend from my Tommy. Nasty little brutes, these hosts." Emboldened, Regan asked for help locating the hybrid. The woman considered him for a long time, and then approached him and placed a heavy arm around his thin shoulders.

        Leading the werewolf out to the backyard, the woman approached a large tree bare of leaves for the winter. She placed her other hand against the trunk, and whispered in Regan's ear. "Now, don't tell the others about this. Your lot don't always take kindly to duguthim." As she spoke, small black flowers began to bud all over the tree's branches. They grew and grew, petals spreading out and sprouting many smaller petals of their own. The stem lengthened and hardened, and the shape of the flowers flowed. Regan realised then that he was staring at a flock of crows, grown out of the tree, and a sinking feeling took hold in his gut as he realised just how powerful this Claimed must be.

        The crows took flight. Telling Regan that the flock would be their eyes, the woman led Regan back into the house, and closed the door against the cold behind her.

                Session 19

                In a small home in the dead of winter, a Werewolf named Regan and a Claimed named Adele drank tea and ate biscuits while they waited for a score of hunting crows to find their prey. Regan learned many new things about his new friend. She worked as a fortune teller, a fact which grew more ominous as he came to understand the nature of the creature sitting across from him. Her essence had long since seeped into the mirrors among which she made her home. The visions seen in them, of an older Regan scarred and haunted, were a manifestation of that essence. His eager questioning revealed slowly that Adele's spirit-half had once been a mighty spirit of ill fortune, an oracle whose hands plucked at the darkest and most rotten strands of the future's web of fate. So when she suggested that Regan stay in school, Regan certainly listened... albeit not sure whether the advice was for his benefit or Adele's.

                At last, Adele's eyes began to glaze. Her crows had found the hosts squatting in an old clubhouse not far from where they were sitting. She relayed the information to Regan, who took the opportunity to excuse himself from the rather nerve-wracking confines of the powerful Claimed's home. He slipped into the darkness and took off, shifting into Urhan as he drew as close to the clubhouse as he dared. The low structure was boarded up, the door sealed, and there wasn't a single light on inside. In fact, scanning the street, Regan noted that every street lamp had been smashed, and that bats clung to the poles. None of the creatures appeared to take notice to the Irraka in their midst, but it was clear that Adele was not lying about this being the source of the Suthilu. Slinking as close as he dared, Regan noted a large hole in the roof facing away from the main street, and a heavy tarp bundled to one side that could be used to close off the rooftop exit.

                Regan returned to his packmates to share what he'd learned. They disbanded for the night (with Regan using his gifts to return to being a girl), and returned the next morning to lay their plans. Aaron was meticulous, pouring over every public record he could get about the clubhouse and its surrounding area. They mapped out the sewers, worked out where the Suthilu had most likely breached into the sewers (because, thanks to Wade, they were certain there would be a sewer exit as well), and took the daylight hours to scope out the streets.

                Sunday morning was when they decided to make their play. It would be daylight outside, but there would be fewer people in the streets while mass was taking place. They loaded the van borrowed from Lizzie's band with the amplifiers and boomboxes Aed had purchased. When they reached the clubhouse, they made sure the surrounding area was clear before Aed hoisted the massive, weighty amps onto his shoulders and launched himself into the air. He landed on the roof with a muffled thump, and placed the equipment there. Regan followed in his wake, carrying the rest of the equipment on her back, but without Aed's unnatural strength she had to scale the side of the building by hand. All seemed quiet inside, and she reached the roof and took up her perch beside the tarp. Aed had bought her, and Aaron, digital watches to synchronise so that everyone's part in the plan could be coordinated to perfection, so she settled down to wait.

                The two older men then descended into the sewers. They had to retreat a few streets over to find the first manhole cover, but they slipped underground without being seen. They didn't have to retrace their steps far before they started to encounter the sleeping bats. Step by step they crept until they were directly beneath the clubhouse, and sure enough they found a crude hole torn through the basement to allow the Suthilu to move underground unseen during the day. And there they settled in to wait, letting the last few minutes tick past. Bats carpeted the floor and ceiling around them, the air reeked of guano with a sharper, rotten stench coming from above, but they held their position until the appointed time.

                Crooned, garbled voices drifted down to them while they waited. The words were in the first tongue, but Aaron could have almost believed it was a hymn. The voices themselves were coarse and slurred the words badly at times, as though speaking with mouths ill-equipped for language. The service, or whatever it was, was just beginning to wind down when three digital watches began to beep.

                Music roared to life, flooding down the hole in the roof and echoing throughout the entire building as Regan kicked every part of the assembled equipment into overdrive. The bats in the sewers beneath stirred and woke, squealing in confusion. The confusion turned to fear a second later as Aaron and Aed lit up the flares they'd carried down with them. They pushed forward, not wanting to get distracted by the Suthilu's attendants, leaving a burning flare behind them to cover their backs. They stepped up into the basement and froze when they spotted three crude, homemade bombs sitting alongside duffel bags not unlike those the Scream of Slaughter had used during its last attack. Aaron opened his mouth, ready to suggest they leave the flare behind rather than carry the sparking stick past the bombs, but Aed pushed forward regardless. By pure luck, and certainly no skill, Aed kept any sparks from touching the bombs.

                (OOC: Rolled a chance die, got a 10. We all laughed. Everyone tried not to think about how close they'd just come to blowing themselves up.)

                And then they were through. The main room of the building was completely dark, save for the light of their flares. They could make out five larger figures in the room, turning at their approach. Three were immense Suthilu hybrids and one, the smallest, they recognised by its colouration and scars as the Scream of Slaughter. A larger hybrid had it pinned to the wall and was snarling in its face. Alongside them were two men, nails and lips torn and bitten to shreds, who were standing over a pile of bite-ridden bodies. And clinging to those bodies, fur matted with blood and bloated from its gorging, was a single Xikaba host. The familiar stench of the Herald reached Aed's nose.

                The Scream reacted first, and acted true to its name. A high-pitched cry split the air, making Aaron's head pound and his ears bleed. Deafened, he was voiced to shift into Garou immediately to keep on his feet. He charged straight for one large hybrid still standing on its own, and the two great beasts slammed into one another with a heavy thud. Claws and fangs ripped and tore into each other's flesh as they each sought to overwhelm the other.

                The Herald took the opportunity to take wing. The light from the Harbingers' flares only lit about half the room, and the Suthilu took its opportunity to escape. Its flight was erratic, weighed down by its feast and and disorientated by the cacophony of noise filtering down from the roof, but when it went beyond the reach of the flare's light it vanished into the darkness. The larger hybrid holding Scream seemed to react to the presence of Garou wolves and acted on instinct, fleeing in the wake of the Herald in the same manner.

                The two human hosts tried to break past Aed, one rushing him and trying to barge him out of the way. The Cahalith stood his ground, shifting into Urshul and seizing the running man's thigh in his jaws. Her mind teetered on the edge of the Death Rage as she hurled herself at the leader of the wolf pack, but he was startlingly prepared for her attack. He dodged every blow with consummate ease, never quite where her talons sought to land, ducking and weaving and letting her drive him back until she was flanked by his fellow soldiers. Bullets shattered harmlessly across her chest as the fourth soldier opened fire with his assault weapon, the bullets that punched through the stone forming across her skin simply pushed out moment's later.

                                                            Behind her, Braghdar had launched himself at Aed. The large Broken seemed intent on incapacitating the Cahalith before he, too, could take the war form, but Aed was prepared for the attack. He seized the Broken's forearm and forced its away from his chest, and then unleashed a roar from the depths of his chest. Enforced by the might of his Renown, the echoes of the command cracked the stone of the quarry and sent the Harbingers' attackers staggering away. "Run," he told them. "Run back to your world." And the humans obeyed, Braghdar close on their heels. Orla kept herself under control, claws twitching and teeth gnashing, until the last soldier had vanished through the fissure in the stone. Then, calling on the balance in her Half-Moon soul, she relinquished the brimming rage and shifted back to the Hishu.

                                                            The two wolves retreated back to where they had left Aaron, and relayed the story. Aaron took it as an opportunity to lay into Orla for starting the fight and for breaking the Oaths in revealing herself to humans, though Aed came to her defence. She hadn't started the fight, just reacted faster than he had when it became clear the humans were going to attack. And besides, the humans were working with a spirit and understood the term "Uratha". She probably hadn't done any harm through her actions.

                                                            The pack retreated back to Aed's home and the Locus there, shifting back across the Gauntlet. Aed got in contact with Regan and managed to persuade her, however reluctantly, to try and summon her grandfather Reynard so that they could ask some questions about ancient weapons capable of sundering the Gauntlet. Aaron, for his part, waited his turn to contact Iule and let her know what had happened. He tried to follow up with Arthur to see if the Wolfblooded constable had experienced any more issues with the Murder Crow or other spirits, but Arthur wasn't at home. Or, well, he didn't pick up the phone if he was.

                                                            Regan's message had asked Reynard to come that evening, so the pack holed up at Aed's home to watch movies and drink. Reynard didn't show up, and when the night devolved into a drinking contest between Aed and Lizzie the others took their chance to slip away. When Aed woke the next morning, however, he woke to Reynard knocking on his door. The old man apologised for not being able to make it the previous evening, and Aed made a few quick calls to the rest of the pack to summon them. The two men chatted at the kitchen table while they waited, and Aed learned that Reynard was very proud of the recent academic achievements of a granddaughter named Maddy. Regan's half-sister, he assumed.

                                                            When the entire pack had arrived and seated at the table, Aed explained what they had learned about the tear in the Gauntlet and the activities of these humans. He asked Reynard what he knew about such weapons. Reynard was happy enough to help, explaining that legends of such weapons abounded. They had most likely been made in ancient times, not long after the fall of Pangaea. The packs that would later become the Pure, those that longed for the lost world, had sought out rites and magics that could breach the new divide between worlds and let them reclaim what had been lost. The weapon they'd mentioned was surely a Fetish from that time. He didn't know where it might lie today for certain, but his research on the subject indicated that deep within the Spirit Wilds around Shankill there lay a vault of some kind. The spirits refused to speak of what it contained, but his efforts to track the old weapons had never turned up anything more promising than the vault. The Balelight Howl kept a watch in the area, and he was certain there was some kind of cache of ancient artefacts contained inside.

                                                            While Aaron was interested in learning all he could about the weapon (his constant interruptions earning a short demand from Reynard that he be allowed to tell his story first), Aed was less concerned. As he pointed out, the weapon was only useful if they wanted to make more holes in the Gauntlet, and that was not their intention. He asked Reynard how to go about closing a breach in the Gauntlet like the one he described. Reynard hesitated, and then mentioned the Azlu. Most of the pack were unfamiliar with the Spider Hosts, so Reynard regaled them with the story of the Spinner Hag and their ability to thicken the Gauntlet. It would be a dangerous gambit, compelling an Azlu to repair the Gauntlet, but they were the only creatures Reynard could think of that might have the ability to repair the breach. He offered the help of the Kallakalat, claiming that Joseph was an expert in the Hosts and would be able to locate one and drive it in their direction if need be, but the offer was shot down. Aaron seemed a bit put out after Reynard snapped at him, and told him that the Harbingers didn't need the help of a Hunter in Darkness who had allowed the Suthilu to run rampant for so long. Regan, who had been quiet throughout most of her grandfather's visit, was delighted to see her packmates turning on him. When Aaron finished by claiming that they didn't need help from anyone, Reynard offered a polite smile, said his goodbyes, and left. As Aed was showing him out, the Wolf-blooded turned to him and asked that if they did uncover the vault he had mentioned, could they please let him know in return for his help? He was certain there were great things to discover there. Aed gave him his word, and the loremaster of the Kallakalat headed off into the bright Sunday morning.
