                    Originally posted by Barbrarilen View Post

                    Mmmyesss? >.> intrigued.

                    I am imagining totem-building to be a mini-game, like building a mance or hallow, where you decide its strengths? I love subsystems like that. Then again, I also save-scum in Civilization 5 so I can get as many ancient ruins as possible, and let the clock run dangerously low in x-com so I can build my base just-so...
                    Well, you've seen the Totemic Empowerment Rite in the Rites preview, as one of the new mechanical interactions with the totem. The other one, the Facet, is... kinda the opposite, in many ways.

                    Totem-building isn't much of a mini-game; you do get to build the totem, which is a spirit, and chose what benefits you gain from it. I hope we have a chance in a future book to do a much more detailed and intricate system, but the strength of the new version compared to 1e's totem rules is that they're simpler and more easily allow for the development of the totem itself - unlike 1e where raising totem stats was painfully expensive.

                    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                    So, Lunacy, how's that different?
                    Lunacy does work off Conditions, as others have suggested, and there are some Facets that let you modify the Conditions you inflict (like the Rage Facet I spoiled that allows you to apply the Berserk Condition to victims, leaving them in a furious rage with problems telling friend from foe).

                    Lunacy also no longer works as a rough 'You must have THIS much Willpower to be a threat to a werewolf' gatekeeper that the old one did. Being strong-willed still helps, but just because you've inflicted Lunacy on humans doesn't mean they're no threat. Lunacy has more varied and interesting outcomes now. There are OTHER reasons for not going round hurling Lunacy all over the place than just 'someone might be strong-willed enough to remember what they've seen.'

                    - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                            Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                            As too returning to spoilers... We read about Primal Urge, got some answers on Lunacy, sooo...
                            Maybe some info on Siskur-Dah?
                            Things we've revealed about Siskur-Dah:
                            - It's a rite.
                            - The original text revealed in the rites spoiler has now changed. As well as the ability to eat spirits for Essence/gain Gifts or Rites from them, there is now a specific benefit given to the pack that depends on the Tribe of the ritemaster. These specific benefits lean towards the general type of prey that the Tribe are focused on. The 'can hit ephemeral entities' is the Bone Shadow one. There are four others, one for each Forsaken Tribe.

                            Ghost Wolves do not get a specific benefit. Currently I have not written up rules for the Siskur-Dah of the Pure Tribes as that's a little more involved given their different style of prey and, in particular, is highly relevant for the Predator Kings - something to be covered in a post-release web blog or supplement, I think.

                            The Siskur-Dah rite applies the Sacred Hunt Condition to all participants. It's this that contains the Tribe-specific benefit, and another particular benefit as well (you can probably guess what this other benefit is).

                            Originally posted by reseru View Post
                            So, to stay on topic and not see this turn into an image thread again, what's a Harmony 10 Primal Urge 10 Werewolf like? Being the perfect hunter doesn't help when you don't hunt and have difficulty shapeshifting at all
                            Damn, I'm not sure. Maintaining Harmony 10 for any length of time as Primal Urge 10 would be really, really difficult. You'd have to be absolutely dedicated to aping humanity to an extreme, almost to a caricature, given how hard you'd have to press the breaking points towards human. You also have the fundamental problem that you can't actually get any sustenance without hunting.

                            Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post

                            Edit: Also, recently we had a bevy of gifts spoiled. Any odds on seeing more Rites or Merits?
                            Poke Stew and see if he relents

                            Originally posted by nofather View Post
                            I was thinking before, since gifts tend to be associated with certain choirs (or am I mistaken?), would it make sense for there to be sort of stand-alone gifts, similar to the goblin contracts of Changeling?
                            Spirits can't bestow Gifts that aren't associated with what they represent; a spirit of bliss can't impart the Gift of Rage, for example. Wolf Gifts are 'standalone' in that they are inherent to Uratha.

                            Because of the way Gifts interface with the spirit and Essence of the Uratha, they always come in a set of five Facets, each associated with one of the Renowns.

                            Originally posted by Ursufgutha Storm-Born View Post
                            About Rites... Are people looking to learn them required to seek out Uratha mentors, or could they be acquired from spirits as well? This is because as far as I remember, Rites were passed down from Uratha to Uratha; this was one of the things supposed to make them special (and I thought it was a nice touch myself). Just wondering if this has changed.
                            Rites can be taught by both Uratha and spirits. A lone wolf can acquire new rites without ever meeting another werewolf; they can travel the Shadow, hunt down spirits to demand their secrets, and observe how the laws of the Shadow interact to reveal the rites that underlie them.

                            Originally posted by Red 9 View Post
                            Has there been any word on how Fetishes have been altered in the new system?
                            Probably the biggest change re fetishes is the role talens play.

                            Previously, they just held the 'one-shot magic item' slot, without much guidance about what sort of power level they should have. They also lacked a good framework for their acquisition; for something so minor, you still had to fully bind spirits into talens, a significant step to take.

                            The new talens have 1-5 dots with according effects, largely focusing on two particular types of power. You can still do the old-style random grab bag of miscellaneous stuff (like the bullets that turned into snakes) but the core talen rules have a much stronger focus on the main powerset they provide. They also offer a clearer method of getting talens (you don't need the Fetish Rite for it) that offers more interaction with the denizens of the Shadow, the politics therein, and the pack-to-pack economy of bartering fetishes, talens and favours.

                            We offer a small number of sample fetishes and talens from 1 dot all the way to 5 dots. I'm pretty sure it's an entirely new list of items; I don't think I ended up copying any of the 1e corebook's selection.


                            - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
