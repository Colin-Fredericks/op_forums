            Well, Lion came to mind in part because it is one of the oldest represented animals in cave paintings and myth. Even the pairing of bull and lion (depicting the death of bull) is a widespread symbol. Sumerian representations used it, and fixed the zodiac for Bull such that Sun would pass through it during the vernal equinox (spring) and the zodiac for Lion such that Sun would pass through it during the summer solstice.

            This is all to say: you're not wrong, whichever interpretation you want to take for the Sun. It could most accurately be said that Bull represented life and the Lion, war or death. Various sources (and I'm doing a deeper dive as I'm typing) speculate that the motif is perhaps as old as that of the Great Goddess and predates Sumerian civilization by thousands of years. Several (linked I assume) articles assert that "In the symbolic language of Sumer, the lion is the great solar animal." --That's a pretty strong assertion that I haven't found the JSTOR material to substantiate.

            The point is that Lion is important, and I'm a little surprised he hasn't come up in Pangaean discussion so far.

            And yes, if you go further east, the Chinese replaced Lion with Tiger in the symbolism of rulership animals, just as the terrain of the animals' ranges replaced each other. (Chinese "lions" look more like a kind of dog breed, than they do actual lions.)

            --Khanwulf
