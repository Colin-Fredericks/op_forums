    Reworking Werewolf Regeneration

    09-23-2021, 09:59 AM
    I'm looking at tweaking a few aspects of Werewolves in 2e based on several years of play experience.

    One of the first areas I want to focus on is their regeneration. Werewolf regeneration got a fairly hefty boost in second edition, with the amount of bashing healed per turn now scaling with Primal Urge (instead of fixed at one), the ability to heal an equal amount of lethal damage for one essence, and the incredible power of Gauru form to heal all lethal and bashing each turn.

    Although Werewolves are on paper much better at regeneration in 2e than in 1e, some of the 2e changes have counteracted this, and I think their regeneration needs to be tweaked to take these changes into account.

    Out of Combat

    There are several things here that I don't think should be changed. I think it is a good decision for the splat as a whole to not allow any kind of agg regeneration in combat. This keeps silver and other banes suitably scary for Werewolves.

    I also think the passive out-of-combat healing speeds for Werewolves do not need any serious adjustment. Healing one lethal every 15 minutes seems about right for a Werewolf who isn't doing anything extra to speed their healing up.

    In Combat

    The biggest issue here is that Werewolves' passive regeneration only addresses bashing damage. In 1e, there were many sources of bashing damage both in and out of combat. Lethal damage was reserved for powerful abilities, and weapons were partly balanced based on whether they dealt bashing or lethal damage.

    In 2e though, lethal damage is much more common. All weapons now do lethal, as do many more abilities. In fact, I can't ever remember taking bashing damage in combat in 2e, despite playing in 2 chronicles over 5 years. With rare exceptions, such as characters with ballistics armour or supernatural abilities like Vampire's damage reduction, all combat damage in 2e is lethal.

    This makes Werewolves' natural regeneration (outside of Gauru form and spending essence, which I'll get to) rarely come up in combat, which doesn't seem right. Werewolves should be able to leverage their regeneration in combat to deal with lethal damage.

    The way Werewolf 2e addresses this is by giving Werewolves the ability to spend essence to heal and this is where I think 2e makes one of its biggest missteps. Werewolves can spend an essence, and instead of healing bashing damage that turn, they instead heal an equal amount of lethal damage. I'm pretty sure the idea here is to give Werewolves cheap healing - if the Werewolf normally regenerates 3 bashing per turn then they get 3 lethal healed for just 1 essence, which is very efficient compared to other splats.

    The issue though is that Werewolves can't exceed their natural healing rate. So while a Blood Potency 3 Vampire can heal up to 3 lethal per turn for a cost of 3 Vitae, a Primal Urge 3 Werewolf cannot heal 3 lethal in one turn, no matter how much essence they have. They would instead need to spend 3 essence over 3 turns, which is patently worse than the Vampire's healing ability. Werewolf regeneration is one of their iconic abilities and so they should at least be on par with most of the other splats.

    One of the reasons this doesn't work is that the way Werewolf healing scales with Primal Urge is unbalanced. Werewolves heal 1 bashing per turn at PU 1 which increases all the way to 6 bashing per turn at PU 10. However this doesn't scale linearly.

    I picked PU 3 in the example because I think this is where the scaling is at its worst. Werewolves only start healing 2 bashing per turn at PU 4 and 3 bashing per turn at PU 6. This makes the chart ridiculously top-heavy. A Werewolf at Primal Urge 5 probably has some of the worst in-combat healing of any splat at that power level.

    In general I don't think that good regeneration should be restricted to Werewolves at the higher levels of Primal Urge simply because I have never seen a player character exceed 5 in a power stat in any game, even in my Vampire game where the passing of centuries and occasional diablerie has given them free boosts to Blood Potency. This is especially true in Werewolf since in 5 years I never once saw a player buy a single dot in PU, much less 5+ dots.

    Conclusion

    The effect of the situation above is that players use Gauru form as the primary means of healing in Werewolf. This both cheapens the normal regeneration that Werewolves are supposed to always possess, and makes the killing form feel rather silly since it gets used in every combat not to deal the killing blow, but to recover after taking a face full of lead. Players will even do things like shift into Urshul first so that they can fill as many health boxes with lethal as possible before cashing in on the regeneration provided by Gauru form.

    Solutions

    There are a number of ways I have considered solving the issues above:

    1. Fix regeneration scaling with Primal Urge

    This one is fairly straightforward. The obvious solution here is to make the amount of bashing healed per turn equal to Primal Urge, so at PU 4 a Werewolf would heal 4 bashing per turn, and for 1 essence they could heal 4 lethal instead. One of the nice things about this solution is that it makes Werewolves' per-turn healing ability keeps pace with Vampires while remaining much cheaper.

    However I'm not sure I like how much of a buff this is to Werewolf regeneration. At the low end it makes bashing damage in combat almost irrelevant, but I don't mind that too much since bashing is already irrelevant to Werewolves in most combats. However, even being able to heal 4 or 5 lethal for a single essence would make low PU Werewolves much tougher.

    It also makes high PU Werewolves nearly impossible to kill in any form without Silver. This concerns me a little bit more. Would high PU Werewolves eschew the Killing Form? It has numerous other bonuses so perhaps not. However I still don't like the idea that something like an Idigam would have a really hard time killing a Hishu form Werewolf if their Primal Urge was near the maximum.

    If I was going to implement this, I think healing 10 bashing damage per turn at PU 10 is a little silly. Instead, I think I would simply make PU 10 Werewolves heal all bashing every turn, which would mean for 1 essence they could heal all lethal, which would be a nice capstone.

    2. Give Werewolves the ability to heal/downgrade lethal naturally without spending essence

    I don't like this since it would mean also buffing Werewolf healing out of combat, and I think out of combat Werewolf healing is exactly where it needs to be.

    3. Give Werewolves damage reduction like Vampires

    This is nice because it lets Werewolves keep their regeneration as only applying to bashing, but I don't think it's appropriate for Werewolves. Werewolf already has merits and a few Gift Facets to make them tougher. The only splats that get this as a natural ability are Vampires and Mummies, and in both cases it's due to their undead nature - which Werewolves most certainly are not.

    4. Replace the essence spend effect on Werewolf regeneration

    I think this is the solution I like best. Instead of spending one essence and healing an amount of lethal damage determined by their Primal Urge, the Werewolf would spend one essence to downgrade all lethal damage to bashing.

    This is nice for several reasons. One is that it is a very-cost effective way to heal. I don't think any other splat has a natural healing ability that is this cheap.

    The second is that it is not much of a change to the power level of Werewolves. It ends up being a bit of a buff at the low end and a slight nerf at the high end. It also preserves the value of Gauru form since it becomes the only way to instantly heal a large amount of lethal.

    The third is that is preserves the value and the flavour of the Werewolf's natural per-turn bashing regeneration. Now you first downgrade lethal to bashing by spending essence, and then your natural regeneration heals it over a few turns. I would probably also tweak the per-turn regeneration of werewolves here, so that the amount healed per turn goes up with every second dot of PU (i.e. 2 of PU 3, 3 at PU 5 etc.).

    I'm also fond of splats having unique ways of accomplishing the same goal. Most splats can spend their essence equivalent to heal, but none of them do it quite like this.

    I'd be very interested to hear if anyone has thoughts on the above or has come up with alternative solutions.

      I feel like before I can warrant any acceleration of regeneration, I kind of have to ask questions about how the game is being played, because even for as violent a game as Werewolf can be...it seems like there's a weird approach to violence at work here that might be skewing with the effects.

      First off, the three tiers of damage are here for an escalation of tension, and the mention of rarely coming across bashing tells me that the two chronicles are much more bloodthirsty than the average game. Violence usually comes about as failure of all other solutions to a scene, or as a expedient solution for those who can adroitly use it to their benefit (ie, is it going to be easier to get in by punch out the guards versus sneaking past or lying your way past them somehow? for some, it is.) and most people are less interested in murder and more interested in just getting someone out of the way. Lethal comes by and large as a deterrent or as a way of forcing choices (do you go after someone or stop your comrade from bleeding out, as a basic one), usually to facilitate one side getting to do that thing they want to do*. Aggravated by and large is there when either murder is the point, or both sides are playing with absolute stakes, really and truly "Do or die" situations

      How splats heal alters the effect of this in regards to their themes and motifs to the effect of both the horror they portray and the horror they experience. In the case of werewolf, basic regeneration of bashing is there to basically get the pleasantries out of the way and emphasize the horror of the Uratha. For Lethal, that shifts a little-a werewolf can become even more at home with the violence of their lives at the cost of leaning harder into their spiritual aspects. a Werewolf who's constantly relying on quick healing lethal is going to spend more time at the Loci, in the Shadow hunting prey spirits, and otherwise doing things that drive them away from humanity, with the ultimate expression of that being pressuring the Uratha to waste their precious time of killing form and risk Rage. A werewolf can, of course, pump the breaks and let their still advanced healing do the work, and that may even appeal to their Bone Anchor, but has the consequences of messing with the hunt and pushing them away from their spiritual side.

      All of which is to say "how much of the conflict is being played really build tension and express monstrosity, and how often is a moment of violence used to push the internal conflict of an Uratha trying to maintain Harmony?"

      The second part that builds on the first one is that Werewolves are scapels, not swords. They tend towards skirmishes rather than outright tanky combat. In this light, while a werewolf might take a big hit, a lot of times, that large hit is not going to be so important that they can't hang back and heal up-again, as the long term hunt of harrying and wearing down a prey and their resources goes, the werewolves have the advantage-they can always pull back, wait a bit, and then come back to find the prey only having just caught their breath to have another go at dividing them up, tiring them out, cutting off their options.

      This means that in order to create those opportunities to force the conflict, the prey needs to create situations that force the Uratha to spend more than they normally would in a moment of conflict, make a situation where sticking around and facing the conflict of sliding spiritually and/or risking Rage is the better option that harrying over time. And so my question there is "is that happening?"

      If we assume that Forsaken is being run more like a traditional combat focused game, and the goal is to make it clear that the Forsaken are Apex Predators in that arena, then yeah, the hacks work fine-but it feels to me more like there's probably misconceptions on how Forsaken runs it's violence.

      EDITL as a general rule of thumb, it's best to assume that even supernatural baddies who can handle violence better than the average human opt to try and avoid prolonged direct conflict on account of most things dying when they are killed. Even if you can walk it off, it's better when you don't have to. Most things hew in that direction.

      *with the curve thing.

                          THe first thing to keep in mind that for as often any given prey represents either a major source of imbalance in a territory, or is at least the major beneficiary of a major source of imbalance-for every one of those, there's usually prey who are up in your grill because of that imbalance. One spirit flees into the Flesh and becomes a Claimed because a shift in spirit courts made them likely prey, one swarm of shartha shows up because some Uratha or human's development project next door caused them to flee into your territory. These things might be amenable to getting back where it came from (or so help you) and backtracking to original states-if a spirit doesn't have to worry about being lunch and can instead go back to eating happily at a loci watering hole, then they don't need to Claim that human anymore, and if a shartha swarm can go back to their original den instead of being hunted down by an apex predator, it's not hard to see the cost/benefit ratio there. And for the werewolvesit generally means they get to keep ahold of any resources they might have wasted on an inferior problem while another problem just gets to build over them-and hell, it might let them get in as much of a first turn advantage against said potential future prey as possible and let them nip problems in the bud quicker than they might otherwise be. And this conversations open other conversations that might be beneficial.

                          That neatly leads into the second thing-sure, the thing you are facing down is probably an unholy abomination of flesh and spirit, but it's also an entity that has power and influence to shape your territory in ways that may be beneficial to you. Sure, the Claimed in front of your snoze isn't pretty, but gosh darnit it, wouldn't you know that it has influence over a purview that you were looking to improve upon over in the shopping district. THe Azlu definitely aren't pretty, but hey, you know, the Gauntlet is a little weak in some of these areas, and it would also be a huge boon to reshaping that one spirit court if we deepened the Gauntlet in their seat of power. A lot of prey have the ability to contribute something to your territory, and if you can bargain for that rather than trying to harry them over to a place and corral them to force them to do that work, why the hell not?

                          THird thing-so, okay, maybe not a victim of environmental shift, maybe not the best advantage for your territory. But you know what any given prey is? Another tool against your enemies, that's what. Sure, this Claimed's a butt, but the Pure next door suck more. Sure, you'll never get the Azlu out of your hair, but that one Spirit Court's really pressing their advantage. Given a choice between being hunted down and being a provisional aid in some other conflict, any given prey's likely to act on that deal because, you know-one of them is getting hunted down. It saves you resources, or augments them for really big problems.

                          Now, does this mean you can just talk to them to resolve issues? No usaully, it'll involve some conflict to get into a talking mood for some of them. Does it mean the results are always pretty? No, there's plenty of backstabbing and outmaneuvering and flanking and what not. Hell, sometimes it's a trap. And sometimes, particularly in the case of Idiam-afflicted entities, there may not actually be the recourse of discourse. The thing of the matter, though, of being an Apex predator in a world where people die when they are killed and people would rather avoid that is, given the choice being killed until dead and working with other options.....a shockingly large number of things are gonna wanna talk rather than risk the hunt. For the Uratha, it often means getting at least some information they might not otherwise have and at most gives them vital ways to affect the rivers of spiritual resonance that define their territory that would otherwise take a shit ton of work to do. It also doesn't hurt that that reputation builds up over time, which means that for every prey that wants to cut the legs out from under a werewolf, there's gonna be those who know that working with the pack has benefits and payouts, which will result in more ready allies in times of need than not. Every prey is a resource, and just hunting them is wasting a lot of potential resource.

                                              Aurumae, first, I completely support you (or anyone else) changing what works for their game, so move forward with the changes if that's what works.

                                              With that said, the issue I see with the changes are that they're based on missing a fundamental point about Werewolf: The Forsaken. Despite how tough they appear, the Uratha aren't designed to be fighters. They're hunters. Forsaken aren't the Garou. They're not warriors for Gaia who hit hard and take damage and regenerate in moments with their Rage. Uratha are thinking, pack hunting predators. They avoid getting into drawn out fights where their lethal regeneration may be slightly less than the prey they're taking down. They harry the prey, work it into a corner, take away its advantages, then they strike when it's vulnerable. They use their forms to their advantage in wearing it down, and when they strike they do it hard with Gauru form and they kill quickly while absorbing damage for a very short time.

                                              Like other pack hunters, if they can't do that they act intelligently. If one is injured, that individual falls back and the pack carries on if it's feasible. If more are injured, or if the kill in no longer feasible, they all retreat and regroup. They take the time for 15 minutes per lethal to take effect. They heal up very quickly without any additional resource expenditure, and they continue to harry the foe while it's also trying to regroup. They don't give it time to rest and recouperate, they apply the pressure until they're ready to attack again.

                                              The Uratha's Gifts and powers revolve around this concept. They keep the prey off balance and weakened. The Uratha may need to fall back to heal but they're not letting that vampire (or whatever) take its leisure to drink some blood and regain its strength. They scare off its prey, they keep humans away, they bring Gifts to bear and if it does find a victim to drain they hit it while it's vulnerable because the loss of resource is worse for the vampire than it is for them.

                                              If your several years of play experience is finding the Uratha characters falling behind because they can't just stand in a drawn out fight and always go punch-for-punch with their foes, by all means make the changes. But I'd suggest that the players need to lean more into what makes Uratha werewolves, and stop trying to play Garou werewolves. (And I acknowledge that I make this statement without first hand knowledge of how your games are structured, based purely on the information you've shared.)

                                                          Thanks for the feedback everyone.                                                           Gift of Regeneration

                                                          FEIGN WEAKNESS (CUNNING)
                                                          The Uratha stifles her regeneration to lure her prey in for the killing blow.
                                                          Cost: 1 Essence
                                                          Action: Reflexive
                                                          The Uratha sacrifices her regeneration to gain an equal number of bonus dice on her next attack. For example if she would heal 3 damage she instead gains 3 additional dice. This Facet cannot be used if the Uratha is at full health.

                                                          KNIT FLESH (GLORY)
                                                          By focusing her regeneration the Uratha can speed the healing of grievous injuries.
                                                          Cost: 1 Essence
                                                          Action: Reflexive
                                                          Upon activating this Facet the Uratha downgrades a number of points of lethal damage to bashing. The number of points downgraded is equal to 1 plus her Glory Renown.

                                                          FUSE BONE (HONOR)
                                                          With this Facet the Uratha has learned to share her regenerative abilities with her packmates.
                                                          Cost: 1 Essence
                                                          Action: Instant
                                                          By touching a packmate and using this Facet, the Uratha heals her packmate of a number of points of lethal damage equal to her Honor Renown. The Uratha may not benefit from her own regeneration on the same turn that she activates this Facet.

                                                          PRIMAL HEALING (PURITY)
                                                          This Facet draws upon the ancestral power of Urfarah, enhancing the Werewolf’s natural healing abilities.
                                                          Cost: None
                                                          Duration: Permanent
                                                          Whenever the Uratha would heal due to regeneration, she regains one additional health point. The amount of bashing healed per turn increases by one, and she heals an additional point of lethal damage every fifteen minutes, and an additional point of aggravated damage every four days.

                                                          MOTHER’S EMBRACE (WISDOM)
                                                          Under her mother’s caring gaze the Uratha finds her worst injuries are soothed.
                                                          Cost: 2 Essence
                                                          Action: Instant
                                                          In place of the Uratha’s usual regeneration, she instead downgrades a single point of aggravated damage to lethal. This Facet may only be used while the Uratha’s Auspice Moon is in the sky.
