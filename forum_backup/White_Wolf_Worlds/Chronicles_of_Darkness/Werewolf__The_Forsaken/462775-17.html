    Originally posted by Amravanti View Post
    But the rite Totemic Empowerment specifically states that 'The empowered pack member can see spirits in twilight clearly', which suggests that she couldn't before.
    That's because you can use it on non-werewolves, such as wolf-blooded and humans and vampires and whoever else is in your pack that might not ordinarily be able to see spirits in Twilight.

    Also, the Whisper Knife fetish says it 'allows an Uratha to strike entities in Twilight, but it does nothing to let the user see such beings' I'm paraphrasing for the sake of brevity, but these are both things that seem to contradict your conclusion. Errors, then you think? I'm guessing so but I just want a second opinion, I guess.
    It's probably an artifact, yes, but spirits can also attempt to hide. 'Unless the spirit is intentionally trying to hide, Uratha sense spirits in Twilight as well as they would manifested.'

    Thanks (both of you) for your answers though. Especially about the spirit motives, that really makes a lot of sense. Last bit on that - Is there a benefit to materializing over just staying in twilight? Is it just the ability to affect things? Is there a change in how spirits interact with resonant essence in either form?
    Materializing allows you to confront mortals and actually do stuff. While in Twilight Form spirits are invisible to most and can't really come into contact with anything physical except their bane. So if you want to light that match and drive that car you'll need to materialize. Though there is a Telekinesis Numen, it's incapable of fine control. Aside from the invisibility and incorporeality, I think it's the same. So if a spirit seeks common essence, like of air or cars, they might be fine just hovering in Twilight form and harvesting it free of competition. But a spirit of something more specific might require a direct action to create to a surfeit of essence. Once in the material they start bleeding essence if they aren't in an area with an appropriate condition, so finding a steady source is a priority. And no problem for the answers, it seems like one of the few rules questions I've been able to somewhat handle here.

                                    tl;dr 'Fluffwise,' what makes a facet a facet of a particular renown? Why is Memento Mori an Honor facet, while Bone Gnaw is a Purity one?

                                    The sections on renown are a little sparse in regards to the meaning of them. As I understand it, the spirit magic of gifts is not really a spirit thing. It is the magic of certain spirits as seen through the lens of the werewolf's soul, the soul of a hunter. So most facets are really built towards that and a few other things. And what you get is, with the spirit magic of a death spirit, refracted through the Cunning brands in the soul of a werewolf, an ability that allows you to use death in a cunning fashion. Wisdom gives you insight, awareness of certain things and knowledge. Glory tends to be overwhelming, big and grand or forceful. I'm not quite sure if those are right but that's how it seems just working off memory. Honor and Purity are a little more hazy for me. Is Honor more working 'within the confines' of the spirit magic? And Purity, maybe, more of the 'spirit side' of the gift?

                                    I was thinking about a second edition version of the Wound-born gift, for instance, and I figured a neat ability would be the ability to use a Wound as a locus for the purposes of Reaching, with a bonus to the dice pool based on the renown. But I couldn't figure if it would be Purity, symbolizing the Uratha's spirit side being attuned to the Wound, or Honor representing an 'agreement' with the Wound. Even Cunning seems like it could fit.

                                    I apologize if that's unclear, I know I can be that way at times.
