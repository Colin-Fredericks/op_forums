    Originally posted by Claire Redfield View Post
    The 2E Gifts are really a thing of beauty. One of the biggest complaints with Werewolf: The Forsaken was the dreaded "What do we do?" Or some variant involving spirit cops. It was charged with not really jumping out at you, nothing sinking its fangs into your throat. While I loved even first edition, second is much, much stronger. It's better designed mechanically, as is the way of the 2E games. It's much better designed thematically, as everything has been rebuilt to really give a strong focus on theme and meaning. When you read it, you'll no longer wonder just what you do with it. You'll write up a pack, get that blood pumping and those paws thumping. You'll have drama as you and your closest family follow your calling. You have lots of reason to interact with humans as well as the strange spirits across the Gauntlet. As a werewolf, both in-character and out of character, you'll get to experience a lot of different perspectives in a single character. Everything reinforces this theme, too, from Gifts to werewolf template conditions to the hunt itself.

    I can't wait to get the reactions from folks once 2E drops.
    Interesting observation, since I always considered Werewolf to be fairly intuitive with the "What do we do" question. And have enjoyed the drama unfolding (that you describe at the end of your post) from the very beginning of Forsaken. Maybe I am just odd... Maybe our whole group is just Odd... would not be the first time.

    Thanks for taking the time.

        I don't mean that it was impossible to figure out what to do, or that themes were nonexistent. I loved Werewolf: The Forsaken 1st edition as much as anyone.                 Originally posted by Tymeaus Jalynsfein View Post

                I can see and understand some of the points you bring up, to be sure.

                As for the "Penalty" for skipping Gifts in the Tree... I don't see it as such. I pay a Pittance of XP to gain something without having to go through something I do not want. In your example... If I don't want to pay for Dominance 2, I do not see the 3 XP cost as a penalty. It is a benefit to me that I get to skip something without purchasing it. It is a boon (because I get to skip purchasing something I do not want), not a penalty. The penalty would be to tell me to suck it up and just purchase Dominance 2, even though I do not want it. Fundamental Differences in Viewpoint, I guess...
                I can see the argument both ways on this. For one, I can argue that I save the experience I would have used to purchase a gift I didn't want at the cost of spending a measly 3 additional experience to purchase the one I do want. On the other side, players like me are thinking about why are we required to pay an additional tax of experience at all? If they are going to allow you to purchase dots without a prerequisite, why bother making them tiered in the first place? The new system proposed is very appealing to me since now it allows me to select the gifts I want for what is thematic, rather than realizing I need to waste 12 experience just to grab that 1 gift at the end of a tree.

                                                Originally posted by Errol216 View Post
                                                Not sure I agree. Having a pack is pretty human.
                                                Humans are social animals. Werewolves are pack animals — psychologically geared to require a hunting party, which spills out into a larger group of Wolf-Blooded and humans. The "typical" model for a werewolf pack in 2e is a gang, but that's just the easiest reference point. Think of any tight-knit group that acts as a surrogate family, from soldiers to investment bankers to Freemasons.

                                                It's a breaking point towards Flesh to run without a pack, but you can get around that by deliberately reinforcing your Uratha nature in other ways to break towards Spirit, thus giving you a chance to maintain the balance. It does make things like the Siskur-Dah harder — it's a rite, and while you can work it alone you only get your tribe's version. You also don't get the benefit of having a powerful totem. The character can make it work, though.
