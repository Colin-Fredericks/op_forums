            Originally posted by Shomaxt View Post
            Coming from Blood and Smoke to WtF 2e is a little jarring. I'd like to play it with a group before I have any final thoughts, but there seems to be a few things off in my opinion.

            First of all, the gifts and facets seem a bit disjointed in my eyes. Unlike in Blood and Smoke, where disciplines follow a clear progression, it seems more like gifts are just a bunch of assorted tricks under one theme, with only a few of them being powerful or useful. They seem interesting at first glance, but from reading a bit deeper into them they just seem mediocre.

            Second, I feel like that characters are going to be homogenized a bit. This may not be a factor in other groups, but mine seems to be "all combat, all the time, plus humor." I feel like if I do play WtF 2E with my group, we're going to get at least 2-3 Rahu Blood Talons maxing out on brawl and Strength so they can kill whatever comes their way in werewolf form.

            Third, the power level in general seems to be lower than in Blood and Smoke. It seems like Werewolves get just as many weaknesses as Vampires without the benefit of becoming super strong or super fast or being able to control minds or whatever. Werewolves seem to be reigned in far more, and I'm unsure if my group will go for that.

            I'm probably on the extreme end of the spectrum when it comes to players/groups interested in WoD, but this sort of thing should be talked about, in my opinion at least.
            That is one of the first things I want to look for two primary reasons. 1. Werewolf the Forsaken 1st edition left the Uratha in somewhat of a bad spot when compared to other game lines. While game lines are not, and should not, be balanced against each other. Even in context of just Werewolf many folks commented on how there were too many lackluster gifts. 2. On the other hand I don't want to relive Werewolf the Apocalypse and my hope is the overall potency of the splat isn't pushed too far in 2e.

            But most of all I want it to be interesting. 1st edition left me cold with it's somewhat bland tribe descriptions,etc.

                                                        To begin, I absolutely love W:tF. It's my favorite game line and will always be. I'm really glad that 2nd ed is out and i'm sure that all of my players can attest to my rampant checking of the website for any shred of an update. Thank you OP for delivering this book.

                                                        That being said, my reflection on this book is a mixed bag.

                                                        The rules update is wonderful. Many of the systems are streamlined and plenty of quality of life improvements that I think are a great addition to the game. The Gifts are great(especially with the facet system) however, there are still a few that I just don't think are worth their weight in salt or simply seem out of place (Maw of Madness I'm looking at you). I like a lot of the fun new powers that the Uratha have access to, including all of the new Auspice powers and the forms are in perfect Harmony.

                                                        Unfortunately, I'm not a fan of the new art. The full color chapter dividers don't really scream werewolf and aren’t nearly as evocative of the setting as Ron Spencer's art did in the 1st Ed book. The man is quite possibly the best artist any of the werewolf books have ever seen (including W:tA) and more of his work being featured on a full page layout would have done some good. As it is, the new art doesn’t really hold a torch to any of the previous pieces in the 1st edition books. I am glad that some of the art from the core book was reused since its just as good as the day it was released. I realize that some of artists that were originally requested were unable to make deadlines or just didn't respond and that's not Onyx Path's fault.

                                                        The emphasis on the hunt and the sheer brutality that is laced in this book is great. It gets you amped up as you read it and you can really feel the pull of a predator's instinct. I like this addition but I also feel that with this theme being so dominant it snuffs out the savage nobility and duty-bound nature that was at the forefront of the Forsaken culture in previous books. 2nd edition feels like it is trying to move away from the "Spirit Cops" feel in favor of promoting "the Hunt" which I understand but is just a shift in emphasis for me.

                                                        The community gave some great feedback over the course of this book's development and I think that we could have given even greater feedback to the authors if perhaps we would have gotten an early release similar to how V:tR did "Reap the Whirlwind". I really liked this approach and it generated new interest in V:tR for me when it was released.

                                                        All in all, I'm still glad that the book was released as I've been dying to start my game with a blank slate so, thank you very much!
