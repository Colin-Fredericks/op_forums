                Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                Sounds more like bugs than features.
                Not really. Maybe I wrote too hastly about what I feel changed about morality. I meant to say - in W:tA and partly in W:tF 1ed you didn't have a reason to break the Oath of the Moon or to do things "wrong". GM would have to really raiload you to have some moral dilemma since everything was pretty one dimensional "don't eat humans unless you want to end up dancing the black spiral", "respect hierarchy you a-hole".

                Now your characters have more dimension. Disregarding the fact that every PC has to choose at the level of character creation 1 thing that ties it to the spiritual side and 1 thing that ties it to the human/physical side as well as how he behaves off and on the hunt, now players have morality mechanics that actively influence them and determine a number of things, among others how easily it is to change shape for them or how long and strong they can resist Death Rage and if they are more suspectible to it or not. Some merits also require you to maintain certain Harmony level.

                As a result some "sins" against Harmony seem to be a fair exchange over the boon they give you.

                As I said, in W:tA and W:tF 1ed nobody had a reason to eat anyone. Now eating another werewolf might be a way to make aggrevated damage and replenish essence in battle by substantial amount and it really makes a difference, especially against stronger opponents. Same with killing someone with silver weapons - you actually feel like the risk is worth it since again, there's not many other ways in which you can do aggrevated damage to werewolf (compared to previous editions).

                If you feel your PC becomes too much inhuman spirit more and more alien to the world, you could choose to say "Enough, I don't want to hunt! I don't care what Luna wants! I can't live like this, I need time...I'm going to my ex" and effectively break the Oath of the Moon and one of the few sacred, fundamental things about werewolf life (have to hunt). Now though that change might be beneficial for you - you move on the Harmony scale towards Flesh, and meeting again with your old love effectively makes you more of a balanced individual, not so prone to rage constantly and having more control over when you shift to other form. Mechanics directly supports narration.

                Sure, you did wrong thing in the eyes of other characters. It's up to the NPCs, game world and other PCs to judge this deedm but now the problem isn't as one dimensional as before. In old editions a PC would be "Luna's traitor" and objectively evil. Now there's a clear reason for his behaviour - actually regaining a grip on his life - and everybody should understand that, especially other players since the same mechanics affects them. I don't see lots of "how could youuuuuuu" being shouted now at the gaming table even though choices might be even more dramatic. Suddenly a Lune hunting a packmate to punish him might not be right...

                %%%

                Things with nature...well, depends on what you like. You definietly could go for ecological themes too in W:tF 2ed if you wish so, but by default you don't have to. You aren't there to protect nature, but to protect your territory. You aren't nature's warrior, you are a hunter that is part of nature. You see, accents are laid out differently.

                Example: Nature's Gift in W:tF 1ed. There's talking with animals, there's controlling plant growth and suggestion that you can make a treehouse out of it (like elves), a power that makes you "one with the forest", sensing surrounding etc.

                You have this shamanic and "wise native" and nature-loving vibes from it.

                Compare it to W:tF 2ed: a power that compells a prey to enter the wild, lures it into the forest. A power that makes prey unable to escape the territory, go around the forest and always end up in the same place. A power that makes predator(s) be part of your pack, so you can run with wolves or have bloody grizzly bears aid you with your attack on police station.

                Both editions have a power that allows you to control some animal or beast.

                See the difference? In 2ed everything was about being a predator. Nature works in that way - as predatory, mysterious, savage, scary. Sure, it might be your home and something really precious to you and you can certainly put emphasis on it in your game - but it's up to you. As default, 2ed gives nature teeth - and it's fitting, since the wolf resides in the forest

                    I've always loved Apocalypse, but it's almost always been heavy with metaplot, dogmatically oppressive cultures, and plain disinterest in actual real-world mythology. This was a lingering feature of CWoD games, so it's not a specific complaint, but the Garou were probably the biggest departure of any of those monsters.

                    Not that 1e Forsaken were much better, but they were measured against their fellow inhabitants, weren't thrust into a weirdly racialized society of eco-warriors, and weren't under the thumb of the elders to fight a very real and demonstrable threat to all of existence.

                    Now 2e Forsaken are bloody fucking werewolves. They lose their shit on the regular, they hunt because they have to, and they live in fear of becoming lost to their extremes. They aren't furry superheroes, they aren't insular spirit cops treading lightly between worlds, they are primal rage monsters barely contained by the Flesh and basically trying to keep the rest of the world from becoming as messed up as themselves. They do find some beauty and revel in their existence and purpose, and the human left in them creates culture and ritual, but it's not the heart of their existence as the Garou experience it.

                    Given a choice, I would go for the Uratha over the Garou. The Garou are zealots, unmoored from human concerns, and largely unconcerned by the violence they unleash. The Forsaken live in a grayer world, with fewer allies and less clear purpose, and don't have some merciful and loving goddess to forgive their transgressions, but only the company of demanding Totems and the warped unfortunates with Wolf's blood. There's more innocents in the path of the Forsaken, forcing players into more complex solutions or much riskier violence, or both. The evil in the world isn't at the urging of an over-arching cosmic being, but the natural tendencies of humans and spirits going unchecked. At its heart, these obstacles ground Forsaken in more relatable dilemmas than which Wyrm-creature to hunt down or which hidebound elder needs to be appeased. They can still resort to violence and carnage, but it comes at a cost the militant Garou rarely consider.

                    LFP: Foreign Bodies (Deviant: the Renegades)

                        Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                        ​So, let's talk some more about Bale Hounds. Seems like the closest thing to BSDs. How much info is there on them?
                        They're only really expanded upon in the Blasphemies book. Myself and other fans are eager to see their second edition interpretation.

                        In first edition, they exist in a few different forms. They range from werewolves who have made deals with the Maeljin or their lieutenants the Maeltinet, which sort of act like demon pacts since the Maeljin are comparable to demon lords. Blasphemies offers a few more options, with you letting you choose which one is more appropriate for your game. You have the Lodge, which acts probably the most like the 'its just a pact' thing, where outside of your dealings with the Maeljin you are basically a normal werewolf in a tribe going about your business. Then you have the Dark Pantheism option, which has them as rabid servants of the Maeljin, either sworn to one or a group of them. And my favorite option, though I use all of them, was the Ninth Tribe, which has them serving one of the Firstborn and existing as another tribe entirely, keeping their affiliation secret.

                        Each of the Maeljin were associated with one of the 9 sins of werewolves. Lust, Envy, Pride, Wrath, Greed, Gluttony, Sloth, Violence and Deceit. Second edition seems to have opened this up so that there's more than first edition's 9. The Dark Pantheism option seemed to take the most violent angle with them, as they are out there tearing open Wounds and doing evil. The other options had them more as corruptors, getting others to succumb to the sins of their masters. In exchange, it is rumored they would become Maeladar, more spiritually in touch with their Maeljin master as an immortal spirit aspect of them.

                        I suppose the term Maeljin would be another artifact.

                                        Apocalypse is an adventure game, Forsaken is a horror game. It really boils down to that. Prior to Exalted 1e if you wanted a WW game of epic heros and glorious battles Apocalypse was your go to game, but while it featured werewolves it really wasn't about being werewolves, it was about being part of a warrior culture. Forsaken 2e concentrates on what it's like to be a werewolf, a hunter rather than a warrior. Between the two, even though Forsaken is a horror game it's less pessimistic in tone. I prefer that. I liked Apocalypse (I even still have Freak Legion on the shelf next to me) but it was very much a product of the 90's and my tastes have changed since then.

                                        They're just two very different experiences, highlighted most strongly by the fact that Apocalypse encourages players to stay away from their mortal family while to one of the Forsaken your family is often part of your pack and is integral to your existence. Forsaken feels a lot more personal in scope and it also seems to give the players a bit more agency.

                                            Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                                            Anything that comes across as "Forsaken is better off without that thing that made Apocalypse AWESOME" gets me upset.
                                            It shouldn't make you upset.
                                            Not anymore than saying "Zelda is better off without the thing that made Mario AWESOME." Or maybe a better analogy is "Castlevania is better off not being Metroid," because that analogy is closer.

                                            Different games made by the same company using many of the same base elements. And they're better off having their own unique flavors, their own twists, their own turns.

                                            The thing is, you come here expecting that this new edition should be closer to Apocalypse under the assumption that since Apocalypse worked in your eyes, Forsaken should be like the original game that made werewolves a solid RPG.

                                            The truth of the very matter is that Forsaken 2e is a very different game from Apocalypse... and it's still a world different from Forsaken 1e, it's immediate predacessor!

                                            The things that made Apocalypse good wouldn't work for Forsaken due to the very nature of Forsaken being so utterly different than Apocalypse. They're practically in different genres all together.

                                            You might as well be say inserting Chucky the killer doll in Toy Story 2, Mister Freeze from Batman and Robin into the Dark Knight Returns, Scooby Doo in Air Bud, or even Tarzan into Planet of the Apes and Gundams into WALL-E.
                                            Each of those elements above has something tangentally related to the other, and all of them are great characters in their own rights in their own worlds. Their inclusions however would massively warp the very nature of another setting if they were ever seriously added into them. And this is the situation that adding in elements of Apocalypse would create.


                                            So, yes. "Forsaken is better off without the thing that made Apocalypse AWESOME". In much the same way the different Batman movies are better off without elements from a different film continuity. Seriously, Adam West Batman would not be good for modern Dark Knight Films or the Arkham games.
