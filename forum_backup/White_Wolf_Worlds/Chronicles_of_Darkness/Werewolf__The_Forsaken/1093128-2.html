                                    People seem to be conflating the mantling of the Maters as Roman gods of diverse Influence with the fact that they were getting fed a bunch of spirits whose only common ground was that they were from territories that had recently been taken by Rome; there also seems to be a lot of treating spirits as abstract manifestations their portfolios going on, which, while not wholly wrong, misses a couple of key things about why magath are a problem.

                                    Spirits don't, strictly speaking, possess free will. They're the actors that collect Essence and other spiritual power in reciprocal support of one or more connected phenomena; the weakest and most isolated spirits tend to the objects and emotions that gave birth to them, while more powerful beings like the Dihir forge places for themselves out of their ability to drastically alter the world around them through their purviews. As actors born from and steered by the constituents of the natural order, they generally have concepts whose association with them makes sense and harmonizes to their nature easily; this in turn feeds their behavior in fashions that steer their impact on the world toward a coherent outcome if moderated by competing spirits or other supernatural intervention.

                                    Magath happen when a spirit chomps itself into a liminal space between categories, hence why the most prominent examples are hybrids of completely different choirs. They stand only for themselves, lacking the spiritual breadth and discretion to keep their conflicting aspects from interfering with each other — most magath are lesser nobles in the Shadow's hierarchy rather than Incarnae or Celestines, and their inability to live in isolation means they have to deal with wildly different sets of rules without the ability to pick and choose where they mesh with the rest of the world.

                                    Spirits can and do form with otherwise-unrelated Influences without being magath, but that's a byproduct of unusual circumstances, substantial power, or a lengthy period of gradual acclimation, not conscious mimicry of human mythologies — the most prominent example is the nemur sakar from Skinchangers, born from a widespread hunting practice and pretty much a bunch of abnormal specimens by definition, much like the Maters having foreign spirits funneled their way by a legendary werewolf in support of a growing empire and reveling in the feast. These are monsters who feed on an take cues from concepts, not simply pure condensed clouds of dog/fire/architecture Resonance; the existence of a hypothetical bridge between concepts with a tenuous natural overlap does little to change the severity of a sudden change in diet any more than a teetotaling chain-smoker who switches entirely to cold-turkey binge-drinking in a single night will have a swell time of things.

                                    Magath represent disruptions to the natural order and thus are disruptive to the spiritual political landscape. It's why they're shunned. Human worship isn't going to change that.

                                            To answer the original question:
                                            In light of the darwinian nature of the Shadow's ecology I imagine the answer is simply that it happens as often as it pays off. If that's something spirits can do to get a meal and they can get away with it I don't see why it wouldn't happen or wouldn't eventually come to pass if left available for long enough. It's probably not as common in the modern day where secularism has a wider reach, but you might get the occasional cheeky faith spirit or some such. Cults are hardly rare in the world of darkness either, but then spirits don't even necessarily need to play dress up for those - some just straight up worship spirits.

                                            To speculate on the magath...argument:
                                            For a long time I've thought that as a spirits grows in power and its purview naturally extends outward to include more and more things it starts to become more nuanced and complex and a bit nutty even by spirit standards. Eventually you get to beings like Luna whose purviews encompass such wildly disparate concepts that it becomes easier to point out things that she's not at least tangentially connected to rather than everything that exists within her influence. I imagine you need that power and nuance to keep the various conflicting aspects from tearing the spirit apart. Luna, as quirky and insane as she is, at least maps out onto reality because the moon exists and can encompass those concepts.

                                            If you just start out with a lightning spirit and a cereal spirit, you're going to get something pretty nutty - but if you grow a lightning spirit into an industrial spirit and then into a corporate spirit you might just get some wacky but internally consistent spirit that uses Blast with lightning bolt marshmallow puffs.

                                            The firstborn don't really have to worry about this because, as stated by others, they started out as pangaeans so they don't necessarily follow the same rulebook.

                                            Doubling down on speculation I do wonder if this would put magath and idigam closer together on a hypothetical family tree. Regardless of their actual origins one of the core concepts of the idigam seems to be that they don't have direct reflections in the real world and often seem to be nonsensical mash ups of things. Magath also no longer directly reflect something but instead are an amalgamation of things that shouldn't be stuck together. I imagine there's still a distinction between the two, I don't think they're actually the same thing, but if you just kept lumping spirits together that don't fit I can imagine a magath would eventually start looking like an idigam.
