        Translated!

        "Shakunuku kal giragul!": "We are the hammer!"

        Firdu-Farah or Dhuunum-Farah: Father Fortress (for a Rank 5+ spirit; the latter doesn't have a strictly military connontation)
        Firdu-Aya or Dhuunum-Aya: Father Fortress (for anything below a Rank 5+ spirit; the latter doesn't have a strictly military connontation)
        Esthadu-Hirfathra: Bone-that-Binds (lit. "bone that is binding")
        Nafara-Lulafuna: Grey-Mantle (lit. "cloaked/mantled with/in grey")
        skeru: runt, weakling, whelp; the runt of the litter
        dhuunum: another name for a fortress, or any sort of large, impenetrable building; doesn't necessarily have to have the military connotation of firdu

        And some random Mage words, just because:

        Umfaras: The Primal Wild, home of the Sar Umfisan'na-Anzaghar, or the Watchtower of the Stone Book

        Pulhatas: The Aether, home of the Guzenheshla-Anzaghar, or Watchtower of the Golden Key

        Pilukas: Arcadia, home of the Idugukat-Anzaghar, or the Watchtower of the Lunargent Thorn (and possibly the Fae)

        Mapa'as: Pandemonium, home of the Anfarathi Kuth-Anzaghar, or the Watchtower of the Iron Gauntlet

        Zidigas: Stygia, home of the Anaguthima-Anzaghar, or the Watchtower of the Lead Coin

        The Lone Watchtower is known as the Numunamzu-Anzaghar, which literally translates to "the Watchtower of Nameless Wisdom".

                                                        75/105 kennings translated as of this post:

                                                        Namzalagul'u: Balder's gift = mistletoe
                                                        hafal-susk: war-garments = armour
                                                        abis-thirikdu: one who makes cattle fall = cattle-tripper = a wolf
                                                        namsusu-kuthdu: one who is cleaving dead bodies = carrion-cleaver = a raven or eagle
                                                        suthargagh: the bloodied-red mouth of a predator = red jowl = another kenning for a wolf
                                                        hafaldur: children of battle = soldiers or raiders
                                                        sahkthir: head-forest = hair
                                                        gafukaskkurag: apple of the chest = apple of the breast = the heart
                                                        rih-hirimazinum: bear-shirt = berserker
                                                        ramaamudu: one who is roaring = roarer = a bear
                                                        afful: old one = another name for an eagle

                                                        And derived terms:

                                                        Namzalagul: the god Balder (lit. "the one of brightness")
                                                        u: a gift, in the material sense; to be blessed with or to be given the qualities of something (may also be written as 'u)
                                                        susk: clothing, clothes, garments
                                                        namsusu: carrion or dead bodies
                                                        ukaskkurag: apple fruit
                                                        ramaamu: to roar, to bellow; to growl or snarl loudly

                                                        And some canon terms to add:

                                                        Wasu-Im: Soft Rage, literally "the rage meant to terrify". First stage of Kuruth, the Death Rage.
                                                        Basu-Im: Also known as the Death Rage, it means "the rage meant to lay low", and the sufferer tears mindlessly into anything.

                                                        And some more words:

                                                        isibh: a spiritual warrior like a paladin, a warrior-priest or a warrior-monk; not to be confused with a hafal-isib, which is a military chaplain, though a chaplain could also be an isibh
                                                        zaanyah: a presence or aura that inspires fear; to be of a terrifying presence; something that inspires fear in someone by its very existence; compare yeh'ren, which refers to something that frightens by an inherent offness, and uthkuk, which means to intimidate through a display of something or a show of force
                                                        kirek: albinism; those who have albinism
                                                        hafsu: to be bad-smelling; to have a sickly or stinking body from disease
                                                        zif-umamu: meaning "beast's mark", it refers to the little signs that might betray a shapeshifter's true nature, such as the Bestial Reflection or Blackened Eyes Metis conditions of W:tA, or the eyeshine from someone in Dalu
                                                        esthasur: a conjoined twin; a condition in which a twin is absorbed in the womb, and manifests as a tumour or underdeveloped parts in an adult body
                                                        sur: a half of something
                                                        suzif: a birthmark (lit. "body-mark")
                                                        fuful-umfin: refers to claws that are soft, blunt or fragile; those born with no claws in any form are known as numfin
                                                        uzakal-muth: hemophilia, one who bleeds easily; lit. means "vanishing blood"
                                                        hirisuguf: a hoof
                                                        lu'ukhaziha: "the human-face curse", in which a human face presents itself in all of a shapeshifter's forms; also known as erekhaziha to the werewolves
                                                        sunudu: the senses, such as the five senses (taste, smell, hearing, touch)
                                                        kasunudu: to be hypersensory, to have enhanced senses; kasunutu is the opposite of the canon naestu, and means "to be able to smell and taste greatly"
                                                        salkuk: thin, loose or fragile teeth; those born with no teeth are known as nukuk
                                                        ithakusgidu-sanimbathra: kyphosis, or a hunchback; lit. means "bow-back"
                                                        umamsu: to be monstrously ugly; to be hideous; to be transformed into something hideous
                                                        numalu: hairlessness, baldness
                                                        naafutu: to be lost
                                                        kuluhuziha: seizures, lit. "the shaking curse"; absence seizures are known as naafutigiziha (lit. "the curse of lost eyes")
                                                        thimigi: the third eye; may refer to a third eye physically in the forehead of some creatures
                                                        illu uzu: transparent or translucent flesh
