    Quasi-Idigam Chronicles Wolf-Blooded

    04-29-2014, 06:07 PM
    So I have "satanic" chronicle idea to run Forsaken game, and was really pumped up to see this after the Idigam Chronices to come out, as I would be glad for all the fixes and especially new Wolf-Blooded take on. But Onyx Path now moved IC from July to September and I'm afraid more delays could come up in mean time. So as I got players, and probably my Mage game can go on hiatus, I need the new take on Wolf-Blooded before the devs will end the book. All from now on is fan take on the subject, no way as official spoilers to Idigam Chronicles!

    Now I will take the quotes from original Wolf-Blooded article to what we strive.

    Originally posted by Idigam Chronicles Wolf-Blooded
    We’ve got about eight Tells worked out, though we’re still firming up the mechanics. Some evoke classic werewolf stories and moves — one can take Urhan form, but must shapeshift when the full moon rises. Another can take Dalu, but runs the risk of kuruth. One regenerates, but suffers penalties from silver like the Uratha.

    Others give a Wolf-Blooded closer links to the Shadow — one can act as a 1-dot Locus to allow Reaching, though doing so leaves her Open. Another can allow a spirit to speak through her, though she suffers the spirit’s ban for doing so. Another can Reach and bring others with her, but acts like a beacon for spirits when she does.
    As to general mechanics, I would used the rules for Ghouls from Blood & Smoke as most recent lesser template and GMC compatible.

    Here is my Wolf-Blooded rules proposition then...

    Wolf-Blooded
    Relatives of werewolves, or humans that uncover they primal heritage. One night they get lesser First Change, and they life is changed forever. Wolf-Blooded have strange powers and curses of werewolfes from legends and movies a like. But they strengh is limited. And there is always risk of full Death Rage when they use them.

    Wolf-Blooded Traits

    Tell: Your wolf-blooded has a mad inspiration from Mother Luna/Father Moon in his veins, and it's bestow part of Uratha blessings on you. You have one power based on your ancestry or exposure to Luna's mad light. Tell is both blessing and curse, often leading to more problems for Wolf-Blooded life. There are two kinds of Tells - Flesh-based that are connected to shapeshifting abilities of proper Uratha and Spirit-based that are about perciving and connecting with Shadow Realm of People. Each activation of Tell power cost one Essence.

    Primal Urge: Your character gains the Primal Urge trait at 0, allowing him to store and spend Essence. Character is also totally immune on Lunacy effects. This trait cannot be increased with Experiences or time.

    Gifts: Character can learn Moon and Wolf Gifts, but not Shadow ones. But they don't start with any.

    Kuruth: Every Wolf-Blooded, when using his Tell, is risking Death Rage. If not stated otherwise, for each scene ( or about one hour of active power ) make test on Kuruth of character. Character then enter Wasu-Im Rage and need to follow normal werewolves rules on this subject.

    Senses: All Flesh-based Wolf-Blooded have heightened smell and hearing senses. They can use them to tracking, even in Hishu form, just like Uratha. All Spirit-based Wolf-Blooded can perceive Gantlet and Shadow Realm, just like Uratha. For more rules on both kind scenes see Werewolf: the Forsaken corebook.

    Flesh-based Tells list:

    Urhan Moon Shadow – Choose one Auspice. Under this moon phase your character can assume form of Urhan, stalking the prey, with no Essence cost. On other nights, he can assume Urhan with cost of one Essence. Each hour in this form character is risking Death Rage.

    Dalu of Rage – When anger, this Wolf-Blooded can assume Dalu form. He must make Death Rage roll each minute he maintains it or he ends in Wasu-Im rage( see below ).

    Luna's Silver Blessing – This Wolf-Blooded has regeneration abilities. He can heal one point of Lethal damage for one Essence he spends. Each point used in this way may generate Death Rage in character.

    What do you think about it? Does it sounds enough “Hollywood werewolf” to you?

                  You definitely missed something. Here's how it works, as described in the article on Gifts:

                  Gift: A group of powers (like a Kindred Discipline)
                  Facet: A specific power within a Gift (like a particular level of a Discipline)

                  An Uratha starts out with one Moon Gift (determined by Auspice), two Shadow Gifts (chosen from a list based on their Tribe and Auspice), and both Wolf Gifts. New Shadow Gifts can be bought with experiences.

                  Moon Gifts have a 1-5 tiered progression of Facets, and every time an Uratha gains a dot in their Auspice Renown, they gain the next Facet of their Moon Gift automatically. In order for a Wolf-Blooded to have a Moon Gift, they would have to have an Auspice. Then, once they have access to the Gift, they would have to increase the associated Renown in order to gain any Facets in it, and a Gift doesn't do anything on its own if you don't have any Facets.

                  Shadow Gifts have five non-tiered Facets, each associated with a different Renown, and every time an Uratha gains a dot in any Renown, they get a free Facet associated with that Renown in one of the Shadow Gifts they know. A Wolf-Blooded would need to have a Tribe and an Auspice to get the free Gifts at character creation, though they might theoretically be able to buy Shadow Gifts with experiences. But again, without Renown they wouldn't be able to gain any actual Facets in those Gifts, so it would still be useless to them.

                  Wolf Gifts work identically to Shadow Gifts, except that all Uratha start out with access to them, regardless of Tribe and Auspice. So a Wolf-Blooded could easily have Wolf Gifts. But once again, without Renown, how are they going to get Facets, and without Facets, what good are Gifts?

                  I'm not sure what you mean by how will Wolf-Blooded become full Uratha? The same way they always have, I assume.

                          Just for clarity's sake:
                          - You need to have at least 1 dot in a particular Renown to be able to take Facets associated with that Renown.
                          - Wolf Gift Facets are linked to Renown in the same way as Shadow Gifts.

                          Now obviously you can just ignore that and give access to Wolf Gifts regardless, however it will impact on some dice pools that certain Wolf Gifts use; some Wolf Gifts also have effects that are directly based on the Renown rank.
