    Recommended Reading for Werewolf

    04-29-2015, 04:39 PM
    I'm not a big werewolf fan outside of Werewolf the Forsaken. I've seen movies, but never really been that impressed, I read some books a long time ago but they're fairly forgettable. In fact before Forsaken, Werewolf was probably the least interesting line of the World of Darkness to me. Still, when I was getting ready for second edition I decided to find a book or two about werewolves to put me in the mood and I stumbled across Autobiography of a Werewolf Hunter. It's from Permuted Press, which seems to publish supernatural post apocalypses and books about monster hunters, and I wasn't expecting much. Technically it's not even about werewolves, the focus is clearly on the werewolf hunter.

    But it's a fantastic book. And I've been sick lately, which is why I've been posting so much, and the last book in the trilogy came out so with my downtime I've gotten through it and I have to heartily recommend it.

    With one major caveat.

    The book and its two sequels are very well written, the author seems to take into account a wide variety of werewolf legends, and though this seems to be his first full length work it's fantastic. But a lot of people might be turned off by the content, because the werewolf hunter is fairly hardcore. He has his good sides, especially in the first book, but when it comes down to him versus werewolves he's like the Punisher. There is no compromise, no give. To him, werewolves are personifications of absolute evil, blasphemies on Earth.

    And the thing is, he's right. These werewolves aren't the Forsaken, and they put the Pure to shame. They are Evil with a capital E and a cruel accent and a bunch of l's and a maniacal cackle echoing in the background as it's said. So between him being a hardcore hunter, and them being evil, the books can get pretty grim and dark. Still, if one is looking for inspiration for evil werewolves, it's probably the best source out there, as the wolves have their own culture and beliefs and notable individuals.

    If any of you end up reading it I'm sure you'll like it.

    Any questions, feel free to ask. I was thinking about just talking briefly about the werewolves in case people aren't interested in reading an entire trilogy to find out about them.

                      My recommendations are The Wolf's Hour and The Hunter from the Woods. The second is a collection of short stories about the character from The Wolf's Hour throughout his life and is also a fantastic read.

                                  Bitten and Stolen, the first two books of the Women of the Otherworld series, by Kelly Armstrong. Great example of werewolves.

                                  The basic idea is that the main character, Elena, was bitten and turned into a werewolf. There are born werewolves, but only males can inherit the gene. Werewolves usually bite people for the express purpose of killing them, and even if they don't, there's a pretty slim chance of surviving the bite, so Elena is the ONLY female werewolf in existence. At the start of the first book, she's left the Pack, and is trying to live a normal life, which is difficult when you HAVE to go out and change into a werewolf every week or so. She gets called back to help the Pack out with a serious problem.

                                  The Pack is a relatively small group of werewolves, but they are THE authority over all werewolves. There are many non-Pack werewolves, called mutts, and the Pack makes sure they don't draw attention to werewolves in general, and keep them on their toes (one of many rules: only Pack werewolves are allowed to claim territory.) Some great scenes about werewolves hunting, and the kind of family dynamic a Pack has. Particularly interesting, I think, is the character of Clay, who was bitten as a young boy and lived completely feral in the swamps of Louisiana until Jeremy, the current Pack Alpha, found him. Clay has a genius-level IQ and a degree in anthropology, specializing in anthropomorphic religions. He also has no clue about most human behavior, and doesn't care to blend in most of the time. Really fascinating character concept.

                                  It's definitely in the "urban fantasy/paranormal romance" genre, so that might turn some folks off, but for my money, it's the best werewolf fiction I've read recently. The sequel, Stolen, opens up the world, adding in other supernaturals and asking the question "what would happen if a rich eccentric computer/video game dude funded a group of parapsychologists."
