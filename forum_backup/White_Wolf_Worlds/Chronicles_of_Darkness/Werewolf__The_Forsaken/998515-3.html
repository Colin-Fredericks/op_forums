                    Originally posted by nofather View Post
                    I think the rules are pretty clear on that, they shouldn't feel cheated if they've read them. The only way to break out of it is to get an Exceptional Success. Plus there's external abilities that assist these rolls.
                    I was referring to what I understood as Charlaquin's suggestion of doing a couple rolls and then not rolling anymore. As written in the book, players may keep rolling until they either:

                    Fail - At which point they enter Basu-Im.
                    Succeed - At which point they get some turns without needing to destroy something.
                    Succeed Exceptionally - At which pont they are free from Kuruth.
                    Reach the Harmony Limit - At which point they enter Basu-Im.

                    It seems that the intention is to build up a scene where the Uratha is desperately fighting against his rage, until he can't hold anymore and loses it. It will work for extreme Harmony characters, because 1 Turn or 10 Turns of Resolve+Composure tests are fast to narrate and might work like combat. However, when you enter the realm of 5 to 15 minutes, rolling each turn is a pretty bad way of representing the action. Centered Harmony Uratha seem much more suited to extended tests, which are made to resume the action encompassing those minutes, like Chases, Investigations and Social Maneuvers. The system just breaks down when characters approach Harmony 5, as it allows, as written, hundreds of tests... It is like asking a player to roll for a 10 minute Speech each time his character finishes a sentence.

                    If I just remove several rolls from them to make the scene quicker as Charlaquin suggested, I will be house-ruling it anyway. Better to spend some time on it.

                    Originally posted by nofather View Post
                    Chain Rage allows members of a pack to snap out of Wasu-Im if they make five turns of lucidity. Still Small Voice can be used on others in Wasu-Im to help them break out of it. The Controlled Burn Merit allows you to break out of it with a turn of lucidity and a Willpower.
                    I seem to have not expressed my self clearly, sorry for that. I have no problem with how often Uratha risk Kuruth, and I like it! I just don't feel the mechanics, as described in the book, do a good job portraying the intended mood on Resisting Kuruth Scenes.

                    Originally posted by nofather View Post
                    With your alternative idea, there's far less tension about the rage in general. It might be appropriate for games where people want to cut it out entirely. And attacking the source doesn't really fit, as the source is not always a tangible thing one can attack.
                    I disagree on the tension! My experience using conditions is that they are very good for applying feeling to the scene, and with a system like that, I'm just applying a "Wasu-Im" condition on the player, and allowing for beats to come! Balance aside (which I might have done wrong), it does everything that is needed for the mood. It provides a way to resolve the situation with a single roll, to avoid dragging the game down. It allows for all the intended possible scenes, and also can be modified by situations that influence the characters. I don't believe tension is added by a ton of rolls.

                    Originally posted by nofather View Post
                    And attacking the source doesn't really fit, as the source is not always a tangible thing one can attack.
                    Yes, which mean that if you fail the test, you will certainly enter Basu-Im on those cases. Also, that's why it has the added component of really messing up any social interactions, and I would even add that the player may chose to fail those actions on purpose to earn Beats. An Uratha isolated somewhere resisting Death Rage isn't a very dramatic scene anyway, since he already got far from any possible victims. The focus here is this:

                    You get a single roll that sets to mood for the scene, and then you enter a condition based on that roll. The tension comes from being in one of those conditions and near people you don't want to hurt. As I wrote earlier:

                    Dramatic Failure: Instantly enter Basu-Im, going straight for the source of the trigger. Even after Basu-Im ends, you suffer from the Atavism condition towards any sources of the same trigger.
                    Failure: Enter Wasu-Im, suffering from the same Rage limitations from Gauru. After the Harmony timer expires, you enter Basu-Im.
                    Success: Enter Wasu-Im, but do not suffer the Rage limitations from Gauru. However, each turn in the presence of the trigger forces a Resolve+Composure roll. On a failure on this roll, make when attack against the source. Willpower may be spent to avoid the attack automatically. If your character takes measures to avoid the trigger, he does not need to roll. Also, you react poorly to social interactions. Make the same Resolve+Composure roll whenever someone tries to interact with you, attacking once on a failure. After the Harmony timer expires, you enter Basu-Im.
                    Exceptional Success: You lose the turn, but snap out of Wasu-Im an cannot suffer a Kuruth Trigger Reaction from the same trigger for the scene.
                    Most characters will succeed, since the test usually has 4 dice + willpower. So, most of the time, this will result in a tense scene where the Uratha needs to hold his rage while getting away from everyone, while possibly hurting people in the process. All this without the need to keep on rolling several times. When the rare Exceptional Success appears, they resist fully. And otherwise they suffer similar fates to a player that has failed one of those dozens of Resolve+Composure rolls that would be required in a scene by default.

                    Again, I might be wrong with those modifiers and the applied conditions, but the concept is what matters: Allowing for the same intention without the needless rolls.

                        Originally posted by BinAly View Post
                        I was referring to what I understood as Charlaquin's suggestion of doing a couple rolls and then not rolling anymore.
                        This is a pretty significant oversimplification of what I was saying.

                        Originally posted by BinAly View Post
                        As written in the book, players may keep rolling until they either:

                        Fail - At which point they enter Basu-Im.
                        Succeed - At which point they get some turns without needing to destroy something.
                        Succeed Exceptionally - At which pont they are free from Kuruth.
                        Reach the Harmony Limit - At which point they enter Basu-Im.
                        Correct. But, there are two other conditions under which you stop asking for rolls:

                        The Outcome Ceases to be Uncertain - At which point you narrate the only possible result
                        The Action Ceases to have Dramatic Consequences - At which point you adjudicate a result and move on with the story.

                        It's not written that way explicitly in the text, because it's adjudication 101 and a fundamental skill every GM is expected to know. To be fair, I think a lot more RPGs could benefit from explicitly teaching these things to new GMs who might be reading.


                        Originally posted by BinAly View Post

                        If I just remove several rolls from them to make the scene quicker as Charlaquin suggested, I will be house-ruling it anyway. Better to spend some time on it.
                        Maybe it would help if I gave an example of what I'm suggesting.

                        Storyteller: As the silver blade tears into your skin, the sting of your bane sears your soul just as it cleaves into your flesh. The pain awakens something in you, the fight or flight instincts kick in, and you begin to fall into the throes of Wasu-Im. What do you do?
                        Player: What are my options again?
                        Storyteller: You can attack the nearest active target, move towards the nearest target if you can't reach them, or roll Resolve + Composure to try to gain one turn of lucidity per success. Anything else will trigger a Reflexive Resolve + Composure roll, and failure on either roll will trigger Bassu-Im.
                        Player: Ok, I guess I'll try to pull myself together.
                        Storyteller: Let's see that sweet, sweet Resolve + Composure roll.
                        Player: Come on, exceptional success! Big bucks, no whammies... *Rolls 4 successes* Damn, just missed it.
                        Storyteller: How far can you run in four turns?
                        Player: Let's see, in this form... I've got 17 speed, so 34 meters per turn if I use my action to run... Times four turns, that's... 136 meters.
                        Storyteller: Alright, that's enough to get you pretty far from the fight. And there are animals out here that will probably be closer than the fight, so at that point it's pretty unlikely your instincts will drive you back to the fray.
                        Player: That sounds better than sticking around the fight in Basu-Im, at least.
                        Storyteller: Sounds good. With some effort, you tear yourself back to your senses, if only for a few brief moments.

                        At that point, the Storyteller continues to resolve the combat in progress, making sure to double-check with the player each round if they still plan to keep running as far from the fight as possible. At the end of those four turns, the storyteller can cross the character's name off the initiative roster. Whether the player exceptionally succeeds during those 15 minutes or not doesn't make a practical difference to the story at this point, so there's no need to continue rolling dice. Either way, he's out of the fight and off in the woods somewhere. After the fight, the storyteller could even ask the player if they were able to pull themselves out of it or fell to Basu-Im, the latter potentially refilling their Willpower for their Blood Archetype, but giving the Storyteller an opportunity to introduce story complications caused by the player's Death Rage.
