    Werewolf the Forsaken: The New Oskana Chronicle Overhaul (post GMC)

    02-19-2014, 03:41 PM
    Werewolf: the Forsaken New Oskana Overhaul Document

    While this is what we're using for our own chronicle, primarily it's a full blown W:tF overhaul. It could use input and commentary as much of it needs ongoing use, playtesting and tinkering given it's still in it's draft stages.

    Some of you saw the initial Instincts for Werewolf: The Forsaken post I tossed up some time back, and during that time (since August of last year) myself and my Co-ST have been getting as much further done as humanly possible with the time we did have. Many of these changes address aspects of our own long-term New Oskana chronicle (it's pushed past 5 years now with our players) we wanted to see or had complaints or input on with the prior. Some of these won't be to everyone's tastes as a result, but the document is intended to give people some means of taking what they like from it and use it while not needing the stuff that doesn't work for their own games. It's over 100 pages currently; there's a lot of stuff in there for system updates and overhauls.

    We currently have the first incarnation mostly complete (at least in regards to the major areas we wanted to update or change above and beyond a GMC conversion) and ready to view. Some other mechanics haven't been fully set into the document -just- yet, as we're giving our players and others a chance to chime in; there are some incomplete areas mechanics and idea wise that we've got a good idea of what we want to do, but haven't finalized yet. There are a bunch of areas that need further work and completion, but the vast majority of it is complete enough to work with and run things by, porting in portions ones like to existing games.

    This document is currently primarily mechanics; there's a longer section on Loci, Totems and Resonance that we had a hard look at, as well as a Tribe blurb for each of the Forsaken intended to more clearly emphasize what we were after from them. This isn't the final document; with Idigam Chronicles I imagine we may want to alter it further especially once our players have more time to play with what we do have set so far.

    Following this post is a list of the areas we're still hammering out into a usable form in a later post for conversions, ideas and the like that need completion, further ideas and further input.

    A general gist of some of the changes addressed are as follows:

    -We have a quick redux of the Tribes; we've reinforced or tweaked the thematics in some cases of philosophy (Blood Talons in particular) but they should all be very recognizable. While it hasn't been tweaked to First Tongue, we've got some Babylonian terms to encompass the philosophy of each tribe tossed up. I will be adding a multiple tribal origin myth section as time allows; Blood and Smoke has a lovely section on a clan's origins with various versions that may not be mutually exclusive; the Tribes are getting the same. The Father Wolf Myth will be one each will have, but there will be more presented that will be considered -equally- as valid. We'll be formatting these Tribe writeups to be more in line with the Blood and Smoke style; it's a really nice way to present them.

    -Renown is gone, flat out. Loathed the mechanic, loathed how it railroaded games, loathed it's cap, just flat out loathed it. Our players loathed it too, so there's utterly no reason for us to keep it. It's nowhere to be seen in this, so fair warning for those that enjoyed it. Standing among spirits is handled via Honorary Renown; we're a bit harsher with how Forsaken are regarded like by the Shadow being half flesh, so we're going by half their Primal Urge (spiritual and supernatural potency) rounded up; Pure gain the Pure merit, which increases this effective rating in a few respects by +1 (it needs to tinkered with a bit more regarding Totem etc. though). Forsaken however, do gain access to the Auspice Merit from deals with Lunes at least. Status: Tribe covers standing in one's tribe. Status: Lodge handles that area, and increases the benefits you get by dot rating like a Cult merit (we didn't like the skewed prerequisite system of before at all). There are currently 2 merits each for each Tribe, some that increase depending on your Tribal Status. We don't have any for Pure yet, but we're still working on them and their alternate Instinct levels.

    -We are not using the Honorary Renown mechanics from GMC. While the system likely needs more playtesting and tweaking, right now werewolves can sheer away Essence along with Corpus from spirits. Want to kill a spirit? Get rid of it's Essence + Corpus and werewolves are good at that when need be. I'm still a bit iffy on the mechanic behind it, but sorry; half-fleshed aren't some frightening, bane-inducing badasses to low ranking spirits in the Shadow. They're cousins, outsiders, bastards, even abominations by the standards of some spirits like Shartha; they don't get the benefits and then little of the drawbacks. This in part is also to reinforce that no... there is no "this myth is a myth, but in system terms is not". How werewolves can so readily harm spirits is much more up in the air now; they don't do it in the same way spirits do. It should call into question just how valid the notion of being "half spirit" is, for ST's that want to downplay that, or present a strictly different origin to what werewolves are.

    -A lot of this diverges -heavily- from prior Forsaken; the necessity of the Shadow is down-toned greatly, greatly ported into something a character or ST -could- deal with, but can be utterly irrelevant. You don't need spirits to learn your main powers (though you can go down that route via individual Numina via hunting spirits or gaining Totem blessings). You don't need spirits whatsoever for proper packs (though you can still get totems). You can go out and be an effective Werewolf without touching spirits at all. Mechanically it should be no effort to do a spirit heavy game, or one where it's entirely absent without having to do more than basically... just not have it come up. It may cut out the usefulness of rites and numina etc. but really, you're go-to stuff has no need of it. Instincts and your basic abilities along with many of the basic merits.

    -Rites are near entirely predicated on Essence manipulation and interactions with the Shadow; some overlap with existing Rites, but if it was a power that should have been a Numina, or some effect not predicated on spirits, the shadow and essence... they're gone. Requiring it for social rituals between packs and Tribes etc? Gone. In a way they're more akin to some of the essence manipulation effects of Idigam; they play heavily with resonance, and the like, and any healing they manage is mostly to get rid of conditions via Shadow-based aspects. A few rites are now Merits, and in particular fetishes and talens have changed drastically.

    -Loci are no longer giant ridiculous Essence batteries as before; they're watering holes for spirits, thin areas where heavy essence flows specifically have worn the Gauntlet thin, but Werewolves cannot tap this. They're hunters, and predators. If they want Essence, primarily they're consuming spirits, humans, other werewolves or similar critters that can garner it. There is no "Sitting at a loci to farm essence" beyond keeping an eye out for spirits drawn to one like wolves making a den near a deer path for easier hunting. Primarily if you want to keep one secure, you entreat a spirit to be a Totem and guard it for you. You get to go out and do your own shit instead of sticking nearby -just in case- and babysitting it like an essence farming. That's done. Loci are where you keep your spirit charged fetishes intact, where you keep your Totem spirit content instead of leaking essence like a sieve, and, assuming it's a potent enough one, get bonuses in hunting down spirit-prey for Essence like a Feeding Ground.

    -Humans are common (and problematic) prey; spirits aren't all over Twilight. They aren't a common easily accessible source, though great if you do. This is intentional; the risk of attacking and killing humans is fairly grim. Loci become important (if you can find one) for those that want to isolate themselves at least in part from this issue, but even then you're trading it for the other big issue; spirits just don't give you Essence, and your Totem sure as hell won't if you manage to get one. It's one big-game/deadliest prey hunt traded for another. Unless you take to eating wolves... good luck there given their low numbers. Or go cannibal and start eating other werewolves; still a horrendous issue for anyone who does. All werewolves have a mechanic to reflect flesh addiction to humans and wolves as well. If you're out of Essence and Death Rage... go figure, you're gonna start hunting, killing and eating prey.

    -Werewolves are -risks- now to those around them, there's a reason why many isolate themselves from humans, even if that can cause Harmony issues from a social animal (wolf and human) going against it's nature. They're tragic, vicious, and dangerous, and the lower their Harmony, the harder they resist Death Rage. On the one end, they want to be with their packs, around their families and friends, they're social animals. On the other? They're monstrous, instinct and rage driven hunters. They creep out humans as, go figure, humans instinctively realize their is a predator in their midst that can and possibly will kill them. When they become more and more potent on their spiritual side (which Primal Urge primarily focuses on now, with Harmony predominantly handling the wolf and human aspects), this starts to bleed over on those who keep long-term company with them. Ever been in a gang where tensions constantly run high due to aggressive members? That one violent, angry friend who seems to ramp up your own issues? That's a high PU werewolf.

    -Also, Essence Bleed, or more accurately, Hunger for Essence isn't a high PU only thing; while at low PU you can gain Essence from prey animals, you don't just sit on it until used; you're half spirit, half flesh. Your flesh wants flesh, food, drink, the biological necessities. Your supernatural half though... it wants and -needs- Essence. You need to keep both satisfied. Starving of essence is just as bad (worse in some instances) than for food. While you burn through it slower than a full spirit, eventually... you're going to -need- to hunt. It's unavoidable. You're a werewolf. You hunt. The longer you don't, the more likely you are to snap and enter Death Rage, or spin out of control. It's treated like food and water deprivation and imposes some -ugly- penalties to avoiding Death Rage, much like hunger and starvation does for vampires.

    -Harmony needs more example breaking points, but for the most part, actions that make you act more like a spirit without considering the needs of a human or wolf are what fucks it up. Things that mess around with your Essence to shift it away from what you are can at higher examples; consistently eating a bunch of death and car spirits is going to create some spiritual dissonance between all your aspects, and jumping headlong into being some rage-driven, instinctive spirit sure as hell won't either. It's a balancing act to have all your contradictory tugs work in Harmony (go figure). The lower it is... the more likely you are to Death Rage. It caps your Death Rage resistances (on par with Blood and Smoke Gangrel); the less you tend to your human and wolf needs, the more that unbridled killer instinct starts to color everything you do. The more you act like that spirit, the more you become like it. It's not calm. It's not rational. It is like any other spirit; it is it's nature in action and form, and their best victuals are their own kind. It's the same with werewolves now.

    -Totems are not needed for Werewolves to become a pack; that's handled by the Pack Bonded merit. Totems instead are spirits drawn to you or, more likely, pressed into your service. They give you minor spiritual blessings and advantages; no attribute boosts, no Numina, powers from the chronicler's guide showing a way to tweak. We reprinted much of them so people knew what we after with some changes. Want your Totem to do more for you? Buy it as a Mentor. Totems are also reinforced as not somehow losing the common spirit aspects because; totem bond magic; they have an agenda, they're self serving, and they want fucking Essence. The stronger the spirit is than you, the more -you- can get influenced and reshaped by it. You only keep these around consistently by having a Loci; they can comfortably exist there, with enough essence and spirit prey that they're not starving. That's the general commonly deal; you got a Totem because you left one spirit to thrive at that spot over the rest. It doesn't like to leave because that means Essence loss without a nearby Open condition. Without using rites, it's generally what handles your Loci issues, leaving the PC werewolves to go out and do shit beyond babysitting a loci all the damn time.

    -Auspices are handled as a merit now for Forsaken; it's easy to just say that Forsaken players get a free dot of the Auspice merit and Tribal Status. That's not true of our game, but it's a minor adjustment. Aspects are a merit with the Auspice prerequisite; specializations from Signs of the Moon to more readily show how the Lunes have blessed and transfigured you along with your own tendencies. You get Auspices from pacts and favor of Lunes; it doesn't just happen anymore. The Lunes also don't give it free of any hooks; they're still spirits in service to a massive, alien, mad one after all. So, on your moon-phase nights, you lose a bit of Essence, that's the tithe to them, and they take it, period. It's quite possible that a Lune will make this deal or pact with a first-changed werewolf before they realize what they're getting into; it's also possible for Ghost Wolves to do so later on in life. Lunes are bastards; they're still mad spirits, they have little qualms recruiting a vulnerable werewolf into their conflicts, even if it's the player's choice out of character.

    -Fetishes now have a ritual act of chinimage that can be used to offset using Essence to activate them and briefly tap the spirit within. It's all or nothing; not willing to gift the Spirit Essence to affect the world around it? Then you're doing an act to generate that Essence for it. They're also not permanent until broken; outside of those made by legendary rite masters, they'll start to degenerate outside of the Shadow or the influence zones of Loci; they work best in those areas of strong influence, and stay intact there without issue, often working better too. Outside? Eventually the bindings will unravel and the spirit will escape. Talens differ in that they have one use before the spirit is released, or items that absorbed some heavily resonance infused essence but otherwise aren't as different anymore. Many of the example fetishes are heavily revised from their prior incarnations.

    -Gauru form did get some boosts; nothing too heavily, but some, keeping in mind what can be achieved with some of the New Instincts in tandem with it. It has no time limit anymore; you get your Resolve + PU in turns without any additional Death Rage penalties. Any further turns in it during a scene add another -1 Penalty to Death Rage cumulatively. The more you use it, the more likely you are to Death Rage, simple as that. The biggest difference is Lunacy is -gone-. Flat out. You get your Predatory Aura now with Fight, Flight and Competitive conditions. Humans already have built in issues dealing with something as frightening as Gauru as part of Integrity, and they can use it to capitalize more with the Flight aura. It's not a group effect; most people will run by common sense, it needs no reinforcement, and if multiple people need to be affected, well... werewolves usually run in packs. We have differing mechanics relating to how speed is calculated with primarily humanoid and bipedal forms, so some of those bonuses reflect that. Urhan got simplified to being something fast and sensory, smaller and stealthier, but without changes, or sudden attribute boosts. Some may want to revert this to the old style, but the document uses what we have in place for our game. We don't have Dalu or Urshul as a form all werewolves can take in our game; some will want to immediately change this, and that's fine, but per the document you get them via the Shapeshifting 2 instinct that's affinity for all Werewolves.

    -There is some reprinted material from Book of Spirits to show examples of spiritual sicknesses; we loved the mechanics and ideas, though they're tweaked and adjusted heavily. Resonance is overhauled and much more important now along with it. We have systems to show how high levels of ambient essence can start infecting and otherwise really doing a number on you. Some of the Book of Spirit material is rewritten to a greater or lesser degree.

    -The XP values in it are not GMC; we're keeping the old XP system for now, in that the costs are still using multipliers, we're using Beats triggers to instead give 1 experience via the old system. All that GMC wise though is intact and all the systems are compatible otherwise.

      The areas we still have to mechanically address or explain in general in the document:

      -Wolf Blooded; I've discussed some of the ideas we'll be using for these in other threads, but we're definitely doing a lot more on them. They're going to be people with stronger shapeshifting and/or spiritual -potential-; they'll get a lot more love and become a LOT more important.

      -Lodge Conversions: a lot of work, we're doing them on a case by case basis right now starting with the Lodge of the Lost for one of our players.

      -Alternate/Additional Instincts: We're leaving room for more. As is, they're a 1-5 listing; we're going by the understanding that they have something akin to single level devotions like vampires in a way, so individual Instinct lists will have more options akin to Promethean Transmutations, we just needed to get the bread-and-butter ones done first. Odds are many Pure favor other Instinct iterations over the default ones we have. It's quite possible there are Instincts that developed that are wildly divergent for most if not all levels.

      -Additional Merits; because, there's a few that are just ideas at the moment, but need to be mechanically completed. There's a lot more room for these, they just haven't been prioritized.

      -Additional Fetish and Talen conversions; because having more examples is always good.

      -Discussion on using the current Honorary Renown mechanics in GMC vs our alternative along with some other things related to that eventually.

      -Further refined Harmony Breaking Points; I'm not quite satisfied personally with what we have; I want a few more examples at each Harmony rating, but I think it reinforces the basic ideas so far. The old Harmony listings, however, aren't likely to be used much.

      -Multiple tribal origin stories; yep, like Clans have for vampires. This will also push the idea that there are seperate -races- of werewolves; some stories will present a Tribe as being it's own thing unrelated to another Tribe for ST's wanting to go more divergent. Father Wolf stories will just be one mythic thread, and not the most commonly accepted one as a given.

      -Style setup akin to Blood and Smoke; Why you'd want to be Tribe X, Why others Fear you, why you fear yourself etc.

      -Further work on Shartha and Idigam; they need updates, and the former suffer from too much combat-related aspects, and not enough others.

      -Expansion on Bale Hounds and their relevance; these may be expanded into being the default of being a spirit-ridden werewolf instead of just related to Wounds and the Maeljin. We already have spirit influencing mechanics via Totems; it's possible you get rogue packs of "bale hounds" that really just are spiritually hollowed out or twisted werewolves now being extensions of said spirit totem.

      -Additional Rites and a Numina conversion section. This will also discuss the issues of eating -too many- divergent spirits and taking their powers into you in the form of bans/compulsions.

      -Contagious Rage, more clearly presented Essence Deprivation mechanics, cleaned up Predatory Senses and an explanation of how entering the Shadow works with additional options like: no access, requiring a Numina, a merit, innate, etc. for playing around with the base chronicle assumptions.

      -Cleaned up flesh addiction/cannibalism mechanics: the Flesh Addicted and Cannibalistic Hunger conditions need to be defined; I wasn't satisfied with the original tinkering using the standard Addicted and Deprived listings, so they'll have some work done ASAP given their importance.

      -Offspring; those fucked up werewolf spirits won't be the only potential bad news; we're using The Cursed from the Chronicler's guide to represent the fully flesh offspring that occasionally result instead (or in addition to) with some tweaks. We're also presenting the notion that sometimes you do get a wolf-blooded; you just don't always know, especially given The Cursed generally don't change until after puberty. This will be riskier territory; just up and killing or abandoning your offspring might be a very bad idea rather than the sensible one.

      -Anything else that comes to mind, as this list likely is missing some things myself or the co-writer may have overlooked/forgotten.

              Their partial spirit aspects would make it that Wolves would be always good safe sources of essence (safe in terms of resonance), so they're included for that very reason. A wolf spirit doesn't have problems eating other wolf spirits, and this transfers over to Forsaken; it might mess with their Harmony some, but prey animals are intended to refer to prey animals for wolves; deer, rabbits and so on, not other wolves in of itself (they're always valid sources along with humans and werewolves etc). It's a bit cludgy, I realize given it doesn't have a smooth progression; that said, it seemed fair that a young new werewolf could potentially get by on prey animals for a while. The kicker is if there should be a system for -lowering- Primal Urge; it's not one we've addressed yet.

              The other issue was that while getting essence from natural prey animal spirits carries no resonance issues (as mentioned regarding how magath come about, and what types of spirit prey), that's covered by getting Essence from spirits; eventually material animal prey ceases to do anything but nourish the body. Just like spirits they can gain essence from those of effectively incompatible resonance, but eventually it'll take its toll (we're still sussing out that mechanic, however, beyond the quick high Harmony mention).

              I wanted to avoid the evolution turning out like a vampire's requirements, however; if it was only spirits then they become far more essential. If it was only other werewolves, the threat level to humans goes way down as common prey. I suppose the mechanics vampire has regarding vitae and diminishing returns is a possibility for non spirit, non-werewolf prey, but it hasn't seemed to quite gel as anything beyond "well, vampire has a system like that".

              I figure when it comes to Werewolf prey, that's when you get some badass who offset that breaking point went loopy all Ravenous style and got a taste for them rather than something enforced by Primal Urge under the current draft ideas.

                          -The current Sense of Prey is something of a placemat; looking at an existing primal, predatory sense in Blood and Smoke seemed a good initial model to use in the draft; we need something functional and that works until a better alternative comes up. Given our game currently has Vampire and Changeling PCs, that's why we directly addressed that; the automatic bonuses and similar are primarily supposed to apply to their common prey; animals if low enough PU, but Spirits, Humans, Shartha and similar things for the rest. We wanted to reinforce that no; some beings out there don't count as prey to werewolves.

                          -Hunger for Essence I'm 50/50 on. The other author Gerard felt that for our game it's only fair and helps reinforce that eventually a werewolf -needs- to hunt on a spiritual level. No walking essence batteries, no farming loci, no gaining it during moon phases or that sort of thing. Definitely won't be to everyone's liking, though we've applied similar to Changelings in it given it's a crossover. The intent there is to reinforce that Werewolves are predators; they can't just expect to coast along without -needing- Essence. Originally I wanted no reference to prey animals to PU 1 & 2; the co-author Gerard figured it was only fair and that a werewolf -could- survive just on prey animals for quite a while if they didn't cultivate their spiritual (PU) potency. The chart isn't too sleek though otherwise, but I'd like to see it played around with before shaking it up too much.

                          -Predatory Aura isn't exactly the same; Fight, Flight, Competitive, Not Wanton, Bestial Competitive. There is overlap, but that's due to the systems -working- as intended.
