    Depends on how the Storyteller chooses to interpret 'honor nothing' - in 1e I seem to recall it was elucidated upon as the intention being, as Bunyip says, use of anything made by humans, but I don't think the game will break or anything if an ST decides 'honoring' a thing means more than just using it, but considering it worthy in and of itself. One might decide that just entering a human dwelling to get shelter from the rain is fine, but that intentionally acquiring a human abode for long-term residence, being house-proud of it, etc, is more in breach of the ban.

    ​But then, an ST's ability to interpret something is one of the great variables of any game, and will change from table to table.

    - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                ​But then, an ST's ability to interpret something is one of the great variables of any game, and will change from table to table.
                Right. And wearing clothing is utilitarian, while going shopping for style is honoring the clothes (and a very human process). Learning to drive a car is a skill that can help your pack hunt better, by traveling faster than feet will take them (even with gifts). Pimpin' y0 ride is honoring the work of human hands; for that matter so is bitch-slapping the dude who sits on your car, or keyed it. A gun, specifically chosen for a task you know the pack can't do another way (and this is more of a stretch) is just a tool; packing heat as a matter of course because that's how you expect things to go down is honoring man's implements of war. Ditto for edged weaponry.

                If you approach PK's ban from a functional and attitudinal perspective you can even imagine one of them using computers on a daily basis, because they have to for the work that supports the pack and its hunts. Using a computer to surf YouTube vids of Big Bang Theory re-runs would be a breaking point towards flesh.

                Human work is to be exploited only as much as necessary, and discarded immediately thereafter. Think of them as the Sith of Father Wolf's children: they form no attachments to human work, and destroy such things if they grow attached to them. (Now, that fetish you just made is another matter, as is the hand-spun fiber garment your packmate gave you for Wolfmas.)

                In some respects the PK could be easier to represent than the Fire-Touched, because while the PK need to screw their attitudes to the right place with action that follows, the FT need to constantly attend to themselves that they do not knowlingly lie. The tongue is notoriously hard to govern....

                --Khanwulf

                PS Edit: Obviously there's a wide range of portrayals that could be used, however "feral tribe of the wood" is a trope that only fits part of the time and I think the developers would prefer that each of the Pure be viable in any given setting, with consideration. Otherwise they're pigeon-holed badly, and that seems to be what 2e wants to escape from, broadly speaking.
