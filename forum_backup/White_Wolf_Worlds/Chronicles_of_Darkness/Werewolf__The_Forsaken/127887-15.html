                        Originally posted by Seidmadr View Post
                        Pretty funny, the word used in Sweden today for wolf, 'varg', is originally a kenning. 'Varg' means pretty much 'reaver'.

                        The word for a bear has something similar in several languages. The French name for a fox, "renard", is also an example of this phenomenon — it's taken from the name of a talking fable version of the animal.

                        More kennings, 41/101 now translated:

                        Ur-Rihdu: "feeder of the wolf" = the god Tyr
                        Uthumamu-Farah: "father of raging beasts" = "father of monsters" = the god Loki
                        Manamzu-Uhamusenir: "greatly wise raven lord" = "all-wise raven ruler" = the god Odin
                        Kusk-Zum: "red-beard" = the god Thor
                        Kaulum-Ul: "the greatly-fruitful one" = "the fruitful" = the god Freyr
                        Uthanna-Erdu: "crier of gold" = the god Freyja
                        Ilimmahan-Mu'u'adur: "son of nine mothers" = the god Heimdall
                        Badu-Ilalum: "founder of songs" = "first maker of poetry" = the god Bragi
                        Harnir-Nidur: "daughter of the earth" = "daughter of Fjörgynn" = the goddess Frigg
                        Skekhesir-Nir: "lady of the snowshoes" = the goddess Skadi


                        And some derived terms from the kennings, plus a few others:

                        ilalum: poetry, ballads, songs, hymns; the spoken word
                        gah: mouth, jaws, gullet
                        gislak: a clearing; a cleared piece of land
                        lulafuna: a cloak
                        nihunua: a thread, a string
                        umamu: beasts, animals; monsters as a whole may be known as "uthumamu"
                        anah: a drink
                        gu'ur: wood, logs
                        ersu: a bed; a place to sleep
                        ulum: to be fruitful; to be fertile
                        unu: hall
                        Hafaferunu: Valhalla (lit. "hall of the battle-chosen")
                        esir: shoe
                        skekhesir: snowshoe
                        zum: beard, facial hair
                        feru: to choose, to pick specifically, to take as part of privilege
                        Harnir = the earth deity Fjörgynn (feminine form is Haramahan)

                                                        54/105 kennings now translated (including Seidrmadr's):

                                                        giazu-athal: "secret-sharer" = a friend
                                                        hasthaf-namsigir: "holds wise words firmly" = "wielder of counsel" = a wise man
                                                        irillu-su: "melting body" = "melting one" = a weak man
                                                        matha-nazarul: "land warder" = a king
                                                        ur-anah: "drink of the wolf" = blood
                                                        garum-nidu: "girl of the houses" = a wife
                                                        dagukurum-hirfathra: "river-bone" = a rock
                                                        endenag-asde kasinfu: "winter spear" = an icicle
                                                        hafadur: "children of battle" = soldiers or warriors
                                                        nusuthikhi: "no blood-drenched face" = "un-blooded" = one not yet tested in battle

                                                        And derived words:

                                                        giazu: to share, to divide, to allot
                                                        athal: a secret
                                                        hasthaf: to hold firmly; to wield; to have at the ready
                                                        namsigir: counsel, advice
                                                        irillu: to melt, to become a liquid; to lose composure, to break down emotionally (lit. "water-manifest")
                                                        nazaru: a guard, a protector; a warden, a watchman; the act of watching or protection
