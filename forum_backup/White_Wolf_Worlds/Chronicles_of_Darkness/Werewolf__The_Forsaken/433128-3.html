                        Charlaquin has some really good points. I'd like to take a moment to chime in about the wait of "the target lives and keeps doing what they're doing."
                        It's a sacred hunt. If you called it, there was a reason for it. Most of the time, it's because something needed to be stopped or otherwise go down. In the case discussed earlier of the semi-unknown prey that turned out to be Rambo, why were you hunting Rambo in the first place? Is it because he kidnapped a pack mate? Did he kill them? Is he going to do it again? If he lives and keeps doing what he's doing, more of your loved ones die. Or more of your territory gets destroyed. Or more of your allies turn against you.

                        Now sometimes the stakes aren't that high. The wolf must hunt, this is establshed by theme and by Primal Urge mechanics. As such, sometimes you sacredly hunt "that tasty deer spirit I saw last week that looked ripe with essence". To do the whole sacred thing right, you need to wait until you're ready to focus solely on that hunt (any major actions to anything else does end the Siskur-Dah Condition, after all...) and then focus on that. But what if something that seems more pressing comes up? You don't honestly care if that deer spirit gets away. You weren't really going to kill it, after all; spirits come back after a couple of days if you don't devour all their essence and corpus and you had no reason to do that.
                        The main in-book consequence to this softball scenario is that it's a breaking point towards flesh. Right out of character creation those are dangerous things. Later on, maybe that would be helpful. By then, though, you might well have a much higher Primal Urge and maybe you really did need to finish that hunt or start feeling the essence-starvation.

                        In short I see a handful of bad thing that could come of skipping out on a hunt. Piling on a couple conditions to make it really clear to the players probably wouldn't hurt, but I bet an ST could make them feel it without them if they wanted to. I like the conditions idea; I don't know that the game demands it.
