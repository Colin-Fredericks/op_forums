                    So, I'm gonna cheat a bit and start with something I posted elsewhere on the Forsaken forums.

                    When I first picked up Werewolf: the Forsaken, I thought the experience was going to me much like the one I had with Vampire: the Requiem. I expected a lot of what was in Werewolf: the Apocalypse to be brought over, but stripped down to archetypes and not stereotypes, and focused on the individual player-character level struggles, not the world-spanning problems. I had very little interest in Apocalypse, but since I've almost spent more time as an ST than as a player, I made sure to always have the corebooks from all the lines in case I was asked to run one of them or introduce elements from that game. So, I bought Forsaken expecting it to collect dust more often than be read.

                    What I got was many of the conventions of Apocalypse flipped on its head and instead of fuzzy superheroes, what I was given was Uratha, The People. People with a duty to their family, their tribe (or Tribes) and to their race as a whole. I got a family with one big promise expected of the children - keep up the family tradition. I got a proper respect of the heritage of shape-shifting (it comes from animism, folks, it's that damn old!) and all the old traditions that come with it. I got The People, who understand that the separations between flesh and spirit or animal and man are false and they know it because they can see it. Every day, all around them, right there in front of their eyes. I got the children of Father Wolf and Mother Luna, the Great Hunter and the Queen of Air and Darkness. Gods of the Shadow Realm giving birth to tiny, fleeting lives because they wanted to be part of the cycle of life. Those children being the products of passion and gaining the gifts (and Gifts) of such a heritage. The power of the Wolf, the flexibility of the Moon, the drive to Hunt, and all while being something that's fully human or animal or flesh or spirit, but a balancing force between all those things. Gone were the spirits to continually praised their "oh so worthy protectors" who tried to claim that everything that came after them was "unclean". Instead I got a Spirit Realm that placed werewolves firmly within the food chain, no better or worse than any other predators, as long as they could hold their own. Also gone were the wolf-men running around in full war form developing elaborate martial arts and complex rituals. The beast-man was now for one thing - kill everything in sight - and like all such weapons, it was dangerous and not something to be toyed with. There were no more Rage traits, just rage. There were no more Great Caerns, just "our territory". There were no more epic warriors, just The People. People who struggle every day with what they see around them and use what advantages they have to make their little corner of the world better.
                    These were the first few things that drew me into Forsaken and that last statement is bolded for a reason. It's what is the foundation of Forsaken to me. Territory, Tribes, The People....all these terms make it clear to me what their focus is supposed to be - home and family. Threats from without and threats from within are always looming because the Uratha only have so much they can call their own and so many things are trying to take it away from them. Spirits, Ridden, Pure, your own Rage.....all of them could easily bring the entire house of cards down.

                    My experiences playing it have been limited (I got to run it, not be a character), but the entire game with the Forsaken was all about that. "I am a monster, but there are worse monsters out there and they can tear my world apart for no other reason for the gits and shiggles." Yeah, it can be kinda similar to Hunter, but the Uratha are hunters, so of course there will be crossover. The difference is that in Hunter, the monsters are "them". In Forsaken, the monsters are "us". Pure and Bale Hounds are Uratha, too. Spirits are "cousins" of a sort. Ridden are another "kin" of sorts as another half-flesh/half-spirit being. The threats can just as easily come from behind the borders of the territory as outside of it. In fact, they are more likely to come from within because the spirits can slip in easily and the Pure and the Bale-Hounds aren't always marked as such. The monster from within is a part of Forsaken not just because of the threats of Rage, but also the threats of family. It's another kind of personal horror. A vampire only has he own unlife to lose, an Uratha has so much more that can be destroyed and still leave him around to suffer.

Its fair to say that the best stories happen when you play the "desperation" card in regards to this troupe, that people only go talk to the evil power source when they have no choice or feel they have no choice. Alternatively the "hubris" idea works well too, someone believing they are so strong willed or righteous that they can use the evil power without being corrupted by it (and being inevitably proven wrong) also works.

                                                Even then though, that leaves the desperate, hubristic, kinda evil or blind to fall into the Maeljin's clutches. That's a pretty select group and really only half its number are sympathetic in any way.

                                                Like I said before, I really enjoy bits of Werewolf's fluff but the Maeljin are really the bit that has never sat well with me. I like the concept of Bale Hounds, because I am a big believer in the "dark reflection" troupe. I'm hoping that they get shipped over as Werewolf followers of the Idigam rather then the Maeljin in the new Chronicle book.

                                                    Bale Hounds can ascend to a Maeladar after their deaths, becoming a Maeltinet and pretty much immortal. Considering it's the World of Darkness, joining the Bale Hounds is hedging your bets with the winning side. Forsaken doesn't have the Apocalypse, but there's no sign of the world becoming a better place anytime soon.

                                                    From there it depends on which view of the Bale Hounds you decide to go with. While there are some examples in the NPCs, they're very few in number, and most fall into the line of 'they meant for the best, made a deal they probably shouldn't have.' Whereas werewolves, like humans, are individuals first and foremost. It wasn't a saintly act that led characters into their First Change. It was chance and genetics. A person who gets a thrill by telling lies may find that, when the existence of what is essentially a god of lies is revealed to them, that their little pleasures can be put to great purpose, and turned from a temporary thrill to a deeply, spiritually rewarding act.

                                                    As for a more intellectual or analytic part of the game, spirits don't have nametags. You'd probably be right if you guessed that that red, white and blue eagle spirit is somehow related to the United States of America, but that walking mirror lined with thorns that screams a glass-shattering scream might be another matter. And while you might be able to gang up on that mirror, as a spirit it's functionally immortal. You can tear it to pieces, but the moment whatever got it fired up in the first place happens again, it'll go around causing trouble again. Sure, there's a lot of leaps, but solving the mysteries behind some of the more problematic spirits can involve very rigorous investigations. And if it turns out whatever's causing that mirror to pop up isn't something you can fix (unless you want to burn down the mirror-factory turned orphanage), then you have to investigate further for a ban, or find some sort of diplomatic way to solve the problem. And diplomacy with spirits....well I'd say it fits more under the Wits stat than Intelligence. Trying to find out a ban can be a long and arduous journey as well, uncovering ancient secrets and symbols, making deals you might not want to make deals with (why would I ever make a deal with a Maeltinet? What if it's the only way to stop this thing?), or a willingness to make other sacrifices.

                                                    Beyond that, you have the whole power vacuum thing going on, except with wider scale consequences. Maybe the Prince died and there was a furor until a new Prince came up, and they favor one covenant over another. But maybe you manage to find the ban of the shrieking mirror and bind it to one place. Now you have to deal with whatever ripples outward from that. Maybe with the shift in resonances, the Silence is growing more powerful. Perhaps due to all the broken windows now there's swarms of shrieking shard spirits slithering through the streets. Maybe the whole mirror thing happened years ago, and the thing was bound beneath the orphanage before the pack disappeared mysteriously. You don't know about that, just that the pack was just like yours, they saw a problem and they went out and dealt with it. And now its your turn, because there's a violent gang of kids out there with mirrors for eyes.
