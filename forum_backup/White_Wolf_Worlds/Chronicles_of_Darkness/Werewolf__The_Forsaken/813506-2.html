    Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
    So, I'm getting a lot of "pick up W:tF 2ed", but I need to point out that until Onyx Path books are sold in stores again, that is NOT happening. I already bought 1ed and never use it, NO WAY am I buying a non-refundable PDF with no guarantee that I won't be wasting my money.
    No one seems to have mentioned the Idigam, the big, bad, world fucking engines of destruction that are the main large scale antagonists of the book. There's plenty of backstory available in the corebook, along with multiple setting locale information for those that want to play in an area of the world that are different from home. The difference between them and the Wyrm is that you don't require the entire Garou Nation to go down in order to achieve a victory against them, but you do need some outside co-operation to get it done. I like games that give the PC's a realistic chance of still being alive at the end of the story, something that wasn't really possible if you went directly against the Wyrm. If you let metaplot get in the way the PC's were observers in the grand scheme of things, real change was always brought about by signature characters. Forsaken lets the PC's take center stage in a way that Apocalypse rarely did, and Apocalypse was my main gameline in CWoD. I liked following the metaplot, but playing it wasn't as entertaining as reading it. Forsaken gets away form that and that's a selling point to me.

    Now, if you like the Cosmic level antagonists they are still available, but the burden of setting up the conflict resides with the ST. It's their job to make sure there is a point to the story, not some random supplement that may suddenly change the direction you were taking the chronicle because new info invalidates events in your story (if you are trying to use the 'canon' setting material). The Maeljin are still present, but are left up to the ST to define at the moment beyond some very brief write-ups in 1e (Blasphemies and Lore of the Forsaken) and Inferno. They aren't statted up so that players can just kill to add another notch to their "how many gods I killed" belt. If your ST wants to stat them up so their players can do that there is nothing stopping them from doing so, and if they do they aren't "playing the game wrong". You can have your cake and eat it too, but you have to bake the cake yourself. Feel free to steal that as a sig.

    I'm also a fan of the shift of focus from being spirit cops to alpha predators/hunters, Gifts power scaling based on Renown so that Renown isn't an XP sink, Gift lists being thematically tied together, non werewolves now being able to participate in the pack, and the overall toughness buff that werewolves received compared to 1e. At the moment there is literally only one thing I would change from the core rules and it's a minor point (I have Gauru form claws count as magic based weaponry for certain game effects, like Vampires' damage downgrading, but not the other forms' claws. Personal preference that doesn't impact the game all that much.)

    As for future supplements I'd like to see, a Predators like book that covers the Pure in more depth along with all of the Firstborn totems and other spirit pro/antagonists, another that covers Bale Hounds and the Maeljin (to add the Apocalypse like flavor back, some people did like those elements, just not in the core rules), and then something to go over the way Lodges work in the current rules setup (I kind of expect The Pack to have a bit of what I'm looking for, Chris, Claire and Bunyip may have info they can share on that at some future date). A big Hunting Grounds book that covers differences in the way Tribes go about their hunts in urban vs rural environments would be nice too.

        I'm gonna be honest, the "Uratha are just spirit border patrol cops" thing has about as much validity to it as "Gauru are just furry planeteers". When you boil the central conflict of a setting down to a simple glib analogy, of course it's going to make the setting sound lame.

        What Forsaken 2e did to win me over that neither 1e or Apocalypse was able to do to my satisfaction was let me play a horror movie werewolf. Then, it took it a step further and gave me a versimilar reason for my horror movie werewolf to transition into a proud member of the complex werewolf culture that the game set up. I can start out a clueless bloke who just went through his first change and is terrified of what he's become, tries to fight his nature, and then gets introduced to the ways of The People, who teach him to balance his human side with his spirit/wolf side. He can slowly learn why it's important to maintain the barrier between the worlds of spirit and flesh within his territory, he can join a Tribe that knows why a particular kind of prey is most dangerous and how best to hunt them. He can take time to learn about his newfound cultural heritage, and it will all make sense from a character development perspective.

        That's what I love about Forsaken, and about 2e in particular. It completely skirts the comparisons to Captain Planet and Yu Yu Hakusho by presenting a clear and intuitive-feeling path from ordinary human to Luna's chosen predator (or Gaia's chosen warrior, if you like). Instead of that abrupt, jarring "You didn't think it would be like in the movies, did you?" Moment, it eases you into learning what it means to be a werewolf in this setting. And that is fucking awesome.

                                                ​I disagree to a small degree here, because when NWoD was launched it was specifically stated that the devs were designing the games how they would have developed the original lines if they had the knowledge they accumulated over 10+ years of development, the new lines were meant as replacements for the old lines.                                                             EDIT

                                                            Originally posted by reseru View Post
                                                            So simply out of the sake of curiosity, what were some holdovers from the 'previous' game?
                                                            W:tF 1ed felt directionless. Differences are really just a number of small things that overall add a lot to how the game is approached and played. In W:tA tribes were defined by their ideology and cultural aspects: Uktena were indians, Black Furies were feminists, Children of Gaia were human-clinging Greenpeace activists, Bone Gnawers were hobbos living on the street (I'm purposefully simplifying, please don't explode over those parallels). It carried in some way to the W:tF, differences were significant between editions, but a lot felt that new tribes are just a mix of portions of old tribes and given a motto by which they lived, while being mostly stripped from the ideological/cultural background of the predecessors. That's why Iron Masters could be percieved by some as mix of Bone Gnawers and Glass Walkers or Blood Talons as mix of Fianna, Get of Fenris and Red Talons.

                                                            Tribes were there on the stage of character creation, but their overall impact on the game itself wasn't big, since it was all about the pack now and not about the tribes anymore. But they were in a new game, put in a bit of a limbo of being significant and at the same time not at all.

                                                            In 2ed tribes from 1ed were defined by what they hunt. It was one of those "small, but big" changes since suddenly when you were playing Hunter in the Darkness you weren't "a stealhy hunter guy who protects the wilds and who never belonged before first change, but also sometimes inhabits city and protects significant things there and also...", but "you are psycho killer from slasher films killing swarms of rat/spider demons". Now when in your chronicle pack of Hunters in the Darkness will appear in a territory, your players will immediately think "OH MY GOLLY, @%#^ IS GOING DOWN" because everybody knows why they are here and what they go for.

                                                            In W:tA there were alphas as leaders of the pack, W:tF 1ed mentions those in few places (actually even at the beginning, in the tribes description) and even though from the read you are supposed to think that packs function a bit differently and alpha concept isn't as significant in W:tF, a concept still is present there and packs are made of werewolves only with some sort of leader on the hunt. Different than W:tA, but there are similarities. So while W:tF wolf-blooded aren't kinfolk, they fundamentally still function as breeding machines for furry superhumans.

                                                            2ed puts alpha term to trash and packs can consist of werewolves, wolf-blooded and even (non)ordinary humans and now everybody adds to the hunt. PCs with different tribes add to it different and wolf-blooded have some powers that werewolves never will have. Every wolf-blooded now has some distinct, werewolfy feature (Tell) that defines it. Moreover, people can become wolf-blooded due to the exposure to werewolves now, it's not only about blood lineage as previously.
                                                            This is a game changer, since wolf-blooded can have very big impact on the chronicle and not be only "ultimately a bit better than normal human helpers". Every wolf-blooded power is designed in a way to be potential plothook. Werewolves of which pack are murdering people on PCs territory? (None, it's a guy who became wolf-blooded after last PC action and who can now take one of the werewolf forms). There's a guy who is Pure slave that can sense hosts, every single one of them from 10 miles radius (Host-Ache tell). Forsaken must steal him to find out if they really destroyed Shartha during their last adventure.
                                                            Oh and wolf-blooded and humans can perform or be part of some werewolf rituals. Suddenly capable and well organised wolf/moon cults are a thing in 2ed.

                                                            Gifts. In W:tA and W:tF they worked on a scale from 1 to 5. In W:tF 2ed there's only linear progression in Moon Gifts and you get them for free, so you don't have curse of "I will never get 5th!". Other Gifts (Shadow and Wolf) have Facets and they are unlocked if you have appropriate renown. They are also designed in specific way, more here:


                                                            http://theonyxpath.com/the-giver-of-...-the-forsaken/
                                                            http://theonyxpath.com/the-giver-of-...-the-forsaken/

                                                            It's a big difference now between 1ed and 2ed, gifts are less of superpowers and more like aspects of wolf/werewolf that are in tales and trust me, they are very, very well designed. Hell, there's even a power that allows you to blow houses like a wolf from "Three little pigs"

                                                            "Nature worth saving" trope from W:tA was a bit present in W:tF 1ed, especially as I said before around Hunters in the Darkness. Now there's 0 of that. Nature works as an ally of wolf/werewolf, but is a fucking scary place and a tool of hunt. Also a place that will terrify PCs themselves (Touchstone: Call of the Wild)

                                                            Harmony is another aspect in which W:tF had some influence from W:tA even though W:tA didn't have morality mechanics. Oath of the Moon was a big deal in W:tA and remained in W:tF 1ed. In 2ed it seems to be less important for morality mechanics which by the way was redesigned completely and "you should not eat human flesh" is more of a social and narrative problem and mechanics itself show now cannibalism as a dillemma that is there and can be counted into PCs actions. In W:tA and W:tF it really wasn't a problem, no sane player would even think about it or eat anybody bar the really extreme situations.

                                                            Erm...those were only on top of my head and already post seems to be too long.

                                                            Conclusion: There's a lot of minor things in 1ed that were a tip towards W:tA, but that overall were unnecessary in W:tF. 2ed got rid of it altogether and gave game clear direction and distinct mood and atmosphere.
