              A weapon that acts as a bane against everything and does aggravated damage against everything would certainly be considered overkill.

              Still, it might fit as a five dot fetish, with a Rank 5 spirit of violence or slaughter inside it. The way it works now is that the dot value of the fetish corresponds to the Rank of the spirit inside. Four dot fetishes can be, in terms of weapons, 'A weapon with a powerful enhancement such as increased damage,' but I think for aggravated damage against everything you'll need to go higher, five dot fetishes are considered, 'A truly deadly weapon like one that cuts Essence or Willpower as well as flesh,' and can also be, 'something that allows the Uratha to outright break some rules such as using their Defense against mental attacks or to count themselves as the bane of something that doesn't usually suffer a bane.'

              Reason suggests they're trying to limit it to one thing, like a bane to humans, and not everything in the World of Darkness, so you're pushing it, but it's your game to allow or forbid. At the point where you're doing aggravated damage like that, you wouldn't need to join the Lodge of Arms for a paltry +3 dice to combat rolls you could get elsewhere without making a potentially life-long commitment. Just max out your Weaponry and Strength, grab a specialization, and use an All Out Attack or Willpower for +3 a pop. Or both for +6. Odds are anything you hit with it isn't going to be standing around and fighting back anyways.

                  I'm personally a bit leery with allowing the aggravated damage to everything bit. On the one hand, it takes a level 5 Rite with major drawbacks in order to deal aggravated against other wolves (which can be regenerated), and you need Rank plus certain Warding Gifts to qualify for dealing aggravated against most spirits, or going on quests to seek out a Bane for more powerful spirits. Aggravated damage is a handy thing in Werewolf because it does something additional to spirit prey that it doesn't do to anyone else - destroys essence. Aggravated damage can actually cause a spirit to flat out perish instead of just falling into slumber.


                  On the other hand, agg damage from only one source against a spirit and other lethal won't be enough to consume all the essence and you'll either need to drain the essence another way, or put the spirit to slumber - its almost all or nothing. The Hosts discorporate. Humans are already easy prey in combat, its getting them into claw range. Werewolves can just be fought using silver if you want agg damage. I don't think the Ridden are any different from regular humans when it comes to the difference between lethal and agg - there's the occasional Dread Power that has limited benefit, but most aren't an issue against a sacred hunt.


                  Honestly, I don't think it'll be too mechanically unbalancing to allow it from a combat standpoint. The problem that I can see, though? It would bypass plot. Spirit banes are designed to require quests to fulfill, which require hunts of a kind. Wolves have taboo about using either the ritual or silver against their own. Idigam are scary things that should be difficult to understand, and a desperate search for a way to kill; finding a fetish that acts as a bane would be an awesome story for a way to kill it. Generalized agg-dealing weapons have the potential to shift the tone of the game from a hunt, to just going out to slaughter enemies in combat machine mode. That would lead to an "arms race" between players and ST with their stats, and... well, I just think you need to consider if agg dealing fetishes are thematically appropriate for the setting.

Two legendary blacksmiths, arguing over which was the better, challenged each other by placed their finest swords in a river. The Muramasa would cleanly slice through any fallen leaf or flower that touched its blade, destroying it. The Masamune, however, didn't slice a single leaf.                                                   As has already been mentioned several times, one of the examples of the capacity for a 5 dot fetish is to "to count themselves as the bane of something that doesn’t usually suffer a bane."

                                                  Now there's only one thing that could mean, which is that it does aggravated damage to something. Allowing it to do aggravated damage to everything would surely fit the idea of a legendary weapon fit for a hero, harnessing the power of a demigod bound in iron to fell his adversaries, but it does present an issue of breaking one of the main themes of the game by providing a one-button solution to every hunt.

                                                  Still though, the example is there. It'd be silly to print that line and then tell people to avoid that logical conclusion entirely. I suggest putting a restriction or a hazard on it. Like it acts automatically as the bane of a certain kind of creature (Vampires, Earth Spirits, etc.), or it has to taste the blood/essence of something before it can act as its bane (thus retaining at least some of the aspects of the hunt), or every attack with it is a kuruth trigger.

                                                  Personally as long as you're running the game strictly, make the pack hunt down an appropriate Dihir and run through the logical consequences of such an action as well as deal with the aftermath, I'd be fine with it. That's in the hands of the ST though, the pack should have to go through hell to get and keep it.

                                                  Optimally I think such a weapon should be something that the pack is weary to take on the hunt. Something that is hidden beneath floorboards or in places that aren't for only the most dire of circumstances or dangerous of hunts.
