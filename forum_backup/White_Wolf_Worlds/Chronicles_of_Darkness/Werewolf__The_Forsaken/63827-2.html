    I think Lunacy as an idea is good, terror at seeing something clearly supernatural AND clearly triggering all deeply buried reflexes to run (because it is a hunter and you are the prey) works.

    I think it should be a bit more generous though, mostly to make things a bit easier for Hunters and the like.
    With the system as is, you NEED to be of near perfect willpower to really be a threat to a werewolf (or you can use the optional rules, which bring the threshold down a bit), which I think is a bit too easy.

    Toning it down a bit, maybe adding some modifiers so that it makes it easier for the enemy hunter (and still potent on, say, the crowd of angry bikers) would be nice.

    I do not think it should be an active power, but maybe having gifts to augment it would work well (which then would need to be activated).


    Remembering the second book of the Dresden Files, I think even with just normal fear and such and the general desire of almost any supernatural group not to have the supernatural made public, it is not that hard to make no one really believing in Werewolves, Vampires and the like work in the current age.

    Though, I think one thing should not be overlooked.
    Lunacy, like its predecessor the Delirium, should always be a hindrance as well as a tool.
    For every mess up it covers, it also can end up driving humans you wanted to protect right into the arms of danger because they panicked, it is what makes relationships with normal mortals very difficult, it is what potentially drives even your wolfblooded family away...


    As for Gauru, I am all for scrapping the limit.
    I found the near Wolf useful enough the way it was in Apocalypse, with the near Man filling for fancy weapon use (but, admittedly, being the ones form I always thought least useful in both lines).
    It is also what people usually expect when they think "werewolf in a fight".
    I do think making it a form were Death Rage is more likely, limiting the use of tools (not to a ridiculous extend) and maybe giving the other forms their niches should suffice.
    Let the near wolf make use of its greater dexterity to give them more defence (higher instead of lower of dex/wits), maybe give them the better bite again and do not neglect the fact that they really can move fast (both better Ini as well as better speed).

        I took the timer off personally; there's no hard limit. You get a grace period of your Stamina + Primal Urge (though this may be shifted to Resolve + Primal Urge as a possibility to reflect less physical strain and more "I'm fighting not to drop into Death Rage everytime I go this route"), and every turn past that (without Instinct or Gift etc. usage) increases Death Rage penalties by -1 for provocations cumulatively in the scene. So long as you can "Keep your partial cool" you're good to go, but in actual gameplay it means that you still don't want to constantly run around in that form; you're gonna snap, and it's gonna be -really- ugly, so most use it sparingly regardless of any exhaustion or hard "unless you Death Rage, you're done".

        I've always seen as adopting the Gauru form as pretty much Riding the Wave ala Vampire anyhow; I'm not too big on a hard once per scene or x number of turns limitation for it, I much prefer something more Risk vs Reward to tempt players to skirt the line. The more you use it scene, the more aggressive you become until you snap. I'm with PenDragon on this one; in the meantime this seems to be the best solution for me to deal with an inbetween "Can't just run around in it without issues, but have extended times and more chances to adopt it". Want to keep using it for prolonged periods? Prepare to hunt and kill uncontrollably unless you've really got a good grip on yourself.

        As for Lunacy; I just wrapped what it was into the Predatory Aura; it has Fight, Flight and Competitive as aspects; I could see a bonus to its use being added on while in Gauru though if one wanted to reinforce that it's that much more raw rage, terror and so on, even if just to the Flight lash-out.

                A house rule I've recently trying out is removing the effect Stamina+Primal Urge limit on Gauru. Instead, you simply roll a cumulative number of dice for every turn you're in. So 1 turn/1 dice, 2 turns-2 dice, and so on. If you hit your Harmony rating in successes (or your Resolve+Primal urge, whichever is higher) then you immediately fall to Death Rage. No take-backsies, no quick shift to another form. Just a Death Rage.

                What I've found is it gives a risk-vs-reward scenario. You no longer know exactly how long you're going to be in Gauru, and you could push your luck if you've got a High Harmony rating; but every roll of the dice is a potential doomsday button. Gifts that add extra turns in Gauru or remove turns just affect the dice. If you remove 3 turns from someone's roll, then they start out immediately with 3 dice on their plate and go from there. If your auspice moon gives you extra turns, you don't roll the dice until you've hit that buffer.

                So far, it's been fairly interesting! But I'm always eyeballing it.

                        Reading through the flavor text in the core book it seems to me that the war-form was intended to be a form that you didn't want to use if you didn't have to thematically. I feel like they were almost tempted to just not allow it as a form that you could slip into voluntarily, that it was what you became when you lost all control, but obviously that would run too much against the grain. Even I would have grimaced had that been rendered the case and I kind of like the idea, as strange as that sounds.

                        I could perhaps stomach a mechanic similar to Riding the Wave that vampires have, reigning in one's rage however briefly into a kind of lucid fury. Something a newly Changed werewolf might struggle with but also something that as they grow older and more powerful manage to bring under a degree of control. However, to balance things out, when an elder wolf does fall to kuruth it should be cataclysmic. If the mechanics were elegant and fun, this I would not mind. I might even love it.

                        As it is currently, barring updates from the GMC, the main problem with the war-form is not the time limit. It's clunky and intrusive and game-y, but it functions. The main problem is the lack of extra attacks whilst in it; if you want to optimize damage, pick a fighting style that allows you to make extra attacks. With the proper merits, both Dalu and Urshul forms become more deadly than Gauru even with the stat differences. The main benefit of the war-form is that it's somewhat tankier, not that it can dish out more damage. This is a thematic breakdown for the game.

                        Now so far in the GMC-books that have come out, extra-attacks seem to be done away with. None of the fighting styles currently written using it (the GMC rules) allow for them and I've yet to see any powers or rules that allow for it (if I'm wrong somebody please tell me, I've been known to miss pretty important things on first read-throughs of books). As such a direct translation would at least return Gauru to a sort of theoretically optimal combat form, but that would be a pretty immense cop-out considering the opportunity presented. Beefing it up, perhaps making it scale better in various ways, and ultimately making it more risky whilst doing away with the time limit are all things I'm in favor for.

                        Completely unnecessary to the conversation, but the way it's handled in the larp is thus: There's a damage cap, both a soft one that's pertinent to your character and a hard one that's universal. The hard damage cap is 5, so even with a beastly built character you can only ever do 5 damage in a single turn. To beef up Gauru form, they doubled the equipment modifiers of its natural attacks (so +4 bite and +2 claw) plus a +2 to your damage cap, thus raising the hard cap to 7. It's very gamey and meta, but it does make Gauru form genuinely terrifying and regardless of your character's build objective the most potentially dangerous. I wouldn't recommend this sort of system for a table-top, but I thought it worth mentioning.

                        As for Lunacy, I'd like to see it altered somewhat but not done away with. Supernaturals do need ways to keep themselves secret from mortal society, whether they're built into their templates or arise from internal factions that make certain that it just never hits youtube. I've never been a fan with how Lunacy works as is, the fact that mortals can just sort of graduate out of it essentially means there's an upper echelon of hunters that you actually need to worry about and then a whole slew that will crap their pants immediately if you go Gauru. Giving it modifiers, conditions, and allowing the werewolf to manipulate it in some way would be interesting but ultimately I'd like to see it be something that the character doesn't actually have any control over.

                        My recommendation? Tie it to the effect that Primal Urge has on normal mortals. Newly Changed werewolves give off a slight but tangible predatory aura that unnerves people even when in human form, but nothing that would incite a bout of temporary insanity that witnessing Gauru should. Meanwhile elders can barely go into town even in their human form without causing bouts of bedlam in the local population. As previously mentioned, adding powers and merits that allowed a werewolf to further manipulate it would be pretty boss too.
