One of them is a Bicycle/Madness spirit and the other is a House Fly/Human lust spirit. So yeah the various ilthums of the local hisil are going to be really mad at this pack cuz aiding and sheltering magath. Now, the player wants their character to learn rites from the two spirits in question....so I wanted suggestions for rites that could come from these two magaths or magaths in general.

      Since Magath are fusions of incompatible concepts, enacting their Rites may cause some unintended consequences. You may choose to make some of these Rites have trade-offs for the Packmates, or cause some sort of contamination within the Shadow.

      The Pack consecrates a bicycle by reading genuine transcripts of mad conversations and spinning the wheels like in a Tibetan prayer. The next person to move the bicycle is stricken with the same mental condition as the author of the text. This may act as a lure for thieves, or as a barrier against the Pack's enemies.

      The Pack anoints a mentally ill person with bike grease and adorns them with reflective accessories. When treated as a travel guide, that person may lead the Pack to a site tied to that mental strain, either symbolically, or through personal experience. All participants must ride there on bicycles, and this can be done even if the destination is unknown, or otherwise blocked off.

      The Pack can mark a place, so that acts of lust within it will attract flies. It requires making a picture of a fly drawn with lipstick. Food will be filled with maggots, buzzing will fill the air and crawling and flying of insects will distract from carnal activities. This Rite is mostly used as punishment, sabotage or a very lazy way of chaperoning.

      The Pack marks a person to make them center of attention to flies and human beings. This aids in social endeavours, to the point that people are unbothered by the swarming insects. That indifference does not extend to the subject of the Rite, who has to grit their teeth all the way through. Also, after the Rite lapses, witnesses will realize something was amiss. Some have concluded that that the fly-covered figure they were wooed by was Beelzebub himself.

                      It sure was.

                      If you need more Rites, have these two. Again, I assume performing them will wreck the local Resonance, somehow:

                      The Pack meticulously cleans up an area, sterilizing all possible surfaces and objects, and then installing a sanitizer dispenser. That location then acts as if it possessed the Slick Environmental Tilt manifesting as invisible mud - thick and slippery. Only two types of people are immune to these effects. They must be similarly purified, up to their worn and carried belongings. Blood and tears do not disrupt that immunity, but sweat does. Alternatively, they must be thoroughly soiled - they, everything they have on them, and even their eyes covered in dirt or mud.

                      The Pack acquires a manual for disinfection - even a poster from a toilet will do. They then read from it, following the instructions to the letter, while smearing their subject in mud. They then bury that person within a layer of dirt inside a place of healing - any place with the right Resonance will do. While giving them means to breathe is not technically necessary for the Rite, it's strongly recommended. Over the next few hours, the soil pulls out all pathogens from the patient, curing minor infections and helping against major ones.

                      As a side-note. I can imagine their new Magath friend standing in the corner with Cum-Fly, both doing nervous rubbing motions with their forelimbs. They may not understand each other or even themselves anymore, but this one thing feels safe and familiar.

                                      Rite design isn't my forte, but an observation on magath that may help generally: If you look at example magath throughout the books, a trend that emerges is that hybrids who make it work tend to have common ground to their component resonances that imply a theme.

                                      Magath are ostracized by most spirits because their portfolios don't emerge from natural associations, but even desperate feeding circumstances will still see a spirit try to eat something kind of related to what it is, metaphorically strained or not — and if that's not how it got there, knowing how it got there (particularly if some spiritually-traumatic event or other non-"a wizard did it" origin is involved) will provide symbolic grounding for What This Magath Is.

                                      Spirits may not be the same thing as ghosts, but they are creatures of history and the sum of their parts; gradual divergence from repeating the circumstances of their birth (and death and rebirth) is how they shuffle into the impurity of the higher Ranks.

                                          I don't mind homebrew that breaks away from the normal rules, but where the rules are misunderstood I feel compelled to clarify. Frankly, this is a case where the rules are ambiguous. Where do Rites come from? Aside from the obvious, that is. Some entrees in the Rites section of the second edition make it sound as though Rites come from actions and broad patterns, others describe them as a "pact." Maybe they have multiple origins? In any case I am not any more convinced that mere magaths give rise to new Rites than I am convinced that they give rise to new Gifts, though I can't say you're wrong in any version I remember.

                                                      Since it's a Rite for the antagonist, I will skip over how it's performed and go straight to the effects:

                                                      The first Rite is simple - the Occultist prepares a party, but replacing some of the food with small rocks or grit. If successful, any unwarned participants of the party will believe the minerals to be normal food, even as their teeth break and their insides are shredded. Any medical emergencies or incongruities stemming from that source alone, like people slurring their speech or lying in pain on the floor will be ignored by the revelers until sunrise.

                                                      The second Rite must take place within a prominent Stone Resonance, be it a cave or an obvious stone circle. As the Rite culminates, all unwary or willing participants are placed in a suspended animation of sorts - immobile, unaging and experiencing only an ecstatic stupor. This lasts indefinitely until somebody tries to physically "wake" one of the revelers. The unfortunate(?) rescue subject then turns to stone permanently (still ensouled and experiencing the joy of the party). All other party goers return to normal.
