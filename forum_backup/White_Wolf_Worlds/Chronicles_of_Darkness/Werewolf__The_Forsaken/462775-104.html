                    It really depends on the werewolves in question. There's an entire Lodge dedicated to making up urban legends to scare people away from sacred places and to give some 'cause' for the bodies that pop up. But plenty of loci are going to be in places people will stumble upon. A big one in first edition was a painting on the ground floor basement of a tall building. People would be passing by it and watching it all the time. In a lot of cases constant human presence are why things become loci in the first place. There's some people here who balk at the idea of playing characters that kill humans, and some who play characters who enjoy it.

                    Also I don't believe there's any protective Rites, in the core at least. Might be thinking of Wards, from the Warding Gifts, and having someone just ignore those would be a huge red flag.

                    At the very least, the werewolves would take note and observe them, using their various powers and senses to pull apart their secrets and figure out what they are and why they're there. Hunters in Darkness, especially, would keep an eye out for any violation of their sacred places.

                    My character was never particularly territorial but if he was he'd probably eat them.

                                                Anything that has banes probably qualifies as having "weakness to mystical sources". I have to assume that "weakness to mystical sources" does not mean a generic weakness to all mystical sources. Just that some mystical sources of damage are extra potent. Otherwise it would make no sense for vampires to explicitly take lethal from werewolf bites since vampires do not have a generic weakness to all mystical sources. They have very specific vulnerabilities against sunlight, fire and personal banes.
