    Fourth Faction of Werewolves - Neither Forsaken, Pure Nor Bale Hound

    01-31-2014, 10:22 AM
    So I was thinking over the possible Ghost Wolf-born tribes in The Rage, and it got me thinking of how these tribes might fall into the political landscape of Werewolf: the Forsaken. From what I have read, they are presented as options for the Forsaken side of things, but I had a more interesting idea: they fall onto neither existing side. These three tribes (I believe there are three, please correct me if I am wrong), if used in a game, could potentially shake things up by neither identifying with the Forsaken or the Pure.

    How might this be possible?

    The three new tribes do not like the politics of either faction, seeing them as extremes. They aim to adopt a centrist position among the tribes, neither pro- nor anti-Luna in full, but leaning a little to either side. They are also divided on Shadow-Policing vs. Shadow-Serving, and whether or not Pangaea is a good or a bad thing for the modern world. Here's how I imagine things:

    Tower Hounds (Anzaghar Ilthum): The Tower Hounds are presented as a secret police, the "watchers who watch the watchdog". This, in my mind, makes them a "beta" pack to the "alpha" back of the Speakers of the Vine, who are presented as diplomats (see below). They are the most neutral towards the Pure and the Forsaken, as everyone needs someone to call out their bullcrap, but believe in the Forsaken stance of policing the Shadow. Father Wolf was the watchdog against the spirits, after all, and it is only proper that werewolves keep a lid on things. Why let things like Hosts and magath run rampant when there are more important things to do? And, as an extension of that, they are neutral on Pangaea; their problem is the immediate now, not things mythical and long-gone.

    Speakers of the Vine (Khuranu Dhugu): The Speakers of the Vine are, by oath, diplomats and arbitrators before anything else. They are the alphas of this third faction, as they take a leadership stance not unlike the Storm Lords' — that said, the Speakers see showing weakness as acceptance that one is imperfect. They lean towards the Pure because they see wisdom and fair points in their beliefs and arguments; towards the Shadow, they are neutral on whether it is policed or pandered to. In their minds, anyone can be reasoned with, and diplomacy in the Shadow is similar to diplomacy among werewolves. That being said, they feel Pangaea would be a very bad idea to bring back, as the balance of the world is already upset and needing fixing.

    Savage Priests (Idimathu): The Savage Priests are a reflection of the Fire-Touched among the Pure. Like the Fire-Touched, they accept new converts into the Forsaken fold with open arms — and, on occasion, some well-hidden silver. Their very nature and tribal oath leans them towards the Forsaken, as they see the Pure misguided and hurt from wounds that are millenniums old. Their views on the Shadow might seem odd — why would Father Wolf's own priests want to serve that which he had to control? — but there are many powerful allies in the Shadow. It takes a few honeyed words and dollops of promised Essence, but the Savage Priests have turned many of the Pure's allies to their side. They very much would like a return of Pangaea, as it is was their Father's paradise.

    Ghost Wolves: The Forsaken often look upon Ghost Wolves with disrespect or pity. The Pure outright loathe them and refer to them as "half-wolves". This third faction, however? Being born from the aspirations of Ghost Wolves who did not fit in, Ghost Wolves are accepted or outright embraced. If the Forsaken are a middle-ground and the Pure a hateful extreme, then the views on Ghost Wolves are extremely idealistic in this faction. Even the Savage Priests will leave Ghost Wolves alone, on the basis of, "A Ghost Wolf without brands is neither Pure nor Forsaken, and a Ghost Wolf with their brands is already Forsaken by nature."

    Bale Hounds: Still universally reviled. You will be hard-pressed to find wolves in this third faction that believe any of these can be redeemed. The Speakers do not trust their words or motives, the Tower Hounds want to kill every one of them for being complete monsters, and the Priests consider them heretics that should be scoured by silver and burned at the stake.


    Thoughts, anyone?
