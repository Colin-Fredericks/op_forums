    Sell me on the Tribes.

    03-02-2015, 11:02 PM
    I freely admit that WtF is my least-favorite nWoD game. Don't take that as an attack on you guys; I'm here because I want to learn.

    One of my biggest stumbling blocks with WtF is the Tribes. I'm afraid I don't get what role they're supposed to serve in a narrative; unlike the ethnic-political entities the Tribes of WtA were, they feel like vague collections of traits based around spirit individuals incredibly unlikely to ever come up in the average chronicle (the Firstborn also have the unfortunate side effect of acting as living proof of the Father Wolf myth). Many feel redundant; Blood Talons sort of just feel like "the combat guys," Hunters in Darkness feel like "the sneaky guys," Bone Shadows feel like "the spirit guys," so on and so forth. I struggle to grasp them as any sort of identity beyond a sort of spin on how you pursue your Auspice, and don't see any way for a bunch of freshly-Changed Forsaken to have a reason to join one of the Tribes other than an older werewolf telling them that they're supposed to.

    Help me out, everyone. Make me feel like Tribes are just as important as the y-splat of every other game. Give me a reason to not assume all Forsaken would be better off as Ghost Wolves.

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

          In 2e Auspice and Tribe act as two major descriptors.

          Auspice answers the question of How you hunt, and your place within a hunt in a pack.

          Tribe answers the question of What you hunt, at least most regularly and with the most focus. Each Tribe has a particular group of Sacred Prey who are considered the most dangerous prey, and which their traditions focus around preparing their members to deal with.

          For instance, the Blood Talons' favored prey is other werewolves, whether they be Pure or Bale Hounds or even other Forsaken.

          Bone Shadows' favored prey is Spirits, untainted by claims of flesh.

          Hunters in Darkness' favored prey are the Shartha, the Rat and Spider (and Lamprey, cough cough) hosts who strive to infest all life and in some way ruin Gauntlet.

          Iron Masters' favored prey is humans, and more pointedly human organizations.

          Storm Lords' favored prey are the Spirit-Ridden, those whose flesh has been usurped by the inhabitants of the Shadow until they become twisted half-spirit hybrids who are far too similar to werewolves for them to be comfortable.

          Why join the Tribes? Because their choice of prey resonates with you. You agree with what they mean with this statement: This prey is the most dangerous prey.

          This is a somewhat threadbare description, but the Onyx Path open development blog gives a somewhat more fleshed out idea here: http://theonyxpath.com/houses-of-the-moon/

                  Those are the common stigmas of the tribes, and indeed I can see it (sure did when I first read the core) but they're presented differently in tribes of the moon. One of the new aspects is the desired prey.

                  Think of it less as ethnicity (not to say there aren't families of forsaken and bloodlines) and more as religious and personal identity. Some uratha don't resonate with any of the tribal ideals and end up staying ghost wolves, some try to join and fail and end up ghost wolves for that reason. Ghost wolves are not a tribe (though some areas they act in full packs and tribe esque).

                  Blood Talons: Clear warrior tribe, but not every Blood Talon is a master killer. They have their historians, their spirit workers and jokers like any other tribe, but their focus is on werewolf prey. The Rahu will know the best way to attack them directly, the cahalith will know the desired lore of the enemy (And the war songs of their heroes). The elodoth will know the enemies connections, their life. They'll have the connections to "net" them.
                  Their tribal ban is "offer no surrender you would not accept yourself." Originating from when the first blood talons tracked Fenris-ur to his lair and fought him until death to receive his blessing. A good analog for the Talons would be the Fenrir and Wendigo combined, and generalized away from ethnicity. Every Talon can fight but not every Talon is a master warrior. It does not consume them, but they are damn good at it.

                  Bone Shadows are the curious. Curious to the point of fault. Originally they had no ban (so it goes) until they pissed off the hisil prodding at everything. Thus their ban is paying respect to spirits. In 2e their focus is, naturally, on spirits. I'm less familiar with these tribes admittedly, though I'm sure someone will pop up detailing how amazing they are (and any other tribe they love as well). From the translation guide, they're similar to the stargazers, uktena and silent striders.

                  Hunters in Darkness value the purity in their territory. Thus they hunt the 'hosts.' Spiders and rats that strengthen and weaken the gauntlet respectively. They are the stalkers in the wilderness, the wild men of Forsaken. True all tribes can have these sorts of individuals but the Hunters excel in the wilderness environs.

                  Iron Masters are most like the Glass Walkers, in that they are the most urban. Their prey is humanity (Drug dealers, bad folks, etc etc) so they excel in cities. That isn't to suggest they're more human than the others. They aren't, and people can tell. They just have an easier time with the cities.

                  Storm Lords are akin to the leader tribes. Their first born totem is the alpha of the forsaken, and the beta of Father Wolf's first born. One of their tribal themes is Defiance. Defiance of death. Defiance of the enemy. Defiance of pain. Their ban is to allow no one to tend their weakness. They are to be the strongest in the pack in the way that matters. They take the lead because fuck everything else. Their rites of passage involve tests of endurance, strength and mental fortitude. They must excel at everything because it is their duty.



                  Personally I love the Blood Talons. The warrior historian rubs me in all the right ways with my passion for anthropology and viking age lore. At the same time I love the defiant theme of the Storm Lords. One of the examples in the book is a storm lord waiting to die, he fought his way to his packs safety and was ready to die in glory. Then the spirit laughs, and the rage builds.

                  Not sure if these ramblings helped at all. on a side note if you still don't love them you can pick up the translation guide for 3$ and infuse a bit o' apocalypse tribes into the Forsaken world. Granted it's as lodges but still rocking it. My world def. has the Fenrir lodge.

                                  When considering the tribes, it is important to take into account the basic situation of a newly changed werewolf and of the setting at a larger scale.

                                  You've changed and you've probably killed someone. You already know what it's like to be hunted - strange shadowy beings stalking you, trying to kill you. Spirits and rats infesting your home and trying to get close to the people you care about. You've probably already fought for your life once or twice - and won, obviously, but it doesn't take much smarts to figure out that the next time could well be different.

                                  Your skin doesn't act the way it used to, and neither do your emotions, senses and appetites, even. You can't help looking at other people and, sometimes, thinking "food". You feel claustrophobic in your human skin, and when previously you felt like hitting somebody who pissed you off, now it's a challenge not to tear his throat out.

                                  This is where the tribes come in. This is what the tribes have to offer:

                                  As a werewolf, you'll have to fight for your life; probably on a regular basis. The Blood Talons show you how. They know how to protect yourself and those close to you; Fenris-Ur will teach you how to be an effective warrior. They also give you ways how to deal with the burning rage affecting all werewolves. The Blood Talons are the ones you approach when you don't want to wake up in the blood of your husband one day, and when you want to survive the next time the Predator Kings show up from nowhere and start biting huge chunks of flesh out of your flesh.

                                  This scary, hostile world of predator and prey you are now a part of? The Bone Shadows best understand how it works and how to make sense of it. They explain spirits. They tell you which places in the Shadow to avoid and where you'll be safe. Death Wolf provides you with the means of fighting your enemies with ways other than using your claws. And they also show you how to make sense of the death and pain and grief that always seems to follow the Forsaken around. They provide meaning in a world that doesn't appear to have any, at the first glance.

                                  There's a wolf in your heart, your bones and your flesh, and it'll never go away. The Hunters in Darkness know that wolf better than any other tribe. They know how to make sense of the rage, the new senses, instincts, demands of the wolf-heart. They also teach you how to hunt effectively, and how to survive when you're prey. Things like where to best hide, how to best track your prey, how to kill prey in as little time as possible are natural to the children of the Black Wolf.

                                  Werewolves are not human. The human skin doesn't change their basic nature; but despite the wolf-heart and spirit-soul there's still a human mind. After the traumatic experience of the First Change, which more often than not, ends with lots of blood, the Iron Masters are the ones who help a young werewolf to reintegrate into the human culture and world and to reconcile wolf-heart with human-mind. They're the ones who know how a wolf hides and hunts among sheep. Red Wolf knows the spirits that hunt in the cities and close to humanity, and his children will teach you how to hunt with cunning, how to adapt to situations that cannot otherwise be dealt with.

                                  The Storm Lords don't pick you up when you're weak. They won't comfort you in your time of need. Winter Wolf does not care about your weakness and failings. The Storm Lords cannot soothe any pain you feel from the First Change or from being what you are. The Winter Wolf will instead be here in your moments of strength and endurance. His children show you how to be your best, strongest self - how to stare this cold, dark, hostile world that wants you dead at every turn, in the face, and how to make it back down with an alpha's growl. They know how to fight. They know the spirit world, the worlds of wolf and men. All of these things are part of their responsibility.

                                  You've been beaten and bruised by this world. There's nothing that feels right. Friends? Jobs? Relationships? Family? Nothing has ever worked out for you. The world of men has let you down and failed you more often than you can count.
                                  No more. Something's got to give.
                                  Your old life is gone. You might have killed those you love during your first change. You're a monster and a hunter now. There's no way back.
                                  It's not your fault. It's THEM. This world isn't how it should be.
                                  There is hope. You've got the oldest, nastiest, toughest of all Wolf Gods on your side, giving you strength, rage, purpose and a mission - rebuild this world in your - in his - image. Make them all pay what they did to you. It's what you should do. It's what's natural to do.

                                  The First Change isn't the end of your old life, it's you reaching your destiny and becoming a true member of your family. It's when you realize your potential and the universe gives you what you're owed. The Ivory Claws teach you how people are not born equal. There are those whose blood and ancestry matters more. You're one of the lucky ones. You're special - the purest of the Pure. You're better than other people. It's quite simply genetics.
                                  This world belongs to its true rulers. The Chosen Ones, those selected by Silver Wolf to run this world, must take command.
                                  Go hunt those who would refuse to obey.

                                  Spirits stalk and try to kill you. The Hosts are at your heels, only waiting to take you down once you're weak. Werewolves hunt you as the competition you are.
                                  It's good, so very good, to have friends in this world. People who look out for you. Spirits who let you live as long as you abase yourself before them instead of attempting, vainly, to keep them in check. The Fire-Touched have more to offer than just protection from the spirits, strength in numbers and sympathetic ears for all your troubles and worries. They also have faith, insight and the certainty that their cause is the right one. And they're always, without exception, honest to you - Rabid Wolf sees lying as one of the worst of sins.
                                  Only a lunatic would refuse to take their offer and join them.

                                      If the 2e pdf is as imminent as has been indicated, you'll have the new material to draw on to answer this soon.

                                      Broadly, though, the Tribes can be seen as powerful, welcoming cults of brother/sisterhood and purpose. They offer you a goal and direction that matches with your newly emerged instincts to hunt, and give you like-minded compatriots who share your love or hate of the prey that has so enthralled you. They offer you communion with a very real demigod of the Shadow who, even if you never meet them 'in person', will be watching you, and who informs the mythology and rites that your new tribe follows. They offer you answers in the strange, mad world you've found yourself, and training, and comrades. They offer you secrets and mysteries that others do not have. They offer you a place, and they offer you power.

                                      - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
