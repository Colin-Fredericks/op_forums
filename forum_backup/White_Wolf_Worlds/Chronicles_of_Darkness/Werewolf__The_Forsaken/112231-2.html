    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    See, this I think gets at the heart of it very well. I think that's a fantastic example of what Forsaken is all about, and as you point out, no werewolves. Every time I've asked or seen someone ask this question, the answers include a variety of great examples of books, movies, tv shows, etc. that really encapsulate the essence of Forsaken. Very few, if any, of those examples actually contain werewolves. The pop culture image of what werewolves are like, and the themes that are focused on in Forsaken are just different things. That's ok, there's nothing wrong with a unique take on a folkloric creature (and truthfully, the take in Forsaken is in a lot of ways closer to original werewolf lore than the pop culture image of one is). But it does make it difficult for some people to recognize them as that creature if they're only familiar with the pop culture version. Fortunately, it sounds like the new Wolf Blooded are giving us that iconic movie werewolf that many people expect, and the new Harmony is giving us a way for a recently first changed Uratha to go through a process of learning to control her nature by embracing what she is instead of fighting it. Exactly the tools that I feel Forsaken needs to bridge the gap that a lot of newcomers have.
    Personally, I think there are two reasons for the new take on werewolves in WoD.

    1. The traditional view is very close to Vampire. Vampires and werewolves used to be essentially the same thing in European myths, and it still shows. It does not help that while vampires got developed into their own thing, for a long time werewolves stayed as the Jekyll and Hyde type. Having a game that focuses on man vs wolf as the monster is quite farmiliar to how vampire all ready works. To make the games differ, WtA focused on much more the Native American and non-european myths and spirituality. Forsaken follows this route.

    2. Traditional Werewolves would not make a good WoD game. I'll be honest, one of the best things about WoD is how deep into weirdness it can go. Alongside to being similar to VtR (or B&S), it would not really be very WoD game to have the main splat essentially be normal and then be ST controlled during the full moon. You do need the society there to go all WoD.

        I've heard this argument before, but I disagree. Werewolf and vampire are (or should be, in my opinion) both about being somewhere between human and monster, and the personal horror comes from reconciling the two. The difference is that vampire is about the descent into monstrosity - you start out mostly human, and over time the reality of having to live as a monster, hiding from the sun and feeding on others to survive, coupled with the long years of immortality grinds away at your Humanity, making you more and more jaded until one day there's nothing left.

        Werewolf, on the other hand, you're totally human right up until you're not. And then you are again, only now you're lying naked in the middle of nowhere covered in your husband's blood. You're not holding off against a slow descent into monstrosity, you have a full-blown monster slumbering inside of you from the moment you're bitten, and you need to either find a way to keep it asleep for good, or at least find a way to gain some semblance of control when you do lose it. And just like with repressed anger, the more you try to hold it back, the worse it gets when it finally breaks free, the only way to live with it is to accept it, to let it out regularly, in the proper time and place, to put your rage to use against the real monsters that are out there. At least, that's what those nutjobs who call themselves "The People" and claim you were born to be one of them keep telling you. Maybe they're right, but is it worth the risk to try to find out? At least, that's what I've always wanted Werewolf to be. And I really don't think it's all that similar to Vampire beyond a very superficial "you frenzy sometimes" mechanic. However...

        Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post
        2. Traditional Werewolves would not make a good WoD game. I'll be honest, one of the best things about WoD is how deep into weirdness it can go.         This is absolutely true. The game I want would really not be all that fun for most people, and it doesn't really have enough to it to make a full-blown game line around. Which is why I'm so happy to see that it's one of the many play style options that are being accommodated in Idigam. I have always been able to play that character in Forsaken, but it took me a long time to realize that, because it's totally there, the books just don't really focus on it. Now, it's getting more tools to make it easier to shift the focus onto that aspect of the game, without taking away from the heroic or animistic, or fraternal, or any other aspect that also exists in the game.

                    Originally posted by Ana Mizuki View Post

                    I think the question here is, then; what would your ideal werewolf be able to do outside changes? Is the only thing that makes them werewolf the curse and the full moon changes?
                    There would probably be some residual animalistic traits. Heightened senses, more in touch with base instincts... Ideally, the more the character embraced her wolf side, the stronger those powers would become when she wasn't in wolf form. If her Harmony was out of whack enough, she might not even have any powers in human form at all. But the more she lets the wolf out, the more it affects her human life, giving her advantages like using the higher of Wits or Dexterity for defense like animals do, bonuses to perception rolls for having heightened senses. Maybe bring the animism element into it a bit, become steadily more in touch with the spirit world as you become more wolf-like. But it's also possible to take that too far, become too wolf-like, too spirit. Start taking penalties on normal human social interaction, picking up Bans, etc.

                    Also, I wouldn't restrict transformation to full moon only. I actually really like the idea of Auspice, and would have the change happen on the character's Auspice moon, and also with sufficient negative stimuli. This would also be balanced against Harmony. The more in touch with the wolf she is, the easier it would be to trigger a transformation, but the more control over herself she would have when transformed. On one end of the spectrum, she'd only be forced to transform on Auspice moon rise, but when she did it would be full-on Death Rage, no control, no memory of the events afterwords. On the opposite end she'd always be fully in control, but would have a very difficult time keeping from transforming any time something irritates her, which causes its own problems.

                    Fortunately, this looks like pretty much exactly the direction Harmony is going in, and even if the details are not quite my ideal version, at least it will have the kind of feel I want from that internal struggle, and the balancing act a Werewolf needs to perform to keep herself from becoming a monster.

                                                            Originally posted by nofather View Post
                                                            I'm sure we'll get a spoiler one Friday or another.

                                                            I'm in agreement that it was weird, as well, especially given how important it was in regards to getting Gifts, which we've already established had wonky mechanics themselves.
                                                            Also the return in investment in 'honorary spirit rank' was pretty underwhelming.

                                                            I could see some persistent renown conditions being available, but we'll see. One of the best things about GMC/Blood and Smoke, is that it definitely encourages more player/storyteller interaction, rather than encouraging the more common, almost antagonistic relationship it's easy to fall into. So hopefully we'll get more of that.
                                                            I'm of the opinion that Renown and Gifts should not really be interrelated in the new system. Precisely for the reason you mentioned nofather: Conditions. That's a new aspect of the NWoD system post GMC and I think tying Renown to its own set of Conditions that give you a variety of useful effects is a much better use of the idea of Renown.

                                                            As I mentioned previously on this thread, I have issues with Gifts. In that they seemed really unfocused and in a lot of case un-Werewolf to me. Some made sense and I liked, ones that enhance your wolf nature or help with shapeshifting, or manipulate the Shadow or weather. That all felt kinda "Werewolf" to me. Others...not so much. A lot seemed to be powers that were present "just because" in a lot of ways and that ticked me off. Not to mention they were divided by 101 categories: General, Auspice, Tribe, Renown etc, it all needed to be streamlined.

                                                            Needless to say I'm looking forward to the Friday blog this week, I've got high hopes that at least some of the issues I have are straightened out.

                                                            I guess one thing that bugged me about Gifts was the same thing that bugged me about Requiem 1.0 Disciplines: You needed these powers to do the sorta thing that these Supernatural beings just sorta...do in the legends and stories.

                                                            For me one of the biggest improvements Blood and Smoke introduced was that it made the base Vampire template include a lot more stuff off the bat: Heightened Senses, Night Vision, Predators Aura and the Frenzy alterations. You get all that without putting a single dot of Disciplines on a Blood and Smoke Kindred, so "out of the box" they already feel like the the Vampires presented in a lot of modern media and stories.

                                                            I'm hoping Werewolf gets a similar "bulking" to its template. Shape shifting and regeneration at base are nice, but something like Heightened Sense, a Predators Aura sorta affect and some other things I can't think of off the top of my head would be awesome. That way, even on a "gift lite" Werewolf, you still feel like you get plenty of Supernatural bang for your buck.

                                                            I just finished re-reading the Friday blogs that have already been posted. I completely missed the one on Tribes. I like the idea posited then Tribes are now "what you hunt" while Auspice is "how you hunt". Likewise, I am very in favour of the idea of Tribe as a loose confederation of Werewolf packs that have "bought into" the concept of the Sacred Hunt for a particular prey. With the idea that the Firstborn act as "living testament" that allows the Tribe specific lore of the Sacred Hunt to survive and propagate among the Werewolf population.

                                                            That's the sorta more decentralized idea that I can see working with Werewolf. I've always felt trying to apply a solid government to them in the same way Vampires have Covenants was a foolish idea. This way Tribe can be global but remain culturally influenced and also be more of an "oath of duty" rather then a political statement (making them more coherent then say Prometheans or Demons but less so then Vampires and Mages, I guess similar level to Changelings and Mummys).

                                                            So yeah, Idigam's got a good vibe for me when it comes to Harmony, Tribe, Wolf Blooded and Pack so far. Really the more the game stresses the idea of Packs being the main social unit while being inclusive of Humans in the form of a cult or gang or organisation with Tribe being very much a background thing, I think I can buy the idea of a "society" of Werewolves. I'm not completely sold, I still want to see what's done with Gifts, Renown and the Idigam themselves (well the antagonists in general really) but its doing for me then any version of Werewolf that's come before it.

                                                            Out of interest what are you guys thoughts on Tribe? Both the original Forsaken flavour and the new Idigam take on them?

                                                            ​Also what would you guys like to see as part of the "inbuilt" Werewolf Template in Idigam?
