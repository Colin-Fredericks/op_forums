                                    Bale Hounds are often maligned as pure evil, way out there freaks by people who misunderstand them or haven't read the books; but, if anything, it's far easier to join the Bale Hounds or to understand the Bale Hound way of thinking in out of character terms in comparison to the Pure.

                                    Bale Hounds are people. A Bale Hound is every person, on a bad day.

                                    You don't join the Bale Hounds by approaching the gods of sin and asking to join up, you join them by being yourself - on a bad day.

                                    Your grandfather dies. There's grief and there's shock, but there's also rage, rage at the doctors who botched the surgery that should have been a very easy operation, who didn't take the necessary precautions.

                                    The new girl gets the promotion that you have been promised. It's probably simply because she's more attractive than you.

                                    Your wife has stopped sleeping with you. She simply doesn't find you interesting any more. She's probably sleeping around.

                                    Doesn't that make you angry? Doesn't that make you feel wronged? Shouldn't someone pay?

                                    Add to this the emotional, deeply passionate nature of the Forsaken race. We all have felt really, really angry at one point or another in our lives. When werewolves feel angry, they don't feel like shouting or hitting someone the way we do, they feel like transforming into 3 meters of raging wolf-death, tearing someone's throat out and drowning him in his own blood - and they sometimes cannot help doing exactly that.

                                    The Bale Hound philosophy isn't scary because it's about alienation from humanity or worship of Outer Evil, it's scary because it's about human vices, human emotions and human feelings gone wrong. What makes the Bale Hounds tragic figures is that their horror is human horror.

                                                    The Fire-Touched, who receive the most converts, have a belief in a Hunter's Paradise called Taga Dan that all Pure may choose to go to when they die in good faith. The Forsaken, they say, become mad Lunes upon their deaths as their Auspice renown bands become shackles that enslave them to the moon.
