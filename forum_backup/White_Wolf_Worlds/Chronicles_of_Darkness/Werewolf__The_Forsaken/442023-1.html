                        Originally posted by FlailBot View Post
                        I'll press for Door usage much more strongly in my upcoming WW game, because I think it's easy to fall into the trap of RPing all the conversations and not fully taking into account how crap that NPC should actually be at manipulation or whatever.
                        I usually don't let players "roleplay through" things, unless the task is trivially easy or has no interesting consequences for failure. The roleplay helps me determine what roll to call for.

                        My policy for task resolution in any RPG is: the player describes or acts out an action, then the GM determines if the outcome of that action is uncertain, and if a chance of failure makes the scene more dramatically interesting. If (and only if) both of those things are true, the GM decides based on the player's description or roleplay what dice roll is appropriate to determine the success or failure of the action, and the consequences of success or failure on the roll, and relays this information to the player. A failed roll does not have to mean the player fails to achieve their goal, but it should add some sort of setback or complication. The player makes the roll, and the GM narrates the results.

                        If the players "roleplay through" a social interaction without making any rolls in my games, it's either because there was no chance of them failing anyway, or because the risk of failure would not have made the story more interesting.

                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                    How often do you use forcing doors? Or using soft leverage to raise the impression level to a point where you can roll at hour or turn intervals? In my experience, most people's big beef with Social Maneuvering in 2e is the long intervals at average to good impression levels. Realistically though, if you go into trying to maneuver someone totally cold, you're not going to get results right away, unless you pressure them (force doors).
                                    I agree, but it depends a bit.
                                    It is hard to say how many times we use forcing doors per story or session. Maybe on average one or two times per story / session.

                                    When forcing doors I normally penalize the player with some form of resistance from the NPC (if he/she got any). A tough roll would be -3, it is very player driven. I normally add the penalties and let them get on with the rolls and prepare for the "shit hits the fan" scenarios.

                                    Yesterday we played a story where main character Wolfblooded got into a tough situation with a drug-runner on another packs turf.
                                    She wanted some money that had been stolen off one of the humans in the extended pack. So she meets this Drug-runner out in a partied out ranch in the middle of nowhere.
                                    Now she has backup, but decides to go straight up and bully the guy.
                                    This one was a bit tough for me, as i knew the NPC could get really pissed and the scenario could lead to a lot of combat, and quite possibly some deaths, mainly on the players side.
                                    I asked if this was what she wanted before rolling the dices.She goes hell yes.
                                    Now her character is quite strong socially, but I penalized her for a couple of things:
                                    1 they are on another packs turf, bullying their puppet.
                                    2 this pack hates the player-controlled pack
                                    3 She is forcing a door with physical threats of violence that (most likely) she cant back up.

                                    I let her roll and she gets 5 success!

                                    The drug-runner caves in and gives back the money.
                                    I write in my notes he is "soft-hearted" as a home-made condition. He lets the player go.
                                    It feels like we were playing one of those hail mary scenes from Breaking bad.
                                    Now the Drug-runner is not gonna forget this. He will try to get back on the player for this. She might have gotten a dangerous rival in the long run and she has trashed one of the potential connections the pack use to move dangerous shit in and out of the turf. The Drug-runner goes from being potential ally on enemy turf to a full blown nemesis for player character.

                                    Beats are dropped.
                                    Player loved it. I must say it was the best use of a forced door we have had up until now.
