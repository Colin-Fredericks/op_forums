    I made some rites for 2e

    09-03-2026, 12:53 PM
    So I'm STing a 2e game and I had a vague memory that the 1e rites were more expansive and gave a sense of culture to uratha society, I made these with some of those vague recollections (I know Rite of Contrition was in 1e, didn't look up what it did, I don't still have the books), the idea is situational rites that give a sense of culture, powerful enough to be impactful but primarily useful under particular circumstances. I have tried to ape the style used in the books.I would love any feedback or suggestions.

    Homebrew Rites

    Rite of Contrition


    WOLF RITE ••

    The Rite of Contrition offers a werewolf who has broken their word or otherwise wronged another a dangerous means of restoring the agreement they violated. The rite does not compel forgiveness, erase the transgression, or repair whatever affection or trust has been lost. Instead, it places supernatural weight behind an apology and a renewed undertaking. The injured party is discouraged from seeking retribution for the original wrong, but should the offender break their word again, the rite gives that injured party terrible licence to exact vengeance.

    Symbols: Prostration, Blood, Oaths, Retribution

    Sample Rite: The ritemaster pours drinks for everyone present, but does not touch their own. They rise and offer a formal toast recounting the wrong they committed without excuse, acknowledge the injured party before those assembled, and repeat the promise they previously broke. Only after drinking to the other party's name do they take their own seat. Roll Presence + Socialize, contested by an appropriate Resistance Attribute of the wronged party.

    Alternatively, the ritemaster writes a formal letter setting out the original undertaking, acknowledging precisely how they failed to honour it, and renewing their promise. The wording must admit the transgression rather than explain it away. The ritemaster signs the letter in their own blood and delivers it into the hands of the wronged party. Roll Manipulation + Academics, contested by an appropriate Resistance Attribute of the wronged party.

    Other performances are possible. The dice pool should reflect the form the contrition takes, and the wronged party resists with the Attribute most appropriate to resisting that particular appeal. Spirits use Resistance.

    Action: Contested; appropriate Attribute + Skill versus an appropriate Resistance Attribute

    Success: The original agreement is restored and the rite remains in effect until the renewed obligation is fulfilled or the offender breaches it again. The wronged party suffers a –3 penalty to any dice pool made to seek retribution against the offender for the original transgression. The rite does not require them to forgive the offender, restore a relationship, conceal what happened, or otherwise act against their own interests; it restrains retribution for the wrong that occasioned the rite.

    If the offender breaches the renewed obligation, this protection immediately ends. Thereafter, attacks made by the wronged party as part of seeking retribution for that breach upgrade their damage by one type: bashing damage becomes lethal, and lethal damage becomes aggravated. By invoking the rite, the offender has demanded that their word be trusted a second time and staked their own flesh upon keeping it.
    Homecoming


    PACK RITE •

    Homecoming turns absence from the pack's territory into a hunter's advantage upon returning. The rite can only be performed while the territory is sanctified by the Hunting Ground rite. Any pack member who has remained outside that territory for long enough for the phase of the moon to change is considered a Homecomer for the purposes of this rite. Homecomers need not be Uratha.

    Symbols: Meeting, Welcoming, the Voyage, Removing Shoes

    Sample Rite: The Homecomers arrange to perform at an open-mic or karaoke night within the Hunting Ground. Taking the stage together, they sing Thin Lizzy's The Boys Are Back in Town, dedicating the performance to everyone they know in the audience and making their return as conspicuous as possible. Roll Presence + Expression.

    Action: Extended (5 successes, +2 for each additional Homecomer; each roll represents 10 minutes)

    Success: Each Homecomer participating in the rite gains a +2 bonus to rolls against residents of the Hunting Ground. A resident can be human, animal, spirit, Uratha, Claimed, or any other being that genuinely makes its home within the sanctified territory. Only Homecomers receive this bonus; pack members who remained within the territory do not benefit from the rite.

    The bonus lasts until the phase of the moon next changes. If the Hunting Ground rite ceases to sanctify the territory before then, Homecoming ends immediately.

    Exceptional Success: The bonus lasts for one full lunar month instead.
    Funeral Rite


    WOLF RITE ••

    The People do not allow a renowned wolf to pass from the world without marking what has been lost. The Funeral Rite commits the body of a dead Uratha and fixes one aspect of their Renown in the memories of those who gather to mourn them. The rite takes one of two forms. A Celebration remembers what the dead wolf did: hunts, victories, tricks, oaths, discoveries and impossible deeds are recounted so that those who remain might live up to them. A Lament dwells upon what has been taken from the People: the mourners howl their grief, name what must now be endured without their comrade and, where blame can be laid, recount the wrongs done to the dead and swear vengeance. A Lament for a murdered wolf may sound less like a funeral than the beginning of a war.

    The ritemaster chooses one of the deceased's Renown to be the focus of the funeral. The dead wolf must possess at least one dot in that Renown. The rite need not focus upon their highest Renown; what matters is how the People gathered choose to remember them.

    Symbols: Remembrance, Loss, Renown, Fire, Earth or Sea

    Sample Rite: The People gather around the body and speak in turn. At a Celebration they recount the deeds of the dead, competing in boasts and stories until the ritemaster names the Renown by which the departed will be remembered. At a Lament they name what has been lost, howl their grief and, where blame can be laid, recount the wrongs done to their comrade and swear what will be done in answer. Finally the People raise their voices together as the body is committed to fire, earth or sea. Roll Presence + Expression.

    Action: Extended (5 successes; each roll represents 10 minutes)

    Success: The body must be committed as part of the successful rite, whether by burial, cremation, burial at sea, or another appropriate means of giving the remains of the dead over to fire, earth or sea.

    Every Uratha present receives a Funeral Condition associated with the Renown chosen by the ritemaster. The Condition contains a number of automatic successes equal to the deceased's dots in that Renown. Once, when it is narratively appropriate to invoke the aspect of the dead wolf being remembered, the mourner may add all of these automatic successes to a single action. The Storyteller determines whether the action is appropriate.

    The following are guidelines rather than restrictions:
    Cunning Celebration
    Deception, improvisation, exploiting weakness    		 Lament​
    Detecting deceit, escaping traps, avoiding being outmanoeuvred
    Glory Audacity, battle, confronting overwhelming danger Resisting fear, intimidation or despair
    Honor Keeping one's word, mediation, meeting a challenge properly Resisting coercion, betrayal or pressure to abandon an obligation
    Purity Hunting, pursuing prey, acting according to Uratha nature Resisting distraction from duty, prey or purpose
    Wisdom Investigation, occult insight, understanding spirits and the Shadow Resisting supernatural deception, confusion or disastrous error
    When the automatic successes are invoked, the mourner regains 1 Willpower and the Funeral Condition resolves. If the funeral was a Celebration, the mourner then gains the Inspired Condition. If it was a Lament, the mourner instead gains the Steadfast Condition.

    The Funeral Condition is intended to resolve in play. The deeds of a renowned wolf continue to shape the People after death: the memory of a legend can inspire exceptional deeds, and those deeds may in turn become the beginnings of new Renown.
