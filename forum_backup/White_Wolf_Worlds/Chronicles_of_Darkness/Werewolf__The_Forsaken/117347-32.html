                                        I think I have a favourite Lodge for every Tribe, actually. In no particular order:

                                        Iron Masters: Lodge of Lightning. I like determination, and I like cleverness. These guys take the best traits of Iron Masters and Storm Lords and fuse into one. I am unable to dislike that combination.

                                        Blood Talons: Lodge of Garm. Where the Lightning Dancers branched out from their main path, these guys turned inwards, and focused even more on the warrior ideal. I also like the Norse symbolism, because, hey, fan of that.

                                        Storm Lords: Lodge of Winter. Wise rulers. Similar to the Iron Master example, this is a good fusion between two tribe mentalities. Storm Lords on one hand, and Bone shadows on the other. I also really like the "Pillars of the Storm" concept, of having the tribe core basically being one of the three pillar-lodges.

                                        Bone Shadows: Lodge of Death. No question here. Mystic werewolf-necromancers of the old school? With lots of dark lore and secrets? Simply great. I especially like the little hints that they don't have a full spirit as lodge totem, but something else, something that allows them to travel through the Underworld without the normal problems werewolves have. A Geist perhaps? Hmm. Mystery indeed. (Or was that just the Pickering pack? Ah well, Lodge of Death mysteries anyhow.)

                                        Hunters in Darkness: Lodge of Harmony. This one was harder for me, since they are the only Tribe I haven't used extensively. But the theme of purification and warding they have is something I really like.


                                        Overall, I like the Lodges, they are nice little niches inside the Tribes. When I first thought about it, my initial reaction was that they were good, but many of them were... restraining in what kind of character could join, or in the actions they'd have to take. But as I glanced through the Lodge books I realized that wasn't the case, and those that required that much dedication were a minority.
                                        All in all, I like them because they are yet another way of defining character.

                                        What I don't like about them, however, is the benefits. It's not just that they are often quite weak, they are extremely inconsistent as well. Lodge of Death count their Harmony as 2 higher to resist Death Rage, while Lodge of Night gets XP cost reduction, night vision, and an ability to ignore 1 Defence, or that the Lodge of Doors get a completely unique Gift List.

                                        I don't need them to be either strong or weak, but at least make most of them consistent. I don't want my players to think "Hmm, this fits my character better, but the benefits of that lodge are simply too good to pass up." It's ok if a few, like Lodge of Scars, are special, because they are, but most of them should be at least approximately the same.

                                        It would also be nice if Lodges were something one could come and go from. Perhaps be a member of them for a few years, but then when one has changed as a person, it'd be ok to leave, perhaps to found a lodge of your own, or join another that fits who you've become more. It would also make it contrast nicely with Legacies and Bloodlines.

                                        And that said, Rant over. Hope this was the kind of thing you wanted.

                                                            Three big things I like about lodges. The first is when they offer a motivation for a character that can conceivably put them at odds with the rest of their pack. They want to hunt a prey the others don't see an issue with, they want to explore something the others might think is too dangerous, they believe in something that the others find disagreeable. The other (this isn't with all the lodges) is that they offer a totem that, while not as close to heart as a pack totem, is often far more close to the werewolf than Luna or their tribal totem. They speak with the members of their lodge. This can help with my first like, since everyone in the pack is involved with their totem, but the lodge member is involved with them and this other one. And lastly, I like how there are kind of two types of lodges, the public ones where you may not advertise yourself but people know of the lodges and might seek you out for it, or the shadow lodges, where you're the member of a sort of secret cabal that few people know exist, let alone able to recognize that you're part of it.

                                                            I like a lot of lodges, some that stand out are the Lodge of Mania and Wires, the Hidden Hunt and Maelstrom, Shadow Throne and Spirit Chains, Storms Eye and Cerberus. The Pure lodges seem very cool, I've always liked the Lodges of Fevered Light and Night's Fear. I have to agree with some that the benefits can seem to vary too much from lodge to lodge. But the only thing I really don't like about some lodges is their hyper-specialization, so to speak. Something like the Lodge of the Shield, which exists solely for the werewolf members of the LAPD as a sort of support group to help cover each others backs. It's a valid idea, I guess, but seems like the kind of thing that could be wrapped up in a paragraph suggesting that it's a phenomenon common to werewolves in law enforcement all over, rather than just in this one city, and it seems more like a sort of common sense thing rather than something that might require a spiritual totem. A few other lodges have that problem, like the lodge of Silence and the Lodge of the Lake, which seems more like a large pack.
