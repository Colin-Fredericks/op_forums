                    As totems are now I find them fairly narrow, and more restrictive than necessary. Not all packs need to be defined through a single spirit, removing the requirement of a single totem opens up additional possibilities, while providing the option to have one retains the story-hooks that were already there.

                    Some pack structures I've used that don't work with the current approach, but are totally appropriate to the setting:
                    • A pack of ronin like peacekeepers who venerate no spirit above others. They enforce peace and stability on both sides of the Gauntlet without picking favorites.
                    • A 'Wolves First' pack that believes Uratha are the true inheritors of the earth, and spirits that intend to live there must be subservient. They take the roll of totem themselves, subjugating and ruling over a swath of lesser spirits.
                    • A nomadic mercenary pack, that travels territory to territory offering power in exchange for essence. They form a temporary bond with a single spirit, accomplish the goal it desires, collect their essence tithe and move on.
                    • The trickster wolf of many totems, who secretly venerates several different spirits at a time, gaining power from each (and hoping none figure out what he's doing).

                                    The thematics of the setting work against successful totem-hopping that doesn't include pack-hopping. Spirits give their respect to werewolves on an individual basis, for the most part, and exhibit a strong degree of expectation for roles and rules to be adhered to — the magath are reviled because they stepped out of the bounds of their nature as much as because they violate the conventional dictates of what is predator and prey to what. Predators take hesitation as weakness and treat the hesitant accordingly.

                                    The Uratha are typically yanked out of their human lives and, courtesy of the same tenet that says they can't let anybody find out about them, thrust into an environment that is strange and unfamiliar to them. They don't properly belong in either world and the Border Marches are gone — that's what a liminal state is, it's stressful as hell for a psyche to maintain, and it doesn't tend to lead to healthy behavior with a lasting constructive impact unless the state is transitioned out of and a new identity constructed; until then, the changes that stick are more likely to be destructive.

                                    Spirits are rocks in the stream of the Shadow. Despite their gradual change over time, they're solid objects upon which werewolves can ground their organizations. Going without a totem is like trying to swim from one end of a pool to another without pushing off from any midpoints when there's barely any water resistance and you've only got half an idea of how to swim — the going is slower and the highest odds are on you thrashing around fruitlessly and drowning, possibly killing fellow swimmers in the process.

                                    The reward for not having a pack totem is exactly the same as the reward for being a Ghost Wolf: it's one less ban to attend to. That's all you should get for it; passing up on paired advantages and disadvantages typically means playing without any of those advantages.

                                                        So, Ephsy can sit in his corner, suck his thumb and cope; the game is going to change, and in some areas drastically from the sourcebooks that currently exist and his -personal- interpretations on them. Others are going to present new ideas or try to readdress ideas in ways he doesn't like. Once again, he just needs to cope. I've had my game running literally for years with the same people and newcomers coming to it with much of this stemming from their input; I'm pretty sure my ST skills or my Co-St's aren't actually an issue.

                                                        It's looking at existing material, and seeing ways of improving upon them, or otherwise pushing the envelope to see what further potential there may be to bring the game in line with the needs of the players and ST's running it, or simply to reinforce the themes of the game. You know. Like "The Wolf Must Hunt". Hence making Essence a much rarer commodity -that requires hunting for it to gain it- and reinforcing the ideas that spirits are consistently after more further; you know... so they keep not only surviving but growing in power? So they rise the Rank Totem pole? To emphasize the cycles of predation the Shadow has just a -bit- further? That? Much of this material -is- already addressed; it's kind of a no-shit Sherlock situation where the majority of us are well aware of the material that's already in print. It's not a matter of reiteration; it's a matter of looking at the impressions others have gained about the game as is, and ways one can address them via changes in the game. In a lot of areas, it's emphasis less than it is gutting the system and changing it. In others, it's just that.
