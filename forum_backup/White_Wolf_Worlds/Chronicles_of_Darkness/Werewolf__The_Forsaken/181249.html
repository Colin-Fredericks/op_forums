    "Tribes being Auspices" problem

    06-26-2014, 05:40 PM
    One of bigger things I have problem with Forsaken setting is that most Tribes are working as "second Auspice" for character. Blood Talons are basically Rahu, Bone Shadows are Ithaeur, Iron Masters are Irraka, etc. Worse, they can be chosen for double role, so the Blood Talons Rahu is what? Most Rahu of Rahus? And Iron Masters Irraka is as such secretive, it can't be found?

    It get to me when my players were choosing Tribes for they characters. Most choose just Tribe that is like "second Auspice" of they PC, the same they have. It could be even to overcome, if Renown weren't also connected with Tribes as "second Auspice". Of course Storm Lords are having Honor, because Elodoth have them.

    I know the Favorites Preys are add in Idigam Chronicles to Tribes to help mitigate this, but I still see it, and somehow it made me cringe on this part of setting. Other game lines have they X-Splat and Y-Splat totally unconnected, so characters are more interesting. In Werewolf it looks... well, a bit dull. You can be "super Rahu", "super Ithaeur" and a like.

      To think that auspice is the same as tribe is to misunderstand a crucial part of the setting. Tribes can be chosen. No werewolf choses his auspice. A Rahu is a person imbued with the destiny of a killer. He might not like it. He might even struggle with his auspice. He might eventually come to terms with it, or not. The auspice cannot be changed. A Blood Talon makes the intentional choice to emulate the destroyer wolf and to live by his Code. His is a choice, one that can be reverted. To confuse the two is to misunderstand the setting.

                              I’ve always found it’s easy to get past this issue by breaking them down to the essentials.

                              The Auspices are the traditional tribal roles:
                              Rahu – warrior, fighter, protector
                              Cahalith – storyteller, lore keeper, artist
                              Elodoth – advisor, elder of the tribe, councilman
                              Ithaeur – shaman, healer, wise man
                              Irraka – hunter, thief, spy

                              The Tribes are based on wolf/werewolf symbolism:
                              1) Storm Lords are The Wolf as Protector/Ruler. Romulus and Remus owe their lives to a she-wolf, as does little Mowgli, so that one is fairly wide-spread. Many a king claimed to dream of wolves, be safe in the company of wolves, or be part wolf themselves.
                              2) Blood Talons are The Wolf as Hunter/Warrior. The wolf-skin warriors all over Indo-European myths, several Arctic tribes revered wolves. This one is so common it doesn’t really need explanation.
                              3) Hunters in Darkness are The Wolf as Devourer/Predator. The above is the positive spin; this one is the negative spin, which is also quite common. The Big Bad Wolf being the number 1 example, closely followed by Fenris. The Fear of the Darkness made manifest.
                              4) Iron Masters are The Wolf as Companion/Partner. This is that middle ground between becoming the loyal dog and continuing to be the wild animal. Tame enough to protect what is considered “their property” but still wild enough to be dangerous.
                              5) Bone Shadows are The Wolf as Omen/Death. One of the lesser known ones, mostly in Native American myths, but there are a few that place dogs/wolves as guardians of the Underworld or psychopomps guiding people (or taking them by force) to the lands of the dead.

                              The Auspice is the natural skills and tendencies they're born with. The Tribe is how they choose to apply those skills and tendencies they've got.

                                                          Actually, I think the tribes act as a guiding point for how you choose to follow your auspices, rather than acting as a second auspice itself.

                                                          For example, the shaman role. If they joined the Bone Shadows, they'd probably take on the role of being a wiseman or a spiritual leader of the People. If they joined the Blood Talons, they might become a witchdoctor, riling up the spirits and turning them against their enemies.


                                                          The role of the warrior could also be changed depending on their tribe. Sure if you joined the Blood Talons, you could be a savage barbarian, but if you joined the Hunters in Darkness, you could be a deadly assassin, or a trained commando if you joined the Iron Masters.


                                                          Tribes should be treated as a way of life for your character, not simply treating them like a power boost. If all you focus on is the bonuses, you'll never be able to fully immerse yourself in the game, it becomes a power play at that point, as opposed to role playing.
