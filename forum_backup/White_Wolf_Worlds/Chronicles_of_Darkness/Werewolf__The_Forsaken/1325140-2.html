    All right, so, let's look into the Spirit Parents and try to diversify the jags...

    Night Father/Nox is a god of secrets, hiding, and obfuscation. His trickster nature is all about exploiting people's flaws to his own end and making deals with hidden caveats. Here's an origin story...

    This story is true. Nox is the older brother of Helios and Luna. When the world was new, Nox helped his siblings cull the world of the more undesirable and esoteric spirits, but the two were afraid of his power, and thought of banishing him to the Outer Void. Nox offered them a deal. During the day, Nox would not have sway wherever Helios' light shone. And, during the night, he would allow Luna to watch over him. The two were mollified, and agreed to this deal. Nox, however, offered this deal knowing that proud Helios wouldn't think of the fact that, during the day, his light would cast shadows wherever he struck. He also knew that mercurial Luna would only show the fullness of her light one night of her cycle, and that would wane until there was a night where her light didn't shine at all. This meant that even under Helios' rays, there would always be a place where Nox held power, and that and that, at least one night each month, he would be able to act with no one watching.

    What are his ultimate goals? He's the god of secrets, how am I supposed to know? Rather, his goals can fit into your game however you want. Maybe he's an overgrown, overly charming Void Spirit who's attempting to swallow the sun and cover the world in eternal night. Maybe he's a pragmatist doing what he can to survive. Maybe he's truly on the side of his siblings and maintains the Shadow in his own way. Maybe he's trying to protect necessary-but-unpleasant Spirits from being culled. Just know that if his Nocturnes show up, especially if they have an offer, there's a catch. Perhaps the catch won't hurt you, but there's a catch.

    Mother Jaguar was powerful. Her roar was thunder, and the shaking earth. Her eyes were fire. Her claws and teeth certain death. She was master of her environment, ruling from trees above and water below. Above all else, she was the indomitable drive to survive.

    Wolf and Jaguar both hunted, but they differed in their methods. Where Wolf was the guardian of borders, Jaguar was the shaper and master of her domain. Where Wolf was strength through the pack, Jaguar was overwhelming personal strength tempered by an iron will.

    Jaguars do not mate as wolves do, so, while the Yawatha call her the Night's wife, it wasn't the same as the relationship between Wolf and the Moon. Still, Night was and still remains the jaguar's greatest ally. Amahan Yawa went on her hunts cloaked by Ngifarah's shadows, and she, in turn, uncovered secrets and slew obstacles. Thus, Mother Jaguar had her own Firstborn, who each had grown, separated, and hunted their own ways.

    No one knows the exact circumstances of Mother Jaguar's injury. Some say as the Border Marches fell, her Flesh and Spirit were severed. Some say she was hunting a dangerous foe and Father Wolf's death howl distracted her at a critical moment. Some say that the Uratha who slew Father Wolf saw her as a threat and attacked her afterwards. Some say the Yawatha took the opportunity to usurp their own parent for their own gain. Some say that upon seeing the world change, Father Night felt her usefulness was at an end. Some say that Mother Jaguar's Firstborn were injured as well, and that she chose to survive through her children.

    Mother Jaguar is a tough nut to crack because jaguars can represent a LOT to many peoples, which is why I want there to be Auspices/Tribes. So, broad symbolism for her, combined with biological facts. There's a great book about jaguars called An Indomitable Beast, by Alan Rabinowitz, a foremost jaguar expert, and it contains a lot of aspects of the jaguar that set it apart from other big cats.

    The idea of being a master of one's domain comes from the fact that jaguars hunt on land, in trees, and in water, not to mention, they're extremely alert and cautious. It also refers to jaguars being associated with elemental/cosmic power and are often seen as the master of forests or other animals. Jaguars are solitary and secretive, as well as possessing the most powerful bite of any large cat. A 90-150 pound jaguar in Central America can still take down a bull. So they're lonesome and powerful. And they're tenacious, willing to go to any lengths to survive.

    Let's look at these Spirits and determine Renown.

    Cunning is definitely among the Renown the Yawatha strive for, from both parents. Glory fits as well, due to their physical prowess and audacity. Wisdom also fits, being creatures of Spirit that deal in secret knowledge.

    Honor is definitely not something the jaguars care about. Purity is also iffy, especially since 2e Purity relies on the Oath of the Moon, and I never intended for the jags to have a comparable Oath. So, if it's not the same Purity as wolves, why call it that?

    There might be possibilities of alternate types of Renown. The best idea I have there is Tenacity, the jaguar's drive to survive. I could also do something like Command or Mastery, but that's more iffy.

    I'll be re-evaluating Auspices and Tribes (which I may call Bloodlines or something), but I will say that they will still exist, with Auspices being how jaguars are anthropomorphized in various cultures (warriors/brutes, shamans, hunters, kings) and Tribes/Bloodlines/Whatever being the powers and mythic roles jaguars are ascribed (destroyers, guardians, underworld journeymen, etc.)

            So, the next step. I'm sticking with four types of Renown for werejaguars; Cunning, Glory, Tenacity, and Wisdom. This will lead to four Auspices and four Tribes*. This has synergy with Aztec and Maya symbolism, as these mythologies included deities revolving around the four cardinal directions, the Maya especially having deities in fourfold aspects. So, as a result, I'm axing the Nasi Auspice and the Sentinels of the Way Tribe, and adjusting the remaining four.

            *Tribe remains the best word. Most others, like Bloodline, Lineage, or Family, are used by other splats. Breed is more in line with changing breeds of old, and Race... not doing that. Tribes are determined by which Firstborn Yawatha you choose to emulate. And you DO have to choose one. No Ghost Jaguars. If a jaguar refuses to choose, a Nocturne or one of the Firstborn forces the issue. You can thank the various ways Ghost Wolves can be twisted in Shunned by the Moon. Ngifarah ain't letting that happen to his kids.

            Auspices don't have a direct analogue to something like the Moon's phases, but each one has an aspect that Nox has deemed important to his children.
            • Ora
              • Jaguar as Warrior, Brute, Ogre.
              • Tenacity Renown
              • Aspect of the Night: Deadly
            • Toleso
              • Jaguar as Hunter, Spy, Traitor
              • Cunning Renown
              • Aspect of the Night: Hidden
            • Lamba
              • Jaguar as Noble, King, Tyrant
              • Glory Renown
              • Aspect of the Night: Fearsome
            • Kuruntu
              • Jaguar as Shaman, Trickster, Sorcerer
              • Wisdom Renown
              • Aspect of the Night: Secretive
            I actually think these Aspects mirror each other in certain ways. Deadly and Fearsome could be two sides of the same coin, the night containing physical dangers and the fear those dangers provoke. Likewise, Hidden represents concealing one's actual self, while Secretive conceals knowledge.

            Now we have the "Tribes". While Yawatha rarely congregate together, each one is marked as kin to one of the four Firstborn of Mother Jaguar.

            EDIT: These are now referred to as Directions. I don't know if I should assign each one to a cardinal direction, but I'll save that thought for later
            • Eternal Furies
              • Angikue (lit. "roar of heaven and earth")
              • Tenacity Renown
              • Jaguars as forces of nature, sources of elements, masters of animals
              • Kin of Jade Jaguar
                • Shupa-Yawa
            • Soul Eaters
              • Agalu Hissu (lit. "ghost eaters")
              • Glory Renown
              • Jaguars as destroyers, forces of chaos, forces of war
              • Kin of Blood Jaguar
                • Umun-Yawa
            • Star-Pelts
              • Mulsigi (lit. "bright haired")
              • Wisdom Renown
              • Jaguars as cosmic forces, otherworldly travelers, gateways between worlds
              • Kin of Black Jaguar
                • Hikaon-Yawa
            • Warden Fangs
              • Zudal (lit. "fang protectors")
              • Cunning Renown
              • Jaguars as creators, guardians, tutelary spirits
              • Kin of Shield Jaguar
                • Kapapum-Yawa
            This provides opportunities for various jaguar myths of many cultures to be usable depending on context. The Yanomami jaguar as a thuggish destructive brute is compatible with the Juruna jaguar as a wise and protective creator-ancestor.

            So, now that I have the splats figured out, the next step I need to take is: How to deal with Gifts?

                griffinguy24 it looks like your evolutions are solid and you're shaving away things that are "not Jaguar' from the concept and implementation. I really like your Nox and wonder how he might be interpreted by other cultures/creatures in other parts of the world. For example: Leopard. This is rhetorical --not a derail for now, though you might ponder how the wolves know him and what they think of him and his choir.

                Instead of tribes, call them Clans? Tribes are an extended grouping of families, while a clan is a household or blood-related family close enough that they do not marry within themselves. In that sense all of Jaguar's were-progeny could be considered a tribe, while the actual blood-divisions you're angling toward are clans. So it would make perfect sense for no Yawatha to be "ghost" (though a Ghost Jaguar sounds like a cool, "Shunned by the Night" variant).

                I also like the idea of them hunting across the Underworld at night and the Hisil during the day. Their territory extends in directions that the Wolves cannot perceive let alone follow, which would make them hideously difficult to pin down and "tree" by a pack. It could explain why wolves don't talk or even know that much about them... Uratha tend to be very narrow-minded when something falls outside their purview.

                Bunyip's suggestion of "gifts" through the consumption of spiritual essence and regurgitation is inspired. That's, um, kinda what cats do after all: they hock up something left after they've extracted what they want from their prey. It means that the Yawatha could acquire ad hoc "gifts" from many sources: spirits (who would at least be grateful the ritual treatment doesn't involve destruction ala Uratha), ghosts, and even (via ritual) the hearts of other creatures--all for a limited time. Though, coughing up a partially-digested heart won't do much good to its previous owner, most of the time.

                I think you're onto some things that will really make them more than "wolves in jag clothing". That said, one of the "hacks" I've suggested in other were-cat threads is to consider all other predators the bastard children of Wolf and whatever else... only such Firstborn (perhaps earlier, after the Geryo) "took after" other animal forms, while still carrying the spiritual weight of the essence of the hunt.

                                        How about this for Gifts? The werejaguars can swallow and 'hold' a variety of gifts based on their Primal Urge for a certain period of time (a time period that would have significance for a Mesoamerican society). When that time passes, or if the jaguar decides to, they can regurgitate the spirit. That means the jaguar can't access the previous Gift but can now choose a different Gift or hunt the same type of spirit to hold onto the same Gift. Experience spent on Gifts would instead open Gift slots with the number of Gifts determined by Primal Urge. Renown would open facet slots that can be bought for a Gift slot.

                                        As an example, a jaguar has Gift A and has bought the Cunning and Wisdom facets so the jaguar has the Cunning and Wisdom facets of Gift A. Then the spirit is regurgitated, the jaguar loses Gift A, but then hunts down and eats (or convinces) Spirit B to join with the jaguar and the jaguar now has the Cunning and Wisdom facets of Gift B.

                                        Perhaps you could also have one Gift per auspice and tribe that is automatically given to the jaguar as it is inherent to that auspice and tribe. The jaguar could then have a number of Gift slots for interchangeable Gifts equal to Primal Urge.

                                                        Acquiring Gifts

                                                        So, as Bunyip initially suggested, I think that werejaguars will primarily gain Gifts from consuming Spirits. Occasionally, when in a Yawatha's clutches, a Spirit will offer a gift in exchange for freedom. If the jaguar is feeling magnanimous, they'll accept, if not, they can just consume it and take it anyway.

                                                        I'd mentioned that Ngifarah and his Nocturnes love to make deals with hidden caveats. So, for Renown and Night Gifts, the jaguars typically have to agree to deals with Nocturnes, doing their bidding or occasionally being granted favor for advancing their agenda unintentionally. However, I'll give jaguars some sort of bonus (8-again, perhaps) when dealing with Nocturnes, with their Auspice determining exactly how they can get the upper hand when dealing with them (Kuruntu realizing the catch and haggling, Lamba intimidating them into giving them what they need, Toleso stealing Renown/Gifts rather than negotiating, and Ora just eating them)

                                                        Additionally, Mother Jaguar was stated as being the ultimate master of her domain (which is a concept I need to find a new phrase for because Seinfeld ruined it), shaping her territory as she saw fit. So, by shaping resonance and cultivating Spirits in their territory, Yawatha could simply be granted Gifts as a tithe.

                                                        In any case, rather than scars, Yawatha already have markings in their flesh. Rather than a scar, a Gift is seared into one of the werejaguar's spots.

                                                        Mechanics of Gifts

                                                        I'll start with the easiest option: Just give Yawatha access to plain Shadow Gifts, keep Cunning, Glory, and Wisdom Facets, use Purity Facets for Tenacity, block all Honor Gifts, then call it a day.

                                                        Second easiest option is to come up with new Tenacity Facets for each Shadow Gift.

                                                        Another option, is that since Spirits gain their power by eating lesser Spirits, is to forgo Gifts and give Yawatha Influences from the spirits they consume.

                                                        A harder option is to look at all the gifts and entirely revamp them for use with werejaguars

                                                        I could also say that eating Uratha flesh or blood can allow the Yawatha to learn an Honor Facet that the Uratha knows (replacing Honor with their honorary Spirit Rank for dice/enhancements/penalties). In some cases the Uratha might do this willingly.

                                                        On an Unrelated Note

                                                        So, something I just wanted to clarify about werejaguars is that, while there is a definite theme of the lone predator here, that isn't the only thing. Like the Uratha, werejaguars are half-human, and the human half has lived a life needing human company. There is a struggle between Flesh and Spirit, but sociable Yawatha are a rarity, not an impossibility.

                                                        Typically, newly-changed Yawatha will band together to learn the ropes. At lower Primal Urge and/or higher Harmony, a group of werejaguars may be found roaming the Hisil. Somewhat older jaguars may even take these cubs under their wing for a brief period of time. And, if things get apocalyptic, you might get a group of experienced, powerful jags putting aside their differences up to battle something (pray that it isn't you). So if you're a bunch of PC werejaguars starting an adventure, that's a common enough thing. Though, if you manage to last a while sticking together and growing in power, you're going to turn some heads.

                                                        Furthermore, it's not unusual for a Yawatha to do their level best to avoid another Yawatha, but interact with humans fairly frequently. They can move silently through the Herd, charm the humans they encounter, and even become elite, powerful individuals in a community (they do say it's lonely at the top).

                                                        And while they often clash, some Uratha packs have been able to make alliances with individual Yawatha, and in rare occasions, induct one into the Pack. Whenever this happens, the Yawatha is an aloof ally, sometimes doing their part without ever interacting with the other Pack members, but if your Pack makes this happen, you've hit the damn jackpot.
