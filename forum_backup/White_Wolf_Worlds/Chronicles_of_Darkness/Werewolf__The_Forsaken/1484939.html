    How do Uratha actually fight one another?

    06-25-2022, 12:26 AM
    Been a long time since I've been here. Had to take a break from tabletop just because of real life and shit. Recently started my first Werewolf game as a storyteller. Been pretty fun so far.

    I have run a few werewolf one-shots and I've run into this problem before. So I wanna ask what people think both in terms of general and specific to my situation.

    So the idea of the campaign is that the players are all siblings in a real big family growing up on a farm in late 70's rural west virginia. Their last name is, of course, Wolfe. The name of the campaign is, of course, "Who is afraid of the West Virgnia Wolves?"

    Yes, their parents are named George and Martha. Yes, I am a hack, thank you for noticing.

    So part of the set-up of the campaign is this is a super small rural town, with nothing in it. The only werewolves the characters have ever encountered is each other, their siblings. They have a very young brother who has not undergone a change, but there is this expectation amongst the pack that he will because like... he has five older siblings who have all turned. And he's more than a little weird.

    So I have this encounter planned at some point that a pure pack from outside the town has taken notice of the pack, has taken notice of their one non-uratha sibling, and has come to the same conclusion as them. He's gonna turn at some point. So they're gonna attack the farm and try to abduct the little brother.

    So planning for this adventure I kinda keep coming to the same conclusion. A werewolf who spends too long in Gauru death rages. A werewolf who death rages will attack anything except another death-raging werewolf. But if two werewolves are fighting, then like... at some point, someone has to go into gauru. Because if you're losing, and you don't, the other guy kills you. And at that point, he has to go gauru, because if he doesn't, you kill him. And if you both go gauru, then likely someone goes into a death rage, because y'all probably can't do enough damage to counter gauru's healing factor in one round, and if you drop gauru before the other guy, then he kills you. And once you death rage, everyone in the fight basically starts to chain reaction death rage.

    Basically, it seems like any fight between two uratha packs would logically devolve into both packs inevitably death raging and decimating everything around *except* the other pack that they showed up to fight. But in lore, packs fight and kill each other all the time? Maybe it's just that everyone starts packing silver knives and putting dots into weaponry, but I feel like, as far as flavor/narrative, werewolves *should* be tearing each other apart with claws and fangs.

    I might be reading something wrong? How do other storytellers handle pack v. pack combat? Any particular tactics the pure tribe should use? For context, it's five starting level players (Plus like 2 adventures worth of xp) vs 1 very strong uratha from each pure tribe. Ivory Claw Alpha. Predator King who's the actual big tank combat threat. Fire Touched who was a former irraka and favors stealth/hit and run tactics using disease gift and a few disease spirit allies.

    Though more interested in generally like... how do werewolves actually manage to kill one another without everyone death raging and demolishing a city block while leaving their enemies alive?

              Originally posted by Neckbeard the Pirate View Post
              Though more interested in generally like... how do werewolves actually manage to kill one another without everyone death raging and demolishing a city block while leaving their enemies alive?
              The tactical answer is "by using the Killing Form for the thing that it's named for after using the other forms to whittle them down instead of treating Gauru as the go-to combat form," i.e. the "Uratha are hunters, not warriors, and they act like it" answer. Controlled Gauru is for the finishing blow; multiple allied werewolves in Gauru, who deal at least three lethal damage on every successful hit with their claws and fangs, can basically disintegrate any single foe with a focused strike, particularly with Merits like Living Weapon, Efficient Killer, Relentless Assault, and Tactical Shifting. Forsaken packs likewise have Auspice benefits and Moon Gifts to further bump their effectiveness, particularly if they've called a hunt on the enemy.

              Leading up to that, packs engage in ambushes, hit-and-run, sabotage, and other fight-smarter-not-harder moves, imposing Tilts with Urshul's Weaken the Prey ability, closing the distance to grapple with Urhan's Chase Down, attacking the pack's mortal members or engineering the enemy werewolves' descent into Kuruth in inconvenient places (particularly at higher Primal Urge), and otherwise controlling the field of conflict beyond the immediate fight.

              Not all fights are to the death, and discretion is the better part of valor. If the Pure want to take someone, they don't have to make an open move or accomplish their goal in one go unless there's a specific reason for them to do so. Converts after the First Change are converts just as much as folks who were prevented from ever getting an Auspice stamped into their Essence, there's as much Renown in stealing someone out of their family home without anybody noticing until the morning as in killing everyone around them, and working on a wolf-blooded prospect away from their family so he leaves of his own accord offers just as much drama as physically kidnapping him.

              tl;dr Not all forms with fangs and claws are Gauru fangs and claws, not all conflicts have to be to the death or even physical, and unless you have a reason for it engaging in a straight fight is a sucker's game.

                          1/ in the fiction collection of short stories "the Idigam chronicles", there is a story called "The worst kind of enemy" which illustrates a fight between 2 packs. It involves shotguns filled with silver buckshot and Uratha accidently killing their own packmates in Kuruth. There is even a paragraph at the beginning describing the virtues of silver shot. This story is a good starting point to show how 2 packs would fight each other. One leads the other into a trap and springs an ambush...using silver packed shotguns. The Pure might also have allies such as spirits ( for Fire touched), and humans ( for Ivory Claws). As was probably mentioned in other posts, a dozen humans with automatic weapons can make short work of an Uratha pack.

                          2/ In another battle we fought, the Uratha just stayed in Urshul form and just bit each other causing aggravated damage. Sure harmony will suffer, but the battle was done quick with only 1 Pure still alive

                          3/ Finally, in a battle between 2 packs, I would think that the prudent thing to do if the other pack falls to Kuruth would be to run away. Since Urshal is faster than Garou, and the Garou form can only attack whatever is around it in Kuruth, the pack that maintained control can flee and come back when the Kuruth timer runs out and the enemy collapses from exhaustion. This is where planning ahead and having an escape route works. Better yet, the rage gift "incite fury" can trigger Wasu-ilm at a distance and start the mayhem.

                                      I think the trick is to remember that the wolf must hunt rather than the wolf must brawl.

                                      Being hunters, planning an ambush is much more efficient than starting a bar fight. Using high powered rifles from a hidden position can probably eliminate a few Uratha before they even have a chance to change to Garou, evening up the odds immensely.

                                      Remember also that many Uratha resources are finite such as essence, willpower, or rage, so sending in a wave of minions or allies to soften up the other pack and expend their resources is a good way to prepare for going in for the kill. The Pure often use humans and spirits, but forsaken can use strategies too.

                                      As the book says, the garou killing blow should be done once the prey is already cornered and weakened. Any good prey will know to flee the garou form much like a rabbit knows to flee a pack of wolves.

                                      One reason why other Uratha are such dangerous prey is that they have the same playbook. They know that the garou form has a timer and they know that it is a form that is incapable of rational thought. The smart pack use that against their enemies. Trick the other pack into going into Kuruth, dodge out of the way, plan an avenue of escape, and flee. If one raging Uratha catches up, dog pile on him to take him out before going after the other pack. Once the other pack collapses after their Kuruth, then go in for the kill.

                                      Werewolf offers lots of tactical flexibility and rewards planning. The trick is using your imagination to win rather than everyone going into a frenzy and making a big mess

                                          Silver weapons being considered "profane" or against any oath is a holdover from first edition and doesn't appear in the second edition, where it mentions silver blades being used to hurt other werewolves for punishment. It's a Breaking Point to flesh, like staying out of the Hisil for a week. Local culture will depend on your setting but it's just as okay for your pack to think it's the worst thing imaginable as it is for another pack to think it's perfectly sensible and even divinely encouraged.

                                          But with the advances of ancient technology from rocks to swords and the astonishing skills of PCs there are ways to kill werewolves beyond using silver. Even in gauru, a combat-oriented werewolf can cause damage to roll over to Aggravated without resorting to eating flesh, and that's after the possibility that another werewolf might pop in between two about to throw down and say, "Hey, that's not how we settle things, calm the fuck down."

                                          Packs wiping out other packs is as common as you need it to be, but in the area OP described you seem to be dealing with a lot of people who know each other and are basically family, so despite their surface level animosity probably don't want to kill everyone they know and love and have some level of restraint. Just like in real life how not every family argument leads to someone pulling out a gun and killing everyone they happen to share a last name with. At least outside of America.

                                          That said while I think ways for werewolves to "fight" as in using the combat system and standing there wailing on one another until one dies are pretty simple, though they've been covered in other posts.
                                          http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...-at-each-other
                                          http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...nd-interesting
                                          http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...ill-a-werewolf

                                          But really if you just want to "win" a confrontation with a werewolf often killing them isn't the best method (especially since hey you might have to live with the fact that they were right, and you can never prove to them that you weren't a cowardly troll) so you can think outside of the Combat-system box. Death raging werewolves tend to be pretty single-minded, which is why it's often avoided, as single-mindedness can lead to one falling into traps like those created with Mouldywarp, suddenly you're in a big pit and no matter how angry you are there's nothing to kill but time. A werewolf that's built more for social or speed can "Taunt" the target, luring them on a chase until they're out of harms way. Basu-Im lasts 10 minutes at Primal Urge 1, and that can allow for a lot of combat in something like a chase you're not dealing with round-by-round time, and each segment of a chase can be a minute of luring the death raging werewolf away from, say, the town full of innocent human meatsacks and into an abandoned mine or deep dark woods.

                                          tl;dr How they fight is different from how they kill, and both are going to take vastly different approaches. If you want to fight then you're not trying to eradicate their existence and you can declare a winner before death rage becomes a thing. If you want to kill them just stab them to death while they sleep.
