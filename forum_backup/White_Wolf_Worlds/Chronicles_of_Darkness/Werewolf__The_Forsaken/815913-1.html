                        As I was mentioned in OP, I'll take voice. I say openly – I’m fanboy of Bale Hounds!
                        They were most interesting antagonists to me in 1ED corebook – and now are equally intersting as Idigams, but from totally different reasons. In reality they are what I wanted from Black Spiral Dancers, but done much, much better. Here is quick list of this reasons:
                        1. Paranoia – Everyone can be in the cult. Your packmate, your lover, your alpha – even your Tribes Elders. More than that – Bale Hounds are only one of two enemies that even Pure will help you get rid of. They are Outsiders that are Inside. And Hounds are best test of pack strength – if you survive one of you betraying you to the Maeljins, your new family will survive anything. But do you survive?
                        2. Hell – I’m sucker for good “damned by Hell” stories. Inferno is one of mine favorite general WoD supplements, I even won it’s copy in our Polish contstest. Mine typical Hunter stories are mix of Exorcist and Supernatural – so I’m clearly fanboy of dark powers. But question is - Why Hell is so much appeling to me? It’s because it’s highlight how our own actions can lead to doom – and how Vices can be our undoing. Great gothic theme to explore.
                        3. “Humanity” – By serving Lords of Sin, Bale Hounds are much closer to humans than most Uratha. When you look over Tribes – both Pure and Forsaken - and their philosophy – they are taking very much spirits stance. Weird Bans, hunting monsters or even other humans. Only veteran Bale Hounds will truly understand the Lust you feel to this pretty girl that do not even set eyes on you. Only Hound will arrange you to get this new shinny car and better job – you only need to give him three innocent children for next ritual. It is Evil, but it’s Human Evil. I would also propose to let Bale Hounds choose third Anchor as Vice, after Blood and Bone. Or at least instead of Bone.
                        4. History - Bale Hounds adds interesting spin on historic events for the Forsaken's setting. It's written that Bale Hounds are large problem in mine "home" Europe, probably coming from the spread of Christianity leading to banning everything related to animism. When looking over this point, you can understand the Middle Ages werewolves that went by First Change in small communities. You were man becoming wolf, possessed by "demon", seeing "demons" for half your life now ( as in Hisil ), calling "monster" by every story you know. Nothing surprising in the fact that many of Uratha might think that they are the Children of Satan and should lead the Hell on Earth.
                        5. Silent Hill - I love the game series and Wounds are to Shadow Realm, just like Otherworld is to Fog Silent Hill. If you want to appreciate much more the Wounds idea - just play those games or watch ( first ) movie. It's really scary and great stuff!
                        6. Foes for the Tribes - I also just realized that Bale Hounds will be greatest problem for Iron Masters, mine own favorites from Tribes of Moon, so it’s also this on plus side.

                                Wyrdhamster's point (3) about humanity seems the most interesting.

                                I view werewolves as a mixture of crime story (like Narcos or The Shield), an animistic legend, and a nature documentary.

                                Each one of those gets less and less tragic. Crime stories are mostly tragedies, which is great drama. The animistic legends are somewhat tragic, but more in the since of "This is what we had to do", not "This is where I went wrong". Nature documentaries can be sad, but I don't see most animals as having tragic natures.

                                Bale Hounds are all tragedy, and they represent great flaws and take power from the upswings of those tragic arcs and claim that the downsides were inevitable anyways. I see them taking the vice mechanic and narrative device and sewing through everything in the chronicle. Not only are they tragic, their enemies begin to take on tragic characteristics as well as they fight.

                                                    Here is my answer to "sell" you:

                                                    Werewolves with truly alternate belief systems and values.

                                                    Bale hounds do not have the cookie cutter beliefs and/or values of other Uratha. Most Pure have strong ideological ties and as do most Forsaken as well. There is a reason bale hounds are detailed in the Blasphemies book. Bale hounds are outsiders and infiltrators to both groups. Their motivations tend to be much more personal and selfish than they are ideological indoctrination of the mainstream strange cult-like subcultures of Uratha. Werewolves go through a traumatic transformation that drives a wedge between them and their former lives, leaves them with instincts and emotions that rack their hearts, minds, and souls and preyed upon by groups social predators who brainwash these psychologically vulnerable with tribal rituals design to enforce conformity of thought and beliefs. The same may or may not be true of bale hounds but bale hounds have in some part broken free of those normative paradigm to more than a standard deviation from average, characteristically towards being more self-centered and self-serving than even the most selfish Pure or Forsaken. They are defined by vice and sins that color their worldview until it in a very real sense defines a major portion of who they are. These are not werewolves with flaws of moral characters, these are living flaws of moral character walking around wearing a husk of a supernatural predator that once was a mighty spiritual warrior fighting ideological battles for either balance or vengeance. Their sin has hollowed them out from the inside and now they can't even understand why what they do is wrong, nor would they care if they did; they are, depending on storyteller interpretation, past most chances of redemption, barring some epic saga or extreme contrivance of plot. They are the big bad wolves that brutally victimizes and violates the other wolves that thought they were big and bad, as if werewolf was a disturbing prison movie, just because they are nastier, meaner, and absolutely without qualm towards foul tactics. If other werewolves were to fight as dirty as bale hounds, they'd by definition be bale hounds.

                                                    Also they bring morality themes into focus for the game with the most violence of any splat so far scheduled to be in the second edition. Geist: Sin-Eater gives werewolf a run for its money in the violence department due to dealing with death, frequently violent death, either your own or ghost's are central to that game. The hunt is a theme about primal savagery which can muddle morality themes. Bale hounds re-inject the theme of madness, sins and emotional damage caused by unspeakable acts and facing horrifying forces that is in the core Chronicles of Darkness back into the werewolf game by making it crystal clear that while even savage violence for ideological reasons can be normative justified within a society with the proper context, there are things which go too far and are not acceptable by anyone who is sane and/or moral.
