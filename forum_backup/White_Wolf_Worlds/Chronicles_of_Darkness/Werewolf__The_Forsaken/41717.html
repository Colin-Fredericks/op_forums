          Well, there's Luna too, and it was present at the time - on the other hand, Luna is hardly well-known for clear and concise communication, and is presumably just as capable of lying as any Firstborn or Host. The same is true of any elder spiritual or worldly being - and even if you manage to net one with something that forces them to tell the truth, you can't be sure that they're not just wrong, mistaken or have really foggy memories.

          However, the Uratha do not have a piece of scientific proof they can point to. Let's say that Uratha unearth a massive, gnarled, spiritual tooth - a fang of Father Wolf himself! It overflows with Essence of wolfly resonance, and has power over boundaries and barriers. Except there's not actually proof that it's Father Wolf instead of some other ancient spiritual monster. Even if it's covered in carvings saying 'Father Wolf Woz 'Ere', that's not definitive *proof*.

          On the other hand, there is a certain amount of proof about Pangaea as an uber-continent, a unified world, if one associates it with the actual, geological movement of the continents as researched by human scientists. There's no evidence that it was a paradise of hunters, that the spirit and flesh were close together, etc, but you could argue the unified continents as matching at least part of the old tale.

          But is all that even relevant? Let's take a step back. What is the *purpose* of the stories that Uratha tell of their origins? There's truth, and then there's truth. How much do the exact facts of the distant past matter, as long as the lesson being taught to the present is the right lesson, one that resonates with the modern situation that the Uratha find themselves in?

          Does it matter whether Father Wolf really existed, when the stories of Father Wolf fit what the Forsaken need in coming to term with their duties and responsibilities, in dealing with the Firstborn and the many foes that cluster at their threshold?

          - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                                                          Originally posted by ErikModi View Post
                                                          If we apply the scientific method to this problem, we come away with the inescapable conclusion that the myth, at least in part, MUST be true.
                                                          I'm sorry; side note, but important gripe.

                                                          For anything to use the scientific method, there has to be a testable hypothesis and conditions for it to be supported and discounted by the evidence. Designing those kind of tests to study the origins of werewolves is going to be hard. An example of how it'd be tricky: "uratha's origins are bound to the moon in spiritually significant ways." when you use auspices as a test, you find werewolves without auspices. when you use silver as a test, the ones without auspices are Much more vulnerable to it. One conclusion from that can be (given the nature of banes) that werewolves are opposed to the moon by nature, and that the Forsaken made a deal in order to overcome their weaknesses.

                                                          Logical arguments can be made, but they can't be called scientific until they're tested (and only if you test for "pass" and "fail" conditions; pseudoscience only tests for one).
                                                          Like I said, sorry about the gripe, but its important.



                                                          Also (because I don't like to weigh in just to gripe),

                                                          I'd find it to be an interesting story to focus a chronicle around werewolves who don't buy the huge mythic origins; its one of the themes in my campaign, actually. I'm sure many werewolves don't think about it that much because it doesn't directly impact them, but I'm talking about those who care and who doubt and who want to know the truth. That sort of chronicle has a lot of really awesome possibilities to it.

                                                          Characters who want to find answers to those questions would probably start by asking all the tribal and lodge lore that they can - some of which gets Really interesting. In Tribes of the Moon, there's a story that there are actually two Black Wolves (the Hunter In Darkness totem), and a story that Destroy Wolf had a counterpart called Creator Wolf who went insane after Urfarah's death (or should I say Rabid?). One of my favorite Lodges, in Blasphemies, is the Valkyrja Mot, who claim Father Wolf isn't dead but trying to heal, and will return for the Final Battle. [I have a player whose character is in that lodge, actually, and who suffered a crisis of faith when she fought in the War for Denver and Father Wolf didn't return.]

                                                          They could seek out ancient spirits - elementals in particular; the Rocky Mountains have been forming for about 300 million years. That'd be a lot of knowledge. If a spirit's been awake all that time, and they remember it, and they don't lie. Hunting Grounds the Rockies has an interesting plot hook that could also be used - an ancient tyrannosaur spirit trapped in its as-yet-undiscovered fossils: a spirit which once was The Hunter God - did it also have Father Wolf's purpose?

                                                          Maybe those characters would ask the Hosts for what they know (I wrote myths from their perspective, for the creepy). Maybe they'd ask the Pure (a Pure lodge thinks that Father Wolf's bones do exist. And Predator Kings look for a way to restore Pangaea). Or Mages for that matter.

                                                          And maybe characters like this would hear a lot of stories, which prove that they were right not to trust the Forsaken myths. That slowly chip away at everything they thought was true. Maybe they find hope and meaning in some very... unusual myths. And maybe they're found by people who understand and can teach them the true way; a way that keeps very quiet about what it knows, because the Lies of the Eight Tribes are so embedded that they kill to protect them. Manipulative people that know the right way to hook this kind of desperation and lack of faith, and push the edges of what they're willing to do until the soul breaks and they can be shown the Lords of Sin.


                                                          White Wolf / Onyx Path is never going to say what really happened or what proof exists in the setting. But that doesn't mean proof can't exist in your setting; looking for it can make a good story.
