    Idigam Chronicles Weekly Discussion: Houses of the Moon(Tribes)

    03-28-2014, 06:41 PM
    It's not how we hunt, but what we hunt.

    That little comment is the biggest distinction betwen the old take on tribes and the new one, and I think it's mostly effective...Sort of. The Hunters and Lords feel a little weird to me, but I can get over that. The main thing I'm curious about is what fulfilling your tribal sacred hunt will do-it's easy to ascribe them as gifts that relate to your prey, but I imagine the entire thing is actually more ritualistic, if freeflowing, than that.

    Also, my love for the Iron Masters has only grown.

    On the whole, I like it. But that's me. As for y'all? Discuss!

    PS: Also, I love the use of the word know in these descriptions. It's very pointed, like when a religious person says "I know [insert beliefs here] are true"-it just invites contestation between wolves of different tribes, but not, necessarily, enough to provoke serious bloodshed.

                                      So, I wanted few things changed for the tribes, or simply clarified. A)Why and how werewolves join them, b) make them all different.

                                      And, this CERTAINLY answers all the questions in a very simple yet clever way, tying it all into being wereWOLVES again. No longer are the tribes mash-ups of the Apocalypse ones that are unclear in their role in Uratha society. Now, each has its own purpose and goal, and tribe has a clear definition.
                                      I like that its tied to the prey, because WtF has so many great foes to use, and it is easy to see werewolves define themselves with the prey they hunt as they are supposed to be replacing Father Wolf. Specialization is helpful.

                                      For the specific tribes, Blood Talons hunting werewolves makes sense. They are the most martial of the tribes and so would be the most ready TO fight other werewolves. Bone Shadows are almost a given.

                                      Now, Iron Masters are the most interesting out of the five in my opinion. Instead of being -for- humanity, they are instead against its worse aspects. This changes them from the most human of tribes to the tribe most vary of humans using humanity's own methods to hunt. Clever.

                                      Hunters hunting those who would hurt the balance makes them the most balance oriented tribe. And Lords hunting the unnatural, well, they are the tribe most likely to go on such a hero quest.

                                      I can now totally see how tribal gatherings work, as well. The tribe members gather to compare notes, then leave for their packs with the added knowledge.

                                      All in all, I really like this.

                                      My gallery.
