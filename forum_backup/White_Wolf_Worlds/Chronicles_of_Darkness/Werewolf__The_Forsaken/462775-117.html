            Originally posted by Cainite View Post
            Using Tongue of Flame with Purity 3 --> Influence level 3. Able to control fire. How would you use this in combat? I'm imagining it basically being like a firebender from Avatar, being able to move existing fire along flammable materials and even probably short distances over air to spread the fire. I assume the fire that is directly under your control will not burn the user, but stuff that has previously caught fire will have normal fire on it and will burn you.
            Control can probably let you move it over longer distances. Might add a penalty, if you want. But basically it seems like it would be a damaging effect. You can look to Fire in the Chronicles core book to see how fire causes damage, because it's not like clubbing someone over the head with a crowbar. It 'automatically inflicts lethal damage per turn of exposure.' Said damage depends on the size and intensity of the flame, with Size going from Torch (1 Damage) to Bonfire (2) to Inferno (3). Then the heat of the fire adds to that damage, with a candle not adding any, a torch adding +1, bunsen burner +2, chemical fire or molten metal +3. 'So, a fire the size of a bonfire (2) and with the Intensity of a torch (+1) inflicts three damage per turn of contact.' Heat of fire would probably rely on the source of the fire, but keep in mind with Control you can make it burn hotter. There's also some rules for combustion, to keep the fire burning.

            Hurt Locker adds a 'Burning' Condition, which represents you being on fire, how much damage you take per turn, and adds the Blinded tilt.

            What would you use as a limit in combat for how far/much fire you can control? Maybe base it on Willpower (Resolve+Composure) and Purity Renown? Those combined in yards in a turn, or maybe WP*Purity yards? Also, need to have line of sight to it and each turn spend your action to control the fire?
            I'd probably break it up among the successes for the Influence roll, with successes letting you change it further, either adding to the heat level or moving it more than ten feet from its source or do something intricate like spell out a warning in the air.

            Could a packmate use Catastrophe at level 1 to strengthen that fire, add like +1 damage to it? Would it only strengthen the fire being controlled or even the stuff caught on fire?
            I think Catastrophe affects the users Influences, not those of others, even packmates. Though a simple fetish could solve that problem. That said, what Catastrophe does is pretty clear, it would allow you to use the Influence without any line of sight and drastically expand range limitations (to a radius in miles equal to twice their Glory). Basically all fires in that area could be affected by Control.

                First, you don't need Catastrophe to Strengthen a flame, you already have that influence at Purity 1, but you may need it if you want to be actually impervious to immolation.

                            Originally posted by Chandrak View Post
                            1. Are Claimed and / or Spirit-Ridden supposed affected by the banes or bans of the spirits possessing them? Our ST wasn't sure, but he noted that the entry he was using (some kind of glass spirit our of a 1e book) didn't have anything mentioned there, nor did the example Ridden in WtF2e have them listed. My suspicion is that its because each spirit may have different ones even if they're the same ilthum.
                            The thing is first edition didn't have banes and bans, it just had bans. And yes, they weren't put into the spirit-ridden example antagonists.

                            Also, Claimed are a type of spirit-ridden, where the spirit has used the Claim Manifestation, described on page 191. The entry also has the following quote, 'The hybrid being that results has the spirit’s Essence trait, ban, and bane, but is a material being.' For an Urged or Fettered spirit, which first edition would call a Ridden or Urged, the person the spirit is connected to doesn't have the ban or bane, however, on p185 where it describes how bans and banes interaction with entities it says, 'If the bane comes into contact with an item or person linked to a Fettered spirit, the spirit suffers one point of lethal damage per turn as long as contact holds. The spirit must use the Unfetter Manifestation Effect to escape.' and 'If the bane is used as a weapon against the spirit, the spirit suffers aggravated wounds if both bane and spirit are in the Hisil or the bane is in the physical world and the spirit is using a Manifestation. The wounds are lethal for spirits still in Twilight when the bane is physical.' So a spirit would want to be careful around these banes, as they're sort of hazard areas for them as long as they're in Twilight.

                            2. If they are, are there any changes to how that's supposed to work from spirits that are in Twilight or simply Fettered to something? What happens when you wield a bane against them?
                            I think the above quote answered these questions, but you can check the referenced pages for more details.

                            3. When paying gathra to a spirit, does the essence the werewolf is giving up need to be Resonant to the spirit somehow, since spirits seem to be supposed to consume only essence (and other spirits) of kinds similar to their natures?
                            They would likely only accept gathra Resonant to them. A fire spirit would probably just not accept a gift of a portable icemaker.

                            4. Can werewolves transport prepared essence somehow to a spirit for the purposes of paying gathra? (Like say, using Resonance Shaping when you know you're going to talk to a fire spirit to bring it a gift of fire-resonant essence).
                            I think the spirit would need to take the Essence. Werewolves don't have much control over their own Essence, and there's a Gift to transfer it.

                            5. When performing the Sacred Hunt ritual, are spirits aware that it is being enacted, particularly the targets? I vaguely remember it being mentioned at least somewhere that spirits aware of the ritual 'tremble and pray they arent the targets' or somesuch, but when I went back to find it, I could see anything. This came up as a question because since my Ithaeur Boneshadow has a a reliably high chance of quick successes and can enact the ritual in only 304 minutes, when we come across prey it is our duty to put down, it seems like I should quickly perform the rite. At the the time there was a question of whether the Spirit-Ridden in question would know they were being targeted.
                            No, unless they're informed of it through other means.

                            6. As a followup to that, are there any requirements for location or presence of a totem to conduct a Sacred Hunt ritual? We are a VERY newly minted pack, don't even have our totem yet, that's going to be part of our prelude, more or less. All we have is an assigned (by other packs) home, which we havent even gotten to yet, and instructions to form whatever territory we can control, since the region is largely empty.
                            No. First edition did require a spirit to be part of things, but second edition, like Elfive says, just requires someone who knows how to do the rite.

                            1. Are there any mechanic merits that relate to the Buddy Spirit touchstone? There seem to be some options here and there that mention spirit companions, like Buddy Spirit or the Wolf-Blooded Familiar, or Ambitious Totem, but nothing about any kind of mechanics involved, like 'How do they follow me around at all times if they bleed essence every day?' or 'Do I need to invest merit points in this?'. Or is that just handwaved since CoD is more freeform and cares more about the story than mechanics?
                            I prefer to do it freeform. However, mechanically for a totem, you can just use the Totem Merit (which specifically says can be used for a personal totem, as well as a pack totem), and for a familiar of some sort, you can use the Retainer Merit (or Mage's Familiar Merit).
