    I pretty much 100% disagree with you on this one Claire. Spending Essence is always a conscious choice. Even when the action is reflexive, it requires the tiniest amount of thought to do it. This can't happen when unconscious.

    p. 171 says nothing to negate this. Nothing stops a werewolf from regenerating. They always regenerate. While unconscious, they'll regenerate 1B per turn, and 1L every 15 minutes. As long as the werewolf can regenerate her rightmost box means that she'll be back up in very little time, but not instantly. That exact sentence makes perfect sense without needing to spend essence while unconscious.

    The way I'd suggest running it is that the werewolf won't bleed out at all. If she fails the Stamina roll, she falls unconscious from the lethal damage (with whatever wraps around into aggravated), and that's as far as it goes. As long as enemies don't keep attacking her, she doesn't keep adding damage until she dies. After 15 minutes she regenerates that right-most lethal box and wakes up. If she spent the essence immediately before falling unconscious, she regenerates that box next turn and wakes up.

    Edit: I'd also suggest that if the intention was for Uratha to keep bouncing back instantly from unconsciousness, we wouldn't have written they behaved exactly like humans in forms other than Gauru. We'd probably have said something like:

    The Uratha's regenerative powers refuse to allow her to succumb to unconsciousness for long. A werewolf never makes a Stamina roll to check for unconsciousness from basking or lethal damage as long as she has Essence. For bashing, she slumps for a moment until her regeneration forces her back into the fight. For lethal, she drops for a moment as her body instinctively kicks into overdrive to regenerate lethal damage. Either way, the Uratha is awake and ready to act next round.

                                                Originally posted by Claire Redfield View Post
                                                From the 1E core, but since the overall intent was to make werewolves tougher, this is the most likely interpretation of the 2E rules.
                                                Respectfully, I disagree. I consider 1E irrelevant for anything that we have a 2E version of. The intent was to make werewolves tougher, yes. That's why we beefed Gauru. That's the point of its massive regeneration and immunity to unconsciousness. Werewolf was never meant to be that scene from Highlander, so Hishu get stabbed, groan and slump, then immediately stand up and giggle. Werewolves concerned about the real risk of succumbing to their wounds and failing their back burn Essence before waiting for the Stamina roll, or they go Gauru, with all the risks that entails. Gauru is meant to be the double-edged sword, the constant temptation. It's meant to be more like The Avengers - when you release the Hulk, you'll win, but will it be worth the cost?

                                                Originally posted by Claire Redfield View Post
                                                Trust me on that one. I worked on the book, and even had input on the healing section. A sentence about werewolves being able to heal reflexively while bleeding out didn't make it in for whatever reason, but it's the intent, I assure you.
                                                Yes, I know. I was there. This specific scenario never came up, and I know that 2E places great importance on the purpose of each form. Every form has regeneration, Gauru is the *I will not yield* form.

                                                I'm not about making Uratha weaker in 2E, but I am about drama and player choice. Uratha have the risk of falling unconscious and being captured by their foes. That's drama - it makes the pack fight harder, think harder, and consider the risks going into battle. If the player wants to risk it all on the Sta roll, go ahead, but that's the drama of letting the dice decide. If she wants to guarantee another round, spend that Essence before making the roll. If she's already spent Essence this turn and can't spend more, that's the consequence of her choices as well. If her Harmony is right, she can choose to shift to Gauru in this instance.

                                                IMO, the sentence about reflexive actions requiring no thought (and therefore being possible when unconscious) is us writers being a little flippant with language and not considering at the time situations like we have here.

                                                As usual, I'd advise groups to use whichever interpretation best suits their game, but apply it consistently. Neither interpretation will break the setting, but each will open up tactical opportunities, while closing others.

                                                    With all due respect (and I mean that sincerely), your argument has precious little backing it, and a lot of circumstantial evidence against it (I wish that line had made it in there).

                                                    1) Werewolves regenerate regardless of whether they're conscious. The sped up version of regeneration, where Essence burns to supercharge it, is still part of their natural regeneration. The fluff describing it, as well as the rules, explicitly tell us that it's not even something a werewolf fully controls. It's the survival need of the predator kicked up to 11.
                                                    2) Reflexive actions don't always need to be a conscious decision. Otherwise, should you automatically fail any resistance roll to a supernatural power you can neither see coming nor anticipate?
                                                    3) An explicit design goal in 2E was to make werewolves much tougher. This precludes taking away one of their biggest strengths from 1E Forsaken.
                                                    3a) I don't know how anyone would think a werewolf getting knocked down, and rising again every time is comedic. It'd be terrifying if you're in that combat. You put the beast down, you think you're safe, she's up again and attacking. Your weapons can't stop her. This was something mentioned in the 1E book, as well, about how attackers might feel they're safe, only for the werewolf to lash out again.
                                                    4) Gauru is only usable once a scene and never once described as the sole method by which werewolves can survive lethal damage. It is stated that Gauru form werewolves don't have to roll to remain conscious and won't bleed out, though the latter is a consequence of the fact that they'll regenerate all their lethal damage every turn.

                                                    I will admit the book lacks a line clearly stating whether werewolves can spend Essence to regenerate while unconscious and bleeding out. There's a whole lot of stuff heavily implying (or outright saying, if you read the stuff I quoted as being a direct statement) that werewolves can do this, and not very much to imply that they can't. I did email Stew to get his thoughts on the matter, but he's a busy man so we might not hear back for a while.
