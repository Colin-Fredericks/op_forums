    I have come up with a bunch more variant Auspices. The thinner ones follow.

    The Blessed Moon: wolves of this Auspice are religious leaders & cult leaders. Their charismatic & give off a holy feel, like they're acting in service to some divine power, which is sometimes true & sometimes false.
    The Harvest Moon: farmers of essence. Very useful to any pack, Harvest Moons are sadly very rare & tend to be looked down upon automatically by any Predator King who knows of the nature of the Auspice because of how very "human" it appears to be (& indeed there is apparently evidence showing how the very existence of the Auspice can be attributed to humanity).
    The Tidal Moon: Whilst never very popular, the Tidal Moon Auspice has all but disappeared since the Begotten-Spawned serpent-shark shapeshifters started appearing. After all, they practically do the same job, & the serpent-sharks do it so much better.

    The Micromoon: When the moon in the sky is extra small.
    The Super-Moon: When the moon in the sky is extra big.

    All details on the serpent-sharks can be found on the thread "The Begotten-Spawned" on the 10th post.

            The Artificial Moon
            Soooo, there was (or maybe is, who knows with that guy) a genius, billionaire inventor who decided to give the world artificial moonlight.
            Seriously. And it wasn't even his weirdest idea. It was however, the one that bankrupted him, maybe.
            Despite, this (& the fact that artificial moonlight has no marketable value) the stuff is here to stay, somehow. Seriously, the stuff just keeps turning up despite the repeated attempts of hostile parties.
            In his attempts to make artificial moonlight, he succeeded more than anyone thought he would & the light can have an effect upon wolf-blooded, kicking them off into First Change.
            The wolves of the artificial moon are hackers, mechanics, engineers, technicians, & tech-junkies. They're the sort of people who will take apart a gaming peripheral, just to see how it works. The sort of people who will build a brand new car out of discarded bits-&-pieces from a hundred different broken ones. The sort of people who will write computer while completely asleep.
            The future is here.
            Hunter's Aspect: Malfunction - this Hunter's Aspect causes tech to malfunction when the affected individual is wielding the tech in question.

                The Double Moon
                The Double Moon Auspice. When you see it in the sky with your eyes, there's a niggling in the back of your mind telling you you're seeing something that isn't there. Maybe mundane scientists have confirmed the existence of two moons orbiting the Earth, but what you're seeing is something else entirely. Because that nighling in the back of your mind is telling you the truth. What you're seeing is an optical illusion.
                Wolves of the Double Moon Auspice can mess with the senses of others, not by removing sights & sounds & smells & other, but by adding them.
                When you face a wolf of the Double Moon, be careful not to trust your senses.

                            I recently started thinking about Christianity & Uratha. There currently is one interpretation(?) of this mix in canon, that is the Lodge of the Savior from Lodges: The Faithful. But that, all by itself, just didn't feel like it was enough, or something. I don't think I'm explaining myself very well here.
                            Anyway, the result is that I've come up with ideas for other Uratha Lodges that deal with Christianity, this post is about them.

                            The Lodge of the Adversary: werewolves are descended from the Devil, who is a tempter of the innocent. They follow this path & tempt the "innocent".

                            The Lodge of the Bottomless Pit: originally part of the Lodge of the Adversary, they believe much the same things save that the Devil is not a tempter of the innocent but a punisher of the guilty. They follow this path & punish those that they determine are guilty.

                            The Lodge of Christ: Jesus was a werewolf who managed to calm the rage to the point where it never truly bothered him. They have their own interpretation of the Bible which reconciles this fact with the events that are detailed in its pages.

                            The Lodge of Perdition: the Shadow is just another face of Hell. To be a werewolf is to bear a curse that was first levied upon the Romans who were cruel enough to torment good Christians. The First Tongue is but the language of Satan. We must not fall prey to the lies of the bitch-queen the moon demon, or to the lies about Father Wolf. We are nature damned lest we accept God in our hearts & reject all the lies of the demons of the Shadow.

                            The Lodge of Revelations: beliefs focused on the Book of Revelations. The Apocalypse is approaching/here & the Uratha have a sacred task to fight the servants of Hell off & get humanity through this. They have the 4 Horsemen of the Apocalypse, the Whore of Babylon, the Beast, & the Antichrist to fight, among others. They've identified the Plague King/beshilu as being Pestilence of the 4 Horsemen.

                                            ● Theiss of Kaltenbrun seems to have been a real person & the trial did happen, in case anyone was wondering.

                                                    The Lodge of the Hogan: Werewolves & all other shapeshifters are the children of skin-walkers.
                                                    See, sometimes skin-walkers would still have sex with ordinary people, some of them had that desire. Sometimes babies resulted & some of those babies inherited a tiny fragment of what made a skin-walker a skin-walker. Not enough to be an actual skin-walker, but there was still something there.
                                                    At some point, one of the Diné Holy People (probably Coyote, though no one knows for sure) noticed these descendants of the skin-walkers & decided to "stir the pot", strengthening the heritage, & thus were the first shapeshifters born. The reason for the "stirring" is unknown, though a recent theory is that Coyote (or whoever it was) wanted to see what would happen.
                                                    As for the differing Tribes, Auspices, & Lodges? Gifts from the Diné Holy People.
                                                    The lodge targets witches (in the Diné sense), fellow shapeshifters who "succumb to their skin-walker blood", & any true skin-walkers they can find.
                                                    ● Silver & rage are remnants of the evil of their skin-walker ancestors.
                                                    ● The shartha might be the attempt of skin-walkers to replicate the creation of Uratha & other shapeshifters to have some dangerous servants.
                                                    ● Stuff like Idigam & Geryo are monstrous spirits creatures. Geryo are noted to have some connection to the spirits that helped make skin-walkers.

                                                    I said I'm not happy with the Lodge of the Asura.
                                                    I am happy with how the Lodge of the Hogan turned out.
                                                    It has a coherent, fleshed out backstory without feeling cumbersome.
                                                    It gives them off a cultural feel & comes up with plausible reasons for the various foes werewolves must face, excluding the wider setting.
                                                    The name makes a sort of sense, 'cause I read how stories featuring skin-walkers often end with the skin-walker approaching a hogan & being scared away.
                                                    The name is the commonly used, European name for this lodge. There is a second name which I am not going to come up with because I don't know enough about the appropriate language, or any language beyond English.
                                                    It predates Europeans coming to the Americas & have a feeling of age to it.
