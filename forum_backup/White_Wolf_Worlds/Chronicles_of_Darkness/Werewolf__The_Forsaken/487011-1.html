            Actually, the quote from the section Errol216 is referencing is not really a rule, so much as it is fluff text. Specifically, it says:

            An Uratha might, soon after his First Change, undertake massive efforts to impress his peers. Many young Forsaken die less than a year after the First Change in efforts to impress their new families. Sometimes, success results in downright epic accomplishments that shower a young werewolf with Renown. The problem is, a character sets her own bar for Renown. With every increase, she raises that bar. To go further, she must improve upon her previous achievements. Some Uratha accomplish themselves into a proverbial corner this way; They do something so remarkable that they can’t hope to do better.
            It's specifically because of "Story First, Rules Second" that the above quote should influence what justification the ST requires of the players in order to earn their Renown purchases, since by actual RAW you can spend experiences on whatever you want.

                            That was my thought. And I get what you're saying about wanting to really get rolling with the game after two sessions of character creation. Luckily, if you have your players' contact information, this is not something you need to do on game night. Let your players know that it would be helpful to know what they've done and what they think would be appropriate for Renown gains and have them email you their thoughts. That way, you'll get both the story and the game time that's dedicated to playing.

                            @iamthenight: The point of this fluff is to be a massive plot hook. You fell from orbit to get your most recent Glory dot? Well think of something super sweet for your next one. Then you get to DO that thing that's more Glorious than surviving a fall from orbit. How cool is that? It just takes a little bit of cooperation between the GM and the players. It also requires the players jumping on the chance when they see it, but that's what makes the stories good.
                            That said, there definitely is room for your slow-bleed-of-Renown idea. There was a thread at one point asking about how to raise Purity, pointing out that the "take a human mate" suggestion seemed like it could never be good for much other than the first or maybe second dot. My counterpoint to that was that, if your character had dutifully held to every other tenant of the Oath of the Moon other than "Wolf shall cleave to Human", taking a human mate could mean bringing your Purity to a sense of completion and fullness, thereby warranting the fifth dot.
                            In this schema, you'd think of the first dot as "oh, I get this concept", the second as "I have done serious work with this concept", the second as "This concept is something I'm good at", the fourth as "I am an exemplar of this concept", and the fifth as "This concept is a rule of life for me."

                            So yes, story before rules. Stop and think how the rules could enhance the story before you throw them out, though.

                                    @Tiarn: If a Renown dot awards you a Gift as it did in 1st edition, I'd recommend asking them to spend XP. Otherwise they might end up solely going after feats of Renown to get free gift, which might unbalance your game if you can't compensate, though it's perfectly alright if you can compensate for it.

                                    If it doesn't reward a Gift, by all means, go full story! Less hassle for both you and your players.

                                    That being said, here are the 1st ed Feats of Renown. Not sure if this is legal or not, but I've seen others quoting stuff directly, so here goes.

                                    Feats of Cunning: Killing a foe and making his allies believe the death was accidental; convincing one foe to attack another; avoiding potential human discovery of the Uratha without the use of violence; a new and original battle strategy; any monumental act of stealth, manipulation, cleverness or diplomacy.

                                    Feats of Glory: Winning a battle or outmaneuvering a foe in a spectacular or especially triumphant way; winning a battle in the name of a lover, ancestor or totem; telling an epic story that is later repeated by others; telling a stirring rendition of a traditional tale; acting as inspiration to fellow Forsaken; any other monumental feat of bravery, tradition, valor or daring.

                                    Feats of Honor: Adjudicating a fair challenge between other werewolves; performing one’s duties to the pack and totem in the face of extreme adversity; participating in a just challenge; uncovering taint or injustice; striking a diplomatic truce with a potent rival spirit; any monumental feat of justice, truth or fairness.

                                    Feats of Purity: Leading a righteous crusade against enemies of Luna and the People; slaying a true foe in proper combat; saving the lives of other werewolves or the wolf-blooded; remaining true to the Oath even when tempted or coerced to do otherwise; any other feat of monumental self-restraint, zeal or morality (as expressed via Harmony).

                                    Feats of Wisdom: Solving a great spiritual riddle; developing a new rite; binding a potent spirit; discovering and exploiting an enemy spirit’s ban; creating or strengthening
                                    a locus; negotiating a suitable compromise; any monumental act of logic, intelligence, memory or intuition.




                                    @TerraBen: Please reread my original post, since I've already answered that falling from orbit issue. I'm sure any ST worth his salt can extrapolate.

                                    Originally posted by iamthenight View Post
                                    Haven't read 2nd ed yet, so Beats are beyond me.

                                    1st ed had a few examples of feats for each specific Renown. If 2nd ed has the same things, you can offer them as examples for the players.

                                    I've found that instead of telling the players what they need to do to earn Renown, it's best I just drive on with the story, and at the end of the session(or at home) I'll go through the feats of Renown, see if the players have performed any and note down what Renown they're eligible to buy when they have enough XP.

                                    If you're doing this and the players know what to aim for, your players will soon have no trouble getting some Glory.

                                    Also, as someone above suggested, lower the expectations for renown, even down to a few hard rolls if possible (after they've role-played intensively of course).

                                    As for increasingly harder feats required for higher renown, why bother with that headache? You've got 6 players already, that should be headache enough. Just rule that feats of Renown are cumulative, with many small stories of glory adding ONE BIG GLORY RENOWN. As a possible variation of this, a particularly notable glorious feat nets the player/players eligibility for two dots of the Glory Renown.

                                    If you've implemented any of this and are worried about an explosion of renown, just make it more expensive to buy XP-wise.

                                    One man's opinion, folks. Don't bite my head off. Skull is hard to chew anyway.




                                    @Charlaquin: I only used it to combat a rule lawyer's reference of a rule which turned out to be a fluff. The rule/fluff confusion was his, not mine.

                                    To be honest, it's the only "rule"(metarule, really!) I ever use nowadays. My games are quite different from the basic RAW, even fluff-wise. I no longer see the point of being stuck in a rule/fluff cage, and my players are fine with that.
