                                        Since this is the Gifts thread, and thus to do halfway between a teaser and an on-topic post for the current discussion of undead etc...

                                        So the old Death Gifts, right? I love the notion of a Gift that's symbolic of Death reflected through the spirit and Essence of a werewolf, but that wasn't really what Death did under the old rules. We had 2.5 Gifts in its list that were basically all about dealing with undead - The .5 is Death Sight, then there was Ghost Knife and Word of Quiet. That's an awful lot of focus for a game-line that doesn't even feature undead antagonists in its core book. The stuff for interacting with ghosts felt legit enough, but it was too much emphasis on the undead and not enough emphasis on the general symbolism of death. That conceptual ground ended up being covered by other, later Gifts like Bone, if I remember correctly.

                                        Now, Stew spoiled some of the Death Facets already in his Gifts article - Cold Embrace (Cunning), Bone Gnaw (Purity) and Eyes of the Dead (Wisdom). None of those are about directly fighting/interacting with undead (although Cold Embrace has some utility there). That leaves two Facets unspoiled. Well, one of those two Facets is all about dealing with undead. It rolls together stuff for the corporeal and incorporeal types under one roof and yes, a werewolf with that Facet is going to be pretty effective against vampires, among other things.

                                        So if you're in a Chronicle featuring undead and ghosts, or a crossover game where it's important for your werewolf to be able to combat those types of entities, everything you need is in the one Facet. That freed me up to have the other four Death Facets dealing with various interesting death-related elements, rather than the whole Death's Gift basically being Undead: The Fightening.

                                        Usual Disclaimer: This is as of first draft, second draft may change everything, do not quote me on this, I was never there, you didn't see nuffink guv.

                                        - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                                                What I was worried about a bit when reading the gifts was, well, how universal will the theme of "the wolf must hunt" be?

                                                I do get that is the core of what is intended for the new werewolf iteration, but...

                                                To get straight to the point, I would definitely loose a ot of my interest in the Idigam chronicles if by rules and / or flavour it was ALL werewolves are supposed to do.

                                                Wolves are predators and all, well and good, but that is not the end to them, not even when you rephrase it to "pack hunter".

                                                Will gifts and flavour also support the aspect of a wolf as protecting his territory and pack (and not just hunting what enters / crosses those)? Will it support the aspect of the survivor (above being really good at bringing down prey to eat), or the aspect of being a very social animal (caring for kin and family and such)?
                                                Will the less inherently "wolfy" werewolf themes of being a shaman or being in tune with nature still be something that gets some inherent value instead of just being about the hunt?

                                                I guess mostly the nature gifts dissatisfied me a bit in that they seemed a bit too directly focused on the hunt.
                                                What I mean is: there is a difference between a power being very good for hunting and being about the hunt.
                                                I think too much of the latter might run werewolf into a trap of crippling overspecialization.
                                                Theme IS important, but when it comes down to it, you need to face the reality that players will want different things (meaning, note very player in the group wanting the same), so it may really hamper the appeal if the game comes off as too much of just one thing.

                                                As a comparison, look at Vampire:
                                                It is often said it is a game of extreme intrigue and similar and, yes, that is supported aplenty by setting and rules.
                                                But it does have very distinct vampire archetypes, the Gagnrel being much more focused on the physical and the animal aspect, the Nosferatu being about fear, the Mekhet about the unseen and the shadow, the Daeva about seduction, etc.
                                                But even within one clan, there is enough room to be a very different beast (A Daeva with a very physical focus for example and less Majesty or similar).

                                                I admit, if auspices and tribes boil down to JUST being whether you are a "social predator", a "stalking hunter" or a "spirit chaser", it would be less fun for me at least...

                                                Of course, so far, we only have a glimpse of the gifts, but I really think that making the gifts more "tools that work well for the hunt" instead of "tools just for hunting" would not be bad.

                                                My own predilections aside, gifts should, I think, not run danger of being too narrow, we already had that before with some and I think it may be a turn off compared to more "universal" disciplines.

                                                            Right, I'll address some points here:

                                                            Originally posted by Ray White View Post

                                                            Will gifts and flavour also support the aspect of a wolf as protecting his territory and pack (and not just hunting what enters / crosses those)?
                                                            Yes; Warding Gifts have been given a serious kick to make them better on a pack and territory scale.

                                                            Will it support the aspect of the survivor (above being really good at bringing down prey to eat), or the aspect of being a very social animal (caring for kin and family and such)?
                                                            Yes, to my mind, although you'll need to define what you consider to be aspects of being a survivor a bit better for me to be able to say for sure, and yes, there are a number of social Facets. As I've said before (in this very thread, I think?) there are lots of Gifts that haven't been spoiled yet and some, like Inspiration and Dominance, fold into the social side.

                                                            Will the less inherently "wolfy" werewolf themes of being a shaman or being in tune with nature still be something that gets some inherent value instead of just being about the hunt?
                                                            Yes and yes, although being in tune with nature really does still focus around hunting given that, you know, wolves are hunters and this is a principle element of their natural existence. Hell, in shamanic terms there's stuff right there in the spoiled Facets - the 'riding' Facets for animals and machines - and Elemental's Gift should help cover some of that. In general, because I've drawn more on the symbolism of a Gift than the older style's random-knicknacks approach, the shamanistic element should be stronger across Gifts than before.

                                                            It is often said it is a game of extreme intrigue and similar and, yes, that is supported aplenty by setting and rules.
                                                            But it does have very distinct vampire archetypes, the Gagnrel being much more focused on the physical and the animal aspect, the Nosferatu being about fear, the Mekhet about the unseen and the shadow, the Daeva about seduction, etc.
                                                            But even within one clan, there is enough room to be a very different beast (A Daeva with a very physical focus for example and less Majesty or similar).
                                                            So here's a thing.

                                                            Vampire has 38 discreet supernatural powers in its Disciplines section.

                                                            Werewolf has 110.

                                                            To a certain extent you will see individual Facets being more focused than Vampire powers because there are far more of them. If they weren't more focused then werewolves would have a supernatural power for every possibility under the sun.

                                                            Originally posted by Ray White View Post
                                                            Beast Speech and Beast Ride offered a lot of animal interaction powers,
                                                            Beast Ride is right there in the new Facet list for Nature. Beast Speech exists somewhere else now - hell, might as well spoil it - it's no longer a 'Nature' power because it's far more core to the werewolf's essence and thus is available to all Uratha as a Wolf Gift Facet.

                                                            forest communion was great for both a hunt but also looking for someone, guarding an area (the territory protection aspect was strong there)
                                                            It also crossed over with a very similar Knowledge Gift in the old lists. These two Gifts have now been blended together and upgunned, and are now a Knowledge Facet that is more broadly applicable because the old Nature version couldn't be used in urban environments.

                                                            In other words, the new take on this is more broadly useful than the old Gift was.

                                                            while plant growths and nature's vengeance also had lot of potential for more than hunting.
                                                            Eh, plant growth is still sorta there in Call of the Wild, but I'm not massively keen for it to be a Nature Facet still. If you want that sort of thing, you can go looking for a nature spirit of the right type and get them to use their Influences to gain the effects you want; no need to take up a valuable Nature Facet slot with it. As for Nature's Vengeance, it's nasically an area debuff/attack; there are Facets that already cover the kind of penalties it throws around, and as I've said elsewhere (in this thread? can't remember) we've generally dropped the Killing You Gifts where a werewolf waggles his fingers and a target takes damage remotely.
