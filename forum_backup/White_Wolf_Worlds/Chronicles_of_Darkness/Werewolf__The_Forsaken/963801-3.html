    Oof... that's a very impressive bit of homebrew there. O.o

    Huh... It's... kind of odd that nobody seems to have actually done a Wasp Host yet. Almost a crime against homebrew. Not entirely sure what they'd do if I were to structure them myself, but of course they'd be very nasty and very, very visceral doing it, even for a Host.

    - - - - - - -

    Dandelion Hosts were admittedly kind of a half-joke at first, but thinking about it they could prove a genuine issue despite not having the mobility of animal-themed shartha... crowding out natural plants by attracting pollinators to them instead, contaminating the air with "pollen" that in turn warps local resonance and possibly threatens to taint spirits in the local Twilight, infecting allergy-prone or otherwise at-risk mortals and producing hybrids at a distance through that same pollen... there's a lot they could get up to. And yet, given the flower has history as a medicinal herb, they could probably be harvested at a certain point in their life cycle for various purposes. Which of course means that ambitious werewolves (or mages, in crossover), probably at the suggestion and encouragement of a Dandelion hybrid itself, have probably tried to find ways to safely cultivate them.

    Tried.

    There's also potential for weird, visceral horror in the design of Dandelion Host hybrids, in part because of just how disparate animal and plant anatomy are. Flowers growing out of eye sockets; root systems spread throughout the body and twisting out of the scalp in a mockery of hair; clouds of noxious pollen trailing behind them as they move and breathe; sticky, milky sap instead of blood, working both to clot wounds quickly and impair an attacker; the skin coated in patches of petals or white parachute-seeds that occasionally let go and drift off, potentially to become a new shard and start the cycle elsewhere...

    Their history might take some clever work to make, well... work, but they could easily be the fragments of a Pangaean linked to Fertility or Spring that went a bit overboard. Overall I'm mostly spitballing at the moment and taking some notes, but... there's definitely material to work with and plenty of room to work in.

        These things exist, I've seen 'em. It's not exactly the most popular thing to say, mind you: there are hunters out there that maybe do what we do but do it better than us. If anything's a candidate, though, it's them. The Abazu, the Mantis Hosts. Their legends are kind of scary. They say that no Wolf-Father came for their progenitor. These things were just too badass. The world changed, so did they. Most everything got smaller, but here's the thing: it didn't get any less hungry.

        Abazu are nasty buggers, and I mean that literally. You almost don't notice they're there. They can blend in so well on either side of the Gauntlet, and if the stories are to be believed, they even sometimes hunt inside the Gauntlet. Yeah, imagine that. Things in there that shouldn't be, and these things that really shouldn't be are in there eatin' 'em. Or other things that try to cross over. Spirits, Claimed, witches, werewolves, what-the-fuck-ever goes where it shouldn't, suddenly you might find yourself locked in the arms of one of these guys.

        They don't hate. Not us, at least. They don't particularly seem to dislike us, any more than they do anyone or anythin' else. Makes it all the creepier when they're eatin' your friend, or that prey you've been chasin' for a couple days. Just complete ice. No fear, no rage, no emotion as they tie something up and begin to eat it. Faster than anything you've ever seen, too. No, I don't care what. No vampires, no lightning-spirit, nothin'. Absolutely nothing comes close to how fast the Abazu can grab you. They move so fast the Gauntlet sometimes opens up in their wake, like they sliced it right open. Just usually means more food for them. And once you're locked in, ain't nothin' is gettin' you back out unless you have your pack nearby.

        What do they want? To eat. Man, they just eat the supernatural, and don't care what it is. If it's magic, it's mealtime. What I wonder is just what they're doin' with all that energy, you know? Are they building up these stores of power to do something with some day? If so, what? The old sayin' goes that you are what you eat, yeah? These things eat anything mystical that wanders into their clutches. What's that make them, huh? What do they want? Knowledge? More power? I'll tell ya what I do know it makes 'em.

        Hungry.

                                                    Termite Hosts

                                                    Few among the People know of the horribly dangerous Ant Hosts, but those Elders of the Hunters in Darkness who do sometimes tell a little known legend of the Gisilu's Warrior Progenitor. While the Pangean known as the Warrior was an entity of death an ruination, it also held dominion over new life and rebirth, which makes the fact that the ant-hosts only seem to focus on destruction highly peculiar - though loremasters have a simple answer: The death of the Warrior did not mark the birth of one breed of shartha.. but two.

                                                    The termite hosts are rare, but like their ant cousins they congregate in large numbers, though their purpose couldn't be any more different. Where the ants kill and raze and decimate the spiritual and material worlds around them, the termites instead work to sustain the spiritual world and to help it thrive. While they will devour humans and kill that which threatens them as all Hosts do, the termite-shartha are much more concerned with their instinctual imperative to nourish the Shadow. While on the surface this does not seem to be so bad, and in fact sounds to be quite beneficial.. the truth, as always, is not so simple. How the shartha accomplish this feat is both elegant and disruptive, the hosts locate a locus and set up shop on its Hisil side, from there they build towering nests that greatly resemble chimneys, which serve to funnel spiritual energies and Resonance from the material world and convert it into Essence which billows from the tops of these 'chimneys' as great multicolored clouds that settle down on the surrounding spiritscape. This process is not without drawbacks, and the material world suffers for it; people and animals in the vicinity of these nests grow increasingly listless and lethargic, and objects and structures in the surrounding area are subjected to accelerated structural deterioration. Even worse, the termites use humans as living batteries and amplifiers for their chimney-nests, and their Hybrids are known to be some of the most variable and adaptable of any breed of shartha, including their Gisilu cousins, each successive generation growing more and more adept at protecting their nests from outside aggressors with ever-evolving bio-spiritual weaponry.

                                                    Despite the threat to a local area should these monsters set down roots, they possess a dilemma for many Uratha. While the Meninna will undoubtedly move to destroy these abominations, as will Iron Masters for the threat to their territories, other packs might, might, be tempted to let these things persist for a little while, reassured that the vast influx of new Essence will alow the material side to eventually recover once the termites have finished serving a packs designs. This practice is far more commonly implemented by the Pure than the Forsaken, as many Anshega view the termites to be beneficial, so long as they are strictly controlled and contained... a prospect that often proves to be more difficult than they anticipate.
