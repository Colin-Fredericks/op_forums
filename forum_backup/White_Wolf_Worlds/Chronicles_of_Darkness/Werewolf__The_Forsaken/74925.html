    Is Werewolf: The Forsaken the game you want it to be?

    02-19-2014, 09:16 AM
    I've been thinking about this for a little while now, and I think the focus on the spirit world over connections to both humans and wolves may make the werewolf title entirely misleading. For the most part when one approaches a werewolf story they expect it to be from the perspective of humans hunting down a beasts that eats them, this is no different from what used to be the standard approach to vampire stories.

    World of Darkness is different in that it makes the monster the protagonist, but I've been thinking lately the perhaps a big part of Werewolf’s problem is that it's too different from expectations. Now spirits have come up frequently as a subject in other werewolf fiction, but it's never to the committed extent werewolf adheres too. In fact, werewolf throws out a lot of the drama of the lycanthropic situation from a horror perspective. Replacing it with a mythic heritage that involves a strange Eden, an active overzealous father and an insane mother (unfortunate implications ahoy!), alternative creation myths aren’t really given any attention, this is just considered to be ‘the truth’, because… werewolf oral traditions are so reliable? The spirits (your enemies) told you so? What? It falls a little flat with too much thinking, in my opinion.

    Werewolves are one of the most removed from human creatures the World of Darkness creators have come up with, with the only justification for human involvement seeming to be for the purpose of reproduction. I don't know if I'm alone in this but that seems uncomfortable and like it can go in a lot of dark, but not necessarily fun directions. And when you think about it, why are Werewolves the most inhuman of the world of darkness creatures? They’re at least half human, yet their morality holds them farther apart than any of the other supernaturals. Now, certainly spirits can be compelling and interesting antagonists and reference points, but it seems like a lot has been lost because of them.

    Gifts and Renown, the werewolf magic tied intimately to spirits has to be one of the least interesting point of the game, and isn't anywhere near as compelling as other supernatural powers. Rites, being more versatile and less tied directly to spirits fair slightly better. Lunacy is another mechanic attributed to spirits (well, one spirit) that seems to mostly get in the way of telling interesting stories, reducing the threat of humans finding out about werewolves to what amount to a DM Fiat with how difficult it is to overcome.

    Werewolves pack structure is represented in most werewolf stories, but it's also based entirely off of modern myths, when the reality might actually be more interesting. I mean, if werewolves behaved like actual wolves and their pack was literally their blood relations, each werewolf has a substantially weaker family that they're responsible for, a family that likely is human(more or less) that has human issues with their monster relative who’s just trying to do the best for them (even when they’re not right about what that is). And honestly, I don't think I'm the only one that thinks that werewolf-human interaction might make for a more interesting story than bargaining with spirits that are arbitrarily unreasonable.

    Of the main three gamelines, despite being usually the second in release, Werewolf has had the least material printed for it before going to PDF format.

    This isn't meant to drum up hate towards the game, I’m looking forward to the new edition, but… has anyone asked these questions to the developers? Because this is the sort of reaction I get from my players when I bring up running a werewolf game.

    So what do you think? Is Werewolf the game you want it to be? And if not, what should werewolf be about?

      Concerns like this have been brought up in the what do you want to see in the idigam chronicle thread. I made a similar comment there. Ultimately though, I think the animistic spirit side of werewolf is a central part of the game's identity. Fans of werewolf as it stands currently would likely not be interested in the update if it did away with the spirit aspect, and I wouldn't blame them -it would not be the same game without it. I do think, however, that an effort should be made to play up the human/wolf dichotomy to a similar extent that the flesh/spirit dichotomy currently is. I think which aspect gets more focus should be left up to the ST, so chronicles focused on either are equally easy to run. And I believe that concern has been heard and is being taken into consideration.

                      Do not forget about the human perspective. There should be a disconnect and contrast between all things human and all things werewolf, and this should scare the PCs, especially if they're these newly changed werewolves with all their pre-change baggage, trying to adapt to being a predator in the vast, uncaring Darwinist nightmare that is the setting.

                      Werewolves do crazy scary things, sometimes because they can't stop themselves from doing so, sometimes because they choose to do so. They kill strangers who lack the requisite pure blood for the sin of loving and desiring their wolfblooded kin. They follow arcane laws of the Shadow that are hard to understand but which breaking, even if you don't know you've done it, gets the wolves and spirits bearing down on you. They eat people, because nothing is as fulfilling and spiritually potent and addictive to them as human meat.

                      This should terrify the protagonists. They should be thinking "is this what I am going to become?" How will they balance the needs of everyday human existence - friends, family, hobbies, a job, money, children - with the demands of being a hunter and a killer? This duality is what makes the game interesting.

                      Werewolves, being the least human mindsetted supernaturals in the game, have the most human ties. While Vampires and Mages often sever or alienate their prevous life, family, and such, werewolves latch on and hold onto it with rabid fervor. It's these little things they can't just leave, and abandon to go fight some war of attrition on the other side of the world. And to know that htose hateful things will try and hurt them, just to hurt you, is also interesting ot me. The Azlu that eats your sister's brain to utterly mess with you. The spirit that Urged your brother to commit suicide. The Ivory Talon who manipulated the bank to forclose your childhood home. These are things you just don't see. The war is much more personal, more lowkey, and to me, more interesting, as its something I can see myself trying to fight for as a person, rather then the Apocalypse "Why do we even try?" feel.

                                          For me, as I think I said in the thread about "what we want to see in the Idigam chronicle", no, it was not.

                                          To be fair though, Apocalypse was not quite what I wanted either, even though I think it appealed to me more all things said.

                                          I think there is a rather big problem:

                                          Werewolves do not have the same easily adaptable sources as vampires or mages or even changelings do.

                                          We got the story of the vampire's side with "Interview with the Vampire" and similar books, that worked rather well.

                                          Werewolves though have a lot of horror stories that are either about them being the monster to be hunted, OR of them not really being in control at all, being a victim and a monster.
                                          That does not really work for an rpg line (you can run it, but it will likely be a bit short and not easy to built a long story out of), while Vampire could adapt that formula of narrating the eternal unlife of its stars.

                                          Classical legends of werewolves often ave a strong link with curses, or magic, devil pacts (werewolf trials in the middle ages) and such, some of that might have served as a good basis, but this, I think is what we got with Skin Changers in a rather acceptable form.

                                          Both Apocalypse and Forsaken did a lot of inventing for the very basic concepts, I think that is why they have the problem of not being necessarily what a werewolf fan wants.


                                          For my part, if I have to cite a good werewolf story which I think is a nice basis for an roleplaying game, it would most likely be the anime Wolf's Rain.
                                          It was technically the very opposite of werewolves (wolves who assumed a human "glamour" instead of actually changing shape), but it did the sense of living among humans but not being one quite well, the struggle, the drama, the issue of forming a pack out of wildly different personalities...
                                          It also explored the connection between humans and wolves quite well and was, amusingly also about a kind of coming Apocalypse and the role wolves played in it.

                                          I do think that the aspect of animism is not a bad choice, shapeshifter legends tend to go well with that shamanistic vibe, not only in the Americas. Spirits, the way they are presented, can really encompass a great amount of supernatural beings over the whole world, you can do a lot with them and I think if Forsaken has a problem about spirits, then it is perhaps that it has too much generalizations.
                                          Their relationship with the Urathra, their hierarchies, their behaviour...

                                          I also think the Art of Forsaken was not really my thing.
                                          It did try to be freaky, full of body horror and similar a bit too much for my taste...
                                          I would have preferred a bit more of a "natural terror", more of the scaryness of tooth and claw and the dark instead...
                                          Most of the other lines had art that spoke to me, werewolf did too, but I guess it was rather evident just from the visuals that we would not really get along well...

                                          The game has its mood, it is not quite the one I had been hoping for...
