    I'm a very new DM to Chronicles of Darkness, and a new DM to tabletops in general, I've been pouring over the resource material and I had a couple of questions, I'm sorry if maybe these are easy to answer but with so much information I'm feeling a bit lost (I apologize if these questions have been asked before):

    1. Wounds, from what I understand they are places that permeate this horrific aura that detracts spirits of pain, and usually they're the sites of atrocities like the wound in Rwanda where the genocide happened - But my question is what is significant enough to make a wound, area there specific requirements and how best would it be to go about establishing one in a game?

    Additionally, there's the notation of "rare wounds" which seems vague, how would one be able to distinguish a rare wound if they don't know it was one?

    Also how does one go about closing a wound?

    2. I'm running a cross over with Sin-eaters, mainly because one of my players really want to play a sin-eater and I was wondering how well they would get along with werewolves and vice versa?
    I know they can share some commonalities and I believe my player wants to do an advocate-based sin-eater.

    Is there anything I should be aware of in terms of dynamics with sin-eaters and werewolves?

        Originally posted by Talona View Post
        1. Wounds, from what I understand they are places that permeate this horrific aura that detracts spirits of pain, and usually they're the sites of atrocities like the wound in Rwanda where the genocide happened - But my question is what is significant enough to make a wound, area there specific requirements and how best would it be to go about establishing one in a game?
        From Blasphemies, 'Opening new Wounds requires localized bursts of negative emotion and behavior.' Bale Hounds, who enjoy having Wounds around, often would help them get established by encouraging negative emotions or behavior. Keep in mind this doesn't require any human impact. A plague pushing wildlife in an isolated area to extinction or erratic behavior can be enough. But the site of a horrific and unnatural or rare human/werewolf-led event can do it too, the place where Father Wolf was killed is supposed to be one.

        Additionally, there's the notation of "rare wounds" which seems vague, how would one be able to distinguish a rare wound if they don't know it was one?
        I'm not sure where 'rare wounds' come up, do you have a book reference or page number or something?

        Also how does one go about closing a wound?
        Time and effort. The Resonance Shaper Merit can help, but you're basically attempting to heal the metaphysical 'memory' of a horrific act. Getting those who live in the area to move beyond it would help, but you'd still have a lot of work to do keeping Wounded spirits from making it worse.

        2. I'm running a cross over with Sin-eaters, mainly because one of my players really want to play a sin-eater and I was wondering how well they would get along with werewolves and vice versa?
        I know they can share some commonalities and I believe my player wants to do an advocate-based sin-eater.

        Is there anything I should be aware of in terms of dynamics with sin-eaters and werewolves?
        If you're using first edition, Sin-Eaters are mechanically overpowered, compared to werewolves. But they could be good for hunting down ghosts, and I believe hunting abmortals is a side job of Sin-Eaters that werewolves may be good at. It's possible other werewolves wouldn't like the deathly energy they were about. The Pack also gets into some details about how it can work out, or not. Werewolves don't belong in the Underworld, though, so trips there would be easy on the Sin-Eater and bad on them.

        There's no in-grained hatred or animosity though.

            I agree Sin-Eater suffered from Power Creep in first edition. Here is some of what I did to bring them back inline with my interpretation of the other game lines*:

            *Disclaimer: Includes references to homebrew rules and are built for Frailty Chronicle themes and context click the spoiler to continue

            Originally posted by Pale_Crusader View Post
            Sin-Eater Banes
            Netherworld Maelstrom
            The geist within sin-eaters are extremely unnatural beings and act as conduit of the power of the underworld. Sin-eaters are extremely susceptible to ghostly numina and ghosts gain power while within their presence. A ghost outside of the underworld gain an additional rank in Intensity while within 300ft of a sin-eater. Whether within the underworld or elsewhere the sin-eaters suffers a penalty to resist ghostly numina equal to Permanent Atrocity + 10 – Synergy, and take the same penalty while

            This bane may be temporarily suspended for a Sin-Eater that Opens a Deathgate. See Opens a Deathgate for more information.

            Backseat Driver
            Every Geist has a Deadly Aspiration which reflects its nature as an avatar of a certain type of death, using involving promoting the type of death it represents. Upon character creation the Storyteller assigns this Aspiration based on collaboration of the back story of the Geist done by the player and Storyteller. This Ossurary Aspiration applies to the Geist, not necessarily the Sin-eater, (though the Sin-Eater is free to take a Personal Aspiration in line with or identical to Geist if they choose) and does not apply for earning beats for the Sin-Eater (though if the Sin-Eater opted for mirror their Geist in Personal Aspirations, those apply as normal).

            Why the Geist’s Aspiration is important to track is because it provides tips for role playing interactions with Geist, and when the Sin-Eater has Synergy (ei number of Supports) less than Permanent Atrocity plus Temporary Atrocity, they gain the Leveraged condition from their Geist once per chapter, which is always applied towards furthering this Aspiration to get its resolution. Frequently this causes a feedback loop of gaining more Temporary Atrocity unless the Sin-Eater can raise their Synergy or dump Temporary Atrocity. Giving in to this Leveraged condition does not count as supernaturally compulsion when interacting with Hesitation, Compunction and Post Traumatic Stress Disorder.

            (Switch-able) Echoes of Change
            Each supernatural being with this bane has a set of circumstances described in a brief phrase that relates to how they gained their major template. This phrase is approved by the storyteller and recorded on the character sheet. Injuries inflicted when the circumstances described by the brief phrase are applicable cannot be regenerated or healed via supernatural means, it counts as resistant damage of the type normally associated with the damage inflicted. Supernaturally provided armors and supernatural powers don't provide protection from damage gained during circumstances described by the brief phrase. While circumstances described by the brief phrase are applicable the supernatural being's max die pool for any action is capped by their Morality Advantage. In addition while circumstances described by the brief phrase are applicable to the supernatural takes 1 point of Aggravated damage per Permanent Atrocity they have per combat turn. Supernatural powers used by a being with this bane do not work while the bane is in contact with the being, or the circumstance is applicable to the being. Abilities of a supernatural to rise from the dead do not work if this bane was involved with their death.
            .
            to convert to a system that doesn't use Atrocity you could instead use 10-Stability Advantage as the place holder of the value when Permanent Atrocity is mention, meaning a Maximum Stability Advantage (Syntheses in the case of Sin-Eaters) wouldn't take damage per round in the same way a vampire in my setting wouldn't take Aggravated from Sunlight unless they had acquired a Permanent Atrocity. Also I hve the follow Sin Eater and Werewolf Ban which increase as they gain power stat.

            Originally posted by Pale_Crusader View Post
            Supernatural Bans
            This is a a list of Standard Bans each Major Template has per dot of Power Stat, and are Cumulative.

            Werewolf
            1. Spend 1 Essence for each night without a Pack or outside your Pack’s Territory (or if the Pack has no Territory)
            2. Spend 1 Essence for each month without Siskur-Dah
            3. Spend 1 Essence for each month without time in the Material world
            4. Spend 1 Essence for each month without time in the Hisil
            5. Spend 1 additional Essence for each phase of the moon that passes without the Siskur-Dah.
            6. Never remain in the Material World unless supported by an Environmental Condition Resonant with Siskur-Dah of their Pack. (Pulled into nearest Hisil Verge when support collapses)
            7. Never remain in the Material World unless supported by an Open Environmental Condition Resonant with Siskur-Dah of their Pack. (Pulled into nearest Hisil Verge when support collapses)
            8. Never remain in the Material World unless supported by a Shadow Gateway which relies upon an Environmental Condition Resonant with Siskur-Dah of their Pack. (Pulled into nearest Hisil Verge when support collapses)
            9. Never remain in areas in the Hisil that reflects the Material World unless supported by an Environmental Condition Resonant with Siskur-Dah of their Pack. (Pulled into nearest section of the Hisil that doesn’t reflect the Material World when support collapses)
            10. Never remain in areas in the Hisil that reflects the Material World unless supported by an Open Environmental Condition Resonant with Siskur-Dah of their Pack. (Pulled into nearest section of the Hisil that doesn’t reflect the Material World when support collapses)


            Sin-Eater
            1. Spend 1 Plasm for each week without gaining a Clue for a Death focused Mystery
            2. Spend 1 Plasm for each night without spending time in a place with a Menace that matches your Threshold
            3. Spend 1 Plasm for each week without a Krew
            4. Spend 1 Plasm for each month without time in the Material world
            5. Spend 1 Plasm for each month without time in the Great Below
            6. Never remain in the Material World unless supported by an Environmental Condition Menace that matches your Threshold. (Pulled into nearest Avernian Gate when support collapses)
            7. Spend 1 Plasm for each day without ferrying a dead soul deeper into the Great Below
            8. Never remain in the Material World unless supported by an Open Environmental Condition Menace that matches your Threshold. (Pulled into nearest Avernian Gate when support collapses)
            9. Spend 1 Plasm each day that time was spent above ground in the Material World
            10. Never remain in the Material World unless supported by an Avernian Gateway which relies upon an Environmental Condition Menace that matches your Threshold. (Pulled into nearest Avernian Gate when support collapses)


            One of my Homebrew rules I'd recommend regardless of whether you use the above to help highlight the differences of mindset the breaking points of Harmony versus Sins against Synergy is this:

            Originally posted by Pale_Crusader View Post
            Compunction, Hesitation, and Post Traumatic Stress Disorder
            To initiate using lethal force during a scene that would be a breaking point for your characters Stability Advantage if the subjects of your violence were to die you must succeed a reflexive Resolve + Composure check, or spend a point of Willpower. If you fail you may spend an Instant Action to reroll (with the standard -1 for the previous failure). All further re-rolls within the same scene to avoid natural compunction against this type of violence are also instant actions. This does not apply when they character is acting on compulsions caused by the supernatural powers of others, or internal supernatural urges beyond their control. Being supernaturally controlled towards this type of violence, whether internal or externally, is always a breaking point due to its traumatic nature and damage to sense of identity.

            Should a character need to initiate lethal force when they are out of Willpower, they may do so but must take one of the following Conditions at the end of the Scene: Guilty, Shaken, or Spooked. If they already have all these conditions they instead choose from this list: Broken, Fugue, or Madness. If they are unfortunate enough to have all those conditions they instead roll three basic stress against a Support. Once a character has only has one Support left they take a Minor Undertaking as a Slasher, and when a character has no Supports they take Major Undertaking as a Slasher and no longer count as an ensoulled being with breaking points. For more details check the Slasher Section.

            This mechanic reflects that character's within this iteration of World of Darkness are not video game characters that perpetrate gruesome violence easily without care, and to also increase the value of refreshing Willpower for violent characters, which fits trope of within retelling of the stories characters that regularly engage in battle which is portrayed as gritty, to include real life war-stories, and some police dramas. (think of people indulging vices to "blow off steam" or commit acts of charity because "I had to make sense of it all") It also increases how dangerous creatures that lack breaking points that allow violence seem compared to those that have them; it literally costs them nothing to harm others. It also highlights how compulsions from supernatural forces towards violence are even more frightening for the loss of control. This also helps portray the mental stresses of combat, and battle fatigue.

                                Depends on the Primal Urge. Just because you only get nourishment from it doesn't mean you have to automatically go for the lowest possible choice. Humans have loads of food above spam and soylent. Dogs are omnivores, so if you're getting raw meat you can get it straight from a butcher.
