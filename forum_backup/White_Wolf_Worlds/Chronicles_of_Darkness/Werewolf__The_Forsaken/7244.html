                  In my chronicle that I run, we use 2 players and an NPC controlled by me to make it a 3rd. Basically, in order to avoid plot points where they just defer to my character for everything, I intentionally had my character give good and bad ideas. At first the players did everything my character said, boy do they regret that now. On the other hand, if you don't do something like that, what you have your character say will typically be seen as more important and the players will do it.

                              Going with the topic; A useful tip that i would give you for making so called GMPCs is this: don't make them at all protagonic, make them the kind of character that works best as a suporting cast. Don't make them combat characters, and if posible make them incompetent in the areas where the characters are competent, also dont make them super charismatic because youll end up talking to yourself, enjoy that characters suffering so you wont make him/her your pet. What i found is that the best kind of GMPC is a know it all, socially awkward, clumsy but totally lovable nerd, he can dump exposition about the things the characters don't know and is often a coward in combat, also bad luck makes the players love him more... its a nice set up for when you inevitably kill them in a horrible way, just the look in the players face is ... like chocolate to me

                              Alternatively and this depends on the group, if you are introducing them to werewolf you can make another character be the abusive alpha, just make him a total dick, that way you can introduce them to the setting ("you have the first change and this guy explains it while beating you up with a crowbar because tough love") and have the players eventually conront him on his bullshit or have him sacrifice himself for them to toy with their emotions "but he was an asshole!" "yeah but deep down he love you all and died for your sins"
