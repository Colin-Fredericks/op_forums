                            Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post

                            Okay i have to ask, how is more difficult to written setting than mechanics? I am honestly curious, because mechanics require to make them compelling and require tons of playtesting. Fluff however dont. If we were talking about the city books, i could get it because its tons of npcs and factions that interact with each other and they are hard to pull off right (Denver being the only good one i have read) but things like the city blurb in the new cores? How are they more difficult to write than mechanics? They are basically a little plot seed with little to no explanation or depth to them. They seems more like elevators pitches than anything else.
                            Good question and fair question. From my own experience (strictly not as a game writer/designer/developer). I find writing a monster in the CR 25-30 range for Pathfinder to be easier than writing compelling fiction. I have examples that I can work from, I can do the math to figure out statistically how often it can do damage, and I can ballpark the range that something should fall in to be balanced.

                            Writing compelling fiction, on the other hand, I find challenging. Predicting what will resonate with people, covering my bases for plotholes, making something compelling, leaving interesting plothooks for people to grab on to... its hard work. I spend so much time editing and second guessing my own work, even for short flash fiction. Sometimes the stuff that I think is awesome barely gets any attention, while the stuff that I run off in 15 minutes gets tons of appreciation. The only think that I can do to try and improve myself is keep writing more and more and more. There's no standard curve for it.

                            Here's the analogy I use. Crunch is linear in difficulty. It boils down to math. You can easily predict it if you know the rules and you've done enough practice.

                            Setting involves more human interaction. You have to speak to your reader in a greater way than you do mechanically. Because of the human element, it is quadratic. The learning curve is much, much steeper. Historical setting hits you with the additional whammy of having to do research, much of which is contradictory, and can easily blow your plans to shit.

                            In my opinion: setting is much, much harder to write.

                                            Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post

                                            Okay i have to ask, how is more difficult to written setting than mechanics? I am honestly curious, because mechanics require to make them compelling and require tons of playtesting. Fluff however dont. If we were talking about the city books, i could get it because its tons of npcs and factions that interact with each other and they are hard to pull off right (Denver being the only good one i have read) but things like the city blurb in the new cores? How are they more difficult to write than mechanics? They are basically a little plot seed with little to no explanation or depth to them. They seems more like elevators pitches than anything else.
                                            A power simply has to work on it's own terms. Most material of this sort aren't tested outside of cores and more just rationally double-checked.

                                            Setting has to mesh with what's been established while expanding the setting in ways that are interesting and generate conversation, and it has to be written well.

                                            What's more, most of the money-making audience is in for exactly this stuff-if mechanics are a little sloppy, oh well, but if the setting goes wonky, LORD HELP YOU.(Most payers do not actually play the game, and instead enjoy these games as basically open fiction and daydream spaces)

                                            And if you're doing historical setting, not only do you have to get your fantasy to mesh with reality, but you have research the fuck out of the time period to make sure you get things right, because if you don't, not only do you risk turning away your history loving crowd(larger than you'd think), but you also run risks of horribly making light of real lives. And on top of that, you have to balance that accuracy with the needs of play, of how much of what on each side has to go out.

                                            Mechanics are stupid simple once you've found something fun to play with, and they are inherently easier to fiddle with-the knobs, at least, are all labeled. Setting-setting is a bitch to write.

                                            EDIT: Also, consider-writers currently only get paid for the words used in the end(that might change, who knows). Writers and devs don't get paid for the amount of time put into research and revision that goes on. Between the two, mechanics usually end up requiring less research and revision than setting.*

                                            *Obvious exceptions includes the developer's work into the initial systems-consider this post primarily focused on supplements. And even then, setting STILL is just as arduous, and can still be more so depending on the nature of that setting.
