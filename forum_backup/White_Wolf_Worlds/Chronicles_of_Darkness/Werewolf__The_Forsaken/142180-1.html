    The Wise thing makes sense to me. It's akin to having a speaker get up on the platform and give a talk. At the end, there's a Q&A, not just for clarification, but for voicing disagreement and problems noticed during the talk. But the Wise listens to these and responds to them, either acknowledging the problems or explaining how they're not really problems. Putting yourself out there naturally invites more critical analysis.

    ...or a blog thread. /cough

    The most obvious situation I could see for this is having each member of an investigative team flaring brands and giving a pep talk shortly before actually digging in. A smattering of peer review and bonuses all around for the actual info-hunt.

    I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".
    An explanation how to use Social Manuevering.

                                                    Originally posted by Joker View Post
                                                    In my experience we rarely have more than one investigation roll in a scene, but to have someone question your reasoning your must have already done your roll, right?
                                                    The way the Conditions are worded, someone needs to challenge you on it before you can get the benefits; yes. So an effective pack with upon stepping into a situation where the Wisdom heavy character is about to roll their Investigation will challenge them before the investigation starts so that they're sharp and focused to prove themselves on the roll (getting all those extra successes).

                                                    So you come into a scene, roll to investigate it, then someone gets a die bonus to question your roll*, and after that you sit on those extra successes for possibly a few sessions waiting for the next investigation roll to use them on**?
                                                    You, the way it is phrased, could sit on them for a bit. But you have three Skills to pick from. If they challenge you based on an investigation you did, it doesn't mean you can only apply the bonus to Investigation rolls.

                                                    For example, you do an Investigation roll, your pack mate challenges you for not digging deeper or looking for more clues, and then you channel the bonus successes to an Occult roll to figure out a hidden supernatural meaning behind the events you just investigated.

                                                    Or do they apply retroactively to the investigation roll you already made?
                                                    Don't seem to be written that way.

                                                    And can you still apply them if someone successfully questioned your knowledge?
                                                    The mechanics aren't written such that it matters if the challenge itself is successful or not. Just that it happened.

                                                    Is there a way to get rid of the extra diceroll that the 'question their knowledge' step seems to require? I generally like to keep the amount of dicerolling to a minimum.
                                                    I guess? There's no reason why you have to roll it that we've seen so far. Though the full final rules in the book might have more details on what the challenging roll looks like and means. Maybe David will shed some light on this for us?
