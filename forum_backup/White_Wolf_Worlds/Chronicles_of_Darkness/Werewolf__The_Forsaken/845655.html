              It should be deeper than "Every Storm Lord is a paragon of Honor", otherwise no Uratha would be able to acquire both renowns. Also, cunning doesn't always mean cheating. Cunning might mean achieving victory using an unexpected advantage. For example, setting the local Azlu against your Beshilu enemies is a feat of Cunning, and might also mean Honor if at the same time you kept your promisse of eliminating the enemies of your neighbors.

              Cunning is adaptation, Honor is being trust worthy. You can adapt while being Honorable, is just a little harder, which fits the Storm Lords.

                  Cunning doesn't have to mean dishonorable and Honor doesn't have to mean stupidity. A character who has integrity and stands for what is right is honorable, whether or not he acts like a knight in shining armor all the time. A character who is clever and outsmarts his prey rather than just using brute force is Cunning, whether or not he acts like a ninja all the time. They're by no means mutually exclusive, and can in fact compliment each other very well.

                                              That's true of all the renowns, incidentally. Honor is primarily about belonging, about making sure that everything is in its correct place. That means keeping tabs on your fellow pack-members and it means dealing like for like with spirits. It also necessitates that you understand (or at least have an opinion) on what the correct place for something is. That's law. The Oath is a big deal because it helps define who belongs where. (The Oath is something else for Purity.) For a Storm Lord, a big facet of Honor is about the Forsaken and their place in the bigger picture. Inside that is a lot of manueverability for picking and choosing what you really believe belongs where and.

                                              Whereas Cunning is more about effectiveness, about being able to dispassionately contemplate all possible solutions to a problem and choosing the one that works best.

                                              There's room for the two ideals to clash, but there's also a lot of room for the two ideals to work together. That could mean a challenge to single combat after you've exhaustively locked down every avenue of escape for your target, or a willingness to fight dirty when no rules were explicitly set. Nothing about Honor demands that a fight is fair, and if you're an Irraka and they're dumb enough to turn their back on you, there's nothing wrong with coldcocking them without hesitation. Walking out of the shadows at the enemy's back? That's where you belong.

                                              I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".
