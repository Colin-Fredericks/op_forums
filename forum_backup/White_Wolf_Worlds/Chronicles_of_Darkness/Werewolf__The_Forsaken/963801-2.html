                    Originally posted by Pale_Crusader View Post
                    The rules of thumb that I've made for Hosts is that they all can take control of humans via an organ that represents control, Brain, Heart, Stomach, Eyes, ect... That those organs shouldn't overlap with other Host types, and the organ should be tied to the symbolism of the Host's drive.
                    ... And that concept just helped me expand a bit more on an idea I've been working on for a few weeks.

                    (Mind, I came into Werewolf with the release of 2e, and with only very limited knowledge of established Hosts from 1e or info about them, I don't know if the following is redundant or something.)

                    As mentioned a few posts above, the Hosts are more than likely the fragments of not-quite-slain Pangaeans, and with the latter's absence in the modern day it's very unlikely any new Host "species" could possibly come into being... but that doesn't mean we can't have something fascinated enough with them that it tries to join their number regardless, no?

                    Enter an Idigam I've been poking at for a little while now, whose experiments are all dedicated to the end-goal of becoming a Host (Ant in particular; I'm sure someone else thought of it long before me, official material or otherwise, but hopefully my own take on it holds up okay), intentionally instead of out of desperation like the Azlu and Beshilu presumably did. The Idigam itself has some history and preliminary stats outlined, but now I've started expanding on the hypothetical end result beyond spitballed bullet-points.

                    I figure the Ant Host's "thing" would be to scour the Flesh and the Shadow alike for essence (represented in part by the "Devourer" Dread Power being their "signature", corresponding to "Gauntlet Web" or "Gnaw Gauntlet" for the Azlu and Beshilu respectively). They'd also build large nests around loci that they'd proceed to taint with the essence they harvested, and link those poisoned loci together to expand their feeding range (and as such, an established colony always has a bloated "queen" Shard with Locus Manipulation, and older hybrids semi-frequently develop it as well, despite Essence-Shaping appearing to be beyond the usual reach of non-Idigam). What I wasn't too sure about was the precise details of their hybrids, but thanks to the quoted concept I've nailed it down to them nesting in the abdominal cavity of the unfortunate mortal fleshbag, starting in the stomach, and compelling them to eat anything they can remotely fit down their throat. Of course, every single scrap gets converted into essence for the Ants by way of their, ah, broad palette, so the mortal slowly starves while they themselves multiply and colonize the entire gut, organ by organ, before migrating through the rest of the body. Mature hybrids are, of course, something to behold - heavy mandibles protruding around broken, yellowed teeth; too many eyes glittering in each socket; extra limbs with too many joints; skin covered in chitinous plates, or bristly hairs, or thousands of tiny, twitchy, useless wings; countless ants pouring into and out of every orifice... real charmers, you get the idea.

                    Side note: Individual shards aren't very bright and colonies tend to be focused on sating their collective appetites, but hybrids tend to develop a curious streak and taste for tinkering echoing their kind's former nature, often hungering for esoterica and freakish experimentation almost as intensely as they do for flesh and ephemera. Unlike other hosts, they don't really want to restore their original form in its entirety, as that would defeat the purpose of being Hosts to begin with, but they do maintain contact with others of their kind so as to expand their collective knowledge and to constantly remind themselves just how hard-pressed a hypothetical force would be to wipe them out as they are now.

                    (Long story short, I wanted to mix two of my favorite antagonist types - the Idigam and the Hosts - and this is part of the outcome of that. I'm not terribly familiar with the game though, so I can't help but shake the feeling I've missed several things.)

                    I've got a bit of a bad habit of extensively editing my posts after the fact. Please try and bear with me...
                    In case you're comfortable being more friendly and casual about things, my name's Seren, or Es/Es for short. They/Them or Fae/Faer, please.

                                    Originally posted by Pale_Crusader View Post
                                    I have an idea about an Idigam that catches Hosts and transform them into things other than Spiders and Rats, which also changes what how they fundamentally work. Basically a plot device to keep all the Host lore being focused on just Bishilu and Azlu, but new Hosts cropping up because once created the new Host types have a life cycle that allows them to multiply.

                                    The rules of thumb that I've made for Hosts is that they all can take control of humans via an organ that represents control, Brain, Heart, Stomach, Eyes, ect... That those organs shouldn't overlap with other Host types, and the organ should be tied to the symbolism of the Host's drive.

                                    I've made a matching pair of Host types, Cockroach Hosts, and Squirrel Hosts that control their hosts in a particularly grotesque ways, They are biologically sex specific due to the symbolism. Cockroaches Breed as their primary drive(which is manifest in rapid spreading of the smallest type of this Host), and Squirrels Collect (which is manifest as the ability to collect multiple organs from many individuals and influence/control them all). For obvious reasons these particular horrors are not Parental Guidance Rated game friendly, but worked as a way to extrapolate the design principles.

                                    I really like the Aggressive Plant idea, and think the Spine would be the organ I'd assign them, with a Drive to Connect (with each other to create networks/briar patches). The Loci drying up just being a side effect of the massive resources it takes to fuel them.

                                    (Squirrel Host by the way will be featured in Shadows Over Longview, a fan setting book about my home town.)
