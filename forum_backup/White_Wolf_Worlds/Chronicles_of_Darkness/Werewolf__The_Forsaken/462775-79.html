                                Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                I was under the impression that, with the exception of Wolf-Blooded who become so due to Lunacy or Luna's whims, wolf-blooded are born as such, even if their Tells don't always manifest until later in life. Is this not the case?
                                My apologies, I was being too flippantly brief in replying.

                                Let me be clearer with the reasoning. All werewolves may come from wolf-blooded (lower case, don't have Tells, and don't get a separate mention in the 2nd ed book, because they're glossed over as not really existing, but by definition must), but they definitely don't all come from Wolf-Blooded (the upper case, with the Tells).

                                How do we know? Let's look at basic character creation (pp 81-88, not the narrative versions later in the book), and Wolf-Blooded (p. 296+).

                                Step One: Concept says 'Express concept as a few words, a short epithet. Think about your character as the protagonist of a novel. How would a reviewer describe your character’s role in the story? Is she the Disgruntled Ex-Cop? Is he the Penitent Thug? Is she the Startup CEO With A Heart of Gold? Keep it simple, but unique enough that your character doesn’t sound like just anyone from off the streets. This should imply some identity, some individuality. Your concept doesn’t need to have anything to do with your character’s identity as Forsaken, though. It can, if that’s core to her very being.'

                                No mention of being a Wolf-Blooded. No mention of formative years in a pack. Apparently being a Wolf-Blooded has quite an impact on a character's life.
                                From p. 296:
                                - From birth onward, wolf children see things no one else can. Their wide, pale eyes trace movements over their mother’s shoulder while nursing, like someone is walking by, and send chills down mother’s spine. They’ll speak to no one at all before they’ll speak to other people, and sometimes they whisper things in words that sound so foreign to human ears that they must be possessed or demonic.
                                - As these Wolf-Blooded grow, even if they find homes with packs and territories, family and friends, they never cease to be strange. They remain off-putting in ways that are hard to put a finger on. They talk to themselves, they twitch, they eat their meals strangely or eat things not food and it’s always as if they’re in on some cosmic joke no one around them knows about, let alone gets the punch line to.
                                - A baby Wolf-Blooded, rejected by his mother because he was born with a full set of teeth. A child who kills another on the playground when she simply doesn’t understand her own strength. A teenager who flies into a rage and wakes days later, in another town, covered in human blood and little else. These special, suffering souls are the bastard and beloved children of Luna.
                                - They find ways to cope, and often, revel in their condition. Some spend their lives aching to be Forsaken so that at least they have something like control — that is, if they know that the Forsaken exist. Those who never find a pack to join have the worst, most tragic stories to tell.
                                - On occasion, Luna or Father Wolf adopts a perfectly normal person. A run-in with the world of the Forsaken or the spirit changes a perfectly mundane human being, turning her into a
                                Wolf-Blooded. This is always traumatic; no one is adopted into the world of wolves without blood and screaming, and it is a miracle that any of these fresh-born Wolf-Blooded survive with
                                anything like their lives and minds intact.

                                I could grab more, but that's illustrative. Whether a Wolf-Blooded grows up knowing what Uratha are, or grows up without knowing, it's a big f'ing deal.

                                Go back to Concept. Is she an ex-cop? A startup CEO? Not, what did she think of the fact she always healed faster than anyone else but developed hives when her family pulled out the silverware? (A Wolf's Meat) Not, how did she cover that wound that never healed right, or explain the strangers who would come from miles away to experience visions after tasting blood from the scar? (Bitten). Or, did she think she was going crazy from the constant whispers before she underwent the First Change and discovered spirits were real? (Horse)

                                Tells are pretty important. Tells are pretty noticeable to the Wolf-Blooded. Every Wolf-Blooded has at least one Tell. (p. 300 tells us that Having a Tell is what, essentially, makes a Wolf-Blooded a Wolf-Blooded.) Yet Tells rate zero mention in basic character creation.

                                While we're talking Tells, there's no 'What did your character think when she lost her Tell while undergoing the First Change? Relief, or was she disappointed that her unborn twin abandoned the werewolf she became?' (Shadow Twin) Tells are a big deal. She loses it if she becomes Uratha. That would probably be something that the character would find noteworthy in her existence.

                                On the First Change, 'Sometimes Wolf-Blooded Change. Late in life, early on, with no rhyme or reason, some Wolf-Blooded just become werewolves.' (p. 297) The wording suggests Wolf-Blooded becoming Uratha is relatively uncommon. Uncommon enough that there's never any mention of the social dynamic between the 'successful' Uratha who came from Wolf-Blooded, and the 'failure' Wolf-Blooded who never underwent the First Change. The implication is that Wolf-Blooded are a state of existence, that sometimes change, but never with enough frequency that it's something anyone expects.

                                So, summarising - players character Uratha have no mention of Tells or their existence as Wolf-Blooded. Very few (if any) NPC Uratha have any consideration of their early years as Wolf-Blooded, and how that shaped their werewolf experience. Wolf-Blooded characters have no real expectation of every becoming Uratha. It's theoretically possible, but so is winning the lottery, so you might as well buy tickets and hope for that.

                                *But*, werewolves aren't human, and apart from the rare cases of Luna just being fickle on some poor bastard, never were human. Putting that all together, yes, all werewolves come from wolf-blooded (meaning somewhere way back when in the family, they had some kind of werewolf relative, minus that tiny percentage of 'moon-gifted/cursed' that means not really *all*), but actually very few werewolves come from Wolf-Blooded (which was the original question, but I had to unpack a lot of thinking that really wasn't captured in my thumb-typed phone-posted 'No'.)

                                                    The mechanics are on page 186, under Influence. A fire can do whatever a fire can do. They're good for killing vampires and Prometheans and mummies. You can use further Influences to Manipulate or Control or Strengthen said fire. As for how much, size and intensity, keeping in mind you're only creating one fire it would likely be based on your successes, with 1 success granting you the equivalent of a torch or small campfire. If you were creating a fire on someone, successes would correlate to damage.

                                                    There should be a scale chart somewhere but I think they only put it up in Mage.

                                                    'The spirit pays the listed cost in Essence and rolls Power + Finesse, with success creating the desired effect. If the Influence is altering the thoughts or emotions of a sentient being, the roll is contested by Resolve or Composure (whichever is higher) + Primal Urge.'

                                                    On that same token, once the fire is created, does it then start burning things and become natural fire? Or does the whole thing vanish once the duration of the influence has expired? At Influence (Fire) 4, creating fire from nothing could only be done for one minute per success (which, granted, is a lot of rounds), but during that minute does the manifested fire behave like normal fire? Igniting things and spreading like fire would?
                                                    Fire would behave like normal fire. Whatever fire 'spread' from it would probably be normal, but the 'base' fire would disappear when the duration ran out.

                                                    The durations are also on p186.

                                                    To that end, what happens if said character then used further manifestations of Influence (Fire) after initially creating it? What does adding ten minutes per success do, for instance, on Control? Or one day per success on Strengthen? Or are these effects ultimately limited by the initial duration of the Creation effect?
                                                    If you add Duration onto Create, the starting point of the fire lasts longer. If you add it to Control, whatever Control you use lasts longer, with that fire always doing that particular thing as long as the Duration lasts. Strengthening a fire would have it burn more viciously or be harder to put out depending on how you used it.

                                                    If a character had Purity 5, and thus Influence (Fire) 5, could they presumably make a "Permanent" fire? An everliving flame that will never extinguish?
                                                    Indefinite would probably be a better word. The fire would last unnaturally long, it would not run out of fuel, but I imagine it could be extinguished by external forces working against it. Fire is delicate that way, but I don't think anyone would crucify you for deciding the other way, that the fire was an everliving one that would never extinguish.

                                                            Originally posted by Caedus View Post
                                                            Could someone just help explaining the sacred hunt to me? Like how does it work and why is it so vital to every single uratha on the planet.
                                                            They're part spirit of the hunt. Part of their soul demands the hunt. They are also part human, however, and can't 'eat' hunt Resonance the way a spirit might. They were able to in Pangaea, but it no longer exists. So they need the Siskur-Dah ritual to fulfill that part of them.

                                                            Like what time is the right time to call one?
                                                            Arguably whenever you need. Werewolves can't go without hunting for long or they start losing Essence and making Harmony checks towards the Flesh, which makes further hunts harder.

                                                            Each tribe has a favored prey of sorts that is likely to spur them into hunting it. There's also the demands of the territory which can require action in the form of a hunt. Sometimes there's a glorious prey, sometimes a vicious enemy. Sometimes there's a personal issue, sometimes it's an existential threat. The hunt means something, but it's up to the individual werewolf, or their pack, to decide what needs to be hunted.

                                                            How does it work if you call it on a concept like let's say death or dishonesty as that also seems to be possible based on posts here.
                                                            They hunt spirits, spirits can be of concepts. If you were going to defeat death or dishonesty in their highest form you would be going up against high-ranking conceptuals like Pseulak himself. Generally you wouldn't start a Siskur-Dah against something like that unless you had at least a basic idea of how to go about it, or were planning on spending all your time working on a way to go about it.

                                                            A hunt against death or dishonesty from a group of scientist-werewolves could be an attempt to create a death vaccine, or a working lie detector.

                                                            What effect does ghost wolves that go on a sacred hunt have compared to the effects the different tribe memberships give when they go on a sacred hunt?
                                                            None. It seems to be granted by a tribal totem. No tribal totem? No Siskur-Dah blessing. However if you are part of a lodge you might gain a special benefit that way.
