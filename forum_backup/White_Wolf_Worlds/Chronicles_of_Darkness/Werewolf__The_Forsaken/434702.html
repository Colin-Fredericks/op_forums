    Question about 2nd Ed Forsaken

    03-09-2015, 06:27 PM
    First I love Forsaken as a game. The below is far less of a criticism as something that confuses me about the world itself.

    But their was an issue I saw with first edition and I'm curious if it is still present in second edition.

    In first edition the core idea was the werwolves were basically spirit cops. Their duty was to keep spirits on one side, and humans on the other.
    This makes sense but it breaks down a bit if you think of it too much. How?

    Well the book continues to argue the lack of forsaken. So this means that their must be areas of the world where their are no Forsaken present. This means technically
    the spirit world is left unchecked? If this is true then has the world in those areas collapsed? If not then really what purpose do the Forsaken have is it just busy work? Similarly what about areas where the Forsaken are not really equipped to be present, say deep in the ocean? While there may be pure present they have far different views of how the spirit world is supposed to be.

    So first. Am I missing something in the above logic? Or is this just one of those, just accept it it's the conceit to make the game work view.

    Second did 2nd ed do anything to approach this break in the game world?

          Second edition doesn't remove this idea of the Forsaken as inheriting the duty of monitoring the divide between flesh and spirit, but it does shift the emphasis some. There's much more emphasis on the instincts of the Uratha and their inborn nature as predators and hunters; the unifying theme of the book is "the wolf must hunt," and there's an understanding that werewolves don't really do these things primarily out of a feeling of spiritual obligation. That feeling is still communicated, but ultimately, the Uratha assemble into packs and bring low men and spirits because that's what they do; it's natural to them in a way that isn't shared with mortal humans.

          Aside from that, yeah; there are many places where the Forsaken have no presence or influence, but the result isn't spirits pushing through the Gauntlet willy-nilly, as falco says. There's some conveyance of the feeling that spirits spend a lot of time half dormant, so while an area may be thickly populated in the Shadow, not all of those spirits are conversing or scheming or running around at any given time. When they do push through the Gauntlet, which is difficult without outside influence, they can cause bizarre supernatural predation and chaos, yes, but there is a vision of the World of Darkness as a place where that simply happens in a lot of areas. People keep their heads down. If a spirit insists on doing something that people can't keep their heads down about, well, the Hunter: the Vigil book is right next door, but usually, people find a way to keep themselves mentally in a world where those things can be pushed out of mind.

          The effect of having a Forsaken pack watching over your neighborhood isn't (usually; but game campaigns tend to focus on exceptionally interesting places and times!) to save it from becoming an unrecognizable, otherworldly bloodbath. It's to sweep the corners and curb excesses. It prevents having that dark spot in the alley or the back of the forest where people disappear every so often, or come back changed, not remembering what happened to them. It makes sure the flow of Essence doesn't dry up and enervate the will of the people living there. It makes things calmer and more reasonable.

          Assuming that's what the pack wants to accomplish and they're good at accomplishing it. Neither of those are givens, either. Maybe they just want to rule the roost. That's very human, after all, and they're half human.

                      Humans generate more Essence. It's their nature. The way their emotions explode so vividly is what creates large amounts of Essence and the way their emotions fluctuate wildly thickens the Gauntlet. In the wilderness and in the deep ocean, there's less Essence being generated, so most of the spirits go dormant despite the weaker Gauntlet. With fewer spirits around, what Essence exists isn't as hotly contested, so there's much less impetus to flee from predation or manipulate material-side creatures.

                      Uratha defend humans. Hishu is based on humans for this reason: the Herd is human. Most other creatures have spirits of their own and it's much less likely that they'll be preyed upon by spirits, but humans are particularly vulnerable to spiritual predation because of the breadth of emotion they are capable of generating: most spirits can find a way to make a human generate Essence resonant with their desired munchies, whereas a spirit of loyalty is going to be completely stymied if they try to Urge an angler fish.

                          Kind of an interesting question. First edition was ostensibly more about dark mystery than dark brutality. It wasn't like oWoD, where it's our world, but worse, with sin run rampant. It was more our world, but more mysterious. There were conspiracies and things in the shadows, and stranger things behind those things, and even they had strings being pulled by mysterious forces.

                          Second edition seems to have kept the mystery but gone and embraced the more evil aspects. The Strix are bad enough when they prey on vampires, but what they do to humanity is often worse. The cults of the God Machine often involve weird sacrifice, maybe even that of life. Of course, it's all happenin through the lens of dark mystery. Or else we wouldn't have so many glorious 'What is this thing?' threads.

                          Whether other parts of the world have gone to hell or not is going to depend entirely on your game, but it certainly seems like if there's not werewolves around, the claimed, shartha, ridden or Pure or Bale Hounds or idigam are. Did you read the fiction? Thousands died. It's pretty brutal even when the werewolves are on the hunt. While the writers probably wouldn't do it, it's easy to imagine a lot of the bad aspects of our world magnified by the spirits and other Things of the World of Darkness.

                          As for the ocean, I don't think nature on its own is a perfect balance. Stuff happens, even without humanity. But there's a lot less stuff probably happening down there, or if it is happening, werewolves wouldn't really be able to see the results until the stars are right or nuclear testing awakens some ancient beast.
