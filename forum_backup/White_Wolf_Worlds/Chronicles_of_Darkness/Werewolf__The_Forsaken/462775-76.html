            Originally posted by Arcanist View Post
            1) Totems probably shouldn't teach Gifts because it runs the risk of disrupting the totem bond, but if they do teach Gifts they can always sense the general distance and direction of the individual they have marked. Is this something exclusive to the unique nature of Totems or is this true of all Spirits? Because if it's true of all Spirits you had better be careful about declaring Siskur-Dah to force a Spirit to teach you a Gift.
            I think this is just a totem thing, perhaps due to their already-close link to Uratha. But I wouldn't be adverse to having some spirits have access to a similar ability. The thing with the Siskur-Dah is that, like most rituals, providing you're going by the rules and playing fair, it's an accepted part of the spirit world rules. A spirit might resent being on the losing end of the hunt, but they don't seem like they would hold a grudge afterwards for it.

            Could a Mage with Active Mage Sight attuned to Spirit also be able to sense the totem bond?
            Probably. They might not know what it meant without focused sight or prior experience.

            3) How far can you push a Totem Spirit's Influences? Could you, conceivably, give them Influences normally reserved for Rank 6+ if you're willing to pump the Experiences into it?
            I think that just means a Rank 3 totem can have 3 dots of Influence: Fire and 3 dots of Influence: Passion.

            From the core book, p186, 'Although a spirit's Rank is also the Maximum rating for an Influence, a spirit can split its dots to have more than one Influence...Spirits may reduce their number of Numina granted by Rank to increase Influence dots at a cost of one Numen per dot.'

            As you can see on p64 in The Pack, you can purchase additional Numina and Influences for the totem.

            4) If an Idigam used Forge Fleshbound Spirit on a Totem, would that sever the totem bond, or would the pack now have a slightly more powerful patron? Would the Totem still regain Essence from the bond, or would it still need to feed exclusively on living flesh?
            Keep in mind it's also a lot more vulnerable, as it can now be killed and probably won't come back. It lacks the ability to shift into Twilight as well.

            I'd say the totem's protection from essence bleed keeps them from the double Essence-bleed effect of the ability but I could see Storytellers deciding that it would now bleed 'normally.'

            It's really in the werewolf's best interest to bring it back. Grabbing onto it and Reaching shouldn't be too hard.

            What form of gathra would you consider acceptable? Would it just involve praying to/paying homage to the Totem for up to five hours a week? Slip a prayer here, a minor sacrifice there, until it all adds up?
            The form is going to depend on the individual spirit. Odds are it is going to require a specific act, more than simple prayer unless it's something associated with prayer. You might look at the core book for it, p182 'a change to the material world that will produce a small amount of Essence are the bare minimum.'

            What metaphysical/spiritual changes need to be wrought to change a Pack Totem to a Lodge Totem?
            Arguably the spirits' ambition has a lot to do with it. Pack totems are a member of the pack. They do get benefits from it, but they're just one of the pack, even if they may be a revered one. Lodge totems are the bosses. They are not part of the lodge like everyone else, they are at the top of it. Lodge members are called adherents for a reason, and dedicate their Siskur-Dah, and often the rest of their lives, to the lodge totem.

            A pack totem is like an alien in your group of friends. A lodge totem is like a cult leader in a cult.

                                        When narrating the interior of a home in the spiritual realm is a little tricky to understand what really reflects so things are exactly as they are? If so what happens to objects that move in the material realm? Cars, bags, phones, etc.
                                        While it's easy to imagine a Disney scenario where every item has some animated feature that runs around singing its virtues, it's important to remember that things aren't inherently friendly there, they're predatory. Odds are most of the silverware has been fed upon by Kitchen and Gluttony spirits to the point that they would need to be awakened or something. A lot is going to depend on the situation. In a bachelor pad maybe only the tv and the bed are really active, everything else being drowned out by spirits of loneliness. In a family house it might appear as a chaotic whirling storm of objects flying around as trinkets and toys are constantly moved, with a stable 'eye' in the family room and a dark presence in one of the bedrooms. The Hisil is an opportunity to show, not tell. In a house you can imply what is important to the occupants by having it be a centerpiece.

                                        But even in a 'stable' home, the Hisil isn't a perfect reflection. Everything is going to be slightly off, tainted by the resonance it collects. A phone used for phone sex might appear phallic, beaded with sweat and emit heavy breathing sounds when you listen to the receiver, or the voice of an old flame. A kitchen might seem to have an inordinate amount of knives, beckoning you to use them, and blood across the floor, as it's where the previous owner slashed their family to death. A television emits a constant stream of hypnotic sounds and sights, and if you just look at it a minute you can find yourself trapped on the couch, staring into it for hours and days as it feeds off your mindless attention.

                                            The best analogy I've ever read of the shadow is that it's like a drawing done from memory. Things that stand out, things that are important to the artist, are going to be more defined and things that are subtle or unimportant will be less detailed, or forgotten alltogether. The way the artist feels about the things he's drawing often has more impact on how the drawing turns out than what those things are actually like. And, it's a self-reenforcing process as the artist draws and re-draws the same picture from memory over and over again. The things he remembers better, he draws more consistently, and every time he draws them they stick in his memory more and become easier to keep drawing consistently. The things he forgets to draw get harder and harder to remember. Then keep in mind that it's not just one artist, but a collaboration between everyone and everything in the space, putting it together from the collective influence of all of their memories. Some of the collaborators are probably more abstract or impressionistic artists, so some things will be more symbolic than literal representations, and those differences will be further compounded by the constant reproduction-from-memory.

                                            I'm not sure if this actually answers your questions at all, but it's how I think about spirits working.
