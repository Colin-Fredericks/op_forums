            Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
            Maybe me, I still have problems with Social Maneuvering - but in two days we will have game session opened on this witch NPC I mentioned before and players will try to convince her. Maybe after this I will have more thoughts about.

            For now, a small subtopic - Is typical "dear spirits, heal me cause I'm dying from wounds!" the Social Maneuvering roll or just standard persuasion quick check?
            Strictly speaking, Social Maneuvering is never required if you don't want to use it. But for a situation like that, I would use Social Maneuvering because you are trying to convince another character (the spirit) to do something (heal you).

            Since you're seriously injured, this is obviously an urgent matter and you don't have time to slowly work to make a spirit like you. Your best option would be to call upon a spirit you already have a positive relationship with, like your pack's totem. That might not require any roll, or it might be a few quick rolls over a couple of turns at a Perfect Impression.

            On the other hand, if no allied spirits are available to help, you'll need to express how urgent the situation is to what ever random spirit you're trying to convince. Telling them "I need healing RIGHT NOW, are you going to help me or not?" would be a Forcing Doors roll.

            Forcing Doors is just a single roll (of what ever dice pool you deem appropriate) with a penalty equal to the Mark's Doors. On a successful roll the Mark does what you want, on a failed roll they don't, and you can't try again. It's almost identical to a standard non-social-maneuvering Social Roll, the only way it really interacts with the SM system is that the penalty is based on the target's Doors (i.e. The lower of their Resolve or Composure, with potential additional penalties if you're asking a lot of them.)

            Though, remember, a Spirit can't offer services without being given proper Gartha first.

                    Another metaphor:

                    Forcing Doors is to Social Maneuvering as Down & Dirty is to Physical Combat.

                    That one die roll is Forcing Doors. Heck, if you don't want to play with the Social Maneuvering system as it stands, just steal the modifiers to the number of doors, and use those as guidelines for bonuses and penalties to social rolls.

                    So convincing someone to do something would be a Presence + Persuasion - Resolve roll, where you'd get a penalty if it opposes their Virtue or one of their Aspirations, and would be impossible if they suspected you were leading them on or lying to them.
