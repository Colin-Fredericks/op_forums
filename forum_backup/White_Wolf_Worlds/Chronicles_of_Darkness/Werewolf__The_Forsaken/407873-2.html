    When considering playing the Pure, don't forget their Totems and the crippling restrictions they give the packs they enslave. The Pure book mentions, as examples of totem bans that apply to individual Pure packs, offering their Totem children's fingers every couple days; having to fuck whenever there's a chance, having to consume human or wolf flesh once a week while still keeping their Harmony higher than 5; burning down at least one building per week, etc. Pure totem bans take away a significant part of a werewolf's freedom. That's the price the Pure pay for an existence that is safer and less plagued by moral quandaries and concerns than that of the Forsaken.

    --

    You can't have any real peace. The entire ethos behind the Pure is that they believe they will have their redemption and victory when all of the Forsaken are converted or dead.

    Muslims and Christians can have peace. They worship the same god even if they kill each other over the details sometimes. But Christians and Al Qaeda will never have peace, since it's the stated goal of Al Qaeda to kill Westerners in the name of a global Islamic movement.

    That's the Pure. They actively believe that genocide - genocide - is a valid way for them to come out on top and achieve the way of life they want. Ironically, they're already on top. The Forsaken are the underdogs, savagely fighting for survival. The Pure are already dominant, yet they whine about being martyrs.

    Ugh.

    Look, ultimately Werewolf is about telling stories. A story needs compelling antagonists in order to make the struggles of the main characters worthwhile. Eric Draven and his crow had Top Dollar and the gang members, for example. They were not good people who got a bad rap or had excellent reasons for their actions. They were shitheads who did awful things because they wanted to, because no-one ever stopped them before. You didn't watch The Crow and think "Wow, they've got it right. A film about these guys would be so much edgier and cooler." Same thing with the Pure.

    Yes, Werewolf is a game. But it is a game about telling stories. In this regard, people misunderstanding the Pure are not saying "I want to use a blue counter instead of a red counter" or "I want to be the top hat instead of the battleship." They're saying "I want to be the story's antagonists, like Top Dollar, or Sephiroth, or Syndrome." These are all characters designed to be fantastic antagonists because of their beliefs and their actions driving them to do evil things. That's what's cool about them.

    The Pure are not shallow Chaotic Evil bad guys who exist solely to kill things. But they are compelling antagonist characters in a story - and that's what they were always meant to be. They are by equal measures evil, selfish, misguided and fanatical. They do horrible things because they believe - wrongly - that they are the right things to do. And that's the key point. What they believe is essentially a lie. Their beliefs rise from a moment that their own ancestors failed to do the right thing. They compound that failure by insisting that things will be great for them again - no matter at what cost to humans - if they succeed in genocide or converting the Forsaken. The world of humans will be at the mercy of the Shadow, if that ever happened.

    I don't buy into the Forsaken en masse as goody-goody heroes, though that's a concept with a lot of mileage in some groups and character backgrounds. They're desperate, almost-beaten people like you and me, looking to survive in a world that hates them. Simple as that. Part of the world that hates them is the Pure, who press on their crusade with howls of martyrdom, much like the people who crashed those planes into the Twin Towers.

    "You've wronged us!" they cry. Uh, well...I haven't wronged them. Neither have you. Neither have the Forsaken. Yet we'd all pay when the Pure come to town, much like 3,000 innocent people paid when planes smashed into their buildings. The guys flying those planes had their reasons for doing it, but their reasons were fucking weak. They were wrong.

    The Pure and the Forsaken are not the Camarilla and Sabbat. They are not the opposite sides of the same coin, with both sides holding noble beliefs that the other side can't understand. Eric Draven's enemies were not just guys who saw things differently to him. They were malicious characters who did terrible things and that made them great antagonists. The Pure have their reasons for what they do, but their reasons are equal parts just lies and misguided excuses for horrendous acts. It's part of their tragedy and their terror.

    Put simply: the good guys are the ones who are taking responsibility for making the world around them a better place. The bad guys are the ones who are killing people based on the deeds of their ancestors, or at the very least forcing said people to accept their dogma. The lines are very clear here.

                                        On the topic of playing the Pure:

                                        Short version: not much interested.

                                        Longer version: I've played a whole lot of bad guys in my function as a storyteller, and enjoyed most of it. As a player, I've played a few pretty "dark" Forsaken characters. There was also this one Bale Hound PC whose main storyline was something like "fall from grace" followed by eventual redemption and sacrifice. All good fun.

                                        I'm far less interested in playing Pure.

                                        For one, the Pure don't have that much in the way of interesting antagonists. They're the top dogs, right? Who are they reasonably afraid of? The Forsaken are in a weaker position than the Pure; spirits generally don't mess with them. Far more interesting to play the underdogs.

                                        Let's also not forget their totems, their culture and the crippling restrictions thereof. Ultra-limiting bans? Frequent rites of purification? A strong chain of command where you get to take orders and do what you're told? There is quite simply less of a range for characters to be played with the Pure.

                                        The only scenario I could see as being interesting in the context of playing the Pure is exploring the motivations for individual Pure doing what they do - which might be similar to, say, exploring the motivations for ISIS fighters or terrorists.

                                        Note how this is specifically different from glorifying the ideologies or playing for squick value - I have seen posters on this forum advocate Pure games featuring play styles whose basic message boiled down to "hey those Pure are so much more awesome than the Forsaken!". I'm not interested in that shit, and, frankly, I'm never going to game with people like that. I find that sort of thing to be disgusting. A Pure game shouldn't be about violence and victimization solely because evil is so awesome. The Pure serve a different role in the setting. If you think that the Pure are better (morally better, that is, or "right") than the Forsaken, then you're not a person I am interested in spending any time with.
