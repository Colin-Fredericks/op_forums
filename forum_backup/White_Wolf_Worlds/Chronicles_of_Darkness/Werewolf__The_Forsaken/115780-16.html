        Ok, I have the go-ahead to give you some more info about a few Gifts Note that these may change between now and final print - not only am I still putting the finishing touches to my second draft but obviously play-testing feedback, editing etc may alter them further.

        For starters, let's go with a brief outline of the Gift of Rage.

        For Cunning, we have Incite Rage. This lets you try and goad a target into Wasu-Im, the 'soft' Rage. It's a Contested dice pool, and the target has to be able to hear you mocking or challenging them. But wait, what if you target someone who is not a werewolf? Well, in that case success applies the Berserk Condition to them. The Condition does what it says on the tin - sends them into a berserk rage.

        Are you a werewolf with Glory? Then try Berserker's Might, useable in Dalu and Gauru. It's a reflexive action that, once per turn, lets you either reduce damage from an attack by your Glory dots or lets you immediately heal a physical Tilt. Arm Wrack? Gone! Persistent Arm Wrack? Also gone! I mean, werewolves can heal severed limbs etc anyway, but this lets you do it in a trice. Oh, and the damage reduction doesn't care what kind of damage it is, whether bashing, lethal or aggravated.

        For the Honorable werewolf, Perfected Rage is nice and straightforward. Add your Honor to the number of turns you can maintain Gauru for. Be a regenerating killing machine for longer.

        Purity gets right to the essence of what Rage is all about with Slaughterer. You can only use Slaughterer in Gauru when you hit with a Brawl attack, whereupon you add your Purity dots to the damage inflicted.

        And finally, for Wisdom, Raging Lunacy lets you choose to apply the Berserk Condition on people affected by your Lunacy instead of the usual Lunacy Conditions.

        Something else to note; several Facets, like both Berserker's Might and Slaughterer, get activated for free and used instinctively when you enter full-bore Death Rage. Shoving an enemy werewolf into Basu-Im can actually make them even scarier...

        So for the other Gifts I'll reveal the contents of, I'm currently thinking Strength, Inspiration and then one of the three Moon Gifts we haven't revealed as yet. Cahalith? Rahu? Ithaeur?


        - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                    Anyway, so, the Gift of Inspiration next.

                    For Cunning you have Lunatic Inspiration. Touch a human or Wolf-Blooded and succeed at the contested roll, and you slap the Inspiration and Madness Conditions on them. The target is driven by the moon-mad inspiration that strikes him each night in his dreams, driven to create a work of art from the revelations he is receiving. The Inspiration Condition renews each day. If he doesn't manage it by the next full moon, the Inspiration stops coming - and the Madness becomes persistent.

                    For Glory it's Fearless Hunter. Not only does the Uratha add her Glory to her resistance and contested dice pools against fear and mind-influencing effects, but she leads by example - if you pass a contested roll against such an effect, you can spend Essence so that your packmates who fail actually succeed instead.

                    When it comes to Honor, Inspiration provides you with Pack Triumphs Together. You can use this at the beginning of a combat by spending Essence depending on how many packmates you're affecting. Once Initiative is rolled, all affected packmates can move up to the highest Initiative rolled by any packmate. Out of combat it can be used to add 8-again to teamwork actions.

                    The Purity Facet is Unity. Is one of your pack being targeted by a social manoeuver? Step in with Unity and add your Purity to the number of Doors the social manoeuver has to break through for it to be successful.

                    Finally, the Wisdom of Inspiration is Still Small Voice. This contested roll lets you drag werewolves who are in Wasu-Im, the 'soft' Rage, out of their growing fury and return them to calm.

                    - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
