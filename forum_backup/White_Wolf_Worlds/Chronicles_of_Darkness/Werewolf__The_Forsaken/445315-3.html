    I can see why some people take this as a joke. That is OK with me.

    Werewolf sustenance makes just as much sense as a game about vampires feeding.
    It is a role-playing game. Reality was left at the door when you entered this realm of weirdness.

    It is pretty clear from the texts in the core book how a Werewolf feeds.
    Sustenance needs become more harsher to balance the power that stems from being a high PU Werewolf.
    This creates a lot of fun gaming stuff to use.

    I for one have already added a high PU lone wolf cannibal as an antagonist in our game.

    Feeding has yet to be a problem in our game. Then again, our game is set in the state of Wyoming. Lots of four legged food!

                                            Originally posted by TwitchTheRat View Post
                                            They can partially digest a wide variety of things, including oils and trace minerals. Some things they can digest but not absorb, which aids in creating feces which facilitates the expulsion of material they definitely cannot digest. It entirely depends on how resistant the swallowed material is to a given animals PH level / relevant enzymes.
                                            You're parsing this a bit off.

                                            First off "digest" biologically means, "process so it can be absorbed." Something you can "digest but not absorb" only occurs if you consume so much you can't absorb it effectively, or there's something else getting in the way. Processing things like fiber to aid excretion is not digestion.

                                            Second, the book says "meat." It does not get into details on nutrients and macromolecules. Meat is tissue that contains proteins, enzymes, minerals, lipids, vitamins, carbohydrates, and so on. Claiming that Uratha (at least until they need Essence) can't absorb oils or trace minerals doesn't add up because those things are part of meat tissues. The marbling on a steak isn't fat added to meat. It is part of that cut of meat. When a wolf eats a deer's liver, it gets a big hit of iron and vitamins from that organ meat.

                                            It's not that easy, because their bodies would not be able to partially digest a huge number of materials and create feces that would facilitate bowel movements.
                                            Why not? The book doesn't say this. The book says it can't get nourishment from those materials, not that the Uratha's stomach doesn't have effective gastric juices to break apart materials. It says they can't absorb anything that's not meat.

                                            Other Uratha are hopefully not going to be a problem, but the world involves interacting with a lot more people that just other Uratha, unless you're the intentional recluse mentioned before.
                                            There's a big difference between "interacting" and "constantly being around enough for people to bother paying attention to your bathroom habits." Uratha are written as being fairly reclusive from mainstream human society as is. The descriptions of packs in 2e are things like gangs, or tight knit police units; the people you spend your day-to-day with are your pack. You're not an office worker during the day that goes to be with the pack at night.

                                            Though running around in Hishu does that the advantage of penalizing attempts to notice you being out of place in the crowd. A mid-range PU Uratha is going to have most minor quirks covered up by their human form.
