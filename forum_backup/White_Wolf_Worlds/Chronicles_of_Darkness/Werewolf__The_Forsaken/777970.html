    Mystery Cult Initiation: Walkers of the Silver Strands

    12-01-2015, 07:40 PM
    Strands of Silver
    The Silver Strands are the most fortunate flies, for they are given the Spider's grace. They were caught in the Spider's web, but instead of draining them dry, the Spider chose them to pluck the strings of its ephemeral webs. They have seen the true face of progress, a dream only realized by closing off the chaos of the spirit realm. Silver Strand cultists work tirelessly to strengthen the Gauntlet, promoting reckless urban expansion and feeding on the resulting decay. Like the Spider, they are skillful infiltrators, spinning webs of deceit, corruption, and stagnation to ensnare whole cities.
    Cultists: City mayor, jaded police chief, defense attorneys, city planners, construction crewman, inner city drug dealer, "incompetent" exterminator
    Initiation Benefits
    • Newly ensnared flies are taught to be as patient as the Spider in its web. They gain the Patient Merit.
    •• Proven members are taught the secrets of spinning webs of convolution to cover their tracks. They gain the Untouchable Merit.
    ••• Enveloped in the world of schemes, illicit deals, and greased political wheels, the cultist becomes a Strandwalker, capable of moving through this sticky world like a predator rather than prey. He or she gains 1 dot in Subterfuge.
    •••• Leaders of the Silver Strand have learned to read people and places as the Spider senses victims in its web. They gain the 3-dot Aura Reading Merit. If the cultist is touching the same object or surface as the subject of scrutiny, he or she may study the target's aura surreptitiously, feeling the metaphysical vibrations cast off by the prey.
    ••••• High priests of the Strand have truly become masters of the Web, ascending to the ranks of Spiders weaving calcifying webs over the Gauntlet. These members become Azarath, the powerful spider-human hybrids of the Azlu, in a special ritual (see below). Unlike most Azarath, this ritual grants honored Strandwalkers a power to assume human form for a time.

    Purpose & Doctrine
    The Silver Strand is ancient, born perhaps in the time of Pangaea, after the great wolf god slew the Spinner Hag and her essence scattered on countless spindly legs. Her children grew fat drinking the Essence of spirits, animals, even Pangaeans. Sometimes they hid among the skulls of frightened hunter-gatherers in their villages, so full of dark nooks for spiders to hide, using their stolen bodies until they could sustain them no more. The spiritual and ritualistic people of the Neolithic came to fear the Spider in its many forms, speaking darkly of huge, fat spiders in the forests and caves, whose webs could ensnare the mightiest hunters, the light of day, even hope itself. But the Azlu knew that some of these little flies might prove useful, serving as catalysts for unholy transformations, or as servants among their buzzing brethren.

    The Strandwalkers one and all believe in the creation myth of the Great Web. Mother Spider, it is said, wove a vast celestial web when the universe was young. Its luminous strands held worlds in place, served as the firmament among which the stars were hung like jewels, and the vibrations of those that lived upon the Web rippled to Mother Spider at the heart of her nest. So she knew when bodies mortal and celestial perished, and she descended upon them in the form of the Spider, drinking in their Essence that she might use it to weave an ever expanding Web. Sometimes those caught in this Web thrashed and struggled and threatened Mother Spider's delicately-woven cosmic stability. The Wolf was one such beast, ever savage and hungry, envious of Mother Spider's lofty realm. The Wolf wanted to feast upon flesh and souls alike, but he could not weave strands to the Web. He could only tear and break, plunging whole realms down into ruin. At last he caused so much damage that Mother Spider herself descended upon a glittering string to battle him. She was valiant but the Wolf was a ravenous beast, and in their struggle he snapped the threads that held apart the world of spirits and the world of mortals. Stricken by Mother Spider's poison, he tore her to pieces before himself succumbing to her righteous wrath. Now the worlds of spirit and flesh are colliding together, merging where they were meant to remain forever apart.

    The Strandwalkers seek to correct that cosmic imbalance and restore Mother Spider. They must work to repair the Web and seal off the spirit world lest the whole universe fray into formless chaos. Humanity's greatest nests strengthen the strands of the Web, and so the Strandwalkers ever push forward. Progress is healing, they say, but because of the Great Wolf's selfish savagery, the Strandwalkers can now only operate by furthering the corruption that began in his slavering jaws. It's the only way, by casting these webs of deceit and avarice, that they can effect the healing of the greatest Web.

    Relic: Silver Strand
    Each cell of the cult is given a piece of the cosmic strands once woven by Mother Spider herself. These strands are as long as a snake, sometimes retaining vague hints of the beautiful pattern they once held, encrusted with starry jewels. Prospective Strandwalkers all partake in a ritual wherein they touch the strand; if it clings to them, they are thrown into pits full of millions of spiders, consumed by the crawling mass. Those who touch the strand and can pull away from it at will are considered worthy of Mother Spider and accepted into the swarm. This same strand also factors into the cult's most important ritual: the Rite of the Mother's Visage.

    This rite involves the joining of Mother Spider's children with her favored servants, those who have proven themselves infallibly loyal to the cause. The high priest consumes a piece of the strand before the Azlu spins a cocoon of web about them both. Over the following days a metamorphosis takes place, and soon an Azarath emerges. These creatures are always monstrously powerful among their kind, gifted with knowledge of the human world's inner workings and the supernatural might to battle Mother Spider's fiercest foes: the Wolf's descendants. Further, these monsters remain active in their cult's operations, leading attacks on the spirit world, binding the Gauntlet in areas where it's weakest, and injecting their venom into society.

    New Dread Power: Man-Guise (Azarath only)
    The greatest Strandwalkers transform into monstrous amalgams of human and spider. Their hideous shapes recall the glory of Mother Spider, but few can look upon them without quavering. In order to maintain their webs of secrets, sometimes these high priests must take on a human form. By spending 1 Willpower, they change back into their original human form, almost perfectly concealing their true nature. Minor tells give away the unusual nature of these people: a spider crawling into the priest's mouth glimpsed in the corner of one's eyes, cobwebs bending and swaying toward the character even in absence of wind, and so on. Perceptive observers may note these things as unusual, but few realize the implications. This disguise is taxing to maintain, however, like trying to bind prey too big in one's web. After a number of days equal to the spider-priests's Stamina, the disguise breaks and frees the horror trapped within. He or she may attempt to maintain the disguise beyond this time with great effort; a Resolve + Composure is required, penalized by the number of days she has been disguised beyond the limit. Even if she succeeds, signs begin to creep through: spiders emerge from open wounds on the character's body, webs form in his hair, his canines elongate or his mustache grows to resemble the hairy pedipalps of a spider. If the cultist fails the roll, his inner spider erupts in a bloody shedding of skin, growing to its full size and terrible splendor.
