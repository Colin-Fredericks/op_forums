    Idigam Chronicles Weekly Discussion: Harmony

    03-22-2014, 02:03 PM
    Hey everyone,

    Since Stew has been giving us weekly votes to see which aspect of the upcoming Werewolf rework we want to learn about, I thought this would be a good place to discuss reactions, questions, concerns, benefits, and so on of the current revealed topic.

    My personal option with the new Harmony system is HELL YES!

    What I really love about the new Harmony system is that Werewolves were always an experience sponge. You could throw hundreds of experience into them for little benefit before simply due to the massive amount of stuff they could purchase. With Harmony out of the experience picture, it gives players an opportunity for serious roleplay without the nagging thought of experience on their mind. Before, while I may want to try playing a Werewolf who lives to destroy the pure out of revenge for his dead pack, as a player, I knew that it was a 1 way trip down experience hell if I ever wanted to raise it again.

    Now, while there are obviously certain breaking points which we are unaware of yet, the simple fact that experience is no longer the deciding factor in raising or lowing it provides a far richer environment with which to work in.

    This, and the fact that rituals are no longer paired with Harmony opens the floor greatly to their use for all werewolves and not just the ones with high Harmony

          This is a huge step towards making Forsakem the game I've always wanted it to be, but never really felt it played like. Obviously a lot will depend on the specific Breaking Points, but since they're customizable I'm not too worried about that. Personally, my favorite part is the consequences of having your Harmony too high or to low. Before, the Spirit Cop game was the only way to play (that was supported by the mechanics, anyway). But with Harmony affecting your Death Rage Conditions, your Bans, and your ability to shapeshift, it sounds like you will be able to capture the feel of the classic Wolfman story just by playing a low Harmony character. Or you can crank up the spirit world side of things even more by playing a high Harmony one. And since it slides both ways by breaking points, you can safely experiment with your character moving more towards one side or the other without fear of having to pay Experiences to adjust.

              Obviously the breaking parts will be a huge part of it and the optimist in my wants to have confidence in them to provide a fun gameplay experience regardless of the path you choose to take...flesh or spirit. Certain mechanics based upon Harmony are obviously really useful such as reflexive and dice free shapeshifting sound massively appealing and help keep the role-play element of being a fur covered war machine alive.

              Now obviously if I learn more towards the human side of things, I WANT the penalty to shapeshift as it can add the roleplay aspect of suppressing the wolf side to my character should I wish to do so. Perhaps my character gave in to the wolf too much one day and his best friend was killed as a result. Now, I want to toss away that heritage and try to live as a normal human. On the flip side, perhaps I was too focused on being human and neglected the spirit side of things. Suddenly, my entire life, family, and friends are in danger from spiritual predation and I have no way to fight back without sacrificing the last 15 years of my life's work. The possibilities are endless when experience isn't involved to limit you.

                              One thing that fascinates me is the idea of how involved Harmony shifts are. While it's always been thematically okay to raise a Morality/Integrity Stat if it was appropriate, a lot of it came down to exp expenditure, as mentioned in the first post. Now, rises and falls are regular things that are evoked by neither EXP nor big moments, but the regular activities of the players, making it more alive and vivid and involved in PC lives. Add in the idea of just how hard they're implying it is for a stat to stay in place, and it creates the impression that being a werewolf is like living in a endless storm-which is a metaphor I can completely get behind.
