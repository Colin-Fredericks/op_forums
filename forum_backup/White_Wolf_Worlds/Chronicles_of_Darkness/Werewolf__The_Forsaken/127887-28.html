                                                    @CatDoom: I do have some rough notes written down on how I do things, so if you'd like to take a look and offer input, that would be cool.

                                                    Translations:

                                                    dawu: to ravel; to wind; to twist; to wind up
                                                    kudawu: to unwind; to undo; to be undone, like a ball of yarn or string
                                                    Kulkaggabhu-Ur: Star-Crossed Wolf (totem)
                                                    Ur Kulumulan/Ur-Kulumalan/Urkulumalan: Star-Crossed Wolf (non-totem)
                                                    Kudawu Khil-Ulke: Unravelling Night-Sky
                                                    sengh: to cook, to prepare food
                                                    tuth: to slap, to smack, to swat
                                                    sketuth: lit. meaning "cook-slap", it refers to the act in Baltic myths of a werewolf cooking their meat by slapping someone in the face with it
                                                    Sunluhal: The crippled boys of Baltic myth who lead werewolves/The Hounds of God to their Christmas parties (lit. "the humble masters")
                                                    gindu lalarak: hall closet
                                                    gaz'gal: a basket, sturdier than decorative ones, used for carrying loads such as food or clothing; a hamper
                                                    assi: a hinge, a strap
                                                    ubbu: a hose; tubing
                                                    sakanafur: an inglenook
                                                    sigi: fleece, fur, fluff; padding, insulation; to be fluffy or furry
                                                    gugh: a ladder
                                                    isihar: a lamp, a lantern; a torch
                                                    mudhur: to be dirty; filthy, dirty, unclean
                                                    mudhur susk: dirty laundry, dirty clothes
                                                    suskthus: laundry, laundry room (lit. "a bathing area for clothes")
                                                    zas: grass, turf
                                                    zaskur: lawnmower (lit. "grass-reaper")
                                                    garum umfisah: library (lit. "home of scholars")
                                                    dubhur: light switch (lit. "makes light")
                                                    fantha: any sort of building support or supporting device, such as a support beam or lintel
                                                    cahha: a story, a tale

                                                    "Cahha kal nihhina": "This story is true", a common beginning to most werewolf stories. It's roughly equivalent to "once upon a time", "once..." or "long ago", but with more historical and important connotations.

                                                            inu: duty; an obligation; responsibility, accountability; to be responsible or accountable for something (Litheon, if you could change the existing inu entry to this, that would be great)
                                                            men'u: affection, caring, fondness
                                                            namzeh: agreeableness; to be diplomatic or agreeable
                                                            zeh: diplomacy
                                                            nihthuh: amicability, geniality; to be pleasant or good to be around
                                                            zi'il: benevolence; to be of a benevolent nature
                                                            namsum: charity, giving
                                                            namluk: cleanliness; to be clean
                                                            hugidam: confidence; to be confident
                                                            ummus: forethought, planning, consideration; to be considerate
                                                            shusuf: to gather together, to pull together; teamwork, working together
                                                            kal astagdu: the Uratha version of "shusuf", referring to teamwork and meaning "to become one hunter"
                                                            nihdhi: courtesy, politeness; good manners or behaviour
                                                            mudhhal: creativity; to be creative
                                                            u'se: to be curious, to wonder; wondering, curiosity, pondering
                                                            aru: to be dedicated to; devotion, dedication
                                                            basdu: dignity, self-respect
                                                            takus: a werewolf-oriented word that means to hunt persistently, to be diligent, or to persevere in one's duties; the werewolf version of agus
                                                            agus: to work, to toil; to persevere, to be diligent
                                                            skirin: discretion, caution, guardedness
