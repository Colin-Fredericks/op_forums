    (And this is what happens when I drink too much coffee.)

    Shayne held her breath as she tried to stifle the anticipation within her chest. Behind her, members of the tribe yipped and danced around the bonfire as the ritual reached it's zenith. The landscape of the junkyard twisted and whirled as wicked winds worked themselves into a frenzy. The dancers forms shifted, flesh, bone, and sinew reordered themselves to give exotic outlines against the brightness of the fire. She looked at her spear and worked harden rebar against the blackened blade. Her tighten grip doing it's best to hide the anxiety within her. She looked up at the moon mother and steered herself for the trial ahead.

    The dancers died down as did the wind. The ritual complete, they awaited in silence, even the ever restless spirits of fire talked to themselves in hushed crackling tones. A few moments past, the moon mother's light glaring upon them all. She gritted her teeth as the second past. Did they do something wrong she wonder? Did a dancer take a misstep? Did the site location repulse their intended guest? A thousand questions ran through her mind like a herd of rabbits fleeing the hawk's shadow. She again sharpened her blade, feeling the spirit's thirst for blood.

    A light snap broke the stillness. She looked up to find the enormous red wolf materializing from the wreckage. He gave a quick sniff upon the wind and pawed a burnt out husk of a car. After testing it's weight, he settled on top of it, much like a king upon a throne. It's metallic shell shrieking as his bulk fell upon it. He turns his darken amber eyes upon the tribe. Shayne casts aside the rebar and sheaths her spear upon her back. She rubs her fingers together, trying to find some outlet for her nervous anticipation    .

    The Firstborn eyes the site, as if looking upon a trophy that his pack has given him, then grunts. "A scavenger's den, crude but understandable for our purposes." His eyes his pack again. "So for what purpose has my adopted children called to me for? An enemy vexes you? A bothersome question? or perhaps some new enemy to rend apart and disassemble?" Ethereal drool drops from his lips. "The last sounds most appealing."

    Shanye continued to fidget. An elder appeared before the great wolf, appealing and lobbying for the right to include her into the tribe. She did her best to look composed as she heard the fire continue to crack and burn behind her. Moments seem to turn into hours before she felt those amber eyes burn into hers. She nodded and approached, her arms outstretched, her head bowed as she did.

    She felt the Firstborn nostrils upon her, taking in her scent. "Quiet, Athletic, Young, like a fresh pup." He pauses. "No scent of victory, no scent of deeds."

    She gritted her teeth, panicked thoughts started to race within the confines of her mind. "Pack leader," she said in a trembling voice. "I brought down a pain spirit which troubled the mortal world, I gave the killing blow and salted the area so no of it's tainted essence could grow."

    The Firstborn chuckled. "Indeed, you have and you would make a great Suthar Anzuth or Iminir but such a deed does not place you well in my pack."

    Shanye felt her fists curl into balls, her Rage rising. She had deed! She had victories! By what reason and right was Sagrim-Ur judging her on? She opened her mouth to give an angry retort when a sudden crash pierced the conversation. The sound of rude almost musical notes that traveled behind it added insult to injury. Both she and the wolf god turned to the source, a small human figure climbed it's self back upon the railing. A snarling rang up from the tribe and each member gave an inhuman predatory look upon the intruding human. They all heard the word that followed. A base and human word. Muffled a bit by it's distance but still keeping it's bite.

    "FUCK!"

    She heard a snarl behind her, Sagrim-Ur's pinned back, his mouth turned into a misshapen grin. "Human" he whispered and gave a howl that cracked the sky. The tribe spurred into motion, the vanguards already working them way up the railing, their Dalu forms swinging up the metal work like great apes. Shanye's body tightened as she ran in Urhan only to feel a strong bite pull her back. She turned and growled only to face Sagirm-Ur's toothy grin.

    "Patience young one, we wait for now."

    Huffing in irritation and baring her teeth, she sat next to the great wolf. Waiting for the others to return. Rage began to build. She was being denied the hunt! She was being denied her birthright! By what reason did this spirit to deny the very urge that writhed within her blood and bones? She gave dull growl to show her displeasure. Red Wolf gave no notice, his eyes and ears upon the water tower, waiting for his adopted children to return.

    And slowly return they did. One by one, they climbed back down. Mild disappointment fell from a few faces but others gave excited yips, yelps, and words. They got to run, they got briefly got to hunt. She noted and sulked. Two of their number came before Red Wolf. One wiping egg off his face while another, blood. They gave excited chatter among themselves as they proceed to prostrate. He eyed them both curiously. "I'm assuming that the quarry gone to ground? Or perhaps he was claimed by a spirit of eggs that my pack then scrambled?"

    The blood faced one spoke first. "Elder, the quarry is a mortal man. I tasted no spirit among his blood, flesh, nor scent. However, I've heard local nearby spirits chatter. They know his face, they've been to his home. They gossip to themselves that he is an observer, an outsider who plans on publishing his travels." He looks upon the Firstborn, teeth bared. "We will tear out this cancer before it spreads."

    A long moment past, the once raging bonfire now a series of embers crackling against the dark of the night. Shanye continued to remain silent, doing her best to contain her annoyance as her role this night was placed into a more passive one. Sagrim-Ur gave a series of thumps with his paw that rang out upon the metallic husk of the car. He stopped, and all the tribe turned toward him, snarling and grinning at the forgone reply.

    "No.'"

    A look of puzzlement and confusion flash upon the faces of the tribe, their demeanor shifting from one of confidence and twitching claws to one of hesitance and discord. War forms shifted shifted back into passive ones. Confusion started to give way to Rage and Shayne's was starting to boil over like an ill kept cooking pot. Words began to frayed into raw emotion and she was just about at her limit when the egg faced one spoke up.

    "Pack master! My pack owns the territory of whi-"

    Shanye blinked. Within the jaws of the Firstborn lay the neck of the speaker. He gave a pitiful yelp, like a dog who's leg was caught in a trap. Red Wolf kept that posed for a second or two before spitting the offending Uratha out. He glared at the rest of his tribe.

    "You are my adopted children but you are still my children. While you are my child, you are in my pack." He snarled. "And while you are in my pack. YOU WILL FOLLOW MY ORDERS!" He turned toward Shanye. "Is this clear?"

    A light mummer of acknowledgments and growls rang out from the crowd. Shanye gulped and gave hers.

    The wolf god turned toward his castigate. "Take the spirits, go to this mortals home. Burn all traces of his life. Leave his tools."

    He turned toward Shanye. "Follow him, Watch this mortal. If he takes his tools, hunt him down. When you have him trapped, call to me. Consider this your trial."

    Shanye got up and trailed after the waiting Uratha, his bite marks already healing. Shanye gritted her teeth and balled her hands then relaxed. She got her trial, she got Red Wolf's approval. All that was needed was to hunt down this damn mortal. She licked her lips. How hard could that be?

    ************************************************** ************************************************** **************************************************

    Shanye cursed. It was three weeks into this trial and this was the third time her quarry slipped her grasp. She gave a sharp kicked to the bedding, sending a sizable crack up it's frame.

    "He's clever" said one of her improvised pack mates. "I'll give him that."

    Shanye gave a growl. "We had him. We choked off his exits and tracked him down here. How in the everlasting holy fuck did he get away this time?!"

    "Are you sure this is a mortal? Not a mage or shaman?" said one of another as he probed the stacks of empty canned food.

    Another one sipped on a plastic bottle filled with dark yellow liquid. "He's stressed, lack of sleep most likely. Still mortal and most likely not a wizard."

    "What makes you say that?"

    "He wouldn't be pissing in bottles for one thing. Would've tasted it in his blood and scent for another."

    Shanye ignored the banter as she eyes a series of discarded notebooks. She began to flip through them. Her curiosity piking. She pointed out the general ledger the first part of the notebook, diagrams and graphs on the second, and a hierarchy chart in the last.

    "Someone's been busy." she muttered. A tiny bit of respect for her prey burrowing out of the contempt of her mind. She looked over the first section. The contacts were noted in simple dots, no names, no symbols, no identifying markers but below them were items, indicators of trade. She eyed the items. A fistful seeds, a few burnt out CDs, Tree bark thriced struck by skyfire. As she read, her mind began to open, she began to understand.

    "    Chiminage. He's trading with chiminage."

    Her pack mates turned towards her, eyes confused.

    "No wonder we can't ever seem to pin this asshole down! He's trading with spirits! They're covering his ass!"

    Movement within the room halted. The rustling of pages echoed as she frantically flipped to the charts. She pointed at names underlined. "Marcus, Liquine, Tonya, Default..." she stopped at hers, underlined in red. Rage crept within her but so did another feeling, one she hadn't felt for a long while. It's icy sensation moved along her spine as the Rage grew within her. She dropped the book as she slammed her fist into the metal wall twice. Rage begin to drain leaving the cold, hard feeling in her guts. Her mind took a moment to remember what it was. It was fear. She was feeling fear.

    "He knows about us. I don't know how he got the information or who he bribed but he knows."

    She took a moment to again compose herself and flipped over to last section. Details of the tribe were laid before her. The elders, the novices, etc, etc, and above it all was an outline of Red Wolf. She began to trace all of the outlines. "This isn't true. This certainly is. Here, here, and here..."

    "I trust the hunt is going well."

    She looked up to spy a muscular figure clad the local uniform, complete with mop and bucket. His eyes flashed red as he scanned at the room, demeanor as if he was an audience member in art gallery, looking over an exhibit. "Bit spartan for my liking but it would do in a pinch." he muttered to himself.

    She bowed her head, the best display of respect that she could give on short notice. "Elder, we just got done tracking him down here but he slipped the net via one of those damned doorways. We've managed to find some interesting notes however."

    The Firstborn licked his lips. "Do tell."

    She handed him the ragged notebook. "From the first section, we've seen that he's been trading with spirits to remain in the Shadow long term. Hence his ability to survive long term in it. He doesn't seem to be able to use essence but understands the value of     chiminage in transactions." She spied the buckets of human waste in the corner. "I believe he's using his naturally produced waste to trade for food. It would make good fertilizer that the plant choir would find acceptable."

    The Firstborn nodded. "Crude but clever. What else?" as he flipped through the notebook.

    "He keeps his contacts secret, apparently a trait from his journalistic days, but his records are plain to see. He trades with the data choir, the plant choir, and the city choir."

    The false janitor cocked an eyebrow. "Meaning..."

    "If we shake down or bribe these choirs, we can find these spirits and cut them out.

    The janitor gave an approving smirk. A toothy smile crept across his face. "What else?"

    She panted, trying to recall the data that she just read. Her brain reeled as wheels started to turn. "He's done his homework on us. He knows our people, he knows our pack, he knows our tribe..." She looked up at the janitor. "He knows our names elder, he knows you."

    To her shock, he simply nodded. "How are you plugging the holes in which he travels?"

    She stammered a bit, the Firstborn's indifference rattling her nerves, and pulled out a map "We..We've mapped most of them out. He seems to track here and here. I think we've gotten most of them mapped. Less he starts finding new ones to open."

    The Firstborn casually opened the document, flipping through to the relationship graphs and tables. He gave a smirk at the end and gave it back to Shayne. "Continue the hunt."

    She looked up on the Firstborn, her weary expression showing upon her face. "As you wish elder." She gave a puzzled look. "Do you always follow our trials so closely?"

    The reply was instant and flat. "No."

    The pack resumed it's pillage of the lair as the janitor left the hide out, mopping the floor as he went. She heard a just audio sigh outside the door. "He would have made a good Uratha..."

    ************************************************** ************************************************** **************************************************

    Shanye threw her spear and was rewarded with a scream of agony. The spirit flew true and it manage to snag the mortal's left thigh. He hobbled towards he door, his spoon-keys fumbling in hand. She fell four legs as she shifted. This was the fifth time they've burned him out and she was dead certain to make it the last. With a sudden turn, he threw a glass drinking jar filled with a green substance at her. It connected and she fell as a serpent spirit coiled around her. She gave a mix of wolfish yip and a human scream as it coiled around her, locking her legs. Her prey was so close. So damn close that she could smell his blood, his flesh, his fear.

    With a quick motion, she subdued the serpent and broke it's neck before looking up. The door was shutting and with a quick throw, she lodge the serpents body against it, preventing it from shutting. She quickly grabbed her spear and, with panting breath, race after the mortal.

    She felt the cool night air against her chest as followed the blood trail out of the gas station, her Rage growing to match her body. There were no doors in this area of the city to hide in, no escape. She continued to hungry follow the blood. Her form growing as large as a car as it crashed into the tower's lobby. Had she been paying attention, she would've caught a sweet scent in the air, somewhat...pure but her mind simply caught the closing of the stair door and rush in after it.

    She barged into the stairway, her Rage overcoming her, shifting into her war form. She heard her prey slinging insult at delusions, asking prayers to figures that didn't exist. As she climbed up the stairs, her Rage...subsided. Something in the air took the edge off her nerves. More control. She shook her head briefly. Guru always led to Rage didn't it? Rage was Guru and Guru was Rage. This shouldn't be happening. Why is this...

    The desire to hunt kicked back in as she heard her prey barge out the door. She continued upwards, and busted the door off it's hinges with a massive shove. She spied him a few feet away, writing his name in the tower with his blood.

    Trembling, she drew here spear. She called out Red Wolf's name under her breath as she stood beside him and leveled it at the throat of his prone form. His eyes rapidly blinking, his body torn and ragged. His clothes in not much better shape. His tools crude, and esoteric. His words babbling about fever dreams before slipping into sleep. She gave a series of taps to keep him awake. Her features soften. He so pitiful when he's not such a major pain in the ass.

    Behind her, her pack mates assembled, coming to see the being which until recently, vexed them all greatly. They huddled around the babbling prone figure, giving curious looks and sideways glances. They began to talk among themselves.

    "Is this the mortal which outran us for so long? Doesn't look like much now."

    "How did he know so much about the damn city? He made his damn home everywhere were we couldn't see!"

    "Just throw him off the building or slice his throat. Just get it over with, it's damn cold up here without any pants."

    They hushed up as Sagrim-Ur materialized and eyed the prone figure. The man's head seem to lift for a second. "Sagrim...?"

    The Firstborn moved closer, tail giving slight wags as he proceeds to take in the babbling man's scent. "Quite the chase, little one. Breaking tenets you do not know, Stumbling around into places your kind should have no knowledge from. Quite clever I must admit" Shayne remains in position, frozen as the Firstborn examines her prey.

    He gives another sniff and cocks his head to the side. A puzzled look. "Curious, I smelt a mortal before this hunt began and now I smell a umma." Shanye twists her spear. She's heard the stories. Red Wolf glares at him above his head. "Where have you been little umma? What lands have you just got back from traveling". He gives a quick sniff behind his ears, pausing as he seems to catch something he does not expect. She again hears a whisper. "Pangaea?"

    The Firstborn gives a growl as he raises his head towards the rest of his tribe. The intention clear, they huddle out, trekking down the stairway. Shanye takes the spear off the throat but sits down beside them. The gentle cool night air matching their breathing.

    "Tell me everything umma."

    It was as if a dam of information had been breached. The human started from his early days. Of journalism, told of how he discovered the Shadow. Of his host and spoon keys. Of his mysterious guest and his demented travels. He told of the secret areas between material and Shadow. Of the Azlu nest on Baker street and the nest of rats working their way through Mission District. He told of his narrow escapes and sleepless nights. Of his desire for knowledge of the city. The way it moved, beat, worked, and thrived. He told of his secret doors and somber moments. He told of his attraction to Shanye (of which she blushed) and his network of spirits. He told of which were working to make war on the forsaken and which were friends. Lastly, he spoke of his fever dreams and honey suckled paradise. He told of a watchtower and of names and then, after all that, he told of their tribe.

    The Firstborn looked upon Shayne, paw pressing down upon the babbling figure, asked "What is our bane? Dear umma, what is our creed?"

    The reply was sharp and instant. "Honor your territory in all things."

    Shayne glanced at both, for the first time, for a long time, her mind was quiet.

    At last did the new umma's voice started to wither, started to fall. Sagrim-Ur's jaws went tight off and on through out the night to prod the umma's to wakefulness but even now that seem to be growing ineffective. The voice was barely above a whisper before his story finally ended and went limp.

    The Firstborn looked at the limp umma and took him up as a wolf takes a pup by the scruff. He beckoned Shanye to follow which she did dutifully. They took the secret passageways through the Shadow, a cut here, a trip there. They dodge angry street cars and tyrannical signs here decay and crack spirits there, of business and hope in one door, of birds and trees in another. They took in the city and the city took them in. It was as Helios was starting to rise did they finally reach the movie studio. Shanye looked at him tenderly as the Firstborn dropped him out of the Shadow, on to the material floor. They then scampered out of the way, less the Magi take greater interest.

    They stopped upon a broken half pipe, Helios well risen in the sky. That is when Shayne finally spoke.

    "You planned this all along."

    The Firstborn gave a playful smile. "I did no such thing."

    Shanye cocked an eyebrow at the massive wolf. "Really? The prey that you send me on just so happens to have three years of experience     in the Shadow prior and worked as a journalist before that and thus giving him enormous amounts of incredible use as we are hunting him and you expect me to believe that.

    The Firstborn gave a playful chuckle. "Well, I may have some part to play in it but certainly not him learning about us nor him becoming umma. While you were hunting him, I was hunting as well."

    Shanye gave an annoyed huff. "And what was that?"

    Sagirm-Ur looked out the horizon. "Too much fighting, so many petty power struggles. Everyone turning on each other and their focus towards outer threats lingers and festers. Weakness should not be cuddle, this is true but our enemies are many, our nightmares organized. Does anyone know what the spider bitches were doing before this? Where the rats were keeping their nests? What choirs were throwing their allegiance     for those three bastard mongrel tribes instead of us." He turned toward Shanye. "Even my tribe forgets it's creed at times and must be shown what they pledge to do."

    A long moment passed between them, spirit gulls cooed and cawed.

    "And perhaps some inking where the umma get their damnable powers." He said. "It quite vexing."

    He turned toward Shanye. "Gather the tribe, tell them to hunt. You're targets are many, and the limits of my patience thin. We will route these threats, Farsil Luhal, we will route them all."

    Shanye bowed, and scampered off to the tribe. As she did, she finally understood. Honor your territory in all things. She finally understood.
