                    Thanks for the responses. So still trying to distill the essence (haha) of this, and their ecology. What I gather is:

                    * All spirits can perceive the material world, if dimly.

                    * All spirits greatly prefer to consume essence of their own flavor. Sorta like an environment where you only eat other people of your own racial and social-economic background, and find the idea of vegetables to be abhorrent if, yes, nourishing in some permanently tainting way.

                    * Rank 1-2 spirits are essentially insects in motivations. They eat, they grow, they.. um, "molt" into higher ranks. They greatly prefer to choose the least-risky avenue for essence--a loci if possible, a mote otherwise, and then onward to escalating levels of trouble/danger. This means they can sense danger, and at least understand that everything else could, theoretically, eat them. Their level of social organization is roughly limited to either (warily) ignoring other spirits or intimidating ones of a similar or lesser rank into crapping out a bit of essence in terror (and hoping the stronger one can't catch them after consuming it). Similarly, higher-ranked spirits "squeeze" these guys for a little essence and if in a good mood let them continue to graze.

                    * Rank 3+ spirits are more intelligent, more capable of social organization to make their consumption quest efficient, and more empowered to reach across (assuming they can pass the gauntlet). They're also more noticeable and affect the environment around them more, so are concerned by the idea that bigger spirits will find and consume them. Hence a motivation to farm lesser spirits, escape into the material, or join a stable (i.e. feudal) structure so someone will nominally care they still exist.

                    * No one likes the fuzz... uzz-y werewolves. Spoilsports. Keeping a good spirit down in Twilight and away from tasty, tasty Man.

                    * A self-aware spirit realizes that manifesting is costly, it has to fetter/claim to stabilize itself, and that's a commitment that brings unwanted attention. Particularly bright spirits understand they can just as easily eat themselves out of lunch in the material, but can also easily create the circumstances to make more. Few ponder the consequences on material order that will follow, or the unwanted attention.

What drives a spirit to hunt other than pure desire to molt into higher, less vulnerable forms? They don't naturally reproduce--fissioning off essence for a new spirit-mote.

Maybe Werewolf is really a giant game of Hawaii Fur-O or Hisil Vice and the fact the police exist is enough to make the spiritual grifters and gangster and punks a tiny bit cautious...? But if you're a butterfly mote, "raised" in an environment without Uratha coming around, who's going to tell you it's a bad idea? Maybe that killjoy spirit who hangs out in the field with you? (The side road is famous for discrete teenage affairs....)

                                    I wouldn't say werewolves are Spirit Police, nor that a Werewolf game is Hawaii Fur-O (love the names, by the way). This was a vibe that came across pretty strongly in 1E, but is less prominent in 2E, mostly because it has so clearly defined what the Uratha are- the Uratha are predators, the natural apex predators of Flesh and Shadow. Werewolves are part of the spiritual ecology, and in my opinion they act as a keystone species for the Shadow, much as actual wolves do in the real world.

                                    To quote Wikipedia:
                                    "A classic keystone species is a small predator that prevents a particular herbivorous species from eliminating dominant plant species. Since the prey numbers are low, the keystone predator numbers can be even lower and still be effective. Yet without the predators, the herbivorous prey would explode in numbers, wipe out the dominant plants, and dramatically alter the character of the ecosystem. The exact scenario changes in each example, but the central idea remains that through a chain of interactions, a non-abundant species has an out-sized impact on ecosystem functions."

                                    Let's carry this metaphor through a little bit. So, the Uratha are the small predator, the spirits, collectively, are the herbivorous species, and the entire gurihal (IE. The Flesh/Earth) are the plants- or, to be more specific, the Resonance produced by things in the gurihal are the plants. As nofather mentions above, a Fire Spirit is only after Fire Resonance. This means they would be more than happy to burn everything to the ground in search of more Essence, but other spirits would protest because burning everything means that the Fire Spirit is directly impacting their own food supply. They respond the same way many species would to their food supply being threatened, increased aggression and retaliation against the competitors. Lions going after hyenas, all that good stuff. This would result in a lot of chaos and collateral damage across the Flesh that wouldn't be desirable, because every single spirit would be doing the exact same thing as that example Fire Spirit, and eventually the spirits would come to some sort of ecological equilibrium, but not before the entire world went to pot. Think of what happened in Yellowstone after the wolf culling in a best case scenario, and what's happening in the Everglades with Burmese Pythons in a worst case scenario.

                                    Werewolves, then, acting on their instincts as natural predators, deter spirits from becoming super-persistent invasive populations that obliterate everything in their path, mostly by preying upon the ones that isolate themselves- the ones that leave the relative 'safety' of their herds/broods/courts in a hostile environment for a shot at more easily accessible food in an equally (but differently) hostile environment. Sometimes, much like actual predators, instead of doing the smart thing and going after the weak and the isolated, Werewolves go after the strong and healthy, spirits that think more like people (or gods) than animals or children. The death of such a powerful spirit, temporary or otherwise, opens up massive social and ecological niches that such a powerful spirit may have been holding for centuries, even millennia. I think it's even mentioned in the books that the death of a powerful spirit might even result in a mass awakening of spirits of related Resonance as Essence floods the area.

                                    It's also helpful to think of the Shadow as an environment that recently experienced a mass extinction event. The Sundering only occurred a mere 7,000 years ago, which is the blink of an eye, geologically speaking, and for many spirits this might even count as being within their 'lifetime' or generation. Much like the K-T extinction event, an entire superfamily of spiritual megafauna, the Pangaeans, went extinct, leaving a massive gaping hole in the Shadow's ecology, and spirits are still rushing to fill the gap. Werewolves are kind of like living fossils, the last, adaptable remnant of a much larger biological clade. When the Shadow eventually calms itself back down, which isn't likely to happen for tens of thousands of years, it might end up looking very different. For all we know, it will never calm down, because there are metaphysical and cosmological implications to the Pangaeans and spirits that don't fit into evolutionary or ecological models.
