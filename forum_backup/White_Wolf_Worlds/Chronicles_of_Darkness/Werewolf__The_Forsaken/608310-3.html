            I disagree. The purpose of Gauru is to close in and make the kill after the prey is weakened - you get the job done and regeneration protects you so you're not damaged for the next hunt. (There's good reason for predators ambushing, striking fast, or harrying their prey before moving in for the kill. Struggling or desperate prey can injure the predator, reducing its effectiveness and possibly leading to its death.)

            Werewolf vs werewolf (hunting) is more complicated (assuming things are equal between packs). Your tactic is to do everything you can to wear the prey down and force it to take Gauru first, limiting its options for later, and ensuring you can maintain Gauru after it has exhausted its reserve. You keep pinging at it, knowing that it can withstand the damage but also knowing it's running on a clock.

            Under my original understanding of Basu-Im targets, if the prey runs into Kuruth, you take Gauru and go toe-to-toe for as long as possible, as it tears chunks of you and you hold off Kuruth of your own just that little bit longer, knowing that you'll win the fight when it collapses into Hishu before you and you destroy it. The biggest risk of both of you being in Kuruth is that you'll probably start chewing down chunks of each other for the Aggravated damage, and when one is dead you'll rampage against everything else until your time is up. IMO, this scenario is like a tactical nuclear strike - it will almost certainly cause collateral damage to innocents, but you can at least target it. Exciting, and means the Blood Talons have to work hard to become masters of choosing the right moment, chasing down the most dangerous foes, putting their lives on the line to tip the prey over the edge first, and taking responsibility for the impossible cost/benefit analysis of how many innocent deaths this werewolf is worth.

            Under the official version, Kuruth isn't a nuclear option. It's almost pointless. Nuclear weapons are constructed as deterrents to prevent escalation, but if a country gets desperate enough and pushed far enough, they'll deploy the nuke knowing that they enemy will be dead, but so will untold numbers of innocents, and likely themselves when the enemy counter strikes. In this version, Kuruth is the (near) guarantee you'll walk away, and so will your enemy. There will still be collateral damage, but not to the actual prey. You launch your nuke, they launch their nuke, you come back next week and do it again. In this scenario, the most effective hunting tactic for Blood Talons is to arm all their Wolf-Blooded and humans with silver - don't bother with timing or tactics, just draw them into a fight and hit them with silver. If they go Kuruth, run away while firing silver. There's not much point doing anything else. You can't gain advantage by harrying them or forcing them to a poor choice first. You want the aggravated option first time, every time. No Blood Talon should ever travel without her gang of tooled-up, silver bearing humans.

            Edit:
            Originally posted by Amravanti View Post
            Garou is the war form, it's the form you want to use for war.
            Gauru is the killing form, not the war form. War form is from 1st edition, and 1st ed Gauru was really poor at being used for war. We specifically went out of our way to ensure 2nd ed Gauru had purpose in the hunt, and that purpose is killing.

                    A gang of silver-armed goons is still a good anti-werewolf tactic. Under current Kuruth, it's still one of the only viable tactics, as few Uratha will let themselves be killed by less than aggravated damage without going to Gauru to try and avoid it, and then Kuruth if all seems lost; you might as well hit them with damage they can't instantly heal before they lose themselves to the rage.

                    Under RAW, the highest PU is still likely to win - unless your (non-Uratha) pack somehow manages to channel you off on your own, or the Storyteller gives contrived reasons for the multiple Gauru to split up, you all rage together until the weakest PU falls over, then the others kill him as he's (technically) no longer protected. the protection against this is to try and stay close enough to watch for your packmate to drop, but distant enough that they don't target you. When they drop, attack the remainder so they focus on the threat and not the fresh meat.

                    The Totem's Wrath facet of the Gift of Pack is a good bet when facing a Death Raged Uratha - it gives your side a rampaging Gauru who doesn't recognise another pack's immunity and can happily gulp down werewolf flesh until the enemy is no more or it runs out of Essence (which can take some time while it's chowing down on chunks of werewolf).

                    Edit: I forgot to add, this difficulty is part of what makes Blood Talons awesome, and their sacred prey most dangerous. Harry the prey, hit and run. Push him to get angrier, keep at it until he's worn out and goes Gauru. Keep control, keep hitting, push him to his limit. Then out of desperation he goes Kuruth. You'd better have done your homework as well, and worked out his likely PU.

                    "Sir, he's going Kuruth."
                    "Start the clock. We've got to keep him distracted for 10 minutes, then he's ours. Let's try and keep him contained, no need to destroy everything just yet."
