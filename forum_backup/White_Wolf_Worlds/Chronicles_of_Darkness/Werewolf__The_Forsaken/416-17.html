                            It doesn't need to be one or the other, just more clearly explained how one can turn to the other and back. I can see very isolationist packs in a city at each others throats over territory and more. I can also see them banding together for the short term or long term to deal with larger threats. I mean, look at Lodges; honestly they appear to be what happens when one pack or multiple packs go "Well shit, this is a problem..." for quite a few of them, and get together with others to remedy the situation. Though I hate using the term, it's less one or the other, and simply a more "organic" presentation without there being one singular default assumption.

                            Right now it's a mish-mash because of writing inconsistency; it can be either (or both honestly in a city with some at each others throats and others in larger scale alliances), but the -presentation- in some areas make it seem like it -should- be this or that by one writer's presentation or writing style, then contradicted by another who wants to go the other road. It does have some trouble with consistency in that area, at least in a way that allows for both to be options without a hard elbow to the ribs due to a writing shift and author bias. Then again, it's W:tF; it suffers from early-series-itis even once you get into a few of the supplements, so the focus is muddled. The game shouldn't railroad you on the social structure in a city. Cutting out one or the other isn't going to help much compared to a more internally consistent explanation on how it shifts gears between social dynamics without it being a square peg in a round hole. With the core book you should have clear, concise rules off the get go to do both without rubbing against the grain.

                            The game doesn't need a 'stand' here, it just needs some more focus in the corebook on how this all works, shifts around, Tribal lines get into conflict or hazy, packs expand, shrink, combine and become Lodges, how Tribes may form (yep, you heard me) so you can address these at least in part with the main book without getting the conflicting "pack vs larger structure" impressions many of us have gotten. It's talked about some, but it really could use a cleaner treatment at base given it -is- going to come up consistently in the game. Definitely not an area to shunt off more focus into another supplement instead of hammering it out in the base book to stand on it's own two (four?) feet. Luckily when it comes to areas that just require emphasis, focus or cleaning up, it's fairly easy compared to the areas needing full-tilt overhauls.

                            I'd rather have that area be a toolbox, rather than just a power-drill if that makes sense.

                                Werewolves should be very much a different species mentality-wise, but I do like how they often try to integrate on some level with the surrounding humanity. I like them brushing up against humanity psychologically-speaking, and having opinions on them, and making friends and enemies and allies and coping with it all in their werewolfy-heads. I like how the werewolves, at best, see us the way we see pets.

                                They can grieve and rage when we die, as individuals, but just as the average person doesn't freak out internally when seeing a dead cat by the road like we would if we saw a dead human, the same is true for them. (At least, in my opinion)

                                So don't overpay the wolf so much as to make them incapable of forming attachments to humans. Don't cut that angle out of the game. Werewolf seems to talk about the human-werewolf connection issue book-wise way more than Changeling (books focus on the hedge), vampire (they're dead, Jim) and even Mage (focus is on the rest of their life). I like that. It gives me ideas on how to incorporate humanity into the world with them and how to deal with any relationships that come up. The wolf must cleave with the man, after all.

                                                The issue with allowing access to Numina at a flat cost is there is wildly different utility between Numina. They were designed as a short-hand tool for NPCs, who don't really have to worry about experience costs or power progression, and so you end up with "any supernatural effect you desire" as a dot-less Power + Finesse roll. The variation in Numina literally covers from "-2 to perception rolls to being spotted" to "resurrect someone from the dead, making them mortal in the process". Even at a more moderate range, there's Manipulate Element, Abduct, Blast, Control Animal, Telepathy, Material/Spiritual Vision, Clasp..... All for the same cost?

                                                I'm running a game where I allow Numina to be purchased, and I quickly ran into problems with balance to the point where I started assigning dot values to Numina. Even that wouldn't work under the GMC experience, though, because having a 'experience per dot' value equates to having a 1-5 dot power without the benefit of getting powers at the lower dots.


                                                I understand the argument for Gifts not making sense as the only system of supernatural powers, and I think the Instincts system is a great way for having 'werewolf-y' powers independent from the spirit world. But replacing Gifts with Numina outright has some serious issues with cost and balance.

                                                    Yep, same cost; their mechanics don't -directly- translate over; they'll run a bit differently for the half-fleshed Werewolves and the like. That said, no, it's not having a variable cost. You -only- get them nominally by rank spirit pacts or most commonly hunting down and consuming a spirit wholesale; if you want to hunt down and successfully consume some with the resurrection Numina, more power to you; odds are you deserve what you get without jacking the XP cost higher. They aren't intended to be afterthoughts with what me and another are tinkering with; they're their own plotlines and plot-seeds. I'm still discussing what would be the best way to translate a Numina to a "gift", but some limitations and tweaks will definitely need to be applied; on the flipside, the Essence costs for most of the nastier Numina makes them prohibitive in common use as well; it's a personal treatment, but it's a precious resource with risky actions to acquire; there is no soaking up Essence from Loci in the games myself and the other ST run, and given you're not going to have an easy time just finding spirits in the material realm -all- the time, that means if you want to blow 3-5 Essence or more (likely limited by your Primal Urge)... it's going to add up with its own issues.

                                                    Many of the beefier Numina are -very- costly in Essence; it's not really an internalized self-balancing system, but those 'Wow' types, assuming you can take down the spirit that has it to begin with, assuming you can find one with it, assimilate it's essence and spend the xp to use it, assuming you consistently have the Essence to blow on the thing... I don't have a big issue with the player having it at a flat cost.

                                                    So yeah, the potency is not consistent even if XP costs are, but it's generally a risk vs reward situation regardless; you're not going to just casually be able to snag them like snacks, and if you got them from a pact... it's because the spirit radically altered your essence/resonance and of course favors for it; that has it's own issues. There isn't any easy way that's not very, very risky. Keep in mind the Rank of the spirits that have those beefier types; you don't get it from discorporating the thing and it sure as hell won't give it up easily. Odds are if you did manage a pact with it, you're on the losing end of the bargain or getting more than you bargained for anyhow when trucking spirits of greater equivalent rank than you.

                                                    The equivalent of Materialize (de-materialize to go into the Shadow) and similar may be common and easy to gain off a low ranking spirit but... how often are you going to see things running around with Blast, Abduct, the various resurrection types and stronger manipulations that -aren't- going to pose a pretty big threat? It's not like going down to the spirit grocery market and saying "I want this numina today", tossing down some xp and walking out of the store. Some Numina are likely to simply be incompatible too. It's not a direct translation anymore than Aspects etc. for Shartha are. Numina acquisition is meant to be meaningful; trophies of war, battle scars, life changing events of a sort; you fundamentally change each time you take one. If they're just doled out or acquired through 'down time' actions, then they lose much of their impact.

                                                    That said, it really depends on the XP value using GMC experience; buying an old Gift that does something nuts (mass supernatural powers shutdown from what was it, Alpha Gift Lists, been a while since I peeked that one?) is the precise same xp value in GMC if doing a straight translation to learning a Gift that gives you a minor bonus. It'd be costlier to skip ahead to get the beefier, but I wouldn't say by -that- much. The issue isn't the XP value being flat; it's ST's doling out access to the specific Numina like they were candy. Me? If someone wants to take on a spirit capable of mortal resurrection (assuming you can find one with that to begin with), more power to them if they succeed with their pack, let alone individually. It's going to be something pretty epic.

                                                    It sure won't be something puny that I put them up against, the XP will be an afterthought compared to surviving the encounter and who claims the kill to get the Numina. I'm also going by the idea that the number of Numina one can have is going to be related to one's Primal Urge; we're still crunching out what would be the best cap, but given they're fundamental shifts to ones spiritual nature, they're not going to be in endless supply. Most aren't going to have many of them, or consistently potent ones given the difficulty of acquiring them. Seriously, the thing's probably going to be a Rank 5 spirit in a lot of instances for the "holy shit" numina; some of these conflicts to get these numina are equivalent to the old Milestone gifts in scope of what's necessary to get them. The Xp value is -almost- unimportant at that point in all honesty.

                                                    If it's just "And the player says they want it, it's glossed over with a downtime action and the xp is spent to get one -nasty- Numina" then yeah... -that- I see as a problem, or doling out low ranking spirits with consistently potent numina without them being story specific and part of the ongoing plots (not saying you're doing any of this all by the way Seraph, I'm just using a worst case scenario here that definitely makes it more of a problem). Players have access to what's presented as existing in the setting, and what's around, and they need to -work- to get these things within the context of the story. It may seem all golden if they find a beefy spirit with a juicy numina that they believe they can get via a pact/barter/intimidation, but you may well get a situation worse than Dar-Us's at that point. Their acquisition is meant to drive stories; a variable xp level or keeping it high cost isn't really going to fix something when they don't scale naturally and the story doesn't support their access to begin with.
