    Converting your 1E game to 2E.

    03-23-2015, 10:55 PM
    Any tips? We had been hesitant to convert to The God Machine/2nd Edition system initially. We took the Social Doors system and wanted to do beats but couldn't strike a balance between trait conversion.

    We're really far in the game so we can't just switch over everyone's gifts without major weirdness happening. What we've done is basically hybridized. We're using beats and doors. We've made it so that the tiered gifts in 1st Ed now cost 1 experience per dot level of the gift with no penalty for skipping, and unlocking the Technology gift in 1E counts as having it unlocked re:facets and so on. This was the biggest change we had to make. We haven't yet gone over to the defense and weapon system, because it seems weird and counter intuitive, though I'm starting to warm up to it.

    Anyone run into any other problems or made any houserules to aid in their conversions?

                                  That's a pretty common complaint from what I've seen. It mostly comes from the abstraction of Skills. But your house rule there works pretty well, or you can just not add a Skill to Defense, since you also don't like the higher defenses.

                                  Originally posted by Amravanti View Post
                                  I also don't like the dodge system, because as I understand it, dodging is usually just less effective than using your normal defense (if I'm wrong about that I'd love to better understand that system). That is perhaps the most counter intuitive aspect of the system for me.
                                  Dodging took me a couple of reads to understand ("wait, so you can either use Defense normally or roll double it as a dice pool? Wouldn't that leave you with lower Defense value on average?"). It's important to remember though, defense only reduces your opponent's dice pool. Each success on your dodge roll cancels out one of your opponent's successes on their attack roll. So if your Defense isn't enough to reduce your opponent's dice pool below 3, they're still most likely to get one success, and then they're going do a bunch of damage from their weapon. But if you Dodge, you can keep them from doing any damage if you roll equal or more successes than your attacker. And even if you don't, you'll still at least reduce the damage somewhat, whereas static Defense is all-or-nothing. (This also conveniently addresses the "no grazing blows" problem.)

                                  Originally posted by Amravanti View Post
                                  And then I just don't like how much it slows down battles. Battles are already long enough as is, increasing defense (about double for most of the character in our campaign) just doesn't seem necessary. This along with decreased dice pools due to automatic damage on weapons just doesn't seem like it'll lead to efficient play.
                                  I think this is a matter of the new system requiring different combat tactics than the old system. You can't just roll dice until someone gets enough 8's 9's or 10's to fill up the other guy's health boxes any more. You have to use the environment to gain advantages, you have to focus your party's attacks on a single opponent to reduce their Defense, you have to spend Willpower to increase your attack dice pools, you have to apply Tilts, etc. This system can be just as efficient, but the players have to use different tactics than they may be used to, and the ST has to be willing to hand out situational bonuses and penalties. Of course, not everyone likes that style, so like I said earlier you could just leave out the +Athletics to Defense and combat will likely go much faster.

                                  Originally posted by Amravanti View Post
                                  And then in general, I just think fighting privileges the attacker over the defender. Almost universally, so it made sense before that it was easier to increase offense than defense. So I don't know. It just doesn't track for me.
                                  It does. In the old system and in the new system, a fight between equally matched opponents always favors the attacker, although admittedly somewhat moreso in the old system. It seems like most of your issues revolve around Defense, so I would say just try without adding Athletics to Defense.

                                  Also, have you tried out the Down and Dirty Combat rules and/or the Beaten Down and Surrender rules? These help streamline combat tremendously when the combat is against unimportant mooks or when the goal is something other than carnage.

                                      My game is fairly far along, I've run six stories each between 6 and 10 hours long with about that many left in the chronicle arch. I haven't converted over - still reading and creating my own sheets - but my plan is to go through the list of each stat from attributes to primal urge and convert every extra dot, merit, gift over to experience (2e experience). Then with the new sheet we'll create our characters as if new and my players can spend that experience to advance their characters. I won't however convert Renown to experience, I'll take each dot beyond the initial 3 and let them 'spend' their Renown differently so they can qualify for the Gifts they want. Within reason anyway, I'm not going to let the Blood Talon Rahu who's never been close to gaining Wisdom renown in game suddenly have 4 dots in it.

                                      I'll let them - within reason - go however they want to with their new experience, so long as they stick to the theme of their (old) characters. Hell even if they want to change Tribe because they don't like the idea of hunting people as Iron Masters, no problem. So long as they aren't suddenly RP'ing entirely different people.

                                      In regards to the whole 'never have grazing shots' argument.. the idea of a bullet wound that is just a deep scratch being a 'grazing shot' is a movie & TV myth. The real power of a moderate-caliber bullet isn't so much the bullet itself, it's in the shockwave. In reality a 'grazing shot' is a serious flesh-wound. A real grazing shot is a bullet that missed but left a wound from the shockwave. If you want a more movie-style gun-play in your game there are certainly options available, house rules, etc.

                                                          I literally love all aspects about 2nd edition but the combat changes, so I would really like to change my opinions on this. I'd rather just adopt the whole system, but it just doesn't work for me.

                                                          Your points about the dodge do make sense, but I mean dodge sacrifices an attack action. That feels so much less effective and so rarely worth the choice and it makes choosing to defend such a risky move that rarely breaks you more than even. I don't know.                                                           Aside from the obvious changes with weapon damage, initiative modifiers and that they always deal lethal? I'd say one of the best improvements would be how the fighting style merits were changed to mesh with these new rules. Defensive styles that if you reduce your attacker's successes to 0 while dodging makes you redirect the attack to a nearby opponent (unarmed defense) is a lot better than it was. Also, with higher defense comes higher dodge DP. I've made quite a few characters who reached 14 dices on dodge rolls.

                                                          Some maneuvers also inflict an automatic Tilt. Instead of dodging, you use Strength + Brawl and should you score more successes than your attacker, you do your extra successes as damage and inflict an automatic Arm Wrack (drop item held and suffer off hand penalties on most rools) or Leg Wrack (Half speed & -2 penalty on physical rools in regards to movement).

                                                          In short, I find making defensive PC who know how to fight to be much more useful than in the 1E system. (Plus, I recently played a type of peaceful monk that always preaches peace and diplomacy but would love to humiliate street punks who tried to gang up on him)

                                                          As for high defense players being a problem, I like to use my "sand in the eyes" tactic. Blinded Tilt on both eyes for number of turn equal to successes on a Dexterity + Athletics roll and... POOF! No more Defense.
