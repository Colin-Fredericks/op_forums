    Variations on Renown and Harmony - critique and ideas?

    07-17-2015, 11:07 AM
    A bit of background: Our WoD chronicle is centered on a pack of Changing Breeds. We were drawing heavily from WtA, and I'm deliberately casting the Changing Breeds in this incarnation of the story as being the remnants of wars among those Luna had called to be equals. (Think, War of Rage, etc)

    For all of that, we've adopted the way the Garou / Uratha handle many of the shapeshifter systems - it's just much better supported and developed. All of our Fera are eligible for Gifts, and so on.

    Some of the things that we've been trying to improve on for our particular chronicle are Renown and Harmony.



    The translations among Renowns and Respects was confusing, so we simplified down to just one stat, Renown. Renown measures how far and wide your reputation has spread. That counts for chatter among Spirits, among the People, and so on. It also counts for gaining Gifts, and so on.

    Because people who are renowned are renowned for doing things, we borrowed the idea of a specialization from the rules for skills. All of the previously existing Renowns and Respects are available as specializations, and there is always the possibility of more. Many of them overlap, but that's alright because we're used to that from dealing with skills.

    As an aside at this point, if Changing Breeds ever gets an update to Second Edition levels, I would implore the authors to use common terms between CB and WtF in all possible cases!




    For Harmony, we weren't entirely satisfied by either implementation so far: the higher-is-better from WtF1 or the balanced extremities of WtF2. Both seemed to be missing something. What we came up with was to split Harmony in two: Harmony of Spirit and Harmony of Flesh.

    Each is on a scale of 1 to 5. We're operating on the idea that you lose something if one drops to 1 dot, and lose a lot if one drops to zero; and that you gain something if one goes to four dots and gain again if one goes to five dots. One or zero dots shuts down your ability to do certain shapeshifter things. Four dots grants a specialization of sorts for shapeshifting or Umbral or Spirit activities. Five dots grants a second specialization.

    For all rolls requiring a total Harmony, the two are added together. We're still using First-Edition rules as our basis for the time being, so Rites and Rituals for example would be a combined Harmony roll.

    Harmony of Spirit aids in entering the Umbra and in shapeshifting toward the spirit-animal side of the shapeshifting spectrum. Possible specializations would be bonuses toward controlling Kuruth, stepping sideways into the Umbra, cross-Gauntlet perception into the Umbra, or making Partial Change or a shapechange toward the spirit-animal side Reflexive.

    Harmony of Flesh aids in exiting the Umbra and in shapeshifting toward the human side of the spectrum. Possible specializations would be bonuses toward extending Kuruth, stepping sideways out of the Umbra, cross-Gauntlet perception out of the Umbra, or making a Partial Change or shapechange toward the human side Reflexive.



    This is all in an early test with my group.

    What do you guys think? Obvious holes? Other possibilities that are opened up? Lots of ideas for specializations?

      I doubt that they will use common terms among CB and Forsaken. Or that would be good. Even tough I agree the Fera approach is interesting, the Changing Breeds was designed to a broader approach, and it would be lost with too much of Werewolf rules in it.

      But your take on Harmony is interesting. Can't say I didn't liked 2nd ed Harmony, tough it could have a better Breaking Points list, but your taking is also a valid and fascinating one.

      What I don't agree is about Renown. Its a balance issue. You see, you're talking about the core supernatural set of attributes of the game. In every line, you have a Power Trait and a Supernatural Set of Attributes, lets call it SSA. Vampires have Disciplines, Mages have Arcana, Changelings have Contracts, and so on.

      The SSA have a clear function on the game, the same function for all of them, indeed. First, it defines how and when you have access to your monster "magic", the powers that aren't common of just existing as that kind of creature. Second, they are the Rolling Trait for such powers. All rolls to use this "magic" substitutes Equip bonus for this Rolling Trait. And one of the things to both differentiate characters inside a splat and balance splats is to have a variety in this SSA. So you can't start being good at every magic, and it costs a lot to just trying that.

      You break that core principle if you make all Renown into just one. It seems feesible when you compare to the Renown system of Apocalypse, but in truth is more similar to Requiem Disciplines or Awakening Arcana.

                  Yeah, Breaking Points are the new name for sins. Its to make it more neutral. The core idea is that the things that incur a Breaking Point aren't because you "did wrong", but because they tax your mental integrity, they traumatize you on some level. In WoD Core 2ed, by the way, Morality was changed to Integrity, and a lot of rules change.

                  Is an interesting change, specialy for humans. For Werewolfs I think the change could be better, but is already better than 1ed Harmony (its still Harmony, though).

                  And yes, I know the list isn't exaustive. I still think it could be better.

                  Well, the thing about Renown is the distinction between its description and its mechanical function. If you read the rules on both editions, you'll see there are no rule to deal socialy with spirits using Renown. What passes for that is just the rule for Rank. But them, go read the books about spirits, specialy the "blue" Book of Spirits, and you'll see something very, very important: Rank isn't social status, in any way, among spirits. Its a metter of strength. A spirit knows instinctively if another is of equal, higher or lower Rank, and that says to it the comparative power of the other and that's all.

                  So, Rank is a show of strength. An Uratha with higher Rank isn't by himself more or less than other on anyone's eyes. The books will promptly say that each Pack deals with social organization as they see fit. A Rank 5 werewolf will probably be known ina wide area and respected, but because of the things he did to be Rank 5, not because of the Rank per se. What it WILL do for you is that a Rank 4 spirit instinctively recognize a Rank 4 Uratha as an equal in power. He will be cautious, because the Uratha can defeat him in battle with some effort.

                  That said, on both editions Renown have precisely the same function, even with the gifts themselves changing a lot:

                  1- You need Renown at X level to buy an X level Gift. As you need Discipline in X level to access its X level power, or Arcana X level to access its X level rotes.

                  2- When you use the Gift, you roll Attribute + Hability + Renown, as a Vampire uses Attribute + Hability + Discipline and Mage uses Attribute + Hability + Arcana.

                  3- To raise your mojo, you pays X to raise Renown. It instantly gives you a new Gift. Well, not only it work the same way in Vamp and Mage, go look in the books: they cost the same!

                  Can you see it working? No matter how its described, it does have a clear function: to set your way to do your custom magic, the one that set you apart from other of you own splat. You'll will allways have a set of Names that are Ranked to give you Dice & set your Power Level. You'll fill the rest of the dice pool with Attribute + Hability. Sometimes you'll use Power Trait instead, as Changelings use Wyrd + Contract and Mages use Gnosis + Arcana, but the basic rules are the same.

                  No, nWoD Changing Breeds wheren't meant to interact with spirits that way. It wasn't at any time the intent of them. Instead, we have a function (why they did them, for what) and a reasoning (how they explain it on the setting). The nWoD CB function is for you to have a plethora of options to CREATE unique characters and creatures based on the idea of a born shapechanger. So they can have access to spirit magic, but you could also avoid completely dealing with spirits and make a lineage of sorcerous werecats with Mage-like rotes, or you could avoid both and make a werewolf with feral capabilities and nothing to do whatsoever with any kind of magic. You do what you want to do. The nWoD CB reasoning is that sometimes people are born with a kind of Animal-Soul not really linked to the Shadow, but only to his soul indeed, and this gives him a kind of link with that animal species and the power to shapechange. In that way, the CBs are more feral, actualy, than the Uratha, because they are something of the Material animal world, whyle Uratha are spirit-beings, lnked to spirit-wolfs, not to real wolfs. They are similar, but the difference is relevant.

                  For sure, the Uratha mechanicaly have five Arcana-like measurements of "magical" progress. It already does make it easier for them compared with another splats. By myself, I like to reason them not as fame between spirits or other creatures, but toward Luna itself. Mother Luna see her children and grant them the power of Gifts, for spirits are entitled with the hability to awake this power, but not to perform it. So Luna have to see your value to make the Renown possible as a spiritual mark in your ephemera, even if is a spirit who awake the power of the Gift, or another Uratha whom perform the rite to reveal the mark Luna put upon you.
