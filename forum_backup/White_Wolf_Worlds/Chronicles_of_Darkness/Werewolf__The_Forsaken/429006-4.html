                                Originally posted by Tymeaus Jalynsfein View Post
                                Tragedy is coming out of your rage and finding out that you are lying in the remains of 50 children at the orphanage because that is where you ended up before coming out of your rage. Tragedy is finding out that you ate 4 people because you were lost in the Rage. Tragedy is waking up in your home and finding all your loved ones strewn about the house and yard because they were just not fast enough to get out of your way. Etcetera... Death Rage should not be the culmination of having a good time. It is a ravening feast of violence and bloodshed. It is in NO WAY a Glorious (or Cunning, or Wise, or Pure, or Honorable) thing at all. It is death incarnate stalking among the Sheeple.

                                It is EXACTLY as bad as I think it is... It is a game of Savage Horror after all, not Fuzzy Superheroes.


                                EDIT: Maybe I missed something here... You enter Garou for Combat and then get hit by a trigger, putting you in the Soft Rage, and then get hit by another Trigger (Say Aggravated Damage inflicted by Silver). You Immediately enter Hard Rage, yes? No rolls to control anymore from what I am understanding. And you no longer control your actions (Kuruth). Since you cannot resist Kuruth anymore once triggered, you have far less control than you do in 1st Edition (And even in 1st Edition, once in Kuruth, you no longer have control either). Except NOW, your pack shortly follows you into Kuruth because it is viral. So tell me how it is "not as bad as I think it is" now. Because maybe I did miss something.
                                What I'm getting from this is that you want to play with the Putting It On The Line rules from the storytelling chapter. Not literally, since you don't play 2e, but you do seem to want to tell that kind of story in what ever edition you play. One where you come to your senses after having raged and find that you've lost something or someone important to you, or done something horrible, almost every time.

                                I agree with you, for me that is one of the most interesting parts of werewolf, and a big part of where the horror comes in. But the pack raging together seems to help with that, since now they can't just pin you down and wait until it's over. They either have to keep their distance and just let you go about destroying everything in your path, or risk going into the rage themselves if they get too close.

                                Here's how it works, just in case you did miss anything. If you encounter a death rage trigger in any form other than Gauru, you go into soft rage, no roll to resist. In soft rage you're stuck in either Dalu or Urshul, and have to attack an active opponent every turn as if you were in Gauru. Instead of attacking, you can use your action to try to get a grip on yourself (Resolve + Composure roll). If you fail, you enter hard rage immediately, otherwise you gain a turn of not having to attack something per success rolled, and end the rage if it was an exceptional success or if you have a talisman from the Chain Rage rite.

                                If you encounter a death rage trigger or take Gauru form while in soft rage, you enter hard rage, no roll to resist. You can remain in soft rage for an amount of time based on your Harmony, and at the end of that time if you haven't ended the rage, you go to hard rage.

                                In Gauru form, you skip soft rage and go straight to hard rage if you encounter a trigger. If you stay in the form for more than your (Hishu) Stamina + Primal Urge turns, you automatically shift to Dalu or Urshul and enter soft rage.

                                In hard rage, you're stuck in Gauru, everything except other packmates that are also in hard rage becomes a valid "opponent" for your obligatory attack each turn. Packmates within 10 yards who can see or smell you have to succeed on a Resolve + Composure roll or go straight to Hard Rage. This lasts an amount of time based on your Primal Urge.

                                Normally you play out your death rage, but there's an option in the storytelling chapter where you can keep notecards of people and things that are important to your character, and you give one to your ST as permission to make you lose that person or thing as a result of death rage. You can also, under certain circumstances, give the ST another one of these cards and receive a small Benny. Then when you enter hard rage, you black out, and when you come to, you find that you've destroyed or lost one of the things on the cards that have been given to the ST (maybe one of your cards, maybe one of your packmates').
