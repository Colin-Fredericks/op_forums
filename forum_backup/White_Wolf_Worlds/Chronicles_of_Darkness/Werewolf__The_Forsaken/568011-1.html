        I don't think a good idea to make the numbers that way, because even in those terms there are a lot of possibilities. In a game I had already an Uratha without no combat training slaugthering six armed goons, without gifts or Lunacy (they had luck and passed the check, the bastards). But they where Mister Average Guys, with 2 on every single relevant trait. And that was 1st edition.

        But them you have to remember that most people aren't average. And the guys that seek problems tend to be problems themselfs. Make the sheets of the spirits or humans in a certain way and you can have the perfect killing machine against your players, or just another piece of junk (battle-wise, I mean). A lone sniper with the right tools can take an entire pack without so much problem, and not even a single supernatural power (just Killing Blows).

        As a rule of thumb for challenge, I assume that the combat isn't the challenge. Werewolfs are born killers, when the battle starts, they are winners already. But context can change this, and that is my challenge to my players. The spirit will not use it's powers to win a battle. It will use them to control context. Even powerful spirits will resort on hit & run, because they don't want to get hurt, even if victory is achieved (they also have their own enemies to worry about, after all).

        Hit & Run is allways a good way to make battles more challenging, if you want to really play the final boss encounter. Well played, even lesser spirits can harm the pack. And remember that with Discorporation death is just another Run. Also heavily emphasise on using tricks to separate the pack. Heading into a bunch of enemies is a bad proposition to anyone, even to werewolfs. The challenge can be the pack remaining united to begin with, or else they will fall one by one.

        Waves and opportunistic enemies are other thing. If your players are specially successful in dealing with their enemies, make those enemies always around waiting for an opportunity. You can increase greatly the threat of a battle just by letting them know that they need to keep enough resources to deal with other enemies, or those enemies are bound to leap on their throats very soon.

                                    It's really going to depend on the pack, considering some might be full of rahu with full combat builds and experimental mechanics from the Hurt Locker while others might be more diplomatic or mental characters whose sole combat effectiveness comes from shapechanging.

                                    Even if you accounted for that it's going to depend on the intelligence of the encounter. Did the wolves stumble onto one ratman in a dead end alley? Or did they lure the werewolves to a run-down tenement where beshilu can conceivably come pouring out the walls by the hundreds? Werewolves are predators, they hunt, but azlu are trappers. Odds are if a werewolf sees one it's because the azlu wanted to be seen so that it could lure them into its web.

                                    Looking at the spirits, the Sparkblood Seneschal shouldn't be hard at all to make into an interesting combat encounter, even if it's not necessarily meant to be part of one. The Shadow Wraiths also could work from both angles, following the storytelling hint of having them in groups of three you have them instigate those around the pack with fear, or instill paranoia in the pack about those around them. The cop who's passed by their lair every day for months now pauses, looking in the windows suspiciously. Some ruffians at the corner look increasingly capable of violence and that glint of metal may be silver.

                                        I did some scenarios with a single werewolf fighting a rank 3, 4, and then 5 Spirit. I made him kind of tough, 3 in Brawl, 3 in Strength, and a Stamina plus Primal Urge of 5. He then fought it out with the 3 spirits, one at a time, here were the results.

                                        Rank 3 spirit : The Spirit won, but the werewolf was getting bad rolls. He was able to hit him once and inflict damage. I figure if he was tougher and luckier, he might have won.

                                        Rank 4 spirit: The Spirit won, the werewolf was throwing chance dice and even botched once. The Spirit was able to crush him after he finished his Garou war form.

                                        Rank 5 spirit: The Spirit won, This was the scenario where the spirit might have been able to cause aggravated damage to the werewolf when he was in garou form. He was able to squish him like a bug once the werewolf was forced to change forms.

                                        Generally, the werewolf was able to shift into Garou form at the beginning of the battle and enjoy 5 rounds of power. After that, he shifted into Urshul form to continue the fight. Against a rank 3 spirit, he might have been able to cause more damage, but he was missing many of his hits. The rank 4 and 5 spirits were still chance die and were unable to do anything. Here were some impressions

                                        -I can see why the garou form was changed to have super powered regeneration, without it, the werewolf would have been completely demolished earlier on.

                                        - Assuming the garou form made me think of Pac Man eating a power pellet, he is invincible for about 10 seconds and all the ghosts are afraid of him, but when that time runs out he is very vulnerable. I also had images in my mind of Mario turning into Super Mario for a short time, not sure if that is thematically correct...

                                        - I only had one werewolf so he was at a disadvantage. I am sure a pack would have helped, but sometimes I wonder if the whole pack would need to turn to garou at the same time to bring down some of the bigger spirits...what if they can not in the time allocated?

                                        - I can now see Kuruth as being the big red button, the ultimate temptation. I can see several situations where a werewolf is about to run out of rage and still needs to defeat a big enemy. He is tempted to fly into the death rage because that is the only way he can defeat his enemy. Of course, that is when the beast takes over and tragedy happens. I wonder if this was intentional ("I can win this battle if I give into the rage...but at what cost????")

                                            Your exercise said nothing, first because you didn't seem to have used Gifts, second because you didn't had a Pack, and third because there are too much more factors. There are things you can test in white-rooms, there are things you can't. What you did managed was to show that Rank 3+ Spirits work as they where intended to work.

                                            But lets see... I'll give you the hard numbers you want.

                                            At Rank 1, a spirit is just a minion. It should only be a challenge for Average Joe alone in the darkness. A Rank 2 spirit is roughly equal a starting Major Splat, and a combat focused Uratha should be able to overtake most of those, though a starting one can have a hard time against the tougher spirits of the Rank. A Rank 3 spirit is a major problem, meant for an entire pack of starting Uratha or a high level one to take care. A Rank 4 spirit is really dangerous and no Uratha should think to take it alone, unless in possession of a Bane or Ban. A Rank 5 spirit is a challenge for the most experienced packs, and even them with the Ban or Bane at hand just in case.

                                            Of course, none of that will actually help you. You see, the game works with relatively high lethality, and a lot of circumstantial bonuses. That means that you are plainly ignoring a lot of actually relevant variables. I'm not the one to say that this game doesn't work that way because I think the system is the Holy Grail of role-play. The system doesn't work like that because it makes the variables of combat too unyielding for predictions, first by making a rather large range of values for intrinsic variables (the things in your sheet, whatever they are), and second by making equally large ranges for circumstantial variables. So combat is too context-sensitive for any White-Room prediction to actually work.

                                                I was thinking about how to create a mathematical model of a boss fight between a pack and a spirit which might be exciting, but can allow the PC's to win without them defeating their enemy. I tried to do this mathematically. I see that a spirit with a dice pool of about 20 can inflict about 5 health levels of damage to a werewolf after defense (average of 5). Since a werewolf usually has about 10 to 12 health levels in Urshul form, on average, that would mean about 2 hits. However, since a werewolf can "reset" its health by shifting into Garou form, then it would take about 20 health levels of damage to bring one down.

                                                Against a pack of 5 werewolves, it would mean that over the course of the battle the spirit would need to inflict about 100 levels of damage, or inflict damage for 20 rounds.

                                                The pack would need to inflict about 20 health levels over the same period of time, assuming the spirit has 20 health. The way to average this out is to make the defense of the spirit high enough so that the last werewolf hitting him can roll one dice. Let me explain, if the pack is ganging up on a spirit, each one after the first will be able to reduce the spirit defense by 1, what arrangement will allow the last werewolf to hit him to roll 1 dice while the other pack members role chance dice? The reason for rolling one dice is that it will average one hit ever 3 rounds, with 2L due to claws, to cause an average of 1 health level a round.

                                                I assume that all werewolves will be in Urshul form since that is the popular harrying form. The spirit will have a defense and health high enough to hang on in the fight until it has inflicted enough damage to pummel the pack.

                                                Of course, in this scenario, the werewolves will win because I did not take into account things like gifts, the Garou form, or any other special abilities they may have. However, it would probably extend the battle a bit and make it exciting since the pack will need to think tactically. The good thing about spirits is that they can buff their stats with essence so they can "power up" to get to that sweet spot.

                                                If this seems too easy, maybe the spirit can increase it's defense by 1 more to ensure that the last werewolf rolls a chance die. That would mean that the pack would need to use gifts, the garou form, other abilities, and maybe Kuruth to win the battle.

                                                Anyways, this was just a brainstorm and my attempt to create a mathematical model to equalize a battle between a pack and a powerful spirit.

                                                            Providing they're higher thank rank 1, spirit Defense is the lesser of Power or Finesse unless they have the Stalwart Numina, which lets them use their Resistance. At ranks 2 and higher they have 9-14, 15-25, 26-35, 36-45 Attribute dots to divide among their three Attributes. As far as I've seen in the books they don't usually have an equal divide, but they don't usually have that much of a variance, either, unless one aspect is essential to their Influence. There's a Maximum Attribute limit of 7, 9, 12, and 15 at those ranks. So at the highest end of rank 5, you're going to have, at most but not counting temporary 'buffs,' a spirit with a 15/15/15 spread with a Defense of 15.
