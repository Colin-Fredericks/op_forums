    E2 my thoughts (please share your own)

    03-05-2015, 02:13 PM
    okay bought the book last night, gave it a quick read and chucks of it got a more thorough go between now and then.
    my thoughts? little underwhelmed;

    I'm really against it being a standalone book, there is about 44 pages of basic rules that could have been used for real content. the pure and hosts are practically absent and as always spirits need more fluff. 44 for pages of rules i own in three other books is a waste at best and an intentional means to bulk up the book with out writing anything new at worst.
    About a third of the art is recycled, annoying but whatever all the new art is great.
    it's not all bad by any means the big change ie the gift system is fantastic. How rites and fetishes are presented along with their practical usefulness are also way up there as my favourite part of the book.
    love the new harmony system it's simple, original and make sense.
    Tribes and auspices; the write ups are clearer with less problematic overlap, Signs/Tribes of the Moon fills the word count for content, where this book lacks.
    and again the lifelong problem with books word count is lower then I'd like covering the five forms and shapeshifting, but i do like the new form bonuses.
    seemingly scattered throughout the book (and of course the appendix) is a focus on large packs potentially filled with not just werewolves but wolf-blooded, spirits, animals, and normal both people knowing and unknowing. that really does inflate the idea of werewolves as a culture. which as a concept seems to dip up and down through out all the different books.
    now what I'd jump to comparing it to is Blood and Smoke and the first Werewolf book. And it's no Blood and Smoke by any means but it does manage to stand as a good replacement for E1 werewolf core.

      I only read through the "common core" rules a bit yesterday (rest of the book obviously got more attention), but it has a few differences from Requiem 2E's common rules section, like the details on spirits, i.e. the last 19 of those 44 pages are new.
      I understand your point however, but it's largely an unsolvable problem. If they require a core book, people who want to try out the world of darkness might be a bit put off by the requirement of an extra purchase (people often see things like that as a money grab) and then there would be complaints about the franchise being unfriendly to new players.

      As for the book itself, I never really played werewolf too much outside of two once off sessions, they just showed up as NPCs in Vampire or Mage games I've played/ran. However I am very excited to play this edition, much more than the first edition for the following reasons (note: I have not read the werewolf supplements much, this is 1E core vs 2E core):

      (a) The purpose of the five forms is much clearer to me from the text and the mechanics.They're a good bit beyond the +n stuff of before. I particularly mean the Urshul and Urhan forms here. Before I thought "little wolf", "big wolf", but the roles of "chaser" and "harrier" are much more evocative.
      EDIT: The Gauru form really benefits from the 2E combat, fights being resolved with the Down and Dirty rules really give the impression of an absolute monster.

      (b) The book is brutal. Werewolves aren't spirit police, but violent predators with five bodies and often a strong sense of justice. This is a general second edition improvement, the writing is fresher in the God Machine, Demon and Vampire 2E as well.

      (c) The Idigam are a fantastic bad guy. A clear narrative purpose, terrifying and several possible origins that simultaneously make sense, would be worth exploring and are not mutual exclusive.

      (d) The nineteen pages on spirits I mentioned above actually clarify their world and behaviour for me.

      ​(e) Purely emotional: The cover!

      I'm still digesting the Auspices and Gifts (that's my homework for today )

                  Coming from Blood and Smoke to WtF 2e is a little jarring. I'd like to play it with a group before I have any final thoughts, but there seems to be a few things off in my opinion.

                  First of all, the gifts and facets seem a bit disjointed in my eyes. Unlike in Blood and Smoke, where disciplines follow a clear progression, it seems more like gifts are just a bunch of assorted tricks under one theme, with only a few of them being powerful or useful. They seem interesting at first glance, but from reading a bit deeper into them they just seem mediocre.

                  Second, I feel like that characters are going to be homogenized a bit. This may not be a factor in other groups, but mine seems to be "all combat, all the time, plus humor." I feel like if I do play WtF 2E with my group, we're going to get at least 2-3 Rahu Blood Talons maxing out on brawl and Strength so they can kill whatever comes their way in werewolf form.

                  Third, the power level in general seems to be lower than in Blood and Smoke. It seems like Werewolves get just as many weaknesses as Vampires without the benefit of becoming super strong or super fast or being able to control minds or whatever. Werewolves seem to be reigned in far more, and I'm unsure if my group will go for that.

                  I'm probably on the extreme end of the spectrum when it comes to players/groups interested in WoD, but this sort of thing should be talked about, in my opinion at least.

                                                  I understand what people are talking about in regards to stuff they wanted to see, but for me, WtF was about coming back to the basics and the original characters, the central characters of the line, and bringing them up to snuff, alongside the ideas that were initially presented with them.                                                       I'm honestly... a little disappointed?

                                                      I felt that there was too much room given over to the Idigam, instead of stuff like the Shartha who are will probably end up being used more often. I mean, sure, have them in there (it was going to be called the Idigam Chronicles, after all), but I don't think we needed to have five of them in the core, with each getting 5+ pages. I would have been happy with just three of them (probably dropping the last two) and more pages dedicated to building the different Prey; heck, even just having the Idigam section be "here's how you go from an idea for an Idigam to a fully fleshed out one!"

                                                      The art felt like it was poorly distributed throughout the book; I think the Idigam section should have gotten more art (especially for the False Father, who I have a bit of trouble visualizing), while some of the art in amongst the Gifts felt... out of place?

                                                      I can't complain about the mechanics for Werewolves; however, I feel that the Dread Powers should have either been called something else, or they should have had dot ratings. As it stands, they are a different beast than Hunter Dread Powers, which I think is kinda confusing.

                                                      To bring up a comparison to Blood and Smoke, the only reason that book got away with focusing so much on the Strix was because Vampires have a "simpler" ecology; you've got Humans, Ghouls, Vampires, and Strix. Done. You can afford to give each group their own section, because there are four of them, and one of those is just the default.

                                                      Same for Demon; you've got Humans, Angels, Demons, Stigmatics, and Cryptids. Really, that's more like Angels, Demons, and Cryptids, since Stigmatics are just a really simple template you can slap on a Human.

                                                      For Werewolf, you've gotta cover Werewolves, Spirits, Claimed, Hosts, Humans, Wolfblooded, and Idigam. Information on more common foes (the Pure, Hosts, Claimed...) was cut in deference to the Idigam, where you're going to have maybe two of them in a Chronicle, if you are really stretching things. And the thing is? You can't really use the ones in the book for full effect if your players have also read the book.

                                                      I mean, sure, they're an out-of-context problem, so you have to be ingenious in how you deal with them; however, that's exactly why I think too much wordcount was spent on them. A section that walked you through the process of creating an Idigam would have ultimately been more helpful.
                                                      In summary:

                                                      1. I feel too much wordcount was spent on the Idigam.
                                                      2. Not enough wordcount was spent on Prey other than Spirits.
                                                      3. The art didn't feel like it was well-distributed.
                                                      4. Otherwise, the rules were good.
                                                      5. I can't comment on either the Hunting Grounds or the stories, because that kinda stuff isn't really in my field of interest.

                                                      I have decided, after some thought, that I don't really feel happy on these forums. I might decide to come back to post. Who knows - but right now, I'm gone.

                                                      So good bye, good luck, and have a nice day.
