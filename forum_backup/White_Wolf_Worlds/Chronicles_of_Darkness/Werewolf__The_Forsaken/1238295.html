    [2e Lodge] The Lodge of Odacon

    07-24-2018, 02:25 PM
    The Lodge of Odacon
    Abgal, Akhlut, Quinotaurs

    The adherents of the Lodge of Odacon are brine-drooling nightmares, lupine hunters that shudder and writhe through the water in pursuit of their prey. They’re the zealous devotees of Odacon, Child of Ocean, caught in rapturous worship of the divine sage and the endless hunger of the hadal waters. Quinotaurs take their prey from those who step into Odacon’s tidal domain, a continual defiance against Luna’s dominion over the world’s waves; they prowl coastlines, hunt through the tangled metal bones of offshore rigs, and drag screaming victims to drown in the embrace of frigid lakes’ depths. Yet the Lodge’s cultists follow a dual path, considering themselves as intermediaries between Odacon’s wisdom and their fellow Pure—guidance that other anshega often sorely need. Thus are Quinotaurs posed as mad sages of the sea, water-logged wolves possessed of crazy wisdom that they burble and whisper into the ears of those Pure who would be king or queen over a hunting ground.

    Other werewolves fear the Quinotaurs because they dabble with monstrous powers that even the Pure are wary of. No-one is entirely sure if Mother Ocean, the supposed terrible and ancient hunger of the depths, truly exists—but if there is one evident truth of the world, it is that the seas and the oceans do contain monstrous and unfathomable forces, a relentless and ravenous appetite that can and should terrify a werewolf that possesses even an ounce of wisdom. Quinotaurs make pacts with submerged presences that cannot be witnessed in their totality, endless masses of sodden flesh and twitching tendrils and bioluminescent light; they hurl victims from storm-tossed promontaries into the sea, where the sacrifices are met by shapes that scratch at the eye and hint at a landscape beneath the surface that cannot possibly be; they dredge ancient gold from riverbeds in violation of old agreements, barter with writhing, crimson-slick [REDACTED], and dig far beneath the earth to the inky black stillness of lakes that were locked away from the sun for a reason.

    Most of the Lodge of Odacon are drawn from the Fire-Touched, their burning passion and zeal for the spirit world making them well-suited as converts to the worship of Odacon. A few stem from the Ivory Claws, particularly those of old bloodlines perched at the liminal point where land meets water, revering ancient pacts and hunting for their prey. A surprising number, though, are Predator Kings, their own ravenous hunger resonating with the thread of Ocean’s eternal maw that Odacon claims to bear. These ninna farakh adherents stalk the places where humanity arrogantly seeks to extend its dominance over the wild, untameable seas, and maraud port cities and harbours as bogeymen and mad-eyed priests of the ocean.

    Totem: Odacon
    Odacon is a very powerful totem of the Pure, afforded respect beyond its simple status as the master of the Lodge. It and its siblings hold influential positions in the mad webwork of the totemic courts, serving as a major pillar of those great totems who support the Pure because of the shattering of Pangaea and their subsequent opposition to Luna. Odacon is a spirit god of water and wisdom, one of seven such entities that crawled through the ooze and the muck and the brine in the time before time, and who writhed through the worlds to whisper secrets into the ears of the creatures of Flesh. The great, dreaming sea-nightmares of Odacon’s brood envisaged the land enslaved to the sea, its dry expanse rendered slick and wet with the sea-water of living creatures’ blood in a great civilisation-engine dedicated to producing and sacrificing to the waters. That dream still sometimes flickers through their insane minds.

    Odacon is a vast, towering thing, a seeming amalgamation of fish and man wrought on colossal scale. The squamous horror glistens brilliantly with iridescent scales, twitches its tendrils and tentacles in spasmodic madness, and its expression swiftly flits between furious scowl and serene, passive mask depending on the mood that has taken it in the moment. Sometimes it quails and quivers, though, when something even vaster and hungrier lays its attention upon the totem for a brief moment, and the air ripples with moisture and the crushing metaphysical weight of that enormous presence.

    However, Odacon is usually asleep somewhere in the deep Shadow, caught in turgid dreams. In its slumbering state, the totem reaches out to all of its adherents, watching through their eyes and reaching through their spirits to experience the world. It desires the waters’ depths to remain a mystery to mankind, a source of fear and wonder, a great unknown that they cannot tame, and turns its adherents’ efforts towards that end. More than that, it still seeks the establishment of a great kingdom of blood upon the shores, and so it teaches its cultists to gather wisdom themselves, to become advisors to the Pure as it once whispered lessons into human ears to raise them up from the muck, and thus to forge a new dominion of the anshega that will rise up over humanity and lead them into sacrificial worship of the endless waves.

    Bonds
    Blessing: By spending 1 point of Willpower, a Quinotaur can allow Odacon’s dreaming presence to manifest in the scene. This manifests as rising damp, water dripping from sodden corners, the twisting shadows of tendrils at the edge of vision, vast and glistening eyes blinking for a moment from beyond a window, a gentle and wet touch upon the neck, something boneless coiling round a limb for a second, a burble like running water in the back of the mind, and other ethereal hints at the totem’s presence. Once, during the scene, the Quinotaur may allow another character present, to whom they have given advice or guidance, to achieve an exceptional success on a Mental skill roll or an Expression roll on 3 successes rather than 5. Additionally, at a rate of 1 Essence per turn, the Quinotaur can cause a portion of Odacon’s dreaming, leviathan bulk to directly manifest into the scene; it will attempt to grapple a single target using the Quinotaur’s Intelligence + Occult as its dice pool.
    Aspiration: To create an organized hierarchy or structure larger than a mere pack.
    Ban: Quinotaurs must sleep in water.

    Sacred Hunt
    The Lodge Sacred Hunt allows your character to move at full Speed through the water in pursuit of their prey, ignoring up to their Primal Urge in penalties that might be imposed on them from being in the water, its tides, or its currents, and can hold their breath for ten times the usual duration while on the hunt.

    Secrets of the Sea God
    • A few dare whisper—not in earshot of the Quinotaurs, unless they want a fight—that Odacon is a fraud, a charlatan, a fake. The totem may be a child of Ocean, but it was never the giver of wisdom to mankind. Perhaps it was supposed to be, or perhaps it simply tried to steal the mantle from a greater, older, even more ghastly god of the waters, but some Pure mutter that the whole thing is merely a masquerade by which the totem can gain respect and influence, a fabricated story pretending to anchor it in mythology. They suggest, quietly, that the god who Odacon thinks it has supplanted may not be so dead as the totem believes, and that a reckoning will soon come.
    • A handful of Ivory Claw adherents maintain old lighthouses upon weather-battered shores. As well as letting them indulge their depredations—and sometimes remind humanity of its place by purposefully luring vessels to destruction—these lighthouse-keepers have a specific duty delivered in their dreams. They pulse out a specific code in light at certain times, under certain conditions. To whom are their lights speaking? What things receive those messages, and what do they mean?
    • A Quinotaur who buries a human alive under a beach or shore as a sacrifice to the waters gains perfect awareness of presences upon that shore for an area of up to their Primal urge in miles along, lasting for a month. These presences press into the werewolf’s mind like feet into sand—a rough impression of location and weight and number of limbs, but little more.
    • By anointing themselves in the blood of a human who naturally drowned at sea, a Quinotaur can gain the rote quality to a single dice pool to lure, seduce, or misdirect via song or voice, as long as they remain at least half-submerged in the water while doing so.
    • The servants of Odacon maintain a stony record of their dealings, literally engraving the names of those they have aided, those who owe them favor, and those who have wronged them onto great granite tablets. These tablets stand amid the muck and silt of lake beds and reefs or rise from desolate beaches, forming wet monuments to the Lodge around which adherents dive and frolic. Someone who riles the Lodge will not only face retribution from the particular cultists they clashed with, but from any other Quinotaur who sees their name
    • By swimming until they are exhausted, then invoking the presence of dreaming Odacon, some Quinotaurs report surreal experiences of being washed ashore on alien coasts, under skies that are of neither Flesh nor Shadow. Others say they have reached such strange shores of yellow sand and red skies by worming through suffocatingly tight passages amid dread coral reefs, or being dragged down by sudden whirlpools. In these bizarre realms, the wayward adherents come across dreamlike presences, nightmarish landscapes, and bizarre entities; those who have survived and returned claim to bring back pearls of wisdom, precious prizes of the mind. Adherents who vanish are often believed, by their peers, to have found a path to this dreaming sea and never returned.

    Tools
    The Lodge of Odacon has access to the Lodge Lorehouse and Lodge Sorcery merits, and may choose Influences from Dreams, Water, and Wisdom.

    Monolith of the Ocean (•••••)
    A monolith of the ocean is a strange lump of stone, a menhir of obsidian or basalt wrought into smooth suggestions of features and ill-aspected shapes. Standing at least six feet tall, such a fetish is extremely heavy and cannot easily be moved from one place to another.
    Effect: Characters who successfully meditate in the presence of an active monolith of the ocean gain the Inspired Condition for a single Mental skill of their choosing; a character can only gain this benefit once per story. Additionally, if placed in a Locus and in contact with a body of water of at least Size 5, the Monolith increases the Locus’s dot rating by 1 for as long as it remains, and taints its resonance with Water if it was not already such. A werewolf can use any body of water of at least Size 5 within the Locus’s new rating x 200 yards as a gateway to the Locus’s point by shedding 1 lethal damage’s worth of human blood into the pool, opening it for a number of rounds equal to the werewolf’s Primal Urge.

    - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
