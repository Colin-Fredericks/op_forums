    Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post

    I would just break out the CofD core book and switch from the Combat rules to the Chase rules (chase rounds are 30 seconds to 1 minute by default as a plus), and if those trying to tire the Kuruth werewolf out win the Chase (which they'll easily do in Urhan or Urshul if available, or as a teamwork action, or both) then stop rolling and just play out the rest of the time. If the Death Raging werewolf catches up, then hit combat until there's a sensible narrative point to them trying to turn it into another Chase.

    Ideally this would play out something like the pace of Terminator 2. The T-1000 catches up with the heroes. The T-100 and Sarah shoot at it a lot knowing they can't really stop it, but enough to hamper it until [plot device] gives them enough distance to flee. Sometimes they flee well enough to get away (for now) sometimes not and it catches up with them quickly resulting in more fruitless combat to try the next escape plan.

    Yep. This is pretty much what I had in mind, except I hadn't gone back to the chase rules to determine the interval was, in fact, ~1 minute. I suppose if you're in combat but not grappled, you could simply spend a round dodging (establishing space to turn and run), then announce that you're going to flee per chase rules and let everyone start flat.

    --Khanwulf

        Originally posted by Bunyip View Post
        Okay, system hack off the top of my head...

        Much like Uratha teeth carry the essence of Urfarah for other creatures, werewolf talons are the equaliser for The People. Injuries inflicted by Uratha claws (regardless of form) effectively ‘block’ those health boxes from Gauru’s enhanced regenerating. Rather than marking those boxes with a / or X ,mark them with a C. Uratha in Gauru form regenerate all bashing and lethal damage in their other health boxes every turn, but not boxes marked C. Boxes marked this way don’t otherwise impede the Gauru, but when all boxes are marked C the Uratha falls unconscious. Once unconscious, boxes marked C begin to regenerate as per lethal damage.

        Other Uratha - even those in Kuruth - instinctively recognise their enemy is no threat. Uratha in Death Rage automatically ignore these unconscious foes. Other Uratha feel a strong compulsion to not harm their vulnerable cousins - they suffer a breaking point towards imbalance at a penalty equal to their highest Renown every time they attempt to harm the weakened Uratha.

        I am back from testing this on my game yesterday. I waited because we had a confrontation that is one of the clear-cut examples I was looking at making this rule.

        Situation: PC-pack is challenged by another Forsaken pack for a piece of territory. The Alpha of the PC pack a Rahu choose combat 1 on 1 vs their Rahu.

        Both shifted to Garou and hit each other. So we ran this combat twice, once trying your rule and another with mine to see which one they like better. The PC won both of the combats (wasnt one sided victory, but he pulled it off). And after the game i ask the whole table their thoughts on both of them.

        Yours they though it was fine, if a little off putting as the whole explanation didnt 100% convince 2 of the players. But the one who plays the Rahu though it was perfectly fine.

        My rule they though it was fine too. They like that it connected with the usual rules for human NPC but with a Uratha-update and the Rahu player like it gave his WP a meaning in the fight (he actually "lost" but spend WP to keep fighting and won) and he described a feeling of pushing the limit each turn to keep on fighting.

        Now, i am not saying this to say "lol my rule is better" I was honestly curious as to what they would prefer and why. So thank you for the rule suggestion.

                Originally posted by Malus View Post
                Why would they go into the Killing form to settle a dispute when the Oath forbids Murder? Why wasn't the conflict until first blood was spilled? What were the rules of engagement? Who officiated the challenge in order to prevent potential collateral damage?
                Well that goes besides the point of testing the mechanics and thus i didnt include it. The way challenges are handle between packs in my setting is as thus. There a challenger (the one who makes the challenge) and a challenged (the one who receives the challenge) sorry if it seems to obvious but the terms are more distinct in my country's language.

                So the Challenger (The NPC pack) issues the challenge "I want X from you", the Challenged (PC pack) them can choose the forms of the challenge, in this case they choose combat until one side give up **. Then the challenger, choose numbers (in this case 1 on 1). Then the Challenged choose weapons, in this case noone.

                *Now why the issue of to the death wasn bring up. First remember we were testing both mechanics (mine and those suggested by Bunyip) so both entered the combat with the intention of not killing and mechanically they could, unless they consciously choose to. second a fight to 1st blood is really unsatisfactory just comparing who has the biggest initiative and its decided on the first roll of combat (normally).

                The killing form.....is not really that lethal to Uratha also in Gauru as I mentioned to Heavy Arms when he said that it was a game changer the equivalent of drawing a gun in a knife fight and i disagree, that "gun" is silver. Gaurus hitting each other have to deal as much damage as the whole health track of the other +1 to start dealing aggravated (meaning they have to deal 9 + Hishu stamina) to start being dangerous and its all or nothing. If the attacking Uratha dont reach those reach overflow each turn then the damage means nothing because it completely regenerates.

                As for who officiate the Challenge, if you meant a 3rd party there wasnt any as 1 of the pack (The NPC pack has little to no allies, just for refference they were Shadow of Smoke and Fire from denver). If you mean in which place it happened, it was in a gym owned by one of the PC's kinfolk, it has a underground dojo for that kind of things and its masquerade friendly.
