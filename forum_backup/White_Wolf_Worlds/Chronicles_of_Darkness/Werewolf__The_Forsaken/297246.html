    Werewolf 2E question for Developers, Writers, and Testers

    10-30-2014, 01:48 PM
    I would like to preface this with a thank you for being a part of making my most anticipated World of Darkness book in probably ever. However, I do have a question for all of you which I assure you is not asking for spoilers or anything that may violated a NDA.

    I am simply curious about your thoughts on EXP growth under the 2E system especially when compared to 1E. It is hard to get a good estimate right now being that I don't have 2E source material for Werewolves, and instead must make conjectures based upon things such as Blood & Smoke (or Vampire 2E).

    Currently, the storyteller decides to give out experience based on certain story objectives such as completing a scene, if you learned anything, roleplay etc. On average, you may wind up with 4 exp per game session. With 2E you have the conditions and beats system where 5 beats generate 1 experience and a flat growth cost as you pay the same amount per dot regardless of level.

    Do you find that with the current system, experience growth is reasonable? I have not switched over to the 2E system out of fear that every scene is simply going to be a player saying "I make it a dramatic failure" simply to collect as many beats as possible (since there can be many scenes per game session and wind up with 10-15 beats per game session once conditions are factored in.

    Point of fact is, I have no basis at the moment as to what a fair or average amount of beats/experience per game is considered normal and if I am going to switch over to 2E with the book release, I would love to know a baseline of sorts for what is average, what is low, and what is abuse.

          Originally posted by Dessembrae View Post
          Do you find that with the current system, experience growth is reasonable?
          Yes. Though it is different. For me this is a good thing. The growth curve is much more oriented towards player engagement and activity levels. The more players are doing with their characters, the faster their grow; in a fashion that is more in the player's hands (thus they have the motivation to move it along) rather than me throwing around arbitrary judgements on their successes.

          I have not switched over to the 2E system out of fear that every scene is simply going to be a player saying "I make it a dramatic failure" simply to collect as many beats as possible (since there can be many scenes per game session and wind up with 10-15 beats per game session once conditions are factored in.
          Keep in mind that most Beat sources have limits on how many times they can come up. Is getting 10-15 Beats per session possible? Yes. However that's a lot of scenes. Consider:

          Aspirations: Should be 1 per session, but lets say 2 because it was a very active one.
          Automatic: 1 per session.
          Conditions: 1 per scene.
          Dramatic Failures: 1 per scene.
          ST award: 1 per instance.
          Lots of Damage: 1 per instance (not actually stated as being 1/scene in the GMCU).

          Unless you're running 4+ scenes, with a decent amount of combat, you're not going to see that many Beats in a session.

          Point of fact is, I have no basis at the moment as to what a fair or average amount of beats/experience per game is considered normal and if I am going to switch over to 2E with the book release, I would love to know a baseline of sorts for what is average, what is low, and what is abuse.
          4-6 seems average to me for a session with 2 scenes. 6-8 if 3 scenes. I've never had 4+ scenes in a session.

          But there's no such thing as abuse here. If they earned 15 beats in a Session then they earned them.

                                  It can be tough to get players used to the idea of intentionally screwing their character over for a benefit when they aren't used to it. When I introduced my gang to stuff like Fate or, more radically, Houses of the Blooded, it took a lot of adjustment. The latter was almost easier, since the game is explicitly about having your character ultimately betrayed and defeated in the most dramatic, operatic way possible. Once they got used to it, though, it became second nature. My advice is to, as you appear to be doing, bring it up to remind them it is an option and they'll probably get the idea. It just takes getting used to. And some people never will. I have played with Fate players who are never interested in accepting compels and that's okay. Some people aren't interested in taking on failures that aren't dictated by the dice because it undermines their fun. Different strokes.

                                      Regarding the Dramatic Failure beat, I could see it depending a lot on the Storyteller or the players previous experiences. If you have a harsh Storyteller, taking a dramatic failure can be a death sentence, and many players and Storytellers seem to come from a mindset where the game is players versus gamemaster, rather than a cooperative storytelling experience. The dramatic failures haven't come up every session but they do a fair amount, we tended to average 4 beats a game session.

                                      We played Changeling before, first edition of course, and when the GMC rules came out we decided to just go with the flat experience costs while keeping our experience the same. This led to more high dot purchases (it was played over the course of two years). Honestly, I would have seen it as a problem if we had started doing that earlier, but most of our games haven't lasted that long and been played so often.

                                      We switched to playing mortal and ghoul using GMC when the Reap the Whirlwind quickstart came out, then Blood and Smoke when that came out. I think the only really noticeable change is that you're not going to be waiting through months of sessions to get things like your Humanity from 6 to 7, or Power Stat to anything above 3. I don't see that as a problem necessarily, but I know some might, and if they tend towards long games with frequent sessions you could find yourself dealing with a high power situation when you're not ready for it. This doesn't break the game or anything, it's just something a storyteller should be aware of so they can know what stories might be appropriate for the PCs.
