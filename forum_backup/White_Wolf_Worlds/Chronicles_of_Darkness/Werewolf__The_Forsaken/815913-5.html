                                    I see what you are saying, and I feel the sentiment behind your words. I would very much like to agree with you as well, in all sincerity and without any sarcasm. Unfortunately, while valuing peace is wonderful, it also can be a path to sin, and that sin is sloth. Sloth does not act, it lets others act and takes risks for it for it. Several virtues are fed by actions that could be deemed violent, such as Justice and Fortitude. Fortitude goes by several other names and one of names is Courage. Sin selfishly harms others, risks harm to others, and/or ignores truths; virtues are self-risking for the sake of moral principles or others as defined within 1e World of Darkness. Predators are not wrathful, all violence is not wrath, and sometimes violence can indeed even be virtuous.

                                    Saying all violence is evil is simplistic doesn't reflect the complexities in the reality of the real life or that of the Chronicles of Darkness. It borders on hyperbole meant to short-circuit reason with false compassion. There is nothing compassionate about refusing to defend the weak and helpless from abusers or self-righteously brush that duty towards action to those less afraid of violence.

                                    The deepening of pacivity of western culture seems a trend that is more about the domesticating of obedient helpless workers than civilizing of civic-minded educated critical thinkers.

                                    All that being said, I absolutely love what you said and it has the potential to be a real source of conflict within the game and sparks meaningful examination of how morally questionable each use of violence is. It raises doubts in the justification behind actions and as such makes a great story. It is my hope you like my point of view for the story opportunities it presents if it were held by characters in game, even though you may or may not agree with it.

                                        Originally posted by Darksider View Post
                                        He'd love you to think that justified violence doesn't feed him. That's how he recruits unwitting Bale Hounds.
                                        I don't think that this is strictly speaking correct. I agree with Pale Crusader's points above, but I also wanted to bring up something from one of the other books.

                                        If he were a spirit of violence, then this would be true, spirits aren't fussy and feed off of any manifestation of their concept. That's just how spirits work and there's no moral dynamic to it. However in Inferno it suggests that demonic spirits (which is implied to maybe include the Maeljin) possess both a spiritual concept and a vice-based "key" which unlocks it. Thus you could have a spirit of Fire, whereas you'd have a demon that feeds off of Fires that were set out of Greed. This makes it harder for them to feed on essence, but the added complexity also allows them to think in more complex fashions than a normal spirit could.
