    Balanced combat encounters for a pack in 2nd Ed

    06-25-2015, 11:38 AM
    Seeing that the mechanics have changed for healing and the war form and the possibility of going into Basu-Ilm which can be pretty powerful. What would be considered a good encounter to challenge a pack (assume 5 werewolves, one if each Auspice). Maybe under the following circumstances

    Easy- Should be no problem for the pack, just a warm up

    Normal- Could be a challenge, the pack is really going to sweat and get scars

    Challenging- One of the pack members might die

    Really challenging - the whole pack might die

    What would be a suitable antagonist(s) in each scenario based on playing experience. In addition, showing where the 5 enemies (werewolves, spirits, hosts, humans, and claimed) are used would be used as an example would be great.

                                      Yeah an entire pack of werewolves in gauru is going to be a nasty proposition. Ideally they can't hold it for long, I think my guy caps at like 3 or 4 turns before risking Kuruth. But if he decides to go Kuruth there's ten minutes more.

                                      It's probably okay to have the werewolves stomp some people easily, you don't want a ravenous 10 foot tall monster in something called 'death rage' to hit with pillows strapped to their hands. But you also don't want, 'I turn gauru,' to always be the win response to any threat. So you can either play with numbers, as many spirits will probably have lesser spirits around them, possibly even a higher boss or equal rank partners. Even a single spirit is likely to have hangers-on, feeding off excess Essence. Or you can play canny. Spirits do know about werewolves, running away or otherwise hiding when one turns gauru might be the sensible response, and spirits do have a lot of ways to do that, hiding in zones of influence or changing form and flying away. Worse comes to worse, they can just jump to another world. A single spirit in meatspace who knows they're going to be up against werewolves might level the playing field other ways, limiting the room available for werewolves to maneuver or taking the fight to someplace public.

                                      Either way, since without a bane you just do bashing damage to spirits, and once it is 'destroyed' it really just goes dormant and hides somewhere until it refreshes enough to come back.

                                      I was hoping the Hurt Locker would have more mechanical advice about this. As stated before, balance is impossible. One of my first antagonists had a dicepool about the size of the healthiest PC's health, and in one roll managed to take it all out. So I decided a rule was, 'If a dicepool can wipe out all health boxes, it might do so.' But of course eight isn't even that big of a dicepool, comparatively, and it never panned out that way again and for a while antagonists and thugs would just chip away one health box at a time.

                                      At a certain point, to make reasonable challenges, you'll just need to be aware of the abilities of the player characters, be willing to beef up baddies with passive bonuses and take advantage of the current implications of battle, like if a werewolf goes gauru in the middle of a school an antagonist could lure them into attacking others. As well as the repercussions. Players should exult in gauru but when or if they hit kuruth they should wake up worrying that at some point they may have done something they really wish they didn't. Not that you should punish the player for it but add some gravitas to the choice.

                                                  Here is my guess, based on mathematics, though mathematics might go out the window given the War form and Kuruth. Let us assume that, in most cases, most werewolves will avoid going into Kuruth, if only because when they finish they will collapse into their human form and be easy prey for anyone who wants to get them, or they might start killing their packmates by accident.

                                                  For werewolves, this would probably be the easiest since you are comparing apples with apples, so an approximate ratio of one to one would be about right. However, if you have a larger pack, that pack would have the advantage since one guy could come out of the war form and allow his buddy to take over while everyone in the other pack is coming out of their rage. If you still assume that the Pure outnumber the Forsaken then there is a possibility that the Pure pack will have one to two more packmembers on average. If they start ganging up on any forsaken then that forsaken might die pretty quickly, war form or not. In addition, if the Pure are not afraid to bite and cause aggravated damage than the battle might be done very quickly.

                                                  For Spirits, I reckon that a rank 2 spirit is a fight for a single werewolf, 2 are a challenge, but can be overcome with the warform and gifts, while 3 rank 2 spirits with Blast will eventually be able to incinerate a Forsaken in Basu-Ilm. Again, this is mostly based on math and statistics.

                                                  For Hosts, this might get complex since they probably favour ambushes and pack tactics. In terms of pure combat, an Azlu would probably be a challenge to a pack if he creates a lair full of webs and attacks them one by one, assuming he has super strenght as well as natural weapons and armour. As for the Rat hosts, one should be no problem, a pack of 5 might give a forsaken in war form a challenge if they swarm him or her.

                                                  As for humans, I assume that a group of humans will probably be easy. Having a half dozen with pistols will probably cause a great deal of damage even to one in the war form. Arming them with silver makes things much more lethal. The write up on Detroit wrote about a group of humans that killed 2 Predator Kings so I imagine that a group of humans with guns and baseball bats can kill werewolves if they act fast and team up on one target at a time.

                                                  For claimed, assuming that they have natural weapons and great strength, and armour, than I assume that one can be a challenge for a werewolf, 2 would be a great challenge and require the war form, and 3 might kill one even if they are in the war form. The claimed dogs in the main book seem pretty tough and capable of swarming a werewolf to cause enough aggrivated damage to bring it down.

                                                      Swarming is great because every wrap-around dot of Aggravated damage a werewolf suffers lowers the amount of lethal damage they need to take to convert another level to Aggravated.

                                                      That is to say:

                                                      If a werewolf has 11 Health Levels, you need to do 12 Lethal in a signle turn to get them to 1 Aggravated. But after that, you need to do 11. And as you progress, you need to do less and less per turn damage to do any lasting damage.

                                                          That is to say:

                                                          If a werewolf has 11 Health Levels, you need to do 12 Lethal in a signle turn to get them to 1 Aggravated. But after that, you need to do 11. And as you progress, you need to do less and less per turn damage to do any lasting damage.
                                                          Exactly. Once you get that aggravated damage going then it becomes a downward spiral for the victim. The trick is getting enough damage done to achieve that because it requires tremendous amounts of damage to hurt the war form.

                                                          Another alternative is to find ways to fight around the war form. One problem I had with Werewolf games, and this goes all the way back to Apocalypse, is that the tempting solution to all problems was to turn into the wolf man form and pummel whatever it was that was causing you difficulties. Get a pack pummelling the same target and there was not much out there that could survive. It was a case of having only a hammer in your toolshed and everything starts to look like a nail.

                                                          With the Forsaken rules, one way to circumvent this is to attack in waves. Have one wave of flunkes whose only purpose is to expend the rage of the werewolves. Once they are out of rage, then you can pop them with another group of adversaries that they must fight. Alternatively, put them in a city covered in CCTV cameras and let them know that exposing their forms to humanity will not be tolerated, then throw something at them.
