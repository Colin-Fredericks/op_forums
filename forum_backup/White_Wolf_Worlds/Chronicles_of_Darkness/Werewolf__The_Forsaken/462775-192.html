                            If an Uratha has Influence water 4 or 5, at this level it's stated he can create a new example of its sphere of influence. Does it means he can create water from nothing? How should that work and what should be the entity of the water created? How the roll determine the effect?
                            Yes, at rank 5 I believe they can create from nothing. Generally I'd say things created with influence have rank 0 sleeping spirits at most. And generally the way you gain your influence will tell you what roll you will need.

                            Does influencing water allow to, in example, revert the flow of a river? or to make a river, lake becoming iced?
                            A river is probably a bigger ask than influence 5 alone would influence. Even if your storyteller does allow it, it's not likely to be a permanent change, the water that flows in to replace the water you moved will follow the path of least resistance which will probably be the river's path.

                            ------------------------

                            how do you determine death for a werewolf? I'm aware that having all health boxes filled out with Lethal normally means that each minute one lethal becomes aggravated and once full of aggravated there's the final death but, as we are talking about werewolves with crazy regenerative skills, can they always heal back lethal or are they unconscious?
                            There's a few things going on here. Werewolves in Gauru form do not suffer wound penalties nor go unconscious due to lethal damage. In other forms they take damage as humans do, though it is important to note their regeneration. Also equally important to note is that they do not "bleed out" so long as they can regenerate their right most health box.

                            Can they always turn into Gauru to fully recover from a health bar full of aggravated?
                            Gauru only heals the bar worth of lethal damage, it has no effect on aggravated damage. There are also important restrictions on when they can shift to Gauru, such as not shape shifting to it more than once per scene.

                            I also remember I read about a rite which allows a pack to resurrect a dead mate leaving him with a permanent injury (losing an attribute). Where is this mentioned in the book (I cannot find it back)?
                            I have not read that, I do not believe it exists in Second Edition. You might have read a homebrew rite online or you might have read it in First Edition, I'm not certain.

                                Originally posted by Marcus View Post
                                If an Uratha has Influence water 4 or 5, at this level it's stated he can create a new example of its sphere of influence. Does it means he can create water from nothing?
                                That is the function of the Create and Mass Create Influence effects, yes.

                                How should that work and what should be the entity of the water created? How the roll determine the effect?
                                Gifts that provide access to an Influence indicate the dicepool that Influence uses — the Shadow Masquerade Facet of the Crescent Moon's Gift uses its own dicepool for copied Influences, for example, and every Facet of the Gift of the Elementals that isn't Catastrophe has a dicepool that that Influence uses — while Influences provided by the Wellspring rite or a talen use Presence + Wits. The rules for scale and duration are described in the Influence Effects and Influence Duration charts on pages 186 and 187 of WtF2e Core.
                                Does influencing water allow to, in example, revert the flow of a river? or to make a river, lake becoming iced?
                                Influences are generally bigger-picture/utility powers compared to Numina, and the spiritual ecosystem generally has bigger actors accomplish bigger results by being able to marshal the resources of those beneath them rather than in single bursts of spiritual activity. A spirit with Influence (Water) 3 can make dramatic changes in water around it, but those changes are short-lived without upkeep (anything of a scale that bothers with stats is managing changes that last for hours at most).​

                                Originally posted by Marcus View Post
                                how do you determine death for a werewolf? I'm aware that having all health boxes filled out with Lethal normally means that each minute one lethal becomes aggravated and once full of aggravated there's the final death but, as we are talking about werewolves with crazy regenerative skills, can they always heal back lethal or are they unconscious?
                                A werewolf outside of Gauru form who is not spending Essence to accelerate their regeneration does not regenerate lethal damage fast enough to outpace bleeding out; there's an ambiguously worded line in the section on healing that says "As long as a werewolf can regenerate her rightmost Health box, she won't bleed out," which could be a simple observation that if you manage to heal the point of lethal damage that is filling your Health track then you've stopped meeting the conditions that cause you to be bleeding out, a statement that a werewolf with any Essence in their system that the could use to instantly heal lethal damage doesn't bleed out whether they spend that Essence or not, or a statement that as long as a werewolf has not had their regeneration stopped somehow (e.g. by the Cold Embrace Facet of the Gift of Death) they aren't subject to the rules for bleeding out.

                                Can they always turn into Gauru to fully recover from a health bar full of aggravated?
                                As with demons assuming full demonic form, aggravated damage is damage that doesn't go away quickly from invoking the "someone is almost certainly going to die" state. Even with overall accelerated healing, the fastest recovery for agg is twelve hours by the 2e version of the Purified introduced in Mummy 2e's Book of Lasting Death, with most other sources of passive recovery (including werewolves') taking three or four days per box.

                                                        Originally posted by ShadowcatX View Post
                                                        I accept that is the intent, but the fact that they had to change the wording seems to indicate that the previous wording was insufficient. Not that it really matters, it wasn't for a game, I had just been reading the rules for it and I was like "something is missing".
                                                        "Performing a killing blow is a premeditated attempt to end a human life without the target having a chance to do anything about it" is pretty clearly not "the Killing Blow mechanic is exclusive to the Efficient Killer Merit" even without the knowledge that the mechanic existed in 1e as well.* It's a very simple mechanic that most characters will not have the opportunity to use in a direct fight because it requires an overwhelming advantage and attempted murder tends to have psychospiritual consequences in these games.

                                                        Werewolf 2e Core was one of the 2e corebooks being worked on before the determination that The Idigam Chronicle could actually be called a Second Edition and the layout decision to include the core mechanics in every gameline's core rulebook. The reformatting is pretty definitely at least partly a space-saving matter — there's fewer subsection headers and more bullet-pointed lists in general, the optional rules for Beaten Down and Surrender are consistently put in a sidebar and the starting step of declaring intent is explicitly labeled as such. This isn't "they had to change the wording because the previous wording of 'a killing blow is when you go to kill someone in a way they can't stop' was insufficient," this is "the gamelines that started development after the God-Machine Chronicle Rules Update stopped being the primary format of Revised Storytelling System rules text available were able to have a new iteration of that text that was clearer overall."

                                                        * "A target who is tied up, unconscious or paralyzed not only gets no Defense, he can be felled by a single blow. A roll need not be made for an attacker; the damage points he inflicts equal his dice pool, modified by any armor worn by the target. Damage done is automatic, as is armor's protection. No attack roll is required."
