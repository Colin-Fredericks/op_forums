      Originally posted by lbeaumanior View Post
      Greetings,My questions are a bit disorganized but essentially amount to: Why and how does a Spirit have a Ghost?
      Because Death Wolf is a particularly powerful spirit whose origins as the child of a Pangaean give her the metaphysical anatomy that makes it possible for spiritual trauma such as that brought on by untimely death to create a self-willed double that inhabits the deathly frequencies of reality.

      Do spirits (at least those above Rank 5), have the Fivefold soul of Mummy?
      Spirits are identified as the Ka of the things they are born from in Mummy's view of the Shadow. Notably, Mekhet: Shadows in the Dark contains a primer on ancient Egyptian soul anatomy (pages 12 and 13); as described in the Era itself, a ghost is usually seen as the Ka sundered from the body and denied nourishment; the Ka of a Hollow Mekhet is the hungry ghost that haunts them (in addition to bearing their shadow, echo, and reflection), and that primer identifies the Ka as the Soul of Sustenance (and, less relevantly, the Ba as the Soul of Mobility).

      This further jives with faith, trust, loyalty, confidence, and similar terms resonating with those mummies who declared their Essence as the defining Pillar of their soul, because the Essence that sustains ghosts is described in Geist as coming from "the instinctive expectation for something to be present" while knowing it won't be. The Ka marks the difference between a thing that is living and a thing that is dead.

      Death Wolf, at least, has a fivefold soul as far as it serves to explain that part of the scenario in Hunger in the Black Land — when she was Seeking Wolf and entered the Underworld and died, she seized her Essence and most of her Heart with the remainder serving to give malignant will to her Shadow, which dwells in the Underworld as an equally powerful ghost while she returned to life.

      Can Death Wolf (or any other Firstborn, Rank 6+ spirit) create "copies" of themselves with other template? i.e. If you are a Rank 7 spirit, can you get also get a Rank 7 Ghost of yourself?
      Death Wolf's ghost was explicitly created from the spiritual violence of her dying and coming back to life. As a lesser Ilusah, within the bounds of her thematic Influences she has the capacity to mess with templates and the interactions of relevant phenomena with subjects, as well as creating self-perpetuating phenomena, but "a godlike being accidentally created an equally powerful evil ghost of herself" is a freak accident that her part in the events of the Era itself is mostly concerned with attempting to reverse that process. It's conceivable that e.g. Silver Wolf might incarnate a portion of his nature into a Deviant, but divine politics and the division of the worlds mean most instances of a god duplicating itself with key differences are going to be either strange events like this or the product of long and careful negotiations.

      What happened with this Rnak 7 ghost? Is it part of the Bone Shadows pact?
      Per the Era's "What Is To Come" section:
      The years are not kind to Shedet or Hawara. While Sobekneferu still lives, the conflict between the cults of Sobek and Wepwawet comes to a head — and the children of Crocodile falter. For all their blasphemous might, the spirit magic of the Forsaken and the treachery of the Pure are their undoing. Death Wolf descends upon Hawara, hungry for reunion with her wayward Sheut. In desperation, the crocodile priests attempt one grotesque last gasp of fusion between their rotting god and the divine symbolism of the pharaonic mantle. Sobekneferu does not survive, and with her dies their hope. The werewolves bury Crocodile once more, and the Sobeki scatter.

      The werewolves bury Crocodile once more, and the Sobeki scatter. In the hour of her triumph, Death Wolf is also denied her prize; the god’s mad Sheut slips the grasp of her jaws, glutted on Crocodile’s Essence and stronger than the Firstborn expects it to be. She howls her frustration, and the Shadow shakes.

                  Satchel covered the question on a technical level perfectly. To add my own meandering home brew to an otherwise perfectly logical and better answer: I've been reading the high end / tier 4 material for Chronicles (mostly 1st Ed. but also a bit of VtR and Chronicles Core) and noticed shared motifs between game lines and their inspirations, so let me offer my interpretation.

                  At its core, Chronicles presents a Sundered World. The Essential plane ripped from it's Quintessential components, basically organs of a perfected being / rank 10, yet those pieces still seek to synchronize with their Essence. These Quintessential Pieces would be spirits comparable in power to Wyld, Wyrm, and Weaver of WoD but are never named, stuff like Arcadia, the Underworld, the Shadow, etc. So, when I have a Rank 6+, what I mean is I have a piece of Shadow so quintessentially bound to the operations of the dynamic world that it has anchors beyond the Shadow, that the other planes are so interconnected to this being that destroying it isn't possible unless all of those interconnected sympathies are also destroyed. What's more, given enough time, those connections alone will reconstitute entities at that power level; granted they will return in a form based on the mutual relation between those connections rather than the being in question.

                  Example: Old Slick: he is the shock felt just after the car crash. She is wet blood felt while staring in disbelief at a severed hand. The Horseman of Nature's patient warden who waits with the dying, allowing their mind to rest after being too shocked or narrow in scope to realize the presence in the room. Old Slick is a minor god of the Underworld but is still tethered more to the Astral as it spends most of its time permeating the awareness of mortals rather than in the Underworld. As such, if its avatar is summoned, it behaves in a manner based on how mortals closely related to the viewer perceive this embodiment of Death in their own lives rather than the anything the Underworld or mythologies have to say. While it is possible to travel into the Astral and destroy Slick's presence in the World Soul, unless you cease all mortals from processing "shock" in death once Slick is reduced to no health, but Slick will naturally reform as souls pour into the Upper Mysteries leaving an impression that will manifest into a more Cthonic form of Slick during the scene. Over the course of a session or two it will reform into the exact same entity to be found in the exact same astral realm.

                  So, Death Wolf. I mean, yeah, Pangaean / first born whatever. But I would suggest the reason that definition matters is because it means Death Wolf didn't exist purely of the Shadow (like an antediluvian may bond with the living world and archmages bond with the supernal), she had a matrix of patterns that constituted her essential being. Basically a soul, but like, it takes up real estate in the invisible realms/ parent spirit.

                              Normally, You'd get a god incarnated, the process of claiming the corpse warping it and destroying it beyond recognition, but Death Wolf would be able to bring their Influences to bear much more directly, trading the spiritual devastation of Death Wolf's presence in the Shadow for a more directed but still devastating mini-apocalypse.
