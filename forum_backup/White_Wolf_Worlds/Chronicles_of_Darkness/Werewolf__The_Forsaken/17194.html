    Werewolf One Shot

    11-24-2013, 02:32 PM
    So I'm planning a one-shot adventure for a pack of three players that will introduce them to WtF and see if I can get them hooked. But due to my own relative inexperience with running Werewolf I decided that I should throw out my idea here and see if there is anything that a more trained eye would consider that's not working with WtF or looks like a big plot hole.

    Background

    Idea is that the pack is relatively new, hence starting characters, and has a territory that consists of a neighborhood on the outskirts of our own city and a large piece of the rural area outside of it. For the latest time they've been occupied with something which has kept them fairly busy, I'm thinking that a Hunters in Darkness pack has given them some prods them to see if they can't just take a nice slice out of the PC pack's territory. This would explain why they've been both distracted for their urban territory.

    So after successfully standing up to their neighbors the pack returns to the city and finds an unpleasant surprise. Before they left and during the beginning of their clash with the HiD, a gang war was fought between the two gangs in the neighborhood where Gang 1* was driven almost to complete destruction. But after their defeat a man approached the remnants of Gang 1 and took control over it, since then called the Witch Man. He has used rituals to bind spirits to him and bind them to some of the gang's members, both as Urged and a few fully fledged Claimed. Armed with these new powers and their new leader, Gang 1 returned with a vengeance to take on Gang 2 and drive them back.. They gained plenty of new recruits, much thanks to the use of spirits, and people from Gang 2 wanting to join the winning side of the mysterious Witch Man.

    As Gang 1 is gaining greater momentum the pack must take on this great threat to the balance between the worlds, defeat the sorcerer and free or deal with the spirits that are bound in his service. One of my players complain that I don't give them enough of a challenge when I GM but I hope that this will be a change to that.

    * I've yet to come up with names for the gangs, so suggestions are welcome, although I figure that after the change in leadership Gang 1 will probably change name, symbolism etc. into something Norse themed

    ** While I was tempted I have decided against this man being an Awakened Mage due to me not wanting to force myself into using two rule sets. Rather I'm thinking that he is a kind of hedge wizard using rituals to manipulate and control the spirits although he himself has little enough real knowledge about the spirit world and how spirits actually work. If that would make sense and could work. Thus if left alone he could accidentally manage to do something really, really stupid beyond what he's already doing.

    Scenario

    Consolidating their grip on the area and making some last pushes against Gang 2, Gang 1 is spread out in minor groups although in their "base" their leader surrounds himself with a number of armed goons, which should hopefully make a frontal attack less than likely to succeed and inspire the PCs to some other course of action. Not to mention the spiritual protection that the Witch Man has gained from the gang's own spirit.

    Gangbangers from Gang 1 can be encountered pretty much almost anywhere during night time and they are forcing people to pay protection money, silence witnesses, settling grievances, selling drugs, organizing prostitution and so on to establish their control and gain a profit. The PCs should have plenty of opportunities to find gang members and engage them in battle if they would want to thin the ranks or gain information although they will soon start to walk in larger groups and look for a target when they notice that their people are getting killed or disappear. And both their leader as well as the Claimed will not be opposed to the idea of werewolves being behind it.

    Gang 1 consists of the following, and the name in my native language as well.

    The Witch Man (Häxmannen) - a man appearing in his middle age who've got fed up with being poor and downtrodden and so decided to use his forbidden lore for improving his situation. I'm thinking about letting him using some kind of rune magic in the belief that he summons creatures like the Valkyries, Norse elves or the spirits of slain warriors who guides his men rather than actually knowing for sure what he's actually doing with his rituals

    Blackcloak (Svartrocken) - a Claimed former gangster wearing a large black cloak and ski mask to conceal his changes wrought by a violence spirit and is a kind of brutality PR specialist for the gang

    Tail (Svansen) - a gangster Claimed by a loyalty spirit who acts as the Witch Man's body guard

    ??? - additional Claimed members, although I haven't decided what kind of spirits might have Claimed them, what to call them etc.

    Lots of Urged and un-Ridden members armed with lighter fire arms, knives, baseball bats, crowbars etc. and a few heavier pieces. Probably around 40-50 of them, or possibly even more.
    The gang's spirit, who is delighted with the increase in power that the Witch Man has accomplished for it. No idea for names, powers, bans etc. for it yet

    Gang 2 is almost finished and can probably muster little more than maybe around 5-7 really scared guys who are more interested in getting out of the city than anything else.

      Looks good to me!

      Ideas:

      Gang 1 is controlled not by a host of smaller spirits but by one single larger spirit that's basically urging all of its members at the same time. Something like a collective conscious that's connecting them. Go scary and weird.

      Gang 1 is, by virtue of having destroyed the power base of Gang 2, actually making the city a safer place for everyone. That doesn't mean it's a good idea to let a huge spirit Urge a whole lot of people.

      Gang 1 absolutely tries to recruit the PCs, because hey, they're in power, and they have all the money, cool cars, hot babes, and wouldn't you want to be part of that? Some NPC werewolves in town gladly join Gang 1.

      Blackcloack is Claimed by a traitor spirit - he's supportive of Gang 1 right now, but he'll eventually bite the hand that feeds him. He's the weak link of Gang 1 - it's only a matter of time until he turns traitor and assists the PC. A skilled Ithaeur PC might pick up the clues and figure that out by himself.
      And, of course, Blackcloak will also betray the PCs as well, because it's in his nature to never be loyal.

      The spirit behind Gang 1 used to be the pack totem of the Hunter in Darkness pack.

                      Hm ..

                      crime stories often feature escalation, right? You bring knives, they bring guns, that sort of thing.

                      So if Gang 1 is assisted by a host of spirits, what if maybe Gang 2 will eventually be found by a cult of Fire-Touched, who give them hope, faith and a cause to believe in (and guns, and hate and all the usual Fire-Touched madness). This also makes the entire situation less binary (PCs vs. Gang 1).

                      You can always substitute other antagonists. Desperate time call for desperate measure and imprudent deals, right? Have Gang 2 bring in the Azlu or Beshilu (definitely my favourite Werewolf antagonists!). Turn them into a cult of sin under the command of a Bale Hound. Have one of them be wolfblooded and in league with a rival Forsaken pack.

                      Alternately - the leader of Gang 2 undergoes his First Change as a Rahu. Give the PCs a chance to educate him on the ways of the Forsaken and win allies.

                      Hell, you could even use the First Change hook on gang 1 - a human, non-Claimed member of Gang 1 experiences the First Change. The Häxmannen wants him on his side, of course. It's up to the PCs to show him what being Forsaken is all about and why creatures/things like the Häxmannen are bad.

                                      Just remember the rule of thumb that total party wipeouts are bad...but killing half of them is totally legit

                                      One thing I would heavily suggest in a scenario such as this is don't railroad your players by making only the option you planned for the only option. If the players think of an extremely creative way to resolve the situation or turn it in their favor, improvisation may be necessary, but the players tend to enjoy it more. This is mostly because it was their brainchild and seeing you run along with it gives them faith that you are playing the game for them, not playing the game for you.

                                      It also sets the stage for the future when only a single solution may be the only answer. If they know that you generally run with good ideas, but this time you are not, they won't hold it against you.

                                      For example, in the situation you described above, if the players make contact with the Azlu, they may decide that its gauntlet strengthening abilities are a valuable tool right now and let it go about its business. By doing so, it helps keep the spirits out of the territory so they can concentrate on the werecats. This turn it into a 1v1 fight almost rather than a 1v3.
