                                    Having better fit, show me.
                                    It's rather difficult to meaningfully diagram television static. Everything has ways into everything else.

                                    The Shadow reflects and feeds off of the Essence generated by memory and emotion in the Flesh, distorting along symbolic lines with high levels of unchecked spiritual activity and along perceptual lines in the absence of regular human presence. Some parts of the Shadow reflect the Flesh on a ratio greater than 1:1, resulting in Places-That-Aren't from expanding the metaphysical contents of a location. These places are still in Shadow, but are not to be confused with places reached through supernatural passageways from Shadow to elsewhere. The Gauntlet is more involved than "veil between worlds," but difficult to get a good look at — Dave's said more information will be presented in the 2e corebook, which has been out of the writing stage for several weeks as of the time of this writing.

                                    The Underworld sustains ghosts through Essence generated by the remembrance of the dead, and the context of earthbound ghosts' anchors are usually emotional and act as a grounding mechanism by which they remember who they were; being spiritlike remnants of humans (who have no direct spiritual reflection, remember), ghosts are not exactly sane by spiritual standards (Geist's notes that they taste awful, combined with the fact that even ghosts with only a singular emotional context anchoring their identities can evince humanlike behavior if they're still sapient, suggests that the law of appropriate consumption means spirits aren't going to want to touch the Essence ghosts feed on unless their concept allows for it or something's gone pear-shaped). The Shadow appears to have laws in place that treat the Underworld something like a prison, and portals from Shadow to the Great Below are unlike those that lead between the lands of the living and the dead in that they're always open.

                                    The Lower Depths are not strictly an actual thing, being an umbrella term for any number of unreal worlds that prey on the world(s) of phenomena and consume and/or corrupt the things they lack; some of these beings, like the Maeljin and their servants, find their way into the Shadow and run afoul of werewolves.

                                    Only the worst Wounds become dense enough to warp metaphysical space and permit a Hellmouth to form, much as loci are not innately intersections between worlds so much as they are points of easy passage. Of course, the trouble with this is that, like any locus, Wounds gradually warp the spirits around them to be more in line with their resonance, which by design eventually results in more atrocities being committed around the place until the Wound has festered enough to bring Hell close to the surface.

                                    Resident Lore-Hound
