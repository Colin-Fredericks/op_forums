    [WtF 2E Actual Play] Spare Me Over

    08-29-2019, 01:10 PM
    Hello Internet! I’ve been spending the last month or so running a Wild West-inspired Werewolf the Forsaken 2E game over on discord, and I’ve enjoyed it enough - so why not showcase this brilliant gameline with another AP?

    My group consists of three longtime friends: Viskarenvisla, GriffinGuy21, and my crazy RL Partner: ZeiGal. We’re all old hats to Werewolf, and we wanted to try a game where the characters were a little more established, and explore the creep factor within Werewolf itself. As Night Horrors: Shunned by the Moon just released - it was the perfect storm for a new game.

    A few tidbits:
    • I have liberated ideas from nearly everywhere that’s got Forsaken content. Predators, both Night Horrors, and the settings from Hunting Grounds: the Rockies and the Quickstart adventure are all bookmarked and referenced with mad glee. NateD’s AP “Home is Where the Bones Are” is also a strong inspiration, as well as Visk’s own APs with Mr. Shopping on RPG.net
    • We run this text-based, so the pacing might feel a bit off at some points. That’s what happens when you’ve got three players who like to have detailed conversations w/ NPCs.
    • As is RPG.net tradition, OOC will be in BLUE as homage to BrokenDiamond.
    • Our starting year is 1863, and though I’m trying to keep as true to the technology and culture of that time; the Rule of Cool is always gonna be picked over the Rule of Historical Accuracy.
    Before I begin, allow me to pepper in the Hook and the characters that make up our vengeance-bound Pack

    The Hook:

    The most dangerous lesson for the Uratha is that they might call the bulk of humanity the Herd, but that humans are not sheep.

    To hear the Urdaga speak of the inciting incident, it was the malicious nature of a pack of Predator Kings, but the actual issue has been lost to rumor, time, and death. What is known is that Someone, Somewhere Severely Wronged their Wolf-Blooded Kin. Blooded Kin that knew far too well the weaknesses and strengths of their Uratha cousins and had only one solution for the rabid beasts in their midst: Execution.

    Since that time, those Wolf-Blooded has become more than a simple family. They've become a Compact that roams far and wide throughout the open frontier of young America following the rumors of Werewolves and are serve as the boogeyman of many a Forsaken pack - native, or otherwise.

    Up until recently, you went along with them. Cursed with the Wolf's Blood, you were a willing participant in the slaughter of Beasts Damned by God.

    Then you Changed.

    You got lucky. You fell in with the Forsaken and were able to be given a purpose to fight for. Maybe you even found a pack who you could belong with. Maybe that Pack even convinced you that you weren't a monster doomed to destroy everything around you.

    But you know the truth: you're Cursed.

    There is no happily ever after for creatures like you. Your happiness was temporary - it couldn’t have lasted. You were discovered, and one by one your previous family hunted down those who took you in.

    It’s been a few months since you buried all that was good about your life. You’ve been running on the hunt since then - and the Siskur-Dah is so tiring, but you've finally found the trail of the one responsible for orchestrating the death of those you cared about.

    Your quest for vengeance is at its end.

    Or is it?

    The Pack:

    Abhraham Apostol, a Cahalith of the Blood Talons, is a man broken by his past as an outlaw who took advantage of the turmoil prior to the War of Northern Aggression. He tried to escape that past, fell in with a pack of military-minded Blood Talons, and thought that he could finally repent. He was wrong. His pack was slaughtered, and though he lost himself to the Death Rage in the last moments of the attack - he still woke up alive … and utterly alone. - Played by Viskarenvisla

    Moose    , a Rahu of the Hunters in Darkness, is our only actual descendant of our Werewolf-Hunting Family: the Argents. He abandoned the lucrative silver-mining business and went into the virgin wilds of the western Kentucky Territory to become a fur trader. He played both sides: werewolves and hunters against each other and finally picked when he Changed under the Full Moon.

    He fell in with a motley pack consisting of outcasts and even got married. Of course, his Family didn’t approve of his new life, and one night after a Hunt, the pack was ambushed. Moose remembers losing himself to the Rage, that he woke up with his pack scalped and left for scavengers, and that his wife was nowhere to be found. Moose has the Giant Merit and plays it up constantly. - Played by GriffinGuy

    Zosia    , an Ithaeur of the Bone Shadows, is a free woman of color. She was born in Haiti, and migrated to Louisiana with her mother after some nasty trouble on the island. There, she noticed the same curse that had afflicted her childhood community had followed to their new life and she met some Hunters who knew how to help her find a way to fix it permanently. Of course, when she found herself Changed under a crescent moon, she fled everything she knew to keep that bloodthirst off her family. She found a small pack of Bone Shadows who, like her, were mostly freed people of color and they found a home in the bayous. When the Argents caught up to her, they made sure Death Wolf’s children met their Firstborn in the most painful ways possible. Zosia has been running ever since. - Played by ZeiGal

    Pally    , a grumpy Great Horned Owl is one of two animal companions that Moose has inducted into the new pack. Pally doesn’t really understand the plight of the ground-bound owlets, but he is happy to soar above them.

    Toro, a young and inquisitive ferret is the second animal companion. Quick and dexterous, he’s slowly learning how to bring back the shinies that Moose is training him to fetch.

    Ixtil, a fire-spirit that has been bound into a fetish-tattoo on Zosia's back. Since then, it's become, well, friendly with the Ithaeur.

      Chapter One: Vengeance

      Wisdom is but the beginning of Fear…

      Three shadows move across the tumbled rocks and broken crevices that define the southern edge of the rail line. The wood and iron are new, still smelling of the dust and manpower that arranged this lattice across the land. None of them have spied rust anywhere on the metal which suggests that it had yet to see a true winter.

      Three shadows are panged by hunger and thirst. Exhaustion snaps at their heels and threatens them with it’s cold, uncaring embrace. One slip-up, one misjudgment, and their hunt would be over - their oaths unfulfilled.

      The front range mountain landscape of the new Colorado territory is mostly virgin old-growth forest and high-altitude prairie. The fevered craze of the Pike’s Peak Gold Rush that welcomed in the 1860s is now a useless fantasy. They’ve passed many a small town abandoned to rot in their grave pits, the skeletons of civilization stripped bare of any life that once inhabited them.

      Three shadows follow the rail-line, tracking the coal-fed beast that sheltered the one man who had his name written on every scrap of correspondence they’d uncovered, had his hands in every plot they unburied, and his stench on every trail they’ve ran down.

      Malcolm Willams. His name was well-known to them now, his aura of menace a haunting caul that coated their restless sleep. They would not dream well, nor know the true meaning of rest until he was brought low under their claws.

      For one of them, this Hunt comes with a heavier burden against their soul.

      For Malcolm Williams is Moose’s younger brother. The Rahu remembers Malcolm with a smile always on his face and eagerness burning in his heart. He had been ambitious, sure, but Moose can’t remember Malcolm consumed with greed.

      Of course, that was over thirty years ago, and Moose has Changed in both morals and physicality. Malcolm is little more than a stranger now.

      (Play began with a -1 to most physical-based rolls to showcase the wear-and-tear of constantly hunting. The Siskur-Dah might be sacred, but it is demanding. They start with a Perception roll. Apostal fails, while Moose scores an exceptional with 3. Zosia gets a respectable 2 )

      Apostol is currently in the Dalu, his Union cap and jacket battered by time and battle. He jogs to keep pace with Moose’s loping stride.

      A city-born boy, Apostol is too used to the dust of the cities clogging his nose, and the noise of civilization pressing always - constantly up against his ears to really know how to open his senses to the world around him. Besides, he’s currently busy running through the names of those he knows has wronged him. He’s too inwardly focused. He knows that Luna wears her Eloduth mask, and will judge the end of this hunt as any Goddess would.

      He checked the action of his shiny, cherry-wood red repeater, a beautiful piece of work with a gilded brass mechanism that fired as many bullets in a minute as a whole squadron working in concert.

      "We're gettin' close," Apostol growls in a tobacco-scarred voice. "How you wanna take on the guards?"

      Zosia is far more used to the wet and humid bayous, and the way scents linger in fur and on skin, but she’s adapted. Currently, she smells the acrid burn of coal on the air without the associated heat - and when she tilts her head to listen along the rail-line, she doesn’t feel vibrations on the track: the train has stopped somewhere up ahead.

      She’s in the small, wiry Urhan-shape, and the endurance of that shape assists her to keep stride with Moose even in his Hishu-guise.

      <Train stopped. Not long.> Zosia growls to the others. It’s hard to convey abstract concepts in the wolf-tongue, but she manages.

      Moose realizes they’re still about several minutes out from catching up to their quarry if the train has stopped. He notices that they’re moving into the shadow of a rock-quarry as the mountain around them is scarred by dynamite. It seems like the destination is one of a hundred mining outposts struck up in the Front Ranges after the devilish promise of gold. However, he doesn’t smell horsemeat, or livestock. There’s not even wood-smoke in the air, just the stale coal slowly drifting off. As he peers around, he notices the fog rolling off the heights like a waterfall, obscuring and painting the dark shrub-brush and the skree of the foothills into ink against the grey-washed night.

      “Scope them out first,” Moose answers Apostol before he shifts down into his enormous Urhan-guise. No one could mistake him for anything but a wolf. As he Changes, he sends a trained signal up into the sky for Pally.

      Pally makes his presence known with only the softest rush of wingbeats, a noise that only Moose can actually hear - and then follows it up with a three-toned call. A blot of ink against the starlit sky, they track him more by the absence of light than anything else at the moment.

      "Ya'll don't want that train goin' anywhere I reckon I got a solution for that," Apostol drawls, and reaches into his army jacket, pulling forth a stick of dynamite with a wild look in his eyes.

      Zosia glances up to catch the dynamite in the Apostol's hand, taking a wary sidestep from him. <Patience.>

      (I allow Zosia and Moose to make a Wits+Survival roll here to determine what, if any advantages they’ll get from their scouting endeavor. I also rolled for Pally. Naturally, no one succeeds but the bird.)

      The area of the front range that the three wait in is mostly shrub-brush that has fought to obtain footholds on the craggy rocks, and what little of the forest is cut back to allow the rail a wide berth on both sides.

      They slow down, but keep moving, and realize they’re moving along a subtle incline and turning around a natural bend in the hill. It’s typical for mountain-construction as companies try and avoid destabilizing the mountain and risking rockfall - and spending money on expensive explosives out here.

      It also blocks any sort of visual vantage they might claim, though.

      Pally sweeps back into view a few minutes later and lands on a jut of rock to chirrup away. Neither Zosia or Apostol can understand the bird yet. <<The snake is resting. I see movement throughout the belly. I see two humans walking along the scales holding their long talons. There is a cave and I see two more humans carrying nests into it.>>

      "What's your critter got?" Apostol is impatient. Typical for his tribe, and it’s just been his nature since he was a kid.

      Moose translates the owl's understanding of the situation, <Train's stopped. Activity inside. Two gunmen outside. Two carrying something into the cave.> He wasn't sure what nests would refer to. Logs? Baskets? He wasn't sure. <Targets in the train, outside train. Need to... hmm... Need to get closer. Need to get one of us onto train. Make sure it doesn't leave...>

      "Then we oughta hit hard and fast. Strike 'em before they have a chance to react and get outta here - we should get set up, I can take a pot-shot at one of the meat bags what's armed," Apostol whispers, adjusting his army-cap and checking the iron-sights of his repeater. "One of us ought to take out the driver...then move in, mop 'em up before they can pull the iduth-fil if we can."

      Zosia starts to speak when Apostol does first and then stops to consider his addition. She frowns, as much as a wolf could, and chuffs a bit at the grass beneath her. <That is up to you two. I will check the cave when you distract them.>

      <You take the gunmen, I get onto the train and turn it into an abattoir, Zo takes the cave.> Moose growls low, codifying his plan as he sneaks into position.

      "[We'll paint the town red,]" Apostol threatens and takes his Urhan-guise, handsome with long, silver-brushed fur. "[Zo you be careful, I know you ain't a soldierin' type like us. If it's too hot you lemme know, this guy can take care of hisself.]"

      (Dex/Wits + Stealth as the pack moves into position. Apostol spends a WP, and everyone scores 2 success.)

      The pack comes across the pitiful reminder of greed-run-rampant. They maneuver into position in the remains of a small mining outpost, left to rot away in the damp and the dust. There's a scattering of buildings, five or six at most. Two larger ones, most likely the worker barracks and the office where the foreman would have sat and brooded on his earnings.

      There are others, one-room rotten-wood squares that give no hint as to what they were used for. This is a dead place, so why would Malcolm come here?

      Apostol and Moose spy two men walking the length of the train with guns braced in their hands. They're on guard, their eyes scanning ... inward towards the outpost and the mine’s opening.

      <There's something else they're afraid of, better watch out for it while we do this> Moose says, looking at the gunmen on the train before he starts to look for a way inside.

      "[Fear is the mind-killer big guy...we'll scare 'em better than any cave boogeyman,]" Apostol replies.

      Moose moves along the train. He counts off six cars, one of which is filled with coal, two of which are typical passenger cars, two closed cars without windows - and a back car that has the caboose door open and revealed shelves of what smells like salted meat and stale produce. Supplies, most likely. The closed cars would require breaking into them, but the others look easy enough to slip in without too much notice.

      Apostol breaks off from Moose and moves with Zosia into the abandoned outpost to find a relatively put-together wood shack that gives him a decent vantage on the opening of the mine shaft and the train, though it blocks Moose from his sight as the Rahu favors the train-side the guards aren't currently scanning.

      (As the pack prepares and gets into position, I throw an Environmental Tilt at them: Fog Rolls In As the fog creeps in off the mountains, sight becomes a problem. -2 to Perception and Rolls Requiring Ranged Accuracy. +2 to Stealth-based rolls. )

      WHHHHHIRRR

      The heavy side-door along the innermost closed-cart moves with a shuddering groan, eeking open and revealing a dark, cramped space within. As it opens, the scent of unwashed flesh pours out and hits Moose, first. He can smell the people within it, the stink of fecal matter and aged urine, the cloying fear, the desperation that pours like molasses into the air.

      Creeping along, Moose looks to the passenger cars, deciding that would be his point of entry. When the stink comes out, he decides that now is the time. Despite his lupine body, he would begin with this form, his smallest form, and from there he would grow.

      "KEEP TOGETHER," A third gunman yells, this one hopping out from the closed car. If the stink of the dark space bothered him, it doesn't show as he lands on the graveled ground and starts to shove out a herd of people.

      There's no one color of destitute that sticks out as the mass is brought forward. Apostol spies cruel slave brands on some of the black folk, from his time running the Plantation heists. He recognizes a smattering of Chinese thanks to his time in the U.S Army drafting them into building the rails on the Kansas territories. There’s also white folk too, bodies dirty and cheeks gaunt from lack of food.

      The two guards up atop the train snap into attention, their sights aimed at the milling crowd gathering below them.

      "[God of the Apocalypse, bringer of Fire and Chaos, to know you is to live and to serve you is to sacrifice our enemies through annihilation. Through your Howl, which will Destroy the Universe, I cry vengeance. Help me to overcome Kuruth and deceit, to establish your law of death and justice. Grant this through the blood I spill for you...]"

      Apostol is still incredibly close to his human origins, and much of his view on his new life is filtered through his Protestant faith. As penance for his misguided youth with the Argents, Apostol also has a hard-line on slavery of any sort.

      He lines up a shot, aiming at the head of the closest guard patrolling the top of the train.

      Zosia barely manages to scramble underneath a broken awning of wood half-lost to the ground before she’s discovered by three men idling around the opening of the two-horse wide gap in the mountain, bordered by newly-reinforced wooden beams: a mine-shaft. A rotted cart-line feeds into it, but they're not using it. Instead, they're carrying sealed boxes out.

      As she catches her breath, she sends her sight across the Gauntlet to see how the memory of this place has affected the Hisil. She senses the heat of her fiery companion, the spirit quiet but active in the tattoo that stretches across the span of her back. The Gauntlet itself is scarred heavy and thick here, unusual for a desolate place out in the wilds. Why is the gauntlet knotted and corded here like you were in the middle of a bustling east-coast town?

      Then her sight is across. She doesn’t see the rail line. She doesn’t see the buildings, even their graves. There is only the mountain with a gaping wound in its side, the darkness like a maw into the abyss itself. There is little activity here, a lattice of shimmering rope stretched across the cave to give off the impression of teeth.

      She is confused, what the hell is happening out here? She’ll figure it out later after they’ve taken down Malcolm. She stays low, growling impatiently.

      Where was the diversion?

      Moose eases into the passenger car and takes note of the private section. He smells Malcolm here, his sweat tinged with the bitter-sweet of alcohol.

      He’s risking losing himself to the Rage, and channels that into a Killer Instinct. This doesn’t need to be strung-out. He just wants Malcolm dead. He bulks into the Urshul and is now roughly the size of a Grizzly. It’s become hard to move in the tight quarters, but he wants the extra weight on his side.

      He waits for the signal.

      -CRACK-

      A bullet whizzes out of the darkness, and the muzzle flares as the familiar retort of a Henry roars through the night. The bullet whizzes forth from the explosion...and sails just beyond Apostol’s target, as the guard turned sharp at the sudden noise.

      "Shit..." Apostol curses, and lines up another shot.

      The crowd bolts. Like spooked sheep, the unwashed masses slam into each other in their panic to seek shelter. Some jump back into the prison they were just fed from, others crowd to the passenger cars, to the buildings, scrambling for cover. Some dive under the train itself.

      The guards react, guns snapping up and eyes scanning for where the shot came from. You're lucky in that the rocks reverberate the noise and hide the direct impact, but you can't rely on that too long.

      (Even with 11 dice and 9-again, Apostol rolls an impressive… 0. Still, our Cahalith activates Pack Triumphs Together and brings Moose up to his Initiative.)

      With the gunfire, Moose takes that as his chance to tear down the door. His claws rake through nearly all of the door,, leaving only splinters to shoulder through - nothing that would slow down his approach. Inside, he sees two people, Malcom who stares at the beast at his door with wide-eyes that shimmer between fear and hatred; and another man, this one black and sitting like he was crunched into his position for far too long. There’s no fear in that second man’s gaze. Just surprise. And hate.

      Mostly hate.

      As much as the enormous wolf can grin, Moose does, as he rushes forward, jaws outstretched to swallow Malcolm whole, snapping down like a steel trap upon his brother's flesh.

      There isn't much room for Malcom to go. He struggles to get his revolver out from the hostler even as he moves to try and escape Moose’s jaws. In that tiny room, there’s no escaping Moose’s jaws.

      In the end, it happens without fanfare.

      Yes, there are screams. Yes, Moose is aware of the stumbling, blind panic of the herd as they race into the car behind him. Yes, he notices something crawling underneath the skin of the dark-skinned man as he pulls his own gun out to take his own shot at Moose’s head.

      Moose hears the explosion of the barrel near his cheeks and barely jerks his head away just in time to avoid having it blown off seconds after achieving vengeance.

      But does that matter? Malcolm is crushed beneath Moose’s jaws, his throat destroyed, his blood trickling down into Moose’s gullet.

      (Moose scores 9L on his attack on Malcolm and thus the brother-killer is brought down. And even with a -3 to Moose’s defense due to size and space, the second man’s revolver-fire gets a zilch.)

      Apostol grins ferally as he takes aim a second time. This time, he strikes a gut-shot on one of the train-guards. His satisfaction is short-lived. He needs to move, as they’ve pinpointed where he’s shooting from.

      As he moves back into cover, wooden shrapnel scratches at his cheeks as a hole is blown through the rotted wood where he just braced against. A second shot sounds off, and this one slams through the wooden barrier and punches hard into his shoulder - tearing muscle. He’ll have a hard time bracing the rifle against it.

      (Apostol scores a 6L on his shot, but payback is a bitch. Even with half-cover and the defense of the Dalu, he takes 5L in return! Apostol activates Berserker’s Might to bring that down to 3L)

      Moose realizes that there’s something twitching underneath the dark-skinned man’s flesh, but he doesn’t quite bring himself to care. He’ll worry about it after the man is dead. He spits out his brother’s spasming corpse and turns that deadly maw onto the second man. He expects it to end roughly the same.

      Moose’s jaws clamp down around the dark-skinned guests' shoulders and he rips and tears, pulling back and expecting to take muscle, sinew, and bone with him. Instead, he feels his teeth crunch around what feels like living rock.

      The dark-skinned man grins. "I appreciate you loosening the vessel for me, though I did like this one." He shudders, and from that wound, Moose watches as one, two, three elongated, sectioned arms covered with fine hairs and a chitinous armor pull out. At the edge of the upper arms, the hands are tipped with long, razor talons best suited for tearing and weaving thick webs... or slicing through the heavy fur of an Uratha. Those blades swing down through the closed space awkwardly but still cut deep, sliding through Moose’s muscle. They stop only when they bounce against bone.

      (3L on Moose)

      Ouch. OUCH.

      Apostol hisses in pain as the round breaks through his shoulder - there's a flare of silver light as a portion of his Renown comes to life, deflecting the round's velocity somewhat and preventing more catastrophic damage. It's nothing he can't handle, but it hurts like hell. The Dalu-shape is fast as he crouches in an army bull-rush after vaulting out the window, breathing heavy and fast, eyes flashing like a pair of torches in the dark as he cranks the handle of his Henry and dashes through another doorway, throwing his back up against a wall and then peeking out.

      "[Fenrir...Hear me roar.]" He squeezes the trigger, watching as another lance of hot lead goes streaking through the flesh of the Argent closing on them. He grins, fangs ivory and sharp in the dark.

      Apostol aims, fires, and then rushes towards another section of broken wood as his shot punches into the gut of the nearest guard at ground level. He hears a grunt that morphs into a chittering cry that pierces through the night. Risking a glance back, he discovers the man peeling at the bullet wound, green-black ichor dripping around his fingers as he widens it, forcing himself through.

      Then he ducks, wood shattering around his head once again as he crouches behind cover, Another bullet slams into him, ripping open a hot graze on your side, red and raw against your ribs.

      (3L on Apostol)

      SKREEEEEE goes the alarm. URATHA!

      He manages to catch a glimpse of the three guards from the mouth of the mine as they disappear into the fog that's now thick and heavy over the battlefield, and his sensitive ears pick out the unloading of cartridges, the plink of bullets hitting the ground. They're reloading.

      Shit.

      “<AZLU!>” Moose roars, too late as he recognizes the oddity of the Gauntlet, how it’s too heavy. He goes for another bite, gambling on his size and power to quickly end the encounter before the Azlu can break fully out of its shell.

      He realizes, perhaps, that he relies too much on simple size in solving a lot of the logistics of a fight. But, he rationalizes, it's hard to mess up just crunching someone's head in one go.

      He manages to rip away one of the arms, the monstrous spider squealing in pain as his teeth snap cleanly through one of its limbs. A spray of green-black ichor comes out and Moose dodges before he realizes he’s doing it. That ichor sizzles against the wall - eating away at the wood.

      "NYRAAAGH, I will eat .... <and tear ... and make your bones into a trophy fitting a king>" The dark-skinned man once had a pleasantly gravel-voice, low-octave and filled with presence, but it is lost to the scratching-squealing-clicking of the Azlu as it fades from English to the First Tongue.

      The man is gone now, replaced by a horrible spider that has no real-world match. The thing is easily as big as a grizzly, easily as big as Moose himself and growing as it sheds the last of the humanity that hid it. Six pairs of eyes stare hatefully at the Rahu as seven pairs of legs come to shake themselves free of their flesh-cage.

      Moose notices two more pairings of those dangerous blades, and as the spider rears up to strike, he sees the shimmer of ichor on one of them. SLICE they go, one after the other.

      He manages to dodge one pair of blades, but the second cuts shallow... but that's all the Azlu needs. His skin burns and his mind wavers as the pain lances up his spine.

      (3A on Moose).

      "THEY'RE BREAKIN' OUT THE IDUTH-FIL!" Apostol roars, guessing at the meaning of a reload before the guards had even taken a single-shot. If they’re bringing out silver, then the pack needs to fall back. Regroup. Reorganize. This went South - quickly.

      He threw his head back and let out a harsh, powerful roar, his distinctive, scarred voice ringing out into the night, a call in the tongue of wolves to fall back.

      Apostol manages to score a nasty shot - his bullet blowing a hole in the head of one of the three as the man fumbles to press the cartridges into the barrel. The man’s form falls to the side, fingers twitching still around the bullet he holds.

      Behind him, the Azlu is free now and is now a spider the size of a pony. The thing is fat and bloated and skitters through the wooden debris like it was made for such a feat. It's fast, too, tearing up the ground and slicing at him with limbs that reach too damned far.

      Then come the shots. One after the other after the other. They're unleashing the full might on him. Where the fuck is Zosia? Where the fuck is Moose?

      Apostol hits the ground hard as several of those too-long limbs reach out and slice at his legs. There goes a tendon. There goes a ligament. His body struggles to heal against the damage but Azlu know how to hunt his kind too well.

      (Apostol gets another 5L on him! He activates Berserker’s Might to throw off the Stunned Tilt and manages to save himself from wrapping into Aggravated. But he’s also burning through essence.)

      The only saving grace is that the bullets whizz through the air above his head, allowing him to avoid the risk of silver striking into his heart.

      The crowd flees now, most of those who wanted to run already gone, while others still cower in place and wait for the monsters to leave. Screams and sobs fill the air. Fresh urine strikes Apostol’s nose as quickly as ichor and the scent of blood does.

      Moose yowls in pain, the blades sinking into him. Still, while Malcolm's death was his original goal, this was his duty. This was the prey he'd been tasked with fighting. And the wolf must hunt.

      Deciding to use both of their unique sizes against his foe, Moose sends forth his snapping jaws as he pushes the spider against the wall, with the intent of crushing it against the train care walls, or else breaking it out of the train entirely.           "More glad I killed that no-good lower-n-a-snake-belly brother a mine," Moose says, handing his packmate a tin of salted meat.

          Apostol takes the tin with silent gratitude, tipping his head as he peeled it open and ate with typical army speed, eyeing Zosia. "...what do you reckon she's up to? Sure is weird."

          "I've had some of her tribe in my pack. Talk to ghosts and spirits more than people, but that's ten kinds o' useful, tell you what." Moose says.

          Zosia tracks through the bodies that are strewn all about and quickly picks out the body with a broken neck and cracked back. Slight heat radiate as Zosia burns with essence again and the glow under her shirt returns. She holds out a hand toward the bodies and with a wave they catch fire. "Rest mama. There is no need to linger." She then returns to the men and nods.

          The bodies come alight and charred flesh hits their nostrils. The shimmering specter underneath the train disappears as her body becomes ash.

          The three shadows stand in the middle of a desolate gravesite - no longer even a memorial to the Company that once stood here, trying to eek out fortune from the rocks.

          The train - well, they could let it rest. Let it rust and decay. It would be simple enough to destroy the track to slow down the discovery of the site by the curious.

          The night is long in the tooth now, the moon dipping low behind the sprawl of the mountains around you. Malcom is dead, and truthfully, all of them have honored the Hunt. Their quarry is no longer a tangible threat.

          However, he has a legacy, and what little they’ve gleaned from the ashes suggests that it's worrisome - and could easily continue without his oversight. But these are questions for later.

          Apostol and Moose both feel the subtle tremors of exhaustion. The Killing-Wolf demands much from one’s body and they’re already down on little reserves.

          Zosia had not taken the form of Death, but even she feels the end of the Hunt as a weight taken off her shoulders - a hole that fatigue easily fills in.

          There's only one question remaining as the three of them stand in the swirling fog:

          What are they going to do with their lives now?

                  New Kid(s) In Town: 2.1

                  (With the prologue out of the way and our intrepid trio beginning to get a grip on their characters, we start a few weeks later, on the tail end of the travel north to the town named in Malcolm’s letters)

                  The wind cuts low and easterly across the grasslands as the pack travels along the section of the Cherokee Trail that cuts north along the Front Range mountains. The weather’s been pretty consistently dreary for a few days now, with distant clouds threatening to break rain sooner or later over the high prairie.

                  The pack takes their time traveling. Apostol does quite a few meet and greets, selling off the excess Henry Rifles and scoring himself a solid chunk of change that will assist them whenever they decide to settle down.

                  He also gets them access to the occasional rest-stop thanks to his connections with the U.S Military out here. Between him and Moose, the pack is never short a helping hand along the way. The further north-east they go, they start to find more and more of the little clusters of humanity along the high, craggy grassland. Here, a trading post, there a military fort, and then the parceled, fence-divided acreages suggestive of ranching. Sure enough, the past three days have been littered with sightings of cattle, and picking out the lonely ranch-estates in the distance.

                  The pack also talks a bit about the Azlu, and the concern that they stumbled over something that wasn’t just an isolated incident. The whole thing stunk of an operation that traveled a little further than any of them were happy with - and their only lead was the town they were approaching.

                  Welcome to Colorado City. It’s a young town, just about near its fifth birthday and it’s already developing an interesting reputation along with the network of trails and wagon-paths that surround it. Right now, it’s barely got a hundred people who consistently live there, but the population fluxes thanks to the multitude of mines that have been struck in the area thanks to the lingering dream of the 1858 Gold Rush.

                  It’s also in a bit of competition with Denver. Colorado City was establishing itself as a town where the oppressive rules and regulations of the encroaching U.S idea of ‘civilization’ weren’t welcomed - which brought in a certain type of entrepreneur. Actual pioneers have died out - preferring the safer, regulated passes closer to Denver.

                  Right now though, the pack just wants to take advantage of the liquor, beds, and food that Colorado City promises to give them.

                  (I’m playing a little fast and loose with the area, and borrowing a lot from Hunting Ground: Rockies here. So, some of the history of the city and the politics that get involved might come a little earlier than expected for anyone who’s a western buff - but that’s ok)

                  As the pack arrives on the main avenue, Moose seems to be in a chatty, reminiscing mood.

                  "Been too long since I'd made a trip inta town. Most times if we needed somethin, someone went ta trade. We were a big ol' pack though, over twenty a us, and we had folks what made most a' the stuff we needed. Even had McFlaherty, a Scots-Irish who made his own whiskey, and better'n most sold in town,"

                  Apostol isn’t as chatty as Moose. He’s the pack’s Cahalith, and his own personal vendetta is still raw and open - now that Malcolm’s dead and it didn’t bring him any closure. His betrayers are still out there. "Twenty in a pack? We never 'ad more than ten, bloods included."

                  Apostol also tries to encourage his fellows to keep some of the guns for themselves. He’s currently the only one with any skill with guns. "'Afore we go much further I reckon I can sell these munitions for a good price, get us some right food and drink...unless one o' ya'll wants one."

                  Moose immediately declines. He much prefers his bow.

                  (We rule Athletics for archery, though I think that might be baseline?)

                  Zosia also declines. She just isn’t that keen on them.

                  Apostol doesn’t take it too hard. "There was this fellow I served with...Indian, Choktaw maybe, I forget. Insisted on keeping this bow he said was passed down through the family when we were out in the field. Showed us a thing 'er too. Guns're fine if you wanna make a statement and blow someone's head off but a bow hits quiet-like...people don't know you're there."

                  He tries out a joke: "Ya'll Menina been stickin' with rock-spears and bows since Pangaea fell, no reason to change yet huh."

                  It goes over well enough. Moose chuckles, at least.

                  (I have the pack roll an Empathy check as they get into town. Zosia succeeds with a 4, while the guys only score 1 each. Along with their passive perceptions, and the pack gets a little feel for the area)

                  The main avenue is little more than a beaten dirt road but there are two dozen wooden buildings lined up alongside it. Apostol quickly distinguishes where the law is, where the saloons are, and where’s the best place to buy and sell whatever the pack needs.

                  As Apostol is the only one who had been a city-boy when he changed, the sudden shift in noise levels doesn't throw him off too badly. The sudden barrage of scents good and bad doesn’t overload him- and though he quickly picks out horseflesh (and excrement), smoke, gun oil, booze, sex, blood, urine, and the basic unwashed scent of civilization - he keeps his head.

                  The other two, well, they’ve forgotten what it’s like to be pressed in by so many noises, sights, smells, and the constant awareness of the humans around them.

                  For such a small town, the press of bodies is unnaturally heavy. Its awkward keeping track of each other as the three of them are jostled as they make their way. Men and women crowd the street almost equally. Boys hawk for their stores while several of the southern saloons have a girl or two outside, fanning themselves lazily in the early morning heat, their interest more on the conversation with each-other than striking up business. It's still early - the regulars wouldn't be walking by for a while to come.

                  For the most part, Apostol and Moose are left alone, but Zosia - the pack can see the looks she receives. Most are open, curious stares as a freed colored woman in this part of the country isn't quite that common - especially with the war going on back east. There are a few that are cruder - eyeing her as something exotic, and some that even stare with open hostility.

                  Moose glares openly back at the crowd.

                  Apostol doesn’t, figuring that a bunch of humans doing what humans tend to do isn’t worth it. Besides, if someone did start trouble, the pack could easily take them on.

                  (Ah, Blood Talons - always gearing up to throw-down).

                  Zosia is used to the looks, and she doesn’t care - though she notes the more hostile stares because it’s better to be aware of someone who could ambush you later. "We should see if the Argent have any standin' here. We'll need to check the Hisil too. Later.”

                  Apostol agrees. "Let's get a drink and have a siddown then eh? I could use some music. We could also do some with some salted pork, more water, maybe I can find some dynamite..." that’s said with a harsh look Moose’s way.

                  Moose doesn’t even acknowledge it. "This is a minin' town. Dynamite's in high demand,"

                  Moose seems to be right. Beyond the hawkers and the working girls, most of the men are dressed in rough coveralls and working gear suggestive of time spent underground or hard at digging.

                  They pick out the rough layout of the town within the first hour, and Gods bless simple-minded town planning, but everything was laid out on a rough northwest-to-southeast grid, with the county seat and jailhouse at one end, and the establishments growing a little more, well, lively, by the time they hit the end of the northwestern stretch of the main road before it turned off to feed back into the main wagon pass up into the mountains.

                  The pack picks out a saloon that seems to have commandeered its’ portion of the street. The music pours out from the place, and there’s the sweet scent of food and drink on the air.

                  (Resonance: Excitement, though none of the pack succeeded on the check.)

                  Apostol promises he won’t start any fights. The other two give him a disagreeing look. As the pack heads in, they banter good-naturedly about bar-brawls, and if fancy was just a term used to jack up liquor prices.

                  As soon as they walk in, they get relief from the heat of the day. It’s an open floor plan, with stairs leading up to an interior balcony that stretches over the ground floor to assist in people-watching. There’s fifteen other folk in there, plus the bartender and the clever-handed man at the piano.

                  Everything about the place encourages them to sit for a bit, relax, have a good time.

                  No one really gives them a second glance as they head inside. The pack, though, can feel that something seems to be watching them.

                  As they come up to the bar, Apostol channels the Voice of Glory again, and begins to chat up the bartender. As he does, Moose does his own meet-and-greet with some of the local men who look like they’ve just got back from mining. As he approaches, he overhears their conversation about a mine that’s recently lost workers and is in danger of being shut down because no one’s coming to work - even with a five dollars a week pay increase.

                  (How else does one seed plots besides strange rumors? This is my favorite tried and true method as the PCs can pick and choose what they follow.)

                  Apostol and the bartender trade idle conversation for a minute or so, and then Apostol starts flashing the coin he made trading. The bartender notices.

                  "Struck it rich, huh? Let me guess, Cripple Creek finally produced something other than salt?"

                  (A scam that some people used in the flurry of the gold rushes was to “salt” the discoveries to bring in bodies, or in layman’s terms: plant gold into the streams to be found and strike up more rumors/investors)

                  Apostol smiles and keeps up the disarming, good ol' Army Boy routine of his. "Heard about some good opportunities out in these parts, though I reckon a lotta other folk have too from the looks of it...you get all sorts of odd people out here don'tcha.”

                  "Sure, that's what we pride ourselves on. There's fortune in the Rockies, apparently, and all those fortune hunters need food, drink, and pleasant company. If it's not gold they're lookin' for, then it's God, or Truth, or hell - anything that draws people out this way."

                  As Apostol chats with the bartender, Zosia notices that strange watching sensation has grown, and sends her sight across the Gauntlet. As she does, she realizes that they’re sitting in the faram - the pale of a locus. Is it Claimed? Are there other Werewolves here already?

                  Zosia looks across and sees a space not too dissimilar from the Unchanging-Side, though the interior of the saloon looks like a literal watering hole, with the grass, rock, and dirt carved into the image of chairs and tables. The bar that they sit at is gone, and replaced by an actual pool of liquor that continuously spills out and onto the floor. She glances down to that mirrored surface and finds that there are what looks to be a thousand shards of colored glass glimmering within it; and the Flesh-side reflected through. All the while strange fleshy-fans flutter around it like moths.

                  (Zosia rolls a decent Occult and learns a few things: they’re at a locus - a well tended one, and that it’s occupied by a few spirits)

                  Zosia sees a giant figure with a rictus grin that stretches from ear to ear. It's dressed in the three-piece waistcoats of an established gentleman, but it is not human. It has four arms, for one, and the face looks carved into soft gold. It seems to be intently staring at the reflection of Apostol in the mirrored pool as it itself stands within it.

                  As the bartender mentions God, Moose takes a break from his conversation to fetch a bottle of whiskey and head back to the conversation with the miners.

                  (We determine here that Moose’s Physical Touchstone is Alcoholism. Drinking helps him to forget, and if he imbibes too much, it helps to suppress the wolf too.)

                  Apostol doesn’t really like the mentioning of God. He’s paranoid - after all, the Argents thought themselves Holy-bound in their fight against Werewolves, so he presses the bartender: "Prospectors come lookin' for God out here huh. Why, you got somebody tellin' folks he's just been waiting out here in Colorado for us this whole time? Here I thought he was back in Kansas."

                  Zosia calls down the ancient tricks of the Ithaeur to whisper to her about the massive spirit that’s a little too keen on Apostol right now. She doesn’t trust it. As she studies the creature from the safety of the Flesh-side, she learns it’s called Kuk-guzen, and it had to lend aid to any favor asked of it - but then ask a favor weighted fairly in return.

                  Seems friendly enough, right?

                  The bartender doesn’t seem to open up to Apostol’s wheedling. "Son," he says, "if you go looking hard enough, God will find you anywhere you desire." He sets out the extra bottle for Moose and doesn't seem to comment on the coin the pack is setting down. Neither, oddly enough, does any of the other patrons. No, they're busy setting bits out for their own drinks.

                  (Apostol has insane social pools, and at the same time, he keeps rolling 1s and 0s.)

                  Apostol knows when he’s being stalled, so he tucks that away for another time. "Mmm, well, must be lookin' wrong then I s'pose."

                  The bartender nods sympathetically. “Your money is appreciated here anytime, and if you're interested in hearin' God speak, well, come back tonight. Might have a sermon prepared for you."

                  Apostol then nudges the shaman next to him. "...what're you gettin' here?"

                  Zosia returns her sight back to the flesh-side. “A spirit that might be of use if paid correctly. It was enraptured by you." Zosia’s eager to cross and interact with it.

                  Apostol, on the other hand, isn’t quite as keen on the spirit-side of werewolf livin’. "Hmm...yeah, a possibility, but we ought be careful which denizens we go talkin' to afore we know if they're beholden to someone if we can. I didn't notice any turf markers on the way in here and neither did Moose or he'd've grunted somethin' about it." He shrugs, "'sides, ain't found a way across."

                  Zosia grins. “Already found it. The mirror. Besides, I know my way around spirits.”

                  Apostol blinks, impressed. “Here’s what I'm thinkin'...we pick up supplies. Dynamite, powder, provisions, maybe stop by a ferrier and pick out a nice brave mule to lighten the load. We should probly strike base outside o' town in the woods, much as I miss an actual bed, hide our stuff...come back tonight, maybe talk to that Denizen you mentioned and see what this uh, 'sermon' is all about."

                  Zosia nods. It’s not a bad idea. Besides, right now Moose seems to be getting lost in conversation and his cups.

                  Apostol throws back another shot, then stands up and decides that it’s time to work his magic on the crowd. He approaches a conversation about the money coming into a ranch just south of a place called Jimmy’s Camp. He points out Moose as he comes over. “That one's late brother came here to buy his uh, 'livestock' if you recall. Might I be able to rustle up a few names since I heard some of that old Dixie money squirmed its way out West?"

                  (I have Visk roll again. 6 dice to see what he can pick up through conversation+mingling. He rolls a fat 0. The dice hate Apostol when he’s trying to not start fights.)

                  So, with that failure looming, we deem that Apostol is far too used to the schmoozing that was born in the east, along the plantations, where every word drips with false-flattery and double-meaning. As soon as he mentions 'Dixie', some of the music seems to falter, and he’s giving a more suspicious look - and his trademarked Union Cap is now well-noticed. Some of the crowd wonders who he’s spying for down here: the Yankees or the Confederates?

                  Conversation remains polite for Apostol, but it all clams up to basic, generics. No one seems willing to openly talk to him about anything now. Apostol finds himself back in that murky divide between North and South, where men spoke one word and meant another entirely. It brings him back to the fact that he never belonged back in that place, and now, even with the moonsong turning the prey to listen to his words - he’s still not one of them, and they seem to know it as well.

                  Still, he makes note of the more successful ranchers, and who deferred to whom when they spoke.

                  Moose strikes out in his own information-gathering. The alcohol is just a little too irresistible, and as he meets with the miners (bringing over bottles to sweeten the first impressions) one of them seems to remember him.

                  (Moose has Professional Training 5 for Fur Trapper. I rolled a % and it came up likely that he’s well known in these parts. He also has the Giant Merit. Kinda hard to forget.)

                  So the miner introduces Moose to his buddies. "This is that Grizzly Man Jose always talked up. Fella," he looks back at you. "You was a lifesaver back in '59 when that frost hit early."

                  "Grizzly Man, nice name, but I go by Moose, I just do what I can. Glad I made a good impression. These days, I'm usually clearin' out varmints and such. In fact, I just cleared out a mine a few days ago. What seems to be the trouble, fellers?"

                  The scrawnier man hits the other man in his ribs with an elbow. The second man, being in the middle of a drink, sputters and glares as the first starts to speak. "See! We can get the place open again, then make enough to head up to Denver, yeah? I'm Paolo, this is Jesse."

                  The second man, Jesse, has a scar running along his left nasal fold which breaks the pattern of his stubble and gives Moose a wary glance. "You just happened to clear out a mine, huh? What did you clear out of it?"

                  (As soon as I mention the scar, Griff OOCly goes: “That guy is an Azlu”. My players are paranoid.)

                  "Rattlesnakes," Moose lies, "Whole messa them parked in the mines. Used ferrets. They're like the mongooses of the 'mericas. Mongeese? Them things what Injuns use ta kill snakes. Not our Injuns, the ones from Inja.”

                  (I had Moose roll for this… uh, interesting conversation. Even with the intriguing prospect of using mongeese to clear out rattlesnakes, Griff rolls a 0 on his social roll as well! The guys cannot catch a break.)

                  "Rattlesnakes." Jesse states flatly, and looks between Moose's bald-face lie and Paolo's exuberant grin. "Paolo, you're an fucking idiota, man." He gets up. "Rattlesnakes."

                  As he heads off, Paolo stares after him, then gives Moose a sympathetic look and an offer to drink with him and the third member, who offers up a name of Carlos. Moose does, and the conversation is steered back to the "good ol' days" as he’s pressed for stories about his time in the mountains, and what exactly a mongeese is. It's hard, with the drink flowing, to really get the conversation back to the mines, but when you've got booze in hand and laughter around you, that sort of questioning can wait, right?

                  (Moose gets lost in his glory days, which leaves us with Zosia.)

                  Zosia watches her compatriots mingle, and indulges in a little bit of people-watching. Mostly, everyone here seems to fall into one of three camps: miners, ranchers, or drifters. There's not much more that she can see at the surface, but she’s got a suspicion that the waters ran pretty deep here. As the boys talked, she's approached by the local working girls. They're friendly, all smiles and earnestness. She’s exotic, they tell her, and if she’s interested in earning a bit of cash that didn't involve digging in the dirt, there was an opportunity here for her.

                  Zosia goes through a quick run of emotions. First, she’s excited that she’s propositioned because she prefers the fairer sex, and then she’s upset because it’s just a job offer. Then, she shotguns a drink and then bluntly sets down a fair bit of coin on the counter.

                  "Unfortunately I already have work but company," She glances to her pack, "I seem to be a bit short on. Care for a drink?"

                  When Zosia turns down the job offer, the working girls nod, understandingly. Then she offers them a job.

                  (Zosia rolls her little pool of 4 dice and gets… 1 success! She’s the only one of the pack to succeed in their social carousing rolls.)

                  "Well, we typically don't have ladies paying for the service..."

                  "But ... coin is coin, is it not?"

                  "Might last more than a few minutes too," the third lass slyly mutters with a wicked grin. Zosia is escorted from the saloon towards the back-section, around where the bar is. She figures she’ll catch up with her Pack later. After all, Apostol's just heading out to buy supplies and Moose is ... well... Moose is deep in his own memories at the moment. She won't be missed for a bit.
