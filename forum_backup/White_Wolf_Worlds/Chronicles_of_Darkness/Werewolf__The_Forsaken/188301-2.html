                                        Considering the nature of the Merit... I find it highly unlikely that a character that isn't interested in the affected Merits would also develop this Merit. Even if it doesn't have a mechanical prerequisite, the narrative one is obvious. How the hell does your character fit the fluff for this Merit without the other Merits in question?

                                        Pitch me the concept in which it is both appropriate that the character religiously avoids these Merits, and still has a reason to have learned Iron Master tricks for shutting down human assets. Because at the moment is sounds more like a player seeing the mechanics and wanting them, not something that makes any sense in character for the character to have learned how to do.

                                        Plenty of characters have no reason to invest in any those Merits... but they'd have the same reasons not to invest in this one.

                                        They can be used in conjunction with actions taken in game to do these things but usually only if appropriate.
                                        Allies doesn't have a particularly strong restriction along these lines. If you want, you can use Allies 5 to shut down another character's Merit indefinitely as well as long as you don't ask any more of your Allies. There's no roll needed. And the target can't do anything to reclaim those dots besides get rid of you or block your Allies with something else.

                                        If someone is using Allies 5 to keep your Mentor in constant legal trouble, you don't get to use the Sanctity of Merits rules to trade your Mentor in for something else. You haven't lost your Mentor.

                                        Neither is actually permanent because both only last as long as the character with the Merit continues to use it to block. Stop that and the blocking stops and you get your Merit back in full force.

                                        As well, Allies isn't all or nothing. If you have Allies 5 and are blocking a 3 dot Merit, you still have 2 dots of favors to ask without getting into trouble. With the Iron Master Merit you can only block. If you can block more than the target has, the extra blocking power is wasted.

                                        Allies can also be bought multiple times, getting around any "this group couldn't do that" issues, and giving you the ability to block even more dots, against even more characters. The Iron Master Merit can't. It'll only ever block one target.
