    NPC ressources issues

    03-04-2014, 03:54 PM
    Hi,

    I'm trying to find a way where I could get some random stats for a bunch of NPC at the same time. For example, let's say I have a "fodder" thief and that my player are never going to meet them, I just assign random stats like that they would have a certain number of health boxes with particular stats, but when they meet certains NPC and they just decide that they want it to be important, having a ressource that would let me assign random stats depending on certain conditions could help a lot...

    For example, they decided that a secretary that was being hunted by a spirit claimed would become one of the mate for the alpha of the pack and he would use it in his future business as a secretary. So, she is now a retainer with stats that could be used for many things. Having stats for her "quickly" could be nice, when I think that they just get a lot of NPC that they decide to find important when I would not think they will take consider it.

    This also apply to "random" encounters... One of my player decided to go see russians to get knuckle fist one game, and some times later, when I was no longer considering them in my head, they just decided to go meet them and hire them to attack another NPC. Having them with basic stats could have been useful for the second encounter where the first I just used the trick "they try to intimitade the player... they have 4 dice because they are somewhat ok at that...." but 3 months later, I just don't remember that they add those kind of dice pool!

    How do you deal with those type of situations if you don't use any tools?

    For Retainers, I'm thinking about about asking the player to "make" the character when it is a new character, and for the secretary, I think I will do that, I don't have any stat for her yet... They just happened to be faster than the spirit claimed that wanted to kill her following its own way of life where it need to do some actions before killing its victim on the first weekend of the month.

    Do you think it's a good way?

    Finally, what would you give as basic stats for a bunch of spirits they they would just have to make their way through? Basic one like a Nocuoth? They might encounter a lot of rank 1 spirits led by a rank 3 spirit on an unknown territory where the spirits will surely try to attack them and I'm just not sure of what to assign to them in either case where they might "negociate" with their claws or with their words... I just want to be easily adjusted to the situations. I have details on what is where in my game, but not necessarily the full content of the place... so in a situation like that, basic stats could help, and I don't think designing basic ban for a rank 1 spirit is too hard, but their other stats is more "painful"...

    Thank you!

      Being able to consistently fudge and guesstimate things really saves bookkeeping with most NPCs. For example, the way GMC handles retainers: they roll twice the merit rating for skills they frequently use or are trained it, and roll just the dots in the merit for any things outside their expertise.
      A similar way that I've seen of doing this is to have a basic idea of how good the NPC is at a given task: roll 2 dice for unfamiliar, 3-4 for familiar-ish, 6 for a skilled professional, or 8-10 for 'best of the best'. (Mind you, these are just ways of fudging Attribute+Skill rolls; equipment and circumstance factors should apply on top of these.)
      I might also consider giving NPCs a handful of descriptive qualities - hobbies, personality traits, quirks, motivations, professional skills. That kind of flavor, even if its just 2 or 3 things, can give a bit part more dimension and be a good guideline for their guessing their dice pools.

      I will generally stat out - or allow my players to stat - recurring NPCs. I'm quite fine with allowing my players to stat their own retainers and such; as long as you retain privilege of approval, they end up with fair and interesting characters, generally. Alternatively, I sometimes let players stat and role-play another person's retainer, if both are okay with that - it can make things much more fun and interesting.
      When letting others stat, I tend to go with "as a starting character", but other useful variations include: stat them as a familiar; stat them as a hedgebeast companion; or give them some proportion of the players current exp total (1/2 usually).

      Finally, when I recently statted out groups of antagonists my players would be dealing with, my approach was to write up their role and tactics (as a group, or as a sublevel in the group), and gave a list of powers/maneuvers which the group has collectively, and fudged things from there. Hope that's useful somehow~

          Rule of thumb here: Assume that the average pool for anything is 4; 5 is above average; 6 is professional; 7 is an specialist; 8 and above are people recognized in the field in question. Then the actual stats come out naturally. Don't forget modifiers.

          For spirits, check out the table which says the average number of traits a spirit has based on rank. A lesser gaffling usually got 7 traits, divided among Power, Finesse and Resistance, and each caps at 5. Roll a die per rank and add them together for how much essence it currently has (gafflings are usually starving and wouldn't risk being discorporated in a fight with say, 2 essence). If it's rank 2 and above, divide it by two and round it up.

          For example, I'm making a lesser fire gaffling. It's gonna have say, Power 3, Finesse 3 and Resistance 1. I roll for it's essence (it can only hold 10 points) and score a 4. It's size 2, so it's corpus is 3. The spirit would be rather welcome to make a bargain with the Halfbreeds for some essence, rather than risk their ire and end up a puff of smoke (discorporated).

          If I'm making the fire spirit's brood leader, a lesser jaggling (rank 3) I'm gonna roll 3 dice, scoring a 10, a 5 and a 6, divide by 2 and round it up, it got 11 essence. Much more of a threat.
