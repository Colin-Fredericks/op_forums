    Originally posted by orathaic View Post
    Yes, I would concur that a Thing from Plane B being able to escape to Place A does not guarantee that a thing from Plane A can survive in Plane B or if they can figure out how to survive (werewolves in this question, possibly mutating/shape shifting/ learning 'gifts' from the local denizens/local spirit? But more generally, things which have some supernatural powers may have some hope of survival...) I am presuppossing that they can figure out how to survive.
    Werewolves have the capacity to learn Gifts as part of the way the Shadow works. As the primary point of reference, on the long list of things Infernal bargains can offer, Gifts and Renown are specifically excluded from what werewolves can get. (Rites and dots of Primal Urge are fair game, but require sacrifices as any other trade does.) Werewolves' adaptability comes from their being shapeshifters who can interface with the spiritual world, whose breadth of purviews is filtered through the spirits that populate it — it does not translate to carte blanche to derive powers from any type of ephemeral being without the aid of a plot device.

    They may not be able to access whatever mechanisms the native use to escape - presumably the entities in question find it difficult to escape in the first place, or they would have over-run the Plane A by now... Or they are so rare in Plane B that them being able to cross freely doesn't make much of a difference population-wise in Plane A?
    The primary examples of how Lower Depths entities escape are that Infernal denizens are occasionally able to exploit people's sins to become a voice in their head that can mildly twist probability around them to steer them toward summoning them in full, fiends in Duat can emerge into the sunlit lands in the presence of depictions of them, and the most powerful Strix in the game can turn an area of complete darkness into a gateway to wherever they're from for a scene, which vampires, ghouls, Strix, and other things with their taint on them can spend a Willpower point to cross into (with the only information on what happens thereafter if they don't cross back over in time being that they're "lost").

    I think Dis / the plane where the Strix seem to come from (or go to?) seems like the least impossible to survive and/or escape from, and werewolves might take an interest in the Strix, especially if they were once spirits of humanity (however much they might have changed as a result of whatever happened that made it so people are the only things without spirits).
    The suggested divergence point between the Strix and humanity is far enough back in time that it pre-dates the Sundering by an order of magnitude — we're talking at least 50,000 BCE.

    We shall set aside the matter of local stage hazards in a place of empty darkness for a moment and instead take a moment to remember that this is a place inhabited by Strix, who are not naturally made of ephemeral and can attempt to possess living hosts with moderate Shadow Potency and a point of Willpower — which paints a rather dire picture whether you interpret the place as full of individual smoke-owls or the unitary source of all the Strix that emerge from its gates.

                                        Originally posted by CreatorFounderMaker View Post
                                        So, in your opinion, thr Lower Depths are 'anti-Supernal Realms', just as much as state of being or frame of consciousness as an actual location?
                                        Collectively they're not even those things.

                                        Some Lower Depths may be "anti-Supernal", although I actually doubt, at least in a cosmologically relevant sense. Duat is certainly a "state of being" and a "frame of consciousness", as you can be there in a way and this removes you from larger reality (so, a state of being), and you can experience it (so, a frame of consciousness). But others, not necessarily.

                                        Most of them aren't meant to be something you enter and then work within, not even something you experience. You either perceive them in a very limited way or completely indirectly, so while you can decide that in your chronicle they're somewhere you can explore through some means, the games are adamant to stop just short of saying "this is not a real thing and you're just trying to enter the picture in the wall".

                                        In a sense they're that, a picture in the wall. It looks like a scene, it seems to have depth, and the figures there seem alive, but ultimately it's just a fine coat of paint on bricks tricking your perception and completely lacking the kind of reality you expect. To be there is not to be transported to the depicted scene, but to be translated into a static blotch of paint in a wall.

                                        But the specifics, and how much this analogy holds, is a case-to-case analysis. In the end what they have in common is:

                                        1- They're phenomena that produces something, and in this sense look like a "place" where those things come from;
                                        2- Only open to limited or indirect interaction that can't really confirm if they're places or not. Thinking about them as places helps explaining what little we know about them;
                                        3- Even Mages can't reach them as places and don't know how to properly affect them with Magic, for now. They can't even go there in the way they go to the Astral.

                                        Nothing else is really guaranteed or common among them, and the ST can give whatever answer they want. But as a matter of fact, the devs didn't intend on making them real places that matter, just things that happen and look like places.

                                                            Originally posted by CreatorFounderMaker View Post

                                                            Same place as fiends: Nowhere that you can visit.

                                                            This is not a difficult concept: none of the stuff that comes from Duat does so through an akhet. The dimension does not have any correspondence with Earthly geography and its primary point of entrance is a nonspecific distance in an unbounded cardinal direction through an ephemeral sandstorm in a perspective that puts two omnipresent mountains at the horizons. Its denizens get out by being summoned or depicted or to punish the unfaithful or from interactions with a mummy or from a mummy doing poorly at the tribunal in their soul between incarnations.

                                                            It is, without exaggeration, the closest thing the games have presented to an afterlife, with the only point of argument on that front being that PCs of an appropriate type routinely return from it as part of what they are, when what they are is ritually entombed corpses empowered by their eternal souls and their service to the type of cosmic beings that can get away with splitting their focus between Hell and Earth.
