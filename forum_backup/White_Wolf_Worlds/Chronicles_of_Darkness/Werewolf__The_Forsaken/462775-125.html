        I can't reconcile in my head that the Crimson Falx is not said to deal aggravated damage, but still removes a limb completely. Permanent damage in a non-supernatural is reflected with aggravated damage, so removing a limb should have at least 1 agg damage associated with it.

        With werewolves, lethal heals so fast that it seems comical to me that you remove a limb completely and a new one sprouts before your next turn if in Gauru. I think I might personally rule that it does cause at least 1 aggravated, unless for example as a werewolf you spend an action to hold the neatly severed limb to the stump, then it would be lethal and you can heal the Wrack as if it was lethal damage.

        In my head, regrowing a limb completely as a Werewolf should be more akin to how Deadpool does it in the movie, and not like Piccolo in Dragonball Z.
        Its ability to sever limbs is really limited. It's not lopping them off every attack, it only does it once per scene to the first enemy attacked. If it did flat Aggravated damage, it might as well not have any magical power at all as that is wildly powerful on its own. And, mechanically, there is no representation for one limb equalling a certain amount of damage, but I don't think anyone's going to care if you decide to apply one at your table. It would just be a clunky system to add to the fetish section.

        I don't think anyone has said that a new limb grows in their next turn, but they do have powers to allow things to be more like Piccolo than Deadpool.

            While aggravated damage is the kind of damage that can easily result in a loss of a limb, losing limbs does not require that you've taken aggravated damage. The fact that your arm is missing isn't killing you. It's the bleeding wound that does (and infections), and blood loss is represented through lethal damage.
            I think loss of limbs should be best represented by Conditions that can be resolved by healing it as if you were healing a number of aggravated damage, but it doesn't take up space on your health track.

                            Originally posted by Malus View Post

                            To direct aggravated damage, yes. Indirect aggravated damage is a different story.

                            And as to the above, regarding having trouble considering limb loss as not aggravated. There are lots of in game examples that consider Arm and Leg Wrack without Aggravated damage. They aren't mutually exclusive.                             If a PC has just had all their limbs dislocated at all joints, and I try and represent that with 4 wrack tilts and 2 bashing damage for each joint (shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, heels) each, with a result that rolls over to Lethal, it doesn't mean she is actually bleeding out, though penalties may still apply.

                                        The Shadow is a warped reflection, not an exact reflection. Time (and Space) don't follow the same rules. A long demolished hospital can still appear in the Shadow decades later, provided that the essence resonated from the hospital was not overwhelmed by newer essences. Inversely, a tiny hovel, which provided strong emotions of security and wellbeing, might look to appear as a fortified palace in the shadow. It's all about the spirits that the essence attracts and feeds. In your case, an asylum could be perfectly fitting provided that the essences of the asylum could support them. A very large asylum which was shut down early in its age and didn't see much particular use would probably not even have a reflection in the Hisil, but one that was used for particularly nasty practices, or that saw decades of persistent use, could easily have reached well beyond its physical grounds, turning a small town (based around the asylum) into one large madhouse.

                                        An example of a feudal court is given in the Book of Spirits, p 100, where a court (Called the Harvest Carnival) is established for only a short time every year, only to rest again till next year. Regardless of the other domains present while it's hibernating, it springs up and lays claim to its domain for only a short time, exerting its rule of law. It even extends beyond its place (the Carnival) and into the township to get the people in town excited about returning to the Carnival.

                                                        Yes, I know there are. That is what causes the issue in my mind.
                                                        A character gets hit by a baseball bat to their arm, causing more lethal damage than their stamina. An Arm Wrack caused by lethal, most likely a break.

                                                        Now, this damage would heal in several days time, healing the Wrack as the last damage is healed. Takes an hour at most for a werewolf.

                                                        Now let's say you use a Crimson Falx that is specifically said to chop off the limb permanently. Not just break the bone. Let's say they wear some armor and only receive one lethal damage. The arm goes flying off, with only 1 damage. If mortal, it takes a few days to heal it. But unlike every other Wrack caused by lethal, it never gets removed even if at full health. In the case of a Werewolf, the damage is healed in a turn or 15 minutes. I try for some sense of realism in my game, arms just growing back without supernatural means does not fit it.

                                                        Now, werewolves are supposed to be able to recover all losses of limbs at some point, just need to remember that they grow a new arm eventually, keep track of it somewhere on the character sheet. I think in my games I might just rule that always the first 1-2 points of damage it deals is aggravated. That way mortals never heal it and takes longer for WW. I also think your overall "health" might be lower if you have chopped off limbs. No offense to any amputees out there.
