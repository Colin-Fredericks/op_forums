            Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post


            On many fronts really. For starters Uratha at least in 1st edition had a very loose society, it gave the impression that most pack were islands on themselves. And for what im seeing in 2e they follow through that, like removing the rites of contrition and funeral one that really dont made sense in how the setting was presented.

            So when Uratha fight the pure what then? they suposelly beat them up and let them go? Sure the drama can be sweet at that but in my experience most players try to find a way to cheat it or we just agree to remove it because well, its werewolf a game in which you are a killing machine, a killing machine that just deliver non lethal blows to their sworn enemies is weird.

            Thats just my taste and experience.
            There's a reason it's worded "The People don't kill The People" and not "Werewolves don't kill Werewolves." There's a lot of room for interpretation. Do the Pure count as The People? They sure don't count the Forsaken among The People, so when they kill your packmates are you just going to turn the other cheek and say "the oath forbids it" or are you going to look for an excuse why it's not technically against the oath? There's a whole tribe now that considers other werewolves their sacred prey (the Blood Talons), so there must be at least some cases where it's acceptable, right? Those kinds of moral questions create dramatic tension, which is exactly why the writers included it in the Oath and left it ambiguous who does or doesn't count as The People. And, of course, with the way Harmony works now, the occasional violation of the Oath can actually bebeneficial and help maintain balanced Harmony. You don't want to do it too much or the Blood Talons might decide you don't count as The People anymore, but that's just more potential for drama.

            Going by Willow now, or Wil for short. She/Her/Hers.
