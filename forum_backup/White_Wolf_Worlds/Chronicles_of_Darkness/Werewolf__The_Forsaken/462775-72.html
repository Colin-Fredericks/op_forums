I mean, the purified are able to get personal totems as well, as much as 1e is a valid influence on the conversation here. I personally would 100% allow a mage cabal or vampire coterie or some other group to take a totem from the hisil, if they were able to convince me that they have actually formed a bond with a spirit. My limitation would be on roleplay, requiring that the non-uratha groups

                1. Have a tie to the hisil / spirits to start: and
                2. Are able to convince / force a spirit to act as totem for their group (which is going to be naturally harder than an uratha pack will find, unless they're at the point where spirits consider the group part of the ecology, like a mage cabal with an adept of spirit, or a group of vampires with Claire Redfield's 2e version of Blood Tenebrous at the right level.)

                Whereas your typical uratha containing pack could just go and find a spirit and either convince them or force them to be totem.

                Good to know your view though, clearly you think of the totem as a far more integral part of the uratha template than I do, my friend who GM's for me would probably agree with you that allowing on-uratha groups to take a totem is cribbing too much on their proprietary stuff.

                                Mages that take totems have to prove they have the spirit's interest at heart, which is considerably more difficult with their burning obsessions. Could they device a spell to do it? Sure they could. Then they have to deal with having an alien creature applying bans on their behaviors and aspirations that will likely conflict with what polite mage society thinks as wise, and that can be exploited. How hard do you think it would be for the local Pylon to figure out your bans? Then what happens when this spell runs out? To relinquish it safely at an indefinite duration is a life-long bond. Can they make that commitment? Even some Uratha are incapable of doing so.

                                The case of a singular mage who joins in a pack is clearly spelled out in The Pack's Other section. The same complications arise, without the pack finding out that the mage is attempting to exert undue manipulation on their literal spirit. That could turn ugly, fast.

                                Vampires? They usually have more pressing matters at hand rather than to appease some shadow entity and running the risk of being labelled demonologists by the Second and First States.

                                If you want to explore all that, go ahead. I'm not going to send ninjas at your table to stop you. I'm just enumerating the complications such a thing creates, which should be considered for a greater experience.

                                                    The baseline metaphor of greatest utility, I think, is amoebas — regardless of what form the consumption takes, a spirit consumes bits of other spirits by engulfing and incorporating them; a spirit is wholly consumed when the last of its directive portions have been subverted.

                                                    There aren't really mechanics for the threshold of total consumption because it's kind of an inherently narrative turning point, but it's important to remember that spirits imbue some of their nature into their Essence by dint of having it and that spirits are what they eat; part of the reason spirit lords set up tithing systems is because it lets them cycle their personal subtype of Essence back into the court and hungry spirits are less likely to turn down Essence that fits their basic nature.

                                                    Feeding is a means of establishing dominance in Shadow ecology, because making other spirits dependent upon you and/or having them continue to exist at your mercy effectively turns them into extensions of yourself in a means that is only made more literal by total incorporation. Keeping that in mind, recognizable-spirit predation tends to be illustrated in fashions that demonstrate the superiority of the predator or mark the prey as subordinate to the predator when successful. Depending on type, a cell-phone spirit might overwhelm its lessers with chatter or force them to give up their secrets; a spirit of graffiti makes an abandoned building its own by tagging its spirit over and other until the walls run over with cheap paint.

                                                        It could have been more directly asked.

                                                        There's no one way, or even a right way, to deal with problems like this.

                                                        An obvious way is to kill 'the boss.' But if you're restricting it to actions in the spirit world you can limit and weaken the spirits of crime. They would just come back later if it was a white room or something, but it's not. It's an ecosystem and it hates a vacuum just like the real world so something else is going to take advantage. Weakened crime spirits are easy prey for police or law spirits, for instance. The police or law spirits can get stronger, and influence the gurihal that way. They're out there using Strengthen on the detective trying to solve a case, Control or Manipulation to make sure the officer sees the evidence.

                                                        'You want to eliminate crime in your territory' is a bit of a leap. Werewolves break laws routinely, and if you have an authoritarian Hisil full of mirror-shaded cop spirits you're going to find it just as hostile as one full of empty-hooded thug spirits lurking in dark alleys to gank you. It's about balance, not extremism, you deal with spirits because killing them is going to upset that balance, while encouraging them to act more moderately is 'safer.' Not that you can't try to eliminate certain aspects of things you don't like, but it's the kind of thing a Storyteller should take advantage of. Someone on the forums pointed out that Forsaken is all about unintended consequences, from the death of Father Wolf to landing on the moon. It's a good thing to keep in mind when Storytelling spirit shenanigans.
