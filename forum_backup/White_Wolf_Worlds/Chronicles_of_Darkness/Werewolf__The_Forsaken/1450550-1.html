        How to keep Werewolf combat interesting? Here are some thoughts

        1/ The Pure: The first edition really went into detail about how dangerous the Pure were. They were more numerous than the Forsaken (outnumbering Forsaken about 2:1), highly organized, and brutal as hell. Blood of the Wolf was going on about demographics and said that a fight between a pack of Forsaken, and a Pack of Pure that outnumbered them by even a few members was going to be a hell of a fight. So...if Werewolves are the baddest of the bad, throw more Werewolves at them. Pure vs. Forsaken is an apples to apples comparison. They know the weaknesses of the Forsaken and can fight on even terms. If there are more Pure than Forsaken, that will add lots of tension to the fight

        2/ Silver: Just use Silver. Craft a silver knife, load a shotgun with Silver Buckshot, use a piece of silverware. Unlike Kryptonite, Silver is everywhere and humans mine thousands of tons of it per year. A little old lady with a silver Letter opener can become a danger to a careless forsaken. If you want inspiration, read the fiction in the Idigam chronicles. There is one part where they said that silver bullets don't work because silver warps in the air, but a silver shotgun is fair game because the pellets scatter so accuracy is not an issue. Then there is a part where a Pure Kinfolk kills a Blood Talon Rahu with 2 shotgun blasts. Silver weakness is an open secret too for Werewolves.

        Even outside of combat, it can be socially inconvenient to be in an antique shop and your character starts screaming because he accidently picked up a silver coin.

        3: Boost your Spirits: Spirits and boost attributes by Rank + 2, pump up your attributes and even a rank 2 spirit can be a tough fight and a pack of them can dogpile and bring down even a werewolf in Kuruth

        4: Emotional Aura Numien: This is a debuff which reduces dicepools of everyone within 5 yards by 2. Suddenly the Garou form is neutralized somewhat and is not so tough now.

            This is sort of another thing I see a lot of questions about 'balancing combat' for. Basically it's very easy for Storytellers to create an antagonist that gets melted down by the protagonists because the ST wasn't aware of the damage the PCs were capable of. You do have the other end of the table with a 'rocks fall everyone dies' or snipers waiting a thousand yards outside of PCs houses ready to blow their heads off the moment they step outside but that is far more unlikely.

            As a Storyteller, if you're wanting to make engaging combats by the rules, you're going to want to know what your PCs are capable of. Just look at their sheets, and check out their combat dicepools and their health. Our first game in Werewolf second edition, I wanted to avoid a previous character's overpoweredness (he was built with Strength and Athletics, being an athlete, but in first edition this means you can basically one shot an elephant with a bow), by making a character who would not be a combat machine. So I had no combat Skills. The ST encouraged me to put at least one dot somewhere and I put it in Brawl. This doesn't seem like much. He had 2 Strength, 1 Brawl. In a bar fight he would maybe be doing 1 Bashing damage, if he was lucky enough to hit considering higher Defenses in second edition, and going with the statistic that with 3 dice you're likely to get 1 success.

            However, as a shapeshifter, everything changes very easily. In most forms he's got a bonus to Strength, and he starts inflicting Lethal damage with his attacks, to the point that in Gauru form he's got +3 to Strength, and for every success he's doing +2L damage and negating Athletics, the big boost to Defense in second edition. This means that the skimpy looking weakling who was throwing down maybe 1 Bashing if he was lucky is more likely to get 2 Successes, and do an extra two Lethal on top of that, giving him something like 4 Lethal damage for one attack. Add something like All-Out for +2, and Willpower for +3, and you're likely filling your average 7 Health human in one shot, if you don't decide to just go Down & Dirty. And that's without any special Fighting Styles or other combat-oriented Merits, with one dot of Skill.

            So, my advice for that is to look at your PCs sheets and write down their likely combat dicepools and their Health with a range. My character, Malcolm White was pretty simple. Brawl 3 to 6, +0B to +2L, 7 to 11 Health. Now I know at a glance and with some quick maths what they're likely to be doing in case I'm running Malcolm and have him attacked by a single entity and I want it to be a challenge. You can always make antagonists tougher by playing on action economy by offering them in a group, or make a single antagonist tougher by adding Defense (which can make them unhittable at points, like above 6 for Malcolm if he's not using WP or All-Out attacks) or Armor (which reduces the damage, making the fight last longer but still keeping the antagonist within the range of hittability, like general armor 3 turns his 4 Lethal to 1), or a mix of all three. And once the other packmates get into the fight, I'd be looking at a list of damage they're capable of.

            There's a lot of ways it can be upended, of course, Gifts, tactics, banes, and planning can melt some of the toughest enemies. But I've found this can help a long way for STs who aren't sure about the 'power levels' of what they're making, or the PCs they're running.

                            Originally posted by maryshelly View Post


                            thanks for the input, I was not sure if it could be used in firearms. Could it be made at home in a mould ( say by some ghouls...)?
                            Filling a shotgun shell is no real problem at all. Filling a hollow or explosive point bullet with silver would take a little more effort and basic gunsmithing equipment. Making a reliable and accurate full-fledged silver bullet would take a custom-mold and a fair amount of gunsmithing experience. Oddly a well-made silver round might have so little deformation it over penetrates your target and isn't as deadly as you'd hope, since you want the silver to stay inside the werewolf. A proper silver round would be a little less accurate at longer ranges and more likely to pass through a target without fragmenting than an equivalent normal bullet.

                            If you're just desperate and in a hurry, making a crappy silver bullet would be relatively easy, but you'd run risk of the round not cycling properly or having crap accuracy, that sort of stuff.

                            In game terms, for a well-made silver bullet, use the -1 damage they had in 1st Edition. For a hollow point or similar round that is just delivering the silver rather than being the entire bullet, use the standard 2nd edition damage. For a silver bullet handmade without the right equipment and knowledge, -1 damage and lower range by one step. Maybe even treat as an Improvised Weapon; -1 damage, -1 initiative.

                            It can also matter what kind of silver is used; a lot of silver coins are composed of alloys intended to make them harder than guns are usually designed for. Raw silver bullion is softer and will take to rifling fine. Ironically the movie "Silver Bullet" gave us all the misleading impression that melting down silver jewelry was the way to go. What you actually want to do is just order regular old normal silver and cast it yourself. Melted down jewelry silver is much more likely to jam your gun.

                            I can totally see a ghoul carving out a useful skillset as "that guy who makes silver bullets for the Invictus." That guy probably makes hollow-point rounds loaded with silver most of the time, fills shotgun shells with silver shot the rest of the time, and maybe does the occasional special-order for something that has to get the silver past body armor.

                                    Plus werewolves can use full Defense against Firearms and Gifts to shut them down.

                                    That said, I honestly recommend against using silver to just make combat more interesting. Aggravated damage lingers for a few days, and so it's not just something that's going to make one encounter interesting. Dangerous, sure, but in this case silver is better used by lesser thugs. The above werewolf, Malcolm, had to infiltrate a vampire elder's lair and get past a number of ghouls armed with silver guns. At that point he had really figured out how to bank on his bite attack, doing Aggravated in Urshul form to disarm targets via their Arm Wrack, usually resulting in the target missing an arm entirely (along with whatever was held in it). Still, while it was relatively easy to sneak around and kill the ones necessary, the pressure was more how a gun could set off an alarm and wake the daysleeping vampire, as well as have a bunch of ghouls that were leisurely on guard suddenly searching for an enemy and defending the entry to their master's lair. Silver acted as more of a deterrent to the 'charge in and chomp them all' style, and added to the tension of my character's stealth mission, keep in mind too that silver sets off Kuruth so it would have ruined the stealthy search and the entire pack's plan and reason why we split up in the first place. This is like what Aurumae was saying, sometimes it's sort of the journey rather than the fight that makes the interesting bit. Because staking an unconscious vampire isn't a spectacularly interesting or dangerous scene. Getting past trained, ghouled guards with silver weapons, then figuring out where in the beat up old farmhouse with what seemed to be regular farmers and their dog the elder was laired, having a shotgun pointed at my character by one while the other was picking up a phone and having to decide which to go after, it's the situation that leads to choices like that that make things fun.

                                    Games like the Arkham franchise, or Metal Gear Solid, often can be used as inspiration for these things. They keep track of how you most often deal with little outposts of baddies, and eventually beef up those baddies so that your usual 'style' meets some hitches. Apparently for MGS it's called the Revenge System. Not all werewolf games are going to have you facing off against a ton of different groups of minions working for the same antagonist but you, as Storyteller, can see what the players are doing often, and easily, and the next time you're planning something throw some bumps in the road for them.

                                                        Recapping everything that was said in this thread:
                                                        1. Falling in Kuruth is not a boost, it's a problem. It should always have bad consequences for pack as a whole, unless pack was very successful in catching prey up with their pants down.
                                                        2. Gauru is not much better than Urshul offensive-wise, it's more powerful defensive-wise due to having regeneration.
                                                        3. Wear them down before the fight with poking strikes; bleed out Essence before main strike.
                                                        4. Use action economy. Swarm Uratha in lesser enemies and even Gauru would not be able to keep up with damage piling up.
                                                        5. Use continuous damage. Being poisoned, drowned, on fire and so on reduces regen factor.
                                                        6. Apply debuff tilts/reduce combat dice. Being blinded, knocked down, hammered with debuff Numina and so on reduces Uratha ability to deal damage.
                                                        7. Bring a healing factor of your own. If werewolves cannot deal a lot of damage and you can heal it, you'll be able to either force them into Kuruth or wear them down.
                                                        8. Do not engage werewolves on their terms; avoid melee, apply debuffs and continuous damage, and most importantly, be ready to call it a day and run away faster than werewolves can follow you, especially if you managed to put them into Kuruth.
                                                        9. Silver is a nuclear option. Use it sparingly.
