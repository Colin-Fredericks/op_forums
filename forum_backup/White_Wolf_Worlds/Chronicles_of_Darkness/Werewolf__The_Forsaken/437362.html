    How Do You Kill a Werewolf?

    03-11-2015, 03:49 PM
    So, I don't know if this has come up yet.

    I mainly run werewolf games, rather than play in them, and it has influenced my perception of the game a great deal. Instead of getting overly excited about big changes, I am very cautious as I understand how much I have to know, and understand, to still tell compelling and challenging stories.

    One of my concerns is the healing factor of Gauru form in the new rule set.

    As the form heals all lethal and bashing at the beginning of each turn, I can see it becoming very difficult to hurt a character with 12 health levels, as even two or three monstrous creatures attacking it are going to come out to around 10 or 12 lethal, which then heals completely. That of course, is giving the attackers a lot of credit considering the higher defense in God Machine, the defense vs firearms and bows, and armor (which I think Gauru still has? It's on the character sheet, but not in the book as far as I or my colleague Handriel can tell.)

    I think this is a powerful and exciting edition that could lead to some truly climatic fights. However, once the short Gauru duration wears out, the character to go into Basu-Im and basically keep the form indefinitely in a combat barring some serious running (which is hard, as Gauru are fast, and the new Father Wolf Speed Strength Gift activates for free in Basu-Im).

    So let me postulate three scenarios, and let me know what you think, because I have trepidations that my foes that I create will be little more than speed bumps unless they are earth-shatteringly powerful:

    1. Werewolf on Werewolf action (not the sexy kind) - I have two werewolves fighting. One goes to Gauru, then deathrages. This pulls the other into Deathrage. They are now beating on one another. I don't see a 'fox frenzy' anymore, and so do the two just wail on one another until someones Basu-Im timer clicks out, and dies horrifically?

    2. Werewolf on anything that doesn't do aggravated - I understand that the harrying phase, werewolves actually heal less now than before, but once the Gauru kicks out, things go nuts. My issue here, is if things ever go sour, the werewolf hits the big red button and kills everything. How does an Azlu or beshilu pose a combative threat? Are in oWod with aggravated all day, because nothing else save dropping a building on the werewolf will even slow them down?

    3. Aggravated: So using silver does the trick. Is the system really pushing for more aggravated to be present? I find that kind of disturbing if its the case, but perhaps I'm still locked in 1.0, where you learn 'aggravated is no no'. I guess the idea of having more things do aggravated kind of unnerves me. In addition, I believe it mentions that werewolves do not take aggravated from anything mundane anymore, only supernatural and silver. Is this the case? I have another question about dealing aggravated, but I'll make a separate topic for it.

    I like to run encounters that involve investigation and social antics, and lead up to combats, same as everyone else. I want to make those combats hard pressed and the victory leaving the players thinking DAMN. Reading the book, unless I'm dealing with other Uratha, I get the feeling the end results is Gauru popping noises and switching to Down and Dirty, because, might as well.

    Could I get some help? I've been thinking about this a lot, and can't wrap my brain around it.

          1. With your two werewolves, the other one wouldn't be pulled into death rage unless they were a packmate of the first. The first wouldn't necessarily death rage unless they were in the form for too long. From there, if one of the werewolves is physically superior, they may win the battle. If either have packmates, they would likely turn the tide. Otherwise if your werewolves are merely standing there trading punch for punch, it's possible they'll end up in a stalemate, and maybe next time one of them will try harder.

          2. The healing works per turn, like at the beginning of theirs. Providing there are multiple enemies, it's entirely possible, and even likely, that they'll manage to push things over into aggravated damage, especially since your scenarios rely on an individual werewolf without combat ability. Plus azlu and beshilu can get really big. Just looking at some of their options for Dread Powers in the book, you can see something like Toxic Bite, which can cause penalties to all actions through the Sickness tilt, or damage through the Poisoned tilt. A smart opponent might send in a minion to inflict a tilt like that while waiting, due to their awareness of the gauru form's danger.

          3. Yes, they don't take aggravated from the things that normally cause it. Only silver and supernatural abilities expressly designed to cause aggravated damage.

                                      Tried some test scenarios against Gauru. Four fairly well trained mooks with semi-automatic guns can take one down....

                                      Granted, most of the time they die horrifically, but they often leave 6-7 aggravated damage from roll-over in their wake which is considerable enough for my tastes.

                                      As was said previous, numbers, tactics and powers can shape the tide of the war form onslaught. Ever faced twenty beshilu? No? Yeah neither did the pack next door and they really weren't prepared for it.

                                      Another thing to really remember is intent in combat. Most combat aside from the Sacred Hunt will probably be to accomplish something other than murdering the shit out of people. Werewolves are also good at murdering the shit out of people. This is okay. Any combat where someone in the pack walks away with one aggravated damage or more is a bad time because that shit sticks around.

                                      So if your plan is to run a combat heavy kind of Chronicle, stagger your encounters and hunts, chip away at them and apply tactics that counter the war form. Spirits, Claimed and Hosts should reasonably know what to expect from Uratha, some hunters as well, depending on their flavour of lunacy or hunting methods. Other supernaturals mileage will vary.

                                      Always remember, anyone can kill a Werewolf with a glock if they roll enough tens.
