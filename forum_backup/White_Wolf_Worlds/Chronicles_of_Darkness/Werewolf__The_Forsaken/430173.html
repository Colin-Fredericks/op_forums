    Hit and Run

    03-06-2015, 09:28 AM
    Under Gift of Evasion (Purity) on page 124.

    "This Facet can be used to interrupt an attack action that is aimed at the Uratha. Before the action is taken, the Uratha may immediately disengage and move up to her full Speed away. This alone may foil an attack, but if she is able to move to a location that takes her out of line-of-sight of all of her prey, she then rolls the Facet’s dice pool against the highest Composure of those prey who could previously see her. The Uratha can only interrupt one action per turn in this way."

    A reflexive gift that costs of 1 Essence. Is it me, or is this basically "one extremely cheap perfect defense per turn that also has a chance to render you immune to all other attacks until the beginning of your next turn"? I understand that, to an extent, Werewolves are suppossed to brutally win combat and crush everything underneath, but this Facet seems to completely negate one on one combat as being a thing (unless both people have this Facet, in which case it becomes a game of wiffing until someone runs out of Essence). If the whole Pack has it (likely) then it negates Pack vs Single Giant Epic Monster storylines as well. Unless the Giant Epic Monster attacks with explosions or doesn't need line of sight.

    Am I reading this facet wrong or missing something? If not, has this come up in playtest and how did it work out at the actual table?

                          I would definitely consider adding in the unaware thing as well, if you are trying to make it equal to Celerity. That's arguably a more important balance point then the limited uses per scene. Technically speaking, Hit and Run needs "Once interrupted, the other character must continue his declared action, if it’s still possible. If the action is no longer possible, he takes no action" in there somewhere to function clearly as intended. Otherwise, if you have both books one would deduce that the attacker can opt to just do something else. I would also recommend adding in, "Alternatively, that character’s player may declare his action a dramatic failure and take a Beat." from Celerity simply because, from my experience, it makes players feel better about being autododged when it's used against them.

                          I would assume that "Likewise she cannot move further in a single turn than her Speed would allow." is deliberately left out.

                                                      They get the Shadow Paranoia Condition for a number of turns equal to the Werewolf's Purity renown. This makes them sort of on edge and jumping at shadows, which are full of enemies slavering for their flesh. A -2 penalty to Perception, if they make a failure with it, it's an automatic dramatic failure and they see something out of the corner of their eye and react accordingly. There's also a penalty to Intelligence and Wits rolls, as the paranoia keeps them from thinking rationally.
