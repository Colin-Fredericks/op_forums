                                            Cahalith, Elodoth, and Ithaeur have always had a bit of overlap. Lore-keepers, truth-seekers, and shamans sort of have intrinsically interconnected roles in the large scheme of things. The way I've always sort of internalized it is something along the lines of the Truth-Seeker discovers what is true, the Shaman dissects it for hidden meanings and purpose, the Lore-Keeper weaves it into a story that it can arrange with others into a narrative. I've found it very hard to make a single werewolf character who can mechanically do all three, they each get something exclusive in one way or another that helps but doesn't cover all the bases.

                                            In short you go to an Ithaeur for a textbook, you go to a Cahalith for a novel.

                                            That said I've generally found that there are a number of ways to work with a character's auspice. It can be a straight forward arrangement, the warrior guy gets the warrior auspice and is like "Okay, cool." It can be somewhat confusing, with the warrior guy getting the Scout's sign for instance...did Luna make a mistake or is she trying to tell you something? Do you embrace it or rebel against it? In your rebellion do you discover that it truly is your calling? If you embrace it, does it just never quite click into place? None of these are inherently bad stories to be told.

                                            That being said I wouldn't be comfortable making such a big character decision for a player. I would let the player decide what they want out of their character's auspice, maybe make some suggestions, and run with it in the story.

                                                Generally speaking, the distinction between Cahalith and Ithaeur seems best represented as a diversity of cause versus effect. A Cahalith understands the multitude of ways a given story can be read based on time and tenor, with the effect on the listener varying accordingly. An Ithaeur understands the ways to make a given thing happen, whether by Gift or rite or spiritual favor or mundane solution.

                                                A Gibbous Moon tells a ghost story around a campfire to spook the mortals in preparation for a Hunt, and can spin that same story in a different context to make his pack weep for the dead. He has a versatile cause with widely varying strengths of effect.

                                                A Crescent Moon uses the Rite of Chosen Ground to evoke a resonance of fear in her lands to make the campers afraid, but calls in a favor from a minor spirit of grief she defeated in a test of endurance to lend the right aura of mourning to the funeral. She knows a number of different causes to use to generate a desired effect.

                                                Why? versus How? may be a more succinct way of phrasing it, I suppose.
