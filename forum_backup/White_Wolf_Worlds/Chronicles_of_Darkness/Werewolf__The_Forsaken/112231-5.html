                You can't defeat the Pure by walking up to them and hitting them until they stop moving. You'll quite simply die trying. Their packs are larger than yours. They have stronger, more numerous spirit allies; and they have hate, determination and the absolute certainty that they're fighting for the right side.

                The Pure can be beat by playing it smart. Isolating them from their spirit allies. Stealing or destroying their resources (loci, fetishes, wolf-blooded, money). Cornering part of the pack in places where you can reasonably kill them off. Breaking their hate, taking away their determination and certainty.

                You can't defeat Bale Hounds by walking up to them and hitting them until they stop moving. How would you know who to hit? Bale Hounds often hide among the Forsaken, and it takes surveillance, intuition, persistence and all kinds of social skills and smarts to figure out who the rat in your pack is.

                You can't defeat spirits by walking up to them and hitting them until they stop moving. Have you had a look at the spirit rules? These things are crazy powerful; meaning that the wiser course of action will often be figuring out their weaknesses and how to best exploit them.
                Moreover, a spirit you tear apart will quite simply reform and come back to haunt you once it has gathered sufficient essence. Remember how spirits are reflections of the Flesh.

                Spirits are fought by changing the Flesh so that they lack appropriate sources of essence. Solve the murder case and bring the murder to justice. Stop the pollution. Conquer your fear. Anything else is just a temporary solution.

                You can't defeat Spirit-Possessed by walking up to them and hitting them until they stop moving. How would you know who to kill? The Possessed cannot easily be perceived, and, as with the Bale Hounds, it takes perception and investigative skills to find them.

                And even if you catch them, they'll just laugh at you and possess the next victim, and you'll be left with a dead human at your feet.

                You can't defeat Spirit-Urged by walking up to them and hitting them until they stop moving. Besides the issue of finding them, Urged are Urged because they in some way attracted a spirit. This is their weakness; this is how you can stop a spirit from Urging a victim. A spirit of hate has no reason or desire to Urge someone who, with the help of his pack, has conquered her hate.

                You can't defeat the Hosts by walking up to them and hitting them until they stop moving. Besides the fact that some are crazy powerful in combat, and all of them cause problems in the Shadow that will only make your job harder, even if you manage tearing one of these critters apart, unless you have prepared yourself well, the Shard will quite simply escape, eat the brain/heart of the next guy and continue to cause trouble. The Hosts attack a pack's territory using methods that don't vary a lot, but they're insanely hard to get rid off permanently. They have been designed so as to be persistent and the next time, they'll know you and your methods, and they'll come prepared.

                You can't defeat Spirit-Claimed by walking up to them and hitting them until they stop moving. Well, actually, you can. Spirit-Claimed explicitely serve the purpose of being mostly low-key opponents that you can actually defeat with nothing but your claws. That doesn't change the fact that they come in wildly varying power levels and with wildly varying abilities, making it almost impossible to predict how they'll cause trouble.

                You can't defeat a person by walking up to him and hitting him until he stops moving. Obviously werewolves can, but in a World of Darkness game, and in a setting where consequences matter, killing humans isn't lightly done.

                Have you noticed how the Idigam also follow this pattern? They're too big and nasty to be killed by walking up to them and hitting them with your claws.

                Also; have you noticed that, of the five auspices, only the Rahu is strongly optimized towards being a fighter?

                It almost seems as if all of the game's antagonists were designed, possibly even intentionally , to require the player characters to be smart and use various skills above and beyond those of the Rahu in their hunts. Anything else will get a pack killed very soon.

                Werewolves hunt. Battle is not their primary business.

                Personally, I also believe that running hunts is, plot- and story-wise, more interesting that running fights.

                                                On the relative sympathetic-ness of the Bale Hounds (I enjoy talking about Bale Hounds. I think every Werewolf game should have one of these neat little critters):

                                                Bale Hounds are people. A Bale Hound is every werewolf, on a bad day.

                                                You don't join the Bale Hounds by approaching the gods of sin and asking to join up, you join them by being yourself - on a bad day.

                                                Your grandfather dies. There's grief and there's shock, but there's also rage, rage at the doctors who botched the surgery that should have been a very easy operation, who didn't take the necessary precautions.

                                                The new girl gets the promotion that you have been promised. It's probably simply because she's more attractive than you.

                                                Your wife has stopped sleeping with you. She simply doesn't find you attractive any more. She's probably sleeping around.

                                                Doesn't that make you angry? Doesn't that make you feel wronged? Shouldn't someone pay?

                                                Add to this the emotional, deeply passionate nature of the Forsaken race. We all have felt really, really angry at one point or another in our lives. When werewolves feel angry, they don't feel like hitting someone the way we do, they feel like transforming into 3 meters of raging wolf-death, tearing someone's throat out and drowning him in his own blood.

                                                The Bale Hound philosophy isn't scary because it's about alienation from humanity or worship of Outer Evil, it's scary because it's about human vices, human emotions and human feelings gone wrong. What makes the Bale Hounds tragic, sympathetic figures is that their horror is human horror.
