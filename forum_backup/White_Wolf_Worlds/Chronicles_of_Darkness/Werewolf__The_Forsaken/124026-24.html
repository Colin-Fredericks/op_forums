                    06-16-2014, 03:07 PM
                    Changes in Initiative penalty take place on the next turn (you can't drop a -3 Init weapon and suddenly go before the other guy).

                    Multiple weapons are only addressed in the GMC rules regarding pairs (increase the penalty of the highest one by 1 if you have two).

                    The problem is that unarmed is assumed to not modify Initiative at all. So 'natural weapons' if they fall into 'unarmed' for the purposes of Initiative would just default to no impact on Initiative mods (they don't add a penalty and they don't count towards multiple weapon penalties assuming that readying more than 2 is possible and extrapolates from the existing rules).

                    I'd assume that's why any Initiative bonus to claws got cut. If they count as unarmed for the purposes of Initiative penalties, they're already a step above dual-wielding two 1L weapons (which would have a penalty of -2 or -3 depending what you're using).

                    None of this really gives a reason to claw over bite, but that never really bothered me because wolves don't default to their claws as their primary weapons. Claws are back-up weapons for when biting isn't a good (or available) idea is fine for me. It's not like having those claws costs you anything.

Yes, I know someones going to try to say that real life wolves mostly rely on their teeth or something like that, but that doesn't really feel like an appropriate excuse to me. Every inch of a spirit wolf is supernatural, so to me it seems like when a werewolf shapeshifts and takes on aspects of said supernatural spirit entity that the traits they gain should be supernatural in nature. I would even be fine with none of their traits being considered supernatural since I could reason that even though they're part spirit beast they're bodies, even when shapeshifted, still maintain their mundane human natures. I can even go along with just having their natural weapons be supernatural, since I'd see that as them partially gaining their half spirit natures supernatural hunting tools, and I'd be fine with that. But having just part of it like this feels same game mechanic-y. It's further aggravated by knowing the reason it's in that weird state.

                                For some reason this is one of those little nit picky things that are disproportionately jarring for me. Mechanically it will almost never matter, and in the situations where it does come up it is neither overpowered nor underpowered regardless of what part of their body is or isn't considered supernatural. It's just something that nags at me every time I think about the Idigam Chronicles. It's also easily houseruled, so regardless it's unlikely to ever be a real problem, but holy hell does it aggravate me for some reason.

                                    Werewolves aren't spirits. They're beings that are part spirit. They also aren't more or less spirit in any given form than others.

                                    I think you're too hung up on the word "supernatural" here. Vampires and werewolves are supernatural. If you're going to interpret this are broadly as you seem to be, a vampire's punch should be a supernatural attack and a werewolf in Dalu kicking you should be a supernatural attack. Because they're attacks made by something that is only possible because of supernatural power.

                                    The point of designating something as a supernatural attack/weapon/etc. rather than an attack/etc. used by a supernatural creature is that this attack/weapon is supernaturally special on its own.

                                    And with that comes the simple point that the line between supernatural attack/weapon only needs to follow the arbitrary and fictional logic of the people writing the game. If the writers decide that bites are supernaturally potent due to the symbolism of wolves and claws aren't? Then that's how it works. Just like elbows, fists, and headbutts aren't special either.
