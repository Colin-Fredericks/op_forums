                Originally posted by nofather View Post
                Some players don't role-play things their characters should be feeling.
                That is an issue to be discussed between games, establish expectations, etc, etc. Not something to be done with pavlov beats (all of this is in my humble opinion, of course).
                Originally posted by nofather View Post
                Conditions gives incentive to do so. Removing them takes them back to playing the character sheet, not the character.
                My anecdotal experience is the opposite. Keeping them takes the player out of the character.
                Originally posted by nofather View Post
                And the biggie. Conditions give players the power to determine how they get Beats and Experience. Removing that places it solely in the hands of the Storyteller, who may deem that they're 'advancing too quickly' and stop rewarding them for the kinds of things that had given them rewards in previous sessions.
                Fulfill Aspiration -- Long Term Aspirations are pretty solidly in the hands of your storyteller, but Short Term Aspirations give players the power to determine how they get Beats and Experience.
                Resolve Condition -- In dispute here.
                Persistent Condition -- I am perfectly cool with this one. Gives the players the power to determine how they get Beats and Experience.
                Dramatic Failure -- Literally a choice the player makes. Gives the players the power to determine how they get Beats and Experience.
                Lethal Damage -- 50/50 here. Players can always turn social encounters into fights and risk getting this. Or go looking for fights. Kind of playing with fire. Werewolves have an easier time of it though.
                End of Session -- Automatic.

                You are free to disagree with me here, but I do not see any shortage of players being able to determine how they get Beats and Experience. Removing beats from resolving conditions does reduce the frequency this happens by the technical definition of reduce, but there is no great deficit created. I mentioned previously that I was in favor of rewarding beats for good roleplaying / good ideas. I actually believe that rewarding good roleplaying / good ideas is a far more effective way to get a player to focus upon her character instead of the sheet (did you know they actually removed the ability to gain beats for roleplaying?) But if your players are concerned about favoritism, then I might encourage you to double the beats gained from some of the other methods listed here (except End of Session). This is especially neat with aspirations and dramatic failures! I'm always open to more options.

                                    Also, if a group is seriously having problems with Condition-to-Beat rates, consider using the GMCRU Beats guideline instead of the ones the 2e core books have used were you can only get one Resolution Beat per scene. There's no incentive to horde them in significant quantity, there's more incentive to spend them since letting them pile up doesn't do you any good, there's far less pressure on expectations (both on players and STs) when everyone's getting one Resolution per scene, and "beat machine" builds aren't really a big deal and/or are most effective if used for the whole group (exceptional successes can be used to give the other PCs Conditions so they have something to resolve because again hording them for yourself doesn't do you much).

                                    Heck, it pretty much eliminates your whole list of problems because it breaks down to, "expect 1 Beat per scene from resolving Conditions." so player and ST expectations are easily aligned; esp. with a simply "don't be a dick" move.
