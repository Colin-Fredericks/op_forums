    Bhurudur: Sea Singers

    10-15-2015, 11:49 AM
    Join us in the Eternal Song

    “It’ll only feel strange the first few times,” she told him.

    He replied only with an awkward grimace. That’s what she said the last time and the time before that. How many times did he have to go through this before this all stopped being weird?

    She grabbed hold of his hand and motioned it over to touch an incoming wave as it battered against the beach.

    The icy cold waters ran over his fingers, sending a cool chill up his arm, the water almost unnaturally cold for this deep into summer. He shook his head. “... think we can set this asside for another time?”

    The woman’s lips twisted into a playful smile. “You know you want it,” she teased.
    Truth be told, he didn’t actually know if he did. On the one hand, this entire setup was always awkward, always felt forced on his part. On the other hand… he gulped and shook his head, beads of sweat trickled down his forehead. “I’m just, not sure about it is all.”
    “Then let me help you.” She grabbed hold of his hand once again and pulled him with a strength that he hardly expected of a woman her size.
    He yelped, attempting to pull himself free, but her grip was too strong and he couldn’t get a goot foothold to resist. He was dragged out into the beach, past the sandbars, past the shallows, past the safety nets and out into waters that were way out of his depth.

    At that point, he couldn’t muster up the courage to let go, lest he drown himself. Still, the young woman kept pulling her deeper and deeper. But eventually she grew tired of staying out on the surface, and dove beneath the waves.

    He could only hold on, keeping his eyes closed to stop the salty waters from stinging his eyes.

    “Don’t worry, relax,” he heard her speak. But at this depth, that would have been impossible for human vocal cords. He didn’t want to relax, did not want to take part of this ritual. Yet he knew sooner or later, he was about to get involved whether he wanted to or not.

    It came to him, slowly at first, a sound that off in the distance felt like a vibrant heartbeat. Yet as he descended, he could hear that it was more than one continuous rhythm. Voices from all over joined together forming a perfect harmony of sound.

    “Join us,” came the woman’s plea.

    “Sing with us,” came the sound of the endless concert.

    He felt himself breaking down on the inside, submitting to the power of the group. He couldn’t restrain himself, he couldn’t hold back the urge that bubbled up within him.

    He opened his eyes, no longer feeling the sting of the salty depths, not that he used them to see everything around him. He didn’t struggle to move in the water, his hands having become more suited to the depths. He no longer felt it so difficult to hold onto his breath. He saw her once more, this time in a different shape.

    She clicked a greeting.

    And he answered back.

    Images flashed into his mind, the writhing mass of the pod gathered around him in concert, visible in the darkened moonlight, all singing. He joined in the song, a wordless welcoming to those who found themselves at sea, free of uncertainty and doubt.




    ======
    Life began in the oceans and to the oceans does life return. The notion that the sea is the originator of life is an old one, dating back atleast as far as Sumerian Mythology’s Tiamat mothering a race of gods and possibly even older. Recent times saw the rise of evolution popularizing the seas having mixed chemicals and elements together into a primordial ooze from which the first microbial life forms arose. For the Bhurudur, their connection to the sea is far stronger.

    The Bhurudur speak many tales of how they came to be, but the most widely accepted one is one that sets them far apart from many other shapeshifters who were in a sense “born” to inherit that part of their nature. The Bhurudur believe in a being called Amahan-Kalifillu, or Mother of the Primordial Waters, the greatest Spirit of the very Oceans of the planet, whom they claim to be the forgotten creator of life itself. In an ancient time, it was said that she grew upset that though she was the one who gave birth to life in totality, she had never once been considered an equal to the Celestial Incarna, often with most of her would be children not knowing she exists. Their story is that when Amahan-Kalifillu wept and raged over her lack of appreciation, some men and women came and sang to her, quelling her anguish. Because of this, she invested a portion of herself into her faithful, making them better able to return to her and to further sing.

    In modern times, the Bhurudur continue to venerate the ancient spirit, offering their songs to the depths. However, what exactly is sung about differs from individual to individuals. While it is often expected for each Bhurudur to sing to Amahan, many believe the spirit desires something more than simple praise. Each Bhurudur adds their own embellishment in their song, speaking about personal events, conflicts, and depressingly, failings towards the ancient spirit. Many feel that the best way to appease their primordial mother, they must offer up a song containing their experiences, and so they must seek them out. Similar to werewolves, they are driven by a purpose, a sort of hunt, but unlike them, their targets are often a different kind of ephemeral.

    Much like other shapechangers, the Bhurudur begin their lives as human beings. Unlike other shapechangers, their First Change is not a sudden dramatic event, but rather a calling and a choice. The would be Bhurudur would hear a siren like call, lulling them over to go out into the deepest part of closest natural body of water, yet at the same time, they know perfectly well that heeding it would have major consequences. Should the recipient refuse, the call would bother them for a length of times, sometimes up to months, before eventually the calls stop coming and the recipient is no longer eligible. Should they accept, the subject becomes bombarded by noise and whalesong while he slowly drown and undergoes their equivalent to the First Change. Once he or she finally gives in and joins in the song, does he realize his own voice is able to contribute to the group.

    The Bhurudur gather together in concerts when they sing together, merging their songs to create a masterpiece greater than the sum of its parts. Concerts are often temporary, lasting no longer than several hours; it is also preferred to be in dolphin form in the ocean when in a concert, though it isn’t required; some prefer flooded pools or using human form.

    Outside of concerts, they gather in pods to pursue their quests and hunts and often consist of members who spend much of their time together. It is often for pods to break up temporarily to join separate concerts, largely as different concerts will often have different topics or requirements, such as Tribe specific concerts.

    Right, so this is my latest project that I am working on. I’ve got a good chunk of fluff and setting material up already, but I need some help in how I want to handle this.

    I’m currently lacking:
    -Tribal information. Not sure how I want to handle it.

    -Auspice information. Considering using tidal fluctuations as Auspice equivalents. Though I feel that’s not significant enough.


    -Form information. While the near man and full dolphin equivalents are more or less decided upon, I’m wondering how I want to handle the hybrid and near dolphin forms..

    I am considering that unlike werewolves, the Bhurudur would be able to have a ranged attack option in place of regeneration in Hybrid form; specifically, the power to use sonar as a weapon.

    Also considering hybrid or near dolphin having some method to “swim” near to the ground to allow it to be used outside of the water. Not true swimming, more like low ground hovering. At Least, assuming Hybrid form can’t just walk as a part of me is kind of wondering if it will be a man dolphin hybrid or something more aquatic. I do want to avoid the problem where 2 / 5 forms are too situational or atleast make it so only one form cannot be used out of the water.

      The near-whale form could be picked from the many ancestral forms whales went through, from the shallow-water ambulocetids to early versions of baleen whales, dolphins, etc.: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_cetaceans

      As for the hybrid form, I think something semi-aquatic/almost amphibious in origin would be interesting. It could resemble a mermaid or merman, but with odd, almost-clumsy legs that split from a powerful, bone-breaking tail whenever they emerge from the water. A sonar attack is also definitely plausible, as it is so vital to cetaceans and their way of life.

      The only issue I see here is handling size in their animal forms and hybrid forms. How big do these guys get? Do they become no bigger than the young of certain species (such as the size of a young orca), or do they limit themselves to smaller species, like pilot whales and porpoises?
