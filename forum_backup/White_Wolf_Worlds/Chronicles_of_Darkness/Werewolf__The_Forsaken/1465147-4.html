    It is more complicated than that. Depends on editorial oversight, how much parallel work is done, reuse of text, schedules of every stage of the job, etc.

    Given the size of OPP and the amount of work being simultaneously done, I'm not surprised. Not exactly elated, but not surprised. It isn't the writers fault, precisely, as management is key here. But this is no easy feat, much less cheap.

    cWoD was much worse. It is easy to ignore when you had years to get used to each book and rule, but it was way worse. 1st and 2nd edition stuff were veritable nightmares on organization, clarity and consistency, hugely due to a period in which the authors followed a design theory (outdated, thank goodness) that deemed it all irrelevant.

    Revised was far better, but couldn't handle properly the legacy of inconsistent material. 20th An fared better, but still have a lot of problems due to legacy and simply being a huge endeavor.

                            You can regenerate a wound without removing what's inside it. In fact, it is important to remove blades, shrapnel and the like from wounds precisely because of that. Regeneration doesn't necessarily mean all the tissue going back to place (although it preferably goes) as much as it means stopping blood loss and closing the wound as possible.

                            I'd say with a bear trap the Uratha would regenerate as normal, but be stuck in the trap because of it. It probably means just they stay pinned, but may need to retake the damage to remove it. If the damage applied a Tilt, this should also stay while trapped as the trap prevents specific tissue from reforming properly.

                                    Originally posted by Bunyip View Post
                                    Werewolf regeneration isn’t pretty, either. Blow the back of a Uratha’s head off with a shotgun and it’ll fall to the ground. Then, when its Essence healing kicks in, tendrils of blood and brain will reach out to each other. Disgusting slurping sounds happen. The gore pulls fragments of skull back together and then drags them back to what’s left of the head. Weird grinding sounds occur as jagged bone bits adjust themselves like a self-assembling jigsaw, trying to work out how they fit back together. And when most of it’s done, flesh and bone start growing into the gaps, leaving some splatter on the ground.
                                    God damn, this is evocative. I love it.

                                    On topic, a quick skim of the books surfaces this info on reflexive actions:

                                    A reflexive action is the sort of thing you don’t even need to think about doing.
                                    An instinctual task that takes no appreciable time, such as reacting to surprise or noticing something out of the corner of your eye.
                                    An example of a reflection action I've heard before is 'breathing', but I can't find a quote for that in the books at the moment. Either way, though - I'd allow the reflexive shift into Gauru or Essence spend on regeneration even while the character was bleeding out, conscious or otherwise, and I believe that's supported by RAW. If you don't have to 'think' about doing it, then you don't have to make a conscious choice, and people's bodies act on subconscious instinct without conscious thought all of the time. Certainly one of those instincts for Uratha is going to be "heal this so I don't die."

                                                        Bunyip is one of the authors and he already stated that Essence expenditure can't be decided upon once unconscious, but can be in the very last conscious thought (between taking damage and rolling for consciousness).

                                                        So we have Word of God on the matter. Is it the same as RAW? No, but helps to define RAI at least. And I personally find this quite a stretch of interpretation. Not every Reflexive Action is reflexive in the sense of not using conscious thought, especially if it involves a decision. And I can't equate the decision of breathing to the decision of expending Essence.

                                                        Point being, at the moment the player may decide to spend it, but if the player decide not to for absolutely any reason, there is no point in letting the character choose freely a later moment to do so.

                                                            Originally posted by Newb95 View Post
                                                            (unless my players manage to figure the werewolf weakness to silver but its not going to be easy).
                                                            Why would it be hard to figure the weakness of a werewolf?

                                                            The setting isn't built upon the supposition that werewolves never made it to pop culture, that's part of what makes their weakness a big thing.

                                                            Silver is obvious, what isn't as obvious is how to get it. You can't simply go to a guns shop and ask silver bullets, that's not a thing. Silver is terrible for making weapons and takes a lot of specialized techniques to do so, because of its differences from true weapon-grade metals.

                                                            I suggest you take a look at threads about silver in the Apocalypse subforum, it is a very interesting discussion, but the gist of it is that if your players don't put a lot of effort to it there will be no silver bullets, much less a silver bear trap. Just a bunch of jewels and silverware that will cost a lot more than cause damage.

                                                            Oh, one last thing about the bear trap: metal don't smell. Metallic scent is actually due to a surface reaction between a metal and our natural oil, but a clean metal have no scent. I'd say that a Science roll with an appropriate penalty/amount of successes would let a character figure out that you can hugely reduce the trap's smell if you clean it thoroughly and then manipulate with clean gloves.
