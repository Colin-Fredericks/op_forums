The PDF is $20, and I'd say there is way more than that in world building, myth, and setting. I'd suggest that if you enjoy reading it then it's not wasted money. That is up to you to decide though.

                            Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                            So, I'm getting a lot of "pick up W:tF 2ed", but I need to point out that until Onyx Path books are sold in stores again, that is NOT happening. I already bought 1ed and never use it, NO WAY am I buying a non-refundable PDF with no guarantee that I won't be wasting my money.

                            Well, to be to fair you went into a forum where everyone is a WtF fan. Of course we would recommend the product to you especially since the majority of us love the change.

                            Now, I understand that whether or not you get use out of the book is a big issue, because ultimately, no one wants to waste money. However, consider this. Even if you don't like the setting of the book, you can jury rig the entire game to play Apocalypse with some relatively easy conversion. The thing comes with its own internal core rule book so you don't need to waste cash getting the new Chronicles of Darkness book if you don't want to invest in it too much. So purchasing the book for the mechanics is a valid choice.




                            In any case, the merits of the Forsaken core are that ultimately, I'd argue that it let's you be a werewolf better than Apocalypse does. Now, I know that may bother you an Apocalypse fan, but hear me out.

                            By your own words, Apocalypse puts you in the role of a Defender of Gaia, one of a chosen many that exists to protect the world. You're given a cosmic charge and you fight against forces of corruption of your world in an effort to try to fix it. The scope is global and you work towards the goal of defeating a cosmological force with your brethren all over the world. Forgive me if I may, but consider... how many other fictional or folklore works other than Apocalypse do that?


                            In constrast, Forsaken is a little more humble. You are a hunter, because you inherited a fraction of an ancient hunter spirit's power. You are driven to pursue your goals and what you hunt is ultimately up to you; whether it be for flesh, power... enjoyment against foes both in the real and spiritual realms. You fight against others of your own kind for territory because territory is a resource, a sign of prestige, and ultimately a tool to help you select targets. You are not a border patrol guard against spirits, no, you chase spirits that get into your territory because ultimately, it's yours and if they're going to be there, they have to follow your rules or scat.

                            Aside from the spiritual backdrop, the idea of Werewolf the Forsaken 2e is much more commonly found in werewolf media and symbolism: the idea of the wolf as the hunter and the master of its territory. When you play the game, this is the role you play, a wolf.

                            I will be the first to admit I might have been overly harsh and judgemental against Apocalypse in the above. It has its merits and it appeals to a different style of play than Forsaken does, but as I said, I do not think Apocalypse portrays you playing a werewolf as cleanly as Forsaken does. You have animalism in both games, that's true, but that's something nearly all of the World of Darkness games can get, the ability to turn into an animal.

                            My statement is thus, does Forsaken cling closer and play true to the ideal of being a werewolf more than Apocalpyse does? And if Forsaken is closer to the ideal, would you, as a self proclaimed fan of werecreatures, consider that a valid reason to purchase the book despite lacking a few of the trappings of Apocalpyse, namely the global scope, the cosmic forces, and the lack of metaplot?

                                                            I've thought long and hard about how to explain what I like about Forsaken when we both come from such different places with what we enjoy or don't enjoy in these two games. I think I've finally come up with something though. You've mentioned you're a fan of the original changing breeds from Apocalypse. What I loved about Changing Breeds has been turned into the entire theme of Forsaken.

                                                            In Apocalypse, back in the beginning the Garou lost control of their inner nature and began the War of Rage against the other Changing Breeds. Because of this mistake they turned their allies against them and broke apart the alliance the Gaia herself had created. It was a terrible horrible mistake that may have doomed the entire world. Except the Garou didn't give up and wring their hands in despair. They did their very best to fix the problem they had created. They took on the duties of the kindred that they slayed, they tried to do things that they'd never been meant to do and they kept at it for millenia. To a lesser extent you can see the same thing in the Imperium, where werewolves hunted mankind to keep the population low but only succeeded in turning mankind against Gaia as well.

                                                            I think that this was a really valuable lesson to include in Apocalypse and it really fit with the environmental themes of that book. After all, we as humans have made terrible mistakes in preserving the environment. We've wiped out species and disrupted the ecological balance of the world. It was a good lesson in how to not dwell on our failure but instead to look around us and start solving the problems that we can solve.

                                                            In Forsaken, this drama took center stage in the death of Father Wolf. The Forsaken were given an impossible choice. The human side of them told them to love their father, the wolf side of them told them to challenge a weak leader. This internal conflict is the new theme of Werewolf for the WtF editions. Now instead of struggling against the external foe of the Wyrm, the true challenge for any Werewolf is now an internal struggle about being divided between Human and Wolf, Flesh and Spirit. Much like the Garou (and unlike the Pure), the Forsaken didn't give up when faced with this impossible challenge. They challenged Father Wolf, as they had to do, but the important part is that they also understood that they would have to pay the price and seek atonement for this action.

                                                            Everything the Forsaken are doing today is continuing this ancient atonement, seeking forgiveness not by endlessly apologizing or dwelling on the past but instead by moving forwards and doing what they can to fix it. Likewise their true enemy isn't spirits, ghosts, shards, the claimed or even the Idigam. The true enemy is their own divided nature and the eternal challenge of seeking Harmony.

                                                            I think the most important thing about Werewolf never changed and that's the lesson that you never give up.

                                                            Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                                            I'm not trying to be more than factual with this, but the X20 books are not a modern rule-set. They're (so far at least) minor updates of the same rule-set from the 90s. W20 has some major improvements in it, but it isn't a new edition that tries to apply lessons learned in 20 years of design to the old games; it's an omnibus that collects Apocalypse core material in one tome with some tweaks here and there.
                                                            It's not that I disagree with you on the facts at all, I just interpret them a little differently. I agree that they were very conservative about changing the rules because they were attempting to preserve something that people loved rather than recreating it anew. They definitely adopted a policy of "if it ain't broke don't fix it" but I still think you can see the twenty years of experience used in recognizing the places where it was broken and finding the best possible way to fix the problems while changing as little as possible.

                                                            While it's not what I'm looking for personally (thus why I play Chronicles of Darkness now), for someone like our OP who is very much in love with the original line but wants some cleaned up systems it could potentially be perfect. Anyway, I thought it worth mentioning just on the off chance that our OP had never heard of them. I know when they first came out I thought there was no point in buying them because I still have my original World of Darkness books. It wasn't until a friend of mine showed me V20 that I realized that they'd made some fixes and streamlined the system.
