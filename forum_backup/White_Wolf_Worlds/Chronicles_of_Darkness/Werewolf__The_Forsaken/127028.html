    Ex-Bale Hound mentor

    05-06-2014, 06:42 PM
    In my planned Werewolf game I put my newly hunted and First Changed PCs against Bale Hounds pack that rules the territory of small Swedish town and nearby villages. Maeljin servants are all representing Deadly Sins and they expelled one of them and replaced her for better werewolf on her place, especially, as she was having doubts and “gone to soft”. I think to make her mentor for PCs – after few months of “banishment” she return to semi-normal Harmony ( I think about maybe 5 ), contacted with Forsaken Firstborn to return to the Tribe, and now she wants cold revenge on Bale Hounds, even if it's partially righteous one. She will hunt and “mark” the Wolf-Blooded people to awoke Wolf in them, so they could fight with Bale Hounds with her. She's a bit manipulative and really do anything to exact her fury – even become one and make more of new Forsaken.

    And here are question I want you help me answer for this game:

    1. In what Maeljin service she should be in her Bale Hounds times? She really is manipulative, love “being the wolf” and hunting, and treat others like her toys often. I was thinking she should work for Carnala, but after reading about her servants I don't think she would gone for it – she isn't so much self-destructive. She love to “have fun” but don't want the all senseless violence. Or get caught for that mater. So what are your though and propositions on this?

    2. What Auspice she should have as mentor figure? From my little knowledge of system, I can't decide. For me she could be First Changed under any moon, Any ideas here?

    3. What Tribe she should join after her return to Forsaken? There was advised to me she should be Blood Talon after she want's to hunt on other werewolves, but I'm not sure here. I could see her also as Hunter in Darkness, for her fondness of “pure wolf experience”. But the new Tribes spoiler point they aren't for inter-werewolves hunts. What do you see on this?

    4. Bonus question – what would be great, twisted name for town in English? I was thinking about something like Small Heaven, to contrast with all evils and sins being center of chronicle. Any ideas?

      1) Go for the one that serves Greed. I can't remember the name, and I might have watched one too many Game of Thrones episodes, but there are a number of greedy people who are excellent, excellent manipulators. Greed is destructive as well — there's a phenomenon among real wolves where they'll go into a fenced-in flock of sheep to kill something for food, but then end up killing a chunk of the flock.

      2) Gishtahus are sociopaths by nature, prone to violence and whose entire Auspice blessing revolves around being a martyring cannibal. Some manage to control their impulses, but I can see one being a Svengali to another wolf. You'll want to look them up in Signs of the Moon.

      3) Here's the issue: it's very, very likely your wolf will remain a Ghost Wolf, and not even the Lunes themselves will tolerate her. There's a section in Blasphemies that compares rehabilitating a Bale Hound as equivalent to trying to let Jeffrey Dahmer be forgiven and let into normal society again — Bale Hounds. Are. Depraved. They can just as evil and horrific as the the acts of oWoD's Black Spiral Dancers, though the Dancers are a tad more off the evil end than they are. You're likely to have your character be the target of a number of siskur-dah rather than be accepted.

      That being said, something that might be interesting is a pack of ex-Bale Hounds who take care of each other and are trying to climb back up the Harmony slope.

      4) Twisted town name? What about Fell? According to the Encyclopedia of Arda's "Old and Rare Words" section, "fell" has three meanings:

      1 merciless, terrifying
      2 animal's hide
      3 moorland hill
      Things like "Eden" or "Paradise" or "Small Heaven" are really, really unsubtle. It can add to the, "Oh, *BLEEP*," factor of a scene if you give foreshadowing/etymology to a place that doesn't kick in until later. It's a well-used horror trope that the quiet little town has a cutesy little name.
