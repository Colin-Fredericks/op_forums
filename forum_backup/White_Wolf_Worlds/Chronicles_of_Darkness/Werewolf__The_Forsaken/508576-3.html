            On the thematic appropriateness of the unihar in the setting:

            "What is our ban, boy?"

            "Ban? But... I thought only spirits got those!"

            "Oh but we are half spirit ourselves... Like Father Wolf before us, we cannot defend ourselves from our murderous offspring. Since we aren't fully spirit, and bans are spiritual things, this applies to our gifts and rites. Thus why our gifts don't work on our offspring."





            Two people mate, the resulting offspring receives one randomly chosen gene from each of its parents' gene pair. What you get is still based on the genes of the mother and the father.

            Two werewolves mate, the resulting offspring is 500 pounds of raging wolf-death with an unstoppable thirst for genocide.

            Lycanthropy is recessive. Every living werewolf bears human blood (lots of it) in addition to what little is left of Father Wolf's lifeblood. With every continuing generation, Father Wolf's bloodline is further diluted.

            Now, if two werewolves mate, what they should produce is a child with a lot more of Father Wolf's blood than what's common these days, right? Instead .. we all know know what happens.

            Makes you think about the nature of lycantrophy and of Father Wolf, doesn't it?

                                            Originally posted by Krobeles View Post
                                            It might just be my memory that isn't as well excercised in this regard as I assumed it was, but to my memory, they were never really "a hefty portion of the game".
                                            The general impression I've gotten off of Werewolf, isn't that its about love dramas and sensual play (which, honestly, is a point very much in favor of removing the Unihar), so they're only really there if anyone wants to explore those themes in the first place. Otherwise, the characers just dont have sex with other Werewolfs, and it doesn't come up.
                                            I really didn't expect this thread to go beyond the yes/no answer it seemed to have asked for. Since it has, though, it's been on my mind a lot.
                                            My wife miscarried our first child. Given that we were both really excited to have kids, this was a really really traumatic experience for us. It's not something I'm chomping at the bit to role-play an extreme version of.
                                            Now, I get the whole "lets play out the hard things in life" idea. It makes sense from certain perspectives and can make for some excellent drama. And facing the things that have been hard in your life can be therapeutic.
                                            Here's the problem with the Unihar, though. You're right; they weren't a big part of the game. You give someone the 1-minute sell (or probably even the half-hour sell) on the game, and you're not going to say "oh and by the way you can't mate with other Werewolves in this game because it'll make a murdering rage beast that will come and kill you both. And the only way to stop it early is to kill the mother. Even it she was a rape victim".
                                            Now the reason that's the problem with them is that the issues this dredges up are also not surface issues. If you know a rape victim, you don't necessarily know that you know a rape victim. I've been on this forum for several months now and posted several hundred times and I don't think anyone else here knew I had been personally touched by a problem pregnancy.
                                            You add those two hidden things together and you get bombs. Werewolf is a cool game where you get to tap the things that go bump in the night on the shoulder and then murder them. There's a lot of apeal to that. But to implant it with this weird sub-theme where if you want to play it and your character does have a relationship with another werewolf, a good pregnancy will never come to pass leads to emotionally dangerous territory.
                                            And Werewolf is supposed to be a sensual game. Wolf senses are hyper-driven, and that's called out a lot, so it should lead to emotionally heady, visceral stories. To make all of those stories lead to "your children will not be your children and you must be killed for being pregnant" isn't good.

                                            They added lots of better options in the 2nd edition book. There's a whole section on dealing with pregnancy, including persistent Conditions for the various trimesters. They make specific mention that, given the supernatural nature of the setting, male characters can get pregnant if they want to. They specifically say to Storytellers "don't put the baby in real jeapordy unless your players want to play that story. Let the player keep playing their character."
                                            This all goes a long way towards living up to the "Don't Say No" storytelling component. As interesting an antagonist as the Unihar might be, their creation at every Werewolf-on-Werewolf pregnancy did more to quash story lines than it did to make new ones. And in a very player-violent way.

                                            I am very much behind the developers' decisions and statements regarding them. And I'm all for the people in this thread putting them back in for their various different reasons and with their various new origins. If you're leaving in their original origin, though, tell your players ahead of time and get their sign-off. Don't kill children just because its the rules.

                                                Have I mentioned that my single biggest suggestion when bringing back the Unihar is to not tie them to werewolves mating with other werewolves? “Werewolves must not mate with werewolves” is a holdover from WtA, and should be left there, the way WtF1e took pains to exclude werewolves mating with wolves from the game.

That keeps the original intent of “the werewolf conducted a forbidden mating, and the result is a Ghost Child” while removing the unfortunate implications of it being a bad thing to mate with someone who is your equal.
