    The Wolf Must Hunt: What do you want to see in Idigam Chronicles?

    11-01-2013, 04:17 PM
    With Werewolf: The Forsaken receiving its own Chronicle treatment, what do you want to see in Idigam Chronicles? Lots of powerful new foes like the Idigam? More focus on the dual-duality of Man/Wolf, Flesh/Spirit? More cowbell? What's on your mind for this, and what would make an already great game even better?

      Here are some of the ideas I posted on the other forum. I'd rather not lose them, so here they are, hunting in a new territory.

      • Wolf. Emphasize it. This is one of the things I love that Werewolf: The Forsaken did, really putting the "wolf" in "werewolf." I'd take it a bit further, emphasizing instincts, primal animism and the savagery of nature in the World of Darkness. Not just running around the woods, mind. On both sides of the Gauntlet even in cities is a rich ecosystem of predators, prey, territories and struggles — and I mean humans, spirits, werewolves and other things.
      • Abominations. No, not vampiric Lupines. Stuff like magath should be more common in this unbalanced post-Urfarah world. I might make it easier for them to escape into the material world. Wolf packs hunt large, dangerous prey. Werewolf packs hunt large, dangerous supernatural prey.
      • Magic. One of the big advantages of werewolves over spirits is the versatility of their magic, born of Flesh and Spirit. They adapt. They're fluid. They're shapeshifters — Luna is ever-changing, and werewolves inherit some of mama's magical prowess. They can learn from spirits, they can develop their own tricks, they are dangerous in that way.
      • The Shadow. I love it. Looooove it. Emphasize it more right from the get-go, with detailed information on how things work there. If it was up to me, I'd change some of the dynamic. Most spirits wouldn't automatically hate werewolves, and Forsaken would in turn be more powerful overall. Mid-rank spirits are still a big threat, and higher-level ones can't be beaten by brute force, but even weaker spirits would be more populous and impossible to control through savagery alone. No pack could hope to control their territory just by killing everything they felt like it and browbeating everything into submission. Same thing goes for spirits, too.
      • The Shadow, pt. 2. Make it integral, as it is in Forsaken, but make it more apparent in what you do and how it fits into a territory and your chronicle. Explore how werewolves gain power and influence in the mortal world by gaining it in the spirit world, and how they grow into lords of their domain through such efforts.
      • Savage fury. Werewolves are hunters, yes. They don't need to be capable of individually tearing apart everything in the world. Still, make them stronger, deadlier, a scary-ass thing to face even one-on-one. Facing a whole pack? You'd better bring it, because they're gonna tear you up. For Gauru in particular, this means either making it usable all the time or giving it much more of an impact in the short while it's used. I favor the latter.

      Oh, another thing I think I'll suggest: shapeshifting should be automatic under normal circumstances. Werewolves are all about shapeshifting. It's what they do. Cutting down on unnecessary rolls works well for play, and also it removes a potential denial of getting to be cool. I mean, where is the fun in wanting to change forms into a wolf and failing because you failed the roll? Werewolves shouldn't have to worry about that except under extreme duress. Otherwise, I think it should just be automatic, though it'd still take an instant action unless Essence is spent. And probably not automatic into Gauru form, but that depends on what ends up happening with Gauru. Partial changes should, I think, still require a roll, because that's a lot trickier.

      And I mean extreme by "extreme duress." Werewolves are no strangers to hunting and combat. Even normal fights and such shouldn't give you so much trouble that you can't instinctually shapeshift. Guys blasting off silver rounds at you while a train thunders by, deafening your lupine senses? Okay, that works. And that's when you spend Essence for a little something extra, because you can't afford the time it takes to shapeshift normally, anyway.

      On Requiem and Masquerade equivalents:

      Reason: This is the face of reason, the influence of Man. Werewolves let this side of their existence guide them in the mortal world, because Man's world is the domain of Reason. It's how inhuman predators keep from falling into unthinking savagery, and how the Uratha use their unique heritage to their advantage. Clever and adaptable is Man the Monkey, and werewolves use that ingenuity to master their world in ways spirits cannot.

      When a werewolf lets Reason find a solution to a problem brute force would only aggravate, she gains a point of Willpower. When committing terrible acts that defy the Harmony of the predator, she regains all spent Willpower points.

      Instinct: The Wolf lurks just beneath the surface and its instincts are strong. Survive, prosper, hunt. A werewolf is born on not one but two axes, Man and Wolf, Spirit and Flesh. Both her Wolf side and her spiritual side are primal, connected to the ancient heartbeat of the world. Wise werewolves learn to trust their instincts, honed over millennia, making them the finest hunters the world has ever known.

      When an Uratha embraces her instinctual side and rejects the tools of Man to overcome a problem, she gains a point of Willpower. If instead she lets instinct dominate her mind in the desperation of survival, she regains all spent Willpower points.

      You are a creature of not just duality, but two kinds of duality set along two different axes: Man and Wolf, Spirit and Flesh. It'd be a shame not to play that up!

          Here's my list, copied from the old forums:


          Splats:

          I'd like to see Auspices reworked to resemble core werewolf archetypes; much like how Venture are the lordly Count Dracula types of vampire and Deeva are the sexy vampires. I would phrase this as Luna giving a Blessing/Curse to each werewolf. It really depends on your point of view (but Forsaken typically say that they're blessings).

          The Y-Splat would be your role in the pack, and would strongly resemble the current Asupices. This does mean that each Archetype would have to be broad enough to cover all five roles, so some tweaks may be required. E.G. the werewolf as a savage monster driven only to hunt is a strong archetype, but dialling it back just a bit to "boundless passion and energy" creates something that can be that savage beast but also a scout or a diplomat.

          For Lodges I'd like to see them as Werewolf university rather than large projects. You don't join a Lodge, you graduate from a Lodge. In addition graduating from a Lodge will give your entire pack bonuses so they can join in your new fun. For example: The Lodge of the Purifying Atom deal with Wounds, like radiation sterilises medical equipment. Graduates get an awesome wound sterilisation power and a passive defence so they can enter wounds and use it, their pack gets the passive defence so that they can also enter wounds and assist.

          Finally, I would like the pack to have it's own splat separate from the individual member's splat. Tribe could work here. The pack's splat would set the goal for the pack. Since Werewolves theme has the strongest bonds between PCs having an extra splat choice that every PC shares would strengthen that theme.





          Powers:

          I'd like to see Werewolves' powers split into Gifts and Instincts:

          Instincts would be anything archetypically werewolf. At the very least: Hunting, tracking, super-senses, fighting and forming a pack. Probably more. A Werewolf can learn instincts through practice and experimentation or being shown by an older werewolf. Instincts also let all the people who want less Spirit in there werewolves hit the important archetypical powers without ever speaking to spirit without making Spirits any lesser for people who like them.

          Gifts would be mostly unchanged, though LOOK would be in charge of writing them I might have more to say about indiivudal Gifts but in general I would want them to come from Spirits, but not just from begging or buying them. There should be at least five different ways to get a Gift. One for each of the (pre-GMC) Asuprices. E.G. Sneak up to Serpent and steal it's skin.

          Rituals: I'd like to see a Blood Sorcery style update to Rituals based around bargaining with the spirits for miracles. It would need to hit the perfect balance, the spirits personality and wishes should matter, but the Werewolves shouldn't feel like they have to beg for magic while vampires and Mages run around doing cool stuff. I'd try and portray Werewolves as spirit millionares, yes they have to haggle the essence price but they control the territory, the loci, so whatever the final price is they can probably afford it. (I've written rules for this already )



          And the big one: Territory

          This is I think the most important one on my list. I think the Chronicles should include both fluff and mechanics to answer the following questions:
          • What is a healthy spiritual ecosystem in harmony with the material?
          • How do you create a healthy spiritual ecosystem.
          • Why do you want a healthy spiritual ecosystem.
          • How do you shape a healthy ecosystem to your packs taste and what are the benefits of doing so?
          • You love your territory's ecosystem. How do you make it better yet the same?
          • What is an unhealthy ecosystem.
          • How is an unhealthy ecosystem created.
          • Why is an unhealthy ecosystem bad.
          • Why would werewolves want an unhealthy ecosystem anyway?

                                          My shopping list is massive on this end (and I don't feel like reiterating it too wholesale from the old forum), but I'm already working on a revamp/overhaul with my Co-ST for the TT and Larp we run to cover those bases. Still lets start with a couple, and go from there later:

                                          -Get rid of Renown. It's a tacked on system. It's a holdover. It's one of the, if not -the- most complained about aspect of W:tF I've ran into. It's unwieldy. What it's presented to do makes little to no sense in the spiritual concept, especially regarding effective rank. As a power cap it makes even -less- sense. It's precepts make little sense in regards to the Shadow and Spirits as a whole. Look at merits like Mantle; Danse Macabre's Status merits and similar. That's more on track. I could get into this much, much more in-depth but lets leave this as is for now and move on.

                                          -Emphasis on Werewolves as hunters of -man- and -beast-. Enough pussyfooting around; they should be a threat to humans, humans shouldn't be a gross exception on the menu of prey items. They're common available prey with Essence, even compared to wolves. Spirits eat things related in concept. Werewolves shouldn't be treating it with quite the level of taboo they are. Flesh addiction much like Vitae addiction should be around. And to emphasize these, the mind-bogglingly -easy- and -abundant- ways werewolves gain Essence should be curtailed. Hunting and eating others should be the focus for this, not farming Essence batteries and hokey rites. Right now there's little incentive or reason for them to be threats to humans, or humans to be relevant. Lets make them what they are; vicious hunters. Things to fear, things humans told horror stories about for a reason. I'm not really getting that enough. There's a reason a lot of new players I find find a disconnect with that game. "Wait, I thought I was playing a werewolf? Why am I a furry spirit cop?". Probably because unlike vampire, it's resemblance to folks expectations is minimal. If you're trying to -sell- the game, you need something more to latch onto coming from the popular culture and mythological references that people consider intrinsic to the critter.

                                          Claire Redfield has posted some ideas that I find help reinforce the tug of instinct and ones manifold nature, but I want it to go further. I also want it to be far more savage in regards to humans and the threats werewolves pose to them (and vice versa for that matter).

                                          -Get rid of Lunacy, flat out. Another bad holdover and system that outright neuters the human threat to werewolves. I've used the system in place from War Against the Pure with other shapeshifters for some time, and even that I've found... isn't necessary. Werewolves should have to be careful about donning their other forms. It should be a -risk- to go into Death Rage above and beyond the usual facts. This build in Jedi-mind-trick is bad baggage. I'd like to see things like this partially dovetailed into the Predatory Aura, and something you specifically target an -individual- with. Making them feel like prey, instinctively fleeing if they get the condition. Mind recoiling in abject terror of being eaten and panicked flight, perhaps with skewed memories due to terror... things like that. "The Secret Must be Kept" is kind of a farce with this in place, and I can safely say the treatment I've seen from Lunacy in regards to Veil issues is that it makes them hardly an issue at all, both from personal experience and the statements of others. Just. Get. Rid. Of. It. There are better ways of treating it beyond an ever present mind-wipe/debuffer, and Predatory Auras are on the right track.

                                          There's quite a few more, but these two are what come first to mind. Things like adjusting the power sets, form tweaks (I'm in the camp that outside of a couple mild adjustments, Gauru form is more than fine as is as far as it's overall stats and duration, though I've seen a couple ways to extend the duration with cumulative Death Rage penalties that I've liked quite a bit), emphasizing Wolf-blooded and diversifying them more, the issues as is between the Pure and Forsaken and quite a few more I'll tackle later.

                                          When things are a bit more complete and ready on our end to playtest, we'll fire them up on these forums for perusal; pretty much everything I want to see in Idigam Chronicles we're already reworking into the game during the wait and in case, you know, it doesn't give us what works best for our games.

                                          Werewolf: the Forsaken Overhaul: A W:tF Shard and House Rules, Pre-Idigam Chronicles and taking different route. Constantly being Updated.
                                          New Bloodlines and Blood and Smoke Updates: Homebrew Bloodlines and updates to prior ones.
                                          Belial's Brood Blood and Smoke: An updated take on the Forsworn.

                                                      Originally posted by the_duck View Post
                                                      Rahuman101, so if you were writing the Idigam Chronicles Gauru form, how would you put the Rage and an unlimited time Gauru form together?
                                                      That is a good question. If I were to do it, I'd use one of two different but similar options.

                                                      Option1: Use the rules in Alec Humphrey's (aka LOOK on the White Wolf forums) Werewolf the Forsaken House Rules google document. With this rule, Gauru can be assumed as often as one likes and be held for as long as one likes. However, being in Gauru form imposes a -3 penalty to resist Death Rage and your Harmony is considered two lower when seeing what stimulis might triigger Death Rage. When Death Rage is triggerd, the player rolls Harmony + Purity Renown to resist. If a Dramatic Failure, you enter Death Rage and attack all present for the scene. Packmates included. If a Failure, Enter Death Rage, but attack enemies first then packmates for the scene. If a Success, enter a Controled Rage. This is mechanically the same thing as Gauru as we know it now. Can only attack or move towards an opponent, ignores wound penalties, etc,etc. You even have a time limit of sorts. You have until Harmony + Primal Urge turns to either escape from your source of Rage or destroy said source before you enter Death Rage as if you failed the roll. On an Exceptional Success, you don't Rage at all.

                                                      Option 2: Use the rules found in the Werewolf Translation Guide for untimed Gauru and Frenzy. Failure means Death Rage, Succes means Gauru as we know it. Otherwise, it's kinda similar to LOOK's idea above.

                                                      Out of the two presented above, I'm more fond of the former. But yeah, this is what I'd do. So you see, it is possible to have Rage without screwing over your most powerful form.
