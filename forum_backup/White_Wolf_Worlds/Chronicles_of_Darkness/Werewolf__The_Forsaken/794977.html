    Spirit & Rite: The Pain Angel

    12-29-2015, 02:34 PM
    Something unpleasant from one of my games that I felt the urge to write up and throw onto the forum.

    The Pain Angel

    The hospital’s car park is a wasteland in the Shadow. It is a great expanse of cracked, broken, scoured asphalt and concrete that stretches across a vast distance to the looming structure at its heart. Lumpen, rusting hulks of ruined vehicles slump and die in its merciless grasp. Occasional trees, gnarled and twisted, writhe up from sundered earth towards the fulminating sky of the Hisil. Tall, tottering shapes move through the gloom on streetlamp legs of metal and plastic, their eyes shining beams of glaring light over the ruined landscape in search of fugitives from the hospital’s tender embrace. The guardian spirits mostly prey on the spiritual overflow from their home, but they’re not above hunting intruders into their grim domain either, hooting and screaming hungrily as they pick their way through the rusting detritus.

    Beyond the park, the hospital itself is a tumourous mass. Built and rebuilt over the decades, its different stages push and bulge from one another, even the oldest still anchored by the original core of the real hospital in the Flesh. In some places, entire wings that no longer exist erupt forth from the broken building’s cracked plaster and concrete, with oily stains marking the wounds where the incompatible structures join. Few lights shine from the windows that pierce the slumbering behemoth’s hide. Most dauntingly, massive chains of spirit-stuff bind the building down, the vast and clattering links arcing out into the car-park wasteland where they sink into the crumbling asphalt.

    Passing into the hospital’s entrance is walking into a maw. Jagged, broken shards of glass and metal, a rough mimicry of the real hospital’s reception front, form an unwelcoming barricade – and the spines and edges point in as well as out. Something like breath, deep and damp, wafts in and out. It carries with it the scents that will become unpleasantly familiar to anyone who enters the hospital’s Shadow reflection – bile, blood and antiseptic. It touches the mind with a hint of despair and a strong urge to leave.

    (An environmental Tilt is in place through much of the hospital, making the first Willpower point that a character spends in a scene add only 2 dice rather than 3 - unless it is augmenting an action intended to leave the hospital)

    The twisting guts of the hospital are as nightmarish as they are varied. The winding halls are contrasts of golden, warming light and stark shadows prowled by headless, sterile mannequins with scalpels for fingers and oozing, suppurating scavengers of disease and blight. Numbing haze rolls through the operating theatres, where the lights are so bright as to be blinding and slithering things with innumerable sharp-taloned arms coil in the corners. Room after room, ward after ward is filled with what look like shrouded corpses, while hungry spirits kneel or slump around them as if in prayer. Half-formed, wild spirits of raw emotion come out of the childbirth wards, appearing in floods driven by barely-understood hunger for Essence. Spirits wrought from delirium and confusion appear as reflections in mirrors, coloured incense or malformed things of syringes and wounds. Sometimes a wall cracks and breaks to reveal a pained, fleshy face beneath, its jaws opening to lap hungrily at the minds and flesh of intruders with a barbed tongue. Where a new wing and an old one are tangled together in a bloody cancer, things of gleaming, sterile metal cut and debride the two apart, decanting the spiritual gore from their surgery into phials to be carried deeper into the structure.

    (In the operating theatres, an Environmental Tilt inflicts a penalty on perception and reflexes, including Defence, from the numbing anaesthetic mist)

    The capering madness of the hospital sees sudden, brutal battles between different spirits; a scalpel-fingered faceless doctor-thing torn apart beneath a pack of ravening, rat-like horrors covered in sores; or something gaunt, looming and patient that has no mouth and sad, bleeding eyes grabs a skulking spirit of bright colour and emotion, methodically hammering it against a wall until both wall and spirit are broken and seeping ichor. A gibbering mass of searing, scouring fluid containing dissolving lumps of muck and flesh within washes down a corridor, burning away every spirit of filth or sickness that it touches. Sometimes a wing is almost empty, just golden light flowing in from half-closed windows and empty rooms in slight disarray. Another is awash in gore and the walls pulse with a distant scream.

    (The cleansing ooze leaves behind a slowly evaporating mucus that is an Environmental Tilt, inflicting a single point of bashing damage each round increased to lethal damage if the character is already sporting any open wounds)

    The denizens of the hospital suspend their cycle of madness in the presence of interlopers, watching carefully to assess how to react – whether to hunt, to purge, to cower or to burble at. Predatory and powerful disease spirits shriek and wrench at their chains in the cells that the hospital cages them in, beating their faces to oozing pulp against the glass of viewing ports and promising anything, anything, to the Uratha who frees them. Muttering, leering spiders of loose skin and plastic tubing scuttle up, taller than a man but with the manner of a wheedling, obsequious conman; perhaps the Uratha would like to change how they look, perhaps they want a new skin to wear for a little while, fresh, just one careful prior user, perhaps a mortal in their family could do with a whole new face to escape the Hosts hunting them down.

    (For a hefty price in Essence and a small patch of skin from the beneficiary, the skinners can change the appearance of a mortal in minor cosmetic ways - enough to potentially add or remove Striking Looks, but at a cost in a breaking point for the recipient, who must be willing and staring into a mirror when the Numina takes hold; the skinners don't mention that the alterations also make recipients smell particularly tantalising and appealing to Hosts. A skinner can also sell a single intact human skin for use in the Skin Thief Facet, a unique appearance made from the little pieces of skin that the skinner has acquired, this will have Striking Looks and will be startlingly, uncannily beautiful, but just like any other skin the Facet will destroy it when the Uratha next changes. A skinner will only exchange this skin for very significant gathra and afterwards will go into hibernation for a year while it weaves a new one in a cocoon)

    At the heart of the hive sits its queen, the Sovereign of Blood and Gold. Its very presence vibrates like a heartbeat, and its liquid form is in constant motion; streams of bright blood intertwined with shining gold that thread through the floor and the ceiling to pierce into the hospital’s stone and meat. Where the threads come together, the shape of the hospital’s spirit of healing exudes forth, manifesting a vaguely humanoid shape to deal with petitioners and invaders. It is haughty, but excited and interested by the wounded and the suffering; it will reach out to caress rent flesh, pluck at stitches and prod sores with delight and without asking permission. It can read what ails a body with a moment’s glance and will happily use that information to its advantage. It considers all the hospital as its domain and any interference by outsiders into its court angers it. It is vaguely interested in striking at the spirit lords of disease that exist beyond the wasteland ringing its fortress home, but only in the most abstract of ways – it is more intrigued by ideas of sabotaging or crippling its own kindred healing spirits, ruining the hospitals and places of care that they dwell in and that draw precious sick and suffering away from its own embrace.

    The main reason anyone would visit this angel of pain and blood is its power over healing. If angered, the Sovereign can bring the body’s natural ability to repair itself to a grisly halt, but should Uratha treat equitably with it then the spirit can aid the wounded greatly. The spirit can heal a single character for up to three points of aggravated damage once per week; it can reach across the Gauntlet to use this power if the bargain is to heal a mortal or packmate currently in the real hospital. When using this on an Uratha in its presence, the Sovereign reaches out with a limb of writhing blood-and-gold tendrils, piercing the flesh of the beneficiary and threading itself into their vitals for an agonising and sickening few moments. If the power is used across the Gauntlet, the target feels the same sensation but sees nothing. The aggravated damage then heals over the course of around an hour.

    The Sovereign is only interested in a couple of forms of payment for such healing. It mostly demands that the bargainers pay five Essence per point of damaged healed and hospitalise a number of mortal victims equal to the points of damage healed; the precise nature of the hospitalisation is flexible, but the victims must be genuinely harmed so that the spirit can draw Essence from their healing. The easiest way is to break a few limbs but the spirit will be intrigued by and well inclined to packs that get creative and start trying to infect victims with serious diseases or the like. This payment must happen within the week of the service or the healed flesh will fester and blight, turning necrotic and foul. The Sovereign is also interested in nearby rival hospitals being sabotaged, on the rough and irrational basis that they are stealing its ‘congregation’ from it. For this sort of service, the spirit will want a significant act of sabotage or damage – actually permanently killing the spirit of healing in one of the rival hospitals will buy a huge amount of favour with it and gain not only payment for the service but also a number of dots of the Ally (Sovereign of Blood and Gold) merit. The Sovereign cannot be forced to heal without payment, whether through intimidation or domination – it is literally incapable of doing so without gathra.

    The Sovereign knows the symbols for the Healing rite, and can be convinced to teach them in return for a significant service, tithe of Essence or if it is successfully defeated in the Sacred Hunt.

    Pack Rite: Healing (•••)
    This rite draws upon Shadow laws of balance and rejuvenation, passing the resilience of a werewolf into a frail mortal vessel.

    Symbols: Blood, healing, grief or loss, time

    Cost: 5 Essence

    Action: Extended (5 successes, each roll represents 30 minutes)

    Duration: Between 1 and 7 days

    Success: The rite targets a single werewolf pack member—or a Wolf-Blooded with the Wolf’s Meat tell—and a single Wolf-Blooded or human pack member who lacks any form of supernatural healing. For the duration of the rite, the Wolf-blooded or human pack member heals at twice the normal rate, and can spend a Willpower point to regenerate as fast as a werewolf with Primal Urge 1 for the duration of a scene. However, the ritemaster must create a focus for the blessing—a trinket, talisman or other anchor—which the recipient must have about her person to be able to access this benefit. If she does not possess the anchor when the rite’s duration expires, then for every day that she has not possessed it, the werewolf from whom she drew the healing power suffers 1 point of Aggravated damage. Being subjected to the rite of healing is a breaking point for a human, but not a Wolf-Blooded. A given werewolf or Wolf-Blooded with the Wolf’s Meat tell can only lend her strength to a single other character at a time. The duration of the rite is set by the ritemaster upon invoking it, and cannot be ended early.




    - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

                                          Note that a clever spirit of healing can be just as horrific through its methodical ruthlessness as the gibbering chaos that a magath can wreak. Consider the healing spirit lording over a busy hospital and eager for every scrap of Essence, but smart enough to restrain itself from guzzling down just any old sort of Essence while it waits for healing to take place:
                                          - People with minor injuries entering the hospital are taking up space for more severely wounded patients who will give more Essence. Best to aggravate the lightly-suffering - cause minor infections or complications at the very least to squeeze more Essence out of them. Servile spirits of pain and disease are marshalled and set to work. Patients who should have simple, straightforward cases suffer greater pain and infections sweep through wards.
                                          - Patients who are suffering from terminal diseases or are in critical conditions? Well, the spirit and its underlings can more easily tell who is going to live and who die. No point having the hospital's Flesh side 'waste' its efforts on patents who aren't going to provide any healing Essence. Kill them to get those hospital beds and staff resources freed up swiftly.
                                          - The really, really smart spirit does its damnest to ensure that every so often, an infectious disease carrier doesn't get properly cured. After all, that way they get sent out into the general population again, triggering another outbreak that brings a fresh wave of meals to the hospital...
