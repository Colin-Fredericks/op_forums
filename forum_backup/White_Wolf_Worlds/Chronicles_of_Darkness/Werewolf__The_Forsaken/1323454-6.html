                    If you want an official clarification, Paradim has the right of it. The particular clarification on the Oath of the Moon and the Pure in Shunned is my work, and it is absolutely my intent that it work that way.

                    The Oath of the Moon is a codification of various transgressions that are spiritually corrosive for Uratha. It doesn't matter if the Pure tell themselves that killing werewolves is fine if they're Forsaken, it's still spiritually corrosive for them to do so, much as it is for Forsaken to kill Pure. The Pure don't get an out just because they want it to be otherwise.

                    You'll note that in 1st edition, the Pure had massive problems with their Harmony and had all manner of methods around scouring themselves of the sins they knew they were committing, attempting to balance what they were doing, and so forth. The same is true in 2nd edition, except it's a more inherent part of werewolf existence - being an Uratha means you're always having to struggle with the two sides of Harmony, and it's (broadly speaking) easier to rectify that balance simply through purposeful or instinctive action.

                    The Pure have never had a spiritual free pass for murdering or torturing Forsaken. Some of them lie to themselves that they do. The honest ones know they don't, and either don't care, or try to mitigate or repent for it because they believe it's a necessity.

                    Honestly that one throwaway line in the 2nd ed core that the Oath of the Moon Harmony breaks are only for Forsaken is very unfortunate, because it's essentially incorrect, and we could honestly do with a slightly revised write-up of how Harmony works to adjust for that.

                    - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
