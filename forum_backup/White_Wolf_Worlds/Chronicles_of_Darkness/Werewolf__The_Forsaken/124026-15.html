    Your reasoning is a matter of double standards, though why you cling to it is because you prefer one game design option over the other, you just happen to favor the vampire side of this issue.. Which is fine, you have your preferences, as does everyone. But you should just say that it's your preference instead of trying to act like it's something else. It's no more or less improper or unfair for vampires to downgrade other splats supernatural ways of dealing damage than it is for them to be able to bypass it.     Again, how would you think if Disquiet made werewolves unable to regenerate without essence?

A vampire's undead body has no effect on whether a werewolf can kill a spirit, human, or anything else. However a werewolves innate ability to ignore a vampire's innate ability is the only one being ignored.

    If a werewolf doesn't innately do that, then no innate ability is being ignored, the damage is still the same regardless, how a vampire's body holds up is what is in question. You keep pushing this, and its a clear bias, because I'm not talking just about werewolf. I think it should apply across all lines that innate abilities do not cancel out innate abilities of others. One should not be made that automatically ignores this. I don't think They should begin this trend with werewolf and end up with werewolf getting shafted by other lines either. It doesn't mean anything to werewolf's other capabilities, it just impacts whether other major gamelines just get shit on by the next one released.

    Its a bad precedent, and has impact on the enjoyment others have. You're falsely calling it a double standard when you've not got all of the features. Werewolves are apparently getting a gift that is highly effective against vampires, I have no issue with that. My issue is the precedent set by this situation of next line has to be better than last. I think ignoring innate abilities in the design of new innate abilities is a bad way to go about it.

    A vampire doesn't innately make you lose the claws, you still have the claws, you can do any number of things with the claws you couldn't before, including making attacks that wouldn't be possible with unarmed damage (tilts and things that require tissue loss) etc. The damage is still done, its simply a matter of how vampires should react to it.

    If you were saying I'm pushing a double standard, I'd be saying werewolves lose claws completely, and this isn't the case. Called shots with claws would still be worse than called shots with fists. There's a major difference there.

                Originally posted by Errol216 View Post

                I think you're sorely underestimating how important survivability is in actually killing things. There's a reason that the scariest weapons we have are measured in terms of range rather than sheer damage output: if the other person can't hit back, then failing to kill them in the first shot means that you can hit them again. And again. And again. Something as simple as a machine gun is more about suppressing return fire than actually killing. The Gauru declines to be suppressed: it just walks through it.

                If you don't have that survivability, then the other person can rock you back onto the defense. When you're invincible, that's no longer an option. The other person can only run away. If you did the rest of the hunt right, they were cornered already and they are out of options.
                This is actually part of my point. Going into gauru is probably a worse idea than simply staying out of range and shooting. A rifle or shotgun will do more damage, with reflexive shifting you can likely stay out of range with the increased speed of wolf-form. Because when you're in gauru all something has to do is tie you up for a bit, by grappling or whomping on the head for a stun or simply moving out of the range before it can hit you. In that last case it may even attack one of its allies, since they may end up close than you, it's just wasting its time in gauru since it doesn't have the damage to quickly kill something that can kill.

                The whole point of the form is killing things quickly and efficiently, but beyond the Down and Dirty combat, the mechanics do not support this. No one's saying being able to full heal isn't great, but survivability does not equate to quick and efficient killing.
