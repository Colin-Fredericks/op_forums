    I couldn't speak for the rules, but the ban really isn't that difficult, my character is basically under it as it is and it doesn't interfere with his interactions with the rest of the pack. We don't all sleep in the same place. It's a little like Promethean, but obviously not as harsh.

    A game with a limited focus on a small town or wilderness area, and obviously Neolithic era games aren't going to have much room for it. But if you were in a larger city or part of a traveling game members would be vital. Our last werewolf game ended up with a massive road trip across the Northeast, the Atlantic, then Europe to Russia, while another game took place entirely within the confines of a tropical island that had one unfinished road that stretched for half a mile. Obviously a group and their storyteller should talk about it beforehand to see if it's appropriate and if plots can be built to take advantage of the lodge.

    However, a werewolf effectively compelled to travel constantly and hunt across territories is certainly challenging for a splat largely designed around holding and protecting discrete geographic territory.

    The Steel Teeth are definitely neat and fun, but absent a lot of player and ST agreement and planning, I also envision serious potential problems if they're part of a mixed group

    I'm also actually more interested in the rules and mechanics, as I surmise they will be applicable to all lodges.

    For instance, if the additional Sacred Hunt lodge benefits can be shared with your pack like a regular Siskur-Dah Condition, the motivation for pack members to join lodges substantially increases.

                        Originally posted by branford View Post

                        Does joining a Lodge and acquiring its benefits cost XP and/or require attribute and skill prerequisites, similar to the new Mage 2e Legacy rules?
                        ​There's no cost in experiences and no hard mechanical set of skill or attribute prerequisites. The first half of the Lodges section in The Pack covers things like how lodges apply standards, requirements, trials and the like for potential adherents, but these aren't linked to particular numbers of dots or the like. It depends a lot on the supplicant, the lodge members dealing with the supplicant and, most importantly of all, the totem.


                        - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
