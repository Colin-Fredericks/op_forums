    Mother: “You know what.”

    Mother: “He showed me the plans for it again yesterday, it’s a really good idea for a city. He just needs a little leg up.”
    Rome: “Mum, Reme is never going to happen. It’s a fantasy.”
    Mother: “Well, not without some help, no.”
    Rome: “Not under any circumstances. If he wanted a city, he should’ve started when I did. He was too busy bumming around Elyria, playing his lute. I’m sorry but it’s too late now, Rome wasn’t build…-”
    Mother: “Yes, yes, so you always say. Look, I’m not asking you to do it for him, all I’m asking is for you to have a word in a few ears.”
    Rome: “But mum it’s a terrible idea for a city! I mean, huge parts of it are just ripped off from Rome anyway, and the bits he’s changed he’s made worse. It’s not going to be a better city because it’s build on eight hills. It’s not how it works.”
    Mother: “Well then help him. Show him where he’s gone wrong.”
    Rome: “He won’t listen to me, he thinks I’m an evil corporate sell out, just because I’ve put in prisons and banks. I’m sorry but try running a city without them. If you actually want to make a city work you’ve got to serve your inhabitants. When in Rome…-”
    Mother: “Yes, I know. You won’t help him at all?”
    Rome: “Oh don’t make it sound like that mum, I would help but it’ll never work! If only because all the infrastructure has been set up for Rome now. There’s no room for Reme.”
    Mother: “Why not?”
    Rome: “Well for a start, all roads lead to-…”
    Mother: “I see! Well Rommy, I’ve listened to your objections and I do understand but I’m putting my foot down. I want you to do whatever you need to do to help your brother make Reme happen. Do I make myself clear?”
    Rome: “Why should I?”
    Mother: “Because I’m your mother.”
    Rome: “And if I don’t?”
    Mother: “Well… then I’ll just have to tear you to pieces with my mighty she wolf jaws! Grrrrrrr!!!”

            (About Hebrew and Moon connection)

            I would translate it myself probably, but I await two major translation requests from my workplace.

            http://www.racjonalista.pl/kk.php/s,...pekty.Judaizmu
            Okay, idea is this - early Hebrew there is odd correlation of they calendar, 28 day month ( 4 times 7 days week ) and New Moon. First Shabbat of the year comes in the New Moon, seventh is most sacred of them all, worshiped very extensively in early Old Testament. At the beginning of books day starts at the dawn ( solar cult ), but later, start of the Shabbat is at the seventh day's dusk ( lunar cult ). In the middle of Old Testament there is mentioned purge in Israel, negating worship of other gods in it. From then on only YHWH is mentioned - but clues from earlier cult were left, and calendar or Shabbat practices remained, pointing on that probably Moon cult was absorbed in to Tetragrammaton itself.

            My take on this idea of Jewish werewolves myth based on it would be this...

            Unholy Sons


            On the beginning was a Word. And Word was a World. The Father created with the Word whole Creation. And it was good. He later created humans in Paradise and gave them free will. But Eve, first women, was tempted by the Snake to obtain the apple from Tree of Knowledge. And they needed to be banished from the Paradise.

            But Father weren’t only having righteous face of the Sun. He was forgiving as the Moon. He couldn’t lifted his own decree, because he knew it was right - humans weren’t suppose to return to Paradise any more. But he also couldn’t left Adam and Eve, and the kind alone in the world. He needed to defend them, and Paradise against them.

            As the Moon, he appeared to the most dangerous of Paradise animal and come to make with it the race of guardians. The Father Moon conceived with Mother Wolf new kind - half man, half wolves. Not pure as humans, but needed to protect them and the world from the man. As sons and daughters of so foul creature as Wolf, they could do what need to be done - even go to the Hell itself, if the need rose.

            But the Wolf Mother wanted more and more of her half human children to behave like a wolves, forsaking they sacred duty from the Father. Then he come to them and told what need to be done. And so they killed they Mother under his command, like Isaac was ready to sacrifice his son.
            This is who we are - we are His children, with duty to him and respect to our Mother memory. We didn’t destroyed the Paradise, but were made to guard it. And now we atone for our sins in the eyes of Father.

            So what do you think about this story? It’s only first draft, I will make it more later.

                                                            This story is true.

                                                            There was a time before all this. Something more primal, more raw. Some would call it purer, or better for it, but that's a lie. Taking away cell phones and microwaves doesn't make something morally better. This was a savage time, of ever-changing horrors you can scarcely imagine. Luna hung high over the Earth day and night, casting not just her light, but her power over the world. Things changed, warped, mutated constantly. The line between spirit and man shifted perpetually, sometimes breaking altogether. Too often. More often than Father Wolf would like.

                                                            The self-styled guardian of borders, Father Wolf's job of guarding the physical from the ephemeral and vice-versa was very nearly impossible under Luna's tyranny. Father Wolf was not meant to be an intelligent thing. He was the Hunter, and wanted little more than to hunt those that would break the law. When the law becomes as immutable as everything else, though, when the world changes at the whims of a mad tyrant-queen with the power to go entirely unopposed, creativity was needed. He had learned cunning, but to protect the borders, to hold his post, he needed more.

                                                            Legends spoke of a city, somewhere in this changing world, a place where the few enlightened among mankind gathered and called down power of their own, a port in the neverending storm. A watchtower. Knowing this was the ally he needed to wage war with the moon, Father Wolf traveled to this city's walls, and waited. His children, the Firstborn, he knew, would stand watch over Luna's madness. They couldn't hold the fort forever, and so on the fifth day, when the Awakened City had still not allowed him entry, Father Wolf laid siege upon the walls, roaring his demand to speak with those who would call themselves gods. In that moment, the city was his prey. He was the Wolf. He would not, could not be denied.

                                                            The Awakened City shattered. Luna's influence poured in, and the single watchtower in an ocean of ever-changing madness fell. Finally, one of the Mages of the city called out to Father Wolf. "Why did you do this," she cried! "Do you know what you have done?"

                                                            "It isn't the Wolf's place to care about his prey." Father Wolf responded. He told them of his unique problem, The Mages may have felt pity, if Father Wolf had not damned them. It would be a slow decline, but with the walls that protected the Awakened City from Luna the Tyrant broken, the Mages would always carry something of her hatred, and that hatred would twist their magics into strange shapes that brought forth the darkest places the world had to offer.

                                                            Father Wolf was not the only one who knew cunning. The mages plotted, and gave the spirit wolfsbane in the guise of an olive branch.

                                                            There was one way to do away with Luna the Tyrant, they told Father Wolf. They would use the last of their power to construct a machine to drain Luna's godly might, make her no more powerful than Father Wolf, who would consume her. A new Luna would be born, for that was her nature, but it would be a weaker, tamer Luna.

                                                            "If you could do such a thing," Father Wolf asked, "Why had you not done it already?"

                                                            The Mages answered. "Because no spirit capable of killing the weakened Tyrant was available to us. And it will cost you your life." This was their revenge. They would give Father Wolf what he wanted, and would make the Fallen World to come a more habitable place in the absence of their paradise. At the same time, it would kill he who damned them. After another five days and another five nights, he accepted. He saw no other path to victory in the war he had started against Luna.

                                                            The Mages built their God-Machine, and Father Wolf returned to his children to find them weakened and broken. He told them of the Mages, and what they had come up with.

                                                            Three of his children, those most wounded by his absence, were furious. Dire Wolf, Rabid Wolf, and Silver Wolf swore that they would never give up the war, and if Father Wolf became one with the Moon, then he would become their enemy.This was acceptable to Father Wolf. The remaining Firstborn agreed to forsake their hatred, if it meant peace, a world they could protect effectively.

                                                            The Mages made their play. Luna was aware of what they intended, but was unable to stop them, as even she could not control her changes, and could not retain her mind long enough to counterattack. The Mages' God-Machine worked as it was meant to, and Luna was mortally wounded. She could no longer force her changing nature upon the world, and her light could only show at night, a dim reflection of what she once was.

                                                            The machine poured its power into Father Wolf, and with his dying breath, gifted this power to his children, and their children, and so forth. Those who descended from the Firstborn were given the power of the Moon, to change shape and walk between the worlds of spirit and man, which were now solidly divided Only the Forsaken, who gave up their hatred for the Moon, were able to use her full might, and it is said that Luna the Broken shows favor to them through her Gifts. The Lunes, spirit memories of Luna the Tyrant, still exist though, aiding and tormenting the Forsaken in equal measure. While they may guide the Forsaken when it aids their twisted agenda, taking one as a totem would surely invite the Tyrant's wrath back into the world.

                                                            This is how it is. The Forsaken carry on their Father's legacy of stewardship and protection, and the Pure carry on their Father's legacy of war against Luna.

                                                            This is how it will always be.
