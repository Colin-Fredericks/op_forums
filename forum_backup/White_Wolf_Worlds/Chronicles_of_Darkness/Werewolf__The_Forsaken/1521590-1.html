        I mean... it's Shadow BS, hyperbole seems as valid a reason as any. Granted, my understanding of Werewolf is cursory at best.
        The Shadow has understandable principles that it works off of-laws of sympathy and representation, the atavistic inclination towards "wild" icons being stronger, the flow of resonance and the way the spirit lords can shape them, so on and so forth. It's not all laid out and is definitely more obtuse, but it's not like Requiem where it seems like the curses in the Vitae can mutate, warp, conglomerate and divide into whatever particular powerset you care for it to have*.

        THe (Watsonian) obtuseness of it all stems from the fact that, unlike with other games, like Awakening where understanding the nuts and bolts of reality and it's particular bits is the point or Lost where occultic traditions still have dues owed to the commonwealth of freeholds, Forsaken both has more immediately pragmatic concerns on the whole, and also has a lot more sense of boundaries when it comes to spiritual facts. THe Uratha are a very here and now sort of people, so that first part is easy enough to grasp. As for the second part, for every faction that has interest in laying out and mapping the exact mechanics of the Shadow, there's often equal and opposite numbers interested in maintaining the privacy and dignity of the Shadow and it's greatest actors-for every Bone Shadow and Iron Master who wants to pry, there's an equal and opposite amount of Hunters in Darkness and Storm Lords preserving the purity of locations and preserving the honor of actors by not paying overt attention to weakness, and the Fire Touched and Predator Kings are both of those things in equal measure as an ideal.

        Anyways, as for the particular strain of discussion, I'm gonna echo Arms' sense of sense about it-it'd be weird to have the bit between "meat" and "Essence, which we are also using to highly encourage you towards cannibalism" to be "weird vegetarian alternative friendly"

        *Which, to be fair, is kind of on theme for Requiem, where in the diversity of curses is so great that loose and weird results is not only the norm, but part of the Kindred's overarching problem.

                            Okay I did not expect the discussion to get this big, but here we are..
                            If I may offer a defense as far as I am currently interested, my entire interest is that you are actually phenomenally right, and even in a way I would deem really narratively interesting-albeit in a bit of a NPC sense.

                            See, the thing is, you've set a premise for a a character who is one of those worst-case-scenarios NPC's to be, in the same vein as the Lady of the Light from Alan Wake. It's functional and really viable, and even outside of my parameters just really usable. Is any of it desirable? THe answer to that is even more broadly variable even if the broad answer is also no, and that conflict is Very Much Why I'm Here.

                            See, the thing of the matter is that, for me personally, you've managed to find a negotiable middle ground to "suffienciently powerful enough to resolve own personal problems" with "being at all existentially concerned with anyone else outside of yourself" at a scale I still find personably relatable. Six to seven is about that scale across the board, and the thing of the matter is that this particular case is pretty interpretable-notice no one has mention "nourishable within the confines of what a human needs" against "nourishable assuming metaphysical bullshit" as a metric. Those two boundaries create a lot of creative and interesting playspace that is, within my reason of limited knowledge, really fun to play around with, because it lets you lay out a way of ways to think about the problem actually in play, and then let you narrow down into a particular expression of conflict, which highlights any of the larger problems at play.

                            To me, that's very much what I like about the franchise-creative answers to problems that engender new questions of conflicts that require you to first gain an appreciation of the older conflicts in play, and consider what is the best answer that accomodates all answers. Humanity is all about recognizing all elements at play, and a good answer to one problem needs to be seen as a problem in other ways and thus recognized on a wholer thing than beyond humanity.

                            Notably, and to counter-argue Shadowcat here, this idea works all the better against imperical and objective truths, because both the audience and the characters are currently straining against objective truths and how much subjectivity can bend us around certain facts. As I mentioned earlier, the Shadow has objective laws, and the relationship between the Flesh, the Gauntlet, and the Shadow has objective consequences-but how much of the case around those has room for Storyteller Fiat, so you can tell this story any number of ways. In the scores of Forsaken one shots I've run over the years (and why is it Forsaken I absolutely know but is fun to "lament" about all the same), I've taken so many myriad points of view on I'm not even sure i can properly list them.

                            Let me put it another way. I've seen so many "answers" to any given gameline, I could fucking snore as a given. You've provided basically the premise for a Night Horrors character, and that's inarguably high praise given how I got into the industry.
