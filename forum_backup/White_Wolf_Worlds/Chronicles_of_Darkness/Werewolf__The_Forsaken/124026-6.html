        And let's not forget that gauru, while near impossible to dammage whitout a way deal aggravated are far from unstoppable.
        For one they aren't that much better at fighting. A 1 die difference over urshul isn't going to help them much. For second there are way to stop a rampaging gaurur that do not involve damage. It is a very good defensive form, and a good offensive form against mooks, but it's not the end all be all of werewolf combat.

        Now for the results of playtesting in an actual game session.
        Forms used: Urhan, Urshul and Gauru.

        Urhan now has some use that is not negated by having partial change, I am delighted by this. The ability to use speed for foot pursuit and the pseudo celerity means very few things are going to be able to escape or catch a werewolf in urhan. The pseudo celerity is nicely balanced by the lack of opposable thumbs, limiting the way it can be abused.

        Urshul: I suspect the ability of urshul to lower the defense of an enemy semi permanently to be quite deadly with a full pack, but since I was the only werewolf around it was of very limited use. I've seen people wonder about someone getting back their defense if they can escape/catch their breath/heal. To which I'd like to remind that the defense lowering happens for a scene, if someone manage to escape a pack of werewolves and catch their breath it would be something I'd consider to be a different scene. I do wonder about supernatural instant healing though.

        I was facing a gunman and the ST was nice enough to not make the bullet silver or I would have been in great trouble. That guy must have had a massive dice pool because he never missed me. So the whole defense against firearms things tell me that it changed things a little from guns being absolutly terrifying to only be scary in the hands of a trained professional. I'm okay with that.

        Gauru: So the bad guy couldn't hurt me anymore. But I had a very hard time hitting him. The dice did not agree with me that night. Using willpower and everything I could squeeze I still failed to hit a couple times in a row. So while Gauru is powerful defensively, offensively it's not that much better than urshul. Which lend me to think that Gauru is the form you take in one of two situations.
        First the fight isn't that critical and you just need to mop up whitout wanting to worry about losing health. Or it's a very important fight and you use Gauru to weaken the opposition whitout exposing yourself too much. You face a super dangerous enemy, a boss of sort, and you need the near invincibility to ensure you can survive long enough to take the enemy down.

        Will post more once I get the chance to see the special ability of Dalu/hishu in action.

                                                            Not my idea of an interesting scenario. I will be surprised if that kind of thing makes it into the final version of the book.
                                                            Although true when both Werewolves are in Gauru I think in a non white-room scenario things might play out a bit differently. Let me try to explain:
                                                            Gauru due to the generation offers effectively two advantages 1. It is a quick mid-battle full heal 2. Due to regen you can (in some cases) ignore defense to go full out on offense, the biggest drawback is the limited time. Sure in the other forms there is also regen but that is unless spending essence (which is limited) only bashing.

                                                            ​So what I think will happen is that both werewolves start out in Urshul eventually one or the other will need to shift to Gauru either to heal or it is the only way to damage the other party in both cases the other parties best options is to stay in Urshul and use his actions to dodge for a couple of turn before turning himself Gauru which will virtually guarantee* that he will be the one outlasting the one who shifted first.

                                                            So with two closely matched Werewolves it will be a tense first couple of turns seeing who will out damage the other after which one has to switch to Gauru or be out of the fight anyway followed by another tense two or three turns to see if the other one is capable of surviving the onslaught and after that it is followed by your example but in that case the fight should already be effectively over (a couple of turns later the first one to shift shift back and is murdered).

                                                            Note that this does not include any gifts/rituals or other aid and assumes it is a duel to the death (which imho should be pretty rare, other type of duel can and should be more common)


                                                            *If you shifted because you are out of health or shifted because otherwise you didn't enough damage you are in all likelihood also the weaker opponent and thus have probably less turns of Gauru (the other options it is a fluke and in the case of relative evenly matched probably means equal turns in which case shifting first is also a loosing strategy)
