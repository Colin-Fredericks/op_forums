    Originally posted by nofather View Post

    Standard defense would be enough, plus their Supernatural Tolerance. There's a misconception about Clash of Wills that it acts as some sort of extra resistance roll. It's really when two conflicting powers clash. Obfuscate vs Auspex, for instance. If someone was trying to turn the smoke into a vampire, sure because that goes directly against the power of the spell, but controlling the smoke wouldn't provoke any Clash of Wills.
    If someone where to dissipate, dilute or press the smoke into a "bottle" then it's surely a power in precise contradiction to another power, so a Clash of Wills should be applicable here. However, dealing damage with Fire Influence would not. But using Wind/Air to press the smoke into a trunk would be.

    That's true, chemically. But it's likely close enough for spirits.
    I just don't agree here, blood is just the normal medium for Vitae to exist in, however breath is another. Chemically it's different, but metaphysically, even more so. If Spirits could manipulate Vitae with "Influence: Blood", werewolves essence would answer to the same "influence: flesh". I just don't see Vitae/Essence falling under Blood/Flesh influence.

    Four dots is really the maximum you'd need in a fight, providing you weren't dealing with a swarm or something. With four, you just create fire on the vampire. But with 3 dots you could take an existing fire, like a torch in your hand, and have it lash out and burn the target. With 2 dots, you could do something more reasonable with it, like if you threw a molotov and missed, have it spread to the vampire providing they were close. But if you hit them with a molotov, you could just use 1 dot to Strengthen it, doing more damage.

    You don't even need the Gift to do it, though. Influences are available via talen, and if you're hunting vampires you'd have reason to stock up. May as well go the extra mile and get some Sun Influence talens. If vampires are going to the lengths of making weapons of pure silver, that's a reasonable expectation.

    I like these things but they're the type of ability I would fall into a trap of using all the time. One day I'll have my fire breathing werewolf, but until then he's commanding cars to run over people and killing vampires during the day, like a normal person.

    While I'm not arguing about the technicalities in how Influence works, its just to arbitrary to use as a good example, because it will work completely different around every gaming table, because the rules guiding them are, quite frankly, missing. In the abstract way, I do agree with your assessment how it works, but we are sure to disagree on the details and workings of the powers when it comes to the actual ruling of it. Influence is by far the worst rule still in CofD, it's insanely arbitrary in how it works. It's an fragment from 1ed that should've got updated long ago.

    Four dots i purity is no small feat or achievement, but perfectly reasonable in a fair fight against an elder vampire, if we're having a white room scenario. But I do not see it that easy to get, that so many people seem to be under the impression that it is.

    But yeah in a warring scenario wolfs Vs. vamps it's reasonable that both sides gets to prepare against their enemies, and Influence Sunlight/Fire etc talens and bargain with spirits is on the table for wolves. An interesting way to root out the vamps are using spirits of curiosity, longing, vengeance and the like to influence the vampires victims to get revenge and tear down their Masques. If every victims brother/sister/mom/friend/coworker etc starts to investigate and being pushed to hunt the vampire, soon it won't really have a blood supply.

        Poseur Influences are meant to be subjective because the Shadow is meant to be a subjective and symbolic realm - I don't need copious amounts of justification in order imagine how Influence: Cold may be used to calm someone, just like Influence: Fire could make someone hungry. Lesser spirits tend to be 1 trick ponies, which is something of an advantage actually sentient individuals have over them: they can see a spirit use an influence in a novel way and try to copy the effect, whereas a spirit trying to emulate such a thing would first need to eat the example user. Blood has water in it, and being able to manipulate it could do all sorts of funny things to your organism, like the myriad of gruesome effects that Leukemia can have a tool in someone's body. Likewise, getting a fetish to shift Influence: Water for Influence: Blood is trivial, hell I could even go for Influence: Vitae if you're particularly bothered. And with 3 dots there I can likely have vitae pouring out of the elder's every pore till they get torpid silly, from the shadow, and in a matter of minutes.

                                                I think we should also apply the various covenant advantages in this discussion as well, at least to make it a bit more even.

                                                The Invictus and Carthians have some of the best social merits hands down, and it can be debated which one beats the other in that regard.
