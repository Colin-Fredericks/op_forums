    GMC defense and armour too powerful?

    03-29-2014, 07:57 PM
    Hi
    I've got a question in regards to defense how your expereinces there are and if someone uses a workaround.

    I've had 2 rounds now that got into melee in medieval times. The first time the chars didnt have good stats so I didnt think much there, but this time around they had acceptable stats
    with base dicepools of 7-10 for hitting enemies. The problem was the damage that was dealt and that if any of the enemies had armour almost none would have been hurt at all.

    Last round
    Attacker: Str 2, melee 1 with spec and a sword. The defender had normal stats and a bit of battle experience 4 defense which reduced the attacking dicepool to 0 (chance roll).
    In combination with leather armour eeven with willpower spent almost no damage.

    This round
    we had a werewolf who had a base dice pool of 7 and a bite of 1L. the defender had 4 defense so only 3 dices to hit. if the defender had any armour (leather as example) then the
    chances to do ANY damage at all would have been almost neglible. Even so the werewolf did almost alyways only 1 damage.

    A demon who had large blades form (4 damage) had way more chance of doing real damage to the enemy.


    So my question is if anyone else ran into the problem that ppl who had only 0-1L attacks were almost effectless in combat (especially with armour) and that
    the high gmc defense reduces normal non master combatants (with 3 strength and 3 dots in the skill + speciality) to levels where you need QUITE some luck to be able to hit at all

    and if so what workarounds you use.

    tnx

          Spend Willpower, fish for situational modifiers, get your dice pool as high as possible, and use called shots. If you aim for areas the armor doesn't cover you don't have to worry about the damage reduction, and if you land a good hit on a called shot you can impose tilts which will make subsequent attacks easier to land. The GMC combat rules were intentionally designed to facilitate and encourage this kind of play. That way you're improvising tactical maneuvers to fish for situational bonuses, making called shots to circumvent armor and impose penalties, and actually spending your resources, instead of just rolling back and forth until someone gets enough 8s 9s or 10s.

              Just to add and re-iterate on the above:
              • Be generous with Willpower use
              • In a combat-heavy chronicle, consider using Fighting Styles, either as Merits as presented or manoeuvres that are available to everyone
              • If a character will roll a chance die no matter what, take all the penalties you can get. If the character does get the 10 he will inflict a Tilt that is likely going to end the match
              • Aim for unarmoured body parts
              • Consider the environment and use it to your advantage
              • Consider allowing the Aim action to be taken in melee, representing combatants lining up their attacks and waiting for the right moment
              • Consider allowing "social attacks" to gain benefits (like goading someone into an All-out Attack)
              • Consider fatigue in longer fights
              • Do not forget the Defense penalty from heavy armour
              • Do not forget even if armour negates all Lethal Damage, the target still always takes at least one point of Bashing Damage

                      Or simply use Down and Dirty and the optional Beaten Down rules (someone taking lethal surrenders).

                      Additionally, consider how specs stack in this setting. Take a character with Strength 3 and Brawl 3... Give him appropriate specialty in say, Urhsul, All Out Attack, Called Shot and bites each with Area of Expertise; that's a +8 to the base of Strength 5 and brawl 3 and 2 from the regular All Out, 18 dice. Toss in something situational such as "packmates" (from hunting like a pack of wolves do) into brawl as well as another specialty and it's a +10 for a base of 20 dice. Even with a -4 from defense, and a -4 from targeting your opponent's hand, you got 12 dice to chew his hand off, giving the opponent further penalties, making him lose his weapon, heck you can inflict it twice. Targeting his leg and accounting for his defense it comes down to -6; That's 14 dice worth of damage, which means you're pretty much guaranteed to eventually fuck his leg up.

                      That's just how scary a Rahu should be.

                                                  You're not misunderstanding, you're just not being generous enough with situational modifiers as ST. Give your players bonus dice for things like using the environment to their advantage, or using tactics like one distracting the enemy and another attacking them from behind when they're off guard. Besides, all it takes is one good hit to cause a Tilt, and then every following attack will be much easier to land, so consider things like allowing the players to use the teamwork mechanic to all focus their attacks on one point. Encourage the players to use all-out attacks for extra dice when they need them. And maybe most important of all, use the Beaten Down and Surrender rules. A lot of people don't like them because they seem like they'll make fights end too fast. They actually improve combat a lot, I'm convinced the GMC combat rules were written assuming Beaten Down was being used by default.
