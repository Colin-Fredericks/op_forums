                So...... I'm at a loss with the Idigam. The writers say they're not Lovcraftian beings... but are pretty much described like one to me. They've been consistently referred to as spirits time and again, yet we're told that they're NOT spirits, since the Bone Shadows don't specialize in hunting them.

                What gives? I'm getting a very strong vibe of "The writer doth protest too much, me thinks." Okay, so we're talking about primordial and/or rival celestial (read: not Lunar/Solar) spirits. They're in flux with difficult-to-get-bans. Okay. What is there about them that makes them special enough to be a titular aspect of the game? How is it encompassing werewolf-hood?

                The strix are the darkest parts of vampires, all the hunger without any restraining Man, a mirror darkly. Angels have a strong thematic tie to demons, their reflection and who they once were. Both speak strongly to the game's core themes.

                What is there about Idigam that makes it so special or important to Werewolf? I'm not feeling it. Sure, you can make a cool antagonist. But... is that it?

                    The beings of Lovecraft represent his philosophical theories about cosmicism, which suggest that, among other things, if there are beings of great power and knowledge out there, they don't care a whit about humanity, as humanity is insignificant in the grand scheme of the universe.

                    The idigam, however, really are interested in the Forsaken (not sure if it's werewolves as a whole), because they hate them. They hate them so much.

                    They are spirits. They are just a very special kind of spirit. They aren't one of the sacred prey because they're so rare, not because they're not spirits, and they have powers that, due to their nature, don't conform to the standard rules people know about spirits. It's the kind of confrontation that's going to involve thinking outside of the standard mindset, like hunting a vampire when you're prepared to hunt humans.

                        As I understand it, they were the first spirits. Born of the chaos of the still-forming universe (personally, I change that to the still-forming earth in my own games to make them a bit less cosmic and to justify their canonically lower rank than a lot of younger and more local spirits). They represent concepts that no longer exist, so their nature is fundamentally different from that of modern spirits. I think they're the central antagonists of the chronicle for a couple of reasons. For one thing, they're super powerful and damn hard to kill, which makes them convenient big bads. They also give ST's an easy way to tie the Idigam chronicle into the God Machine Chronicle and DtD if you're into that kind of crossover, by having their lunar prison be the tomb of the butterfly. But I think most importantly, they are antagonistic to the very nature of werewolves. The Wolf Must Hunt, and the Idigam are the one thing The Wolf Can't Hunt. Even Urfarah had to accept that they were just too inconstant for him to kill, and his only option was to imprison them. Now they're back, they have even more reason to hate you than other spirits, and they're as impossible to kill as ever. They're the out-of-context problem, the one you can't solve by your normal methods. You can't just hunt them, you have to find a way to trick them into coalescing, into accepting the rules of a constant, ordered universe and gaining a Ban, and only then can they be killed. Vampire is a game about internal struggle with your monsterous nature, and the Strix are an external representation of that internal struggle. Werewolf is a game about the need to hunt, and the Idigam as a thing-you-can't-hunt are a representation of your own shortcomings. They're living proof that you are not the perfect hunter you believe yourself to be.

                                                    That's kind of the point. The games have different themes, so they have to have different challenges. The Strix neeed to be deeply connected to the Kindred because Requiem is such an internally focused game; the primary conflict is a struggle against yourself, and the Strix are the worst parts of yourself. The Idigam need to be entirely alien to the Uratha in order to oppose them on the same level the Strix oppose vampires. Werewolves are the apex predator within their frame of reference, so their ultimate antagonist could only be something from outside that frame of reference. They're such a threat specifically because they have no analogue within werewolves' experience. And the only way you can beat them is by forcing them into a context you have experience with, forcing them to accept a Ban.
