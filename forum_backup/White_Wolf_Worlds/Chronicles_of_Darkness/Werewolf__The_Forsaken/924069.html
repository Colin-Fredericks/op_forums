    Reworking Some Themes

    06-30-2016, 04:40 PM
    I've been working on a homebrew version of Werewolf: the Forsaken because while I like the system a lot and most of the fluff, there are just some themes that I'd rather handle a little differently than they are in the core. A lot of this is drawing from some ideas from Apocalypse, or just my own. I've been looking at the translation guide, but that one's made for W:tA to 1e rather than 2e, and there are some differences. Also that I'm an indecisive little baby I like to hear opinions other than my own, so here goes.

    I really like the theming of duality between flesh and spirit, man and wolf. But I feel like the wolf and spirit should be more separated than they are in the core (since wolves are ultimately fleshy beings). So we have two axes between man and wolf and flesh and spirit, rather than just one between man-flesh and spirit-wolf. That's just a theming shift though.

    My first real problem with Forsaken is only having human-born werewolves, which seems kind of silly when werewolves are from two or three worlds. I get that all the players are going to be humans which ultimately limits our own perspective, but as roleplayers we should also be able to try different perspectives if we want to, and when there are three different sides of the coin for werewolves, I'd like to be able to have werewolves be born as wolves or spirits too. There've been quite a few threads on here about the wolfborn (which are great by the way, have been a great help) as well as in the Werewolf Translation Guide. I'm just trying to refine it myself, especially for how spirit-borns... happen? It's kind of weird. Back in Apocalypse I had my own spirit breed called the Umbrid which was born from a wolf-spirit and a werewolf but I feel like that doesn't really make as much sense in the Forsaken Shadow. I was thinking maybe they'd be like the Unihar and be 'born' sometimes when werewolves get together (minus the whole parent killing thing), but I'm not sure.

    Anyway, so I really like the idea of balance, and while the story of Father Wolf sort of captures it, I also sort of like the triat. Not in like the sense that Apocalypse handles it where everything's sort of out of control with two in too much power, but rather a sort of shifting balances between the three? I can also see some parallels with like the 'Wyld' (creation) being associated with the Hisil and Mother Luna, 'Weaver' (order/stasis) with humanity and the world of flesh, the gauntlet, and 'Wyrm' (balance/destruction) being associated with Father Wolf (destroying things that disturb the balance), primal order. I'd like to try to rework my own origin story with the three forces (possibly three parents?!) for the Uratha, but I'm not 100% sure how to go about it.

    This is probably really rambling and silly, so I'll stop here. I'd just like to see some other ideas about how to make this work and stuff! Thanks!

Now, if you feel that particular duality is lacking in the game, you could certainly add a second axis to Harmony, although you'll have to consider what the effects of the extremes of this axis would be and what kind of actions would cause Breaking Points in which direction.

                                      Okay guys, I figured I'd try my hand at a first draft sort of write-up, just to get a kind of sense what I'm going for here? Revisions and critiques are more than welcome. ^^"

                                      Most Uratha agree that creation began with three great spirits, the Shalas-Maguralath, as they are called in the First Tongue. Iduth, the moon with her shifting faces was the spirit of creation, spawned endless new spirits, concepts, creatures, ever-changing in form, not yet given purpose or place. This would be granted by Helios (?), the steadfast, unchanging sun, who imposed order upon creation, giving his sister's creations a place in the young world. However, the world grew full with the children of the Moon and the Sun, unable to sustain the creations of the two. When things grew out of control, the alpha predator, Urfarah stood up to the task. The strongest, most intelligent and steadfast of the hunter spirits, he would pursue those who burdened creation. The old, the infirm, and those who had no place, all were torn down by Father Wolf. This was how balance was maintained.

                                      The People were born when creation was still young, in the days where the Border Marches ran thin, the days where flesh could walk the world of the spirits and spirits could drink the essence in the lands of flesh. Some time had passed, and as the great three spirits continued their duties they found resentment growing between each other, leading to squabbles and disagreements. "It's not fair for you to destroy our creations!" the Sun and Moon would complain. Father Wolf would only say, "Perhaps I would not have to, were there not so many." These fights eventually began to cause disturbances in the balance, with Luna refusing to allow some of her creations to be given a form, lest their perfection be ruined and they become vulnerable to destruction. Helios would solidify the Gauntlet, his heavy, powerful rays choking out both the Hisil and the physical world. Even Father Wolf would kill without reason, attempting to preempt the other two. From these battles, all of creation would suffer as the balance shifted uncontrollably.

                                      One day, a truce was begged for by the beings of both flesh and spirit, led by a child of Helios called Man, a being of flesh. Though puny and weak compared to the great spirits, they possessed an intellect above most creation and a gift for refinement that caught the eye of the Sun, who heeded their call and gathered the other two for a council. It was soon realized that the three would be unable to see clearly how to find balance, as each had become so focused on their own role, though it was plain even for them to see that the world was suffering for their lack of it.

                                      The Shalas-Maguralath decided to create a being who would find balance for them and to maintain it, something gifted by the three of them, together. Helios called upon a human, Urfarah brought a wolf, and Luna, a Lune. The three were brought together, then bound, the three children of the spirits to become one. Luna said unto the three, "I give you the gift of Change, to become what you must to restore balance to our world."
                                      "I give you the gift of Harmony, that you may remember your own place in the world, as well as minding that of others," said Helios.
                                      "I give you Teeth, child," said Father Wolf, "That you may hunt those who break the balance between us."

                                      By the power of the Shalas-Maguralath, the three creatures became one, an Uratha -- part man, part wolf, part spirit. And it became their duty, and all those who came after to maintain the balance of the three spirits. Ours is to hunt those who wander where they do not belong, who disrupt the natural order, who kill without purpose, who take no form. This is our place. These are our prey.

In the modern era, Hishu are most common, followed by the Urhanu, Ulle'u, and Uralathu in that order.

                                              Hishu
                                              Born of humans in the world of flesh. Maybe they have a werewolf parent. Maybe they don't. Regardless, they're werewolves now. They're the same starting as they are in the core. 7 Harmony, no special perks or problems. They're easily the most common.

                                              Urhanu
                                              Urhanu were born as wolves to wolves. Much like how the human born werewolves before their first change find themselves with stronger instincts and senses, so too are Urhanu burdened with an intellect that their pack mates can't seem to understand. This, along with the glimpses into the Hisil may lead a young Urhanu to believe something is 'wrong' with herself, or maybe the world around her. Things become much clearer with the change, however, though they have much to learn in both the ways of the spirits and of humans. An Urhanu cannot choose Mental Skills to be primary at character creation. They begin play with the Persistent Condition 'Raised By Wolves'. Urhanu start with Harmony 7 and Primal Urge 2.

                                              Uralathu (name pending and sort of a misnomer)
                                              The Uralathu begin their lives as spirits, usually wolf-spirits*. No one's exactly sure what triggers them to change, if it's something chosen when they first form, some trigger later on, or something else, but most Ithaeur agree it is a change they are chosen to undertake by Luna. They're scarce and have been since the Sundering. Whether this is because of Luna's remaining grudge against the Uratha or the difficulty now for spirits to pass the barrier, it is uncertain. Most often during the First Change, Uralathu find themselves tearing their way out of the spirit world and are even more bewildered to find themselves to have flesh on the other side. They begin with 3 Harmony and on account of this must contend with a Ban they had, possibly related to their role as a spirit but not necessarily. However, thanks to their natural affinity with the world of spirits, it is easier for them to cross the border back, treating it as a rote action.
                                              *Other spirits have been known to change. Mother Luna isn't exactly known for being picky and indeed, her whims have marked some strange candidates for the change. It is easiest for wolf spirits, as they are already a part of Wolf's brood, it is more natural for them to take the role of the predator to maintain the balance. For some, related spirits, such as other predator spirits like bears, the transformation is not so challenging, as their role is not so different, but the change for some, like it might be for a pain spirit or perhaps even a lune, is dangerous not just for the spirit changing, but also werewolves. Uralathu do remember much of their time as spirits and tend to carry over old quirks. While an Uralathu born as a Lune may be seen as a portentous gift from Luna, their madness can result in an unpredictable Uratha, unable to keep its role to maintain the balance. In addition, the change is traumatic and dangerous, more so for the spirits than any others. For those without a form like one of the five the Uratha take, the change is very radical and often described as painful. Most never are able to take their old form again. Some adapt. Most die. The Gauntlet is also a daunting thing to cross, and the journey becomes more and more challenging where it is thick, such as in cities. As a result, most surviving Uralathu are from the wilderness and are nature spirits (again, mostly wolf-spirits).

                                              Ulle'u
                                              On the off chance two werewolves happen to have a child together, that child is almost invariably a werewolf. However, those born to werewolves lead more challenging lives than most, even for werewolves. All Ulle'u are deformed in some sense. There are three leading theories as to why this is the case. The first camp contends that because all Uratha are children of the same parents, the pairing can be likened to spiritual incest, resulting in the deformities. Others believe that being half-man, half-wolf, half-spirit, werewolves are just naturally 'too much' in one body. The child is essentially overloaded and is unable to bear all that they are in their bodies, leading to malformation. The third theory is similar to the third, but rather relate it to the curse from Luna. Normal werewolves have the curse in their body which sometimes is passed to their children, but having two werewolves for parents results in a child who is doubly cursed, which shows in its body.
                                              Whatever the case, Ulle'u have been a part of werewolf society since at least after the Sundering. Their existence isn't necessarily against any laws, but it's still considered an ethical question of whether or not werewolves should have children together, considering their deformities. Regardless, they are valued by most as any werewolf and tend to be better at being what they are than most, especially newly changed. They are born either in Hishu or Urhanu form (dependent on the mother's) and don't ordinarily shift until their first change which is around puberty. However, strong emotions can cause accidental changes to the neighboring form of the one they're in. Being raised (ideally) by two werewolves and knowing what they are gives the Ulle'u a great advantage, and having this upbringing makes them better able to maintain a balanced Harmony. If they have a breaking point toward Spirit, they only subtract one die for each point of Harmony below 4 instead of the usual 5, and toward Flesh they are penalized for each point above 6. They begin play with 5 Harmony, as well as the Persistent Condition that marks their deformity. Most aren't able to be resolved.
