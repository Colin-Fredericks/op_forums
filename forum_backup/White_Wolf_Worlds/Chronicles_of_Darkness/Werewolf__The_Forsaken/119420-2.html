    Plus, as said before, you don't need to be in a hunt to have dreams. You don't need to be in a hunt to deal with spirits. Just because your powers are useful in a hunt, that doesn't mean it can't be used for personal affairs, and it doesn't mean you are shoehorned in your role. There are plenty of ways to use your auspice power for things other than the hunt, and that's for some Gifts too.
    And if I understood well, your Elodoth can also use his power to end Kuruth during a social situation where two werewolves might be fighting each other for any kind of dispute, be it pack versus pack, be it pack member vs pack member, and things like that.

    Exalted Solar Castes is much similar, and possibly even more similar to "D&D". Hell, you could actually play Exalted in a Dungeon Crawler style. But not all Dawn Caste are "Big stupid fighters". One might have been a smith and have high Crafts. Or it might be a Dawn Sorcerer. Or a nobleman with high social skills. A Twilight Caste might be a sage warrior.
    Still, all Exalted Castes have a "role" power. The Dawn is good at making people back down, the Eclipse is good at dealing with social enconters thanks to supernatural pledges.

    You have powers, and it's your choice what to do with them.

    EDIT:
    On the same example, a Rahu could well be a master of rituals. It might not be as good as an Ithaeur, but hey, rites are rites.

                                    In general I liked the new write up. Auspice was something that desperately needed expansion in the old Forsaken rulebook. So I'm glad that the new core seems to be emphasizing them a great deal more and making them more obviously equivalent to Clans from Vampire.

                                    As a starting point I'd like to say that I've never really "got" the Cahalith before. The old fluff seemed to go with the Storyteller angle a lot, which never really inspired me. With this new write up I enjoy about half of what's talked about.

                                    The focus on the Cahalith as prophets was excellent, really liked it. Love all the talk about them being everything from bombastic prophets to doom-tellers to academic like historians (is science) I felt that gave a lot of range of character for the Auspice. I think the stuff about how they actually interact with and relate to the concept of "stories" needs to be clarified a bit personally, but maybe their Moon Gifts might add more flavour in that regard.

                                    I'm actually kinda surprised how much I like this aspect actually. As a lot of the fluff here (we'll have to wait and see on the crunch) is not a million miles away from the wishy-washy stuff about "fate" and "story narrative shapes reality" in Changeling that turns me complete off that game.

                                    The stuff I don't love is the "larger then life action hero" vibe the other half of the write up keeps pushing. I think it kinda detracts from the other half of the write up. The tiny almost disclaimer like bits about "but not all Cahalith are extrovert" and "but this does not mean they can't be subtle" really, really don't ring true with the rest of the write up and seem almost tacked on.

                                    Personally, I don't like the idea that a group of beings that are tied to prophecy (which is heavily involved with metaphor and interpretation), dreams (which are often used in media and fiction via symbolism and subconscious drives) and stories (which can be as crass or subtle in nature as the storyteller in question decides the tale should be) don't really have a grasp of nuance. Its kinda jarring.

                                    When this aspect is tied a little more into the prophet fluff I warm to it a little. A Cahalith going out of his/her way to make sure that their prey dies in a way they foresaw in a dream or the idea of taking on the "narrative" role of Death in the prey's story is interesting. I think the Predatory Aspect of Monstrous backs this aspect up a bit and could be used very interestingly by an ST and player who are very much in sinc with the idea.

                                    So basically as a first draft, its good, I'd like to see some revision after redlines to tease out the more subtle aspects to do with how Cahalith see stories, dreams and spirits interacting a bit more perhaps. Likewise a little more effort in expanding the explanation of "non extrovert" and "subtle" Cahalith would not go amiss.

                                    As for Wyrdhamsters point:

                                    His/Her comparison between the Auspice ability and Vampire disciplines is not really a valid one. As that's not an accurate comparison, an Auspice blessing is more comparable with the bonus attribute dot and the unique Clan Bane that each clan gives a character.

                                    An even better comparison would be between Auspice and a Demons Agenda which gives a similar once a chapter ability or about comparable power. As a person who has played in a Demon game, these once a Chapter abilities really do not define a characters role in the game or group on a session by session basis. They come up when its appropriate for them to do so due to in game events or character choices.

                                    The unique Moon Gift that each Auspice is getting is the equivalent of an "unique" Clan discipline e.g. Auspex for the Mekhet. The Shadow and Hunt Gifts would be comparable to "common" (Vigor, Resilience, Celerity) and I imagine the Tribe Gifts will be comparable to "uncommon" (Animalism, Obfuscate) disciplines that appear in different clans.

                                    What I will say is that his point has merit if you have a player group that is thinking in a default tactical D&D 4th edition mentality. As of course the optimum thing is to use the Cahalith Auspice Blessing to give +3 when the group needs it the next night! That's one way to play any roleplay game, its not my cup of tea, and I don't think it helps reinforce the WoD's themes and play style, but its an option.
