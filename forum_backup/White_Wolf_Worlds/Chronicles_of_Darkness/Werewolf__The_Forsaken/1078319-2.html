    In this case, the ST-controlled characters wanted to kill the PC.

            It is when the person who said so is an authority on the subject. That's we cite our sources in academic discourse.
            No, not really. Citing the actual rule book is citing your sources. You chose to site a writer who has an opinion about a system, and i'm going out on a limb here, that I am fairly certain he did not design. If he did then it is very poorly written.

            You know what else they give us? An explicit statement of what happens when a character (such as an NPC) succeeds a Down and Dirty Combat roll. 'The character wins the contest. She deals damage equal to the difference in successes plus her weapon modifier and achieves her intent. If her intent includes killing her opponents, then she does so.' NPCs might not be able to initiate Down and Dirty Combat, but they still participate in it, and still reap the benefits of success.
            This is extremely wrong. I will use a few points here.

            To begin, down an dirty combat is used when the PC's have a advantage, or if it isn't really that important.. It is meant to make combat easier for the PC's.

            From the Core book Pg. 87 - "The Storyteller might decide that a player character can get what she wants without focusing on the details of the fight. Maybe she’s picking on people weaker than her. Maybe she’s internalized the mechanics of violence to a degree that far surpasses the average Joe. Maybe the fight’s not the most important thing going on with regard to the character’s intent. In these cases, the Storyteller can opt to use Down and Dirty Combat."

            This give the hint that at least some of the PC's using this rule have an advantage in someway. In Werewolf the advantage is obvious, they are a super strong creature that shrugs off damage from even grievous injuries that would down a human.

            Secondly, If this rule is truly contested in which a NPC can win and kill their opponent, then it is structured entirely wrong. Down and dirty combat has a structure that suggests that it is PC-centric. It has a list of what happens if the PC fails the roll, dramatically fails, etc. This structure provides what happens if the PC fails, and it does not state anything about NPC's achieving their intent period.

            We can assume this is the designers intent because in just a few short pages they describe a true contested action where PC or NPC can win, grappling.

            From Core book Pg. 89 "To grab an opponent, roll Strength + Brawl - Defense. On a success, both characters are grappling. If the attacker rolls an exceptional success, he picks a move from the list below to enact immediately. During each subsequent turn, both grappling characters make a contested Strength + Brawl versus Strength + Brawl action on the higher of the two characters’ Initiatives. The winner picks a move from the list below to enact immediately, or two moves on an exceptional success."

            Grappling does not have the success failure format because it doesn't matter who initiates a grapple, while it does for Down an dirty combat which does use the format.

            Finally let us just use the rule of common sense. Gauru form forces a person into down and dirty combat. Assuming a Str 2 human who had never brawled in her life was rolling that would leave 4 dice. against a skilled boxer with 6 dice. Assuming the boxer's intent is, kill the werewolf. Assuming the boxer is in a empty room with no weapons. How the hell does he kill the werewolf? It just doesn't make any sense whatsoever! The werewolf in normal combat will shrug off blows and once in awhile get lucky enough to deal serious damage to the human, and after Gauru ends wills till be in a form capable of regenerating bashing every turn and dealing serious damage! The rule you are pushing for is not what was intended, and int he case of a Uratha, can come off as ludicrous at times.

                Intent is used for a combat, yes. But in Down and Dirty, only PC Intentions are mentioned. PCs alone may initiate the roll, and the success and failure conditions as written don't ever mention the NPC achieving their goal. Compare the failure and success write-ups. The success write-up mentions the player achieving her intent. The failure write-up provides two options for the NPC - escape or press combat. The NPCs intent would come into play if the PC failed the roll and the NPC pushed combat.

                For the Gauru form, while incredibly dumb on the part of the NPC, I would say that this pushes them into normal combat. NPCs can't initiate Down and Dirty combat, but a failure allows an NPC to press combat, so I'd call the Gauru ability the ability to force the initial roll.
