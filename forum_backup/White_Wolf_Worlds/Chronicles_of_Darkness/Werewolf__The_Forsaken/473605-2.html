                    Originally posted by nofather View Post
                    Most of the Uratha experience on either side is going to be born out in faith. The Uratha have no evidence that Father Wolf wanted them to kill him, or deserved to die, or wanted them to serve Luna, or that Luna didn't poison Father Wolf and weaken him so that she could usurp control over the werewolves.
                    The Forsaken cause isn't about faith, it's not even about pragmatism, it's about making a choice and sticking to it, even if it means suffering the consequences. The Forsaken aren't the good guys because their faith happens to be right, they're the good guys because they have the moral fortitude to try and keep their territory clean and their people safe, unlike the Pure.

                    Or, to quote Ethan Skemp, the guy who developed two Werewolf games a very long time:

                    The price of freedom is eternal vigilance.

                    That's what being Forsaken is about. It's about the hard duty of stepping up on the wall and staring out into the darkness so that the people sleeping behind you can be safe. It's about refusing the devil's bargain that the Pure accepted, gaining power and allies and unity at a terrible cost to your own freedom and to the freedom of anybody you care about unfortunate enough to be born human. That is unquestionably what I love about the psychology werewolves of both W:tA and W:tF, that hardcore loyalty, the fact that just maybe your fury is righteous, defending the right things for the right reasons. It all gets more complicated than that in play, but that kernel of heroism is what matters.

                    The Sabbat? Eternal vigilance was just a catchphrase for them. Their enemy wasn't just outside the walls. And they weren't defending anything but their own damn sorry skins. They were dogs mutilating sheep and fighting other dogs, all the while telling themselves "But hey, this will make us all the stronger when the wolves show up."

                    Yeah, I'll take a World of Darkness. I'll go with player characters that are complicated and have done bad things and will do bad things. But at the core of it, there needs to be some flicker of sacrifice, something greater than the simple "looking out for Number One for as long as I can." And that's why Werewolf is so dear to my heart — that was what it was about before I came on, and it'll be what people are doing with Werewolf maybe even after I'm dead.
