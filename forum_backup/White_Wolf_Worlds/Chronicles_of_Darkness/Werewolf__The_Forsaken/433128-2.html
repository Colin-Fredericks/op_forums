        Starting from the bottom, yes. If your pack is on the proverbial zoning-law hunt and the Rite was led by your Blood Talon, you'd see quite clearly that the lawyer pulling the strings is a werewolf in Hishu form with 5 dots of Cunning and 2 dots of Wisdom. At least that's how I'd take it.

There's a lot of teamwork that goes into it.

                                Yeah, you're right. Sorry Ham, that was uncalled for. I'm grouchy when I wake up.

                                But in seriousness now, putting aside some design choices I question that I'll get back to later, the Talon might lead against the main antagonists, but that doesn't make him the ritemaster/leader, that makes him the high useful specialist. In all reality, everyone else is going to be good at rites pertinent to what they're doing in the pack, and it's not like you need the other rite effects against the Hounds.

                                But more on point, you say you're hunting Bale Hounds like they exist in a vacuum. They don't. For starters, the Hounds work with using and subverting spirits-they pollute the environment with the Infernal/Negative Essence, they craft the local choirs to their liking, they cow them into spying on the Forsaken and other targets. Same thing with humans, they get them to generate essence and otherwise provide resources and information-the Hounds control them like the spirits in order to control the land. And the intersection between them is bound to happen, to generate shit and and overload the Forsaken and other parties and throw them off the scent, so the Claimed are going to be a big part of their plans. THe only ones who they can't use directly are the Shartha, and they can still take advantage of any local Shartha doing their schtick.

                                So my big question is "Why would your pack only learn the Talon's Siskur-Dah when they have an entire network and structure that stands in the way of them?".

                                Let the Bone Shadows take the Hounds Totem and spirit allies, let the Hunters track down the lose ends, give them allies who can lead against the humans and the Claimed while still relying on them to take charge over the operation. Never a problem, always a solution, You have plenty of tools to make this work without having to turn the Siskur-Dah into an overpowered rite by having everything go off.

                                        I brought them up after that paragraph-I wanted to reiterate what you said before asking about the design of the campaign.

                                        Particularly given the extension of packs and the tools given to make them bigger movers, Werewolf is now, more than ever, a game of controlling the terrain. You have a pack that can includes spirits, humans, stranger things still, and even beyond that you can manipulate all aspects of the modern/spiritual ecosystem to create your vision of a harmonized territory.

                                        The Forsaken don't just hunt humans, they use them! They get them to picket and riot and fund and steal and listen and watch.
                                        The Forsaken don't just hunt spirits, they use them! They get them to cultivate and misdirect and bargain and haunt and listen and watch.
                                        The Forsaken don't just hunt shartha, they use them! They shove them around and herd them to strengthen the gauntlet where they wants and weaken it where they wants, shapign the environment, before they try to kill them.

                                        The Claimed and the Uratha are the ones least likely to be used, but 2nd Edition doesn't just ramp up the fact that werewolves hunt these things, it ramps up that they manipulate them to better grasp their territory. (In regards to the claimed, consider that the Forsaken skirt this by having rituals that make temporary Claimed, as an example)

                                        And that's just the good guys! The Bale Hounds, in fact, have to go and create networks and structures and manipulate their territory like this even more so than the Forsaken because of the particulars they depend on. When you say "This game is built around hunting a Bale Hound pack", you have to acknowledge all the things that pack is doing in order to make things work, which includes a lot of things that aren't Bale Hounds.

                                        Forsaken 2nd Edition really hammers in an awareness of ecosystem and food web, that things have their place in relation to each other.

                                        And quite frankly, I also have to ask why there's a blatant Bale Hound pack, considering they play the sneaky sneak slip into other packs, subvert their lives and corrupt animals into falling, but that's not the factor I've honed in on.

                                                        Nofather and I discussed Siskur-Dah quite a bit today. To be fair, he doesn't agree with all my opinions, but he was great for bouncing ideas off of.

                                                        Here's what I came up with:

                                                        Siskur-Dah should be started with an intention of how the hunt will be ended. Whether this is "find" the prey, "kill" the prey, "kiss" the prey. Whatever it is. But when you initiate the rite, you declare or make known your intentions. Then, this is the intention you must fulfill to complete the hunt .

                                                        So if you start off going to kill your prey, but wimp out at the end cause it winds up being Rambo - then you can't declare "Well, we found him anyway" and call off the hunt and consider it 'successful'.

                                                        The second part is what consequences are suffered when you abandon the hunt (which is really the only way to 'fail' a hunt, is to give up).

                                                        We considered a lot of options, but this is what I like: As a wolf, you hunt . Spirits know this. The wolf must hunt. SO! If you declare siskur-dah and fail to complete it, you'll start earning a bad rep among the spirits. Fail once and maybe it's not a big deal, but fail a lot and they may start considering you "toothless". They may demand higher chiminage or just feel they can ignore you utterly. On the other hand, if you complete each hunt you start - that'll get you a rep too. No one will want to be on your bad side eh?

                                                        So my next step for playing is to think about some more formalized rules to follow that concept. I've never been terribly interested in a 'homebrew', but I guess this would count.

                                                            Originally posted by Elfive View Post
                                                            It ends. That's listed as one of the ways to end the condition. I think it still counts. Nobody's expecting a 100% success rate.
                                                            This isn't a rules dispute, we all know how it works.

                                                            The Uratha are part spirit, and spirits do what spirits do. They come from the Predators Paradise. What happens to a spirit that doesn't want enough? It gets gobbled up by other spirits that do. But mechanically and fluff-wise, very little happens to werewolves when they give up the hunt, fail at the hunt, are fickle about the hunt, or just have eyes to big for their mouths. Rules as written, you even get a beat for resolving the Siskur-Dah condition by just breaking off the hunt, putting one failure of a pack at the same place as a pack that managed to bring down ISIS, for example.

                                                            Mother's Love suggested the 'toothless' condition, and I do think the spirits having a lesser opinion of the pack should be a part of it, but I also would like more depth to the Effective Spirit Rank aspect of the game. I was thinking in the case of a failed hunt, where the prey not only manages to get away but trounces or humiliates the pack, the Demoralized condition may work.

                                                            I'm just putting this out there to see if anyone has any ideas for something to do in-game in the case of a failed hunt. And proposing that if Werewolf gets further books, this is something that could be explored, since we do have writers and developers who peruse these boards.

                                                            Though I will add that I do think a reward for a successful Siskur-Dah against a tribe's sacred prey could be an interesting idea. As is, you only get a real benefit if you tackle a spirit. I wouldn't want it to come off mercenary, but it could add an interesting facet to pack dynamics if the 'alpha' of the pack or whoever decides had to keep in mind the needs of other members, and other members had more reason to stand up and demand their sacred prey be hunted. Plus it might put more meaning behind the term 'sacred prey,' which a lot of people, not just here, seem to misunderstand.
