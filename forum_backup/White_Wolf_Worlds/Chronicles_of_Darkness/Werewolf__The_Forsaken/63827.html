    Removing Luncay and Gauru Time Limit

    02-03-2014, 04:43 AM
    A few people on the thread for the Idigam Chronicle ideas have suggested removing Lunacy and/or the time limit for Gauru form.

    I know they'll be many in favour and some vehemently against.

    How do people think that altering the game by removing one or both of these would change the it - what are the pros and cons of doing so?

    If you wouldn't remove either/both would you consider increasing the time limit or weakening the powers of lunacy? If so why?

    I'm going to go suggest for the scope of this thread that nothing else is being under consideration for change.


    ---

    My personal choice would be to 1. see if anyone responds to the thread and see what others think first 2. be inclined to think that I would like to see them both as optional, with perhaps a sidebar in the Idigam Chronicle as to why. 3. I tended to downplay both of them in my game I ran but did include them.


    EDIT: this is just a personal preference, but if Garou form remains time limited I would very much like to see artwork that mirrors this, as the core book doesn't really reflect this (although the artwork is very good)

                                          While I do think that the rules for each concept need work, I'd rather not see either go.

                                          Lunacy is a benefit for the Uratha. One of the few tools they all have. And even then it isn't a perfect shield. Any decent werewolf-hunter (or simply other werewolf) is going to be able to spot Lunacy incidents when they happen. Unless the rules are changed significantly, why would an Uratha every choose not to invoke Lunacy? The only significant circumstance I can think of would be defending someone subject to it that is also a significant factor in the fight (like a highly trained wolf-blooded combatant). But then your enemies (if they are werewolves which make up a lot of your physical enemies) would want to invoke it at them.

                                          I think the easier solution is to let characters choose to take a "Terrified" Condition automatically/use the chart (aka what most NPCs will do) or roll Resolve + Composure (+modifiers for non-Gauru form) and have a wider array of Condition possibilities. Dramatic failure goes back to being Terrified, failure lets the Uratha's player pick from 3 Conditions, success lets the human pick from the same list, and exceptional success lets them off Condition free. Basically have it work similar to a breaking point check with a bit more direction on the difference between regular failures and successes.

                                          I've always been a fan of the time limit hack that treats your base-time limit as your "safe" turns before you start rolling for Kuruth rather than an absolute limit. Given the relatively short durations of most fights in the nWOD (esp. with the GMC rules) the time limit has never been as onerous as I see people make it out to be. The GMC rules also already boost the Gauru forms power via the increased Defense. The extra attack dice generated by the bonus Strength means that blowing through the increased Defense is that much easier to do reliably. Sure it is still only one more than the next best form, but attack dice are essentially worth more now; and armor is worth more now as well.

                                          Giving the Gauru form a bit more oomph (like boosting the damage ratings on its claws/bites or X-again bonuses) wouldn't be a bad thing along with the gamble to stay fighting option. But the time limit doesn't really need to go for any particular reason. And we certainly don't need to return to "sitting around in war form" as a thing.

                                                      Here's the thematic implications of having a Lunacy with a switch on/off button; or removing it entirely: It's implied the mild form of social discomfort that humans suffer and hinder Uratha steams from it. You remove a core element of what makes Uratha known as Forsaken.

                                                      Why don't werewolves don't go around inflicting Lunacy en masse?

                                                      Originally posted by MASS LUNACY
                                                      If a werewolf induces Lunacy in a crowd, the effect runs the risk of backfiring. A crowd stricken with fear might become an angry mob or a panicked stampede. The Storyteller should make a Resolve + Composure roll for the human with the highest Willpower in the crowd. Success indicates that the person may direct the crowd to some degree. If he simply wants to save himself, the entire crowd follows his lead, but if he is driven violent with fear, the mob might turn against the werewolf. Members of the crowd probably still have problems understanding or remembering exactly what’s happening, but they could be incited to riot or swarm a subject.
                                                      This phenomenon is one of the reasons werewolves are unwilling to reveal their presence to humanity. They’ve seen the danger of mob rule.
                                                      Does Lunacy guarantees that the savage activities of the Forsaken will pass unnoticed by only relying on it? Not at all. The scene of a carnage with bloody footprints which do not match any known animal (Gauru) turning into wolf-paws or a shoe-sole? The witness which actually manages to remember the scene? Investigators will prod at the mystery, and another butchery won't fix things.

                                                          Putting Gauru on a timer is probably the cludgiest possible way to discourage it's use. It meshes poorly with the the abstract flow of combat and has a gamey-ness that really detracts from the experience. The objective was, as far as I can tell, to make Gauru seem like a short fuse, a risky form that could snap at any second. But a time limit does not accomplish that, instead it makes Gauru a gamable, measurable state that encourages turn counting and meta-gaming.

                                                          It'd be far more effective to add compounding penalties and heightened risk of Death Rage, making it so that every action there is a growing chance the Werewolf will lose control. That, in my mind, makes the form scarier and shifts the focus on to whether it should be used, not how long it can be used for.
