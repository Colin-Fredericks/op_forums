    Control is fairly expensive because it's powerful - one extra Control die means one extra die for all rolls. It also increases the chance of Control being dominant, meaning that it's safer to use those Rage dice and your gifts. Control is pretty much the "Primal Urge" stat in that it determines a werewolf's overall power.

    Your Rage trait defines how deep your rage can get, but there's also a slight element of control over rage there. A starting level character with Rage 6 is in Death Rage once he has more than 6 red dice; a more experienced werewolf can have more than 6 red dice in his pool without succumbing to Death Rage.

    Higher gift ratings means you get to use more dice when using your gifts - they also give you more flexibility as to how you use your gifts. I wouldn't allow a werewolf with Mother Moon, say, 4, to take on the appearance of a bird; taking on the shape of a small animal (for example) should require a rating of, say, at least 6.

    Buying additional talents is, basically, learning new (mundane) skills. Without the use of a talent or gift, you always roll Control + your current Rage dice. If you have a talent, you can do a minor use and score 1 additional success for free, or take 1 Rage die into your pool and score the number of your Rage dice in your pool as additional successes, which is very powerful.

        Totems!

        Totem give packs certain advantages - in return for loyalty, protection and adhering to specific bans.

        Example benefits:

        .) 1 additional talent - the talent can be used by any one character in the pack at any time. It takes 5 minutes to pass on the talent to a packmate
        In Pure packs, two packmates may use the talent at the same time.

        .) All packmates receive 2 additional points on a certain gift.
        Pure packs receive 3 instead of 2 points.

        .) All pack mates receive 1 additional success when rolling for certain things (should be as broad in scope, narratively, as a talent).
        Pure packs receive 2 instead of 1 successes.

        In addition, as mentioned before, all members of the pack regain all their Wolf points when performing a Totem rite. Totem rites can be performed up to once per month, at a time set by the totem. All members of the pack must be present to perform the rite.

        It should be possible to increase these boni and make the pack bond more powerful (taking the Pure benefits instead of the Forsaken ones) by selecting particularly restrictive bans.

            NPCs!

            Non-werewolf NPCs generally only exist in terms of dice pools. A human character doesn't have a Control or Rage trait, he has, for example, an "Empathy" dice pool of 5 or a "Close Combat" dice pool of 6; whichever is appropriate. When I think up NPCs, I generally write up three appropriate dice pools and improvise the rest.

            Example:
            Anne, wolf-blooded sister of one of the PCs
            Tough girl: 5 dice
            Socialize: 6 dice
            Stealth: 3 dice

            Humans should always have less than 10 dice in their pools - remember that a starting level werewolf character should have a kinda-sorta safe dice pool of around 5 (3 Control + 2 Rage); giving a human more than 7 dice in something is making him very powerful.

            NPCs have any amount of Wounds that seems appropriate - usually 2 for humans, at least 3 for Ridden, Hosts or dangerous spirits.

            Examples:
            Bear-Who-Defends-His-Young, animal spirit
            Ripping things apart: 12 dice
            Sense of smell: 8 dice
            Intimidation: 10 dice
            5 Wounds

            Frighened little Azlu
            Mandibles: 4 dice
            Stealth: 10 dice
            Poison spit: 8 dice
            3 Wounds

            Spirit-Claimed (confusion spirit)
            Spinning webs of lies: 12 dice
            Creating supernatural illusions: 8 dice
            Self-Defense: 3 dice
            Can change shape when unseen
            3 Wounds

                The Pure

                The Pure work very similar to the Forsaken - they are also defined by the trinity of human-mind, wolf-heart and spirit-mind. They don't particularly value their human-mind, though - and they don't have auspices, which is why they aren't as powerful, individually, as the Forsaken. They still have strength in numbers, though, and their totems are (as explained above) much more powerful than those of their ancestral enemies.

                Character generation:
                Think up an interesting character!
                Pick a tribe
                Your starting Control is 3
                Your starting Rage is 6
                Pick a Talent. You get the talent "Shapeshifting" for free.
                Pick two gifts that are affinity gifts for you. Gain a rating of 4 in one of them, and of 2 in the other.

                That's it!

                Example character sheet:
                Young angry Predator King
                Control 3
                Rage 6 - Stealth
                Pack 4, Savagery 2
                Wolf/6: 3
                Wounds 0
                Moon: 0
                XP 0

                The Pure spend 20 XP for increasing Control, and 2 XP for increasing their gifts over 5 dots.

                Affinities:
                Firetouched: Pack, Territory, Father Wolf, Disease, Madness
                Ivory Claws: Pack, Territory, Father Wolf, Scourging
                Predator Kings: Pack, Territory, Father Wolf, Forest, Savagery


                Disease: this is an affinity gift for the Firetouched tribe. Its thematic purviews are disease and religious fervor.

                Disease can be used to identify, influence, heal and cause various diseases, contagious or not, in people, werewolves, spirits and other creatures. Disease may also be used to cause religious fervor, ecstasy and mania in willing targets. Lastly, Disease may also be applied in combat - successes rolled with Shadow dice count as full successes for the purpose of combat.



                Madness: this is an affinity gift for the Firetouched tribe. Its thematic purviews are insight through madness, inspiration and prophecy.

                Madness may be used to detect lies, sense and understand emotions and sins - most importantly, those of the Forsaken -, change, influence and cause feelings and passions in others, to give hope and inspire allies, to receive important and valid information simply by way of intuition and prophecy, etc.



                Scourging: this is an affinity gift for the Ivory Claw tribe. Its thematic purviews are cleansing through pain, purity and lineage.

                Scourging may be used to inflict pain on others - successes rolled with Shadow dice count as half successes for the purpose of combat. Scourging may also be used to intuitively see and understand family relations, bloodlines and lineages of werewolves and men. Uses of Scourging may destroy imperfections in physical objects, purify poisoned food, etc.



                Savagery: this is an affinity gift for the Predator King tribe. Its thematic purviews are rage, frenzy and physical combat.

                Savagery sees its primary use in physical combat - successes rolled with Shadow dice count as full successes for the purpose of combat. Savagery can also be used to increase - but never to decrease - the number of Rage dice a character or an NPC werewolf has or to cause frenzy in other, non-werewolf beings.



                Forest: this is an affinity gift for the Predator King tribe. For more details see also the Forest gift of the Hunter in Darkness tribe.



                Example characters:

                Experienced Firetouched confessor
                Control 4
                Rage 8
                Talents: Persuasion, Subterfuge
                Gifts: Madness 7, Father Wolf 6, Pack 5
                45 XP

                (a very social-heavy Firetouched who might not be too powerful in a fight, comparatively speaking, but can sell snow to Inuit, and probably has headcased a few Forsaken over to the Pure tribes).


                Predator King Maneater
                Control 4
                Rage 9
                Talents: Brawl, Stealth, Hunting
                Gifts: Forest 8, Savagery 8, Territory 4, Pack 5, Father Wolf 4
                70 XP

                (Death on four legs. A very stealthy, combat-savy Predator King elder)

                    Bale Hounds

                    I love Bale Hounds. They're probably my favourite of the antagonist factions of all WoD games.

                    The fun thing about Bale Hounds is that they're people. A Bale Hound is every werewolf, on a bad day. You don't join the Bale Hounds by approaching the gods of sin and asking to join up, you join them by being yourself - on a bad day. The Bale Hound philosophy isn't scary because it's about alienation from humanity or worship of Outer Evil, it's scary because it's about human vices, human emotions and human feelings gone wrong. What makes the Bale Hounds tragic, sympathetic figures is that their horror is human horror.

                    That said - in my system, Bale Hounds are as different from Forsaken as the Pure. They don't have auspices - having turned to the Maeljin, knowingly or otherwise, each Bale Hound has traded his auspice for the support of one of the gods of sin.

                    Bale Hounds have an affinity to the following gifts:
                    Father Wolf, Pack, Territory, Mother Moon, Wound

                    In addition to that, each of the Maeljin gives his children affinity to a certain gift (thereby also encouraging a very different modus operandi for Bale Hounds depending on who they serve):

                    Asmodai, Queen of Wrath Blood
                    Baalphegor, King of Gluttony Savagery
                    Beliar, Slave to Sloth Spirit
                    Carnala, Lady of Lust Dream
                    Lamashtu, Calipha of Pride Storm
                    Maastraac, Master of Envy Hunt
                    Mammon, Feeder of Greed City
                    Pseulak, Minister of Deception Truth
                    Thurifuge, Architect of Violence Savagery

                    Bale Hounds cannot ever possess a Control higher than 3 - they can't spend XP on Control, and a werewolf with Control higher than 3 who decides to worship the Bale Hounds will instantly lose excessive Control dice, permanently. As a consequence, Bale Hounds are generally more prone to insanity and Death Rage than other werewolves.

                    They deal with this fact of their existence by performing the rite of the Sin-Eater - a summoning ritual that every Bale Hound instinctively knows, whether consciously or not.

                    The Bale Hound enters a Wound, howls for the Wolf Without Soul to arrive and offers chiminage. If the chiminage is sufficient, Viruhk-Ur shows up.

                    Chiminage must always be costly, hard to procure and decidedly unpleasant - the fingers of 5 different children, the tears of a spirit of hope, the head of a wolf-blooded, that sort of thing. Anything less will make the the Wolf Without Soul very unhappy.

                    Viruhk-Ur will then eat the Bale Hound's sins.

                    .) He instantly loses all Moon points
                    .) He may discard one additional Moon point at the end of each period of rest
                    .) He may masquerade as a werewolf of any chosen auspice and tribe

                    The advantages last for an entire lunar month, after which the Bale Hound is required to summon the Wolf Without Soul again.



                    Wound: this is an affinity gift for all Bale Hounds. Its thematic purviews are sin, weakness and corruption.

                    Wound may be used by a Bale Hound to walk Wounds unharmed, locate nearby Wounds, summon and influence servants of the Maeljin, corrupt people, spirits and werewolves with the desire to sin and to violate social norms and rules, tell lies more effectively, etc.



                    Example characters:
                    Angry, confused girl
                    Servant of Asmodai, Queen of Wrath
                    Control 3
                    Rage 6 - Stealth
                    Gifts: Blood 6, Mother Moon 4
                    0 XP

                    Mentor of one of the PCs
                    Servant of Pseulak, Minister of Deception
                    Control 3
                    Rage 8 - Presence, Strategy
                    Gifts: Truth 9, Wound 6, Father Wolf 6, Territory 6
                    30 XP

                    Ancient Wolf Witch
                    Servant of Lamashtu, Calipha of Pride
                    Control 3
                    Rage 10 - Occult, Stealth, Presence
                    Gifts: Wound 10, Mother Luna 10, Storm 7, Territory 5, Father Wolf 5
                    60 XP

                        That's pretty much it.

                        The system has been playtested quite a bit, other than for a few details, such as the Prey gift. There have been two chronicles using these rules, one of which I ran for two years, the other one for half a year; in addition to a few oneshots. I can say with confidence that the system works. It definitely requires more handwaving and improvisation than the official system, but that's just how I like it - it's a natural consequence of a system that's a lot simpler, quicker and easier.

                                    This is a quick, short example of play for my DRYH Werewolf rules.

                                    Characters:

                                    Karin, Storm Lord Cahalith
                                    Control 3
                                    Rage 6 - Socialize
                                    Dream 6, Father Wolf 4

                                    Sabine, Blood Talon Irraka
                                    Control 3
                                    Rage 6 - Awareness, Brawl
                                    Mother Moon 6, Blood 4

                                    Not much backstory needed, because this is an example of play that serves as more of a test run (well, hunt) instead of an actual chronicle. Karin is a social-heavy Storm Lord; Sabine an Irraka who can fight and is pretty good at shapechanging.



                                    So a few days ago, some sort of accident happened and a high school burned down - the remains of a boy have been identified. Karin, through Cahalith visions, has seen a spirit come to being in the blaze; and she knows the spirit will try to possess a human and do what it can to start more fires.

                                    The wolves have caught a whiff of their prey near a prominent members-only night club. Karin's Dream gift tells her the spirit must be inside.

                                    By the by, it's a Full Moon, and both characters have a minimum of 2 Rage dice in their pool - making them more effective, but also a little closer to Death Rage than otherwise. Both characters also have 3 Wolf points and a Moon of 0, making them appropriately prepared for the hunt.

                                    So they need to get inside. But it's members only, right?

                                    Both characters try different approaches for getting in. Since this is something like a test run/demonstration of the system, I'm letting them roll dice.

                                    Karin attemps to socialize her way inside, trying a minor use of her socialize Talent. She also takes 2 dice from her Father Wolf gift, adding a little wolf to her voice (remember how the Father Wolf gift can be used when giving commands and influencing people.) She doesn't want to go all in and take her 4 dice, because it's too early a night to get Moon points.

                                    Karin has 3 white dice (Control), 2 red dice (Rage) and 2 black dice (her Father Wolf gift).
                                    She rolls thusly:
                                    Control: 5 6 2
                                    Rage: 5 3
                                    Father Wolf: 1 3

                                    A fantastic roll, pretty much. There's 1 success from her Control pool, 1 from Rage and 2 from Father Wolf - a total of 5, if you include her one additional success from her socialize Talent. In addition, Control is dominant.

                                    I decide that this is enough for Karin to get in.

                                    Meanwhile, Sabine has come prepared - she knows the identity of someone who's actually invited. In a dark corner, she uses her Mother Moon gift to take the physical shape of guy-who's-invited.

                                    I tell the player that for that sort of thing, she's going to need lots of successes, which is why she's using all her 6 Mother Moon dice. Sabine rolls her 3 white Control dice, 2 red Rage dice and 6 black Mother Moon dice.

                                    This is the result:
                                    Control 1 3 1
                                    Rage 2 5
                                    Mother Moon 4 4 3 4 3 1

                                    The upside: 7 success should be sufficient to pull off the shapechange. The downside: Rage is dominant. I decide that she adds an additional Rage die to her pool. The player doesn't want to burn Wolf points that early, so an additional red die it is.

                                    Sabine, wearing the skin of someone who's on the invitation list, gets inside easily.





                                    So then. It's a club. There's loud music, lots of people dancing. Sweat. Heat. No water. Bodies, moving in ecstasy, to the beat, never stopping.
                                    Desire. Lust.

                                    This is not a good place for werewolves on a full moon.

                                    How to find the spirit, then? Both werewolves look into the Shadow, but they see only spirits that fit into the setting - spirits of passion and lust and alcohol, etc. Nothing fiery or burning.

                                    Karin and Sabine assume that the spirit has already found a human host.

                                    Sabine's player asks to roll her Awareness talent to spot someone showing signs of spiritual possession, and this is supposed to be a game that showcases the system, I let her roll.
                                    She does a major use of her Awareness talent, so she takes on an additional Rage die to her pool.

                                    Control 4 5 6
                                    Rage 1 5 1 4

                                    There are a disappointing 6 successes, if you calculate in the 4 successes from her talent. Luckily, Control is dominant, which means that the player gets to discard one of her 4 Rage dice - making her less effective but also less angry, which, at 4 Rage dice, is a good thing.

                                    I decide that 6 successes isn't sufficient to spot any Ridden, but - Sabine sees something else. There's a group of wolfblooded on the dance floor, and one of the tables has been marked, spiritually, by werewolves not known to the pack.

                                    This is Pure territory.

                                    Not good. The pack needs to hurry.

                                    Karin decides to mingle, dance, make a connection with the night clubbers, see up close if there is anything unusual going on with any of them. She performs a minor use of her socialize talent, and adds 3 dice from her Dream gift, using the Gibbous Moon's gift to make people like her.
                                    Control 4 1 4
                                    Rage 3 2
                                    Dream 6 6 4

                                    Doesn't really work all that well, sadly. There are 3 successes, 4 if you count her talent. And Dream is dominant. Karin, like Sabine, decides it's not yet time to burn Wolf points, so she takes a point of Moon. I decide that she kinda loses herself in the music a bit, enjoying the socializing a bit too much to focus on the Hunt.

                                    Meanwhile, Sabine spots something unusual - a girl with glassy eyes touches a guy, who then gets glassy eyes himself. He then leaves to wash his hands, and Sabine - still wearing a man's body, yes? follows him after a minute or so.

                                    There's no light in the toilet, but, because Sabine is an Irraka and has the Awareness talent and all, I decide she gets to dodge the knife coming for her throat. It's the dude she followed, and he doesn't look so - normal. He's shouting something about this being Pure territory, and attacks.

                                    (He's a spirit possessed, by the way. He has an attack dice pool of 6, and he's NOT the one the pack is hunting for.)

                                    Since this is the beginning of a combat, Sabine automatically regains 1 Wolf point.

                                    Sabine feels that it's time to do a minor use of her shapeshifting talent - taking on a near-man shape more suited to combat.
                                    She then mentally thanks Fenris wolf for giving her that neat Blood gift, and decides to use all 4 Blood dice.

                                    Control 1 3 2
                                    Rage 2 4 6
                                    Blood 2 5 5 1

                                    7 successes, if you include her additional success from her shapeshifting talent. She spends 5 on attack and 2 on defense.
                                    Rage is dominant and Sabine takes 1 additional Rage die.

                                    The Possessed rolls 3 successes, spending all on attack, not particularly caring about defense, since the spirit doesn't particularly care about this bag of meat it's using.

                                    The Possessed has a surplus of 2 successes and Sabine takes 1 Wound. Sabine has a surplus of 2 successes as well, and the Possessed takes 1 Wound too. Both are wounded.

                                    Next round! Sabine feels it's time to use her big guns and transforms into a huge dire wolf, performing a major use of her Shapeshifting talent (which gives her an additional Rage dice, bringing her to Rage 5 and dangerously close to Death Rage). She again uses all Blood dice.

                                    Control 4 5 1
                                    Rage 1 5 4 2 5
                                    Blood 4 2 6 2

                                    9 successes, including her major Talent use. Blood is dominant, giving her 1 Moon point, which she gladly accepts - better than Rage being dominant, in this situation. She spends 6 successes on attack and 3 on defense.

                                    The Possessed rolls particularly badly, only 2 successes, all of which go to attack.

                                    2 successes isn't enough for the possessed to hurt that huge dire wolf. Sabine tears the Possessed to pieces. As she takes on a human shape, she hears the distant laughter of the spirit who possessed that poor guy.

                                    And, with 5 Rage dice, she's still very close to Death Rage.

                                    So much for avoiding attention.

                                    Sabine calls Karin, tells her to see the men's room immediately. So she does. She isn't particularly happy about the body. (At this point, Karin's player ponders that maybe having the spirit burn down the place isn't such a bad idea )

                                    They do what they can to hide the body. Karin then uses her Father Wolf gift to create an aura of fear around the men's room - trying to make people avoid this place intuitively.

                                    Control 6 1 1
                                    Rage 4 1
                                    Father Wolf 2 1 3 1

                                    7 successes, with Control being dominant (allowing Karin to discard 1 point of Moon) - nice. The guys will do what they can to stay out of this room in the next couple hours.

                                    Half an hour has passed, and Sabine has naturally healed her wound. She decides it's time for drastic action.

                                    There's a maintenance room in this place, and the best place to get there, if you're not part of the staff, is by being very small. Like a rat. I think that's a nice idea and a nice use of the Mother Moon gift, so I decide she can use it to take the shape of a rat. I tell her to better roll high, though.

                                    Control 3 5 1
                                    Rage 3 1 2 6 3
                                    Mother Moon 1 5 2 1 2 5

                                    9 successes - and with that, Sabine is now a nice, juicy rat. Rage is dominant - this is the time for Sabine to spend a point of Wolf, making no pool dominant.

                                    After scarpering into the maintenance room, fiddling with a few controls, Sabine triggers the fire sprinkler system, soaking everybody in the entire building.

                                    Fire spirits don't particularly like water, she figures - and rightly so.
                                    There's
                                    lots of shouting and swearing and, one by one, the clubbers leave - only, this time, it's easy for the pack to spot the one who shouts and complains the loudest, whose disgust and fear of the water is plain to see. It's a guy in a (very wet) suit - and, as he leaves the building, the pack sweep in.

                                    Karin tells him to "Sir, could you come with us for a moment, please?", using her Father Wolf gift to force him to come, and boosting her pool with a Rage die - feeling that she will very soon need that Rage.

                                    Control 4 2 1
                                    Rage 6 5 2
                                    Father Wolf 3 1 1 2

                                    Rage is dominant, and Karin gladly accepts an additional Rage die. As the building clears out, the pack takes its prey to the toilet, and tear it to pieces. I see no need to use the combat rules - they've weakened the spirit with lots and lots of water, and it shouldn't really be a challenge.

                                    The prey is down. The Hunt has been successful.

                                    Fantastic use of gifts all around! This has been a fun little session.
                                    I also liked how Sabine narrowly avoided Death Rage ..
