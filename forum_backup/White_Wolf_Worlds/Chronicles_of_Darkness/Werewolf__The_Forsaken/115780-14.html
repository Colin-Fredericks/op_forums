        @Acrozatarim: First, I admit that what you say does raise my hopes a bit, at least from the mechanical point of view (which would be enough to buy the Idigam chronicles for me, since good mechanic compared to the rather messy core Forsaken ones would be worth buying even if I still had to fiddle around with the fluff).

        I still am a bit sceptical, but I do hope it is just that the first impression I got was skewed, especially if some of the stuff I thought replaced for a bit too hunting centred gifts is just elsewhere.

        Wolves are great at survival, they are spread through the entire northern hemisphere, they adapt well to different circumstances, be it different prey, scavenging or supplementing their diet with berries and such.
        They also can survive quite long on a single big meal, in short, they are really good all around predators while usually not being THE apex predator in their habitat and having to contend with very strong prey.

        I hope therefore to see some good gifts that emulate these qualities.
        I admit I am not sure if many of those were in the old werewolf core, but I knew Apocalypse had a few good fits.

        I would also like to see a bit more focus perhaps on especially Elodoth functions as intermediary (That is what I liked a bit more about the Philodox) between the packmates, and not just from the position of "dominating" others into behaving.

        About naure and hunting, I am more worried that at least mechanically, many gifts will be too much tilted towards working as a hunting tool rather than a general tool. Communing with nature to gather information, being able to use animals as more than just hunt support (though, that does seem to work from my reading).

        I think it would be kind of nifty if there was a bit of a divide betweent he more esoteric and shamanic powers and the ones that are more about, well, being a better hunter.
        I think there will surely be those players who want a lot of the shaman and others who want a lot of the hunter.
        Admittedly, sometimes I think it migh be worth thinking about making all of the shamanic stuff more a province of rites, but, I am digressing...


        Mainly, I hope for an overall breadth of choices what kind of werewolf to play.
        I think the theme of all wolves being hunters first and foremost instead of having more of a "wolf that defends the territory" "unseen stalker" "social wolf" and such does not sit too well with me...

        Maybe it is a bit like a typical dungeon fantasy where everyone is defined first and foremost as a treasure hunter, even when, while treasure hunting will be a major feature, many tyes of characters might be along mainly for quite different reasons.


        Lastly, I agree with not being too fond of the "attack spell" powers.
        It is exactly that kind of powers which can give werewolves more of a "super" or "magic user" who just happens to be furry feel which can be rather theme diluting.
        That WAS to my opinion one of the main faults of Apocalypse.

                                                This is where the storyteller needs to remember that he does, in some way, serve as karmic retribution. Or more specifically in the case of Werewolf, the storyteller serves as the spirits bowing to the Gift. S/he also needs to remember the functions of successes and failures.

                                                Let me expound on that second one before going back to the first. In writing, in storytelling, every conflict essentially has six answers, with four being more common than not. It breaks down into two aspects, the conflict resolution, and the resolution modifer. Putting it simpler, every conflict is essentially "Does the character get what they want?" to which the answer is either Yes or No, and each resolution(Yes/No) has some kind of catch or addition, manifested by the words But(when there is a small thing of benefit that comes out of failing), And(which means you magnify the benefit or the failing), and ....(which means you let it stand alone). Thus, every conflict has the potential for six resolutions: Yes, Yes But, Yes And, No, No But, and No And. As a general rule in normal storytelling, you avoid Yes resolutions until the end, and really avoid a Yes And unless you've really made the characters earn it. Otherwise, you drive the story predominantly with Yes But's, No's, and No And's, with the occassional No But as needed.

                                                In the World of Darkness, the four results kind of map nicely to the six, and even better these days where Disciplines, Gifts, Embeds and Exploits are written with the sense of making things happen. Exceptional Successes tend towards the Yes And answer, with the occasional simple Yes. Successes are traditionally simple Yes's, but in the case of disciplines, the attitude behind them is now leaning towards Yes But's. Failures are No's, and Dramatic Failures are No And's. No But's appear in various places here and there. At any given point in a game, a Storyteller needs to remember the spirit of what these mean, and enact accordingly.

                                                On Exceptional successes, it's a mixed bag, because spirits, while not bound to the letter of the rule, might be inclined to play that game simply because it's a good way of needling a werewolf, and what's more is that Yes But's drive the story better than Yes's or Yes And's...but the idea is that you earned an Exceptional Success. It comes to the moment of the story to decide if it's more appropriate to ruin a place regardless of what you want, or to give the player what they want out of the response.

                                                Failures I'm a little more hardline on, and I think people should be too. Failures means things don't work out. The general writing is geared towards situations where it would work against you-you aim to harm an enemy and instead help them. If a player is out to exploit that idea, then it's time to ignore the rules(maybe, read on), and enact the spirit of it. In game, a failure means that you haven't harnessed the respect of the spirits enacting the gift, so of course they are going to fuck with you. To do it deliberately? That invites Renown loss and probably a lot of shit, because now you are shitting on the spirits as well as your own fearsome reputation. Either way, a Failure is to be punished.

                                                The exception is treating an abuse like that as a Yes But....with probably a inevitable lead into a No or No And. Okay, you got a bonus to your car repair. The car runs better. Later when driving, you get caught up in the feel, and you get pulled over, and oh shit, the guy who pulled you over? Touched in just a way that you can't easily get him to supernaturally let it go, and probably isn't inclined to letting you go otherwise. You want out of it, it's gonna be a little messy.

                                                Maintain the spirit of the effects in all things.
