    Warped Dominion: The Carp Hosts and the River Tyrant

    08-15-2024, 02:03 PM
    My irrational and personal hatred of the Pacific Northwest's invasive goldfish has manifested in the form of a Host. I've posted this in a few discords already, and I figured this was as good a time as any to try posting them on the forums. Let me know what people think, I'm quite happy with how these turned out and I'd love some feedback.


    THE RIVER TYRANT

    The throne room drowns in silt and sand, choked by the river’s upturned muck. Throngs of petitioners prostrate themselves before it, sinking hands and knees and inhuman appendages into shifting soil. The barest hints of serpentine coils break through hazy air and hazier Gauntlet, revealing the monster lairing in its depths. A blurred outline churns through the antechamber’s rotting mud, only visible through brief flashes of golden, wriggling scales.

    The River Tyrant, unbound semi-god of the Kukulugalu, holds court. All rise.

    Meninna lorekeepers hoard sightings of the beast across time and space, plucking pearls of wisdom from disparate, cataclysmic encounters. A many-finned monster systematically destroying sacred sites along the length of the Mississippi, eradicating their guardian packs and lodges. A war prosecuted against the spirit nobles of the Yangtze during the Spring and Autumn period, a squirming dragon leading hordes of Magath into battle against massed armies of spirits and Uratha. Its week-long fight with one of Black Wolf’s Royal Avatars over the Nile, which ended with a wolf-headed shadow broken in half, the area’s Hunters in Darkness scattered to the four winds, and the River Tyrant nowhere to be seen.

    Each disaster heralds a new discovery. It appears primarily around rivers and port cities, but always when the Carp Hosts are deeply entrenched and heavily endangered. It acts as a guardian spirit, seeking and eradicating the greatest threats to the Kukulugalu’s survival with overwhelming force. If that fails, then it uses finesse, leveraging armies and retainers with the grace of a natural-born commander. Once the danger has passed, the River Tyrant vanishes, breaking apart into shimmering water and iridescent scales. The Carp Host population plummets in its wake, even as mortal loyalists and Magath retainers find squirming shardlets, kept in secret until the next patriarchs can emerge once more. Unfortunately, there’s more than one.

    The Hunters in Darkness have a theory.

    Once in a blue moon, when the Carp choke the rivers and the Hosts choke the Gauntlet, the Kukulugalu start to swim. Their wretched patriarchs, bloated shards that rule over courts of hybrids and lesser ‘princelings’ put aside old grudges and older hungers to join together, streams feeding into the same river. Ancient alchemies create a fraction of a fraction of the Tyrant River, a wriggling dragon with fish for scales, a composite of hundreds of hundreds of shards that holds itself together just long enough to perform the task it was created for.

    When the River Tyrant shatters - it always does - the Carp Hosts go with it. Small mercies. The shards, however, survive. They’re diminished, scattered, but very much alive. They wait, they scheme, they grow, all certain that next time, the Joining will be a success. Next time, they’ll reign forever.

    The Kukulugalu

    The Carp Hosts, or Kukulugalu, are children of the Tyrant River, the god that claimed ownership over Spirit and Flesh alike in the days of Pangaea. Inside each bloated carp that wriggles through the Gauntlet’s mud is the blood of a dragon, and each one of them knows it. They’re so close to seizing his throne.

    The Kukulugalu form a middle ground between the solipsistic monsters of the Azlu and the hysterical Beshilu collective, organizing themselves into squabbling family units. The least of their number are distended goldfish barely the size of a thumb, soft to the touch as they writhe just below the skin of their ‘parent’. These older hosts carefully cultivate broods of lesser shards that swim between leathery skin and desiccated muscle. Once a body is chosen or an example needs to be made, these Hosts’ scaled fingers stab into their unlucky victim, skin bursting apart as ambitious shardlets seek to claim their first piece of territory. Only one canny Kukulugalu will succeed, of course, but the corpses of their siblings serve as a good first meal for the bloodline’s newest son.

    Bodies that have Carp Hosts swimming through their skin quickly thicken and leather as the Kukulugalu grows larger within their body. This natural armor rapidly ages the corpse, leaving them hunched and wizened. Unlike other Hosts, the Carp don’t need to consume other shards to continue their evolution, using potent essence alchemies to grow larger and stronger. To be considered a patriarch, a Kukulugalu must have mastered the secret of fermenting its own essence; an art that goes hand in hand with spawning new shards. At this stage, the only way they can grow farther is by consuming others of their kind; no the mewling princelings or bestial hybrids, but other patriarchs. It’s much harder than it sounds.

    These bloated old Shartha are petty kings of fetid kingdoms, polluting their territories’ Gauntlets with self-indulgent squirming. Decrepit husks disgorge bloated Carp into the Gauntlet, their passing stirring up spiritual silt that chokes the senses and homogenizes essence. These irresistible globs of piscine ephemera are poisoned chalices for the spirits that consume them; eat too much and find yourself forever changed. Every sort of Magath imaginable makes its home in a Carp Host’s kingdom, courtiers and generals that can’t help but serve.

    A Carp Dynasty’s Flesh-side assets are far weaker, limited to newly-Joined hosts that govern sections of their patriarch’s territory and a few bestial hybrids that lurk in the rivers and the Gauntlet. Unlike other Shartha, the Kukulugalu see hybridization as an indignity, not apotheosis. Melding with human corpses prevents a Carp Host from properly polluting the Gauntlet, from creating its own kingdom and its own brood of servant-children, from running with their fellows to conjure a River Tyrant. Hybrids are few and far between, and while they may be powerful, they’re still slaved to their Patriarch’s orders. For their own parts, the Patriarchs treat hybrids like potent weapons, husbanded and hidden until they need to be unleashed.

    Ultimately, Magath and hybrids serve the same purpose. Like other Shartha, the Kukulugalu consume each other. Unlike other Hosts, each one knows exactly what the boundaries of every other Carp’s territory are. Armies are raised, schemes are hatched, and assassins are made, all towards the same ends; annihilation and consumption. Carp Host dynasties twist the Hisil into a warzone.

    Description

    The River Tyrant is a reflection of one of the Old Gods of Pangea: faceted, multifarious, and utterly inhuman. Like everything produced by the Shartha, it’s a mockery - a dragon made wrong. Filmy fins protrude from its body at odd angles, crusted with river muck and roiling essence. Rows upon rows of dull scales slide across its serpentine bodies and malformed claws, coiling and writhing almost independently. Close inspection shows the truth, pairs of eyes protruding from each misshapen scale that gaze out at the world with callous arrogance. Each scale is its own Kukulugalu, a bloated Carp half-fused with its fellows but still with its own malign intelligence. Every so often, the light hits one just right, and it glows a brilliant, gleaming gold. If the Uratha were to pierce its armor, stabbing through hardened Shartha and coiling essence, they’d find nothing beneath. The River Tyrant is hollow.

    The Tyrant’s head, lacking the horns or frills of a dragon, reveals its true origins. Twin soft nubs, filled with some viscous white substance, protrude from an elongated piscine head. Its twisted, distended jawbone juts out in a monstrous underbite, rows of razor-sharp teeth pushed out from a mouth that was never designed to bite. The River Tyrant’s fleshy skin tears when it does so anyways, knitting together to force its jaws closed again.

    When it deigns to speak, the River Tyrant’s voice echoes from a thousand muffled, gurgling throats. It gives ultimatums, makes statements of fact, pronounces doom with cold, detached glee. It neither bargains nor begs with the Uratha.

    Unlike other Shartha, even the Carp Hosts, the River Tyrant doesn’t fear Wolf’s children. It believes itself to be a higher class of being, as far above Uratha as the werewolves are above the least of its lesser kin. The Tyrant carries itself with the bearing of a spirit noble even when forced out of its Gauntlet lair, standing and fighting long past when the most arrogant Hosts would flee. It takes any opportunity it can to break its foes, exploding out of the Gauntlet and using corded claws, legions of Magath, and its own monstrous bulk to annihilate them. The River Tyrant does its best to leave a survivor, to spread the word and tell the stories. It adores being feared.

    Secrets

    The Hunters in Darkness believe that River Tyrants are born from the collective wills of the area’s Carp Hosts and die once their purpose is fulfilled. These guardian gods are thought to be an unfortunately deadly stage in the Kukulugalu’s life cycle, dying and being reborn as hundreds of squirming carp. Technically, they’re even right. What the Uratha fail to realize is that the malign intelligence gleaming in the River Tyrant’s squamous eyes is the same spark every time.

    Whenever the Carp Hosts’ instincts compel them to choke the river with bloated shards, running fast and thick in Gauntlet and Flesh, they call out to the primordial depths. The Tyrant River’s bloated carcass rotted away in the Bordermarch’s silt long ago, but a god’s ambition doesn’t die so easily. The Kukulugalu’s desperation grants that desire for domination a brief moment of lucidity. It inhabits the hollow dragon they create as it fulfills their most fervent desires, a parody of a Claimed that fits together too well to be one.

    The River Tyrant remembers Pangea. It remembers ruling large swathes of the Bordermarches, an unbound God over Flesh and Shadow. It remembers Wolf’s jaws around its neck. It remembers its past incarnations, all of them. It’s beginning to scheme. The River Tyrant is the lingering shade of a Pangean’s desire to rule the worlds, and every time it’s summoned back into the Flesh, it leaves orders when it dies. Servants and Magath are instructed to migrate, to hide, to husband the newest generation of patriarchs around the world’s rivers. Shards are implanted with new suggestions: move, cannibalize, fight for the same territory.

    The Hunters in Darkness have noted a disturbing trend in recent decades: the Kukulugalu are converging. This is by design. Eventually, decades or centuries in the future, the Carp Hosts will find themselves around the same river, carving up Flesh and Shadow alike into petty, feuding dominions. Eventually, they’ll be threatened by something. A massive protectorate, a nation’s government, the concentrated attention of a God of the Shadow. Then, when the Carp choke the river and the Hosts choke the Gauntlet, the River Tyrant will incarnate once more. This time it won’t be hollow. This time, it will live forever.

    In its few free moments, the River Tyrant idly hopes that the river that hosts its glorious rebirth will be the Yangtze. There’d be a nice poetry to that.

    Rumors

    There’s stories of a forum buried deep in a sea of dead links and abandoned domains, one that has all sorts of info, conspiracy theories and manifestos. They say you can only access it if you’ve got info of your own to share. Weirdest fucking thing though, those stories that people say get you past the first page? They’re all about dragons.

    The River Tyrant leaves survivors to tell stories and spread fear. When those survivors are human, they start searching for answers. Hunter cells grow in the Tyrant’s wake, ancient orders and tight-knit clans that persist through generations. One shell-shocked teenager went to the forums for answers in 2009, and these cells began to find each other. Nezha.com is small but focused, shrouded behind layers of masterful cybersecurity and half-decent wards. Members compare stories and track records, slowly putting the pieces together. They think they can predict where it’ll show up next. When it wakes up, they’ll be ready.

    I saw something last time we reached into the Hisil. Some big fucking thing, all fins and scales and teeth, rooting around in the mud. Same shit as normal, yeah? But our Hunter in Darkness swears on Black Wolf that it was a Host. Don’t know if she’s right, and I don’t really want to find out. The bite marks on our Rahu means that we don’t have much of a choice.

    The largest Carp Host hybrids, piscine monstrosities that barely resemble humanity, spend more time in the Shadow than the Flesh. Here, they tend to courts of Magath and scour the Hisil for unknown ends, waiting for their patriarchs to call them back into the Flesh. Their lairs are potent crucibles of varied streams of essence and all sorts of rare treasures of the Deep Shadow. Any pack that could get past such a hybrid’s defenses would find themselves rich beyond imagining.

    You see the new tower in the Hisil? Yeah, the one with all those scales. Our totem can’t keep itself away from the damned thing, keeps on demanding that we ‘crack the secrets’ and ‘reveal the bounty within’. Only reason we haven’t tried yet is that the Eightbound got to it first, and their totem’s an Ensah.

    When Carp Hosts stir up the Gauntlet, sometimes the homogenized Essence they create congeals into scaled structures in the Hisil. These structures are irresistible to spirits, who can feel the bounty of Essence within but can’t see that it’s Magath in nature, and the Hisil around these areas quickly becomes a warzone. The victor, bloodied and broken, cracks the tower open and greedily devours its prize. The resulting Magath quickly becomes one of the Kukulugalu’s most powerful servants.

    Story Hooks
    • The city's Hisil is in disarray. A Kukulugalu hybrid has gone rogue, gorging itself on the dynasty that once shackled it. It has grown far beyond its masters’ reckoning, a scheming, slavering dragon-beast tearing its way through the Carp Hosts’ carefully balanced dominions. The remaining Patriarchs are terrified of what this abomination represents, and their ironclad grips are starting to slip.
    • A set of Carp Host Dynasties have moved into the area, one after another. These patriarchs know that they’re uniquely vulnerable without their normal networks of servants and children, and have been offering to teach the local power players the secrets of essence alchemy in exchange for their aid in killing the other patriarchs. At least one of them has approached the Ivory Claws.
    • The Kukulugalu are on the back foot, harried and pushed back to the edge of the river bank. Days before the city’s protectorate’s final assault on their holdings is set to occur, a horrific Magath approaches the pack, informing them that the Carp Hosts are planning to conjure a River Tyrant. It offers to guide the pack to where the ritual is being performed, so long as they can save it from eternity as an abomination.

    Statline

    The River Tyrant
    • Attributes: Intelligence 6, Wits 4, Resolve 5; Strength 15, Dexterity 7, Stamina 10; Presence 8, Manipulation 3, Composure 4
    • Skills:Animal Ken 2, Athletics 4, Brawl 5, Empathy 2, Intimidation 5, Occult 4, Politics 3, Stealth 2, Subterfuge 1, Survival 3
    • Merits: Air of Menace, Eidetic Memory, Iron Stamina 3, Relentless, Small Unit Tactics
    • Willpower: 9
    • Essence:50
    • Health:35
    • Initiative: 11
    • Defense: 9
    • Size:25
    • Speed: 22
    • Dread Powers: Armored Hide 3, Destructive Aura 2, Discorporate, Dread Presence*, Essence Fermentation*, Flight, Gauntlet Churn* Gauntlet Cloak, Honorary Rank 5*, Monstrous Resilience, Natural Weapons 3, Stir Gauntlet*, Surprise Entrance, Swift, Taint, Tyrannic Echoes*

    DREAD PRESENCE

    The creature is an ancient monster with a potent legend, one that grows fat off of the fear of its lessers. It gains +2 to rolls against creatures with the Shaken or Spooked conditions. Once per target per round, it can spend 1 Essence to trigger the effects of these conditions without resolving them.

    ESSENCE FERMENTATION (KUKULUGALU ONLY)

    Powerful Carp Hosts can manipulate their own internal alchemy, turning their Essence reserves into a roiling cauldron that bubbles endlessly. The Kukulugalu may take 1 bashing damage to regain 2 Essence at the start of its turn. In addition, it may spend a point of Essence to boost an attribute by 1 for a single roll, up to a +3

    GAUNTLET CHURN (KUKULUGALU ONLY)

    By spending an hour in a meditative trance, costing 1 Essence in the process, a Kukulugalu can churn 10 square yards of the Gauntlet into an opaque morass that scrambles Essence flows and chokes visibility. Characters take a -3 penalty to perceiving across the affected area as ethereal silt and muck chokes their senses. In addition, Churned Gauntlets are perilous to travel across; characters must spend a point of essence or be transported to a random location within the affected area.

    Essence produced within the affected area is blended and homogenized, spat out of the Gauntlet as a mix of conflicting resonances. Beings that consume this blended Essence are naturally more inclined towards the Kukulugalu, increasing their impression level to the Carp by 1 for every X points of Essence they consume, where X is their Rank or Honorary Rank. In addition, spirits that eat this Essence gain the Addicted (Blended Essence) Condition until the end of the Chapter. If they become Magath by eating this Essence, the Condition becomes Persistent.

    HONORARY RANK

    The creature is a potent being in the Ephemeral Realms, even if it isn't an ephemeral itself. It counts as having a spirit rank equal to this Dread Power's rating whenever it would benefit from doing so.

    TYRANNIC ECHOES (RIVER TYRANT ONLY)

    Whenever a creature within the same scene as the River Tyrant takes an action to dominate, assert authority over, or subjugate someone else, the River Tyrant can spend 1 Essence to give that action the rote quality. If the action succeeds, the River Tyrant regains all its Willpower and all other parties involved gain the Leveraged (River Tyrant) condition.
