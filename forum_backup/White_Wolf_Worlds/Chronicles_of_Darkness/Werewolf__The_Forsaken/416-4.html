                                    Eh, the Harmony relation to Lunacy isn't the motivating factor. It plays into it a portion, but it's much, much more to do with keeping humans a relevant threat to werewolves. The interpretations of what constitutes a Veil Breach and how it related to Harmony is actually fairly trivial to me by comparison, but you can bet I want to see Harmony heavily revised regardless given the near entire absence of human considerations in regards to it.

                                    Less so the default food, just abundant and an ongoing temptation. Wolves aren't nearly as common as they once were in many areas; werewolves by comparison often have heavy exposure to humans, even compared to spirits. They're an ongoing, tempting and (sometimes) easy source of Essence; most werewolves would prefer instead to go after spirits for what they need. That said, there was constant talk about how tasty humans were, how tempting, even addictive it was to eat one; there just wasn't any mechanical basis for it. I've given them one. Hence why the first time I treat it as usually happening, is your first (or a later) Death Rage when you're out of Essence. You may get some very nasty werewolves that do it willingly (see Predator Kings) with more consistency than others, but for most, it's a violent rampage and the taste of blood and flesh in your mouth after coming back to your senses. It's delicious, viscerally and supernaturally satisfying, and in turn, addictive.

                                    It's not a very hard compulsion to resist unless you indulge in it frequently (and in turn develop a cumulative -1 penalty to each later roll to avoid giving into the hunger). Frequent eaters are very likely to just do it uncontrollably after a while. Those who fight against it eventually triumph over their addiction and the urges until they decide to relapse, or have another Death Rage where they overtax themselves spiritually again. It basically works like this; your first taste of flesh gives you a Resolve + Composure roll (modified depending on circumstances; someone who did so via Death Rage won't start with a penalty; someone who -willingly- decided to eat, may). If you fail, you're addicted. Each time you willingly decide to indulge again, take a -1 Penalty. Each time you're presented with the chance to eat, fail the Resolve + Composure roll to resist eating, take a -1. If you succeed and resist giving in, you gain a +1 bonus to resisting it the next time. Get an Exceptional success at any point, and you shake off the addiction. Get a Dramatic Failure and... well... that varies depending on what seems most appropriate.

                                    One screwup isn't insurmountable. It'll give you cravings, but odds are you'll be able to triumph over them initially. Deciding to give into to consuming other wolves and humans and such though... well, that's when the problems arise. Constantly burning through your Essence reserves and going into Death Rage... that will eventually do a number on you. You don't walk into the game going "mmm, long pork, I want my baby back baby back riiiibs", but it may certainly happen if things aren't kept in check. One thing of note is with the house revision I'm working on, we are thinking of borrowing a nod from Requiem in that prey animals may give you a bit of Essence at 1-2 Primal Urge if it appears that our harsher "you get Essence from Hunting, -maybe- from other rare situations" is too harsh.

                                    As for Tank Tearing... and? Never heard of the Strength, Rage, Shaping or other powers that can let you tear the shit out of things like that? That's not remotely to me any incentive that Gauru form alone can do that. That's effing ridiculous. Go figure, the Blood Talons are also the group that have common Affinity for Rage and Strength. Iron Rending. 'nuff said. It's not so much as being far-fetched; there are powers that will let werewolves do just that, and I've no problem with that. I just don't see it necessary as being an innate thing of being a werewolf. You're also entirely misunderstanding the context I was using; I wasn't talking Werewolves vs Literal Tanks (that's being more than a little pedantic), I was talking the idea of Werewolves as the RPG "tank" sorts. You know, being hit with mortar fire, running over land mines, taking a few shots from an automatic shotgun to the chest and brushing it off... really, really not my thing. To use a Marvel example; when I think Werewolf, I think someone more like Wolverine. He gets hurt, -badly- but he recovers eventually from what would kill a normal person and ready to tear some new assholes. I don't think The Incredible Hulk or Juggernaut (oddly, if I were to make a very, very loose comparison I'd say I think that with Promethean a bit) who pretty much just pushes past it . That could just be my internal paradigm there, but that's where I'm at. Let purchased powers cover the potential to 'tank out'.

                                    I think folks are intentionally going for absolutes that by not being like that, that means your werewolf is weak. On my end, I see little reason for them to band together as packs for strength when each one individually is a "tank" like that. They don't need to be -weaker- compared to their current iteration to justify roaming in packs. On the other end, I don't really see much benefit to turning it into a situation of an antagonist arms-race/escalation to pose their antagonists as consistently challenging or meaningful threats. That's part of the problem when things just start getting beefed up and more beefed up especially out of the gates; there should be incentive beyond simple instinct to need packs, and (this from an experienced W:tA player by the way), I really shouldn't be hitting a situation where literal heavy military weaponry is a cake walk early on. If I bring out a Thunderwyrm (using W:tA here), I expect it to be a big, very challenging, meaningful antagonist. Yet... I've seen a pack of rank 1s and 2s make mincemeat out of it in less than 5 rounds without some crazy ass rolls.

                                    That's precisely the sort of thing I don't want to be running into here if you get what I'm trying to say. I don't want them puny, weak or otherwise pushovers, and I don't believe they are right now remotely within the context of their own game. I don't want them de-buffed as it were all over the place to support their pack natures. I just don't want the equivalent of a nuclear Cold War escalation and proliferation of armaments (ugh, see Exalted in the early editions when new Exalts came out for an example of that, until Solars became less relevant in the role they were intended to be), just because another splat finally got some much needed beefing, or because the werewolves used to be much more tank-like in the games predecessor. Make sense? That's really the reason I bring it up; I've gotten folks constantly pushing that angle and now with Blood and Smoke, it's rearing it's head right back again. Not a fan of that. Not a fan.

                                        On the subject of Lunacy: with the existence of the new Integrity rules for mortals, and particularly their Breaking Points for witnessing violence and/or the supernatural, I'd like to see Lunacy scrapped as a supernatural phenomenon in its own right. People still freak out when they see a man turn into a beast that starts shredding everything around it; but they do so because seeing a person transform is unsettling, and seeing a monster tear things apart is scary — not because werewolves generate some sort of spiritual “aura of fear”. To the extent that special rules should exist for mortals freaking out when they see a werewolf, they should be “mook rules” that are designed to make the Storyteller’s bookkeeping job easier by not having to track Integrity levels and Breaking Point rolls for mooks.
