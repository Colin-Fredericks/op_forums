    triggers, aspirations, breaking points: Wo easy to play?

    06-01-2015, 10:18 AM
    Currently I am preparing to run a Werewolf chronicle after a four year hiatus. The idea was, originally, to use 2e. Now I am not so sure anymore.

    The first two players have made their characters; when I asked for aspirations, I drew blank stares. Logically, because their characters are still fresh, unplayed, and there is not yet any experience of the setting in practical gaming.

    Breaking points and Kuruth triggers elicited concerned stares. In WtF 1e, Kuruth was manageable. As Death Rage is now more present, and is infectious, I envisage more than one chronicle to crash down in the wake of a tornado of destruction, unwanted by the player characters and perhaps even unwarranted.

    Harmony going up and down, Kuruth triggers, Touchstones - how often (I am really curious; this is not a rhetorical question for the sake of criticism) do these things stop the flow of the game? I prefer not to have metagaming slow down things in the midst of adventures.

    And, oh boy, is there really not a Resolve plus Composure roll when a Kuruth trigger comes along? Wasu-Im just sets in? How often to adventures blow up before they really start?

      10 sessions into my Paris game and we've not yet had a single Kuruth trigger so far, thankfully; as long as characters are hanging round the middle of the Harmony scale, Death Rage will rarely be a threat (which is good because when it *does* happen it's a pretty major thing).

      Aspirations come up at the end of every session when we go round checking them for Beats. Early Aspirations can be simple things that get replaced with more important goals later on once the characters flesh themselves out - establish the pack's territory, fight the Pure, strike a bargain with a spirit. Aspirations also don't have to be purely the IC goals of the characters, they can be the OC goals of the players - lose a fight with a spirit, learn the Fetish Rite, and so forth.

      Breaking Points depends a lot on what the players and their characters actually do. In my game, we didn't do any custom Break Points at all in the beginning; I've developed my ideas of what should be Breaking Points for the various characters over the course of play. This hasn't really been an issue.

      Touchstones are great. They ready-supply you with NPCs, plot hooks and places that you can simply throw into the mix to heighten the importance of what's going on in the game.

      - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer

          Hi Rulandor, welcome to the forums!

          I've been running a 2e game with 5-7 players for about 4 sessions now: we've had one rage happen thus far. We've only had a handful of Harmony checks.The rage in question was fine because it was mid-combat, and after the combat was over the player calmed down with decent Resolve+Composure checks to act normally (remember that they can make Resolve+Composure rolls to act non-violently, page 102). I usually call for Harmony checks after the scene is over, before the next one starts (but that's my policy regardless of the game I'm playing, not unique to werewolf).

          Remind players that Aspirations don't have to be in-character, they can be things the players want to see. If they want to figure out how Raging works, say an Aspiration could be "Successfully stave off the Rage". If they want to meet an interesting NPC, have that literally be an Aspiration: "Meet an interesting NPC". Heck, even "Figure out a third Aspiration" could work. I let my players back in Vampire 2E pick that because they were having a hard time coming up with a third one.

          Also remember that only Basu-Im comes with the "Domino" effect. Wasu-Im is fine. Touchstones merely give a bonus to your Breaking Point checks: otherwise they exist to be fun, personalized things to interact with.

          Overall, I don't think any of the rules get in the way of play. That having been said, if you're playing with a group of new players, you may want to ignore some of those rules until you all have a better hang on things, then introduce them to the game one-at-a-time.

          I hope that these answers are helpful, and I hope that your game goes well.

                  I haven't had anything really stop us in our games outside of a few rule questions for object damage and checking up on gifts. We've had our 10th session on Saturday.

                  Aspirations are pretty easy, and something your players can wait until they've gotten the setting figured out. Mine are meeting local law enforcement, finding out about this social event coming up, and finding this hitman who failed to kill us. Essentially, they answer the 'What does your character want?' and 'What do you want for your character?' questions. Ideally the storyteller looks at the players' aspirations and plans for them, but if not a player can just pick easier-to-reach aspirations. The storyteller also factors in on the totem aspiration, ours is ruin some woman's reputation.

                  Breaking points are just a roll, nothing more complicated than Harmony in first edition, except you don't get your character sheet taken away.

                  Death Rage is different but better, essentially, since it doesn't lead as much to intrapack murder and you can personalize your triggers for your character. If your players are really concerned about it, everyone gets two dots in Rituals during character creation and it only takes one person picking up the Chain Rage rite, which should help them avoid full Kuruth. We've had one full group death rage that ended with the enemies torn to shreds.

                          I've been running a 2e Wolf Chronicle every week since 2e was released. Me and my troop came too 2e from 1e after a long hiatus as well. I know this is eleven days after the Op. But I thought I'd give our experiences thus far.


                          Aspirations

                          Aspirations were tricky. A lot of debate was had over how to play Aspirations. My troop was highly concerned about the lack of EXP after the overhaul. One player was fresh to the setting coming from Apoc. So I basically came to the first game with their aspirations prewritten. I would encourage you to follow the advice of giving aspirations away. Two Asps that are short term enough to be completed in the first session. The Third long term and ideally completed in the next session or the third.
                          The first session was somewhat scripted. I gave the players and outline of what the ‘plan’ was for them from an Authority NPC and I told my players the goal was to bring everyone up to speed on the Combat, Gifts, and other basic mechanics. I used the session to get my players pumped about their wolves. I let them feel powerful and I gave them good mystery to solve at the end. At the end we talked Aspirations. Somewhat prewritten. I was able to guarantee the players saw the pattern intended. So the last thirty minutes was me just helping my players write their next aspirations.
                          At this point my player are recording their subsequent Aspirations as the current session plays out. Once an Aspiration is met I let them take the beat and replace it mid game. Thus far I’ve used their Aspiration list to direct the Plot of the Chronicle. Most of what we have accomplished is based on their aspirations, I still have months of plot untouched I originally conceived of having completed by now.
                          For example. Our Bone Shadow found that burial site for a Heroic Hunter in Darkness was constructed symbolically consistent with Rite magic. He wrote down his long term goal as to research what this rite was and why it was built. In our third session he brought up his interest in fulfilling this Aspiration. So I had him roll a Research extended roll. The character is a minmaxed scholar type. So the 25 sux goal was attainable but still challenging. Because the rolls were daily I largely controlled when the research would be completed through IC time. As he went forward I gave him fragmented information at ten and 15 sux. He Esuxed on an early roll, so I created a Condition (Eureka!) Where he could make a single die roll when he was confronted with thematically matching ideas The rite is a Underworld Gate, so anything he experienced related to death like a fresh corpse or a substantial amount of Death resonance.
                          At the end of each session I awarded him the Aspiration and he left it on his sheet as he was actively doing the research he wanted to complete. When he finish the research I awarded him a full EXP as gift for keeping at it AND he now has the write up for the Underworld Gate Rite. He can buy it to use when he wishes.
                          That was five? I think sixe sessions of his character seeking out information and his research driving some of the choices of his pack-mates. And this is but one example of how My player’s aspirations have taken over the narrative of the game. I couldn’t be happier. My plot is more meaningful because I can weave it directly into material they are cultivating through interaction and motivation. My plot is also being stretched longer between the goals they want complete. Aspirations may be the best change of 2e all around.

                          Kuruth Triggers

                          My Players have death-raged only once. Even Wasu-Im is difficult to trigger and as the game progresses I’m trying to intentionally set of their Specific Triggers. One wolf has fallen toward Spirit and another just fell toward Flesh tonight. There have been moments when we all ran to the Core to look up those rules for Kuruth and were relieved (in my case frustrated) to learn the wolves were still of sound mind to act.
                          After so much activity. I sense players are ready to lose it. They are taking greater risks and thus far down some things that would have annihilated them under the old system. The Meta understanding of the balance is as much fun for them as the in game horror of letting it fall out. The routinely talk about how they should get themselves to five to better take advantage of the system. Meaning they are testing the Canon statements of what breaking toward spirit means from seven (six and eight as of tonight). They are so busy trying to get their goals in place I don’t feel I have keep them on track for the session and everything is running smoothly.

                          Harmony

                          As for tonight, a wolf lost a gamble and thus failed the Storm Lord Oath (A break toward Flesh) Concerned they were not living up to Winter Wolf’s ideal the troop summoned a frozen Wolf Spirit named Glacial Fang of the Winter Choir to inspect the Storm Lord. The Spirit asked permission to proceed (without explaining anything) from the Storm Lord then tested the player with an attack they saw coming. The player pinned the spirit to the ground, but felt their hold breaking as the spirit seemed to muster greater strength. The spirit directly threatened to attack the Storm Lord’s pack. So the Storm Lord held strong, but again felt the Spirit’s physical strength increase and the grapple grow harder to manage. So the Storm Lord relented.
                          Thus revealing weakness as the spirit stuck a pack mate (in farce). Inept Dramatic fail latter and the Spirit had the Storm Lord pinned down in reverse. The frozen Spirit cursed at the Storm Lord and told the wolf “No, you are not a child of Winter” Breaking the player’s connection to the tribe. (This was somewhat agreed to before the game started. The player wasn’t sure the Storm Lords was a right for her wolf.)
                          Giving into weakness and allowing a threat to pass him by was a clear violation of the Tribal Oath. RES+COMP -2 -2(for being at 5 +2) rolled a fail and the former Storm Lord fell toward Flesh. As this happened the other players were silent. Afterward they all were ecstatic over how epic the whole thing was. Sure the wolf is now a depressed wreck. But for all three players the Harmony rules came alive. Something I never saw happen in the five years I worked under 1e.

                          Wasu-Im, Basu-Im
                          That RES+COMP roll needs an Esux to float away (without an Elodoth, Our pack has one.) In that time the wolves are rolling like mad to get that Esux. The one time it failed. The Elodoth of all wolves was the one to fail it (Also the now former Storm Lord). The Elodoth was able to keep clear enough to get himself into an elevator. And the Ithuer was smart enough to facet the elevator to break down. (foreseeing the elevator being summoned to another floor and opening like the worst jack-in-the-box ever. My players are quick on their feet.) With that the only thing keeping the pack from having a hang-over the next day (covered in blood) was a flimsy elevator door.
                          It all worked out. The Elodoth lost control was a moment the players went ‘oh shit’ For a brief twenty minutes they were afraid of what their wolves might do. They were afraid. I was beside myself.
