                                        To be fair to Charlaquin, a lot of players I know had issue with the old Harmony system and Werewolf culture in general.

                                        Once again, need to frame this as the fact that they've had terrible experiences with the game thanks to a terrible ST. So they are not inclined to look favorably on the game.

                                        They take issue with the idea that after the first change its kinda like BAM! your on Harmony now, not Morality. Just by dint of you gaining this template you suddenly don't have to play by Human morals anymore. Also some things that would have been inconsequential to you an hour before your first change are now big deals! Oh and you except all this out of hand, because you know Spiritual enlightenment of your true destiny or whatever.

                                        Basically they would prefer a Harmony system that shows a persons gradual "slip" into the Werewolf mindset that is reinforced by a culture that has an objective "right" view on how all this Werewolf stuff works. Luckily, the new version of Harmony seems to do this so I'm pretty sure even a group as "damaged" towards Forsaken as my local one could give Idigam another chance.

                                        As for Tribal bans and such, I kinda hope Idigam takes a page from Demon and has them be "Tribe Conditions" that give you a positive effect in regards to the your specialist type of prey but give you a negative effect as well.

                                        In general, I think it can be said from the accounts of a few different people on this thread that a good portion of people who are ST Forsaken seem to have got the wrong end of the stick when running it. Alternatively one could say they've placed the emphasis of the game in the wrong place. Which might have something to do with the phenomenon that Satchel pointed out previously about the way information is presented in the Werewolf books.

                                        I honestly can't say how common this phenomenon is in general. All I can speak for is my local group of roleplayers and a couple of other groups I've met at conventions here in the UK. Still it seems to be more common over here with Forsaken then say Requiem or Changeling. Which is kinda odd. Also very unfortunate for the game line.

                                                Presumably a more concisely written book would help with storytellers not getting it right.

The thing is, the Pure book didn't come out for a long while after the corebook came out (over a year), and even then you might not have bought it.

                                                        First, my apologies for taking so long to reply. I ended up more than a little busy, unexpectedly. That said, some replies. :-)

                                                        Oh, wait, a disclaimer: this is all my personal opinion ONLY. Do not consider anything I say here as a spoiler for the book, nor is it necessarily how the book is written. I am only one person out of an entire team working on the thing, and I'm certainly not the developer. Further, I'm speaking from the point of view of the game as it is now, not the book coming out. It wouldn't be fair for me to use information in the discussion that I can't expect others to have. Just keep that all in mind, please.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        Not to be presumptive on my part (well maybe a little) but wyrdhamster has already mentioned previously in the thread that he is not a huge fan of the Shadow and its inherent animistic nature. I suspect his issue with Forsaken is that the majority of its antagonists find their home/origin/nature defined to some extent by the Shadow.
                                                        I can understand not liking something; that said, with as much as Forsaken tends to interact with the Shadow as a default, the base game probably just isn't to those folks' taste. I don't say that as a bad thing, nor as some sort of silly "find another game to play" scenario either. It simply is what it is. I don't care for seafood; as such, I tend to not set foot in Red Lobster. Now, I have, because they do have steak and I can eat that (although it still had a bit of a fishy taste to me), but it's not the default assumption of going to Red Lobster for dinner. Similarly, if one does not like spirits or the Shadow, then the default game of Forsaken as currently presented requires some massaging to get it to one's tastes.

                                                        Now, I think that The Idigam Chronicle makes it a bit easier to do that than the previous core book, but then again I'm biased, so take that as you will.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        I can voice my thoughts on the antagonists within the Forsaken gameline with a little more confidence however, I'm kinda fascinated to here a writers thoughts on these same issues so yeah.
                                                        I'm not sure that I necessarily have any greater insight into things other than the fact that I've played and run Forsaken since it came out. Actually, before it came out - I was lucky enough to win a seat in the Golden Ticket LARP 2005 (I was part of the Camarilla at the time, and that was a potential reward for recruiting new members. Bit of a story to explain, I guess.). Regardless, as part of that event, I got to play in a game of Forsaken run by a White Wolf staffer at the time, and we got a bunch of swag. But I don't think my opinion "as a writer" has any more weight on its own merit than someone else's, you know? Everyone's opinion is valued; some merely have more material to work from. It's not like the difference between, say, someone who's a high school science student and Neil Degrasse Tyson. :-)

                                                        Before commenting on individual antagonists/prey, I'll say one thing that governs all of them: you get out of your antagonists what you put into them. In my opinion, that's true of gaming as a whole, but from the ST's perspective, I find that interesting and well thought-out antagonists help drive the story. If your antagonists are flat, one-note villains, then that's all you're going to get out of them.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        Form what I remember (considering I have not read a Forsaken book since about 2010) their Antagonists roughly are:

                                                        Other Werewolves: Divided into: The Pure, Bale Hounds and rival Forsaken Packs your PC's have issues/history with.
                                                        All potentially true. I think these guys provide interesting moral issues. Imru Nu Fir Imru, after all - the People Must Not Murder the People. The characters may certainly choose to kill other werewolves if they like, but it is not only against their own laws, but they also have to check against their Harmony. Plus, the Pure provide a dilemma themselves - from the outside, they appear to have more than the Forsaken. The spirits don't hate them, their packs are bigger, they have more territory, etc. It's only when a werewolf changes sides, thinking the grass is greener, that they find out the truth on the inside, that the Pure are a terrifying cult basically. I tend to play the Pure as a "there but for the grace of Luna go I" antagonist.

                                                        The Bale Hounds I've already shared my opinion on I think.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        Spirits: Divided into: Mageth, inherently "negative" spirits of concepts such as murder, rape etc. Arguably any spirit that screws with stuff on the wrong side of the Gauntlet.
                                                        I actually wouldn't separate spirits into categories. First, they don't necessarily fit neatly into any taxonomy. Second, I like screwing with my players' heads. I helped run a plot in the local Cam game wherein the plot took place in an amusement park within our domain borders. What was happening was that people were having heart attacks or other physical ailments when riding a number of thrill rides in the park - roller coasters and such - and a few had died. One might think the roller coaster spirits had something to do with it, or some "negative" spirit, but no. Spirits of joy were simply using their Influences to make people have even more fun, which sometimes translated into serious health issues when combined with adrenaline on the thrill rides. Taking something most folks see as "good" and flipping it into an antagonist was interesting, and the players were noticeably shocked when they found out what was going on. It was a good time. :-)

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        Spirit Hosts: Divided into: Urged or Claimed
                                                        I've used the Claimed more often than the Urged, I think. Even so, I honestly rarely use them unless the story is going that way. That's just personal preference though I think.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        Shards: Spider, Rat, Raven, Locust, Mushroom, Snake, Wolf? (Not sure about that last one, also sure I am missing some)
                                                        The Adarasharu (the Wolf Hosts) showed up in Night Horrors: Wolfsbane, so you're right there.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        Maeljin: Divided into: Maelinet and Maelin I believe. If your using Inferno then the "possessed" from that book might come under this group as well.
                                                        The Maeltinet are servants or avatars of a sort of the Maeljin, yes. And sure, I could agree with putting the Possessed in that category, dependent upon how one views the Maeljin.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        Idigam: Can't speak to any changes in the new book, but to my understanding at current they are primordial spirits with no reflective aspect in the material world, making them "impossible" my normal spirits standards. May or may not embody the every idea of "change" or "evolution" in some regards, subject to debate. All are monstrously powerful and basically impossible to permanently kill, above and beyond even normal spirits.
                                                        They are certainly very tough to kill, yes. You basically nailed these guys.

                                                        Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                        Out of those 6 categories all but Other Werewolves (and even they involve Spirits (Pack Totems) and the Shadow (using it in warfare) by their nature) are basically spirits in one fashion or another. Just to tie this back to issues wyrdhamster might have for a second.
                                                        Right, although honestly I see the Hosts - or at least the two default ones, as the game doesn't assume a Storyteller is using any more than those - more as physical antagonists. Sure, they interact with the hisil, but most things in Forsaken do because that's the game. Again, the game is just a toolkit and it can be played without the Shadow, but the default assumes it.

                                                        Also, I'd respectfully submit that you've omitted an antagonist. And that's okay because it seems most folks don't count humans as a viable antagonist for werewolves. And certainly, in a face-to-face fight? A human is going to get pasted in short order. So don't use them that way. Use them in oblique ways. Maybe part of the pack's territory is being rezoned by someone in city council. Is it on purpose? Does he know about the werewolves? Or is it just an accident that the territory's resonance is going to change, and new spirits will be created while old ones may die (thus shifting the spirit politics around, and maybe destroying one of the pack's spirit allies)? How does the pack deal with the problem - they can't just walk into a city council meeting, shift into Gauru and unleash hell. Well, okay, they can I guess, but boy does that create more problems than it solves.

                                                        Maybe I'll revitalize my blog at some point and do little advice columns on the various antagonists. That's a thought.

                                                        Hmm....this is getting very long. I think I'll cut this here and do the rest in a second part, just to not hit the character limit. (Hopefully.)

                                                            Okay, continuing!

                                                            Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                            I like the rival pack thing because its very Human and no one is out-and-out the bad guy. Its basically like watching to human gangs go to war in something like The Wire.
                                                            I agree, although personally, I try my best (with differing levels of success) to make sure nobody is an out-and-out bad guy. But then, I like painting with various shades of grey in my games when I can.

                                                            Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                            The Pure never did anything for me, fanatical extremist Werewolves that believe in a return to a more primitive world where they are the top-dogs is a little 1-note for me. They remind me just a little bit to much of some of the 2nd Ed Masquerade stuff on the Sabbat, before they got fleshed out to be a more complex socio-religious apocalypse cult/church of Caine. I can't even put my finger on what I feel is lacking about them, only that they feel slightly redundant to me.
                                                            Fair, although have you read The Pure? That book got me thinking about the Anshega in a different light and opened up new story ideas for me.

                                                            Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                            Bale Hounds, well I've already talked on this issue a bit in this thread. Thought later posters comments have got me thinking about them in more Faust/Dorian Grey aesthetics that I can dig. So I guess I could say I like them as complex self-involved villains and not cackling infernalists.
                                                            In a WoD game, I've yet to see a time where a cackling infernalist is a good idea. That may just be me and my tastes, but still. I like some complex villains.

                                                            Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                            Spirits, kinda already made my supposition here. Either you enjoy Spirit plot or you don't. I don't have a huge issue with it, I know people that do. Only so many times you can play the twenty questions game of "what sort of spirit is it" and "what are its bans and how do we find them" etc. I find making my Shadow more like the other world of the Silent Hill games and its denizens about a similar sorta tone is enough that the creepy aesthetics and atmosphere is enough to keep players engaged for the most part (at least in the context of Hunter and Mage, never run Werewolf).
                                                            I try to play up the spirit politics angle. It's hard sometimes, because spirits have motivations that don't necessarily correspond ours, but I try to make it interesting and a bit alien. In the past, I gave one character a Pyromania Derangement (although man am I glad we're moving away from Derangements) after making a deal with a fire-spirit. Another character found a minor time spirit to ask it a question, and made the mistake of starting the conversation with "May I speak with you a minute?" to which I responded in-character, "Sure. You have one minute. Go." The look on that player's face was priceless. I then got the "You know, I should have frelling knew better than to put things that way" comment.

                                                            Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                            Spirit Hosts add a human element that can be interesting and throw up moral dilemmas for sure. Particularly because possession can make a plot deeply personal I feel. Ran a possession plot as part of a Hunter Campaign and it was the plot that I got the most positive feedback from my players on. I think it grounds or internalized Spirit plot which can otherwise be very, very external in nature.
                                                            Since your next section is Shards, I'm assuming you mean the Urged/Claimed here?

                                                            But yes, that is exactly what I've used them to do, to hit players on a personal level. Loss of control is a terrifying thing, as is hopelessness. Putting those into a story (in a respectful measure of course - it's too easy to go overboard) can really lend it gravity and make the story interesting and cool.

                                                            Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                            Shards are creepy and other and have a slightly different feel from your default Spirit Antagonists, but they are a "known" threat a lot of the time. The People know how to deal with them, what they do and where they come from. I've seen game where clearing out the Rat and Spider nests is almost treated like janitor duty by the PC's because its just seen as "a job" inhered from Father Wolf and with little real feeling of feel (though a healthy respect for the danger involved).
                                                            Well, both Hosts directly do bad things to a territory. In the case of an Azlu infestation, as the Shadow is cut off from the physical by a strengthened Gauntlet, folks start to become, well, less alive. The thickened Gauntlet can lead to Shoals and the like, causing depression and so on, dragging down the whole territory if not stopped. The Beshilu bring with them a whole host of problems, from spirit invasion more likely, to more disease-spirits and the like. When I use these antagonists, I try to make things more personal. Things are happening in the character's turf right now. This isn't because your great great great (keep that going for a while, I'm not typing it out) grandfather didn't get the job done. It's because monsters are messing with what's yours. Tonight. Are you going to do something about it, or are you going to roll over and take it?

                                                            I find that some degree of urgency coupled with affecting things directly helps give an antagonist some "punch," if you will.

                                                            Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                            Maeljin I've already shared my thoughts about. I think they are a bit of an ill fit and feel slightly bolted on to the setting (bit like the Pure actually). I liked the that later books in the NWoD game line kinda explained them more (Inferno) and I'm even happy to include some of the Mage fluff on them (Summoners/Imperial Mysteries). So I guess coming at them from mainly either a core NWoD viewpoint or a Hunter/Mage viewpoint they look a bit odd/off in Werewolf, despite it being the game that introduced them. Let me put it this way, in my games I consider it cannon that the Maeljin and their ephemeral servants are NOT actually Spirits but demons/denizens of a realm within the lower depths.

                                                            I think I'd like them to be refluffed to be more "equal opportunity" bad guys. So instead of just showing up in the Wounds in the Shadow they have a presence in the Astral, Hedge and Underworld that way the game lines that interact with those realms each get a piece of the puzzle in regards to the Maeljin/Archdukes of the Inferno/Denizens of the Lower Depths from their particular supernatural races viewpoint. Still, have said supernaturals be at least a little genre savvy e.g. Werewolves realize that Maeljin stuff is not actually Spirit related, more a case of something "other" intruding into the Shadow.
                                                            Yeah, I don't know what the plan is for the Maeljin going forward. Not really my department at present (although, like I said, if we do a Bale Hounds book, I'd love to work on it).

                                                            Originally posted by Hawthorn View Post
                                                            Idigam I actually like as an idea. Werewolf I think needed a grounded "big bad" that could be held up as such. Requiem likewise needed something similar and the Strix provide that roll really well. I have issue with some of the fluff around them (mutant babies? thrown to the moon? moon landings?) as its a bit weird. The core idea of Spirits that "should not be" and "don't play by the rules" and represent ideas so abstract and primordial then sentient life can't really grasp onto them without going kinda nuts. It gives something that is definitively "other" in a game that is already kinda full of a sense of the "other".

                                                            Needless to say, I'm dying to see what the writers come up with for them in the new book, if they get the same sort of extra development and thematic shot in the arm that the Strix did in Blood and Smoke I'd be tempted to use them as major villains in any game line in the NWoD (save maybe Vampire and Mummy).

                                                            ​This reply got out of hand, was supposed to be shorter then this.
                                                            Well, hopefully you like the idigam in the new book. I think they're interesting and will hopefully prove to be useful and terrifying antagonists for folks' games. But then, I'm a bit biased in that regard.
