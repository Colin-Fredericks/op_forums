          More specifically, Dark Eras will have a section on Aztec Skinchangers. I don't know whether we've had it confirmed that it will have sidebars for Forsaken specifically as yet.

          Cecoyotl, I haven't played a Mesoamerican Werewolf game, I'm afraid, but it certainly seems like a rich setting for the Forsaken. If you're planning on having the game focus on Tenochtitlan itself, there's some interesting directions you could go with the duality between Tlatelolco and Tenochtitlan, perhaps having two feuding city spirits or a single, dual-aspected city spirit reflecting both states.

          The other thing that strikes me, obviously, is the level of spiritual activity around the Aztec practices of sacrifice. The extent to which there are organised spiritual hierarchies feeding off the death and emotion involved, as opposed to simply 'natural' levels of spirit ecology around them, could well determine how the Forsaken have to treat the Hisil around such areas. On the one hand, the Forsaken might see it as their duty to brutally cull and fight back the spirits born from and feeding from sacrifice; alternatively, however, they might decide (or be forced) to integrate themselves into the human structure in order to interact with the spirit courts of blood and death on a more diplomatic level. Add in the likelihood that at least some sacrificial altars would probably become Loci over time and you could actually set the scene for relatively political conflict between Forsaken and Pure over control and influence in the priesthood, with both sides attempting to work within and through human society to sieze the prize at stake (powerful Loci and control over how the spirit courts are deatl with) rather than just duking it out in open violence.

                      Here is the mundane document I used for my own Tenochtitlan game.

                      I gave this to my players so they had a social and historical context for the non-supernatural components of the city.

                      Tenochtitlan - 1474 CE/8 - Tochtli
                      Size: About 12 km2 (4.6 mi2)
                      Population: 203,400 (including Tlateloco)
                      Emperor: Axayacatl

                      Climate: Cool and temperate, with significant rainfall throughout the year. Even the 'dry' months are wet. Winter months see a decent amount of frost by the water.

                      Economy: Primarily textiles, stonework, pottery, featherwork, and military supply; it receives great tribute in metalworks and other commodities. Most of its food is supplied through imports of farm product from the mainland, and by chinampas (platforms on the lake surface where food is cultivated).

                      It is also a large hub of the slave trade due to its temples, and its incorporation of the markets of its recently-conquered sister city Tlatelolco on the northern part of the island. Many transients come to see the ball games or make pilgrimage to the temples. The Tlatelolco markets sees tens of thousands each day.

                      Law Enforcement: Tenochtitlan is the home of the teccalco court where serious crimes are tried from across the empire; crimes involving nobility are tried in the emperor's palace. The Mexica do not have prisons, though its law is enforced by the military.

                      Less serious crimes see the offender required to make restitution to the injured party; this can include becoming their slave for a set period of time. First offenses in serious crimes sometimes result in having your home demolished, or your head shaved. After that a repeat offense will see you put to death by anything from being dragged to a sacrificial altar, strangled to death, or stoned on the spot. The nobility receives far less leeway, often ending up on the altars when wrongdoing is proven in any serious crime.

                      Notable City Laws
                      ⦁ Multiple marriage is legal, with a man having more than one wife.
                      ⦁ Divorce is legal, and the woman gets half of everything while being free to remarry.
                      ⦁ Commoners cannot openly display wealth in their dress or home decoration.
                      ⦁ Public drunkness is illegal except during festivals or if you are over the age of 70.
                      ⦁ Murder (in the sense of non-lawful killing) is by definition illegal.

                      Tlateloco: The Mexica Empire's largest market, on the northern side of the island. Previously it held a large amount of sway over the policy of the empire due to Tenochtitlan's reliance on its markets to fuel the economy, but that seems like it will no longer be an issue. It was conquered just last year when Axayacatl deposed its king Moquihuix; an interim military governor, a quauhtlatoani, will be appointed later this year.

                      Recent Events and Opinions
                      ⦁ Axayacatl is either a true sovereign, and worthy successor to Moctezuma who unified the two cities sharing the island and will bring prosperity to the Mexica people (Most of Tenochtitlan), or an imperialist bastard who annexed their home and is going to replace their king with a puppet (Most of Tlatelolco). In either case the Mexica seem to be looking forward to yet another imperial reign full of expansion and plentiful sacrifices for the gods.
                      ⦁ The sixth version of the Major Temple was just recently completed, and it is glorious. The blood is flowing freely down its steps, and the sun shines brightly on the Mexica for it. A major sacrifice will be performed in a few years time to dedicate it. Praise be to the gods.

                                  Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post

                                  Would not this create the Wounds on this sacrificial alters?
                                  Not necessarily, but they certainly might end up as Wounds. Preventing them from doing so could well be a significant drive for the Forsaken within Tenochtitlan's society. Ways that come to mind of stopping them becoming Wounds involve the obvious human side of things (stopping them sacrificing - good luck with that - or more likely trying to influence the rites and practices of the priests to ensure that the sacrifices are less about agony and suffering, and more about stirring up emotions other than dread amongst those who see them), ensuring that there is a co-operative spirit court willing to drink up the Essence created to prevent it from pooling and saturating the Hisil, rites to counteract any creeping encroachment of Wounded taint, etc.

                                  Also, as far as I know, wolves are not popular in MesoAmerica and Aztec would do not have any Wolf related myths. Maybe stick with this and treat all Uratha as foreign to this region, where normally the Balam are local shapechangers culture? Something like I did with Ursara on my Lapland game, were Uratha are conquerors from centuries before. I would see Uratha as naturally not encontered in this place and they are "intruders" in the Land of Great Cats.
                                  Well, Xolotl is a fairly obvious one to draw on with heavy canine overtones (his dog-headed aspects etc). Interestingly, his areas of influence touch on the preserves of both the Bone Shadows and Fire-Touched, indicating there could well be a significant struggle between the two Tribes over certain aspects of society at the time. You could even go with a Xolotl-related Lodge that crosses both Tribes, engaged in an internal war between Pure and Forsaken members to claim its spiritual heart and direction.

                                  Beyond that, I'm generally less than eager about the presence of werewolves being related to ethnicity in some way, especially given the new ways in 2e that Wolf-Blooded can come into existence. Werewolves are consummate shapechangers and a lack of actual wolves isn't really going to stop them taking whatever forms they need to disguise their presence (and those sightings of Urshul etc can easily be rolled into links with Xolotl) while any bugger who sees Gauru is probably dead or rather unhinged and doesn't have any handily available wolves to compare what he saw to.

                                  Quite often, people have a bit of a habit of going 'but THIS ethnicity/group are different and have their OWN special (shifters/vampires/whatevers). For me, it would probably be more interesting to have the Uratha as being as native to the population as anywhere else - but pitching the Balam or other shapeshifters as the invaders/outsiders/newly formed presence, shoving the Forsaken into serious conflict as these things start appearing and making inroads, harnessing a human culture that already has lots of imagery and symbolism about jaguars that makes it easy for them to sink their claws into it. Hell, with the Blasphemies version of the Balam Colop you could shuffle the timeline and have their actual origin story play out right in front of the eyes of the Forsaken.

                                  Go with a storyline indicating some sort of shenanigans with certain priests - maybe there are hints that Claimed are involved, or other spirits, enough to draw attention. Maybe on purpose, maybe by mistake, the priests end up creating Balam Colop by feeding some sacred jaguars with human sacrifices or hearts. The Forsaken bust down whatever occult trouble the priests have brewing without realising that the jaguars are the focus of it all (or maybe the jaguars are purely a side-product and aren't even relevant to whatever the seeming 'main plot' was). The jaguars get loose or are forgotten about at first; the Balam Colop start to infiltrate society, or find themselves mystically bound to stalk those who wrong priests of Tezcatlipoca, or just try to figure out who the hell they are and what their purpose is. The killings begin, the Forsaken naturally suspect Claimed or Uratha, and are extremely surprised as they find a new and cunning creature on the loose...

                                      It's kind of interesting to note that, while what they did was pretty brutal, the Spanish were much better at handling the colonization of the South Americas than their counterparts to the north were. It's probably due to them incorporating the native peoples into their society, even at a reduced social status, than the northern tendency to plop down somewhere, push Native American's out like they owned the land, then expand and push them away further.

They imply the People used Anybeast to take the form of a jaguar when they needed to keep the herd from noticing.
