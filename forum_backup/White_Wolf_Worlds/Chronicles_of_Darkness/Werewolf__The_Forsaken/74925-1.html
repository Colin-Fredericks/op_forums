    In an attempt to be fair.
    I obviously agree that more succinct information should be in the corebook, as does pretty much everyone else. We'd love extra books (it's been forever since Forsaken got a good one), but the core needs to form the core of information for those books.
    However. nWoD was made as a toolkit. The reason for no, 'this is what happens with your social contacts when you undergo the first change' is probably because it depends entirely on the individual character. For every Uratha that cuts their ties entirely, there's another that's grown up around wolfblooded and Uratha, and another that actually spends less time with their new kind and tries to live their normal lives, except for special occasions. For every Uratha who had friends they can never keep in contact with again, there's another who now has the power to rise to the top of their organization and be the boss of bosses. There's corporate sharks, weapons dealers, doctors who pump themselves full of drugs thinking the whole thing is a psychotic break they have to stave off, kids who get kidnapped and brainwashed by folks their parents hired to fix their behavioral problems, upstanding members of society and family men and women and party boys and girls and police officers and convicts and soldiers and terrorists, and that's just from the NPCs in the books, add your own imagination and there's a billion other things that could happen.
    So putting down a definitive, 'This is what happens,' would only serve to keep you from doing things.
    And all THAT said, Blood of the Wolf goes into the more common psychological and physiological changes upon becoming Uratha.

    So since we've already established what is or isn't in the books, why not give an idea of what you want that aspect of the game to be like? Do you think werewolves should lose all their friends and be loners who can only stand to be around other Uratha? Or the opposite? Or some middle ground?

    But we're really not sticking to the thread topic anymore, which was effectively, 'Do you like Werewolf the Forsaken?'

                            I happen to love the current appearance of Forsaken (over all mind you, I own every book in some form or another). Doesn't mean I don't welcome a rule set. I think people are just too used to linearity of the apocalypse line and are expecting something like that. With Forsaken, I feel it pushing my creativity. The fact that you can completely ignore some history and use others also helps. Again, it's all rumors. The only facts are the things associated with the oath, and that's due in part to the fact that it makes you friggin crazy after a time. A harmony 1 or zi'ir werewolf is a pretty scary concept to become, and all the while you may or may not know it's happening to you.

                            Horror film werewolves may well be low harmony bale hounds of violence... or any other werewolf for that matter with a taste of the herd.

                            A fun typical storyline might be a wolf blooded about to turn, stalked relentlessly by a tribe trying to do damage control in a freshly 'manicured' part of their territory. The wolf blooded thinks they're being haunted by creatures of the night up until the turn, when you can go very "american werewolf in x" with it.

                            As for good werewolf books... try Hemlock Grove. Excellent book. Might also try the howling... Donna Boyd's collection of romance/scifi devoncroix trilogy is nicely tragic (although not exactly proper for this setting). hm... Wolf's Rain isn't perfect by a large margin... Skin Walkers as a movie was a good local Forsaken vs Pure style conflict. Ginger Snaps could make for a good first change reference... What's some other werewolf books I've read... It'll come to me after I close this thread I know it.

                                                        My unpopular opinion is that "Werewolf" conjures up a bunch of preconceptions, and then you get a game that feels much more like "Shaman" than anything else. I know the spirit stuff is core to the game, but it's never been terribly compelling, and certainly isn't the first thing that springs to mind. Fury. Loss of control. The clash of humanity and animal instinct. Waking up with blood in your mouth and on your hands. Being terrified at what you've become, how you can't reasonably fit in with society well. That sort of stuff. The game as-is is difficult to pitch to people, and I wish there was a bit more horror to it. It doesn't help that The Pure are pretty lame antagonists (they feel like early Sabbat more than anything, and not in a good way).
