    Discussion of Future Supplements

    08-17-2014, 05:56 AM
    I figure starting up a thread about future ideas for Onyx Path would be a good way to go of it. Even though Stew & Co. are swamped with W20 stuff I'd dig seeing Second Edition get a full treatment for everything. I figure a Book on the Shadow will come out for the default "blue-book" line like Hurt-Locker so I'll leave that there.

    Book of Packs: Seems a pretty solid idea for a book. The core of the Uratha experience revolves around their pack, and new merits/gifts/rites focusing on this special connection seems good enough for focused treatment. I'd think touching on different examples of Pack layouts around the world would also be good for a chapter. I'm personally interested in seeing non-Totem spirits being part of your Pack, and even animals are included in Gifts and a sidebar. With the Dead Wolf Bloodline from Vampire and the Spiritus Transmutation from Promethean even non-Mortal people should probably have cases of joining the group.

    Book of Prey: What can I say? I frickin' love Predators, and if what the freelancers have said about Dread Powers and the like they don't have enough room in the corebook and would like to do more. Spirits, Claimed, Hosts, and Ancient Horrors that are outside the norm should make a return with new ideas and new ways to put them out in GMC. The Idigam are going to be in the core, but that doesn't stop them from adding even more to this book. What can I say? I like options!

    Book of Lore: I frickin' love magic. Its the thing that keeps making me think of the major place of Werewolves in any setting. The shapeshifting ties them to being monsters and predators, but the magic makes me think of them being something "more" than what's normally seen fiction. Exploring what that magic is, be it Gifts, Rites, or Fetishes, will help to cement it in my mind and give more ideas on how each character can view who and what they are and what they can do.

    Book on Hunting Grounds: It seems a solid space to expand on territories around the world and how the history and locals have an effect on the Shadow and traditions of the People. Also, if the Hunting Grounds are all like the Australian example that was spoiled they should help ever ST to develop their own locations and customize them.

    One thing I felt First Edition was weird about what how they shoved the Tribes/Auspices into singular books with relatively lopsided treatments. Right now you have to read Lore of the Forsaken, The Rage, and Tribes of the Moon/Signs of the Moon for a look at the Tribes and Auspices and the differing views of each and how they can fit. And the refocusing on the Hunt and what this means to each Tribe(and the Uratha's place on the Hunt in relation to their Auspice) as Prey makes me thinks each deserves a retread.

    Rather than cramming everything into a single book I'd rather each one stood on its own as a window into one of the core aspects of the splat. Its a new edition and I want more books. Pretty simple on that front.

    Right there we have about 13 books. Great for any Edition run. The Tribe and Auspice books probably don't need to be overly long, but I figure the Books on Prey/Packs/Lore should hit the 160-200 mark.

    What would other people like to see?

          Maeljin and the Bale Hounds. This is more of a "what would you want from the book when it comes out," since Stew has said he's got something in the pipeline for 2e Bale Hounds. So here's my headspace: Tell me why it's cool and interesting that spirits across the Hisil know the name "Maeljin," though it is foreign to the First Tongue, because "an easter egg from Apocalypse" ain't gonna cut it. Alternately, rename them if you like, that's cool too. Werewolves occupy an interesting niche as the critters among the World of Darkness, I think, whose concerns are closest to those of the Inferno. Mages have a name for it because they like naming things they didn't already have names for, but the Inferno isn't an active presence in the life of the Awakened to any extent greater than other threatening and strange elements of the Fallen World are. Werewolves? Werewolves have to look over their back for an entire secret tribe of infiltrators in praise of the Pit, and Wounds are at least common enough in the Shadow Realm that they have a common term for them and a vague understanding of what they are and who dwells therein. I want to see a book that works with that, tells me why that is. Not only in terms of why it is that Wounds develop and spirits turn to the Maeljin, but in an out of character, thematic way: tell me why Hell on Earth is a theme appropriate to Forsaken. Connect me to the old myths that conflate werewolves, witches and Satanists. I'm not generally a big fan of the "Lower Depths" / "Inferno" stuff, but the Bale Hounds are cool, and they're also a strong conduit through which to convince me that the Inferno is cool, too.
