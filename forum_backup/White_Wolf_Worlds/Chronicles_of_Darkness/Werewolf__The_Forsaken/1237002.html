    [2E Setting Hack] Werewolf: The Curse

    07-19-2018, 11:34 PM
    Hail friends.

    I've been playing Werewolf the Forsaken for a long time and I have a lot of things I like about it and a lot of things I dislike about it. There are things I've had fun with but want to set aside, and things I've never tried but want to. Therefore, because I have too much time, I have created a setting hack that changes some of the focus from the original game from 'Spirit Warden' to 'Bloodthirsty Predators'. I like the Spirit World but I find that it detracts from the grittier, earthier elements of the game that I like - particularly the interpack politics, the conflict between ideologies, the struggle to maintain control of one's humanity, the gustatory need to hunt, and the danger one poses to one's inner circle.

    Here is a link to my work so far (it's still very much in progress). I'm taking parts of Werewolf the Forsaken that I like or that are evocative of the feeling I want (there's also some good writing in there that does a great job) and adding stuff of my own or making minor alterations, while including my own ideas. I'd also appreciate some of your own ideas, particularly for how to add a pervasive feeling of 'having something to do' that Forsaken invokes.

    Before anyone brings it up, I know that lots of people love the Spirit World and disagree with removing it. That's fine, I've heard the arguments both for and against, and I'd rather not revisit it. I'd greatly appreciate ideas to make things more interesting, more diversified, balance mechanics, and add a proper 'Main Foe' if you have ideas for them.

    I got the motivation to start this hack after reading 'Werewolf 2.0 Nine Ways it Could Be Streaminlined' : http://www.musoapbox.net/topic/270/w...be-streamlined

    I went and made my own list of criticisms and improvements like any good imitator, which is in the doc. Here it is so that you can have an idea of what I've been going for before delving into the Hack itself, if you decide to.

    https://docs.google.com/document/d/1dr9klFe1InGiCGWHT22BmweFrMyBhQ3py2lbCMk-ImE/edit?usp=sharing


    Once again, all credit goes to all the artists, authors, producers, and others whose work inspired and went into this document. It's free for anyone's use and nobody is permitted to profit from it. The document is property of Viskarenvisla.
    What is this

    I love Werewolf the Forsaken. It’s a game with a lot of great themes, design elements, some very good storytelling potential, and a lot of really cool mechanics to demonstrate how it is to play a Werewolf. However, it’s a game with lots of flaws that ultimately detract from the whole purpose of the story: being a Werewolf. To remedy this, I will excise the portions of Forsaken that I think are useless or detract from the game, and either leave them be or come up with replacements.

    What detracts from the game?

    1. The Spirit World. I find the Shadow to be an alright concept but in the world of Werewolf it ultimately distracts from the feature of Werewolf I find to be most interesting - that of actually being a Werewolf. The game includes tons of content about the actual Shadow itself, how it should behave, what it should feel like, but it’s often very difficult to put into play and doesn’t support the game itself very well unless done very, very well. Spirits can often be kind of cartoony and feel more ephemeral than is satisfying - hunting spirits is probably the least interesting aspect of the game and doesn’t make you feel like a monster, it makes you feel like a monster-rancher and like you’re not very involved with the world around you. Moreover I find that the Shadow is a good reason to basically have anything happen - ‘why is x/y/z this way? Oh because of the Shadow’ and the explanation basically ends. I think that Werewolf can function just fine without the Shadow, there are plenty of elements to choose from the associated mythos to give the game a sense of weight and information density often associated with WoD games.
    2. Renown. Renown served a good function when used in conjunction with gifts but at this point I think renown ties people down to certain behaviors, can be kind of arbitrary, doesn’t explain how it lends more power to Werewolves, and is just another massive XP sink. Moreover if you don’t decide to stick to one of the two presented factions - namely the Forsaken or the Pure - it’s basically impossible to get renown, gimping you against other Uratha.
    3. Harmony. In 2.0 Harmony served a function that I think becomes a statistical distraction - there are all these different rules associated with having your harmony be hither and yon, and with its effects on your ability to shapeshift, hop between the Hisil and Flesh, and the various bans, I think it essentially isn’t very useful for telling a tale of savage horror. I actually liked 1.0 Harmony because it was a measure of your monstrosity vs. your humanity, which should be a central theme of Werewolf. If there needs to be some sort of morality scale within Werewolf, I think the 1.0 version can be tweaked.
    4. Spirit/Flesh Dualism. The whole descent from a god and his little godling-wolves bit got very confusing. It generally ties in with the discomfort I had with the Spirit World in general, and is frankly less interesting than the Human/Beast dualism.
    5. Fetishes. While I think that having magical items is a great idea in a game to add some more mechanical flavor and a few more tricks, the idea of a magical item having a spirit bound into it feels more cartoony than Werewolfy.
    6. Siskur-Dah. I actually like the concept of marking your chosen prey or goal, but not with some booga-booga ritual. Having it tie in mechanically with cool gifts or features was a great idea, but the whole spirit booga made it less cool.
    7. Numerous Gifts. There are quite a few gifts in the game which are either stupid or useless, and while the concept of gifts could be streamlined and made more interesting with their removal, their presence is still irritating. I’m looking at you gender-flip gift.
    8. Tribes and Auspices. Tribes and auspices are boring. They should enhance gameplay and give you a sense of quality and coolness that others outside your tribe lack, but I find them to be half-assed in their approach to taking on a specific prey. Forsaken tried to solve the ethnic butterfly problem that Apocalypse had, but generally speaking the tribes pigeonhole characters and don’t add much. Moreover, how is it that Blood Talons in India and Australia have so much in common? Oh of course, “the Spirit World”, silly me. Their only real benefit is mechanical, and it’s an unnecessary one. Auspices feel like classes from DnD too, and while I can appreciate the logic I think it is unfit for this type of game.
    9. Wolf-Bloods. I understand the logic here with White Wolf trying to standardize everything - vampires have ghouls, werewolves have wolfbloods, mages have acolytes, etc., but again I think that they just don’t fit in very well with the overall style and feel of Werewolf. They offer kind of a cop-out to the difficulty of dealing with humanity and whom you let close. However, I do believe that the idea of being able to infuse a human with Werewolf power in some way and bind them to your will is a cool idea worth exploring.
    10. Dalu and Urshul. I think that the impact of shapeshifting is increased if there are less forms. The Dalu and Urshul allow more leeway in the Hunt, and I think that decreasing the number of forms means that the other forms are more impactful, important, and decisive - some of the traits of the other forms, like Badass Motherfucker or inflicting Tilts, can be bought as Manifestations for the various forms.
    11. Eat Spirits, not People. I mean...fine, I get it. Eating people definitely pushes the boundary here but if we’re going for a game that brings savage horror to the forefront, I think eating people would definitely do it AND add an element of care to the game that a lot of werewolves can simply avoid by sticking to spirits and animals as prey. Spirits are too weird and alien to function as edible prey and again detracts from the Werewolf experience, while the other various monsters of the game like Hosts, Vampires, Humans, and other Werewolves, in my opinion, make fantastic prey.
    12. Limited Factions. Okay so there’s Forsaken and Pure. That’s it. Everything else is negligible and unimportant in the grand scheme of things. While the Forsaken are kinda whatever and uninteresting, I find that there is great potential in the Pure as a faction, since they represent the most extreme, uncontrolled aspects of being a Werewolf.
    What Would Add to the Game?

    1. No Spirit World. Just get rid of it. It’s not necessary in my opinion. I think an element of supernatural mystery can definitely be attained with implications that Luna is some sort of mad goddess, and that there may be some ghostly things that can mess with Werewolves but this shouldn’t be a primary focus. The idea of being a spirit-cop got old really fast - the Werewolf condition itself is a great source of power and possibility.
    2. The Change as Curse/Disease. Being Uratha is often seen as some sort of blessing mixed with a few negatives - I’d rather balance them out, and not only that, I’d like it to be a Curse that can be transmittable, a la the whole ‘bitten by a werewolf, become a werewolf’ trope. I think that this would be the core change that I’d make for this game, changing the focus of the condition and its source of power from a blessing from the Spirit World into a strange, otherworldly Curse or possibly a Disease that afflicts soul and body, changing the bearer into a ravaging monster that hungers for the flesh of other predators. The Curse itself is mysterious and unexplained - it seems to be partially hereditary, mostly inflicted, and ultimately its source is difficult to pin down, with multiple conflicting tales, steeped in superstition and mired in mistruths and legend. There are numerous ‘primogenitors’ of the various Strains that seem to have cropped up in history, with few shared features - some have been horrible monsters, some have been saintly, some have been simple, normal people, but ultimately unless they have died without passing on the Curse, they create their own Strain of Lycanthropy. As the Curse defies the laws of physics not just in allowing spontaneous generation of matter, high-speed regeneration, and monstrous strength, it also allows for numerous anomalous abilities that aren’t usually accounted for in the tales, giving Werewolves particular edges. Basically, a Werewolf is created when someone in the War-Form bites a mortal and they survive to Change.

      In this way, I am very much stealing from Vampire, whose backstory in both Masquerade and Requiem I think are very good. The mystery and lack of clarity about the condition are far more tantalizing than the way things are basically completely laid out in Forsaken or Apocalypse.
    3. Better gifts. A lot of gifts are useless. This was especially pronounced in 1.0 but I still believe that 2.0’s gifts leave a lot to be desired. Moreover they are often magicky to the point that it doesn’t really fit in well with the Werewolf mythos, which I think should rely less on outward magic and more on biological/curse/subtle themes, and without the Spirit World this can be done much more easily. Ideally, this edit will so do away with things like gifts of the Elements, which were poorly defined since they relied on a poorly defined attribute (spirit influence).
    4. Customizable Forms - Manifestations. This is a test idea and I’m not sure how it’ll work out, but I think that it might be worth giving a try. I got it from Demon the Fallen, and I really liked the way your Apocalyptic Form could be so readily changeable. Rather than sinking XP into renown, which may grant gifts and be used in various dice rolls, I think it’d be cooler to instead be able to emphasize the sheer mercurial nature of Uratha and be able to change aspects of the War Form. Depending on your Primal Urge, you can have additional Manifestations that the player themselves can create. How are these different from gifts? Gifts you have to spend Rage on and tend to have bigger, more complicated effects; Manifestations are static bonuses or tricks you can use in your forms. You can switch them out at will once they’ve been purchased for XP, and STs and Players should work together to create their own Manifestations.
    5. Strains Replacing Tribes. I think that the more diversity there is in a game the better. One of the problems with the CoD series is that they try to compact everything into a few splats like Tribe and Auspice or Clan and Covenant, or whatever. Since these are savage beasts without a Spirit World to unite them, there is endless variance in ideology - we’ll be focusing on the American continent, where there are definitely quite a few different unique Strains, some with extremely ancient roots in Native American myth, some with their origins in Continental Europe and Africa. Strains will also give a tangible, real mechanical benefit as well as a certain amount of definition to just how the Curse manifests. Players and STs should definitely work to create their own Strains as they see fit.
    6. Primal Urge as Power+ Stat. One thing I liked about Primal Urge was that it did a number of good things for you in Werewolf 2.0 - increased your Rage and how much you could use per round, added to your time in Gauru, increased the potency of some gifts, and made it so you had to consume particular kinds of prey to stay functioning. Now by getting rid of Renown Primal Urge will instead function in its place, granting increased power to Gifts and maybe some Manifestations as well. Primal Urge is not increased through XP - instead it is increased through milestones of interacting with the Change, succeeding in particular Hunts, taking down certain Prey, living through certain disastrous events and coming through them...some milestones will be decided upon by the Players and ST.
    7. Enthralled. These would replace Wolfbloods. Rather than having relatives or family that are kinda pseudo-werewolves, you can instead, kind of like Demons or Vampires, pass on a measure of your Curse to normal humans and tie them to your will. This can be done through intense, close interactions, such as sexuality, deep personal relationship, transmitting part of the Curse through a bite, and persistent haunting. This way the Uratha can in fact pass one of their Manifestations on to an Enthralled - further subjugation can add another Manifestation, but it runs the risk of inflicting The Curse wholesale and creating another Werewolf.
    8. Numerous Factions. Rather than just Pure or Forsaken, which had enormous bearing on your progression and the kind of character you could play, I will introduce a number of possible factions that will be somewhat more like philosophical/creed organizations, rather like those in Mage or Vampire. Some war with each other for control of hunting grounds, establishing rules of dominance or trying to impose certain laws upon their fellows. Some are wide-ranging networks that can span entire states - some go no further than a particular neighborhood.
    9. Marking Prey. I liked the idea of Siskur-Dah but I didn’t like how it was carried out. The Chosen Prey of a Werewolf, however, can still be selected and marked with the same benefits of Siskur-Dah. In order to Mark Prey, one must interact with them similarly to Enthrallment, but usually a single bite with the intent to mark the prey is sufficient. If the prey is a particular goal rather than a thing, some sort of small ritual may need to be done, to be tested and seen how it functions.
    10. Magic Items but how? I think that these are important for mechanics and rewards, but how would I implement them???

                      I still need to read more, but this looks similar to one apocalypse (end of the old world, not the WoD gameline) idea I had - Iron Gauntlet. That basically had it where the Gauntlet became near impenetrable, locking out most of the Shadow and its influence. Loci largely dried up, and so what is the other major source of Essence? This was back before Werewolf 2E, though, so the harsh consequences to Harmony were more direct.

                      Just looking over your summary for now,
                      1. I'd retain at least some of the Spirit World, just cut the overwhelming focus and influence. Leaving it in, but rare and optional lets you use (even as a red-herring) the idea of "wolf-spirit-possession" being a mechanism behind the curse. Which might have some nice tie-on elements. It also lets you have have things like Vision Quests, and Totem Spirits, but keep it optional - not every pack might have one. Dial between "Many" and "Rare" as you choose. Basically just leverage the Spirit as an optional backing for whatever magic-like-stuff you may need in the background and ignore it otherwise.
                      3. Since most of the 2E Gifts directly interact with the Spirit, Wolf, or Hunter part of Werewolves, just keep the ones that are thematic, let them buy them individually, and recast them as personally developments/transformations instead of "Gifts".
                      7. This sounds frankly like an attempt at copying Ghouls directly. I suggest just leaving Wolf-blooded as is. They are people that have some genetic tie to Werewolves, which manifests in their own curse and one-off abilities. For "late-bloomer" or "inflicted" Wolf-Blooded, just say that they have a resistance to the Curse, so it doesn't fully manifest.
                      9. I suggest you leverage the heavy focus on the Pack in 2E. Instead of Shadow-Bestowed-or-manipulating-Rites, have the various ad-hoc activities and rituals a pack conducts act as a sort of psychic gestalt lens. Stuff happens (like supernaturally marking prey) because enough people united in one will decide it happens. Think of it like the Pack all metaphysically howling "We are coming for you." If you want to completely cut out the Spirit, you can use that for any magic-like stuff you need too.
                      10. See above for either lingering Spirit influence, psychic-gestalt (strong enough impression might "stain" an item), or just shrug. Before Mummy, Relics just "happened", so why bother to give rando-magic-items a common explanation.

                      Some thoughts:
                      • [Wolf-Spirit] If you are representing the Curse as being "possessed-by-wolf-spirit", they Harmony might be similar to 1E Synergy in concept (two parties being able to work together) and 2E Clarity mechanically (working like a damage track, with differing levels of infractions causing lingering but recoverable harm). Alternatively, you could ditch it entirely as a numeric track and instead use Conditions like Harmony, Disharmony, Conflicted, Berserk, Fugue, etc. They could lead back and forth between each other, or whatever other level of interaction you prefer.
                      • [Wolf-Spirit] For passing on the Curse via bite, you might look at some representations of the "Join-or-be-prey" aspects of the Wild Hunt. Specifically, when a Werewolf on the Hunt bites a target, they subconsciously choose - Fight, Flee, or Join. The prior two are obvious, but the last one means the "victim" has decided to accept a "wolf" inside them too, and become a werewolf (over time).
                      • You might exchange Essence for Hunger, either as a simple renaming, or treating it like an inverse track. Or even trading it for Conditions, with expenditures either automatically swapping them for "hungrier" Conditions, or rolling to risk "getting Hungry".
