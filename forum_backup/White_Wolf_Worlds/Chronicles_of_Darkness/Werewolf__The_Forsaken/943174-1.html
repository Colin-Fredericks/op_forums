                                Originally posted by K9ine View Post
                                The first addition seems to make more sense. Silver is a moon thing right, so it should come from Luna. The Gauntlet makes perfect sense if Father Wolf caused it, what caused it in second edition? I kind of wonder if I might be better off ignoring the story entirley, but it seems like a big part of the game.
                                Most modern werewolves agree since silver is associated with the moon, Luna seems a likely source of their silver Bane. Similarly, most modern werewolves agree that it makes perfect sense that Fater Wolf's death howl would have been what caused the Gauntlet. As such, the prevailing version of the werewolves' origin myth among modern werewolves is that when the First Pack killed Father Wolf, his death howl created the gauntlet and Luna cursed them with the Bane of silver as punishment for their patricidal act. We, as players with access to information the characters don't have, can read the "Sundered World" chapter of Dark Eras and see that, in an era that predates the death of Father Wolf, werewolves already had the bane of silver, something not entirely dissimilar from the gauntlet existed in human settlements, and various factions of werewolves from generations after the First Pack had many different ideas of what to do about Urfarah's waning power, including at least one group with notions about constructing some sort of barrier in Pangaea. Where did the silver Bane really come from? Who really killed Father Wolf? What really caused the fall of Pangea and the rise of the Gauntlet? Those are mysteries for the Storyteller to decide the answer to. Or not, as it's unlikely to come up in an actual game anyway.

                                The thing about Chronicles of Darkness is that it never gives any hard answers, at least not without those answers raising twice as many new questions. The origin myth hasn't significantly changed since 1st edition, but Dark Eras, not Werewolf 2e as a whole, has given us a glimpse into some of the truth behind the myth. In typical CofD fashion, that truth has only served to further obfuscate issues that were formerly thought to be clearly understood. I consider that a good thing, but if you don't, there's really no reason to take the Sundered World Era as absolute truth. It could just be one more version of the myth. Or it might not exist in your game at all. It's up to you.

                                I'm not sure where you got this impression... The Pure use the same mechanics for crossing the Gauntlet as the Forsaken do. Many of them, particularly the Firetouched, have a generally more positive relationship with most spirits than the Forsaken do, but they still have to reach at Loci just like any other werewolves.

                                Originally posted by K9ine View Post
                                So I guess Luna only punished the Uratha, not the Pure?
                                Well, according to the myths, she punished all werewolves. That's why all werewolves are harmed by silver, that's the punishment she (allegedly) gave them. Some unspecified amount of time afterwards, a subset of werewolves made partial amends to Luna by taking the Oath of the Moon. They vowed to follow certain dictates she laid down, and in exchange she granted them Auspices and Moon Gifts, and lessened the sting of silver, but did not completely remove it. Another subset rejected the idea of cowtowing to a mother who cursed every werewolf equally, when some of them didn't participate in the murder of Father Wolf, and even actively opposed the idea. So they refused to take the Oath, and ripped the Auspices from their own souls. They are harmed even more by silver than other werewolves.

                                In Sundered World, we see that even before Father Wolf's death, all werewolves already had Auspices, Moon Gifts, and the lessened version of the silver Bane. Removing the Auspice made the Bane worse. Again, why? We don't know. But it also has pretty minimal relevance to most Forsaken games.

                                Originally posted by K9ine View Post
                                Why do they hate their mother?
                                As stated above, they hate her because they believe she gave them an equal share in the curse she placed on their siblings for slaying their father, when they took no part in the act and even tried to stop it. They're probably wrong, but since no one is alive who remembers what really happened, it makes little difference.

                                                    Ok, gonna try to break down Gifts now. There are three types of Gifts, though two of them function similarly enough that you could kind of think of it as one-and-a-half types.

                                                    The first type is Moon Gifts. Moon Gifts are rated from 1 to 5 dots, and are associated with an Auspice. You automatically get one Moon Gift at character creation, at a dot rating equal to your starting dots in your Auspice's Primary Renown, and can buy additional Moon Gifts associated with your Auspice (which start at one dot when you buy them) for 5 experiences each. There is currently only one Moon Gift per Auspice, but the rule for buying more was included so that if they print additional Moon Gifts in later books they can slot right in with the existing mechanics. Any time you gain a dot in your Auspice's Primary Renown, you get one free dot in one of your Moon Gifts. Since there is currently only one Moon Gift for each Auspice, your Moon Gift should always have the same dot rating as your Auspice's primary Renown. If and when they add more Moon Gifts in the futute, you'll be able to buy dots in them for 2 experiences each, but you can never have more dots in any Moon Gift than you have in your Auspice Primary Renown.

                                                    The second type is Shadow Gifts. They do not have a dot rating, but each has five powers called Facets under its umbrella, one Facet associated with each of the five Renown. Shadow Gifts can be bought for 5 experiences each, or 3 experiences if they are one of the Affinity Gifts for your Auspice or Tribe (you can think of this like the difference between in-Clan and out-of-Clan Disciplines in Requiem.) When you buy a new Gift, you get one free Facet of that Gift, which must be associated with a Renown you have at least one dot in. You can buy additional Facets in Shadow Gifts for 2 experiences each, but you cannot buy Facets associated with any Renown you don't have at least one dot in.!Any time you gain a dot in any Renown, you gain a free Facet associated with that Renown in any Shadow Gift you know. This free Facet can be banked and saved for later if (and only if) you already have the Facet associated with that Renown in all of your known Shadow Gifts.

                                                    The third (or second-and-a-half) type is Wolf Gifts. Wolf Gifts work identically to Shadow Gifts, except that you are automatically considered to "know" all Wolf Gifts (but Facets still have to be bought with Experiences or granted when you gain Renown), and the Facets for Wolf Gifts only cost 1 experience each.

                                                    At character creation, you get one free dot in your Auspice's Primary Renown, one free dot in your Tribe's Primary Renown, and one free dot in any Renown of your choice, but you cannot have three dots in any one Renown (so if your Auspex and your Tribe have the same Primary Renown, you can't put your third free dot in it as well). Since Ghost Wolves don't have a Tribe, they only get one dot in their Auspice's Primary Renown and one dot in any Renown. As per the rules for Moon Gifts, you get one Moon Gift at character creation with dots equal to your dots in your Auspice's Primary Renown. If you only have 1 dot in your Moon Gift, you also get 1 Facet of a Wolf Gift, which must be associated with a Renown you have at least one dot in. Finally, you get two free Shadow Gifts, which must both be from Gifts on your Auspice's or your Tribe's Affinity Gifts list. As per the rules for Shadow Gifts, you get one free Facet of each of these Gifts, which must be associated with a Renown you have at least one dot in.
