    Fighting the Changing Breeds?

    03-08-2021, 08:41 PM
    So, bit of a follow-up to my last question concerning the Changing Breeds and their status with the other splats. Still haven't gotten the books themselves, sadly; store's still closed thanks to the pandemic, but I've been doing my best to study up on the series via reviews and whatnot. One thing I've noticed in said reviews is that the Changing Breeds ted to be... rather unbalanced when it comes to combat, especially in comparison to the other playable races. Namely, the Rajanya (Tigers) and Mhole-Rho (Elephants) have such high stats that they're next to impossible to fight, as far as I can tell; they both can basically one-shot anything, and the later has such stupidly high health (level 29 at creation) that you pretty much can't do anything substantial before getting killed.

    So, the question is; how in the world are you supposed to fight these things?!

    Like I admitted above, I'm still not the most familiar with the setting, though I am trying to learn the combat mechanics; that being said, to my brand-new untrained ass, I can't see many of the other splats being able to fight some of the stronger Feral types in a straight duel. For instance, I imagine a Werewolf pack could handle a Weretiger, but I don't think they have a chance against the Were-Elephant. Meanwhile, Hunters/humans have to rely on melee since the Ferals have an Aspect (Natural Armor) that reduce the effectiveness of guns, I'm unfamiliar with the Vampires, Sin-Eaters, and Mages to the point where I can't really comment on them, (though I've heard that Mages can be rather strong, but given how unbalanced the Ferals are...) and from what I'm familiar with in the lore, I imagine the Ferals wouldn't really interact with a Demon or Mummy (though I'm pretty sure they'd both stand a hell of a chance in a fight), and a Promethean, Changeling, and probably Beast would be more interested in hiding than fighting anyway, from what I can tell.

    So, how would you guys, the more experienced players, go about fighting the Ferals? Admittedly, I'm mostly interested in seeing how a human/Hunter would go about fighting some of the more broken types like the Elephants, since humanity is the Ferals' biggest target, though I would like to see the results for the rest of the splats, if possible. My apologies for both my general noobishness and any mistakes that I've made in the writings above; like I mentioned, I'm still really new, most of my info is second-hand, and RL's not the nicest right now. Massive thanks to anyone willing to tolerate my sorry rear, and equal blessings to anyone willing to help out.

      Well, there's your problem.

      The Chronicles of Darkness is a horror setting. Direct combat is a stressful process designed to chew through your Willpower if you're not specialized for it, most contested rolls favor the aggressor in raw dice potential, and multiple attackers are pretty much the only way the system allows you to attack a single target more than once per turn. Multiple splats hew to basic predatory processes like harrying their prey with hit-and-run tactics, fighting on their home turf, ferreting out mystical/political/emotional weaknesses, and otherwise whittling down the enemy's resources before moving in for the kill. If you're in a straight fight, it's because the consequences of not picking a different battle are more than you can stomach.

      It's been a solid minute since I looked at Changing Breeds and my library's a mess right now, but if memory serves the Ferals are still people, and they mostly have to live in the world and attend to the needs a living social creature has. The question is mostly what you're willing to give up and how far you can go to enforce your will on the other guy — doesn't matter if it's diplomacy or hostage-taking or poisoning their food or cutting a deal with the devil to ruin their life, if you're prepared to go the whole nine yards to get them to leave you and yours alone, you win (and god help you if you're crushed by the fallout).

              Originally posted by Warden View Post
              One thing I've noticed in said reviews is that the Changing Breeds ted to be... rather unbalanced when it comes to combat, especially in comparison to the other playable races. Namely, the Rajanya (Tigers) and Mhole-Rho (Elephants) have such high stats that they're next to impossible to fight, as far as I can tell; they both can basically one-shot anything, and the later has such stupidly high health (level 29 at creation) that you pretty much can't do anything substantial before getting killed.

              So, the question is; how in the world are you supposed to fight these things?!
              I'm going to look at this from the perspective of Werewolf since that's where I have the most experience as a player. Ferals are tough, and as you pointed out the Rajanya and Mholé-Rho have pretty absurd stat adjustments in War-Beast and Primal Beast forms. To be perfectly honest, these things were wildly unbalanced in first edition and if I were to include them in second edition I'd want to make some pretty serious changes to rebalance them

              However, the thing that really makes splats powerful is how they can break the normal rules of the game. For example, the ability of Vampires to treat all non-supernatural sources of lethal damage as bashing is arguably a much better survival trick than a few extra health levels. So when I look at a Mholé-Rho with it's +20 health in Primal Beast form I think that is a lot of health but there are lots of things I (as a Werewolf) can do to negate that advantage. For example, the pack could make use of the Urshul ability to apply free tilts against the prey. All those stat adjustments won't count for much when the Mholé-Rho is suffering from double Arm Wrack, and double Leg Wrack tilts. Likewise, being tough doesn't count for much if you can't effectively damage your opponents. If everything goes to hell, the Werewolves can take Gauru form, and there's really nothing the Mholé-Rho can do against that regeneration.

              The Rajanya are actually a much scarier proposition than the Mholé-Rho because they have access to a much wider array of aspects. Durga's Blessing and Spinebite in particular allow the Weretiger to heal from and deal aggravated damage respectively, however both are restricted and can only realistically be employed once per fight. Still, it's entirely possible for a Weretiger to one-shot an unprepared Werewolf using Spinebite. My approach to the Rajanya would largely be the same as the Mholé-Rho - weaken them with Tilts first and then move in for the kill. Aside from Spinebite, the Rajanya still don't have a good way to deal with Gauru form's regeneration, and don't really have abilities that match the Gifts a well built Rahu will have access to. A Rahu with two or 3 dots in Purity plus Primal Strength might even want to try grappling a Rajanya, which would negate a lot of their advantages.

              For mortal hunters both of these are much more difficult propositions, which is why a Hunter would never engage with them directly. As a Hunter, you want to spend a long time stalking and tracking the prey before you strike. A good idea is to make use of traps to weaken them before the fight begins - I imagine that successful hunters probably get quite adept at making and setting explosives. As hunters you would also want to exploit the Ferals' weakness to Silver, which is something Werewolves are unlikely to do. Even with +20 health levels, getting blown up and then riddled with silver bullets is not something a Mholé-Rho is likely to walk away from.

                          So if basically any supernatural splat decides to go after a Hunter who isn't prepared then that Hunter is pretty much boned. Hunter is very much a game of figuring out who the local supernaturals are and getting them before they can get you.

                          The Natural Armor aspect is interesting. First off, the armour tops out at 4/3 which means it is as much of a hinderance to melee weapons as it is to firearms. It seems like Rajanya can't buy it (which makes sense), and although Mholé-Rho can, they don't get it for free, taking up five of their seven available points to max it out. Now I'll be frank, Natural Armor is another one of those parts of Changing Breeds that is hideously overpowered. For comparison, Werewolf has a 5-dot merit for armour which tops out at 2/2 and only applies to one of the five forms. Were I to run a game featuring Changing breeds, I would bring the Natural Armor aspect down to these kinds of levels - it shouldn't offer better protection than plate mail.

                          Having said that, there are ways to work around it. If you're a Hunter and you're planning on bringing down a Mholé-Rho with Natural Armor 5 it might make sense to invest in a flamethrower - these ignore armour and inflict the burning tilt to boot. If your characters have access to military grade weapons there are also high-explosive and white phosphorous grenades, both of which have high amounts of armour piercing. You mentioned what to do if the Hunters couldn't access explosives, well this is the World of Darkness, we assume that laws are less well enforced, criminals are better supplied, and a determined character can find military-grade equipment being sold somewhere on the black market. Building connections and performing favours to acquire the gear you need is something characters of all kinds need to engage in.

                          Lastly, even if you can't get military-grade gear, armour 4 can be overcome through normal means. A normal rifle will still do at least 1 damage if it hits, and a big game rifle (appropriate for hunting a were-elephant) will do more. Shotguns and assault rifles are both likely to do damage (particularly if automatic fire is used on the assault rifle). Looking at melee weapons, archaic weapons like the battle axe or greatsword would be best, but a standard sword, or even a common tool like a sledgehammer or chainsaw would be perfectly capable of harming the target provided you get at least 2 successes on your attack roll. All of the melee weapons above can be made from or coated in silver to ensure any damage dealt is aggravated.
