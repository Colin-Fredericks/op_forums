    The main challenges with silver bullets are that they require extra time, cost, knowledge and equipment to make in any reliable fashion. Special work is probably also required on the weapon itself, to make sure the bullets don't break when firing.

    An alternative is to give silver to wolf blooded. If they spend a lot of time crossing the Gauntlet back and forth, eventually it becomes lunargent, which can be worked into amalgams without losing its werewolf damaging powers.

    If the hunters have a good diplomat, they can have a mage enchant the silver bullets to reduce the bleed out damage interval of the target. A Ruling of Time could reduce it by half for each level of potency, or to a quarter for a Reach. Suddenly the bullet does massive damage to even the most resilient werewolf.

    Lastly, never underestimate the power of a scientifically inclined adversary. Spray the werewolves with hydrogen peroxide (using a rigged sprinkler system, hose, even water balloons and water guns work). Then hit them with silver bullets, which causes it to ignite. So now they have to deal with the damage from silver while burning and perhaps being locked down by covering fire.

    A well prepared pack of hunters, even mortal ones, can be a nightmare for werewolves.

        A werewolf that's been triggered by silver isn't going to be locked down by covering fire, since it's going to be attacking every round and using it's full defense against firearms. Also wow don't put hydrogen peroxide concentrate in sprinkler systems as silver is not the only metal it reacts to and the moment it hits a pipe you're dealing with your own ignition.

        I appreciate that whenever combat is mentioned in the werewolf forum people like to pop up with their A-Team operators who have armies of people with silver bullets ready to ambush werewolves, while ignoring basically everything about them aside from silver being their bane, but it's not really relevant to the thread. You can have a meteorite of pure silver just smash into their heads and kill them, too. Even astronomy can be a nightmare for werewolves. But it doesn't make combat dangerous or interesting. It ignores combat (and, again, basically everything leading up to it) and just tries to kill the PCs. You don't need silver or hunters for that, you can have them spontaneously combust or die of congenital heart failure.

        This is why, as Dark Archon figured out pretty quickly, it's a nuclear option. It sends them into Kuruth. And it doesn't heal fast. A creature with the Fire Elemental Dread Power is doing 3 Lethal to everything within a yard of it. A PC has to choose to fight it. Gauru gives you a lot of room to deal with it, healing all Bashing and Lethal every turn, but you're closer to that wraparound in case there are other combatants nearby. A PC still has agency there. The Rahu grapples it so it doesn't get close to their more fragile Ithaeur. He goes Gauru because he has more Health to begin with. And once it's killed, it's pressure eased, allowing focus to be shifted and a sense of achievement at being closer to battle.

            Being on fire is continuous damage, which is helpful for keeping regen factor at check. What sucks about setting werewolves on fire is that they prefer to fight in melee (most of them, anyway), and once werewolf has grappled you, you are on fire too. That may not be your largest problem, given that you are going to be literally ripped apart in seconds, but fire doesn't help your case. It's like setting zombies on fire - probably a good idea, if you can keep your distance from them.

Add a couple more people firing assault rifles or shotguns for more dakka. Spirits can probably do that by moving away at full speed from weres while Blasting them. This basically forces to use Urhan or Urshul, but I am not sure how this would be played out by using CofD rules. Is that possible to have chase and fight going on at the same time? Did you make a scene like that in your games?

                        Our first sacred hunt in second edition had the target in a vehicle and shutting them down is very easy for werewolves. To be fair, it is for many supernaturals, to the point that in DaveB's actual plays it became sort of a trope that if you were going after them in a vehicle you were all going to die, and one of the playtests for Werewolf second edition faced a Rahu against a tank. If a werewolf needed to stop you or had the appropriate Gifts or other powers, like Command Artifice or having an Irraka on the team who could just teleport onto the vehicle, then the plan could be cut short. If they didn't, they could simply not chase after the guns, and use their tracking abilities to find out where it and, later, all its occupants went, or allow the police to do so.

                        In our first hunt we just had Command Artifice pull the car into an alley where we finished things.

                        This is where the white rooming gets more complex, as werewolves have a wide range of powers and capabilities beyond regeneration. If you are planning for werewolf PCs, see their Gift lists and play up or around them. There's an entire Lodge dedicated to hunting on the roads in Mad Max-esque fashion.

                        If you wanted, you could use the chase scene rules from the Chronicles core and have the gunfire and clawing available to whoever has the Edge.

                                                        Originally posted by Dark Archon View Post
                                                        Besides, if you want to have combat more than once during your game, you can't limit yourself to idigam, high-ranking spirits and unique Horrors. So, mid-hunt obstacles can be combat obstacles, and those obstacles need to be interesting/dangerous as well. Hence, this thread.
                                                        I want to add that if you do work up towards a huge and important combat scene as the climax of the game, you absolutely need to have combat scenes with pressure to establish themes and tone, to escalate the threat, and to prepare the players. The former two are just how to write a good story, which some might not be as interested in when it comes to RPGs, but the last one is extremely important. Without combat with actual stakes the players wouldn't have a good idea of how their characters actually works when combat comes on the table. Having multiple combat scenes is required to figure out tactics and synergies between characters, and for the GM to get an idea of what the PCs are capable of before they square up against the big bad.

                                                        Besides, all CofD games revolve heavily around combat. Combat is the most elaborate and pervasive subsystem in the entire Storyteller system, the only subsystem that has its derived traits (Defense and Initiative, as well as the main reason for Health boxes) printed directly on the character sheets, and monopolises an entire category of Merits. You certainly can minimise or even ignore combat in a CofD game (including Werewolf), but unless that's stated before you start or join a game, you most certainly should expect combat to feature quite heavily.
