    New Shifter: Kushtaka, the Trickster Wereotters

    11-21-2013, 11:26 PM

    Kushtaka

    (Adhuna Gaes)

    No member of the non-Urathra lycanthropes is particularly well-known, but the lutrinae shapeshifters known to the Alaskan Indians as the Kushtaka are more obscure then most. True, there are always rumors of the otters who are actually men (or is it the other way around?) out there on the ice, and the Tlinglit mothers warn their children away from the water and the ice, lest they be taken by the wily shapeshifters to be their children. But other than that, they are simply another ghost story among thousands, another cryptid that may or may not be.

    This suits the Kushtaka just fine. After all, if more people knew of their existence, then more people would know what to look for-and they wouldn't have to look far. With the acquisition of their native lands by America came American settlers, and when the trading ships went back, the Adhuna Gaes went with them-either as stowaways, or as the recently-turned crew. And with America's ascent as a mercantile empire, the otter-men went everywhere.

    Today, the Kushtaka are some of the more widespread shapeshifters in the world, although they are careful to keep their numbers low enough so that the Urathra don't notice their scheming. This is a testament to the intellect and guile of the tribes, as there is more than a hint of beaver in them-the instincts of the Aduna-Gaes call them to tend to the ecology of the Shadow, to build up the diversity of spirits in the world, to restore the wilderness of the Border Marches-in other words, to create loci. True, many otter-shifters don't particularly want to fulfill their duty as shaman (particularly those "recruited" from humanity), but sooner or later, the Gauntlet begins to drag at their minds, the world seeming hollow and barren without the guidance of spirits. Eventually, they all cave in to their blood, and begin to engineer events that generate ample Essence to pool into loci. Most prefer positive, or at least neutral, but the more power-hungry (or desperate) among them decide that impatience trumps the health of a few dozen people and decide to engineer tragedies, and the wellspring of Essence that results. Admittedly, they are quite good at fixing Wounds (especially those caused by their own recklessness), but an area inhabited by a tribe of Kushtaka quickly becomes a hodgepodge of loci and the warring spirit broods drawn to them, fundamentally incapable of understanding their benefactors' pleads that there will be enough for everyone.

    And then, of course, there are the Urathra drawn to an increasingly chaotic Shadow. Those are never a good sign.

    Appearance: As a side effect of their Illusion (see Aspects), Kushtaka usually don't give off any hints of being anything other than normal humans. Unfortunately for them, their mask is not a perfect thing, and long and bitter experience has taught them that those pure of heart and strong of will can pierce their facade of normality. Upon having an emotionally tense interaction with one of the Adhama Gaes, (Storyteller rules what qualifies, but casual intimacy and heated arguments are a good example), roll a character's Resolve + Integrity (or equivalent) against the Kushtaka's Intelligence + Composure. Success indicates that the person pierces the Illusion and is able to see the Adhama Gaes for what he really looks like for the rest of the scene. Underneath, the Kushtaka resemble their glamors, but their part-spirit nature is obvious-hair might have the sheen of otter fur, their nose might be whiskered, their hands may be webbed. Mortals tend to leap to the conclusion that they are hallucinating, which is why Kushtaka do everything they can to make that theory plausible by arranging nighttime dates or trying to act mystified when they sense their facade is blown. They also try to minimize chances of discovery by mostly dealing with the weak-willed and the criminal elements of society.

    In hybrid form, a Kushtaka is not so monstrous as otherworldly, with their human traits serving only to emphasize their alien nature. A wereotter in this form takes the look of a lutrinaine humanoid with traditionally "spiritual" traits, such as a cloak of mist or glowing tattoos (this is actually based on what the Kushtaka in question thinks is befitting a spirit, not actual spirits). Their bodies, while not muscled or menacing, are sleek and lithe, with long, dexterous claw-hands and a thick, powerful tail. Their eyes, while definitely otter, give more than a hint of intelligence and cunning, more like the metaphorical abyss that stares back if you look into it for too long. Due to their Madness Rage, paranoid Kushtaka spend much of their time in this form, reasoning that a period of obsessive attention to detail is a fair price for being able to fight back or run if necessary from enraged spirits. Attribute modifications start at Strength +1, Dexterity +4, and Stamina +1, Armor 1/1, Health +1 and Perception +2, and may be modified by Kushtaka Aspect.

    In animal form, Kushtaka become otters, but beyond that, attempting to categorize them is a study in futility-one may take the form of a sea otter, another a Eurasian otter, depending on native country, but many traveling Adharma Gaes have learned the trick of changing apparent species based on the predominant species of otter in the area. Inside of that, things like "genetics" and "phenotypes" have no bearing on the wereotters-snow white is a common color among their pelts, as are patches and human hair colors...in their inborn animal forms. Kushtaka retain all their Illusion capacities in all forms, and use it to disguise their supernatural traits in this one. Attribute modifications are Dexterity +3, Size 2, Percerption +3, Health -1, and Kushtaka use the higher of Dexterity or Wits to determine Defense in this form.

      Reproduction: The Kushtaka are well known in Alaskan myth for their ability to turn those dying of cold and starvation into another member of their race, and like many legends, this is not at all far from the truth. Kushtaka can actually do this to anyone facing immanent death, they just prefer people who are lost in the wilderness, as it provides an excellent cover story when they abduct the newborn were-otters to instruct them. While they can breed among themselves and mortals, the baby is a normal human, albeit with Unseen Sense attuned to spirits and the ability to learn the First Tongue. True, said children can be turned, but only after five years of age, and frankly, many Adhama Gaes don't want to force a kid with his whole life ahead of him to serve as the alienated gardeners of an insane Shadow

      The Rite of Shadow Infusion, as it is so called, begins when a Kushtaka (preferably one with the Ritemaster Aspect, but it isn't necessary) finds, or creates through his own actions, a human who has marked his last Health box with lethal damage. Of course, while the Good Samaritan method of simply picking a dying person he comes across at random is charitable, it is both inefficient and unreliable-up to an extent, the Rite relies on the ritemaster knowing the person beforehand, and besides, randomly stumbling across dying people is neither a good way to find ideal qualities in a candidate or finding candidates at all. More likely the Kushtaka scouts out a potential convert well in advance, deciding if it is worth to imbue them with the two-edged gift. Qualities for selection varies from Kushtaka to Kushataka, but ideally include the ability to lie well, ambition, and diplomatic abilities. Realistically, most Adharma Gaes are biased, so it is traditional for a dossier of the potential Kushtaka to be submitted to the tribe and have her potential induction voted on.

      In any case, once the ritemaster has made his decision, he spends three points of Essence to begin the Rite. These points are not actually spent, but are "implanted" in the target's soul as sort of a spiritual embryo, which will hopefully grow into the otter-spirit part of a were-otter's soul. During the rite, the Kushtaka chants the First Tongue in an instinctual liturgy that beseeches the spirits to accept the target as their envoy and ambassador to the human world, and expounds upon her various positive qualities and virtues (which is why a connection to the target is optimal-can't praise what isn't there and expect the spirits to buy it). During the process, the candidate's Health stabilizes and she becomes conscious if she wasn't already, hallucinating that she sees the ritemaster as various loved ones and acquaintances if they were un-glamoured Kushtakas, mixed in with the sporadic Changes as her body and soul is slowly reshaped into a were-otter. The Kushtaka rolls Manipulation + Harmony in an extended contested action against the candidate's Resolve + Composure, with the target number for each being the other's Morality/Harmony + 2.

      Dramatic Failure: The otter Essence separates from the ritemaster-and his humanity. It becomes either a true otter-spirit or a conceptual of illusion or spirituality, and automatically Claims the candidate. The new duguthim immediately steps sideways and flees, to pursue its own, alien agenda, one that invariably relates to loci in some way. This rare bastards of the rite are hunted down by the at-fault ritemaster as a moral duty and punishment for his incompetence.

      Failure: Successes fail to accumulate. Overall failure means the candidate fails to become a Kushtaka, and her Health heals to her first -1 health level in bashing damage. The Adharma Gaes reactions can range from anger (for the obvious) to relief (for those who thought they didn't have a choice at the time-a few of these are even deliberate). In any case, the target has to go back to being on the edge of death before the Kushtaka can try again.

      Success: Successes accumulate. Should the Kushtaka win, the candidate begins her First Change, and will have completely transformed into her hybrid form after two minutes. The sensation of becoming one varies: some feel excruciating pain as their bodies reshape, some feel warmth and relief as their new fur washes over them and protects them from the biting cold, a few feel an almost orgasmic sensation as they feel their (often subconsciously despised) humanity be cast off. The new Kushtaka is at full health, but unconscious for the next five hours as her brain begins to adapt to her new body (during which she remains in hybrid form, which does not count towards Madness Rage). Should the victim win, it is the same as a failure for the Kushtaka, which the ritemaster may not take as a dramatic failure for a Beat (the spiritual embryo has already been destroyed by the candidate's soul).

      Exceptional Success: The ritemaster may take any of the normal bonuses with an extended roll. No other affects.

      Special: Should the candidate have actually agreed to the Rite of Shadow Infusion, in full knowledge of what is about to happen, assume the Kushtaka succeeds automatically on his first roll. The candidate still rolls Resolve + Composure (as it is a function of the soul attempting to fight what it sees as a malign influence), but takes a -3 to rolls as she actively fights her own instincts.

      Possible Modifiers: Candidate agreed but is unwitting as too what is about to happen (+2 for Kushtaka), Candidate is loved one of Kushtaka (+3 for Kushtaka), Kushtaka is performing Rite out of genuine altruism (+4 for Kushtaka), Candidate is fully aware of and is fighting against Kustaka's influence (-2 for Kushtaka), Kushtaka and candidate are personal enemies (-3 for Kushtaka), Kushtaka is performing Rite out of sheer spite (-4 for Kushtaka).

      After a successful Rite, the ritemaster takes the newly-Changed Kushtaka to the tribe's territory, where he gathers them both to welcome the new member and to restrain her if she panics. The events of what happens when the newborn were-otter wakes up varies, but in inevitably varies from the stressful to the sanity-shredding as unfamiliar instincts, an odd body, and the imploring voices of spirits drawn to the neophyte mingle and tear at her mind. When a newly-transformed Kushtaka awakens and realizes what she is-which doesn't take long, spirits are helpful when it comes to that-she immediately rolls Resolve + Composure - number of vital connections to previous life (ST's call):

      Dramatic Failure: The poor creature can't handle the horrible truth, and her mind breaks. She loses an Integrity (now Harmony) dot and becomes dangerously suicidal, wishing desperately to have nothing else but her own death. For the next 24 hours, she tries any way she can think of to bring the sweet embrace of death to her, and even after she recovers (somewhat) she still suffers the effects of a failure. If she lives long enough, that is. While most spirits would like to have a new foot hold in the material world, there are many who find the sweet taste of a Kushtaka driving the metaphorical stake through her heart to be delectable.

      Failure: Even though she manages to handle the paradigm shift without shattering into a mental wreck, there is still now a significant portion of her self-identity now missing, and it shows. She rolls for Harmony degeneration at a -3 penalty, and automatically gains a Persistent Condition such as Broken or Fugue.

      Success: She handles the wake-up call admirably, although she is still significantly shaken by the event. She does not roll for degeneration, but still gains a Persistent Condition, which fades after one month if not resolved beforehand.

      Exceptional Success: Not only does the First Change manage to not make a dent in her, she almost seems...happy...about this course in events. That is terrifying for some in and of itself, but it won't hurt her mental state.

      It should be noted that willing transformees are mentally prepared for the awakening, and while they often have a hard time adjusting to the voices that really aren't in their head, they tend to handle the realization pretty well. Assume that they have a roll one category better than normal (dramatic failures become simple failures, etc.), with an exceptional success meaning that they has an automatic +2 bonus on all negotiations with spirits for the next month, who are impressed by their ease at shifting mindsets.

          Character Creation: Thank to both the selection process and their own nature as architects and ambassadors of the Shadow, most Kushtaka posses Mental or Social attributes as primary, with the other being secondary. Some may prefer Physical attributes-the knight-errant of the spirit world has a certain allure-but thanks to a lack of Regeneration, it's simply preferable to have the presence of mind required to maintain the Illusion or the ability to negotiate with spirits. With Skills, Occult either gets picked up or the were-otter gets dead, but it's more varied with other skills. Computer, Firearms, and Streetwise are common among those who want to live as close to humanity as possible, and Intimidation, Empathy, and Persuasion tend to be those found among the Adhama Gaes who embrace their spirit side. Among all though, Subterfuge is not under-represented (otter-changers make their business in lies, after all), and Academics is often found among the Kushtaka who prefer to know about the human world as well.

          Inborn Aspects: Shapeshifting, Illusion, Totem Guardian

          Illusion: As a people, the Kushtaka are not meant for battle or feats of strength. They are shamans and tricksters, pure and simple. As a result, they do not regenerate, but they gain a potent tool in their mission for subtlety.

          With nothing but a thought, the otter-changers can create a visual illusion that seems utterly realistic to sight and touch within Primal Urge x2 yards, though "touching" it really just replicating the general feel of what the illusion is supposed to be, not actual effects on the body-thus, a fake fire may feel hot and if one sticks his hand in it, there will be pain, but he will not actually be burnt. By spending one Essence, the Kushtaka may add an aural component as well-the aforementioned fire now crackles like one as well. It isn't a perfect solution, though-it doesn't smell like an actual object (unless the Kushtaka has the Illusive Scent Aspect), and close examination will give the observer to see past the trick-roll the Kushtaka's Intelligence + Composure against the observer's Integrity (or equivalent) + Resolve. Success means he sees past the trick, and views the illusion as a translucent object with no actual existence in the realm of any of the other four senses. Only one Illusion may be up at any given time, barring aspects that avert this.

          Common Aspects: Feral Collective, Parallel Numina (Any Suggested), Unsettling Eye

          Rage: Madness Rage (Obsessive Compulsion-the Kushtaka becomes obsessed with the wellbeing of a particular locus and devotes his time to becoming it's custodian and guard)

          Quote: Think you've won, little wolf?

          It's true. My race aren't fighters. I can't heal as quick as you. I'm unarmed.

          But there's one advantage I have over you in this battle, as you are about to find out.

          Not that I blame you-my friends are invisible a lot of the time.

          Territory: Due to the simple fact that the Adhuna Gaes need them to remain sane, the territory of any tribe either contains at least one locus or is quickly being engineered to possess one. If there are multiple, wereotters tend to gravitate towards the biggest, if there's a major size difference between it and the next smallest, or barring that the one with most palatable resonance to human mores (if they were produced from "gardening the Dark Forest", as they like to put it, the most common varieties tend to be based around the Virtues of Faith and Hope or the Vices of Pride or Greed, on the basis those tend to be the easiest to produce via Illusion playing with people). This area, called the "Forest Shrine" or "Home of Cold Water" (both references to Tlingit mythology-the forest symbolizes hidden forces while water is associated with being emotionally cold, something the Kushtaka find extremely useful when keeping a level head while negotiating with spirits) is where the Kushtaka sleep on occasion to refresh their minds, usually in concert on appointed dates to defend against the tribe's spirit enemies (hence the need for size-it's often housing multiple tribespeople, and being able to shift into a fairly small otter only gets so far, particularly when things like "personal space" come into play). It's also here that the tribe ixht (head shaman) performs his most sacred and powerful Rites, decorating the area with symbols of the tribe and pictures showing scenes from Alaskan myth, wherever possible. Beyond that, Kushtaka like waterfront property for obvious reasons, although the definition of "waterfront" can just as easily be "docks", "ships", and "a city on the the waterfront, which is most of them". When it comes down to it, even misanthropic Kushtaka have to deal with humanity's effect on the Shadow, and it's simply sensible to live as close to human habitation as possible to examine it.

              Society: Remember that part in the intro about how Kushtaka are obscure? Well, that's actually a bit misleading-in Alaska, they're actually quite infamous, and the myths told about them are actually rather accurate. True, the name is often spelled a bit differently, and more than one person things Kushtaka is a single, malign spirit who takes humans to be his slaves (probably no relation to the enigmatic beings known as the True Fae), but other than that the Adhuna Gaes tend to shrug their shoulders at the myths and go "Yep. That's us." Hybrid forms even look sort of like the description, with a human torso attached to an otter's lower half and tail, though they neglect to mention the fact that the head is an otter's too. The part about being unable to eat cooked food is completely and utterly wrong, though-Kushtaka can eat raw fish, but it tastes about as good to them as it does to mortals and will only resort to it if they're genuinely starved.

              In Tlingit myth, Kushtaka are often used as a stand-in and metaphor for being lost to the vast, mysterious wilderness that makes up the state and never being found, or drowning in the cold, bitter waters. Naturally, they are viewed as capricious at best and downright evil at worst, but a thing to note is that a clever and noble man can spin their magics to the good of humanity at large. The Kushtaka claim that this is because the man who negotiates with them to a positive end isn't fully human anymore-an Adhuna Gaes that cleaves close to the path of Harmony and the wellbeing of mortals often vastly improves living conditions of the world around her as she makes the area favorable to spirits of positive and life-affirming aspects, and often earns the respect of mortals in the process (not to mention their biological imperative to oppose the Maeljin wherever they be). Also worth noting is the utter lack of a definite origin for them-as far as the Tlingit know, the wereotters simply popped into existence one day and began to work their mischief on the populace, and as far as the wereotters go, that may just be the case. Even their Totems aren't much help in this-while a tribe's totem often begins to manifest traits stereotypical of Native American culture (much to the dismay of actual Tlingit Kushtaka, since any Native American culture is fair game-even those from South America), they gain theirs much as the Urathra do: Find a friendly-ish spirit, ask it to patron them in return for services rendered. Totems tend to be about as powerful as those of the Pure, since spirits realize that more loci can only be a good thing, but their bans tend to be a bit more lenient: Because the Kushtaka arealready working towards an end the vast majority of spirits want, the spirits tend to let them off relatively easy.

              By this point, one may notice that the name of the Tlingit people gets tossed around a lot, and there's a very good reason for that: Besides the fact that, as mentioned, the Adhuna Gaes is the bogeyman of choice for much of Native Alaskan mythology, the Tlingit got off rather easy when it came to colonization-almost all of the Tlingit's culture and society has survived to this day in both stories, artifacts, and well, Tlingit. What's more, the religion of the Tlingit was highly organized and formal, and actually not dissimilar to Christian beliefs, meaning that Tlingit converts were able to reconcile their stories and those of the Bible. The wereotters have thus latched on to the culture for dear life, but the dualism is reversed: The Dark Forest is the Shadow, which is the Adhuna Gaes have a duty and right to tend to, and the Warm River is mortal society, which they must remain separate from or face discovery and disappointment when their true natures are inevitably revealed. This also creeps into the Rite of Shadow Infusion: the kindest thing a wereotter can do for the one he loves, say the myths, is to lead her into the Dark Forest to live a life of wonder and freedom among the trees and mysteries, rather than stuck on a boat in the Warm River, which flows where it wants and damn the people on board. This does not help the hypothetical Kushtaka to sleep at night after his Changed beloved declares her intention to never see him again as long as she lives via claw swipe from hybrid form or eats a bullet, so another thing the myths encourage the wereotters to do is to always question the lessons in such myths lest he blindly wander into the Forest's dangers due to his conviction that this is the right path.

              Often, that particular lesson is not listened to at all. Humans can be quite proud when personal ideals after all, and that includes part-humans.

              The Forest Must Grow

              The Gauntlet is not something the Kushtaka particularly like. It's the bane of their existence, in fact. Even living in the Shadow doesn't help-the instincts of the Adhuna Gaes seem especially attuned to the wall between worlds, to the point where a Kushtaka can make a reflexive Wits + Resolve roll to determine its strength. And their instincts loathe it-it's like the constant, menacing feeling one gets when one walks into the den of a man-eating predator when one knows the predator is there. And it never stops-the Gauntlet touches literally everywhere in the universe, separating the children of spirit and flesh. As one might expects, constant dread of an omnipresent force is not good for anyone's sanity, least of all the already shaky stability of most Kushtaka. So, their instincts tell them, shred and weaken the Gauntlet, weaken its hold on their souls and minds, garden the Dark Forest of the Shadow to create a home away from the awful scar that separates spirit and flesh. For the sake of what mental stability they have, the Kushtaka wholeheartedly embrace this side of their nature.

              An Adhuna Gaes has a number of weeks equal to 12 - Primal Urge in which the Gauntlet, while still a dark presence which weighs on his mind, isn't particularly bad-the occasional glance behind him when he's sure he's being watched by the thing is usually all that happens. After that point, a slowly creeping panic begins to worm it's way into the Kushtaka's lizard brain-he's constantly in a state of irritation born from a desire to move as far away from his current position as possible, he suffer nightmares in which his soul is torn from his body, he naturally gravitates towards enclosed spaces since they would be better hiding spaces from whatever thing is looking for him (that the "thing" in question isn't even an active force is something that never occurs to a wereotter beginning to succumb to Gauntlet dread)-in game terms, he develops a unique Condition, Scar Panic, in which every time the Kushtaka feels threatened in any way, he must roll Resolve + Composure to avoid lashing, verbally or otherwise, out at the threat or running from the source, which can just as easily be a complaint about being slow at the checkout line. This usually provides a damper on any social relations the Kushtaka have with mortals, as their colleagues tend to view such behavior as symptoms of an impending psychotic break-because that's precisely what is happening. After a number of days equal to 10 - the Kushtaka's Harmony (those who have natures more in balance are more vulnerable to the irrational phobia their otter sides possess) down to a minimum of 1, he gets a -2 to all attempts to resist Scar Panic after another interval of the same period. A few intervals of this and the Kushtaka tends to be taken away by nice men in white suits, assuming he doesn't lose it completely, change into hybrid regardless of how many witnesses there are, and then proceed to do a madcap dash towards the nearest locus that makes being fox-ridden look outright Zen (the result of a dramatic failure in resisting Scar Panic).

              The good new is that the locus helps-a lot. Much as how escaping from the predator's lair brings a sense of relief and calm, so does an Adhuna Gaes find a haven of sanity from the Gauntlet in a place where it is weak and neutered. Sleeping for a full eight hours in the area of effect of a locus soothes the wereotter's instincts, resetting the period of time that she has before she begins to lose her mind to dread again, even if she has reached the "embryonic psychotic break" point. If she has developed actual penalties to resist the panic, though, the process is a bit longer-a Kushtaka needs to have a number of sleep cycles equal to 6 - the locus' dot rating before the pleasant dreams caused by sleeping in a location her instincts tell her is safe haven (regardless of how "safe" it actually is) work their magic, causing all stacks of Scar Panic to fade. Unlike many instances of supernatural rest cures, this need not be continuous-so long as the Kushtaka does not reach the point where Gauntlet dread intensifies, she may sleep at a locus at any point she wishes and still mark the "tally" of periods to the point where her mind is put back together. In the case of truly spectacular crackups, the "rest period" tends to be one long session, which the Kushtaka either play up as taking sick days to release stress or a stint in an institution, depending on how obvious the insanity was. Given that the wereotter was likely screaming about the Wall Between Worlds that smothers all life at the top of her lungs and sobbing hysterically in the latter case, most mortals are inclined to believe them.

              Sidebar: In Case It Needs To Be Said
              Keeping a Kushtaka away from a locus in full knowledge he needs it to remain sane is torture, pure and simple. Some Urathra may argue that keeping the Adhuna Gaes away from their "loci fix" long enough will eventually teach them that there's nothing about the Gauntlet to be afraid of, but all but the most callous adherents of this argument tend to have their convictions fade when a wereotter, tears in his eyes, proceeds to pathetically throw himself at his captors' feet and offer anything-anything-to be free of the terror his race feels. A Kushtaka can no more be free of Gauntlet dread then a human can be free of the fear of being eaten, no matter how irrational it may be.

              As with most things, torturing a Kushtaka this way is a sin against Harmony 3/2 (I forget where "torture" lies on the scale), while using the effect Wounds have on them is an outright Harmony 1 violation, as that is treating with the Maeljin purely to cause pain to another being.

              -end Sidebar

                  ...But It Must Grow Healthy

                  As might be obvious, for the Adhuna Gaes, the creation of new loci comes before anything else. It's literally impossible for them to do otherwise and still retain the ability to plan coherently. It is also a well-known fact that tragic events form loci more easily, as negative emotions such as sadness and anger have an easier time being felt strongly by multiple people at the same time, create Essence which can easily pool into a locus. So, asks the newbie, why don't they engineer tragedies? Yes, it grates on their human consciences, and yes, they can hurt people close to them. But Harmony is the path of balancing the otter and the human, not the human purely-it literally doesn't cause as much remorse as it would when they were mortal if they are well and truly convinced it's necessary-such as keeping themselves functional enough to do their duties of tending to the Shadow.

                  The neophyte is then usually asked if he really wants to know. If the neophyte answers yes, he is shown to the nearest Wound, kept safely on the side of the physical world. The guide does not risk degeneration for this-after all, forcing the Kushtaka to realize just how much of an effect recklessness has on the Shadow is a very necessary act. Smart guides also take a buddy to calm down the student before he enters Madness Rage out of sheer panic.

                  If being in the presence of the Gauntlet feels like being in the den of something very big and very hungry, being in the radius of a Wound feels like seeing the beast first hand-and it sees you right back, and is already smacking its jaws. Make that beast something out of HP Lovecraft's fever dreams and you've begun to understand exactly how Kushtakas react when faced with the domain of the Maeljin. Everything inside a Wound, to the wereotters' perception, has a hostile "wrongness" to it. It's not like the "wrongness" of non-Elucidian angles, alien gods, or anything else out of a Cthulhu Mythos novel, either-it's like everything inside of the Wound's radius is an inanimate predator, watching the Adhuna Gaes like a mountain lion watches a deer (or perhaps more accurately, how the antagonist of a good slasher flick watches his hapless prey)-patiently, calmly, waiting for the wereotter to slip up or get close enough to pounce on her and tear out her entrails-preferably slowly, while it enjoys every second of her screams. This eldritch terror filters her perceptions as well-she may hear the sharpening of blades out of dark corners, pedestrians might whisper malevolently as she walks past, anything resembling an eye might twitch briefly to follow her movements, and a First Tongue rune resembling a black spiral pops up everywhere, disappearing when the wereotter does a double take . Whereas Gauntlet dread is purely a feeling, inside the Wounds, it seems like a basic fact that everything and everyone is watching a Kushtaka with ill intent-and given how much of their reputation is built aroundclosing Wounds, this may not be an unjustified fear. Spirits can be and often are everywhere.

                  Inside a Wound, the feelings of paranoia caused by Gauntlet dread intensify tenfold. At the beginning of any scene inside the physical or Shadow radius of a Wound or when enters it, the wereotter must roll Resolve + Composure. Failure indicates that the hallucinations and fear caused by Wound panic distract the Kushtaka as he constantly glances behind, to the side of, and above him, inflicting a -1 internal penalty to all actions except fighting or getting out of the wound. The spiritual side of the Adhuna Gaes is riled up by the Maeljin's touch as well, with rolls to resist Madness Rage being made at a -2 penalty (stacking with the previous one, if applicable). What's more, Wound panic is also the sledgehammer to the Kushtaka's sanity in comparison to the delicate, slow penknife of Gauntlet dread-the same systems apply, with caveat that instead of a week, the interval that slowly ticks away a wereotter's sanity is one hour. As might be obvious, sleeping in a loci which is part of a Wound doesn't help-assuming that the wereotter can even get to sleep inside a Wound, all he gets is nightmares showing him the monstrous courts of the Maeljin Incarna and the perverse decadence therein (often as one of the participants...or more accurately, the victims). The good news is that the insanity so gained can otherwise be treated like Gauntlet dread (though the recovery times remain the same), and it's impossible to mistake Wound panic for anything else-as soon as the Kushtaka is inside the area of effect, he knows exactly what he's entered, and when he leaves, the effect cease immediately.

                  The exception to the rule is when the Adhuna Gaes are under the effects of a special Rite, the Dark Forest Pruning Ritual. Taken with the express intention of sterilizing the Wound and retaking the Shadow from the Maeljin, the Wound panic sets in, but the effects of the Rite channels it into a "fight" response, redoubling the wereotter's resolve to remove this plague from the world at all costs. The penalty for failing the Resolve + Composure roll to remain calm is reversed-nothing occurs on a failure, but a success means that the Kushtaka is filled with grim purpose and clarity, giving her a +1 bonus to all roles related to finding a way to neutralize the dark resonance of a Wound and actually enacting it, and the intervals towards a psychotic break are the same length as normal Gaunlet dread (though sleeping in Wounded loci still have no positive effect, though at least the nightmares don't come). This comes with a downside though-should she break the oaths she takes to focus her on the eradication of the Wound (see below), all benefits from the Rite immediately cease, and furthermore, the Kushtaka gains a version of the Guilty that lasts a number of weeks equal to her Primal Urge score as her shame and guilt is compounded by the mocking laughter of the Maeljin ringing in her ears.

                  Effects on Kushtaka Society

                  Given how similar Gauntlet dread and Wound panic are in the Kushtaka mind, it should come as no surprise that they have a tendency to mentally conflate the two. The Gauntlet is, in the Adhuna Gaes estimation, a direct result of whatever spiritual imbalance that gave birth to the Immundi, and through them, the Maeljin Incarna. And yes, "Immundi", not "banes"-the wereotters are fully aware that sometimes, the spirits of particularly corrupt and vicious things can occasionally develop a thought process that is outright human in it's complexity and capacity for subtlety, and outright psychopathic in it's selfishness and viciousness. While they are not aware that Immundi are symptoms of a larger demonic force (see World of Darkness: Inferno for more on other "true demons"), they are aware that that the Seven Vices have something to do with how a spirit undergoes metamorphosis from something slightly unpleasant into something that enjoys being unpleasant, and can think in the long term about how best to indulge it's unpleasant nature in the most rewarding and devastating manner possible. Thus, say the Adhuna Gaes, the "Gauntlet" is really more of a "Great Scar"-an ugly, discolored patch the world barely managed to knit over the semi-mythical First Wound, with the world losing some of it's function-the ability of spirit and flesh to interact freely and harmlessly.

                  So, the natural conclusion from this is to shred the Gauntlet apart, ripping the ineffectual, insanity-inducing bandage off the First Wound and so heal the world in truth, right? Eh...no.

                  As any doctor with half a credible degree with tell you, ripping a bandage off a wound, before it heals, is a really bad idea-and since the Maeljin are kicking about, it's a safe bet to say the First Wound ain't exactly knitting together well. Destroying the Gauntlet might expose it to further infection by the world's evil, and so cause the whole universe to sicken and die of poisoned Essence. That, and while many wereotters are loathe to admit it, the Gauntlet does in fact, do something vital for the world-even the most pure of spirits of the most benign, life-affirming things are focused on their concept to the point of utterly obsessed at the best of times, and can easily cause extreme amounts of pain and hardship out of simple ignorance of the flesh. Thus, the Kushtakas say, their real goal is to create a Shadow where the Gauntlet is unnecessary-where both the Dark Forest and the Warm River respect each others contributions to their mutual health, and exist in as perfect symbiosis as it is possible to get. Once then, all Wounds will close, as the newfound balance will make it impossible for the spiritual infection to survive, and the Gauntlet will dissolve on its own, leaving a Border March of infinite wonder in its place.

                  How do the Kushtaka get there? Well, as a race, they are about the only once-mortals to posses a more confused and contradictory set of coherent ideologies from tribe to tribe then Sin-Eaters have from krewe to krewe-and Sin-Eaters pride themselves on the unique nature of each krewe's individual ethos.

                  Some people do not believe religious cold wars (as in, on the level of America vs. Russia) are possible-one cannot simultaneously believe in the destruction of the other side because God told them too and still keep a pretense of not officially going to war. These people have never seen what happens when Kushtaka tribes have an incompatible ways of fixing the Shadow.

                      Kushtaka Aspects

                      The wereotters are fundamentally both creatures of the water and of sorcery. Their unique Aspects reflect this, allowing them to become both more amphibious and better magicians.

                      Pollution Bane (***)

                      Normal otters are extremely sensitive to changes in the environment, and even the smallest changes can kill of dozens of them. Kushtaka are not normal otters, and when one's biologically-mandated job is to clean up spiritual pollution, learning to ignore the physical stuff isn't so hard. An Adhuna Gaes who learns this Aspect can spend Essence on a one-to-two basis to reduce the Toxicity rating of any poison not delivered by organic means (ie, a bite or claw), to a minimum of zero, for the duration of the scene. Thus, a Kushtaka swimming through a Toxicity 5 oil spill may spend one Essence to reduce the effective rating to 3, and 3 to become immune altogether. This also applies to ingested poisons-with enough Essence, a Kushtaka can drink a cyanide latte with arsenic creamer and only suffer major indigestion as his body attempts to process the inedible substances. Coincidentally, this Aspect sees a lot of use in city waterways-think living out of the nastier sections of the Thames or China's Yellow River.

                      Lutrinae Sleekness (*)

                      Otters don't treat water like a second home-it is one. Quite naturally, the Land Otter People take after their example, and with this Aspect, the same sleekness that otters possess while swimming is transferred to them. A Kushtaka with this aspect increases his Speed by 5 when swimming.

                      Illusive Scent (**)

                      Smell is often the great enemy of a convincing Illusion-it's hard to make someone think there's say, a rotting body when it smells suspiciously like fear. This aspect counteracts that-by spending two Essence when using the Illusion aspect, a Kushtaka can replicate the sent of the thing he is attempting to forge. The same contested roll applies when attempting to fool someone, however.

                      Ritemaster (* to *****)

                      The werewolves who believe Rites are theirs and theirs alone come off as elitist snobs to Kushtaka-and what's more, undeserving elitist snobs. Not only are the wereotters able to perform rites-even those which invoke the power of Luna-without penalty, a Kushtaka with this Aspect possesses something that many Urathra would kill for-the ability to brute force it. A Ritemaster Kushtaka, when performing rites, rolls his Harmony + Ritemaster rating-the knowledge and wisdom of the Kushtaka supports and supplants the need for spiritual balance, though Zi'ir wereotters still cannot perform them (you may be able to cut using a dull blade if you're that strong and persistant, but you can't cut without any blade at all). Naturally, this also means Adhuna Gaes ritemasters can afford to be just as savage and merciless as their less spiritually inclined fellows-and perhaps more so, since Kushtaka all tend to know a few rites for a quick boost.

                      Sidebar: But I Thought We Were Special!

                      Leaving aside the fact that a Forsaken who thinks that rites are gifts from Idmun Luduth alone needs to be introduced to Pure ritemasters (followed shortly by a dope slap for being unprepared for the enemy due to preconceived notions), keep in mind that the vast majority of rites do not involve her at all-and being a spirit, she's far more likely to care about chiminage than any misplaced nationalism by her children. Well, okay-much of what werewolves use is either useless or actively dangerous to the Kushtaka. Ultimately, the Storyteller should decide which rites the Kushtaka can actually use-a good barometer is any Rite which directly involves a werewolf's physical capabilities (which the wereotters don't have), involves Renown (which the wereotters don't even understand the concept of-since when do spirits care about a person's deeds when it doesn't directly involve them and why do they affect what powers you manifest?), or calls directly on Lunes (who have no particular bond with the wereotters), though not Luna herself (again-delicious chiminage, and the moon has more symbolism than just the mother of werewolves).

                      That still doesn't explain why that, on the occasions that they interact with the Adhuna Gaes, the Lunes treat the Kushtaka as fairly as any werewolf (if a bit miffed by the fact that they do not have a concept of "Renown"), and do not take sides in a conflict unless their Masulunim patron has-and even then, just as many are obligated to throw their support behind theAdhuna Gaes due to some ancient pact. Why this happens is a source of puzzlement to both Kushtaka and Urathra alike-when pressed, the Lunes are as cryptic as always, but hint towards the wereotters being "those who bring together two worlds in harmony, rather than apart for their own good". What this means is anyone's guess-but a few obscure legends tell of the River Seer, who taught men how to willfully give themselves over to spirits, in the misguided belief that this would help humans and spirits live in peace. When his dangerous wisdom was called to the attention of Father Wolf, he greeted the Great Hunt as an old friend might, peacefully accepting judgement for his recklessness. While his power was still destroyed for the danger it posed to Pangaea, Father Wolf saw the capacity for redemption in him. In a rare act of leniency, he allowed the Seer to choose five of his mortal children to carry on a shard of his Essence, and so redeem the sins of their father.

                      Does this mean that the Kushtaka are a form of Host? Perhaps. They certainly aren't as inhuman as the vast majority of spirits, let alone the Azlu or Beshilu, and they actually emphasize with other beings. But, their method of reproduction seems an awful lot like cloning a spirit shard, and they have a similarly interesting relationship with the Gauntlet. As before though, the Lunes are cryptic on this matter-including whether or not that, if true, whether they view themselves as the parole officers of the River Seer's children...or their prison guards.

                      -end sidebar


                      Multiplicity of Shadow (* to *****)

                      Sound scary? Depending on how you interpret Illusion, it could be. Quite simply, every dot in this aspect (bought in sequence-a Kushtaka cannot have Multiplicity of Shadow ** until he has the * dot version), is an extra instance of Illusion he can support at any given time. Someone creating Illusions with aural (or with Illusive Scent, olfactory) components must spend one Essence separately for each Illusion he wishes to buff.

                      Companion (*** or ****)

                      As a people with an amicable relationship with the Shadow, the Adhuna Gaes have been known to make a personal friend out of certain spirits, and with that a potent backup. Of course, that may have something to do with that fact that wereotters can make themselves living fetters for said spirits, allowing them free rein as long as they keep to whatever bargain they and the Kushtaka agreed to. This is functionally the same as Familiar Merits from other WoD books such as Second Sight or Mage the Awakening, and the Companion so gained should be statted as they are-three dots indicate a Twilight spirit, while four dots indicate an Embodied one.

                      Drawback: Read that again. See "Companion", not "Familiar"? The bond between a Kushtaka and her Companion is more symbiotic then a Familiar and its master, in that the spirit is definitely getting something else out of the deal. If it doesn't conflict with any existing orders, a Companion will take any reasonable opportunity to indulge its nature, and part of the Companion pact is that the wereotter cannot specifically order it not to do so. She can certainly be a voice of reason, pointing out when it is against either her or the Companion's long-term interests, but any requests without adequate evidence behind them are likely to be ignored.

                      Fighting Style: Fighting Ghost Dance (* to ****)

                      Perquisites: Kushtaka, Multiplicity of Shadow at least *, Intelligence 3

                      This style of martial arts, unique to the Adhuna Gaes due to their abilities with the Illusion aspect, doesn't actually have a formal name-the actual name is slang referencing the Ghost Dance religious movement, practiced by Western American Indians in an attempt to banish the white man from their lands. As one might guess by reading history, this is a bit of mockery on the wereotters' part-simply dancing in a circle in the hopes that maybe, maybe it might do something is beyond foolishness, hence the Fighting Ghost Dance, which actually works on a real level to ensure you don't die. Of course, many Kushtaka who are also Western American Indians (or Native Americans in general, for that matter) aren't amused by the comparison at all, and would rather call it something like "Raven's Claw" or "Coyote Feint Dance", or after innumerable trickster figures, but the term Fighting Ghost Dance seems to have caught on in the Adhuna Gaes' collective unconscious.

                      Rather then being based around physical technique, the Fighting Ghost Dance is based around equal parts misdirection and playing with an opponent's perceptions, using Illusion to fake attacks while making attacks, convincing them to look behind them for a critical instance using a well-placed shadow, etc. It is a very defensive form of fighting, taught under the assumption that the Kushtaka will be attempting to escape or is backed into a corner and attempting to find a way out. Thus, most of it is based around distracting the opponent or slowing him down, allowing the wereotter time to escape and either lose the scent or start the real tactic he has towards killing someone-tricking it into an ambush or a trap.

                      (*) Illusive Feint: At the start of her training, the Kushtaka learns how to overlay illusions on top of himself to give false cues of what he is attempting to do, confusing enemies and allowing time to dodge. He gains a number of extra points in his Defense equal to her dot rating in Multiplicity of Shadow.

                      (**) Images in Ice: At this rating, the Kushtaka has learned how to create Illusion dopplegangers of himself on the fly, which while only a temporary distraction, allows a few critical moments where she can regain her footing and run-the enemy is attacking the ice that holds it away from the freezing water of the wereotter's victory. Unlike other actions, this Merit is used during an enemy's attack The Kushtaka spends one Essence and rolls Intelligence + Composure + Multiplicity of Shadow rating vs. the attacking opponent's Resolve + Integrity rating (if any) or Resistance. Success indicates one of two things-in melee the distraction causes the opponent's swing to be thrown off, allowing the Kushtaka refresh Defense this turn. In the case of a ranged attack, the aim is thrown instead, allowing the Kushtaka to use his Defense against the attack, though it does not refresh.

                      (***) Phantom Pain: People often say that touch is the qualifier by which they judge something is true. A Kushtaka who learns this technique laughs at their foolishness. Now, the line between the phantom sensations of Illusion is so blurred as to be nonexistent, and with that, it's not hard at all to stop faking pain and cause the genuine, debilitating variety-all without laying a scratch. The Kushtaka rolls Intelligence + Composure + Multiplicity of Shadow vs. Resolve + Integrity (if any)/Resistance of an enemy (within five yards). Threshold successes become internal penalties to the targets until the end of the wereotter's next turn, as phantom knives cleave flesh from bone and phantom fires burn them (at the Storyteller's discretion, that second sensation may force vampires to roll to resist fear frenzy).

                      Drawback: This is an incredibly involved form of Illusion, and require's the Adhuna Gaes' full attention. She loses Defense this turn, and cannot use Images in Ice.

                      (****) Ghost Glare: Master Fighting Ghost Dancers were fully aware of how much the flash of the sun on ice could blind someone, and with this technique, they amplify it into a form most flashbangs would envy. The wereotter spends four Essence and one Willpower (in the case that his Primal Urge doe not permit four points of Essence being spent at once, he must spend up to his limit per turn until he reaches four, and cannot be distracted), and for a brief moment, focuses his will on creating the illusion-and thus, the instance-of purest, white light. Roll Intelligence + Composure + dot rating in Multiplicity of Shadows. All within ten yards (except for the Kushtaka himself) is blinded for (Successes) turns, and takes all penalties for fighting blind. Closing eyes does not help-it's way too bright-but success on a Wits + Dexterity/Finesse roll when one knows what the wereotter is going to do in advance allows one to look away from the Ghost Glare.

                      Drawbacks: All within ten yards-including the people the Kushtaka might not like to blind. He cannot avoid targeting his allies within reach, although he can certainly warn them. Also, despite how useful it would be, Ghost Glare is the only way in which Illusion can create light-all other forms simply rely on manipulating what's already there.

                          Rites

                          Perhaps alone among the non-Urathra shapeshifters, Kushtaka instinctively understand how to use their inner balance to invoke arcane powers of the Shadow-and given the Ritemaster aspect, may even be better at it than werewolves. Besides whatever rites the Storyteller feels does not directly invoke the power of the Lunes or draw on something directly related to being an Urathra (see sidebar above). In addition, they have a few rites of their own that they jealously guard, although they might teach a trusted pack a few.

                          Forest Journey (*)

                          While Kushtaka do not have the inherent ability to step sideways, this easily-performed, quick rite allows the next best thing. By meditating at a locus, an Aduna Gaes calls out to the Shadow itself to ferry him and whatever fellows his designates into the spirit world or back again. That being said, this rite is often the first a wereotter learns, so it's more often that the group performs each Forest Journey rite separately, so as to ease strain on a single shaman attempting to ferry them all across in one fell swoop. It's more likely that they only use the "multiple individuals" version when one of them is too insensate or too drained of Essence in order to preform the rite, or when showing trusted humans the Shadow.

                          Ritual: The ritemaster draws a pair of wavy lines in the dust, then makes a straight slash across them, symbolizing him crossing the "river" of the Gauntlet. Should he wish to bring others across, he marks one side with dots for each extra person other than himself. This does not need to leave actual markings, and in fact, the wereotter can "draw" them on any surface, including his own skin. It does empower the ritual if he actually draws something rather than trace it with his fingers, though this leaves evidence behind.

                          Cost: 1 Essence, +1 for each additional person.

                          Roll: 1 roll per turn, with the Kushtaka needing to reach a target number equal to 3 + the number of people he wishes to involve in the ritual.

                          Dramatic Failure: All successes are lost, and the wereotter is tangled in the Gauntlet for a brief millisecond. Naturally, being shoved headfirst into an instinctual phobia isn't pleasant, and the Kushtaka has a -3 internal dice penalty to all rolls for the next minute.

                          Failure: Successes fail to accumulate.

                          Success: Successes accumulate. Reaching the target number causes the sigil (whether an actual sigil or just where it would be via tracing) to glow for a half-second, before releasing a bright flash that obscures the ritemaster and his designated fellows before fading, leaving only unusually thick shadows which gradually vanish. Apart from the thematic, this is simply a normal step sideways, with shadows gradually fading in at the other side of the locus, which retreat to reveal the rite's targets when the process is complete.

                          Exceptional Success: Many successes accumulate. Should the target number be exceeded by five successes, the step sideways happens instantaneously in the light flash.

                          Suggested Modifiers:

                          +3 Ritemaster makes an actual marking

                          -1 Every target after the fourth.


                          Dark Forest Pruning Ritual (***)

                          This rite, while not necessarily unique to the Kushtaka, is almost a much a part of them as is their Illusion ability, and frankly vital to their instinctual purpose. Without it, they would likely be lead away by nice men in white suits to a place that is likely to contain a Wound of it's very own in the best case scenario. Urathra can perform it, but the Adhuna Gaes' own nature assists them in casting it, and casting it effectively.

                          Vital to the ritual is the taking of vows to focus one's self on the eradication of the polluted Shadow without falling to its level-which, given how the Shadow picks up resonance, would probably make the quest self-defeating. The vows are tailored to every individual Wound, but generally they run along the lines of the Vice that gave rise to its existence (including the ones under Thurifuge or Psulerak-Violence and Deception are means, not motives). The vows should not be something that directly impedes the mission, but they should force the participants to deliberately moderate their actions. Thankfully, circumstance is taken into account-if the Kushtaka was acting against his will (such as through blackmail-unless that is covered through another vow-or supernatural influence), the vow is not broken. Without free will, sin is meaningless, something Immundi are very familiar with.

                          Example vows, and the sins they are meant to counter, include:

                          * I will not harm another sapient being unless they pose legitimate threat and have attacked me first (Wrath)
                          * I shall repay any harm to innocents I have harmed out of necessity during my mission within the week of the initial harm. (Pride)
                          * I will not partake in hallucinogenic drugs, even if they assist me in my supernatural abilities. (Gluttony)

                          While clever Kushtaka may attempt to create vows that do not impede them in any meaningful way, the rite depends upon the denial of temptation, and thus, must actually contain vows which (a) would directly affect the typical modus operandi of at least one affected individual, whether in daily life or actual missions, and (b) do not overlap (such as outlawing LSD altogether when one has already vowed against using hallucinogenic drugs, or stating both that "I will take hallucinogenic drugs only if they assist my abilities" and then "I will not partake of mind-altering substances"). The Storyteller has the final word on this, but he or she should keep in mind that the eradication of a Wound is supposed to be a challenging task, though one that should not detract from the fun of the players.

                          Other oaths are subject to Storyteller approval, but must relate thematically to the Vice which opened the Wound, and ideally the specific nature of the Wound itself. There needs to be a minimum of two vows for the rite to work, but each extra vow results in a number of positive effects.

                          Cost: 4 Essence, 1 Willpower.

                          Roll: One roll per ten minutes, with a target number of 10.

                          Ritual: Before the ritemaster performs the rite, she must investigate the Wound in order to determine its originating Vice, and hopefully, its originating event. That can be difficult for Adhuna Gaes, but most spirits know perfectly well what this rite is meant to do, and many are willing to serve as sources in return for minor chiminage (so long as the Wound is closed-no spirit likes the Maeljin infringing on their territory). Once she has an adequate idea of what the Wound was born from, she drafts a series of vows in line with the defining Vice(s), and she and the other participants sit in a circle. The others proceed to meditate on the vows as she beseeches the spirits of the Warm River to aide the denizens of the Dark Forest to remove the scourge of the Wound from their lands before it spreads to the River in the First Tongue. Should the rite go as planned, a spirit of one of the Virtues (often a Helion) materializes in the center of the circle and proclaims itself the Warm River's representative, and asks the ritemaster for a vow to assure it that she is not attempting a trick. The ritemaster responds by laying out her vows, and the spirit affirms their good faith. Then, the quest to cleanse the Wound can begin.

                          Dramatic Failure: All successes are lost. The spirit appears, but it does not accept the ritemasters' vows, and in fact accuses her of aiding the Wound. What happens next depends on the spirit, but it almost always demands a task to promote its Virtue in the world, and until the completion of which the ritemaster cannot perform this rite. STs should keep this simple-detracting from the experience of a chronicle simply because of bad luck/preparation on the troupe's part is not fun.

                          Failure: Successes fail to accumulate.

                          Success: Successes accumulate. Once the target number is reached, the ritual proceeds as outlined, at the end of which the benefits come into effect. For a number of weeks equal to threshold successes, the ritemaster and any designated participants are driven by the power of the rite towards the purification of the Wound-and an intuitive sense of how this might be accomplished. By entering a meditative state, the participants may attempt to commune with the spiritual patron of the rite in order to gain advice about how to neutralize the dark Resonance of the Maeljin's domain. A participant wanting to take advantage of this spends 1 Willpower and rolls Reslove + Composure + (the number of vows beyond the two necessary, up to a maximum of +5). Every success on this roll allows the player to ask the Storyteller one simple "yes" or "no" question about a course of action that will lead to the closing of the Wound, while Exceptional Success obligates the ST to point out a question the player may not have thought of. What's more, redundant vows focus the participants' magic-any rolls made to activate supernatural powers (Aspects, Gifts, Rites, etc.) gain a bonus equal to the number of redundant vows made in service to fulfilling one of the vows or purifying the Wound (again, to a maximum of +5)

                          If one of the vows is violated (again, of the participant's own free will), the benefits immediately end for that participant, and counts as a sin against Harmony 3.

                          Exceptional Success: Many successes accumulate. If the rite is invoked with five or more threshold successes, the spirit patron that arrives is especially helpful, doubling the number of bonus dice gained by extra vows (though obviously, if there were no extra vows, this doesn't do much).

                          Modifiers:

                          Vows directly correspond to events that lead to the Wound opening (+2 per fitting vow)

                          Ritemaster is a Kushtaka (+3)

                          Rite is being recast for the same Wound after the duration ran it without vow breaking by any participant (+3)

                          Rite is being recast for the same Wound after one or more participants broke vows (-2 for every errant participant)


                          Wicked Spirit Hunt (****)

                          Sometimes, particularly in the case of Immundi, one cannot simply let the spirit be. Sometimes, a spirit has strayed so far from the natural order that it must be cut out like the cancer it has become, with extreme prejudice. This rite's effects are otherwise identical to Rite of the Sacred Hunt, but this is a hunt meant to destroy utterly, not harvest for Essence-the participants automatically gain the ability to wound Corpus rather than have to rely on Blessing of the Spirit Hunt to gain the ability, and if the spirit was captured, the ritemaster is under no obligation to release it. If attempting to track a spirit which is still free, the participants are explicitly allowed to enlist spiritual aide from their quarry's rivals, so long as they agree to let the spirit so enlisted have first pick of the Essence. Because this involves no betrayal of the spirit and is ostensibly a way to restore balance, this is not a Harmony violation (although the ST might call for it if they invoke the rite for selfish reasons).

                          Kushtaka generally rely on their spirit allies in this case-again, they aren't fighters-but they will provide backup with Illusion and other rites.

                          Ritual: Same as the Sacred Hunt, but chiminage is burned to the spirit's rivals and calls to them for aide in destroying the ritemaster's target for the good of all involved. The quarry is then forced to remain in the marked-off area by a dense spectral wood that boxes it in, though it does not need to see it in order to know it has been marked as quarry for this hunt.

                              Kushtaka War Stories:

                              As evinced by their spread of abilities, a full-on violent confrontation with the wereotter shamans is one that has gone very, very wrong for them. Rather, a feud with the Kushtaka is likely to resemble a vicious chess match against a hidden adversary, fought through their spiritual allies and mortal pawns as the Urathra attempts to unravel their web of deceptions to find the Kushtaka, squatting like an Azlu, at the center. Until that point though-and after, if the Adhuna Gaes are clever enough to trick the Urathra into believing they have already been defeated, and don't feel like skipping town in the best plan for whatever reason-the Land Otter People are automatically on higher ground. Their world is one of shadows and legerdemain, and until their adversaries penetrate the magician's disappearing act to find the hidden compartment, the Kushtaka are its masters-they know far more about werewolves than werewolves know about them, and they are willing to press the advantage. Worse, they are masters of psychological warfare-if you can't beat someone in the flesh, they say, break their spirits so they welcome death or surrender. Particularly cruel Kushtaka make a list of everything they can take from a opponent's life-and even the kindest do not see the problem (as it isn't a Harmony violation for them, since it's just part of their nature as tricksters) with gaslighting, using their Illusion to make another doubt their perceptions of the world-and with it, their minds. More than one dispute with the Urathra has been won after the opposing pack voluntarily checked themselves into an asylum.

                              At the same time, one of the themes of the Kushtaka is being unsure, and that includes the morality of the situation, too-while some Urathra might argue that the creation of loci left and right reeks of hubris and disdain for the balance of the Shadow, the simple fact is that the Kushtaka are slaves to their instincts when it comes to this-even leaving aside Gauntlet dread, the deepest part of their spirit souls tell them that this is right, this is what we are supposed to do. While the Urathra are no strangers to the inherent evil of the Hosts, Kushtaka can and do emphasize with humans, and their loci are made with that perspective in mind-even the more negative Resonance they engender has positive aspects (the Envy which implores people to be better than they are, the Greed which motivates them to make a better life for themselves), and oh yes, their inherent hatred-and vaccine-for Wounds. A good Kushtaka war story should have the players questioning who the morally superior side is in the conflict.

                              Below are a couple of stories featuring the Kushtaka as antagonists, either as part of a larger threat or the main force driving the story.

                              The Jigsaw Puzzle Prophet

                              Something is happening to the Gauntlet. Weird, frightening tears in the wall between spirit and flesh are opening suddenly, and then closing again a second later with no explanation. A second, unfortunately, is more than enough to something to stumble through and get trapped, so increasingly humans are finding themselves stranded in the spirit world and spirits are finding their way into the Hisil. Normally, they would be ecstatic, but in a mirror of the Herd's perceptions of reality being terrifyingly widened, the spirits in the effected area are beginning to understand things outside of their purview...but still not possessing the point of view needed to comprehend such insights, driving them to a fearful madness. And the problem is getting worse, even opening portals in the Shadow to strange and frightening Places-That-Aren't, through which old and alien spirits are streaming.

                              The "what" for all this is, in fact, a "who"-or at least, the prototype of a who. While he looks like a cross between a homeless man and a dime-store occultist, anyone who sees him activate his strange and weird powers sees that he looks more like a three-dimensional piece puzzle of a man, with black, gaseous ectoplasm seeping out of the places where the pieces of his body don't quite fit together. He's not doing this intentionally, either-it seems to be a reflection of how he was "born"-the reanimated remains of an entirely different man who was torn to pieces by spirits, and then put back together and given an entirely new mind and life. He's only about five years old (the new mind in his body, not the body, which is closer to 25), and he has the emotional maturity of a gloomy teenager, but he's not a bad person-he was getting ready to leave and let the Gauntlet regenerate itself away from his corrupting influence...but then he met the local Kushtaka tribe.

                              See, another thing his bizarre origin causes is to set people who meet him on edge, causing them to lose their higher reasoning in favor of gut feelings and intuition-which means they view him through the lens of fight or flight, causing them to turn hostile and unfriendly. Everyone except therianthropes, that is-perhaps its because of their intimate understanding of their animal instincts, but werebeasts of all sorts, not just Kushtaka and Urathra, react to him as if he was simply a normal-if creepy and somewhat alien-person. True, Death Rage becomes easier to fall into around him, but it's not actually directed towards him in particular-and Madness Rage is rather small beans compared to the complete lack of Gauntlet dread in the area he effects, as it's a lot harder to be scared of something the wereotters' instincts say is getting increasingly weak and toothless. Thus, the Adhuna Gaes have come to view him as a kind of chosen one, thinking he may finally be the cure to the First Wound their legends say resulted in the Gauntlet, and the madness he spreads is simply the result of the Maeljin fighting back in a panic. The Jigsaw Puzzle Prophet doesn't know what the hell his new friends are talking about, but thanks to the utter loneliness of his existence up until that point, he doesn't mind the expected responsibilities one bit.

                              (as might be obvious to Promethean: The Created players, the Prophet is an Ulgan, an inhuman being made from a human corpse using the rites of Siberian shamans. For those not using Promethean, stat him as a Spirit-Claimed, with an Integrity score and Numina appropriate to a nomad studying both the spirit world and humanity).

                              General Esoterics 101:

                              A new study group has been formed at a local high school. Formed by Maurice Teeyineidí, an Alaskan anthropology professor renowned (/made fun of) for his fascination with Native American mythology of all sorts, the group (based around studying various conceptions of the spirit world) caters to the more occult-fascinated students...which, in the World of Darkness, is a pretty significant portion of the student body. Not everyone is willfully blind, after all. Despite rumors about "satanism" or "witchcraft", the so-called Mythology Club is actually pretty open about what it does, leading for the actually sane observers to dismiss it as just another pack of geeks, being creepy because they think its cool.

                              And then, strange, humiliating things start happening to the bullies. One has his pants fall down in the hallway, seeming like they were tugged. One turns into a wall that he was sure was a door to his class just a second ago. One particularly mean cheerleader misjudges where the other team members are in the cheer pyramid, resulting in in them collapsing during a football game, in front of the entire, snickering, audience. Despite her protests that she was doing everything right, she was still dropped. It takes real talent in failing to think that the person you are attempting to balance on is one foot ahead of you. In any case, no one thinks to link that with how cold and distant some of the Myth Clubbers are getting from their classmates, their non-Clubber friends, and even their families.

                              In fact, the Mythology Club is actually a front for the latest incarnation of the Holy Crow Medicine Society, a tribe of Kushtaka that were decimated by the twisted king of a Wound-born brood about a year ago. Only their leader, Teeyinedi (not his real name-it's actually the name of one of the Tlingit tribes that compose the Raven clan), and their lead shaman (his wife, Janet) survived, and the two were forced to go into hiding. Maurice, not seeing any particularly good candidates for the Rite of Shadow Infusion among the city's occult community, gradually realized that a significant portion of his students were both highly inquisitive about the hidden world and felt an extreme disconnect from the world around them-in other words, the kind of people who the Adhuna Gaes would be happy to turn. Thus, the Society quietly became a mystery cult, snaring students interested in the hidden side of everything and picking out those who desperately wished to be a part of said side and get out of their current lives, initiating them into knowledge of the Shadow and what Mr. and Mrs. Teeyinedi actually are, giving them a choice-become Kushtakas like them and help heal the Shadow, or simply live with the secret of the spirit world's existence. So far, four out of the six students asked have chosen the former option, eager to become what they see as superheroes bringing justice to the Shadow, while the other two are trusted acolytes (one, Ariel Harley, is studying thaumaturgy (Second Sight) from Janet's sister, a Hedge Witch, as well).

                              Now, bringing in teenagers to fight a war against a powerful Wound demon that nearly killed the Society already seems like one of the most amoral things a person could do, if not outright evil. Thing is, Maurice would agree-he's already quietly manipulated someone else to pick up where he left off (this can be your players in a previous story, if you desire), and simply wants to put the Society back together, this time with people who knew full well what they're getting into (unlike Janet and himself) when they received the Rite. The problem is that the power has gone to the Myth Clubbers' heads. Being healthier, stronger, and yes, sexier then one ever could be as a mortal, not to mention the power of magic wereotters command, is one of the things that, generally, cause a hormonal, previously underconfident and lonely teenager to develop an ego problem. While it's unlikely they'll develop a god complex, the new Kushtaka are getting increasingly mean-spirited and cruel with their pranking, and sooner or later one of them is going to cross the line and cause something that's going to lead to a lot of trouble for them down the line. More importantly, they've grown tired of having to make excuses to disappear every few weeks to recuperate from Gauntlet dread in the Teeyinedis' Dark Forest Temple and are trying to turn their homes into loci. The events caused by this are likely going to be what interests the pack. Or perhaps one of their mortal relatives join the Myth Club, and are finally shown what their weird family member gets up to?
