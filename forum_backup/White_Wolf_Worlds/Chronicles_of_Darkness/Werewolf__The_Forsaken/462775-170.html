            Coalesced Idigam take on concepts that are at work in the environment when they Coalesce, but their relationship to that concept is askew from what spirits of those concepts would be.

            Typical spirits basically are what they eat, and as a rule generally seek a propogation of their essence in the environment so as to eat more and more and possibly move up the food chain as they develop courts to sustain them while playing to higher predators above them.

            An Idigam eats what it is, of course, but at the end of the day, it's not really those things, and as such it's more experimental in what it can do/be and how it can fill it's other inscrutable ends. Of course, since this is a very weird thing, they can be obsessive in finding out their results and answers.

            But yeah, a Coalsced Idigam metaphorically pokes it's belly button where a typical spirit doesn't even acknowledge it.

                                    The Builder of Tongues (presumably) Coalesced around concepts that would allow it to facilitate linguistic understanding between different tribes, and, in the version of history where it pupates in the Neolithic and emerges undisturbed, has a new form that descriptively lacks the signifiers that would indicate it as magath to the surrounding choirs. It may well have ceased to be an idigam — at the very least, when it was doing its proto-Earthbound job it almost certainly retained the ability to return to Formlessness — but the significance of the outcome is more in that outcome being "there is now a powerful spirit of a specific concept."

                                    Remember, spirits mostly get less pure as they rise in Rank, such that one of the sources of magath is a spirit making a power-play it doesn't have the bandwidth to back up and becoming powerful but ostracized from the natural order; when we say "there's a spirit for everything," we mostly don't mean that literally, as the fine-slicing of similar concepts tends to sand itself down to more generalized classifications that a spirit then builds outward from toward influence over spirits of related things. The exceptions tend to be correspondingly large; the spirit of a city is powerful because it is attached to a defined phenomenon that is versatile within its territory, old elementals are powerful because they represent ubiquitous and ancient phenomena, and the dragon of thought and contemplation that the Builder of Tongues reforges itself into is the ambitious work of a powerful force of primordial Essence-shaping.

                                    It's rare for a powerful spirit to get to do what Dreamborn do and derive Resonance from a singular defining concept, but certain idigam functionally aim to do exactly that.

                                                            Dante[RU] Attempting to parse through the wall of text to answer holistically rather than pick through to quote and respond to specific queries:

                                                            The vast majority of things that exist and are not sapient beings like humans have a spiritual reflection in the Hisil, though abstract spirits are more reliant on resonant conditions beyond the prosaic for sustenance (i.e. a spirit of murder or jealousy does not have the same easy fallback for continued activity that a spirit of water or dogs does).

                                                            Rank 0 spirits (motes/muthrum) are the spiritual equivalent of algae, with no Attributes or Numina; they're the most straightforward example of how the ecology of the Shadow works, because they only passively accumulate resonant Essence from nearby and other spirits get better returns from eating them than directly harvesting their purviews (in the sense that there's no daily limit on how many motes a spirit can steal from). Some things might not have picked up enough significance to have their own spirits right out the gate, but as set dressing it doesn't take much recognition for a mote to form in the Shadow's record of events; by the same token, there's little stopping a less forward-thinking spirit from consuming a mote whole instead of skimming its Essence stores and letting them grow back.

                                                            A spirit that has a proper Rank and is aware is still the sum of its parts and inclined to act in ways appropriate to its nature; most Gafflings are still tied to a single specimen of their purviews and therefore not likely to become magath on their own initiative, such that desperation brought about by sudden environmental changes or mystical tampering is the more likely cause for hybridizing a minor spirit out of the natural order. This and the relatively simple bans they operate under can be pointed to as one reason why they're the favored level of power for Forsaken pack totems (particularly given the excesses the Pure's patrons often drive them to).

                                                            Stronger spirits tend to be less pure-concept and more of their own personalities, having incorporated enough power to outgrow a singular focus (with the most prominent exceptions being very large things like cities or the elements). Sometimes a spirit is formed at a higher Rank (most animal spirits are at least strong enough to be awake, for example, and an especially major event may birth a Jaggling wholesale), but for the most part spiritual power is aggregated over time through found significance and the weight of history — the spirit of a bonfire may be shocked into wakefulness by the events surrounding it and frantically gather power from the spirits of things burned and burning, but it's probably going to start that feeding frenzy at Rank 1 and maybe work its way up to Rank 2 if it's very well-placed. Maybe a viral rumor instantiates a Place-That-Isn't and an Ensih to safeguard it, but that sort of thing is uncommon

                                                            Despite the Uratha's mandate, spirits tend to work out an equilibrium in most circumstances, such that if there's room in the hospital for two different death spirits to operate they probably won't consume each other out of hand until and unless they grow particularly close to each other and judge that they could command more influence as a single powerful death-Jaggling taking tithe from their lessers than splitting the cut with the guy in the morgue.

                                                            Finer details than Influence and broad concept filter into a spirit through the resonance of what it eats and where it lives, such that urban spirits of the same animals will usually look different than their rural counterparts even before the distortions of memory and perception brought about by the Gauntlet and the type of observers contributing to the spiritual makeup of an area; in the countryside, many animal-spirits and natural features are larger and more extreme because their presence is described by the viewpoints of smaller animals, while the Shadow of a city tends to be a more human-scale stylized impression that turns buildings into sleeping giants and vehicles into fast-moving monsters. Regardless of where they develop, though, spirits tend to have appearances and powers appropriate to their natures, just as the resonance of an area tends to paint it with the brush of its resident concepts — which is why Wounds tend to look really messed up, since they're sites of self-perpetuating negative resonance.

                                                            The Shadow is, it should be noted, a concrete place — even spirits of abstract concepts have ephemeral flesh that is every bit as tangible in their home realm as wood or stone is in the Flesh. Things come into existence and change there through spooky abstract processes that are more art than science, but the natural order of the Shadow is built on a huge esoteric set of agreements between groups of spirits that rites tap into in a way that roughly mirrors the material world's sensibilities — plants grow for reasons that have little to do with the Sun, so the lingering memories of millions of clouds crowd the sky if the Lord of the Day doesn't see fit to throw his weight around, but it's cold in the winter and hot in the summer, the spiders eat flies, the rats hide from cats, and when the Moon is out you can hear the wolves howl.

                                                            If it weren't an ancient playground for dreaming gods sheltering weird remnant populations and dormant fossil specimens, the biggest problems the Uratha would have to deal with would be knock-on complications from catastrophe and human action and the occasional demonic incursion from same, but unfortunately, the Hosts and the idigam hook into the Shadow from older points in its cosmogony and Wolf's whole job seems to have been keeping out the weird shit and culling pieces of runaway history before they got big enough to become dangerous, and most of that isn't the level of Shadow-stuff the average werewolf needs to worry themselves with compared to taking a bite out of occult crime and trimming the cannibal rosebushes.

                                                            tl;dr You've just about got the size of it, bearing in mind that spirits themselves are, while collectively versatile, among the least bizarre things a werewolf can run into when things are operating normally. Mage's perspective on the cosmology puts a nebulous link between the Shadow and the dreams/mind/soul of the world beyond humanity, which contains many psychic presences of a dubious and/or occult nature, but otherwise tends to be the most grounded of the Astral realms in its representation of geography. Some variant on "spooky funhouse-mirror version of material reality" will serve you well enough as long as you keep an individual locale's history, significance, and mood in mind when describing it.
