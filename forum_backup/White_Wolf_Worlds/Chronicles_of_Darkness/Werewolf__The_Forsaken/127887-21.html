            O-KAY!

            Now that I have a chance to look at 2e...I gotta do some clean-up. The devs have a lot of new canon terms, so I have to reshuffle my stand-ins around to mean something better. That being said, SOME WORDS ARE NOW CANON! "Urdur", which means "puppy", HAS THE APPROVAL OF THE WRITERS!

            Here's the new canon terms so far:

            Abni-Gur: The “crucible of becoming.” Storm Lord philosophy that by overcoming hardship they might one day become like Urfarah.
            mus-rah: “Holy killing ground,” a Hunter term for territory.
            Sanghba’zir: An oathbreaker, especially one who violatesthe Oath of the Moon.
            Mir: “Rage,” the killing fury that overcomes werewolves in Gauru form.
            Sugrah: A Storm Lord game of spotting weaknesses in one’s tribemates to show off cunning and strengthen the tribe.
            Urghir: “Dog,” derogatory term for high-Harmony Uratha. Someone who clings too closely to the world of Flesh.
            Uzahal: “Fade,” derogatory term for low-Harmony Uratha. Someone who becomes too spirit-like.
            hurmas-hi: the Cahalith mindset, lit. "submitting to dreams"

            "Nu Sum Ghumur Nu Su Ghid": Offer no surrender you would not accept, the canon Blood Talon oath.
            "Nu Mus Halhala": Let no sacred place in your territory be violated, the canon Hunters In Darkness oath.
            "Kul Kisura Udmeda": Honour your territory in all things, the canon Iron Masters oath.
            "Nu Si Gid Namtar": Allow no one to witness or tend to your weakness, the canon Storm Lords oath.

            gid: to transfer, to pass along; to lay a burden on
            si: to remember, to recall, to recount (replaces sakthaf lifir, which now means "to recall, to recount from rote memory; another name for eidetic memory")
            kisura: border, territory; a boundary line
            kul: to honour a person, a place or thing; to honour a tradition; to pay respects
            udmeda: forever, always (replaces thuri, which now means "immortal, long-lasting, not forgotten" and nusirah, which means "something that inherently cannot be affected by time, or is meant to resist the wear of time, through magic or the means of physics itself; stronger word than thuri")
            mus: to corrupt, to curdle (replaces "viruhthim", which now refers to acts [especially those of Bale Hounds] of turning places into Wounds and Shoals)
            halhala: sacred ground (lit. "divine secret" or "divine inheritance"; replaces "unurifanda", which now refers to extremely powerful locuses or other wellsprings of sacred power only; such places are polar opposites of wounds)
            sum: to give, to present (replaces "sagrik", which now means to offer, to sacrifice and/or to give up something for a greater good)
            humur: a surrender (lit. "honour-fodder"; replaces "sar e habalthu", which means to flee or run from battle like a coward, or to give up in the face of a difficult situation without trying)
            e'ne: from

            Lu'im Iduth: The Deceiver Lune (lit. "the Moon's defiling clay")
            Urdam: Believer Wolves, followers of the Deceiver Lune
            Ili Sugin'dab: The Penitent, first follower of the Deceiver Lune
            Numun Sala: The Lune of Mercy, the Lune Lu'im Iduth claimed to be when it appeared to Ili Sugin'dab
            Sag'suga Isim: The Empty Hunger (lit. "the fat king who needs sustenance")
            Lul'Aya: The False Father (lit. "liar father"), short for Ninglul Aya
            Imadih Shedu: A spirit dedicated to change and chaos, and the original form of Lul'Aya
            Ghal'urfarah Aya: "Great Wolf-Father", the form of Lul'Aya that takes the shape of Urfarah
            Inim-Galag: The heralds (pack) that follows Lul'Aya
            Mash'ng-Idud: "Eyes of the Moon", the twisted spirit-scouts of Lul'Aya
            Gir'shedu: "Berserkers", twisted Gauru-esque werewolves that serve as the fighters of Lul'Aya
            Ansar-zalag: The Heavenly Fire (lit. "the smoking brightness of heaven")
            Lilia'izi: The scouts and occasional shock troops of Ansar-zalag
            Mulan Naminigir: Star Herald, fanatical worshippers of Ansar-zalag that have been gifted with his power
            Gagh-Azur: The Mouth of the Depths (lit. "mouth of the roiling figure")

            And these are the ones I speculate are canon, but I'm not entirely sure:

            shedu: a being, an elemental, a physical representation of something; another name for a spirit
            ghal: great, mighty, all-powerful
            nigir: a herald, a representative
            id-: a suffix denoting things associated with Luna, usually in the form of idu
            Idud: an alternative spelling for Iduth — considering it comes from the name of an idigam's servants, I'd use "Idud" to refer to things that are supposed to be moon-like or represent the moon, but are mockeries of or not quite right
            mulan: a star; I'd treat my existing malan interpretation of it as an alternative spelling

            And finally, the term replacing childbirth (and the act of it) is harsum dur, which means "to present a child to the world". Dursagrik means to sacrifice a child, or give up a child for the greater good, and afterbirth is now known as ipu.

            I linked to the new spirit terms earlier in the thread, and Wasu-Im and Basu-Im are on the previous page.

                                    With gifts like Luna's Embrace now in 2e, I've decided to translate a few words that refer to gender and sexuality. If anyone thinks any of the connotations here are offensive, let me know IMMEDIATELY so I can change it to something more suitable, and also let me know if I missed anything. Translations for male and female can be found in the dictionaries already on the front page.

                                    ala'ma: a helpful spirit with intersexual characteristics
                                    sethu: a helpful spirit with no gender-defining characteristics, or a sexless/gender spirit
                                    munidim: a spirit of intersexuality
                                    nidiim: a spirit of femininity
                                    muniim: a spirit of masculinity
                                    munidh: intersex
                                    nuhasilah: sexless or having no sex
                                    falahasilah: transsexual (lit. "changed parts")
                                    kuhasilah: biological sex (lit. "one's own body parts")
                                    kuhae: gender identity (lit. "one's own sense of self")
                                    nidae: identifying as the female gender
                                    munidhae: identifying as the intersex gender
                                    munae: identifying as the male gender
                                    nuhae: identifying as no gender or genderless
                                    futhae: identifying as a gender that is not female, male, genderless or intergender
                                    za'hae: genderfluid (lit. "moving gender")
                                    falahae: transgender (lit. "changed genders")
                                    ene'ae: one's preferred gender identity
                                    ashae: to have one gender
                                    minhae: to have two genders
                                    peshae: to have three genders
                                    limmuhae: to have four genders
                                    yahae: to have five genders
                                    sharhae: to have many or multiple genders
                                    hae'im: spirits of gender concepts; add 'im to the end of any of the previous gender listings to get the name for a spirit of that gender

                                    gima-kiliteg: homoromantic (lit. "loves the same")
                                    minsur-kiliteg: biromantic (lit. "loves both")
                                    fu'i-kiliteg: heteroromantic (lit. "loves the opposite")
                                    thu-kiliteg: panromantic (lit. "loves all")
                                    netehnar: aromantic (lit. "not attracted romantically", "doesn't have romantic feelings")
                                    gima-ene: homosexual (lit. "finds pleasure in the same")
                                    minsur-ene: bisexual (lit. "finds pleasure in both")
                                    fu'i-ene: heterosexual (lit. "finds pleasure in the opposite")
                                    thu-ene: pansexual (lit. "finds pleasure in all")
                                    neteg: asexual (lit. "not attracted sexually", "doesn't have feelings of attraction")
                                    fu'i: the opposite, the other side
                                    (suffix -im to any of these to get the names of the spirits that represent these concepts)

                                                    Edit: a few things I missed putting up before.

                                                    namtar: consequences; fate, destiny, a decreed fate; that which has befallen another
                                                    ihdar: seer, soothsayer, fortune-teller
                                                    bakkuk: a specific trigger for Kuruth (lit. "fly-bite")
                                                    balalim: a passive trigger for Kuruth (lit. "storm-brewer")
                                                    bazur: a common trigger for Kuruth (lit. "undoing-call")
                                                    sulu: bait, lure
                                                    Hiskabhlu: a spiritual touchstone (lit. "Shadow-link")
                                                    Gurkabhlu: a physical touchstone (lit. "Flesh-link")

                                                    namtar-ni: "fated prey" or "chosen prey", the preferred prey of each tribe's Siskur-Dah
                                                    daha: to hunt fairly, to hunt respectfully; to hunt with daha is to hunt like you should, and to end it like you should, instead of perverting and drawing out the process
                                                    dahskir-uguf: "heedless to the hunting howls" — to ignore or deny the Siskur-Dah and the urge towards it
                                                    neha-im: "calmed storm" or "calmed Rage" — said of a werewolf or other shapeshifter with the Controlled Burn merit
