            I'll give you an example:

            Say your character has Harmony 7, and he spends a whole month without venturing to the shadow. That's a breaking point toward Flesh (base -2), and given he's 2 points above perfect balance and breaking toward that end, he gets another -2. Those modifiers are usually countered by touchstones, at least negating from 1 to 4 points assuming you've taken a merit such as Anchored (normally it'll be a flat +2).

            Consequently, if my character is in Harmony 4, he gets a ban, and killing people is a spirit breaking point at (-1) because of the touchstones and state of harmony.

            All of this is alleviated by the point you're at harmony 8 and above, or 3 and below, because then you get much more easier breaking points toward the other end of the harmony chart.

                                Cainite has it right: Harmony is a scale from 0-10, with 5 sitting as the balance point in the perfect middle, having 5 dots above and below.

                                Is those dots towards each side which act as penalties to slide further in that direction.                                     Originally posted by SieranTheFox View Post
                                    So if you have, I dunno, 9 harmony, you take a penalty to a breaking point roll of 4. This make you -4 more likely to fail the roll, which would slide you toward the spirit.
                                    No. If you have Harmony 9 you have a baseline -4 penalty to the Resolve + Composure roll to avoid gaining Harmony from breaking points toward Flesh. You have no baseline penalty for rolls to avoid losing Harmony from breaking points toward Spirit, because 9 is not below 5.

                                    So if your harmony is two, you receive no penalty toward your roll since you have zero harmony above 5 and the roll is disproportionately more likely to entrench a spirit-oriented character to remain in the spirit, rather than a flesh-oriented character, who is more likely to fail a roll and slide toward the spirit.
                                    This is the reverse of the reading problem above — if your Harmony is 2, you're three points below Harmony 5 and therefore suffer a -3 penalty to the Resolve + Composure roll to avoid losing Harmony, but since 2 is not above 5 you have no inherent penalty for rolls to avoid gaining Harmony from breaking points toward Flesh. A Flesh-oriented character is not more likely to lose Harmony.

                                    You've grasped parts of the dynamic, but what you're missing is that the dynamic goes in both directions — once your Harmony destabilizes from 5, you have a cumulatively harder time resisting gaining Harmony at higher Harmony or losing it at lower Harmony while receiving no such added difficulty in the opposite direction. You have to fail rolls to alter your Harmony in either direction, and it's harder to flip from one end of the scale to the other because the middle is more desirable and exists along the way up or down.
