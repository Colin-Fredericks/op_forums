                                    I would say its a problem if that aspect is not clearly presented in the corebook and you need to buy a supplement for it to be emphasised.

                                    What I really want to see to draw me into a Werewolf game is a strong focus on BEING a Werewolf and the conflict that goes with it. Being a Vampire, Changeling or Mage all have an inherent conflict built into the templete. I think Werewolf needs to explore this more. How do you feel about being a Werewolf. At its best, you are an unstoppable force of nature. You are extremely hard to kill. How does that affect a human psyche? How would a teenager, who has spent all their life being bullied, react to suddenly realising that they never need to be scared of (human) bullies again? That the bullies should be scared of him? Would he lord it over them or not? Would the power overwhelm him? Then the downside. You suddenly feel like tearing out the throat of that guy for simply shoving past you. How many times will this happen before you snap? You find the animal side taking over. The dynamic between the human mind and the animal, raging instincts of a werewolf should be inherently built into the core book and the template. It should be possible to play a chronicle out of the book that has no spirits in it. That has no territory conflicts in it. You should be able to simply play a game about what being a werewolf feels like.

                                    This should NOT be the whole game. Spirits should have a place. Hunting should defintely have a place. Developing territory and tribal political conflicts should have a place. Exploring the balance between the material world and the spirit world should have a place. The Shamanistic aspects should have a place. But if you want to sell people on playing a werewolf game, you need to make that core werewolf experience feel fun and visceral. All that other stuff that makes Forsaken more distinctive and unique can be layered on afterwards.

                                    Vampire has a pretty cool setting with lots of weird stuff in it. Sure if you want to, you can play a blood magic slinging priest of the Lancea Sanctum. But the book at its core presents the dynamic of being a vampire and devotes a lot of focus on this dynamic. The Covenants, Blood Magic, Politics are all in the core book but that stuff is presented as being ON TOP OF the core experience of playing a Vampire. Mage does the same thing. You suddenly realise that you can bend the world to your whim (with some drawbacks) Do you go mad with power? This conflict is inherent to the setting before you add in the Exarchs, faction politics and so forth. I think Werewolf should follow suit and have just as strong a focus on the internal conflict in the core book as it does on external conflicts.

                                    I would like a Werewolf corebook that is similar to Changeling's corebook. It would present several ways you can play the game, internal conflict focused (the experience of being a werewolf), external spirit threats, phyiscal world threats, defending territory and expanding that territory and pack politics should all be viable options straight out of the corebook with supplements giving more focus to each other area as desired. The corebook though should get you fired up to play a certain type of game regardless, you shouldn't need a supplement to play the game in a less narrowly defined way.

                                    I have only read through the corebook and don't own it so it is possible that I am being slightly too harsh on the original book but for contrast I bought the Mage and Changeling corebooks based on their tv tropes description and the impression of the games given on forums. Reading the corebooks I instantly thought of fun possibilities and got a sense of why people liked the game; especially with the Changeling corebook which is brilliantly written and expressed. I like the idea of werewolves and playing werwolves could be fun, it is something I actively looked into but the default corebook just did not do a lot for me. I am sure the game is good, but the corebook seemed weak in my opinion and so I never bought it. I would relook at the game as whole based on the new corebook but I don't want to buy a load of supplements for the game straight away.
