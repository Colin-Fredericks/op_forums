    Originally posted by nofather View Post

    The thing is, while all violence isn't evil, and violence being evil is not a thing World of Darkness suggests, Thurifuge is. And Thurifuge gains off of all violence, because it's a spirit of violence. .'
    I absolutely agree that violence spirits can eat violence essence and can sustain itself in the presence of violence. Thurifuge isn't just any violence spirit though, it is more complex than that. It is a Sin, Wrath, Cruelty, Carnage, War, and Atrocity Spirit just as much if not more than it is a Violence Spirit. All those are in harmony with the resonance and nature of Thurifuge. Self-risking violence done with restrain, due compassionate consideration towards those it is acted upon done for unselfish and righteous reasons would be drastically out of sync with the harmony of the vast majority concepts/essences/resonances that makeup Thurifuge, and from my understanding of spirit ecology would begin to change Thurifuge if it allowed itself to consume or absorb enough without mitigation towards its core self with the appropriate feeding.

    It might be possible cruel wicked violence to consume selfless righteous violence, in the same way sorrow can conceptually consume happiness and happiness can conceptually consume sorrow. In that case I'd be wrong, but then that would mean a great enough force of brave righteous justice violence spirits could be the best hope of getting rid of Thurifuge by devouring it.(or a large enough peace spirit)

    Gathering that force of selfless brave justice violence spirits would be exceedingly difficult when evil violence forces seek to devour and pollute them, but makes an Epic Apocalyptic chronicle idea.

                        I'm sure others have said what they like, and its certainly going to be similar to my opinion, but I'm going to voice it all the same without digging through 7 pages of forum posts (mostly time constraints).

                        I LOVE the enemy within. The ability to play the back stabber, to keep THAT secret from the rest of the pack requires a great deal of acting, subterfuge, not just on the table but off as well. Your players and companions cannot know, else wise they will start lookig for a way to find it in game. It's an alluring power.

                        Then we get into the cultural aspects of them. I love soulless wolf, and his ritual of eating sins. A bit pointless now with 2nd edition harmony change, but its very evocotive all the same. Their portrayal in blasphemies is like a less edgy spirals. If Forsaken are akin to heavy metal then the bale hounds are akin to doom metal. Its the rythm of burning churches and destroying civilization. The aspect in all people that twinges at the idea of anarchy, at freeing the beast. True they're servants in worse ways then the pure, but damn do they win.

                        Hell looking at the first edition tainted moon gift list.... It was bleak and savage without being unnecessary. The black spirals are so steeped in rape, not just of the flesh but of the spirit. The bale hounds more feel like the guy who sold his soul for insane powers, and still got to come out on top. At that point it isn't about being the under dog. Once you make your move you're beyond being touched. Your former pack is dead. You now have an insane home field advantage for any invading pack (if you did your job right), and if you've recruited more bale hounds (assuming its the 9th tribe motif and not the multiple sins motif) then you have an unstoppable force from spiritual hell.

                        It's an allure that appeals to anyone who loved Al Pacino in the devil's advocate. It's that cultural break that goth kids looks for. Its that black metal you love in your early 20's. You were a monster as a forsaken, true, but as a Bale Hound? You can become a god.

                        WoD-Dark Eras!! (Backed for Viking Age Werewolf)

                            Originally posted by Lyrics Of War View Post
                            It's an allure that appeals to anyone who loved Al Pacino in the devil's advocate. It's that cultural break that goth kids looks for. Its that black metal you love in your early 20's. You were a monster as a forsaken, true, but as a Bale Hound? You can become a god.
                            ... then the twist?

                            All this temptation, betrayal, power with a price. It's all wonderful stuff. 110% support it and the stories that come forth from it.

                            The last laugh though, should be had by the Maeljin.



                            Bale Hounds get the perks as long as they keep paying. Vices, torments schemes, bad mojo plans. They keep doing them, the power keeps flowing. Prices slowly (sharply?) increase as tend to happen with evil incarnate. If they keep paying them they're still in good graces.

                            When though, the line is drawn, and the Bale Hound or Hounds say, 'okay, it's been fun, please stop the ride now.' The Maeljin say 'no.'



                            Now you Chronicle of Darkness writers are a brilliant bunch, but what I would love to see in addition to all of this torment, temptation, double-agents of evil deal for Bale Hounds in 2e is what happens when the tables get turned on them. When the Maeljin laughs, its last laugh.

                            Bale Hounds should not just be the horror of the Self, the Other Within, that's the start. The real horror? The aftermath of winning. The true price of power. The Spiritual and Fleshy dissemination of a Bale Hound. Is it a crazy predator wolf? A mewling husk? A new form or shape? The loss of control, of purpose, of self?

                            Power corrupts, absolute power corrupts absolutely. Corrupting others might stave off their own damnation, but what awaits a Bale Hound really at the end?

                            That's what I want to find out in 2e.
