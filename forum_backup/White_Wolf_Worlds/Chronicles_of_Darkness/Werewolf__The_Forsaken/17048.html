    Do the pure make sense?

    11-24-2013, 05:51 AM
    Ok me and my group have what we feel is a decent understanding of the rather confusing cosmology in werewolf. One thing we still can't figure out is how dire, rabid, and silver are still alive. From all the lore in the books we have read we see that dire does nothing of value, and as far as we can tell silver is still burning alive. So that leaves rabid the weakest and most cowardly as the only one actively doing anything. Since most of the forsaken hate rabid wouldn't it be easy for winter to just round everyone up the first time he interfered with the forsaken and kill him. Wouldn't taking out the pure and letting their followers do their job unmolested be the smart thing to do? Are we missing something or just thinking to much?

          I'd like to be annoying and say the title is misleading.

          But this how I look upon it.

          For the first thing, the Firstborn are not there to do the Urathas jobs for them. The werewolves killed Father Wolf and the werewolves must uphold those duties, not the Firstborn. While the Forsaken have gained the aid of the Firstborn they have not made the Firstborn their servants. Thus the Firstborn will not go about doing the Urathas jobs for them but will aid the Uratha when the Uratha follow their duties. If anyone will kill Rabid Wolf it will be the Uratha who will have to do it.

          Secondly I don't think that the Firstborn per necessity think in terms of maximal output for minimal effort, long term strategic planning and all that. They are more instinct and wolf-like in their actions and reasoning. Thus I do see that Winter Wolf can and probably would kick Rabid Wolf's ass if they get to fight but he would most certainly not kill his young brother when Rabid Wolf surrendered. That's not how I see the Forsaken's Firstborn anyway. And since the Firstborn does not form a common pack anymore they have probably little real incentive to go around picking for fights with random wolves outside of their own packs and territories.

          Thirdly I think that you underestimate the benefit that Dire and Silver Wolf gives to their "children". Last I checked both of them provided Gifts to each of the Pure Tribes for instance as well as feeding the Pure the hatred on which their culture thrives and is built upon.

                                      That'd be the "Ways of the Gods" sidebar in Lore of the Forsaken noting that spirits of godlike potency are not just humans writ large.

                                      The Firstborn are wolves, yes, but they're spirit-wolves, and powerful ones at that — even Gafflings can manage intelligence enough to outwit the average mortal, but the priorities of spirits at the level of Incarnae are complicated immensely by the social-metaphysical rules of engagement in whose context they exist. Dire Wolf might well be able to rip skyscrapers from their foundations and gut battleships with the swipe of a mighty paw, but doing so risks the ire of whatever beings had plans for those things — and that of every other god whose allegiance they could bring to bear — and when gods fight in mortal lands, it is seldom the gods who suffer the greatest losses — which risks greater repercussions from third parties.

                                      So they stick to proxy-wars for dominance, because they're wolves sponsoring werewolves, and they echo back about as much sentiment as they supply natively to those werewolves, because they're spirits.
