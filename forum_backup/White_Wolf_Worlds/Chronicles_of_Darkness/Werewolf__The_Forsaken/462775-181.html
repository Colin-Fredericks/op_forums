                        Originally posted by LukeZS View Post
                        What happens if a "portable locus" is transported into the Hisil.? Will it work as a "gateway" to return from the Hisil to the material world?
                        You don't normally have portable loci, since they require a focal point that's likely to be drastically altered if it's moved around. So ruling on it may depend on whatever you have justifying the locus at your table, but you could use something like the Waystone Tell from the core for guidance. A Wolf-Blooded with Waystone isn't a locus (they are not a font of Essence) but they act like one "for the purposes of spirits and others Reaching across to the opposite side."

                            Originally posted by LukeZS View Post
                            What happens if a "portable locus" is transported into the Hisil.? Will it work as a "gateway" to return from the Hisil to the material world?
                            One way to understand the relationship between a locus and location is that the locus isn't just an object, but the relationship between this object and the place it's in.

                            A silly ST I've played with once made an actual d20 into a Locus. The idea was that the d20 gathered the relevant essence through years of playing. The catch was that this dice was always used and kept in the room where the games happened. The locus is the dice, but the room gives it this context.

                            If this dice was ever taken from there, it would be equivalent to breaking the Locus. It may still have a lot of coalesced essence for a time, but is no locus anymore, maybe if returned to that room within a reasonable time frame.

                                    Even if the physical representation of a locus is technically mobile, it can’t be moved very far without damaging the locus’ power. As a general rule, if the physical object at the heart of a locus is moved, the center of the pale doesn’t move with it. If the object is moved, for instance, three feet to the left, the pale would shift its position slowly over the next few days. If the object is taken out of the pale entirely, the locus loses its power; any unharvested Essence remains until taken, but no more will be generated. The connection has been severed.
                                    This is the normal state of affairs. What if you decide to change that?

                                    In even more extraordinary situations, people can become loci. Those rare few who do end up as spiritual lynchpins connecting the realms are never the ones who enacted the emotion: it’s never the murderer, in other words. No, that person is often the focal point of the emotion — the unrequited love, the intended target for assassination, the political figure earning a new investment of hate every second he remains alive. It’s truly uncommon, and those who do become loci don’t often remain that way for long. They become the targets for possession, or are killed for their presumed power or curse.

                                        Wolf-Blooded can be living, portable loci, it’s not that uncommon. For LukeZS, the answer of what happens depends on the ST and the table. Options I can see:
                                        - The portable locus continues to work as a locus, just from the other side.
                                        - The locus never really ‘crosses over’ as it exists on both sides all the time. You’re just moving its location.
                                        - It stops working as it’s a one way gate and now it’s trying to go the wrong way.
                                        - Something else that the ST comes up with.

                                        My preference is probably #2. The nature of a locus always places it on both sides, making it useful but also vulnerable regardless of which side fo the Gauntlet you’re standing on.
