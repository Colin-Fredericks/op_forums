        Not under their own power. Uratha don't have human souls, so don't have an Oneirios and have no cosmological business being in the Temenos, of which the Primordial Dream is part. They do dream, though, and powers that let you enter dreams work on them.

        When Cahalith have one of their prophetic dreams, the Uratha appears in the Anima Mundi's Dreaming Earth, so wherever the Uratha branch away from humans spiritually it's before that.

        Note that Beasts can drag *anyone* into the Temenos by imposing their Lair, inhuman or not.

        Only beings using Integrity, Wisdom, or Satiety have a Temenos. Only those with Integrity or Wisdom have an Oneiros. Almost everything dreams, and only truly aberrant beings like the Strix wouldn't have an Anima Mundi.
        This pretty much flies in the face of Rites of Spring as far as I'm concerned. No one 'has' a Temenos, that's contrary to the entire notion of what 'The' Temenos is. Claiming that werewolves have no Oneiros denies that they are wolf 'and' man. It denies the duality of the concept. The human bit of them is what help makes the game a horror game as they deal with their own actions and the instincts that come from their spirit half.

        Likewise with vampire, I'd be much more willing to accept that as a Vampire's humanity drops, their Oneiros breaks down in some massive way. Vampires very obviously have souls, this is established. Because they have souls, they have an Oneiros.

                                                That's good to know, because Nofather's point came specifically from a first person perspective statement, and it's been my experience that first person narrators in these books are usually trying to compensate for something. Something akin to vampires being dismissive over how important Humanity is, etc. A werewolf can assert that "I'm not human, was never human." But the reality is that they were wolf-blooded before they changed, not werewolves waiting to happen. If that weren't the case, then there would be ways to know if someone is going to change or not, and it would make more sense for auspice to be determined at birth, the way it is in Apocalypse.

                                                Because of this, and because a werewolf's 'soul' is removed exactly the same way a human's is, I believed the themes to swing more toward Human/Wolf-Spirit rather than just Spirit and a generalized 'flesh'. There are other things like - mating with humans is a Harmony breaking point at 3 or Lower, but mating with wolves apparently isn't, so Spirit and Wolf become conflated in my mind.

                                                            The issue is that The Aether is the Supernal Prime realm.
