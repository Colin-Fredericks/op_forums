    Werewolf in DRYH - my system

    04-20-2014, 08:00 AM
    Hey there!

    I've been asked to post a little something about the system I use for Werewolf: the Forsaken.

    So there!

    The system is heavily inspired by the really neat game Don't Rest Your Head - I used DRYH as a starting point, but I extrapolated from there and added lots of stuff from Werewolf as well as my own ideas. This isn't a straight conversion from Werewolf: the Forsaken to DRYH, it's also a reflection of my own setting and system ideas.



    Key points:

    SIMPLICITY - there are no attributes. No skill lists. Character generation should, in terms of systems and rules, take a couple minutes at most. During gameplay, there is one simple mechanic for dice rolling that governs skill checks, uses of supernatural powers and everything else. It's always the same roll.

    MORE POWER = TAKING RISKS. Werewolves are defined by the trinity of human-mind, wolf-heart and spirit-soul. The core dice mechanics are characterized by the interplay of human, wolf and spirit. At the core, there's your human-mind - this is your safe dice pool. Increasing the effectivity of your character and the chance of reaching her goal means tapping into either wolf-heart or spirit-soul, both of which carry grave risks to your body and mind. Werewolves can be very powerful indeed by invoking Rage or spirit powers, but it's never safe to do so.

    WOLF MAGIC - I've emphasized supernatural powers that correspond to the wolf side of things, while slightly putting the "Shaman" aspect of werewolf as well as the more flashy elemental and technological powers, etc, in the background.

    There are three different types of gifts.

    .) Wolf gifts call on a werewolf's connection to the Wolf Father and the Moon - these are innate powers that every werewolf may learn freely.

    Every werewolf has an affinity to four Wolf gifts that define the basic "wolfy" powers available to all werewolves - Father Wolf (associated with dominance, intimidation, presence and various hunting abilities), Territory (abilities pertaining to the senses of the wolf, scanning your territory, being aware of intruders, marking your territory, etc), Pack (communicating with packmates, tapping into the skills of packmates, sharing resources, etc) and Mother Moon (changing one's shape, inflicting madness and passion on others).

    .) Auspice gifts enable a werewolf to hunt in specific ways as per her auspice. Auspice gifts make werewolves better hunters pertaining to their auspice's way of hunting.

    .) Tribal gifts are where some of the flashy, obviously supernatural effects are (of which there are few). The point of tribal gifts is that they transform environments into horror movie territories - the Iron Masters have their City gifts which they can use to make the urban areas hostile to prey, Storm Lords influence weather and elements, etc. Tribal gifts also influence the selected prey of each tribe.

    Put simply, wolf gifts define your relative wolfiness, auspice gifts allow you to be a better hunter, and tribal gifts allow you to influence location, hunting grounds and the prey itself.

    Characters of any given auspice and tribe have 4 Wolf gifts, 1 Auspice gift and 2 Tribe gifts to pick and choose from.

    MAGIC IS FREEFORM - there are no gift lists. Each werewolf has an affinity with 5 (or 7, if you're a member of a tribe) gifts. Gifts come with a general description of the things they can be used for in the game - it's up to the player to come up with specific effects. This is also connected to the "simplicity" key point as mentioned above.

    Players need a number of coloured 6-sided dice to play werewolves - 3 white (Control), 6 red (Rage) and 6 black (Shadow) at the point of character generation.

      Werewolves are defined as a whole by three traits - Control (white dice), Rage (red dice) and Shadow (black dice). In addition, they have a pool of power points, called Wolf; as well as a point mechanic representing madness and closeness to the Shadow, called Moon.

      Werewolves have talents (skills) and gifts (supernatural abilities). Talents are powered by their Rage, while gifts call on their connection to the Shadow.

      All werewolves have one talent for free - Shapeshifting, the ability to take on various wolf-related forms.



      Character Generation:

      Think up an interesting character!

      Pick an auspice and a tribe

      Your starting Control is 3 (meaning that you'll start with 3 white dice)

      Your starting Rage is 6 (meaning that you can get up to 6 red dice). Pick a Talent (representing a skill, some mundane ability you're good at). You get the talent "Shapeshifting" for free.

      Pick two gifts that are affinity gifts for you. Gain a rating of 6 in one of them, and of 4 in the other (this means you can use up to 6 black dice in the first gift, up to 4 in the other).

      Your starting Wolf points (Wolf is your power pool) are 3, you start with 0 wounds and a Moon of 0.

      That's it!

      Example character sheet:
      Karin the Hunter in Darkness Cahalith
      Control 3
      Rage 6 - Streetwise
      Dream 6, Territory 4
      Wolf/6:
      Wounds 0
      Moon: 0
      XP 0

          Dice pools and rolls:

          When rolling an action, always roll all Control and Rage dice available to the character. If the use of a gift is appropriate, you may add a number of black dice up to your gift rating.

          Prior to rolling, you may choose to call on your Rage and take one additional Rage die. This gives you a better chance of success, but it also brings you a little closer to Death Rage.

          Use 6-sided dice. Each die that comes up with a result of 1, 2 or 3 is a success. The number of successes determines success or failure of the action.

          After having counted the number of successes, check for dominance of a dice pool. The highest dice of each color the player rolled are compared to determine whether Control, Rage or Shadow dominate.

          If Control dominates, you may add 1 to the number of successes OR discard one Rage die OR discard one point of Moon.

          If Rage dominates, the storyteller can either inflict mundane, non-supernatural difficulties on the player, or he'll give the player one additional Rage die (which makes it even more likely that Rage will dominate in the future).

          If Shadow dominates, the storyteller can either inflict supernatural, spiritual difficulties on the player, or he'll give the player one point of Moon.

          The player may choose to spend one point of Wolf to ignore the effects of Rage or Shadow being dominant.

          Discard the black (Shadow) dice after your roll, but keep all white (Control) and red (Rage) dice.

          Should the number of Rage dice in your pool ever increase above your Rage rating (6 for starting level characters), bad things happen. Depending on the situation, the storyteller may choose to declare the werewolf to be inactive for the scene (due to exhaustion) or in Death Rage.

          The point is, your Control (white) dice pool is your 'safe' dice pool, but it's also the smallest pool. You increase in effectivity by taking Rage (red) die, but they also carry the risk of Rage being dominant and of you being forced to take even more Rage dice, until you'll eventually snap.

          You can also increase your dice pool by using gifts - your supernatural powers - but those carry the risk of you gaining Moon points and the associated insanity and madness. Being a werewolf means having to find some sort of balance between the forces of Control (human mind), Rage (wolf heart) and Shadow (spirit soul).

              Talents:

              Talents represent things your character is good at. Talents can be used to further augment your dice pool.
              There are minor and major uses of a talent. Minor uses can be used for free - take one additional success.

              (Example: Karin uses her Streetwise talent to find a street contact who might assist her on a hunt. She rolls her Control dice - 3 - plus her current Rage dice - 2. There are 3 successes. Due to her performing a minor use of the Streetwise talent, she effectively has 4 successes.)

              Take one additional Rage die to your pool when performing a major use of your talent. After rolling, you may add the number of Rage dice in your pool as additional successes.

              (Example: Karin uses her Shapeshifting talent to take on the war shape and fight off a Spider the size of a bus. Seeing that she has decided to perform a major use, she adds a Rage die to her pool. She then rolls her Control - 3 white dice - plus her current Rage rating - 3 dice - for 4 successes. She gets an additional 3 successes (her current Rage dice pool) from her major use, so she's at 7 successes.



              Shapeshifting:

              All werewolves possess the Shapeshifting talent. Shapeshifting works like other talents in that it has minor and major uses. It can be used for combat, perception, feats of physical excellence and intimidation.

              There is no correspondence between the 5 forms of the official game and the minor or major uses of the Shapeshifting talent. You can take on the wolf form and do a minor use (fighting), or you can also take on the wolf form for a major use (perception). In system terms, there is no difference.
              Use only this guideline: during a minor use, the character could, if he so chooses, conceivably be mistaken for a human.
              In a major use, the character looks decidedly inhuman.

              If a character makes use of a skill talent IN ADDITION to the Shapeshifting talent, the minor use counts as 2 successes. Successes scored by red dice count double during a major use.

              (Example - John the Blood Talon has the talent "Brawl". He makes a major use of the Brawl talent and the Shapeshifting talent to attack a Pure werewolf. He rolls 2 successes with his 3 Control dice and 3 successes with his 4 Rage die. His total number of successes is 2 (Control dice) + 3 (Rage dice) + 3 (Rage dice, counted double) + 4 (Shapeshifting) = 12).
              Without the Brawl talent, his successes would have been 2 (Control dice) + 3 (Rage dice) + 4 (Shapeshifting) = 9).

                  Moon points:

                  Moon points represent the character's closeness to all things spiritual, supernatural and insane. Moon points are the price a werewolf pays for invoking his gifts and using too many Shadow dice.

                  For each 4 Moon points a character has, the character must exchange one white (Control) die for a black (Shadow) die. This increases the chance of Shadow being dominant in future rolls, making it even more likely to accumulate Moon points.

                  Moon points allow a storyteller to influence a werewolf in thematic, appropriate ways - providing the player agrees. The storyteller might recommend a certain action to the player - if the player agrees, then he gets to discard a number of Moon points set by the storyteller. These actions always have to be in some way thematically appropriate (think mythology, or movie werewolves) and in some way detrimenal to the character's goals or at least distracting.

                  (Example: Karin has gained 3 Moon points as a result of using her Dream gift abundantly. The storyteller tells her player that she'll discard 2 Moon points if she chooses to growl audibly at any prey animal she sees in the next scene. It's up to Karin's player whether or not to accept that bargain.)

                      Wolf points:

                      Wolf points are a werewolf's "power pool". Werewolves may possess up to their Rage rating (6 for starting characters) in Wolf points. Wolf points can be expended by buying off Rage or Shadow dominances and by healing 1 wound per Wolf point.

                      (Example: Karin currently has 5 Rage points. She makes a roll. Rage dominates. The storyteller tell her to add a Rage die to her pool. This would bring her awfully close to Death Rage, so she spends a Wolf point instead.)

                      Wolf points can be regained in various ways.

                      +1 Wolf point when seeing the moon (maximum of 1 point per day)
                      +all Wolf points when seeing your auspice moon (once per day)
                      +1 Wolf point for every predator-like action against your prey (including humans). Heavy intimidation and starting physical combat always count as predator-like actions. (maximum of 1 point per scene).
                      +1 Wolf point by meditating at a locus. Every locus gives up to 1 point per day. Loci don't have ratings.
                      +all Wolf points by eating long pig or wolf. This also gives a character 4 Moon points.
                      +all points by performing a Totem rite. Totem rites can be performed up to once per month, at a time set by the totem. All members of the pack must be present to perform the rite

                      I've designed the Wolf point rules so as to deemphasize the need for loci to regain energy, because, as Stephen Lea Sheppard once said, touching magic rock is dumb an inappropriate for Werewolf. Werewolves regain energy primarily by being wolves - antagonizing prey.
                      Do remember to accurately portray the consequences of predator-like actions. Intimidation and threats will get you locked up and in all kinds of danger in the real world.

                      The Wolf point systems also emphasize the importance of the auspice moon - if the moon corresponds to your auspice, you'll be more powerful than otherwise. Successful packs align their goals with the phases of the moon.

                          Death Rage:

                          Once the number of Rage (red) dice in your pool increases above your Rage rating (6 for starting characters), the storyteller decides whether you're inactive due to exhaustion or in Death Rage.

                          If the storyteller picks death rage - congratulations! You are considered to be in combat with the nearest possible opponent.

                          Use only your Rage dice when attacking - there's no control during Death Rage. Count your Rage dice as successes - you are considered to perform major uses of the Shapeshifting talent all the times.

                          At the start of each combat round, pick someone you really want to attack. The storyteller picks someone he wants you to attack (which is usually an ally).
                          Roll a 6-sided die. At a result of 1-2, you get to attack your preferred target. At 3-4, you attack the target the storyteller picked for you. At 5-6, you attack the same person you've attacked in the previous round.

                          Stay in Death Rage until the end of the scene or until circumstances (such as actions of your pack members) have managed to calm you down. You may not perform any dramatic actions and roll dice until the end of the scene. Discard Rage dice until you reach the limit as defined by the moon phase (see below).

                              Natural abilities

                              All werewolves may see into the Shadow if they so choose. They also detect creatures in Twilight and the scent of werewolves in their presence freely.

                              Werewolves gain 2 additional successes while tracking prey whose blood they have tasted.



                              Auspices:

                              Auspices give certain advantages and innate powers that cannot be replicated with gifts.

                              Full Moon: no wound penalties
                              Gibbous Moon: beneficial dreams and visions
                              Half Moon: free Talent "Diplomacy"
                              Crescent Moon: one extra point in the gift "Spirit". Also, the Crescent Moon may select one gift he doesn't have an affinity for. He gains two points in that gift, and it's now treated as an affinity gift.
                              New Moon: free Talent "Awareness"



                              Auspice moon:

                              When the moon phase corresponds to a werewolf's auspice, she's at the height of her power. She regains Wolf points upon seeing the moon once per day. She also may, once per day, discard or pick up any number of Rage dice at any time (this can be very useful when you're about to Death Rage, or when you're attacked and really need more Rage to fight effectively).

                              The moon also has a maddening influence on her children - seeing your Auspice moon gives you two permanent Moon points that cannot be discarded as long as the moon corresponds to your auspice. This means that a werewolf will be more powerful, but more prone to insanity and weird, wolf-like actions during his auspice moon.

                              The moon determines a werewolf's minimum amount of Rage dice in his pool.

                              New Moon 0
                              Anything else 1
                              Full Moon 2

                              This means that werewolves will be more effective, yet more prone to Death Rage during full moon.

                                  Gifts

                                  This is where the fun is!

                                  There are three different types of gifts.

                                  .) Wolf gifts call on a werewolf's connection to the Wolf Father and the Moon - these are innate powers that every werewolf may learn freely.
                                  .) Auspice gifts enable a werewolf to hunt in specific ways as per their auspice
                                  .) Tribal gifts transform environments into horror film territories - the Iron Masters have their City gifts which they can use to make the urban areas hostile to prey, Storm Lords influence weather and elements, etc. Tribal gifts also influence the selected prey of each tribe.
                                  Gifts can be used to achieve various supernatural effects corresponding to the thematics of each gift. The dice pool is as usual - a werewolf's Control (white) plus his Rage dice (red) plus a number of Shadow dice (black) he chooses to use, up to his number of points in the gift in question.

                                  (Example: Karin has a rating of 6 in the Dream gift. She can roll up to 6 black dice when using the Dream gift, in addition to her Control (white) and Rage (red) dice)

                                  More black dice means more successes and more power, but also a higher chance of Shadow being dominant, which give a werewolf Moon points and associated madness.

                                  Some supernatural effects might require a werewolf to have a certain minimum rating in a gift. The storyteller has the final decision in the matter.

                                  (Example: with her Territory rating of only 4, the storyteller doesn't allow Karin's player to attempt to find the location of a very well hidden spirit in the territory.)

                                      WOLF GIFTS

                                      Territory: This is an affinity gift for all werewolves. Its thematic purviews are territory, a wolf's senses, perception, marking a territory and emotions as pertaining to location.

                                      Territory can be used for scanning the werewolf's immediate surroundings, finding the quickest way to a certain location in your territory, noticing and locating intruders, finding certain targets, marking your territory, making it difficult for spirits or humans to enter certain locations insider your territory, etc.

                                      No matter how good you are at hiding, you can't hide from a werewolf with mastery in Territory. She'll find you no matter where you are.



                                      Pack: This is an affinity gift for all werewolves. Its thematic purviews are the pack, cooperation and community.

                                      Pack can be used for magically communicating with packmates, using the senses of a packmate, swapping resources - Wolf points, Rage dice, Moon points and health, using the talents of a packmate, swapping places with a packmate no matter the physical distance, etc.

                                      When being hunted by a pack featuring a master of the Pack gift, you're being hunted by creatures of perfect cooperation and synergy. The pack hunts as one, and individual weaknesses are made irrelevant by the strength of other packmembers.



                                      Father Wolf: this is an affinity gift for all werewolves. Its thematic purviews are the wolf, dominance and savagery.

                                      Father Wolf can be used for intimidation, giving orders that must be obeyed, talking to and commanding wolves and dogs, running really quick, projecting fear, intimidating prey with your howl or your growl, etc.

                                      There's no point in fighting a master of the Father Wolf gift. He growls - a growl so deep you can feel it in your entire body - and you show your throat and give up.



                                      Mother Moon: this is an affinity gift for all Forsaken. Its thematic purviews are shapechange, body horror and madness.

                                      Mother Moon can be used for changing a werewolf's shape in various ways - changing only a part of the body, taking on animal forms, taking on other human forms, causing madness and passion in others, forcibly transforming others, causing people to act as wolves do, etc.

                                      You never know what a student of the Mother looks like. Maybe he's the crow, watching you from the gnarled branches of the tree. Maybe he's taken the shape of your ally, standing right next you. Maybe you're going crazy.

                                          AUSPICE GIFTS

                                          Hunt: this is an affinity gift for the New Moon auspice. Its thematic purviews are stealth, misdirection and the hunt.

                                          Hunt can be used for hiding, running silently, confusing someone's senses, playing dead convincingly, changing one's scent, transforming into a Shadow, spotting someone's weaknesses, attacking without making a sound, etc.
                                          Hunt also helps in combat against enemies who have been declared as prey prior to the hunt - successes rolled with Shadow dice count as half successes for the purpose of combat.

                                          You don't ever get to see a master of the Hunt gift, while he'll be here all the time, watching you for your moment of weakness, waiting for you to lower your guard. You'll never see her coming - she'll snap your throat with a single bite.



                                          Spirit: this is an affinity gift for the Crescent Moon auspice. Its thematic purviews are shamanism and powers over spirits.

                                          Spirit can be used to detect and scan spirits, to impede the abilities of spirits, to summon, ban and bind spirits, to lock up spirits inside your body, to step into or out of the Shadow without the use of a Locus, to change spirit.
                                          Hunt also helps in combat against spirits - successes rolled with Shadow dice count as half successes for the purpose of combat.

                                          Invisible eyes keep watching you even when not in the presence of the powerful Ithaeur, but it's even worse when he's around. Reality - all of the nearby spirits - are at his command.



                                          Truth: this is an affinity gift for the Half Moon auspice. Its thematic purviews are communication, honesty and judgement.

                                          Truth can be used to detect lies, sense and understand emotions and sins, read the souls of people and understand what makes them tick, make it more difficult for people to resort to violence, etc.
                                          Truth also helps in combat against those who wronged the Half Moon in some way - successes rolled with Shadow dice count as half successes for the purpose of combat.

                                          You can't hide your secrets from an experienced Elodoth. She already knows all your sins, all of the times you've been weak, all your dirty little tricks. You'd run, but she already knows where you'd run to - she probably already has one of her packmates waiting for you in that exact location.



                                          Dream: this is an affinity gift for the Gibbous Moon auspice. Its thematic purviews are dreams, inspiration and prophecy.

                                          Dream can be used to change, influence and cause feelings and passions in others, to enter and influence the dreams of others, to give hope and inspire allies, to influence the Rage rating of allies, to receive important and valid information simply by way of intuition and prophecy, etc.
                                          Dream also helps in combat when assisting allies - a packmate may roll the character's Dream rating, successes rolled with Shadow dice count as half successes for the purpose of combat.

                                          The Cahalith unites the pack by giving it a common purpose - that of the hunt.



                                          Battle: this is an affinity gift for the Full Moon auspice. Its thematic purviews are combat and strategy.

                                          Battle can be used in physical combat - successes rolled with Shadow dice count as full successes for the purpose of combat. Battle can also be used to see the relative strength of an opponent, to stop the enemy from fleeing a fight, etc.

                                          With the Rahu, at least you know what you get. The Full Moons are nightmarishly powerful in their role as killers.

                                              TRIBAL GIFTS

                                              Prey: this is a collection of five different gifts, one for each tribe. Depending on your tribe, the Prey gift can only be used for hunting certain prey. Blood Talons hunt other werewolves, Bone Shadows hunt spirits, Hunters of Darkness hunt the Hosts, Iron Masters hunt men and Storm Lords hunt Spirit-Ridden and magath. The thematic purviews of Prey are perception and influence of prey.

                                              Prey can be used to detect prey, gauge the strength of prey, find weaknesses, protect against powers (mystical or, in the case of Iron Masters, technological) of prey, confuse prey, stop prey from fleeing, locating, stalking and hunting prey.
                                              Prey also helps in combat when fighting the selected prey of your tribe - successes rolled with Shadow dice count as half successes for the purpose of combat.

                                              The greatest hunters of the Forsaken get to counter all the dirty tricks of their chosen prey. They always know where to find you. You can't flee. Your usual abilities dont work on them. And they know all your weaknesses.



                                              Blood: this is an affinity gift for the Blood Talon tribe. Its thematic purviews are combat and rage.

                                              Blood can be used in physical combat - successes rolled with Shadow dice count as full successes for the purpose of combat. Blood can also be used to change the number of Rage dice a character or an NPC werewolf has, to cause frenzy in other, non-werewolf beings or to take away the anger of NPCs.

                                              Blood Talons aren't just terrifying combatants - they have also mastered rage - their own and that of their enemies.



                                              Shadow: this is an affinity gift for the Bone Shadow tribe. Its thematic purviews are death, history and the past.

                                              Shadow can be used to see and talk to the dead, interact with, influence and control the dead, see the influence of death in people and locations, look into the past of objects, places and people, cause objects to age instantly, bring back the dead for short amounts of time, etc.



                                              Forest: this is an affinity gift for the Hunter in Darkness tribe. Its thematic purviews are nature, animals and the forest.

                                              Forest can be used to summon and influence animals, summon and influence natural elements, posses animals and elements, hide effectively in forests and nature, cause animals, plants and elements to attack prey, etc.



                                              City: this is an affinity gift for the Iron Master tribe. Its thematic purviews are the city, streets and technology.

                                              City can be used to influence traffic - make it harder for prey to flee or make it easier for the pack to get to places. Cause urban chaos. Make it impossible for prey to enter a building. Destroy, influence and possess man-made objects (destroying technology is always easier than creating technology for werewolves). Deactivate computers. Turn off lights. Summon and control urban animals, etc.



                                              Storm: this is an affinity gift for the Storm Lord tribe. Its thematic purviews are storms, weather and endurance.

                                              Storm can be used to influence local weather (it's always easier to cause storms and rain rather than to calm weather), cause storms, change nearby temperature, etc. Storm also makes a character more resilient and tough - add an additional number of Health levels equal to the character's Storm rating divided by three, round up.

                                                  Health, healing wounds and Moon

                                                  Characters have three Wound levels. Getting hurt means switching to the next Wound level (0 -> 1 -> 2 -> 3). Characters with more than 3 wounds are inactive or dead, depending on storyteller decision.

                                                  Characters lose (Wounds minus one) successes of all rolls as wound penalties. Thus, a character who has 2 wounds has one less success on all rolls.
                                                  This rule doesn't apply to the Full Moon auspice - Rahu suffer from no wound penalties.

                                                  Werewolves heal 1 Wound every 30 minutes or instantly (by spending 1 Wolf point).

                                                  Rage dissolves in times of peace and calm - the storyteller may give the character the choice to discard a number of Rage points per scene.

                                                  Moon disappears only through sleep and rest - 2-4 points of Moon per night of sleep, depending on how much rest the werewolf gets.



                                                  Combat

                                                  When attacking someone, the basic dice roll is the usual skill roll (Control plus current rage plus any gifts that the character chooses to use, if appropriate).

                                                  Successes can be spent on either attack or defense. Werewolves must spend at least half of all successes on attack.

                                                  Once both combatants have rolled, compare their attack and defense ratings. Inflict 1 Wound for each increment of 3 successes of attack higher than defense (round up).

                                                  (Example - Karin is jumped by an Azlu. She instantly does a major use of her Shapeshifting talent and fights back. She scores 9 successes. She spends 6 successes on attack and 3 successes on defense.
                                                  The Azlu has 3 attack successes and 5 defense successes. The Azlu doesn't cause any damage (3 attack vs. 3 defense), and Karin causes 2 Wounds (9 attack vs. 5 defense.)

                                                          Experience

                                                          Characters receive
                                                          .) 1 XP for free at the end of each session
                                                          .) 1 XP for achieving significant goals
                                                          .) 1 XP if the character rolls a failure and decides to transform it into a dramatic failure (up to once per session).
                                                          .) 1 XP if the werewolf has been in Death Rage at least once during the session
                                                          .) 1 XP if the werewolf has had 4 or more Moon points at least once during the session

                                                          Buying 1 extra Control dice - 10 XP
                                                          Increasing one's Rage rating - 1 XP for each Talent the character possesses (not counting Shapeshifting or talents gained by the auspice selection)
                                                          Additional Talents - the character's Rage rating in XPs
                                                          Increasing one's rating in an affinity gift - 1 XP for gifts below a rating of 7, 2 XPs for gifts higher than 6.
                                                          Gifts that are not affinity gifts are twice as expensive.

                                                          Example mid-level character
                                                          Snap, Storm Lord Irraka
                                                          Control 4
                                                          Rage 8
                                                          Talents: Awareness, Presence, Being Sneaky
                                                          Gifts: Hunt 7, Storm 6, Territory 4, Pack 4, Prey 3
                                                          35 XP

                                                          Example powerful character
                                                          Obadiah Pickering, Bone Shadow Ithaeur
                                                          Control 5
                                                          Rage 9
                                                          Talents: Occult, Presence, Intimidation
                                                          Shadow 10, Spirit 9, Truth 7 (Affinity), Father Wolf 7, Mother Moon 4, Prey 6
                                                          80 XP
