    Hi everybody!

    I'm the storyteller for a nWoD crossover game, prominently featuring Changing Breeds - which I'm casting in a similar role to what they had in cWoD, as the dispersed, former peers of the Uratha themselves. The chronicle is Mirrors, Monsters, and Misfits. I'll try to post about it in a more proper context later!

    First Tongue is showing up prominently as I build up my reveals. In fact, seeing this thread is why I signed up here. (I had an account at White Wolf long ago. I'm sure it's somewhere safe)

    I'd like to help with this project, as much as I'm certain I'll be getting out of it.




    Here's the general way that I'm handling language in my game:

    Anything in nWoD takes precedence over cWoD if there's an overlap, but as with any language there will be reuses of terms.

    cWoD Common Parlance and Vulgar Argot and Old Form and such things are still used. They would only appear as First Tongue if there's a specific proper noun or import word.

    cWoD "First Tongue" terms replaced by nWoD ones become the general terms as used by the Fera or all Therians ( * ) while nWoD ones are specific to the Uratha. For example, a Nuwisha might speak of his crinos form, which is called manabozho, and compare it to the crinos form of the Garou (who call themselves Uratha) which is called gaurau. The same Nuwisha might speak of a Simba Heart-Ripper, and compare her Ahroun auspice to the Ahroun auspice of the Garou, which they call Rahu.

    Some of this is deliberate. I'm casting the Uratha as having the greatest, most-dominant culture of the Therians, but who have lost quite a lot of knowledge in the process. They'll be discovering that some Fera have ideas and terms of which they aren't aware.

    Some of it is also deliberately mismatched. There just isn't a one-to-one matchup of all the concepts involved, and that's just fine.

    Terms from Changing Breeds are included into First Tongue. If they don't match the Sumerian Grimming, that's fine. Languages do funny things. For example, even though the Uratha would scoff at the notion, our Nuwisha and Simba might ask one about their wolf Nahual, or point out when their Aietha is being displayed.



    Here's the first major reveal I was able to drop, using an incarnation of First Tongue. It was delivered by a spirit ally to a character who was briefly separated from her body and starting a spirit quest. She happened to be near a cenotaph, which she couldn't approach because of the Minjur ban (adopted from cWoD Ratkin as a fun surprise they're still cogitating on). I'm sure that a version of it would be greatly improved by the development in this thread, but presented as-written!


    Annitu kashurra,
    Annitu sibum!
    (behold greatness, behold o witness.)

    Abzu imbaru lapan, Tammabukku matum.
    (primeval mists before, in the land of dragons --
    that being lost to time in the land of dragons)
    Rakbu Minjur, suqammumu Minjur, Rakbu im Autochthon.
    (messenger mice, silent mice, messengers beside god-machine)
    Abzu kimah lapan, la'atzu lapan
    (primevalland of mist, land of spirits)
    Ikkibu mitutu lapan, Minjur-zu. Ikkibu Erset la tari, Minjur-zu.
    (Forbidden from the land land of the dead, you Minjur.
    Forbidden from the land of no return, you Minjur)
    Ilat Kishpu Kashurra.
    (Sorcery Supreme and awesome)
    Cacama, cacama, cacama!
    (amen amen amen)






    ( * ) In the context of my game, all of the breeds are Therians. The survivors of the wolves' dominance are the Fera.

                                            Tommy Shoveljaw and the Heart of Ages

                                            (This is from the perspective I’m presenting in my chronicle. It may vary from yours. Specifically, I’m making a point of using cWoD terms and concepts to round out what’s present in nWoD, and wrapping it all up in a crossover chronicle.)

                                            Tommy Shoveljaw was used to being big. Even among the Uratha of his Pack and Tribe, he had few rivals for size or strength.

                                            Looking up, and up, and up, all he could do was whistle through his teeth at the enormous size of the tree that loomed before him. The trunk was as large around as a building. The top was not at all visible.

                                            He was, perhaps, the smallest thing around.

                                            He was no stranger to visiting the Umbra – the Shadow as some called it. This, however, was the deepest he had ventured into the hisil. The forest seemed to enclose him fully, paths forward and back appearing and vanishing like dappled sunlight filtered through leaves.

                                            Tommy had been told that he would know the Heart of Ages when he found it. He was quite sure this was it. His pack’s last several battles had become tougher and tougher. He knew that he needed more understanding, to deal with the spirits and other night folk. The Heart of Ages was supposed to be the wisest entity around.

                                            Just in front of the trunk of the Heart of Ages stood a totem pole. Seven segments composed the pole, each as high as the span of a forearm, and each covered in carved leering faces and strange glyphs. The segments sometimes rotated creakily.

                                            “Oh great tree, Heart of Ages, teach me wisdom!” Tommy called out. The words sounded lame, even to the big Rahu. Communication was never his strong point.

                                            Two of the segments of the totem pole spun, slowly, the creaking carrying words in a soft spoken baritone. “Sangezin namsu duaf. Uremehir.” (Wisdom begins with attention. (use)First Tongue.)

                                            Tommy sighed. “Come on, tree. I barely got a ‘D’ in Spanish. Dos tacos de chupacabras, por favor. Donde es el telefono?”

                                            The Heart of Ages responded not at all, nor did the totem pole speak. Trees were humorless; who would have guessed?

                                            “Uremehir… uhhh. Uremeh-dug…. Uhh… Uremehir no-dug-oh.” (I don’t speak First Tongue, in broken First Tongue. Maybe if Tommy said it louder.)

                                            Tommy waited for a response. It was a long way here and a long way back, just to be foiled by a language barrier. He spared himself a rare moment of self pity. If only there were some sort of glossary or phrase book, he could use to get by.

                                            The situation seemed hopeless. So much for this show. He bowed from the waist. “Thank you, oh great Heart of Ages. I will learn Uremehir and return.” He turned and took a step the way he had come.

                                            The totem pole creaked. “Zu.” (Learn.) The word was shockingly blunt. Tommy turned in his tracks, staring.

                                            The segments of the pole spun, sometimes one or two, never more than three. They would stop, alternate, change directions. Each time it creaked the words of simple Uremehir, one of the faces would be leering at him, watching him. The spoken words were accompanied by images of light and shadow dancing in the depths of the forest, showing him ideas, objects, and concepts.

                                            It was going to be a long Seeking…. The Heart of Ages was going to start at a beginning, and allow no shortcuts.





                                            While Tommy and the Heart of Ages are having lessons in First Tongue, here are a few terms he might have in his phrasebook.


                                            Night Folk: (Common Parlance) People of the night. Any person who has supernatural power, such as a mage, werewolf, vampire, changeling, etc. Sometimes used as well for those without supernatural power, or with a limited form of power, but who are aware.

                                            Khilerin: (First Tongue) lit. “Night Folk.” Some speakers slur the pronunciation like “Khlern.” There is some debate whether the term was adopted from Uremehir or adopted by Uremehir.

                                            Backstage: (Vulgar Argot) A rough synonym for Night Folk. Some supernaturals couch their discussions in show terms. To be backstage, or to be a backstage person, is to be one of the ones in the know. To have a backstage pass is to be a normal mortal who is intentionally or accidentally involved with the supernatural enough to become aware of some truths. To preserve secrecy, backstage passes should be dealt with quickly, otherwise the band will acquire roadies or a fan club.

                                            Band, the: (Vulgar Argot) To be in the band is to be part of a group of supernaturals. Sometimes called an Act, a Troupe, etc.

                                            Audience, the: (Vulgar Argot) Those at or near a Show, but not Backstage. Most backstage folk are careful not to reveal too much to the audience. Some clever Bands will make use of Roadies to help keep the Audience where they belong and limit the growth of the Fan Club.

                                            Show, the: (Vulgar Argot) A whole scene where supernaturals encounter each other. To get ready for the show is to prepare for the encounter, gearing up for battle, intrigue, or diplomacy.

                                            Roadies: (Vulgar Argot) Normal mortals who have become aware of the supernatural intentionally or accidentally. This usually implies that they stay close to a Band for protection. Roadies may also be good mortal allies.

                                            Fan Club: (Vulgar Argot) Normal mortals who have become aware of the supernatural intentionally or accidentally. This usually implies that they have been able to return, more or less, to their normal lives, still aware of the supernatural.

                                            Ansala: (First Tongue) A performer or performance. Used as a First Tongue equivalent to Backstage or Band. Also used generically to refer to the whole scene of a supernatural encounter, and the people and entities therein, as a First Tongue equivalent to Show. Some spirits who interact with people may pick up this term. They probably would not understand the concept of a show, but would use the term to describe an encounter of some interest.

                                            Ansalag: (First Tongue) lit. “Alpha Performer” All of those directly involved in an Ansala, in the sense of a supernatural encounter. A spirit using the term probably means itself, along with others who can directly perceive or affect it.

                                            Ansalatra: (First Tongue) lit. “Beta Performer” All of those indirectly involved in an Ansala. This may include bystanders, victims, or investigators. A spirit using the term probably means others who cannot perceive or affect it, and may use the term to describe mortals under the effects of it’s Influences.
