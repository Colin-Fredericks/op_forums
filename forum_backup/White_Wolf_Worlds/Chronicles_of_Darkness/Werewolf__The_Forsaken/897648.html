    Midumu: Blade-Claimed

    05-24-2016, 05:43 AM
    I noticed an unoccupied niche in the brain space betwixt spirits, ridden, and fetishes as far as antagonists go for Forsaken 2e. So ... why not, I'll share some brain droppings.

    Midumu translates poetically from first tongue as Vessel of the God Weapon's Will or more literally as Divine Weapon Ridden. It's a misnomer, to be clear: any fetish can potentially create a Midumu, but the most frequent perpetrators and the most famous legends involve those that are fashioned into weapons and armor. The exact mechanism by which this contrivance comes about plagues the minds of fetish-smiths, filling them with doubt and uncertainty about their own creations. Did quenching the metal in oil mixed with the blood of a soldier strengthen the binding or provide a tiny window through which the spirit could reach out? Are spirits of rage more or less likely to result in a Midumu than spirits of wrath or war or violence? Surely they would be able to spot it, surely there's a way to know beforehand?

    The simple answer is...no, there isn't an obvious way to know. There are definitely factors that contribute, but to what degree and how they interact is baffling and generally under the purview of narrative necessary. What is known are that weapon fetishes are the most common offenders, especially those of legendary power.

    The process itself follows the general guideline of creating a Ridden, but with the additional factor of a fetish falling into mortal hands. Those mortals who resonate keenly with the spirit bound within such a fetish become susceptible to its normally dormant influences. They naturally gravitate towards the item. Curiosity overcoming common sense when they discover the strange blade tucked away under the floorboards of an old house, taking it for their own until they can decide what to do with it. Noticing a strange, unassuming brass ring amidst a collection at an antique shop, they decide to see how much it is and despite not having the money on hand for the rest of the week it dwells on their thoughts; it just feels right for some reason.

    They advance to the next stage naturally enough, becoming Open to corruption once they've spent enough time with the item or happened upon a way to quicken essence into it. The latter is the rarer of the two, but it does occur; using the item in a way that resonates strongly with the bound spirit can rouse it from slumber just long enough for the connection to take hold. When this happens they feel a natural inclination to keep it close and guard it jealously, finding any real excuse they can to use it. This is often seen as the last safe stage of intervention, simply removing the fetish from the mortal's possession for an extended period of time will deteriorate the connection.

    If allowed to persist the bond between fetish and mortal becomes firmly established. The spirit can now urge the mortal, planting desires and ambitions within them that further the bond and feed it. Particularly insidious spirits have been known to possess their host through this bond, assuming direct control and tasting the world of flesh despite still being locked away inside a magic box. By this point in the development simply removing the item from the mortal is no longer a guarantee that things will return to normal with the passage of time. The mortal dreams about it, receiving visions that grant them the uncanny ability to find it no matter how hard a pack might try to keep them apart. At this stage the mortal can now utilize the fetish much like a Uratha, but only so long as it is within their possession. The bond does not naturally deteriorate from this point on, resulting in many a dead mortal when a Pack recognizes what's happened and weighs their options. Rumor has it that the bond can be passed on by some means, resulting in a legacy of treasure-hunters and fortune-finders seeking out some prize that haunts their dreams.

    The final stage of the Midumu is the Blade-Claimed. If allowed to fester long enough, the bond between fetish and mortal becomes iron; they are effectively one being and the rules for spirit-claimed apply in addition to the power of the original Fetish as well as often terrifying and unique dread powers. Their physical form mutates to accommodate the essence surging through their form with the only commonality being the emphasis always placed upon the fetish that started it all. The lonely butcher that happened upon the hungry blade can never wash the fresh blood from their clothes and the distinctly porcine features of head and hoof become impossible to not notice, speaking nothing of their eating habits. The Midumu no longer requires the fetish itself on their person to utilize its power, indeed a few particularly clever spirits have been known to hide their's away just in case the local Uratha find them out. Many of their unique powers are contingent upon possession of their mystical prison so they will usually keep it on themselves for that reason if not also just peace of mind.

    The primary benefit of being Midumu over Hithimu is the unique mysticism that forms when the fetish becomes metaphysical middle man. Destruction of their physical form is not enough, the abomination's soul takes refuge within the fetish and weaves ambient essence into body in a manner all too uncomfortably similar to how the Uratha regenerate on a microscopic level. It takes time though, usually enough that a savvy pack can come up with a more long-term solution. Possibly worse, the same thing happens to the fetish itself; rendering it nearly indestructible to conventional methods.

    The key is the spirit's Bane. If the pack discovers what it is it becomes a considerably simpler bit of arithmetic. Simply get a hold of the fetish at the heart of the beast and destroy it with the spirit's Bane, forcing its essence wholly into the Midumu's form - greatly weakened and distinctly more mortal now. Alternatively if the Pack wishes to retain the use of the fetish itself, they can reverse the procedure and destroy the Midumu's physical form utilizing the spirit's bane - doing so abruptly ends its existence and returns it to being 'merely' a powerful fetish now hosting a unique spirit. While no sane Uratha would purposefully unleash such a horror, rumor has it that fetishes put through such a crucible are noticeably more potent than they were originally.

    If the Bane cannot be discovered or otherwise utilized then there are still options available. The Shadowbind rite will render the fetish unable to resurrect the Midumu's physical form, though infuriatingly it does not stop a mortal from receiving visions of its resting place while dreaming. Placing the fetish in a Barren has a similar effect, for obvious reasons this isn't utilized very often - however the mortal's dreams do become obfuscated as if by a thick fog when this route is taken.

    As stated previously any fetish can create a Midumu, but there are certain things that will increase the odds.

    1) Weapon/Armor fetishes.
    The odd typewriter fetish might quicken the bond if it happens into the hands of a skilled author who utilizes it to punch up the first draft of a best selling novel, but that takes time and is infuriatingly reliant on the skill of the host to begin with - if it happens into the hands of an amateur writer it can still provide the inspiration, but by the time it's produced something truly worthy of merit the bond might have already formed simply through proximity. Weapons, though, need only be used a few times; a single gunshot to the head floods the local shadow with a cornucopia of essence and solidifies the bond that much quicker. Legends abound of demon swords and holy armor taken up in revenge or righteous crusade, though magic rings and trinkets do occasionally slip into myth.

    2) Powerful fetishes.
    A rule of thumb is that the higher the rank of the fetish the more likely it is to produce a Midumu. Talens, not actually binding a spirit in any capacity, never produce them. Meager fetishes (1 to 2 dots) are almost unheard of. Modest fetishes (3 dots) are usually pretty safe and often require such a long time for a bond to form with a mortal that they're just not worth worrying over. Potent fetishes (4 dots) are where the local fetish-smith starts to double check their techniques, it's still not common but...just better safe than sorry. Legendary fetishes (5 dots) are worrisome in this regard; the spirits bound within them churn just beneath the surface and yearn mightily for even a sip of essence during their slumber.
    All the better reason to keep them out of mortal hands.

    3) Something went very wrong (or very right).
    Sometimes it is really is the fetish-smith's fault. He used a slightly imperfect formula here or messed up a sigil there, perhaps he should have chosen a better spirit either in choir or more willingly bound. In any case, the spirit mumbles in its sleep and its influence seeps out to any receptive mortals who happen across it. Alternatively, and this is what gives many a fetish craftsman nightmares, is something goes all to right with their creation. The bonds resonate so well that item and spirit become that much more intimately linked and all it takes is a surge of activity to rouse it - a mortal that just happens to resonate with the fetish takes to it that much more easily.
    Though sometimes incidence occurs post-creation that has tragic consequences. A dagger forged with the spirit of a serpent from purest silver is turned against its crafter and plunged into their heart, rousing the spirit within with delicious betrayal. A mortal comes across a flintlock pistol stashed away in a rotting chest in the attic, rusted and decrepit the bonds holding the spirit within are tantalizingly loose.

    Then just for the sake of completion, an example. A cliched example to boot, but one that's easy to wrap the mind around.

    John happened across it in the attic with the rest of grandad's souvenirs. He'd been a radio operator out on the Pacific Theater, they didn't let him take the radio back with him but he got to keep most of his stuff when the war was over and he came home to start a family. Now he was gone and John had the misfortune of rooting around the old family house to see if there was anything worth keeping. There just in the back was a sword of all things, he recognized it immediately from the movies he used to watch with samurai warriors and ninjas as a kid. Grandad did say something about a shrine his unit used as impromptu cover on Okinawa, but he never mentioned a sword.

    It was wrapped nice and tight too. A little fumbling and the wrapping came loose, drawing John's attention in two ways. One, the scrawling text along the inside in familiar handwriting: 'Leave it be.' Two, the blood stain in what must have been the imprint of the palm of a hand. John couldn't leave it there though, his curiosity was piqued and the least he could do was look at the blade before deciding on what to do next - probably call up an antique dealer and see if it was worth anything, maybe donating it to a museum or something if it turned out to be real. Maybe just hang it on a wall somewhere in his house.

    It sprang readily enough from the lacquered sheath. A grin slides across his face in spite of himself, taking in the detail - he was, alas, unaware of the more pertinent details of such a blade but he knew that it certainly looked authentic. As if on cue came a knock at the front door that jolted him from his revelry. He snarls, rising from his makeshift seat and moving to leave the attic when he notices that he's still holding the blade...and there's blood on his hand. Oh, he'd cut himself at some point, right across the palm....strange. He hadn't felt anything.

    John wipes the blood off onto his black dress pants and puts the sword down before leaving.

    ...

    Everyone thought John was just going through his midlife crisis. He'd dug out the old VHS tapes from his childhood and set up a little practice area in the woods out back where he could cut rolled up news papers and water-filled milk jugs to his heart's content. It wasn't until he stopped going to work that people started to really worry. Okay, dredging up favorite movies and bygone childhood fantasies was one thing but this was getting a little worrisome. Plus all the cuts on his arms were starting to add up.

    ...

    Nobody knows where John went. Nobody had seen him for months when the landlord went to investigate the premises. To his surprise, despite water and electricity having been turned off to the place there was still sign of life. Someone was sleeping in the bed and while the stench of rot was strong in the kitchen, there was a pile of opened tin cans laying piled up in a corner. If only he'd noticed the blood amidst the red shag carpet or had bothered to open the closet to see the stray animal carcasses.

    Nobody knows what exactly happened to the landlord. The police found his car outside the premises in fine condition. The doors were unlocked. The back one was completely open. Mutilated animals in the closet. Blood stains on the red shag carpet. A little clearing in the woods out back with more blood stains and signs of violence, but no bodies.

    They called off searches by the end of the month. The case is still open to this day.

    ...

    Gilulishim had finally run up against something it couldn't just cut its way through. A pack of Iminir had heard whispers of a beast of blood and steel and shadow a county over and made it their business to find out just what the hell was going on. One of them had fallen, the pup had never seen a Hithimu in the flesh much less a katana-wielding Midumu in perpetual bloodlust. It was nearly two, but the shaman only lost her hand. They buried what was left of him and howled the tale of the life cut short into the darkness that night.

    Turns out the legends about the things were true. They'd tried just breaking the blade. No dice. They took a blow torch to it. Nothing. They called upon the gifts of shaping. Nothing stuck.

    Research had pulled up some interesting factoids and bits of trivia involving blood-thirsty Japanese blades. Investigating the old house and hounding local spirits revealed a grandparent sent overseas who had gotten the thing apparently after a shrine had been destroyed. Nothing about the spirit itself though. They tried their hand at the usual banes for blood and murder spirits, but nothing quite took. With John's body slowly mending itself they decided on a temporary solution.

    So they gathered up what they could and went back to their territory where they could keep a better eye on it. Somewhere just over the mountain there's a small cave that a large dog can squeeze through in a pinch. Inside is a chamber dead to the rest of the world. In the middle of a circle of bones and ash lays a blood-stained Japanese sword in its lacquer sheath chained to the stone floor.

    The shaman knew what could they needed....but they didn't have the luxury of time to make a quick trip across the Pacific.
