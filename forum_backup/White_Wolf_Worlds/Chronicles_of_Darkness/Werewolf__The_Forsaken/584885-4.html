                Thoughts On Y-Splats
                My thoughts on the subject, expanding on from what I said before, is that each Y-Splat offers a different narrative/explanation for what the Elementals are and why. I'm also reminded of the Mage Order spoiler where it was offhandedly mentioned that the Vampire Covenants act as a way to tether a Kindred to Humanity in some way, that each of the Y-Splats might do something similar, giving an Elemental an extra drive/ideal that helps to keep them human. Though it doesn't need to be spelled out in the final product, let's just call it a "Calling" for now. To give examples expanding off the ideas I mentioned earlier, complete with (probably stupid) provisionary names;

                •"The Chosen"- Though the details may change from member to member, the core structure of the narrative remains the same. Long ago, [Higher Power(s)] looked down upon the world and was displeased, and so created the Elementals to act as the agents of [his/her/its/their] will. From the kindly landslide that guards and guides the members of a small ethnic community in the name of their god to the maddened vacuum that slaughters anyone who seeks the truths That Man Was Not Meant To Know, the Chosen are the most purpose driven of the Elementals, laser-focused on what they believe needs to be done. Their "Calling" is Duty; a disaster may be powerful, but it lacks the ability to control or direct its power as it should. The Chosen believe that Elementals are divine agents, not divine weapons, and should comport themselves as such.

                •"The Thousand Signs"- This group is odd in the sense that they are defined by their lack of a narrative. Maybe they were created for some purpose, or just because, or maybe they just always existed, but whatever it was, the Signers say, simply doesn't matter. In the end, what happened in the long-lost past doesn't have a whit to do with the price of beans in China. You aren't defined by some ancient narrative, you define yourself! Fire can mean purification or destruction, water can give life or destroy it; in the end, your nature is what you make of it. The Signers study and swap ideas, meanings, and stories, redefining themselves again and again until they find something truly them. Their "Calling" is Understanding; a landslide can't be anything other than a landslide, a tornado can't decide if it harms or heals. To surrender to your nature is to be defined by another, and that is something the Signers simply cannot allow.

                Another idea might include a group of self-proclaimed Demigods(either in the sense of being titanic Pangeans fractured into many mortal vessels or human bloodlines infused with power by the gods of the Shadow) who believe that their purpose is to grow stronger and eventually ascend, becoming world-shaking Spirits still capable of feeling and reasoning with the full range of humanity, something far greater than their progenitors.


                So, how stupid is this idea?
