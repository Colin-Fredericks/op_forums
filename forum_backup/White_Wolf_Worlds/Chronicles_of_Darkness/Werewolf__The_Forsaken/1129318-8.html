                                                    Alright, I have given myself some time to think about this, so lets see what I think Werewolf-Me would be like. This is going to be a long one.

                                                    tl;dr: I would probably be an Elodoth of the Storm Lords or Iron Masters. I would likely be a pack alpha or second, taking on leadership responsibility as it becomes necessary. Assuming I keep my current knowledge before my First Change, I would be obsessed with the Idigam - I would have immense respect and fear for their power, and I would believe that they are a force that must be hunted and destroyed. While my pack and I handle immediate threats, I will save a portion of my resources, contacts, and favours so that when the time comes to hunt an Idigam, I will not go in blind and empty-handed. My hope would be to form a new tribe that is better equipped to hunt them (at the very least with an appropriate gift and Siskur-Dah rite-master bonus). While there may be other Firstborn totems to find, Creator Wolf represents an excellent totem on a thematic level - wielding the power of order and creation against a force of chaos and destruction. She also makes an easy first choice of a potential totem given that there are already stories about her and Destroyer Wolf. I would also have a less hostile stance towards the Pure for two reasons. The first is that I would rather direct them towards fighting Idigam. The second is that I am unlikely to believe much of the oral history of werewolves, and as such I am likely to see the Pure/Forsaken conflict as a conflict over stupidity. That said, I would not join the Pure unless I were Cahalith (which I would take as Luna telling me that she doesn’t want me to join the Forsaken). Of course, I would not trust the Pure - I mean lets be honest, they wouldn't inherently trust me any more than any other Forsaken werewolf, and would kill me just as readily.

                                                    Auspice: Of all supernatural creatures of the Chronicles of Darkness, the werewolf is the only one I feel confident about picking their supernatural ‘birth’ trait for myself (for example, I would not be comfortable picking a vampire clan because while I would like to be Mekhet in that instance, I don’t get to chose who embraces me). The Auspice still isn’t really something that I get to pick for myself, but it is usually based on the werewolf’s personality so I actually feel like I could answer this with a reasonable chance of being correct.

                                                    I think that I would be an Elodoth. I am pretty good at mediating conflicts, and I am usually very good at seeing every side of a conflict. Also, unlike quite a few of the people who have thus far answered, I lack a belief in the spiritual and supernatural. I am a university student who studies biophysics - I am quite scientifically driven and tend to have a more materialistic mindset that suits the resources and abilities of an Elodoth. When I say materialistic mindset, I mean that I focus on the material world, and I focus on that which can be understood through the scientific method of conducting experiments and seeing how nature responds to my prods. I also find the metaphor of the web of connections to be quite apt: "He never misses an opportunity to study and master the web of connections that binds everything in his territory, because he never knows which string in that web he might have to pull — or break. This, unfortunately, has the disadvantage of making the Elodoth feel personally unconnected to everyone and everything, with the possible exception of his pack.” I also identify with the Elodoth mindset of considering every set of laws and deciding which is best for the situation - whether it be the Oath of the Moon, human law, spirit law, a mix of the above, or whatever else might be appropriate. It’s all a matter of perspective in that regard.

                                                    That said, I would be okay with any auspice except for Cahalith. As an Ithaeur, I could imagine myself as a sort of spirit scientist, and I would treat the Shadow as a newly discovered aspect of the natural world. I would also be very interested in studying the nature of essence - is it a physical thing with properties that can be measured? Can such an instrument be crafted with human technology if one only knows where to look? If I were an Ithaeur, I would be interested in finding out. That said, most Ithaeur are highly spiritual before their first change, and I simply do not fit that bill. I would be okay with Rahu - I would prefer to act as a strategist and fight with ranged weapons. The problem is that most Rahu are confrontational before the first change, and I prefer to avoid confrontation or to be a mediator to other people’s confrontation. I would be okay with Irraka as a very close second, as I am quiet and difficult to notice. I would also consider myself to be asocial (although not antisocial). That said, I don’t really have the Irraka mindset of always looking for and prodding for weakness - maybe I would develop it if I were to become an Irraka werewolf.

                                                    I don’t think I suit Cahalith very well. While I like telling stories and creating well enough, I am too quiet to be Cahalith. I think I could manage the lore keeper aspect of being a Cahalith, but that is only half their role. The inspiration and glory aspect of being Cahalith doesn’t suit me. Being the heart and soul of a pack doesn’t suit me. I know the Cahalith who is jealous of the Rahu is the typical stereotype, but I would be jealous of every other auspice.

                                                    Tribe: I see myself most likely as a member of the Storm Lords or the Iron Masters, although it also depends on the tribal allegiance of other werewolves in my area, and if I can pass their rites of initiation. I find it quite possible that I may just stay as a Ghost Wolf while doing my best to maintain good relations with the Forsaken (and the Pure if at all possible, but then again I doubt it). Assuming I get to keep my knowledge of the World of Darkness when I become a werewolf, the realization that the Idigam exist would be terrifying to me, and I would want cultivate as many allies as possible for the coming storm.

                                                    Regarding Storm Lords, I am the sort of person to take on responsibilities that I believe need to be claimed. Last year I involved myself in my school’s politics, and I did it because no one else would take on the role. I don’t think I would be the sort of stereotypical Storm Lord macho alpha. I would rather be second to a competent leader than to be the leader. That said, I think being a Storm Lord is in part about taking the responsibility of leadership when it is necessary, that they need to be strong for their friends and their pack. The whole point of “Allow no one to witness or tend to your weakness” is about being strong when it is easier to be weak - when your pack sees that you are strong, they will be strong too. I think I can inspire in that sense - leading through action, through taking responsibility and empty positions of leadership that no one else will take.

                                                    Others have mentioned that they don’t care for the whole “sins of the father are levied onto the son” garbage that Luna and the Forsaken dump on each other. I don’t care for it either. I probably wouldn’t even believe in the whole Pangaea myth. I mean the reason that I should care about keeping the balance between Flesh and Shadow is because a bunch of wolf spirits killed a wolf god some time around 5000BCE? Who cares? That's 7000 years ago! Get over it! The fact that spirits seem to agree with this story would have given me pause in the past, but Dark Eras makes it pretty clear that the oral history of the werewolves is pretty flawed, and spirits are hostile enough to werewolves (certainly Forsaken) that I shouldn't take them at their word under most circumstances.

                                                    Instead, I would feel it to be my responsibility to maintain the balance that the Forsaken strive to maintain in order to aid and protect humanity from the Shadow, leading to Iron Masters.

                                                    Regarding Iron Masters, I think I would make a good fit there because of my more scientific and materialistic mindset, as well as my fondness for humanity. Humans in general run the gambit from awful, stupid, and lazy to wonderful, clever, and ambitious, and as a former human I would feel that the powers and abilities I gained should be used to help humanity (which would in turn help myself - for as humanity advances, the Iron Masters adapt with them). I would probably still consider myself to be human, even though werewolves are not considered to be humans. I don’t imagine it would take me too long to get used to hunting humans and human institutions that obstruct humanity either in Flesh or Shadow. Sometimes humanity has to be protected from itself, after all.

                                                    I think it quite possible that I would first join the Iron Masters, and then later join the Storm Lords as an Evocatus. The Tribes of the Moon book present that Storm Lords take in veterans of other tribes, and in fact recreate the prospective werewolf’s initiation rite as a sign of respect to the werewolf’s original tribe (although they also make these recreations much more challenging than before). I’ve also read that Red Wolf doesn’t doesn’t take it too hard when a werewolf leaves the Iron Masters. If I were to take this route, I would essentially act as a member of both tribes, taking advantage of the skills I learned as an Iron Master and doing my best to maintain good relations with them. Later I will bring up the Lodge of Scrolls. I would like to maintain good relations with this lodge - even if I do not join them.

                                                    I could see myself working closely with both of these tribes, but I would do my best to maintain contacts with the other Forsaken Tribes. Each Forsaken Tribe has its use in the hunt. Bone Shadows have a lot of spirit lore, and if I could have access to even a fraction of it I would be quite happy. I would want to learn how to fight other werewolves from the Blood Talons if I could - Empty Wolves are quite scary. There is also a lodge in the Hunters of Darkness called the Lodge of Harmony whose goal of keeping the Idigam locked away coincide with my goal of destroying them or otherwise removing them as a threat. As such, I would be interested in maintaining contacts with the Hunters in Darkness - particularly the Lodge of Harmony.

                                                    I think there is a possibility I would remain as a Ghost Wolf. I would be interested in finding a new Firstborn totem to help me hunt the Idigam. The Creator Wolf fascinated me when I first heard of her, but if she is Rabid Wolf (or otherwise already taken) then I suppose I’d just have to find another Firstborn or something of relatively equal power.

                                                    If Luna deigned to curse me with the Cahalith auspice, I would take it that she wants me to join the Pure Tribes and that she doesn’t want me to be Forsaken. In such a case, the only option for me is Ivory Claws. I am not enough of a fanatic to join the Fire-Touched, and loosing access to the craft of humans is enough that Predator Kings gets an instant pass. To be fair, I don’t think I fit the Ivory Claws all that well aside from my access to resources.

                                                    That said, I have read Korogra's Homebrew Pure Tribe - the Fang-Shapers. Even over the official 3 Pure Tribes, this is the one that I would be most likely to join ( I mean this as a complement; I like your homebrew tribe). I feel like Creator Wolf would have made an excellent opponent to the Idigam thematically, I do think that the servants of the God-Machine was a clever choice for the tribe’s sacred prey, so good on you there. I also think you have it right that Creator Wolf would not be bound to a Forsaken Tribe. It was after the death of Urfarah that Destroyer Wolf and Creator Wolf were separated and Creator Wolf was tormented by spirits of madness.

                                                    Lodges: I like the Lodge of Scrolls, although I would definitely have to get used to writing down everything I see and hear in order to make an effective scroll-keeper. If I didn’t join this lodge, I would still hold respect for it and do my best to support their agenda, especially since I probably wouldn’t put too much stock in the oral history of the Uratha. I say it’s about time that werewolves start writing their history and knowledge down and keeping track of it.

                                                    I also like the Lodge of Salvation to a certain degree. Practically speaking, I think the Pure are more useful as meat-shields against the Idigam than as enemies of the Forsaken. Aside from that, my lack of belief or concern for most of werewolf mythology and oral history would lead me to believe that there is really no good reason for the Forsaken and Pure to be at each other’s throats. I don’t know that I would make an effective member of this lodge, but I would support their actions, and I would try to find a rite that would restore the Pure's auspice, or allow them to convert their Pure renown into Forsaken renown (bad enough that they can't keep their renown). The problem is that I think that efforts I make to convert a member of the Pure would only make things worse. That said, it would be harder for Pure captives - or Pure werewolves in general - to insult me and get under my skin.

                                                    If I knew enough about the Lodge of Harmony, I would be a staunch supporter of their efforts to cage the Idigam that they are aware of.

                                                    I think the Lodge of Crows would appeal to me if I were ever able to find out that it exists. I wouldn’t know if I were being considered as a prospect, but cunning is one of the types of renown that I would favour, and glory is the one renown that I would be happy enough to avoid, so who knows? Of the Three Pillars of the Storm, I think this the one I am most suited to. That said, it is not for me to judge my suitability to join this lodge. I'll just have to do my thing and see what happens.

                                                    Blood and Bone: With regards to Blood, of the available default choices I think it would most likely be Fox. I’m not necessarily comfortable with the idea that I would abandon my pack, but I think it’s fair enough to say that I would rather be cautious and clever than brutal and in-your-face. Perhaps I would be a hybrid of Fox and The Monster? It’s less about running from a fight than it is taking a more advantageous position. Let the Rahu be the Destroyer - I say. I’ll use my connections and resources. I’ll use cleverness and cunning to flank the foe and cut it off from its resources and allies. I’ll be the tactician rather than the brute, and if I have to fight I will fight dirty. I will harass, target weak spots, fight unfairly, and I will use my gifts and my ability to push werewolves in and out of death rage to what I think would be the best effect.

                                                    As for Bone, I think that of the available choices, I would most likely be a Wallflower. It would give me great satisfaction to bring plans into motion from the shadows, to have a solution to problems before my pack brings the problem up. I don’t very much enjoy the spotlight, but I will take it if it is necessary to do so. I would take the position of pack alpha if I felt no one else was competent enough to do so, but I’d rather be the second. I suppose in that sense, Guru would make sense as well, given its focus on practical advice as opposed to spiritual advice. Perhaps a mix of the two, but it’s hard to know for certain what my Blood and Bone would be - after all, who knows what confidence I would gain with the brute strength of a werewolf, but these are my best guesses based on what I am like now.

                                                    Young Wolf: This depends on how much of my previous Chronicles of Darkness and Werewolf the Forsaken knowledge I retain after my First Change. Ultimately it would be necessary for me to join a pack. My goal within the pack would be to rise in the hierarchy either to alpha or second. I would of course manage the Elodoth responsibilities of the pack, but there are aspects of the werewolf condition that fascinate me enough that I would help the Ithaeur with rites and rituals. With regards to renown, I would focus on Honor, Cunning, and Wisdom, and I would leave Glory for the Rahu and Cahalith.

                                                    While rites are the domain of the pack Ithaeur, I may still hold some interest in learning rites and developing at least one of my own. For example, silver serves as a powerful bane against werewolves, and forms terrible wounds that burn and that heal slowly. 1e had the Rite of Healing for the purposes of healing aggravated wounds, but 2e does not. I would be interested in coming up with such a rite, or improving upon an already existing version.

                                                    If I make it through the First Change with my knowledge of the Idigam in tact, I would be obsessed with them. My spiritual touchstone would be the Hunt against the Idigam. I would consider the Idigam to be my own personal sacred prey, and even though they are essentially gods that I could never hope to defeat by myself, I would still focus on learning their weaknesses, finding ways to distinguish them from other prey, spinning a web of contacts and allies that could potentially be of help, and doing whatever else I could to arm myself against these beings. This is where my Wallflower Bone would come into play. While the pack focuses on immediate affairs (and I would of course contribute to dealing with these affairs), I would always keep the Idigam in mind. Here and there I would save some resources for the Idigam fund. I’ll save favours for fighting the Idigam, calling in allies to help me fight and contacts to arm me with knowledge. If I don’t know about Idigam beforehand, I’ll start preparing once I learn about them and once I come to respect and fear their power.

                                                    I would fight the Pure if they got in my way, but I would prefer not to. As I said before, mainly due to my opinions on the oral history of the werewolves, but also because the Pure are useful against the Idigam. Even with this reduced hostility, I would be extra wary of Predatory Kings for hopefully obvious reasons… and I may have to be extra cautious with Ivory Claws, since pretty much all Forsaken count as their sacred prey just by being Forsaken… and then again, who knows when the Fire-Touched will decide that they’re done trying to convert me to the Pure and ready to just try to kill me? Can’t be too careful I suppose. If only they didn’t take their oral history so seriously.

                                                    In another respect, if I had to deal with a Pure individual who really got on my nerves, It would bring me a great feeling of satisfaction to learn what their auspice was/would have been before they were inducted into the Pure. I would call the Pure individual by that auspice instead of their name - and I would also make jabs at them regarding their auspice. “Not much of a spiritual guy, huh Ithaeur? No wonder you decided to join the Pure!". I would enjoy reminding them that all werewolves change in the sight of the moon, and even if they were never touched by "the Whore-Moon’s taint or whatever other cute name you guys like to call the moon”, they would have received an auspice without Pure intervention. Obviously this would be more effective on those Pure who had to have their auspice removed. It wouldn’t really be a worthwhile tactic for practical purposes, but it would get under their skin, and I would enjoy getting under the skin of an annoying Pure werewolf.

                                                    Elder Wolf: Let’s assume that I manage to survive long enough to reach this point for the purpose of discussion. I mean this is all hypothetical anyway. I mean it is certainly possible that my less than absolute hostile attitude towards the Pure and my lackadaisical attitude towards the oral history of the werewolves will make it harder for me to make allies. Then again, which is the shadowed half?

                                                    While I would certainly be happy to give young cubs advice on whatever they are doing, chances are I will still remain active in my territory. I mean for one, werewolves have primal urge that pushes them to hunt, but aside from that - if I see something that needs to be done, I would rather do it myself rather than send someone else to do it. While I like the idea of being a mentor, I also think I wouldn’t make a good mentor if I’m always busy with my own work, but such is the way of things. There is always something that needs to be done, and I don’t trust anyone more than myself (and my pack at this point) to accomplish whatever task needs completing.

                                                    Chances are I would be a member of the Lodge of Crows at this point… not that anyone would know. By this point as well, I will have a massive web of contacts, allies, and resources - including those murder and madness spirits that you planned to send to kill my family and friends. Even so, I wouldn't fuck with you so long as you don’t fuck with me (quid pro quo and all that). So long as it can be helped, I could make better use out of living Pure than dead Pure. They are an excellent warning system against the Idigam. If a bunch of Pure die and none of the Forsaken know why, it’s probably because some Idigam is on the loose. While a Forsaken werewolf doesn’t make it this far by trusting the Pure, they still have their place in the world.

                                                    If things go well for me, I would like it if I could find another Firstborn and bind it to a new tribe that takes the Idigam as their sacred prey. As I said, if Creator Wolf is available for this purpose, I feel like she would be great for thematic reasons. By available I mean not bound to another tribe - possible to find - possible to aid and bind to a tribe at some point, even if it would prove to be my greatest challenge. If Creator Wolf is not available, I’ll find another Firstborn, or I’ll find a Secondborn or something of reasonably comparable power.

                                                    Of course forming a tribe is only the first step. After that, the hunt goes on. I don’t know how many Idigam exist, so I’ll just have to keep on hunting them until I die. It won’t be easy, but hopefully by this point I could hope to challenge one - myself and my web of allies.
