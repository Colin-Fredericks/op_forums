        The exact position that various supernaturals fall under the Tribal interests very much depends on the local expressions of the Tribes.

        For example, take vampires:
        - In one region, the Bone Shadows may consider vampires their prey. The Shadows might normally concern themselves with spirits, but they are also the traditional masters of the Gift of Death, and thus may consider the undead as falling within their portfolio. They might also argue that vampires are clearly related to spirits in some way - after all, there are these wierd ephemeral owl-things that tend to appear around them, and vampires have some sort of spirit inside them that drives them to bestial behaviour.
        - In another region, the Storm Lords consider vampires their prey. Vampires are clearly some sort of Claimed, after all - possessed corpses that are being puppeted by spirits that have broken the Great Wolf's law and breached the Gauntlet. That they act in a different way to other Claimed just means that they're a special sub-type, perhaps a stable 'breed' that has evolved a particular pattern of survival-behaviour. Whenever a vampire 'makes' another vampire, they actually use the Death Essence created by the killing to forge a new death or hunger spirit and ram it into the 'new' vampire's corpse.
        - In yet another region, the Iron Masters claim vampires as their prey. Whatever vampires may be, they stem from humankind and still act in - and through - humanity to a considerable degree. To hunt a vampire you need to know how to hunt a human - in particular you need to know the human that the vampire was and, to some extent, still is. You can't treat a vampire infestation the same way as you would deal with a Claimed invasion - you need a different toolset, a toolset the Iron Masters have.
        - In one particular region in Eastern Europe, it is the Blood Talons' sacred duty to hunt vampires. Local lore holds that vampires are evil spirits created from the souls of dead werewolves who were evil in life or who died in anguish; in short, werewolves create vampires. Since the Blood Talons are the ones who end werewolves' lives, it is their duty to ensure that the dead are sent off appropriately, preventing their restless spirits from rising again. When a vampire is detected, it's the Blood Talons who know that they have failed, that one of the dead People was not placated appropriately, and they must make good on their duty by dealing with it.

        - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
