    I've ran one game of 1e forsaken (it was a short-lived one at that) and i've never had the chance to play in one. So this isnt exactly the voice of experience talking here.
    When 2e came out with its renewed focus on the hunt i was entranced with the idea of unconventional hunts.
    Instead of learning about, finding and killing gribblies, you could have a hunt for a zoning permit that is menacing the bar that the pack uses as a headquarter for example. I'm honestly not sure how that would run in practice (or even if it would be fun) but its something i've been really curious to try.

    Completed campaign: Scion 2nd Edition. Les Légendes Currently playing: Being a dad for a 3 year old daughter and a 2 years old son and now a beautiful new baby.

                There's also hosts, claimed and idigam. Not counting things from other game lines which are always viable prey, or stranger monsters (in the back of the Predators book there's a few neat ones). Hosts are detailed best in Predators but they go into it elsewhere. Claimed have rules all over the place but are probably the easiest to really make as a monster, as spirits corrupting flesh and getting weird powers are all over the place, even fitting in for giants as they (especially Hive Claimed, those claimed by multiple spirits) tend to grow in size. So you could have a spirit of storms claim a person and get a lightning bolt hurling titan, or a spirit of nature could claim someone and give you a dryad or something like Poison Ivy. It could just be a matter of them being ridden, with a spirit not fully entering a person but hanging onto them, urging them to do things and feeding off of the Essence generated.

                Spirits are obviously a big part of the game, with each pack having a totem and getting their various Gifts from spirits and Renown from lunes (moon spirits). But they don't have to be primary antagonists. Each tribe is built to focus on killing one prey or another, and only the Bone Shadows focus on spirits (though they can hunt other things).

                Also, other than protectorates it doesn't look like WtF has anything equivalent to the mage Consilium. I have all the 1st edition books on pdf, and I have Packs, do any of them go into more high level werewolf politics? Obviously I haven't read much outside of the core book.
                That would mostly be in the protectorate, which can be pretty involved but unique to the experience. If you have access to Dark Eras, there's an interesting one in Forsaken by Rome, as the Iron Masters and Ivory Claws teamed up to essentially make the Roman Empire. But you have more immediate things like the one in Bristol in the core book. The Rage, a first edition book, goes into some detail about them though they call them clutches. And obviously the Pack book.

                                        Originally posted by Jeremysbrain View Post
                                        I'd like to know this too. Frankly I'm not stoked about this whole darwinian nightmare take on spirits and the shadow. It doesn't seem to lend itself to having much diversity or nuance.
                                        I'd argue that even such a dreary approach allows for a staggering diversity. Note that the entire Earth's ecosystem has developed through Darwinian processes, and yet resulted in all kinds of adaptations. Consider this as a starting point:

                                        Ecological relationships

                                        Now imagine Eater-of-Carrion cooperating with Death-through-Roadkill. One has the form of a vulture, and uses its flight and keen eye to find prey. The other has the form of a truck, and slaughters their target with its speed and mass. Afterwards, they eat.

                                        Grasping-Seed, a burdock spirit, travels around by attaching parts of itself to passing Spirits. Like its physical counterpart - it doesn't harm them. Unlike its physical counterpart - it actually warns them of danger, so it can be carried farther.

                                        A canny visitor may insert themselves into a relationship like that, with no loss to themselves. That is because unlike most animals, Spirits can be negotiated with. Moreover, all these Spirits will appreciate having their portfolio strengthened in the Flesh, like through leaving food on the highway, or sowing burdock where it won't be razed. If you convince them your word is good, they will easily allow what's more beneficial to them. Although Spirits have to eat, not all Spirits have to eat you specifically, which gives you room to maneuver. Also remember to know your worth. A Spirit will take you out to dry if it can, but it will settle for a fair exchange if you can assert yourself.

                                                        Originally posted by Jeremysbrain View Post
                                                        Another question I thought of. If human lives create resonance, do ghosts do the same thing. Will the ghost of a grieving lover that haunts a house attract spirits of grief to the house?
                                                        The quick reply is "if that makes for a good story, yes"

                                                        Otherwise the books tend to consider ghosts to be less impactful on the Shadow than the living ones. Humans are "closer" to the Shadow than ghosts when it comes to influence. I tend to look at it this way: to the eyes of spirits, the living are colored while the of the dead are black & white. They might still be able to see them, but it does not have the same effect.

                                                        Now, the impact ghosts have on the living does resonate as usual in the Shadow, so an infamous haunted house might attract spirit of fear, curiosity and death, meaning there's easily room to play with ghost and spiritis interacting with each other.

                                                        Real trouble begins with those spirits who mess (or eat) ghosts or the circumstances that fuse the two together. Usually, that's when some really nasty creatures appear.

                                                            2E Advise: Remember there are Wolf-Blooded in game. They were in 1E, but in 2E they were made wholly into useful subrace, with each member having few Tells - typical werewolves powers like changing into particular Form, talking to spirits, wolves fur instead of wounds or seeing ANY ephemeral being. They are not only cool by this - having some powers even Uratha do not have - they are even USEFUL in packs. More to it - you can ask starting players if they do not want to start playing WB instead of Uratha - to ease oneself before this whole 20+ pages of inner powers for 'proper' werewolves. 😎 There is even First Change scenario in 2E corebook in Storytelling chapter about changing from suddenly WB to full Uratha in very bloody night under the Moon... 😉

                                                            And I made just simple game hack that gives each WB ability to perform their Tribes Sacred Hunt Rite - so WB can send his Uratha brothers on his Tribe's Prey. 😉
