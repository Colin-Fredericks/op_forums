                                I hope it's a bit easier, by probability, for a harmonious wolf to avoid kuruth then it used to be. This is an upgrade from a thematic perspective but even more irritating to try to control for in play.

                                I've never really liked kuruth though. The rage never fits into the story, as written, it just takes away your control of the character and either a) removes your ability to fight tactically versus dangerous opponents (this will at least be somewhat better now that gauru doesn't suck) or b) sends you careening into a murderous tangent. The math has ludicrous connotations, eventually. How do urban packs not inevitably slaughter a few dozen people per fiscal quarter? I'd like to play a dangerous beast, not an alcoholic serial killer.

                                One of my favorite memories of playing wolf was the end of my character's blood talon initiation, where he was trapped in a grapple with his prey. She got stronger the more damage she took, to the point where he couldn't fight through the grapple without willpower (which he was out of) and he was running out of essence to keep healing. His vice was gluttony, so I asked the storyteller if I could use a WP to attack if he started eating the woman instead of just attacking her; they laughed and said yes. So he finished her off, and kept eating her, and failed his degeneration roll as he ran off into the night.

                                It was a great twist for the character I'd never really anticipated, and he always knew that as much as he regretted it, he'd do it all over again if his back was to the wall. That's what I want kuruth to feel like - the choice to give in to a terrible rage inside to do something and achieve an end at grim cost, instead of occasionally losing all control because someone hit you hard or you smell blood. It's kind of interesting for deeply off-balance wolves to have weirder, longer raging, but for the harmonious it should be something more relevant to telling a story.

                                The changes here took rage from being something irritating for a pack to manage to something impossible to manage meaningfully, without making it do anything more interesting or worthwhile.

                                    There's also the point that, as long as one player is still in control, it makes sense to play the scene. Imagine watching packmate after packmate fall to Kuruth while you're still struggling to keep your mind your own... and then they turn on you. That sounds like horror to me. That sounds like savage fury to me. Maybe you can't imagine fury unless you're the one in control of it? I don't know.

                                    The timeskip is a way to handle "no one has any control anymore". If I were STing, I'd probably handle it by no longer describing anything and just asking for rolls. Some of the rolls would decide the next target; some would decide damage dealt; some would pick where you run off to when cooling off; etc. Successes might mean you followed a trail, or it might mean you found shelter instinctively, or it might mean nothing. Or maybe I wouldn't handle it that way; it seems like a lot to track for just re-creating the classical "silent and black screen punctuated by horrible screams and roars and flashes of red claws" scene.
