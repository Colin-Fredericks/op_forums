    If Shapechangers were Chosen instead of Bred

    06-09-2014, 09:52 AM
    So I had this idea regarding the changing breeds and werewolves. It sort of came to me after reading notes from the developers of the Idigam Chronicles. What if each shifter, rather than being tied to an ancient bloodline, were instead chosen by agents of the Incarna? If they passed a trial of sorts, they become full-fledged theiromorphs. Each Incarna's trial is different, and some might even be fatal. Other times they seem almost random in their choosing of new candidates. Hell, perhaps some only choose those related to past chosen, giving reason to valuing bloodline heritage like with the Ivory Claws. Where Luna comes in is that she can add an additional blessing to Father Wolf's children, but only if they swear to her Oath. Otherwise the new Uratha is without her patronage.

    What do you guys think?

                              I started writing a werewolf story- unrelated to anything World of Darkness- where becoming a wolf was more complicated. Not only did you have to be born to a line of werewolves, but you had to be receive the bite (suffer a near death wound from another full fledged werewolf) to actually change, and thus become a werewolf. Not everyone who was born a werewolf could become a werewolf, and it was difficult to tell (for sure) who would become a wolf and who would not. Also, surviving the attack wasn't something a mere human was likely to do without major control on the attacker's part, something the monster wasn't known for.

                              The real pain came from the later generations. Children and grand-children of the wolves were well tracked, but if no none of your great grandfather's siblings became the a wolf, no one really paid attention to you. Add a few more generations, with the various wolf family weak bloods intermingling, staying just strong enough to exist but not enough to make any child a wolf. And then, when the child has that prefect amount of weak blood to push him over the threshold pass being human, but not enough to become a wolf, you get a bastard. Should they survive the bite, they find themselves a little stronger, faster, and more perceptive. And when they shift, their get a tiny bite stronger, a tiny bit faster, their bones grow an inch or so, and so on. Their grow fangs (short one) but their jaw doesn't expand to make them useful (you get a mouthful of sharp teeth; you can't open it further, and you certainly can't close it).

                              The whole story was an attempt to make a 'half-breed' story where the main character doesn't get the best of both worlds, and instead gets the worst. Character one began with the hero suffering from the common cold and dog flu at the same time.

                                  Some one liked my post! Awesome!

                                  This idea was spawned from a discussion about half-breeds. Almost all the time when you see a half-breed in fiction, is has the best of both worlds. A half-vampire would have all the vampire benefits (strength, speed, etc) and none of the weaknesses (walk around in daylight, able to eat juicy hamburgers, etc). All animal hybrids have all the benefits of being an animal (super senses, speed, etc) but none of the weaknesses (urge to pee on things, fleas, etc.). These, to me, weren't heroic characters, but super heroes without capes. Also very boring; there isn't any struggle is you are so much better than both of your halves.
