        "Territorial Instincts primarily plays upon the Predatory Aura; that being said, we're still hashing out the mechanics behind it, and we're wanting to divide it up into 3 aspects much like Blood and Smoke does; a physical one, that's all about making an individual wish to flee and feel like prey, having trouble to actions that don't emphasize that; one that impacts the mental with a break down of the rational mind, going somewhat feral; and another for the social that may play up the whole "animalistic dangerous magnetism" aspect to reflect getting mates, or perhaps to reinforce ones position as the dominant predator. If the latter there may be some tweaks to Dominance Instincts."

        The Predatory Auras aren't in a state to be posted just yet, odd given an Instinct that runs off them is roughly completed, I know. Hopefully they should be set within the next few days; what's been bandied about so far hasn't been set into a hard mechanic we want to test out. Same basic idea though, A power Attribute + Primal Urge and standard costs, backing down or fighting in with failed attempts to fight it causing a bonus to the Werewolf to pursue the actions in line with the aura against the individual who gains the condition. I'm not sure about the 1 month duration; it'll depend on what the overall potency of these are. They'll still affect individuals, not groups. I really want to present the "You're my prey" physical one as a good tactic to isolate others from the "herd".

        The rough idea is similar to b&s until we get something more evocative; Individuals suffer a -2 penalty to composure actions beyond fleeing and defending themselves (for the Physical Prey condition) in general if unable to flee, the werewolf gets +2 in pursuit of hunting their quarry and considers 3 successes exceptional successes in this area (the werewolf specifically, not anyone pursuing them). That's the rough mechanic, but I'd prefer to tweak it more. It may actually have the penalty to -any- action beyond fleeing but I'm not sure that's the way to go. It'd be a sense of overwhelming terror that one is -prey- regardless of how they fight or struggle.

        The Irrational one (Mental) would involve actions that require complex thought and memory; you could see it as a mix of PTSD and ones instincts overriding the rational mind. I'm not so sure how much it will resemble the former (assuming the former is kept) where you suffer a -2 Penalty to most Memory rolls and Mental Skills or something to do with Extended rolls; I want to have something that reflects the mind buckling under a wash of instinct and/or fear that isn't just flight. I've been debating with my Co-ST if this should reflect some sort of Lunatic condition instead, a bit more madness-like.

        The social idea is to present that "werewolves have a dangerous allure between their exceptional health, the latent danger etc." that's been bandied about various books. I'd rather it wasn't a direct port over of Wanton, or it may be worth keeping it as a more direct fear affect; the physical aura more towards pursuit and fighting back, the social more towards fleeing? They all need some more thought and potentially differing mechanics; I'm not a big fan of the social one as a lure; predatory seduction seems more of a vampire thing given their nature than a werewolf, but like I said, all these ideas are very, very rough right now. I'd prefer that all of them in one way or another pushed the sense of being prey more, and in how it's presented alters the reaction via a Condition. This could mean some tweaks to Territorial Instincts as a result.

                                    Primal Menace has a certain ring to it as well. Honestly though, the name of the Predatory Aura (and it's necessity on being differently named) is next to nil; from an out-of-game perspective just calling it Predatory Aura as a mechanic to reflect supernatural predators and what they display with some consistency works well enough to reflect who has it and who doesn't. I doubt werewolves (or vampires) would really put much thought into it and naming conventions when it something that's effectively an instinct-driven reaction/action any more than "Touchstones" would be an in-game reference bandied about.

                                    I'm more concerned with Territorial Instincts having enough 'oomph' using it as a basis for some of the effects and if durations get fiddled around with, how that will impact it as well. It may require quite a bit more fiddling, and the Predatory Aura is going to require a bit of looking at in relation with stacking or interactions with Dominance at the early levels. I could see tweaking them, possibly another instinct to reflect an enhancement of one of the 3 Attributes used to spring board from there but that could be unnecessary. I'm still in the mind that breaking them up into a Prey condition (for the Strength using Aura manifestation), Lunatic (for the Intelligence based one showing the break down of the rational mind) and something like Broken-Willed (already using the terms submissive and cowed; for the Presence based, easy enough to rename conditions though).

                                    If we use the existing examples as fairly intact, you can only get hit with one of these once a month; will Territorial then bypass this restriction when folks start messing with your stuff? Given the Resolutions for the examples it may not need to, but this requires some thought. I'm thinking the Aura needs to be kept pretty straight forwards; I don't think it should affect groups (you're on average running in a pack anyhow, and it's been mentioned Teamwork may handle the pack going after someone anyhow with these at increased intensity). Duration wouldn't have a timer; simply the resolution. Less fuss and muss.

                                    This would mean on average someone wanting to fuck around with a marked subject via that Instinct would be needing to spend a Willpower to avoid backing off and leaving it be if the werewolf's Primal Urge was higher than their supernatural power stat (so mortals in general would -have- to spend Willpower to try). The usual contested roll with the appropriate Attribute + Power Stat in response (and the condition cannot be 'reflected back' upon the Instinct user) and if failing, the condition. The werewolf gets a +2 bonus to dicking the affected individual with exceptional successes on 3 rather than 5. The instinctive terror/irrationality/submission of the affected individual is -2 penalty to select rolls. Using Competitive as a basis one could look at how Willpower could interact; the Presence based aura may be the place for this.

                                    I'm imagining the werewolf will know who messed with their shit if they run into them at the lower levels; if they gain the condition, the werewolf who inflicted it knows it was -them- who did so otherwise it loses some clout. This is being added to the clean draft, and some prelim Aura mechanics will be up soon.

                                            It's not a priority until the mechanics are fleshed out further. When we say "predatory aura" right now, everyone knows what it's intended to refer to conceptually given the actual system isn't set yet. When it's finalized, sure, it can be changed in name but it's just not important enough as is to worry about as a "big thing" until something gels more. It's not likely to be bandied about in-character, and out of character it doesn't need a divergent name yet. Not on my priority list, I'll worry about what name rings the best later on and what my co-ST and players also chime in on and more get bandied about.

                                            As for spirits; they're not excised and thrown out. If the were, there wouldn't be an entire section of the Shadow being worked on, let alone the big things on hunting spirits. They just aren't as integral to everything and being far more take-it-or leave it in varying amounts rather than being written right into every basic power. It's still a major part of the setting and a common antagonist set, but the system and setting idea shifts don't make them an essential aspect to worry about if the ST's/players aren't interested in it. The Shadow and spirits is treated more akin to the Hedge and hobgoblins to use a comparison; it's present, it can overlap on a lot of areas, it can be ridiculously important to an individual chronicle but you don't need to focus on it for how you get your powers, or what's going to be going on in the average game. It's just going to be easier to ignore it entirely if it's not an area one wants to mess with.

                                            You don't -need- Totems to form a "proper" pack in this setup . You don't -need- spirits to gain your main power set (though you can get Numina in addition to these if you decide to). You don't -need- Loci, but if you're playing around with Totems, want consistent spirits to hunt (think watering hole) consistently for essence/numina, odds are you're going to want one. Odds are though when you play around with Rites and Fetishes you're digging into the Shadow and spirits again, but if you're not? Meh. Game wise it's a resource, just not one hard-wired into every damn thing a werewolf does like it was before. That's kind of the point in part; keep it available to use as much as the player/ST want to, but not smothering everything as a default assumption.

                                            That's partially why these Instincts are beefed up some and set to be the "bread and butter" foundation; I want folks who focus on these entirely and don't bother with Rites, Fetishes or Numina (I'm leery of calling them Gifts compared to Totem blessings given the common means of gaining them in this) to be good to go without needing to rely on the rest, or spirits and the shadow. Inbuilt allowances to let folks more readily play the game with or without a focus on the Shadow and Spirits to me is a big benefit, not a drawback.

                                                Hmm, on Instincts themselves and the folks that have been looking them over; b are there any stones that have been left unturned for the "bread and butter" powers?

                                                Keep in mind, there will still be Rites, Fetishes, Totem Blessings, Talens (we're going to tinker with what those are slightly) and spirit Numina (converted when used by the Half-fleshed werewolves) to expand them further, alternate levels (think single Discipline Devotions). With that in mind though, do you folks have any ideas for individual Instincts? Right now the base power set beyond the more spirit-orientated goodies is 7 branches. 10-ish once The Pure get theirs potentially. They don't exactly suffer from lack of diversity, but I'm wondering if a third affinity-for-all Instinct may be worth the while.

                                                We considered a "Hunting Instincts" but so many of the Instincts in general all cover that in various ways that it seemed redundant and too generalized. Pack Instincts were in a similar boat; still room for it mind you, but we've found it was better off kept as a Merit than an Instinct.

                                                That said, what about Pure Instincts? Myself and my co-ST have some ideas there, but I want to get something less brain-stormed before posting them up. I want them to diverge more, perhaps one could say seem less 'natural progressions' rather than refinements from existing Instincts.

                                                Think of it in this manner; we're allowing individual alternate powers much like Promethean Refinements and Single Level Devotions for Instincts. My assumption is that the Pure have either a few alternate levels they hone (or perhaps are taught to focus upon more) that supplant the usual ones. They may even supplant every level 1-5 (in which case, it's effectively a new Instinct) and alter the passive benefit. The prior Gifts they had were one inconsistent mish-mash to say the least, but there were definitely some in there with potential. Without pushing into the line of "Wtf, why isn't that a Numina?" Land and keeping the theme that an Instinct expands upon some core facet of being a werewolf, what ideas do you folks have?

                                                Infectious Rage from the Rage instinct would make some sense among the Fire-Touched; they may have a different angle with much of Rage Instincts that's more about infecting others in other rabid illness ways and thought patterns. A dominance power that makes individuals more pliant via a "fever" is possible; given they're big on conversion I could see "temporary" converts via Dominance and others, and some frightening potential with Shapeshifting 5 ideas "spreading the holy mark of the chosen among the flock". I want to play up for these guys a new-ish angle that they're possible more responsible for the notion of "infectious lycanthropy" than the other tribes as a bit more of a spin.

                                                Purging impurities via an alternate take of Feral Instinct's Predator's endurance would make some for the Ivory Claws, and I could see them having a higher level Dominance effect that's all about purging the influences/control of outside entities on others in there. The empowerment through suffering angle works well within Rage Instincts at well to give them some bite (no pun intended) that Silver Wolf's agony drives them on, and I could see a more reward/painful punishment angle pushed via Dominance Instincts to shift the flavour more.

                                                Predator Kings are a bit tougher; a lot of there things already are covered between the existing Instincts; given their distaste for humankind they might have some higher level Instinct that cuts out the use of technology, devolving others into feral minds more readily via Rage, Feral or Dominance at lower levels? perhaps some funkiness that exploits Hishu form in some way (reducing time one can stay in it, to boost the duration of Gauru or another in some odd way? A power that empowers the werewolf the more they exist in a more predatory/feral mindset vs thinking like a human?).

                                                    I’d be inclined to give each of the Pure Tribes a dedicated Instinct of their own. To the extent that you’re keeping the original mythology in place, there were nine members of the First Pack: five of them conspired to slay Father Wolf (giving rise to the Forsaken Tribes), three retaliated against those five (giving rise to the Pure Tribes), and one is a mystery. If there are separate Instincts favored by each of the Forsaken Tribes, this strongly implies that there ought to be separate Instincts favored by the Pure Tribes: giving them “parallel development paths” for existing Tribes carries the implication that the Pure Tribes are in fact variations of the Forsaken Tribes. That said, I’m perfectly fine with the Pure Instincts being darker aspects of the werewolf condition that self-respecting Forsaken are uncomfortable with: something a bit like Pandoran Transmutations, in that they carry the promise of bringing out the werewolf’s potential in ways that make them monstrously dangerous. As I see the Pure Tribes, each one embodies a different kind of violation of Harmony; and their Instincts ought to be things that likewise violate Harmony, at least when used to their fullest potential. If the Pure manage to maintain high Harmony, it should only be through something like Strix Chronicle’s Banes: without some sort of desensitation against their preferred violations of Harmony, a slide into low Harmony should be all but guaranteed by their chosen lifestyles.

                                                        Even outside of the mythology/legends (then again, they cease to be that if they're factual, hard-wired facets of the game) most embody some archetype of being a werewolf with a bit of thought. The 3 Pure Tribes even at their core, outside of Harmony violations (and honestly, the current Harmony listing is -not- going to be kept as is on our end) do represent extremism of these archetypes pretty easily as well, with or without a supporting monomyth. On the one end fully divergent Instincts works well, but -reinforcing- the myths via the mechanics isn't really something I see as a motivating factor to do so. I want myths and legends to be just that; not a misuse of the term where it's really "And it's true; the Father Wolf story, the betrayal, the 9 Tribes, all of it, and the mechanics reinforce this". I see that as boxing options in, rather than opening up the setting for questions, but on the flipside of that, it's nice to bring to the table a set of powers that the assumed PCs aren't going to be familiar with at all to keep them on their toes. I want the myths to still be there including the original presented; I just don't want one given more validity than another via mechanical emphasis if that makes sense.

                                                        I want there to be questions on how accurate they are and their validity; if say, for instance you get a modified Rage-Instinct for the Fire Touched or Predatory Kings; which chain of Honings is the original? Theirs, or the one commonly taught/understood by Forsaken? How can you tell? Some of that is mechanical baggage due to one being presented first rather than an actual setting element; I'm not going by the notion that the default Instincts are the original or 'natural' progressions, though the Forsaken may certainly see it that way and teach their way is somehow more 'right' from a cultural perspective. Even if they're mechanically the default used, it may well be that it's vice-versa. If it makes sense, I don't want to hamstring something into entirely new Instinct sets if they're not actually -needed- for some sort of nebulous in-game symmetry.

                                                        That said.. the issue right now is there hasn't been enough time to give multiple honings for each Instinct level; it's assumed there are. It may well be that the current 1-5s -aren't- the default progression rather than just an example. If they're opened up more it may become less of a problem where one needs to separate what the Pure go after as distinctly, rather than have an example for each Tribe that really encompasses something central via a honing or three. Right now you get some of that clumsiness because each Tribe only has one distinct affinity Instinct; expanding this to include another Affinity Instinct may help but then it comes down to which second Instinct for which Tribe?

                                                        Perhaps just giving them an entirely separate Instinct is the best bet after all; less fuss and muss; symmetry (even if I personally don't see it necessary, it doesn't hurt if it's there) left intact, reinforcing the idea that the Pure have some stark differences. I think you may be right there, Dataweaver; besides, even if it's a separate list there's still ways to play up that say, a Fire Touched Instinct may well have been derived from Rage or vice versa and so on. Thinking on single-Discipline Devotions and how some may be learnable in two different Disciplines, it may well be the same in this instance; some honings may develop from more than once root, which may work best in the end.

                                                        When you have some that have commonalities here and there, it becomes more muddled on who came first, why, and where hidden truths may actually lie. Given Cahalith and the like, I'd honestly like to see more emphasis on digging into history regarding the Forsaken and Pure alike, and less reliance on the validity of their legends, so as long as there are some 'bridges' between the various Instincts, I think it'll still work to suit my chronicle's needs too. In particular, I want the Pure legends and myths to have less in common with the Forsaken's; perhaps some or a lot of overlap, but I actually want to present them as more culturally alien, including in some instances their origin stories to reinforce the divide and to give both sides a finger to point to say "They're not our people even if they once may have been" than they even do now. Whether they're true or not, I do want easy room for tales to exist painting one Tribe as an offshoot of another and something that could be used as fuel for this should it come up.

                                                        The question comes down to; are full, separate sets actually necessary for the Pure, or do they just favour alternate levels? Does which Tribes have what affinities regarding Instincts need to be expanded to encompass 4 rather than 3 given Feral and Shapeshifting are assumed affinity for everyone? Should the Auspice merit (we're using it as such and integrating Aspects into it) give an affinity to an Instinct (I'm a bit unconvinced with this; Forsaken get a free dot of the Auspice merit at creation anyhow)? Or should we just develop 3 more Instincts, perhaps sprinkle some Honings that can be learned via more than one Instinct and go from there? I'm thinking this last one may well be the best bet along with Dataweaver after some thinking, but other perspectives would be appreciated.
