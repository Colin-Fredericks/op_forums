                The point of the Pure is to be that middle-road antagonist; the three primary werewolf antagonist groups were intended to be a sliding scale of sympathetic villain, which The Pure admits in the storytelling chapter. The intended breakdown was this
                -Rival Forsaken: they agree philosophically on mostly everything; they have different tactics and some tribal jealousies and mistrust, but the main reason packs get into conflict is that they want each others' territory, they step on each others toes when their prey cross borders, wanting their stuff, etc. Werewolves naturally fall to tribalism and hot-headedness, so they clash with their neighbors as a matter of instinct, but at the end of the day, they don't have any deep seated disagreements, and are willing to often work with each other.

                -Balehounds: are absolute monsters. There is no depravity they wan't do; they can't be negotiated with; they have no redeeming philosophy or motivation (sure, there are reasons to become one, which Blasphemies gets into, but they start at severely troubled). They infiltrate packs to destroy from within - any pack - which makes them a universal threat for werewolves. Everyone will put their arguments aside to hunt these guys.

                -The Pure: are a balancing antagonist. The default stance any pack should have for them is fear and hatred; this rarely comes from ideological differences, so much as it does the fact that the Pure outnumber them 3 to 1, and have a reputation for eating their Forsaken enemies alive. Ideology is a good, solid mythical explanation for the root of the conflict - the Bone Shadows are all about spirit lore and the Fire Touched are religious zealots, so they take it seriously at least. More important is the horrific and frequent savagery the Pure (reportedly) bear down on them. Not to mention the Forsaken spend their lives in an endless struggle to keep spirits in check; hearing the Pure serve spirits (and not the festering defilement spirits - just ordinary spirits, who selfishly try to make reinforce their natural influences to the point of imbalance) is a valid point of frustration and contention. Its also a valid recruiting tool (you don't have to pointlessly struggle - you can have badass blessing instead) - and therein is the real point of the Pure.
                They are sympathetic antagonists, and they are the most versatile group of enemies in Forsaken. The Pure actually have a lot going for them which a Forsaken might covet (and not desperate Forsaken, down and out on their sanity - just about all of them): large tightly-knit packs, no infighting between tribes, no endless struggle against the spirit world, no need for self restraint. You can be a BAMF, and your 'religion' says you were in the right - which is nice than constant guilt for patricide and world breaking, even if you don't buy into it overmuch.
                The Pure can run the full spectrum of occasional ally to baby-raping monsters if the storyteller wants. But. When they are allies, they are with extreme tension and open hatred toward the players. When they are absolute monsters, they're more like playground bullies than Lovecraftian psychos - you can understand how they got there, you can understand the satisfaction they get from it, and you can imagine what it would take for you to join them. But their true role is as an enemy which is terrifying and truly in conflict with the players as a matter of course, but which aren't irrationally so - they can be negotiated with, even allied with if its truly handled well, and which present a truly tempting group to join if you let them tell their version of it.

                As a story element, the role the Pure occupy is distinct and bloody amazing. They are my favorite antagonist to use, and I don't feel a need for them to be revised at all (though I certainly won't object to them being given more attention).

                As for the Pure's in-character reasons for hatred and continuing the war, they are every bit as widely varied and complicated as an individual might have. Its not all about an ancient feud, but frankly, their version of the myth gives them good reasons: their totems were the Eldest, the Youngest, and the Weakest of the firstborn, who had the most to lose; they tried to stop Father Wolf's murder, but were too few to succeed - but they were still punished equally as much... and then Mother sided with the murderers; they know the true nature of werewolves - the greatest predators, the value of Faith and Truth in a shamanistic world, and how to be masters over humans and oneself. In the generational war, the Pure have taken the same grievous losses as the Forsaken, and have no reason to break bread with people who slander them constantly and murder them on sight. But when you get down to a personal level - "how does that Predator King pack feel about one Forsaken one" or how does an individual regard another - that can vary from zealous hatred, to no hard feelings at all, to genuinely wanting unity and friendship (in fact, that's a key belief of the Fire Touched).



                TLDR: They are an amazing antagonist. Seriously, read their book, especially the sections on what social pressures they use to enforce the conflict, and every line about how they make up religious bile that makes a good rallying cry, but are really acting for petty craven politics.

                                    [Firstly - thank you, you were right to point out hostility.                                     To your questions, Exploding Frogs:

                                    The Pure, as a rule, do not always outnumber the Forsaken. Their book explains that their history has been a tide of back-and-forth every few decades. One side, in one area, gets a solid advantage (numerically and strategically and organizationally), then whatever problem that inspired this is solved, and they relax and fall to complacency and infighting. The other side takes advantage of that, and they switch places. The presentation assumes that things favor the Pure as a default in order to reinforce the Theme of the world being stacked against the players. However, there are reasons they generally have a numbers advantage: the Pure have exclusive rituals that reveal if a wolf-blooded will undergo the First Change, and to point them toward people currently having a First Change in their territory (which they keep very secret from Forsaken). So they usually get to new werewolves first.


                                    The reasons their tribes are more united (in general, because they do have inter-pack/tribe rivalry on occasion, but much less of it) is because that is a socially reinforced ideal. Their propaganda is that they are losing the conflict, regardless of the current situation - they lie about circumstances, rally around every fallen brother, bemoan the Moon and the Forsaken's spirit allies, and do everything else to keep the perspective in all of their minds that in order to survive they need to be united. Multi-tribal packs, regional alliances, and generous trade between packs are all things the Leadership encourages in order to keep the bond - as is psychological torture to develop dependency in their recruits. Like the Forsaken, usually only a serious regional threat really rallies them to a united front, but unlike the Forsaken, they have widespread practices designed to reinforce and keep the bond after the threat is dealt with.


                                    How they avoid attracting attention varies between Tribes (and is kind of accidental).
                                    The Ivory Claws are very, very internally focused - purity of the tribe and its bloodline, purity of the self and body - with a lot of time-consuming private practices to make them the Purest of the Pure. They are the least populated Pure tribe, and they're the leaders, and they're elitist jerks. So, in all they don't do much that attracts overmuch attention.
                                    One of the driving goals of the Fire Touched is recruitment, so they work hard to present their best image; they make themselves visible to the Forsaken, and make themselves look respectable and admirable in order to get people to join. The side-effect is that other factions only see that image as well, when they see it at all. While the Fire Touched do like to do things like infect people with serious disease and set them on fire as a matter of practicing their faith, they do these things in seclusion where it won't discourage recruits - they only display these things to other Pure, where it makes a good rallying cry.
                                    And the Predator Kings, well, they tend to be rather remote from most perspectives. Their tribal ban is "honor no tool of human craft". While some live in the cities, its very uncommon, and those guys do attract a lot of attention. Most of the tribe keeps to the wilds, except on hunting incursions where they use stealth and overwhelming force to take out their targets (like good wolves do).

                                    It balances out to them not necessarily getting any more noticed than Forsaken as a monster that needs to die. There are things that still make life perilous, but bigger packs, better totem benefits, and stronger spirit allies do become advantageous in the face of that sort of thing. Not always - idigam do wreck their day - but in those cases, no one is really in a good position.


                                    Bear in mind, all that is just the default. There are always good reasons for this to not work out - but wouldn't those reasons make an interesting story, more than an interesting status quo?

                                                Some basic things I think would make WtF a bit more interesting for me:

                                                1. Increased emphasis on "The Pack" over "Garuru"

                                                While I am a huge fan of Werewolf: The Apocalypse, I'm cool with the "War Form" of a Uratha no longer being the absolute avatar of Killfuck Soulshitter that it once was. The Stat increases are fine with me (although I'd be fine with 9- and 8- Again on Strength/Intimidation checks while in non-human and non-wolf forms) as they are and I don't think that they require any alterations. However, I'd love to see the Uratha gain a comprehensive "pack tactics" system in place at least on the level of HtV's Tactic's system. Although, something new would be neat as well, giving Uratha a near (or blatantly) supernatural pack "mind."

                                                2. Changes to "Totem"

                                                I'm not really much of a fan of the Totem Merit as it stands (and has stood since WtA). It's, essentially, a Merit/Exp sink in my eyes. I would, instead, like to see (as was suggested before) the pack's Totem "evolve" out of their shared experiences and via Forsaken Culture and Rituals. Perhaps as the Pack defeats enemies, increases their territory, performs Pack, Tribal, and Forsaken rites and rituals, and adheres to Tribal and Pack Bans, the Totem grows in power (perhaps via some Totemic XP system used only for this purpose), increasing the Pack's status (or Status) in the eyes of their fellow Forsaken (i.e. as a replacement for the current Renown system).

                                                The Pack's unity and sense of interconnection would draw directly from their connection to the Totem, which makes it especially dangerous when the Totem becomes manipulative or corrupt in some manner. The only way for a Pack to combat their Totem, a spirit connected to every Uratha in the Pack, would be for them to sever their ties with it and hunt it down like any other spirit (albeit a dangerous one who knows everything about the Pack). This would hurt the Pack's "renown" (if using the idea above), as their job as a Pack would include keeping their Totem in line.

                                                3. Changes to "Harmony"

                                                First off, I'm not too huge a fan of calling it "Harmony," as it, to me, dulls some of the razor edge from the game itself. Secondly, I don't like that there is a difference between "Harmony" and the Oath of the Moon. If anything, I'd like to see the Oath become the bulk of the Uratha's new "Integry" Stat, with additional inclusions based on "Nation" (Forsaken/Pure/Bale Hound), "Tribe," and, potentially, "Lodge."

                                                Another route they could take is remove "Harmony" from the picture entirely, instituting a series of major and minor Bans and Banes in its wake. As the PCs deal with Spirits and/or make Spirit enemies, they would potentially acquire new Bans and Banes in exchange for favors, "gifts," and/or as punishment by powerful spirits/tribal or local Uratha leaders for indiscretions. This would take the place of the regular "downward spiral" effect of Humanity and Integrity. Of course, these Bans and Banes would not be permanent, but could be "worked off," so to speak.

                                                4. Changes to "Gifts"

                                                I'd like to see the general system for Gifts shift away from the WtA/previous model. I, personally, would like to see Gifts as something learned "in-house" between pack and tribal members, with Uratha going to Spirits for such knowledge only out of desperation or greed. A Spirit World actively hostile towards the PCs should not be absolutely required to gain the powers described within the book. Pacts with Spirits (or, at least, WtF Spirits) for Favors and Gifts should be a faustian bargain, not a transaction in payment for chiminage.

                                                I also think it would be neat if Gifts were less like regular powers and abilities and more like Conditions and Tilts. You learn a gift for, like, 3 or 4 Experiences and then, to use it, there is an "Activation" action that, well, activates the Gift. This levies some sort of effect on either the PC or the "battlefield" in some way that remains in effect until the "deactivation" effect occurs. Of course, Essence could be spent in lieu of the "activation" action.

                                                5. Changes to the Uratha Template

                                                Like I mentioned above, I don't mind that the Uratha were "slimmed down" compared to the Garou, but these alterations should have been coupled with increased mechanical support for Pack Tactics from the outset. Another aspect that I don't mind the inclusion of is the "time bomb" aspect of Garuru form. In terms of "buffing" the Uratha, I would like to see the use of this form gain a few additional things: all non-Aggravated Damage is immediately healed, "Garou" Regeneration, and this form is effectively immortal barring damage caused by Banes (such as Silver). Going into Garuru (assuming the rules requiring constant movement toward a target and limited time frame remain in place) should feel like a game changer. Silver shouldn't just be "handy" when taking down a raging Garuru form werewolf, it should be mandatory. This extreme increase in regenerative ability (but only in this form) is what I consider an offset to the Vampire's new "all weapons are Bashing" alteration.

                                                Additionally, I feel that all Uratha who hit their last Health Box (or beyond) via non-Aggravated damage should get a Stamina + Survival + Primal Urge roll to automatically shapeshift into this form (forgive me if this rule, or something like it, already exists... I don't have a book handy).

                                                As for what I'd like to see in the actual "plotline" of the book... I've got no suggestions. If you folks are doing the same level of superior work as you did with the GMC and BAS, I'll be more than happy.
