        Originally posted by Elfive View Post
        To be fair this is at least partially the book conflating “wolfy” and “spirit” somewhat.
        Yeah; on further review, I got it wrong. The Ivory Claws definitely are the Tribe that needs tools for dealing with high Harmony; the Fire-Touched need means of coping with low Harmony.

        The Predator Kings… break the wolf/spirit conflation mentioned above. They’re anti-human, which according to the established Breaking Points would drive them toward low Harmony; but they’re not driven toward the Shadow the way the Fire-Touched are. Picking up Bans, having difficulty maintaining a single form, and being biased toward travel into but not out of the Shadow all seem inappropriate for them. They emphasize the wolf in much the same way that the Fire-Born emphasize the spirit. So for them to function properly, you basically need to rewrite the effects of Harmony to reflect Man/Wolf much more than (and possibly even instead of) Flesh/Spirit.

                                                There is a reason Primal Form as a Facet, whether as one that enhances an existing form or allows access to a new one, does not exist anymore. Because you can get the same effect with the form modifier merits already in the book. Its presence is unnecessary, as the way things are done in the 2e core lets the Pure actually have much easier (and narratively speaking, also more fitting) access to "primal form", and allows for far more flexibility.

                                                Primal Form wasn't made as a Facet for second edition, because it is already accounted for, better than it used to be.

                                                And if the Pure tribes are going to have unique abilities or new Gifts that they have affinity for, I'd rather the writers not waste a Facet or particular power bringing back the literal Primal Form.
