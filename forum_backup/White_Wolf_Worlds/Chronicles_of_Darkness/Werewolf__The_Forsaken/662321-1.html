    Because wolfbloods aren't shock troops. A ghoul is dependent on their regnant to continue to reap the benefits of their new immortality. A wolfblood turned by a werewolf is free to just walk away and continue living his life and may even resent a werewolf who intentionally tuned him. He is under no obligation to join a pack or the pack of the werewolf that turned him. They have no connection or tie or bond with said werewolf. They are no more likely to be Pure or Forsaken. I can think of certain motivations where it would make sense to mass create wolfbloods, but none of them seem very common or very sensible.

                                Originally posted by kindledRadial View Post
                                The fact that humans can be made wolf-blooded just by seeing a werewolf is a huge deal. It means that if you are a werewolf and you want to mass-produce wolf-bloods then all you have to do is chain them to the radiator and walk around in Dalu until someone botches, at which point they stop screaming at the sight of you. You can just keep doing that. No guarantee they will want to help you afterwards, but throw in some dominate gifts and now you have wolf-blooded shock troops.

                                To draw a parallel, vampires can make as many ghouls as they like. This is a big power balance issue which is mostly solved by the fact that the Requiem is arguably one big game of "murder in the dark" and if you start mass producing shock troops someone is going to notice and hog-tie you to a tanning booth. But werewolves don't have that same level of paranoia or oversight. If making wolf-bloods is this easy then why doesn't everyone do it?

                                Also, this just isn't a thing werewolves do? Like I have an infinite wellspring of love for whoever rewrote the gift list from the original Forsaken because Uratha used to be able to do everything from running faster to excreting fresh ground Columbian dark roast from their skin. You pull that last one out during game and suddenly all pretense that were are playing "werewolves the tabletop" is shattered. That's exactly the feeling I'm getting from being able to turn people wolf-blooded on sight.
                                Because such a massive concentration of fear, desperation and trauma - hungry spirits wouldn't become an issue.

                                Uratha are predators: Morally sound actions are predicated by their necessity or lack of thereof, even as heinous as the scenario you just described, and that resembles some terrorist indoctrination and brain-washing tactic. Predators don't need to extend their prey's suffering beyond what is strictly necessary. The sheer idea of trying to "take Luna's matters into your own hands" would be likely more than disgusting for the five choirs of the Moon. I could see it being an effective tactic that the Pure enforce, but the problem with it was outlined by Charlaquin very aptly:

                                The Uratha don't know that a dramatic failure on a lunacy roll can make a human into a wolf-blooded. They don't even know what a dramatic failure or a lunacy roll is. All they know is that sometimes people who come into contact with werewolves become wolf-blooded, and sometimes they don't. It makes a lot of sense that myths would arise to try to explain a common factor among spontaneous adoptions, like "it only happens if the werewolf bites you" or "it only happens on the full moon" or what have you. The truth is Luna is not so consistent as that.

                                                            Page 159 of Werewolf the Forsaken 2nd ed: "Dramatic failure: ... Suffered when you roll a 1 on a chance die. Alternatively, you can take a Beat in exchange for turning a normal failure into a dramatic failure."

                                                            Page 101 of Werewolf the Forsaken 2nd ed, under Lunacy rules: "On a dramatic failure, she becomes Wolf-Blooded,"

                                                            These are the two relevant facts from the rule book we have available. My original question which I consider unanswered as of yet was "why does dramatically failing a Lunacy roll make you a wolf-blood?"

                                                            It was then suggested that this was due to Luna "randomly" selecting people to make wolf-blooded as per the rules for Adoption on page 296. However, the rules for adoption specifically state that there is no rhyme or reason for this. It happens when and if Luna feels so inclined. This is not the case for Lunacy rules, which state quite clearly that any time you dramatically fail a lunacy check you always become wolf-blooded.

                                                            As these two things are categorically incompatible with each other, then one of the two rules is false. If not, then there must be a separate reason why dramatically failing a lunacy check results in wolf-bloods because this system does not meet the requirements for Adoption, i.e. that they must be completely random and non-repeatable. What is the flaw with my logic here?
