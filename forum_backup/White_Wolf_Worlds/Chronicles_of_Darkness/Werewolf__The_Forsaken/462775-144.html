            Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post
            That's actually why I'm asking, funnily enough. What I don't understand is does it mean unlike most others they prefer a quiet story? Or that they are more logical
            Cahalith are usually big and loud. Hunter in Darkness are usually more quiet and focused. When you have someone who is both, rather than lean one way over the other, they mix both. So they won't come charging at you with war cries, and instead be quiet about it. But when it comes to the actual fight, they mix the Hunters ferocity and the Cahaliths passion, and things can get loud.

            Regarding their stories, it seems like they would pivot more towards a story that really hits home with one listener, rather than hits on surface emotions with a crowd. Sort of like the difference between a slow dance between lovers and a mosh pit at a concert.

            That all said it's just tendencies and shouldn't block off a character concept you want to play.

                        I think Gryphon's Feather had a good point, too. They're territorial to begin with, and likely to dot their territories with warnings. they even have the Lodge of the Hook Hand, with hyper-territorial werewolves that usurp the narrative of urban legends. In one game I heard of there was a member of the Hunters in Darkness who protected a house, a sacred place in their territory, by spreading word that it was haunted, which was backed by the people who went in then disappeared, never to be seen again.

                        A lot of first edition information still stands, as well. From Tribes of the Moon, 'Cahalith: Some Gibbous Moons talk their protégés’ ears off, but this isn’t so often the case with the Hunters in Darkness. Hunter Cahalith try to teach their students to listen, and if everything is an anecdote, sooner or later it all sounds the same. These mentors would rather stay quiet, listen to the world and collect information — until it’s time to howl. Hunter Cahalith usually know the history of their territory down to the dirtiest little secret, and because of that familiarity, they often count an inordinate number of places as “sacred.” A student of such a Hunter can expect to spend some time helping guard such places, often without knowing why the area is so important.
