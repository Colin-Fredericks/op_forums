    Originally posted by CoronaBlue View Post
    a. My werewolf is walking down the street, and approaches a spirit in Twilight (which means that the spirit is in the physical realm, but not manifested?); can he just see it, regardless of form or senses being used? Can he do anything to it without it manifesting or some special ability being activated? Does the spirit instantly know he is a werewolf?
    He can sense it regardless of form, but unless he's got an honorary Rank high enough to qualify as its bane he'll have to resort to Gifts or rites or fetishes to attack it without engaging with the same methods mortals use.

    b. Same werewolf, same street, but this time he approaches a human who is being ridden (urged = influenced from across the Gauntlet, ridden = sort of possessed, but not fully claimed?); does he just know? If not, how does he go about verifying?
    Depending on how large the spirit is, he might be able to outright spot an urging or possessing spirit (urging is like constant low-level possession where the spirit is Fettered to the target and maintains some influence over their host, in contrast to the temporary overt takeover of full Possession; "Ridden" is a werewolf umbrella term for "a spirit is manifesting through this creature") on the periphery of its host body, but otherwise you need to be hunting a target under the blessing of a Storm Lord's Sacred Hunt to be able to spot a spirit controlling somebody from within.

    c. The werewolf approaches a werewolf/vampire/mage; does he just know what they are? Do the latter two know what he is?
    The vampire almost assuredly hasn't got the bit of insurance active that stops them from evincing signs of deadness to heightened senses — no heartbeat is a bit of a dead giveaway, and it normally costs to keep that up. The werewolf is going to need to be fairly close and it'll take wolf senses to be sure, but it's otherwise straightforward. The mage is going to require a Gift to spot unless they're actively magicking it up.

    As for the opposite direction, the mage is going to notice if he activates a Gift or changes shape anywhere near him and will certainly notice something's odd about him if they happen to look at him through the Sight, but nothing about just being a werewolf automatically pings the default supernatural senses of a mage. The vampire has no inherent benefit to spotting his magical nature, but unless he lives a weirdly bloodless life as a hunter between the worlds of spirit and flesh he's probably going to catch the interest of the monster that can smell any traces of his last kill that he missed during cleanup. The werewolf has the same limits as he does going the other way.

    a. Something is causing trouble in my werewolf's territory; how does he find out about it? Without gifts/rites, does he just have to patrol around and ask questions?
    Pretty much.

    b. The thing is an Azlu/Beshilu; how does the werewolf find out? These enemies being what they are, I don't imagine they will do anything overt unless they have no choice.
    Changes in Gauntlet density can be registered by their impact on spirit senses and can readily influence the spiritual ecosystem, particularly given that the archetypes of Host complications are "rats chewing through the otherwise-solid wall between worlds in a city" and "spiderwebs gumming up the interface around a locus."

    Resident Lore-Hound

            In the section covering Influence and Manifestation, the book states that a spirit can cause the Open condition via Strengthen Influence, or an Extended Action. However, I can't find any mention of what roll that action requires, just the number of scenes that it takes. Does the spirit simply have to hang around the source of Resonance for a number of scenes, or have I missed something?
            Influence is Power + Finesse. I believe the extended action is talking about just hanging around and working in concert. Certain rituals would probably do it, I imagine that's what it's calling back to.

            So I take it that, like many things, whether or not the signs of vampirism are overt enough to be noticed in passing is determined by what else is going on in the scene? For example, would wolf senses automatically detect blood on the breath of a vampire who has just fed, assuming the vampire has not taken some precaution and there are no other intense smells in the area?
            Provided they were using their wolf senses, sure. That is something the player would have to note. In human form their senses are top notch but still human. Beyond that, it should depend on things. If your werewolf is walking into a busy club full of dancing people and there's a vampire who just drank on the other side of the room, they wouldn't exactly stick out like a sore thumb just based on the senses. There would probably be the scent of blood from various small cuts and other explanations. But if a werewolf and vampire met each other in an otherwise empty room and neither were distracted by anything else, they would probably notice something was up near instantly, though not necessarily what. Vampires look dead, without the use of Blush of Life. They don't have a heartbeat, they have no pulse, no sweat or other natural body odors. They're pale, they're slightly below room temperature. If the werewolf didn't know about vampires it would be easy to think it was a zombie. Blush of Life, an ability vampires in Requiem have that lets them fake life better, puts them at normal human levels. They gain a heartbeat, pulse, they fake all the bodily fluids and everything else. There's where you would have to rely on other things, and wolf senses are really powerful and could probably notice a vampire close to them who has just fed.

            I forget where in the fluff but Solomon Birch, a fairly powerful vampire, has remarked that a lot of vampires don't brush their teeth. While they wouldn't get gum disease or anything, it would probably lead to some nasty breath after years of putting nothing but blood into it.
