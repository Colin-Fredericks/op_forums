          Welp, you've got a lot of concerns. This is how I tackle them in my own games:

          1) You can do Monster of the Week or progress thru a Big Bad, The Wolf Must Hunt means that Uratha will engage in Sacred Hunts regularly. The Big Bad progression is usually a progression of sacred hunts.

          2) Uratha prefer to be the apex predators in their turfs. It's less about "protecting" is more about dictating how it would develop. A pack which thrives on members of the homeless population will have quite a different outlook and notion of what is good for a territory of white-collar universe graduates who do the corporate conga.

          3) Think of the Shadow as the subconscious of the world. The more symbolic weight something has, the easier it is for it to manifest. A recently opened pops shop will have a negligible impact in the Shadow compared to the two decades old Walmart that completely shifted the economy in the town. The more dramatic something is, the higher the chance for it to manifest spiritually.

          4) Technology spirits aren't the same as transport ones (such as computer spirits). Consider that most spirits are shaken awake from traumatic events, such as having their reflection destroyed (or heavily damaged); most cars you'll see in the Shadow are mere reflections, moving above their physical reflections without any display of sentience. The same is true for that ferry. Yes, there can be traffic jams in the Shadow.

          5) The bigger a spirit is, the harder it is that it'll just manifest into a single form. This is true to mountain spirits, to city spirits, to country spirits or to spirits of whole seas. At any rate they're unimportant, unless something shocks them awake.

          6) A blade of grass will manifest spiritually as long as it makes coherent sense in the place in question. If the place is a park? Sure. If it's was just planted besides a Starbucks? doubt it.

          7) There's 5 tribes, actually, not 4. If you want to know how many Uratha there are in some metropolitan area? Take it's population and the 1% of it will be it. Divide as you might in between Forsaken & Pure. Adjust the figure accordingly to story needs.

Bloody blue toilet fluid gushes out, and the things begin to shiver and grow dark as the motorcycle howls into the distance.

                                                  It feels wrong. Not like a Wound, no - this place had been like this for so long that it was an open, blighted sore upon the Hisil. It was the                                                   lil, the spirit of this place, dying and corrupting everything around it. Certainly there were pockets of Wound here, maybe under your feet, for in the Urshul you felt faint tremors of massive machinery down below, of hollow earth and fiery industry, probably somehow feeding the corpse fires.

                                                  You look around, but besides the chill of the Hisil on this patch of high ground, you see no distinct threats waiting for you.

                                                  The two of you descend the glassy, jagged slope, slipping on the bluish guts and blood of the dying lavatory things on either side, the doors opening proudly on the healthy ones, but haphazardly and sometimes not at all on the wounded or dead structures. Waiting for use. As you passed by, you see the toilet bowls shrivel into wrinkled mouths, growing teeth, suckling and snapping angrily as they are deprived of prey, these strange urban Venus mantraps.

                                                  You are careful not to cut yourselves on the ground, but despite your best efforts, Bastian, your paws are sliced up by errant shards of sharp atomic glass, the scent of his blood sharp in the foul air.

                                                  The two of you continue on through the strange, sprawling maze of crumpled streets and ruined city, the crocodile tooth leading you on a haphazard route that seems to be taking you in circles, but you realize you are only winding down and down the crater. You pass by sputtering neon signs in First Tongue, none of them making any sense, reflections of sales and failed commercialism, most of them leaking glowing blood onto the ground from on high. Shrieking numina snaps overhead like tracer rounds in an invisible spirit war, and more than once you need to duck your head to avoid being hit. When you look for who fired, you only see shadows flitting amongst the ruins.

                                                  You slide down slopes made from old car parts crunched up into a fine mulch, fused hubcaps rattling like mouths for you to save them, begging and pleading toothlessly in the First Tongue. Above, you hear the whine of rotors, and a patchwork blimp floats high overhead. It is made of what looks like air bladders of human flesh and faces with supports of giant bone, the back half clockwork engines and propellers. It is dangling with chains attached to the husks of derelict cars. A giant black screen is embedded across the side, scrolling glowing runes of First Tongue words.

                                                  [THIS NIGHT ONLY]

                                                  [EAT DRINK KILL ALL]

                                                  [FATHER WOLF IS DEAD]

                                                  [LONG LIVE THE HUZUR-KAR]

                                                  A blast of flame from a gutter a few yards to your right illuminates the last of the road ahead, broken off into darkness down below. The two of you stand on a flat ledge above a tunnel, looking down onto a blasted neighborhood that teeters on the edge of a vast chasm.
