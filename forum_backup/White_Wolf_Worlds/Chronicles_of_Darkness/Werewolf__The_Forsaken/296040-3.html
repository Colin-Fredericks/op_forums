        My piece meal review. It is very train-of-thought and quickly written, sorry for that:

        Legacy - Great use of themes such as pack standing, pack interrelation, and hunt leader. Also decent use of the Pure theme.

        First criticism, first page. This is my personal preference, I guess, but I feel that the use of Game Proper Nouns that have no context without knowing that Noun is not great for RPG fiction. I'm a huge Werewolf fan, and even I have to stop and think about context. So on the first page of the story (pg. 4) we have "What's the next move?" his Cahalith packmate asked quietly. Besides identifying another werewolf as being Cahalith, what context does using that word bring? IIRC, that there is a Cahalith has no further bearing on the story. This criticism applies generally to all the stories in the Anthology (see The Wolf Must Hunt for generally great use of Game Proper Nouns, though). Use of Game Proper Nouns without having a contextural reason feels inelegant.

        I like the mostly Iron Master pack having this one Blood Talon. Like I said, it is a good used of themes. It also makes for an interesting and realistic set up as to why the burden is placed on the main character.

        I do want to note that the conversations were pretty good. Especially Archibald. I felt you really understood him. Also good use of Rage during the fight. The tactics with the Wolfblooded using silver was really smart, and I liked how the "killsteal" provoked Rage. Also the alpha assuming control when needed was really good.

        I didn't like the part with the "totems"? on pg. 13. It felt like it took away from the story. We didn't get to, as readers, get to see this "fucking terrifying [thing] to behold". Also, police car what? Did I miss something? Is that Monitor... here I am thinking Monitor was a lizard, which also made no sense. Yes, as a reader until now writing this review I did not understand that Monitor was a police car spirit.

        Overall pretty good start to the Anthology.

        First Hunt, Last Hunt - I liked the beginning. The middle kind of went awry, and the end was okay.

        The beginning was pretty tight. The only criticism is the use of Game Proper Nouns having no good surrounding context. I would have preferred, e.g., "Ronnie was already a monstrous wolf in Urhan" or "I could... outrun a game animal, even with my human legs in Hishu." Give us a reason to care why they shifted beyond just use of the Word.

        The finding herself in the middle part just didn't feel right. It felt like there was too much going on, and nothing really fleshed out. I wish I knew how to say it better. I would rather have had parts of the middle section paraphrased "We found another one, and it took my uncle down." so that one thing could have become really strong... most likely the finding herself part.

        The last part was alright. She became a hero, yay. But, I never understood why this smart kid would go after the End Boss. I'm not saying it's wrong, just I, as a reader, never understood her motivation to do that. Also re the Gift... it doesn't seem right. Can a werewolf self-manifest a Gift? From what I know a spirit has to etch her soul with the Gift, which would be something too notable to simply then never use.

        I do like "superclaimed" and how she would come up with her own worldview on things based on her "dorky" experiences. Overall, I feel if the middle part was more about finding herself and glossed over her uncle's death and final words, this story would have been much tighter. It was pretty good all told.

        Confluence - Apologies in advance, Jim. This was tricky. The shifting focus was almost unnecessary. I didn't feel like it added much, especially with the first two prologue portions... and it kind of smashed together in the final section.

        I felt as a reader in the beginning "why am I reading this? why do I care about these two people?" With Annie (and John) it got interesting, I almost forgot about the other two when reading her comparatively massive prologue section.

        For the next two sections it then felt like a mismatched rollercoaster. Up with the action, down with the shifting focus, and then back up, and then down. It didn't feel smooth or necessary. It was interesting see each through the focus's perspective, but I don't feel it was that interesting to smooth over the rough transitions.

        The final section I didn't like. It felt forced, like the "players" were following the GM's railroad tracks and doing their best to talk to one another along the way. The conversation felt like a bad action movie conversation. Or like a conversation I would have in a PbP where I'm trying to get a lot across in one post of my character.

        The transition from the hunt to being hunted was pretty good, and interesting, but again I felt the conversation just wasn't great. Yeah, it felt like the beginning of a campaign where players are testing each other out, but making it forced because they know they are going to become a party / pack / etc.

        One Vengeance - overall pretty good. Decent use of Game Proper Nouns. I liked how Azlu came after the description of what the Azlu was. However, I don't understand this story... or rather I don't understand how these two stories were linked.

        I, as a reader, assumed they were linked somehow reading along. Then the end showed me that there was no linkage, at least that I picked up.

        Last Rites - one of my favorite stories. Very tight and focused. Great view of the spirit world weirdness and politics. Nice use of idigam's... out-of-context problem. Just altogether a great story!

        Desert Dreaming Hunt - this one of my other favorites. Falls pretty to a shotgun of Game Proper Nouns right at the beginning, but they all seem to be used more or less in some context. I like the themes. I like that no werewolf dies.

        Let me digress for a moment. So many of these stories in the Anthology have werewolves dying. One Vengeance" has what like 8 werewolves dying. Seriously I got the feeling throughout the Anthology, like, how are there any werewolves left. It seems like after half-dozen hunts each pack should be decimated.

        Anyway, I like that in Desert Dreaming Hunt things get close to dying, but they make it. Feels very dramatic and good.

        The Worst Kind of Enemy - another good one. What I liked best was the ambiguity in the territory. This is a werewolf vs. werewolf fight, and not necessarily Forsaken vs. Pure. Good story. Good perspective. I especially like the "worst kind of enemy" thoughts throughout the story.

        Only One Way to Win - felt not focused. This could have been another great one, but it just gets up to "good" one. I think because it was supposed to be full of werewolf pack politics, since that was the whole point of Thornpaw's maneuver, I think, but it kind of felt diluted. The writing and conversations were generally good, but I felt the theme could have been more focused because I kept thinking it was locus vs. Pure. Then at the end it felt like it wrapped up way too quickly.

        The Wolf Must Hunt - pretty good. I felt the introduction to the five was a bit much, but I did appreciate the use of the Game Proper Nouns in context. They were well used, I felt throughout the story.

        This story didn't become as bad as "Confluence", but it did feel game-y in bits. Thankfully, these were more reactive bits than player-proactive meta-gamey bits. I guess I felt that the pack was reaching beyond its power. Like in the Rockies in WtF, 1ed., it took the entire Rockies population to take down the Idigam. Here, they find attacks up and down the coast. Seems like a good time to find more werewolves. Anyway, the story did kind of have that RPG adventure feel.

        Otherwise, this was definitely a good end to the Anthology. Showed again a bit of the spirit side, (I love ithilunim!), a bit of the idigam madness, and some pack relationships.

            Thanks for the review, and if I may take a moment to answer a couple of things regarding Legacy...

            Originally posted by Ravious View Post
            My piece meal review. It is very train-of-thought and quickly written, sorry for that:

            Legacy - Great use of themes such as pack standing, pack interrelation, and hunt leader. Also decent use of the Pure theme.

            First criticism, first page. This is my personal preference, I guess, but I feel that the use of Game Proper Nouns that have no context without knowing that Noun is not great for RPG fiction. I'm a huge Werewolf fan, and even I have to stop and think about context. So on the first page of the story (pg. 4) we have "What's the next move?" his Cahalith packmate asked quietly. Besides identifying another werewolf as being Cahalith, what context does using that word bring? IIRC, that there is a Cahalith has no further bearing on the story. This criticism applies generally to all the stories in the Anthology (see The Wolf Must Hunt for generally great use of Game Proper Nouns, though). Use of Game Proper Nouns without having a contextural reason feels inelegant.
            Yeah, I admit that I probably could have been a little smoother with the use of the term, but I didn't want to leave it ambiguous as to the auspices and tribes of the characters. Even with some contextual clues throughout the story (like the Cahalith-specific visions -- which are a plot point), I didn't want anyone scratching their heads at the end of the story going 'wait, what was that one guy?' I do admit I probably could have found a better way to work it in, but at the time I was establishing the characters so it seemed like a good spot.

            Originally posted by Ravious View Post

            I didn't like the part with the "totems"? on pg. 13. It felt like it took away from the story. We didn't get to, as readers, get to see this "fucking terrifying [thing] to behold". Also, police car what? Did I miss something? Is that Monitor... here I am thinking Monitor was a lizard, which also made no sense. Yes, as a reader until now writing this review I did not understand that Monitor was a police car spirit.
            There are a lot of details about both the Forsaken and Pure packs that were sacrificed on the altar to the Great Spirit of Word Count (for instance, three of the members of the Wrong Alley have deed names that weren't relevant to the story). I'm sure there are angles I could have used to work a clearer description of the Wrong Alley's totem into the story, but none came to me at the time (though naturally they come to me now and I'm kicking myself) and I just didn't have the space to get deeper into that while also covering everything else I wanted to cover. I kinda regret that the pack's totem came across like a bit of an afterthought, but the story's focus on physical matters meant that I had to gloss over some of the more spiritual stuff. I'm not proud of it, but I'll own up to having fumbled that particular bit. I'll make a note to do better about that next time.

            For those confused by that part in the story or curious for some of the lost details, the Wrong Alley's totem is called 'The Monitor' like in the sense of 'watcher' or 'guardian.' Its proper spirit name translates differently, but 'monitor' is one of those words you can use in front of humans (because when your territory's in a packed urban area, private conversations aren't always a guarantee) and not necessarily draw attention, but not so common it could mean a dozen different things. It's a spirit of dominance, order, and restraint and is basically the spirit equivalent of that one kid in class who's decided he needs to chaperone everyone and report every little thing to the teacher. Because it's an urban spirit of all these things, it looks a lot like a police car.

            Maybe when Forsaken 2e's properly out, I'll do a write-up of the pack (including totem) if anyone would be curious to see how that would look on paper.

            Thanks again for the feedback and the review.

            Christopher Shaffer, freelancer. Game line contributions: W20, Night Horrors, TC: Æon, Scion (details here)
            Blog post write-ups of my gaming sessions; Currently running Exalted, and also updating old Promethean sessions
