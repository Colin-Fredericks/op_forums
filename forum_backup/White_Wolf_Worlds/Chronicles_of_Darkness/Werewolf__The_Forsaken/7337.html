    The Problems of Werewolves and Space Opera

    11-10-2013, 10:51 AM
    This is a think-tank thread for bouncing ideas. Please help me.

    When developing a Mirror shard that translated werewolves and other theriomorphs into a galactic setting, it became quickly apparent that certain elements would have to be addressed and/or outright changed.

    On one hand, how do you explain theriomorphs being present on Earth, but impossible for other alien civilizations to know or have? Saying it's only relevant to humans would be jarring at best, considering the supernatural shouldn't be a localized phenomenon restricted to our own solar system. With that in mind, how the hell do you go about developing alien theriomorphs? Would there need to be a game mechanic and formula to keep them relatively balanced? If so, how?

    Another question that's been bothering me is what would happen if theriomorphs decided to create their own society; colonize a world, hang a flag, etc. How would you expand the traditions and cultural customs of the Forsaken (or any shapechanger, really) to meet the scale of an entire galaxy? We can reason that Luna encompasses all moons, rather than just Earth's, but what happens when the forsaken see alien versions of themselves in other civilizations? Would it be fair for them to conceive that Luna chooses a guardian spirit for each major world? And thus Father Wolf was Earth's designated protector?

    Finally, there was the issue of whether or not to reveal shapechangers to human society. If alien cultures were at war with them, and had such beings on the front lines, wouldn't it be in the theriomorphs best interests to save humanity? If so, how would their presence affect military hardware? Would they develop starships with Spirit AIs? Able to fade in and out of the Shadow of space? What neat cultural trends would appear in human society both central and colonial?

                      My personal plan for a similar campaign is that the various supernatural templates exist among the various alien species, but their saturation in each race/culture is different.

                      Theriomorphs exist among a number of races, with them gaining the aspects/forms of various apex predators native to their home world. There won't be any "Alien Werewolves" as such, but that's just because they won't turn into WOLVES.
                      My "Klingon"-esqe race has fully integrated the shapechangers into society, they form a sort of religious and societal over class, because their bloodline is "Blessed by the God of War" giving them their powers. As far a humans are concerned, this is simply a "weird alien thing" that our scientists are studying on the sly (so as not to offend them and start a war) but nobody SANE makes the connection between the alien lords shape-changing and silly old myths about WEREWOLVES, pff, what kind of rube do you take me for?
                      The Uratha are, understandably, upset and concerned about this.

                      Another race (based heavily on the typical "Gray" alien) are actually almost completely overrun by vampires. Vampirism isn't NATIVE to their race, but a rather unfortunate abduction a century back (I.E. during our modern age) netted some of their sampling teams a rather unexpected and unwelcome guest. 80% of their society is now Vampiric, the other 20% being kept like cattle for food. We don't know about this, and instead believe that the race's hemophagic diet is natural, and their adverse reaction to sunlight a result of a nocturnal nature and a small dim home sun. Also worth note, since they all stem from a SINGLE vampiric progenitor, they are all from a single clan. The existence of other vampire clans is unknown to them as of yet.
                      Additionally, some other races MAY have native Vampirism, which will result in them having unique CLANS among them.

                      Awakened mages will exist in all races in basically equal numbers. They all share the same watchtowers, interpret the supernal realms the same, and even have the same myth cycle about Atlantis and the world pre-fall. This disturbs them greatly, but also reassures them... Somewhat.

                      Likewise, the True Fae steal from all races, the Pyros that allows a demiurge to create Prometheans is universal, as is the underworld and the Geists that strive to escape death by bonding with a Sin-Eater, and the God Machine and it's fallen Angels. I think, for my own purposes, Mummies will be the one wild card, in that the rite of return is a single very specific ritual so I'm going to say ONLY the lords of Irem discovered it. So, no alien mummies, but that's about it.


                      Anyway, that's just my take for my own campaign

                                                  I tried quoting MrParaduo but the entry got really screwy so here:

                                                  ​Theriomorphs. Why would they be impossible on other planets? If you want them there, put them there, plenty of fiction depicts aliens as being able to shapeshift, even entire races of them. How they shapeshift is up to you. Perhaps they can turn into any living being they come across, perhaps only those they devour, maybe they can only turn a single part of their body into that of an animal, maybe they can only turn into plants, maybe they can only turn into one creature, maybe only a certain subspecies of them can transform.

                                                  Balance is a touchy subject sometimes so I'll just suggest you worry about balance if you think it's important. As for mechanics, if the creatures are going to be interacting with players, you'll need them. If it's just for fanfiction or something, don't bother.

                                                  For colonization, remember the Uratha don't always breed true. Sometimes it skips a generation, sometimes it never comes up. They've been around as long as humanity has, if every child a werewolf made was a werewolf, everyone would be. Or a lot more than there are, at least. As written the Uratha are the minority, and likely always will be. So if they decided to colonize a planet there'd eventually be a disparity between normal humans outnumbering the wolf blooded and werewolves. If the Forsaken set themselves up as rulers and lawmakers, when the humans had access to firearms they might decide they don't want to bother policing the spirit. And of course, the Spirit may not exist on other worlds. I suppose a feasible compromise would be that whoever created it would make some doctrine so that less problems would pop up (wolves are sacred animals, don't kill them).

                                                  As for Forsaken seeing other shapeshifters, it would really depend on the situation. As it is they're a fractured lot, they don't have a universal opinion about anything except to follow in Father Wolf's footsteps, and even then not everyone is interested in that. I suppose in the end how much are you deviating from what we have now, are the Forsaken as they are, individuals thrown into a spiritual duty together, or are they going to be much more unified like the Federation?



                                                  Perhaps not related, but Glen Cook wrote a trilogy called Darkwar. It was about a wolf-woman on a planet full of wolf-people (the people are generally divided by gender and caste), and she gets some...I want to say psychic but it really was a bit spiritual, powers. Turns out most of the women in the priesthood get them, but she was really good at using the powers. They eventually came into contact with humans, but the males, who didn't usually have access to magic, had a massive technocracy on other wolf planets and rather than fighting the humans wanted to deal with them. It ended badly.
