                                                            The idea is they're static because the Firstborn are actually out there, guiding them along. Like Norse pantheism might still exist if Odin and Thor were out there performing miracles for folks and defending them from conversion. There aren't other tribes because there aren't other Firstborn.

                                                            That said, in the books it does discuss the reconciliation of religious werewolves with their changed view of the world once they become werewolves. Not everyone just abandons their previous beliefs, many try to adapt the spirit stuff to their own religion, or vice versa, and an unfortunate other group try to deny it until they go mad. There are mentions of werewolves who have found angels and religious symbols in the Shadow and follow them.

                                                            There's also mentions of werewolves with political differences, which work out pretty much the same. Some may hold their tribe to be a higher priority and not attack others, some may hold their nationality or whatever else to be more important and join in. Some are more concerned about the spirit and so forth. In both cases it really depends on the individual, and perhaps their status in their pack.
