    Sorcerers: the Defilers

    11-15-2013, 02:11 AM
    [Here is my write-up of a new kind of antagonist for Werewolf, based on the discussion by Papa Bear, Dataweaver, Pendragon, and others in the old forums: humans who abuse the Shadow. This is a working draft which I will probably update periodically. Feedback and suggestions from the community are welcomed and appreciated.]



    Sorcerers are humans who have come to learn 'enough' about the spirit world and its denizens to interact with it. Unlike Shamans who seek to mediate a balance, or Priests who offer to serve the spirits' whims, the Sorcerer exerts their knowledge and power to further their own agendas. Spirits are a largely unknown resource with power over, potentially, every phenomenon in the world. Of course, they are willful and dangerous, but a clever sorcerer can achieve success, luxury, and influence without ever working for their desires. They can do anything they want; they can rule the world.

    Since the time of Father Wolf, some humans have tried to enslave spirits to their will for petty gains in their mortal affairs, either ignorant or uncaring as to the affects it has in the spiritual reflection of the world. In modern times, the communication era has made these sorcerers an exponential threat, as everything from recruiting, to developing rituals, to sharing names and bans of spirits can happen instantly, anonymously, all across the world. Anyone curious about the horrific encounters they've had can access a private chatroom or a file-sharing site, learn a dozen ways to abuse the spirit world, and start their own cell of cultists.

    They have no concept of territory. They have no concept of harmony. They can spring up anywhere. They can change sites and disappear. And they know they have an enemy, keeping secrets and oppressing their freedoms.



    Theme: Domination
    At the end of the day, sorcerers want power and they use spirits to get it. To make use of spirits at all, one must at least understand the basic principles of animism and the Shadow: objects, creatures, and even experience have a conscious ephemeral counterpart in mirror world, with supernatural power. For sorcerers, though, this knowledge does not awe them before god-like beings, nor does it evoke empathy for a world that is “aware” and “alive”. They have no consideration for balance in either world or how their actions affect the Shadow. If they see these beings as anything other than tools, they are still clearly inferior.

    In relation to the spirit world, sorcerers are slavers, abusers, and defilers. They trick and chain entities into doing their bidding. They command power over the living world - not their own power, but the power they coerce and threaten out of the spirits they dominate.

    Mood: Tension
    Despite their arrogance, sorcerers know that they are playing a dangerous game. They become quickly familiar with the kind of power spirits can command, and despite the malice they continue to treat them with, they understand the ire and resentment they earn from these beings. If a binding rituals goes wrong, if their attention slips away from the spirit riding them, in some cases if the spirit simply stands up for itself, they might very well lose their life - or worse. Like domination of any kind, sorcerers are caught between a deadly game of wanting more power and needing to maintain control, of using force and intimidation over spirits while relying on them entirely.



    Spirit Cult initiation benefits
    •: in order to draw initiates in and test belief in the cause, the foundation of animism in the Hisil is shared. Initiates gain a free Occult specialty in Spirits.
    ••: once someone has become a trusted and respected member in the cult, they are given access to the constantly updating, randomly moving collection of online knowledge which various cells from around the world contribute to. They gain the Database • merit, which functions as a mix of Library and Contacts for spirits and related phenomenon.
    •••: Their studies progressing, the Initiate finally masters the complicated language of the spirit world. Gain the First Tongue Merit, and a free Persuasion or Intimidation specialty in Spirits. [?]
    ••••: Armed with knowledge about the spirit world, members are given their first taste of the power that comes from using that information. They are given the Empowerment •• ability, which acts as the spirit Influence Strengthen •. This gives the priests and organizers the ability to demonstrate the power of the cult.
    •••••: High priests stand atop their respective cults, movers of the organization. At the start of each story the Sorcerer gains five dots to distribute between Allies, Retainers, or Safe Place. These dots are fleeting, generally lasting no more than a chapter or two, and represent the High Priest's ability to call on her cult directly for aid.


    New Merits:

    Merit: Language: First Tongue (•)
    Prerequisite: Database, Mentor, or Familiar
    Learning the spirit speech is not easy for Sorcerers. Some are able to bribe or coerce spirits into teaching them the language, but they are not the majority. Nevertheless, anyone who frequently interacts with the denizens of the Shadow eventually must learn their language. Most sorcerers only access videos from the database which teach brief, important phrases (justifying an Occult Specialty in First Tongue), gathered from trial-and-error encounters with spirits. Traditionally, those fluent in First Tongue have learned from a Mentor more rooted in the Occult, but those who dedicate enough time to studying the Database, or who have close ties to a spirit also learn.


    Merit: Database (• to •••)
    Prerequisite: Status (Spirit Cult) ••
    The online Database which various spirit cults tap into is a collection of file-sharing groups, blogs, and private chat rooms where users around the world share the information they've collected about spirits. Those who propagate these sites are firm believers in free information (these cults are easy to join), but they randomly change web addresses and networks to avoid being shut down.

    Access to this Database counts as proper literature to research the bans and banes of spiritual entities, and gives a +1 bonus per dot to attempts to do so. This Merit can also be used as a Contact knowledgeable in spirits, as members can ask advice from other cultists. Finally, characters with this Merit are able to purchase Sorcerer Rites; doing so involves a research roll requiring double the Rite’s dots in successes, and the expenditure of Experience.

    As invaluable as this resource is, it does have some limitations. Firstly, as people contribute from all over the world, any individual spirit is likely to be too distant to summon. Individuals can also only be in one place at a time; those named in the database are likely to be already summoned at any given moment (requiring a Chance Die to be successful; Willpower cannot be added). Also, despite giving lip-service to the ideal of free information, most sorcerers are out for personal power; they are unlikely to share information on any rite that can be used against them, or any servant that can be stolen. Therefore, most of the usable information in the database is general information about the weakest spirits (Gafflings; Rank 1 and 2).


    Merit: Empowerment ••
    Those who have learned the secrets of the Shadow realm know that everything has a spirit within. In most things, the spirit is dormant, waiting for enough time or activity to jolt it into awareness. With the right occult knowledge and proper attunement, a person can rouse a spirit, just for a moment, to compel it to act. Fortunately, this has a very tangible benefit: all spirits desire to be more… them, a more perfect representation; Rousing the spirit of a thing can temporarily strengthen it.

    Mechanically, the sorcerer spends a point of Willpower and rolls Presence + Intimidation. They must be able to touch the object they empower. Afterward, for one minute per success, the object gains a +1 equipment bonus: guns are more deadly, cars handle smoother, walls are sturdier, money, even, becomes more valuable - credit cards have a higher spending limit, coins are suddenly from a rare mint. In theory, anything can be empowered, but living creatures tend to have sporadic results. The catch is that the sorcerer must use the object within the duration of its bonus, or they are sapped of another Willpower.

    This ability can also be used to hinder or weaken objects, but it is more difficult. Perhaps the sorcerer wants to stall a car before their enemy drives off, or they want to deactivate the security system of a museum. However, this contradicts the nature of the spirit; they must be bribed or truly threatened to compel in this manner. To turn off, weaken, or make an object more shoddy (by a penalty of one), the sorcerer must either gain an exceptional success on their roll, or successfully use the Sacrifice ritual on a related object.


    [sidebar] Optional Rule: Paving the Way
    Creating an ‘Open’ condition for an ephemeral entity to manifest requires strengthening the resonant condition to be more potently expressed, so that the entity can draw itself into a physical shape. Ordinarily, this requires extended preparation, or the entity can use ‘Influence: Strengthen’ to empower it temporarily. Sorcerers are able to accidentally create this condition with many of their rituals and abilities (most notably Empowerment). When a supernatural ability is used to enhance a phenomenon (such as adding equipment bonuses or intensifying emotion), this creates the ‘Open’ condition for the duration of the effect. Harnessing this power has a side effect of giving many spirits an opening into the physical world, exacerbating the threat sorcerers already pose. [/sidebar]


    [?] Merit: Conviction •••
    Prerequisite: Mortal
    This merit represents a will strong enough to stand against the mysterious powers in the world of darkness, and retain one’s autonomy even against mental invasion and compulsion. This merit gives a mortal character an effective Supernatural Tolerance of 3, increasing their resistance to any mental, emotional or magical manipulation. [?]



    Rituals
    The databases used by spirit cults are full of information on spirits and how to tap into their power here, in this world. Every month, there is a new blog entry about an experimental ritual, a new translated tome full of names and Bans, new recordings of the rites performed by monstrous beastmen; and, of course, tried-and-true tactics are bundled into easily downloaded zip files. Some of these rituals can be practiced by anybody with the right knowledge, taking advantage of the alien rules of the spirit world or phenomenon natural to the world. Others require a discipline of will and a spark of power which quickly develops as members experience the mysteries. The most esoteric require a spiritual power no ritualists can produce themselves and which isn't native to this world.

    The power most members dabble in are among the easiest rituals, which require only the proper preparation to employ. Experienced sorcerers primarily wield the power of the spirits directly, summoning entities which can accomplish a wide range of tasks with little effort on behalf of the ritualist. However, some sorcerers chose to rely on their internal strength, and develop the discipline to call magic themselves; most set the task of memorizing at least a few of the most useful rites in this manner.

    What this means, is that sorcerer characters, through research online or in physical tomes, are able to purchase rituals. To learn a new ritual, the sorcerer must make an extended research roll, Intelligence + Occult, which requires 2 successes per dot and the expenditure of Experience to complete (rituals count as Merits for Experience costs). Due to the global breadth of knowledge they draw from, rituals from any source might be adapted. I recommend primarily using Thaumaturge rituals (Second Sight) and Werewolf rites, however some Spirit Spells (Mage) or even Endowments like Rites du Chuval (Hunter) might be appropriate for a sorcerer. Due to their medium of knowledge, the best rituals to adapt are ones which must be performed in specific ways - those are the easiest to steal; spells which draw on internal power are more difficult to give instructions for.

    Though I make many recommendations below, remember to limit what rituals are actually available for a specific cult or individual to research. The spirit cult databases are not exhaustive, after all, and though this movement was born out of free information sentiments, power-seeking sorcerers are not likely to share any ritual which can be used against them. Besides, sorcerers provide excellent potential for conflict with a number of supernaturals (in part) because of their desire to steal rites and knowledge.

    [sidebar]Optional Rule: Reflecting Magic
    Rituals which directly empower or affect things in the physical world do so by creating a surge of resonance, which carries over into the Shadow. Rituals which create a new phenomenon, like Enchantment or Weather Control, actually create new spirits which come into being in Twilight, and shift back into the Shadow when their Manifestation ends. On-going effects, like those caused by Geomancy, alter the flow of Resonance and can, with further investment, be used to generate Loci.[/sidebar]



    New Rituals:

    Ritual: Harvest (•••)
    Once a sorcerer has summoned and bound a spirit he is able to use this ritual in order to make use of the spirit’s most important resource: Essence. Helpless inside a binding circle, the sorcerer is able to tap directly into the spirit and extract Essence for his own use: as chimiage for other spirits, or as fuel for some of his most powerful rituals. Needless to say this process is infuriating, and often agonizing, for the captive spirit forced to endure the humiliation of its own Essence being carved from it, and the following starvation while it remains captive.

    In order to safely use this ritual, the sorcerer must have a vessel prepared to store the drained Essence. In most cases, he will have a locus or touchstone at the ready, or may even channel the Essence directly to another spirit. If needed, the Essence can be stored in any physical object with a quick preparation of painting the correct sigil with blood, but in order to access Essence thus stored later, the ritualist must destroy the vessel. (Interestingly, the sigil is similar to the brandings Uratha wear, representing the apparent Renown of “Patience”.) A sorcerer could store the Essence within himself, however humans have no means of using Essence within themselves, and doing so has been known to cause bizarre illness.

    After marking the recipient vessel, the sorcerer spends a point of Willpower and rolls [Presence + Resolve] penalized by the target’s Rank. The spirit is only able to contest this, using Resistance, by spending a point of Willpower itself, and may only do so if its Bane is not present; captive spirits are all but at the mercy of a sorcerer. Each success (above the spirit’s) drains one Essence from the target.

    Ritual: Kris Knife (••)
    Quickly into their manipulation of spiritual powers, sorcerers learn that spirits have a nasty habit of making themselves intangible in the middle of “negotiations”. Even bound in place, it’s difficult to intimidate a spirit in Twilight, unless she wants to resort to exploiting its Bane - which is sometimes heavy handed. By stealing the rituals of the Uratha, sorcerers were able to solve this problem by imbuing a ritual knife to strike Twilight beings.

    In her own blood, the sorcerer marks a glyph on the blade and invokes a name* in First Tongue. If she is then able to invest one point of Essence into the blade, it becomes able to strike ephemeral spirits for the scene.

    *: Uratha chant the names of Father Wolf and all the Firstborn; sorcerers, with some trial and error, shortened the prayer to a single word which would produce the effect. Interestingly, either “Urfarah”, “Huzuruth”, or “Fenris-Ur” said alone will enchant the blade. Of course, the sorcerers don’t understand the names or context.



    Talismans
    One of the most useful tools at a sorcerer’s disposal, like their Uratha enemies, are hand-held relics empowered by spirits that give their bearers a measure of power. Those created by werewolves are known as Fetishes; sorcerers create “talismans”. This is not a mere matter of semantics, as the sorcerers’ method varies greatly from the one used by Uratha ritemasters, and has some important practical differences as a result.

    Talismans are objects crafted or modified for the purpose of imprisoning spirits; the rules they exploit are generalized to the nature of Essence and the spirit world (a three-dot ritual to learn), but, like a shackle, require clasping the item around a spirit that is Materialized or in Twilight; naturally, most sorcerers trap spirits they have already summoned and bound in place. From the confines of their prison, the spirit has no way to interact or even perceive the world, but they do not sleep patiently within; they are aware, powerless, and deprived of sensation, but for the occasional whispers of resonance around them. In this state, their captors are able to tap into the restrained abilities of the spirit and use it as though their own.

    Mechanically, a talisman can be used to channel one of the trapped spirit’s Numina; which Numen it employs is decided when the talisman is created and cannot change. Rarely do sorcerers have any control over which of their prisoner’s powers is keyed to the object, but reportedly it will be the Numen the spirit was using at the moment of the binding. Anyone who possesses the talisman (and is aware of its power) is able to spend one Willpower to use the Numina. As the power is channeled through them, the character uses their own talents, substituting an appropriate Attribute + Skill for the activation. This power can only be successfully used once per scene.

    The way this treatment differs from Fetishes has important effects on its use. The most important difference is that is that Fetishes are designed carefully to respect the bound spirit; the nature of the binding and the ritualistic treatment of the item after protects the spirit from any loss of Essence (they can sometimes even be empowered by the arrangement). While talismans do enough to prevent Essence bleed and the daily cost to stay active, their use expends the spirit’s Essence and gives them no treatment to replenish it. The spirit is able to harvest phenomenon around them, which they do reflexively when they sense an Influence, but this can be very rare for some kinds of spirit, and the sorcerers are rarely aware of this need or occurrence. What this means is that talismans have a number of ‘charges’ equal to their Max Essence pool (bear in mind that some Numina cost multiple points), which may recharge infrequently, but which is too frequently exhausted. When this happens, the talisman goes inert, and the spirit enters Slumber.

    The sorcerers who create talismans usually find that they are a cheap, quick, and easy way to tap into the power of the spirit world (especially compared to other methods), and will craft a large and variable collection to make use of. Often disguised as ordinary accessories, they carry an arsenal of spiritual power with them. This does present a risk though: anyone able to sense spirits in Twilight can notice the trapped presence inside, and either steal or destroy the talismans. An arrogant sorcerer disarmed in battle can soon see his arsenal destroyed before him, and then turn against its jailer.

    Talismans are each treated as three-dot Merits, for the purposes of trade value, material cost, and fragility. Storytellers are also encouraged to have them provoke resentment in other spirits, conveying a -1 Social penalty for each one a sorcerer carries, and clearly marking them as a spirit slaver.



    Locus Poisoning
    Detecting or making use of Loci are very difficult feats for sorcerers; they have no innate ways to do so, and few reasons. Access to a pool of Essence does make some of the more difficult rituals easier, and staying near a “door” to the other realm reduces the time they have to wait for summoned minions, but overall, the effort of finding them isn’t worth the benefits. Few even understand Loci as more than a vague concept. What most do understand, though, is that other beings are very invested in them - namely, their enemies.

    Hence comes the idea of restricting these resources.

    The most challenging part is finding a Locus and determining its Resonance. Those with ‘Unseen Sense (Spirits)’ will notice when they stumble over the area of a Locus, and may then use Wits + Investigation to find its center. Any captive spirits can be bullied into revealing what they know, but often the spirit can get away with lying all too easily, without crippling its own resource. For most, it will require the use of a clever tool (such as a talisman with the ‘Seek’ Numen) or subtle manipulation and observation of local spirits and monsters. Once the Locus’ center has been identified, the sorcerer begins her occult research into its likely resonance and its polar opposite.

    Banes are a law of the Shadow well understood and exploited by sorcerers. They’re simple, too: they are material phenomenon in direct opposition or contrast to a spirit’s core Influences. Loci don’t have Banes precisely, but they do have resonances, and introducing an object of opposing resonance into a Locus has some potent effects. If the “Bane” is placed at the Loci’s center, in the physical world, no being is able to cross worlds at the Locus; the strong pool of spiritual energy becomes barred. Furthermore, if an unwitting being attempts to drink from the Locus, they suffer Aggravated damage equal to their successes instead of pulling Essence. The highly sought resource becomes a poisoned trap, and should a sorcerer or their ally ever wish to make use of it, they just move the object for a few minutes.

    This practice does harm the Locus, but not very much. The “Banes” placed are typically small, portable, cheap things, both dwarfed by the area of the Loci and without much spiritual weight to them. Their presence does prevent one point of Essence from coalescing at the Locus each day - but, as it’s no longer drained from spirits or werewolves, even the faintest keep their pool at the same size; those of rating two or more even continue to grow. It’s only when usually large or active materials are used that it can start to dampen the spiritual wellspring.

      Summoning and Binding
      Spirit summoning, in a matter of speaking, can be done by any ordinary human with a little bit of research and preparation. The basic knowledge, theory, instructions, and a wealth of details are available to sorcerers through their online databases on how to perform these “summonings”; because of how easy they are to accomplish, many initiates’ first taste of the cult’s power comes from performing a summoning and binding themselves.

      As explained in The God Machine Chronicles (page 231), mortals are able to “summon” spirits through a simple process to reinforce their influence in an area. After proper research into the Influences, Bans and Banes of an entity, cultists only need to prepare an area wherein the appropriate resonance is amplified, granting the “Open” condition. At this point, spirits with the appropriate Influence can Manifest with ease.

      The shortcoming of this method is that it merely creates an easy condition for the spirit to Manifest, it neither calls nor compels any spirit to the location. Furthermore, spirits must first cross into the physical world to make use of the Condition; unless the entity has special Manifestations, the sorcerer must find a Locus to make best use of this “summoning” - no easy proposition for a human. Because of this, most sorcerers invest time into learning more direct rituals which are actually capable of summoning.


      When it comes to Binding spirits, however, most prefer to rely on the simple, mundane methods (GMC 232). Doing so saves them time and investment (ie, not purchasing a ritual with Experience), and tends to be the most reliable - even if it does require researching the desired entity properly beforehand. The folk warding practices (Second Sight 120) are often only a nuisance, and the monsters’ method (Forsaken 157) require terms to let the spirit go! The mundane method, however, lasts for days and is guaranteed to work… as long as you do it right.

      In order to bind a spirit or keep it out of an area, the sorcerer need only mark a boundary using the entity’s Bane. As he does so, he focuses the innate strength of his soul on creating the spiritual barrier. This is a Presence + Occult roll penalized by the entity’s Rank [modifiers: +1 per Integrity above 7; -1 per Integrity below 7. +1 to ward a six square foot area; +0 to ward a single room or vehicle; -1 to ward a two story building; -1 for each additional space equivalent to a family home]. For one day per success afterward (or until the boundary is disturbed), the spirit gains a Ban which prevents it from crossing or influencing across the boundary.

      Sorcerers should be forewarned: spirits must spend one point of Essence each day or they fall into slumber; furthermore, they bleed Essence outside of resonant conditions while in the physical world. To bind a spirit for an extended period, cultists should do so in a place where their Influences are reinforced (as above), protecting from Essence bleed and granting one point a day; this will keep them awake and trapped. In order to use the spirit, however, such as to cast improvised rituals (below) or to drain Essence from, tribute to its Influences should be offered, or it will eventually fall into slumber.


      Guardian Binding
      One of the advantages of being able to call beings of supernatural power which can turn invisible at will is that they are very good at ambushing one’s enemies. When obedience can be properly ensured, sorcerers prefer sending spirit servants to settle scores, but there’s the rub: so rarely do they obey. More reliable, at least, is getting them to protect locations and objects on one’s behalf. Once they can be leashed, at least you know where to find them to motivate.

      Ephemeral guardians are a time-honored resource for occultists to invest in for security, and sorcerers are no different. It requires an entirely different kind of binding, of course; one which restricts their movement, but allows them to effect things around them - sort of an inverted warding circle. For this, not many solutions have been found (and the experiments don’t often go well). One reliable ritual can be found in the databases, at least.

      There is a rumor, though, that the best way to chain a spirit [or any ephemeral being, it turns out] is with a collar and a rock. There are some objects (and it's doubtful any are natural) which exist in Twilight at the same time as in the Material. Happening across one is well against the odds, and few sorcerers would notice if they did, but the best means to make one is by investing Essence into something. If one point of Essence is poured into an object per point of Structure within a scene, it develops enough spiritual weight to exist in Twilight; if a spirit is collared by one, only discorporation will free them. Finding a way to manage this, however, isn't easy for most human practitioners.

      As for the stone, any raw iron will work. The only explanations as to why are some mythic nonsense about being the “most physical metal” or the blood of a celestial or only existing in this realm.*

      *: feel free to choose your own material and explanation. This simply works for my chronicle.


      New Ritual: Tether Spirit (••••)
      The tried-and-true method of chaining a spirit to a location so that it may act as a guard is an ancient ritual which probably originated from a different branch of spirit-talkers. Among shamans and priests, it is most often used in conjunction with Invocation (••), asking the spirit to approach them while offering the Fetter Numen [an alternative ritual to Invocation (••••) which offers Possession]. The process of tethering is slow, as ritualized prayer and offerings are used to align the chosen Fetter to the spirit’s Influences - changing the resonance of the object for the duration of the binding.

      Sorcerers are often loathe to resort to the offerings and homage their counterparts do, and often don’t wish to give the spirit access to a Numen (which lets it decide when to end the effect) so much as nail it to the spot. More aggressive practitioners have found a work-around: if they have a particular spirit in mind prior to the ritual, they are able to select the spirit’s bane as its Fetter, which both prevents the spirit from affecting it and keeps control of the binding in the sorcerer’s hand (and allows him to humiliate himself with the attunement privately, rather than in front of the entity).

      Spend a point of Willpower and roll [Presence + Occult] penalized by the spirit’s Resistance (if it resists). Each roll represents ten minutes of attuning the spirit to the Fetter. The ritual is successful at four successes.

      Living beings can be made a Fetter as well, but their will must also be overcome. All living Fetters resist reflexively by penalizing the roll with their Resolve trait. The spirit may lend itself to either side in this effort, adding its Power to the sorcerer if it is accomplice in the ritual, or subtracting its Resistance.
      Dice Pool (live Fetters): [Presence + Persuasion] - [Resolve + Resistance] or [Presence + Persuasion + Power] - Resolve

      Once bound, the spirit must stay within [Rank x 10] yards of its Fetter, and cannot interact with it by any means. Any other target within line of sight may be affected by its Influences and Numina, or otherwise interacted with. The Fetter counts as a persistent Open condition (only for that spirit), and also radiates its Influence; this protects it from Essence Bleed and grants one Essence a day, in addition to allowing the spirit to Materialize or shift to Twilight at will. This condition lasts for a number of days equal to the sorcerer’s Willpower, though they may chose to end it early.

      Drawback: The downside to the use of this ritual should be quite obvious: if the spirit can interact with anything in its surrounding except its Fetter, then what stops it from killing the sorcerer? Nothing except intimidation and lies. Sorcerers like to give the impression that only he can end the effect, and only if he’s alive. Sometimes they buy it. Sometimes they only just let him live. Alas, there aren't any foolproof methods to designate other things the spirit cannot target, though they’re certainly motivated to try. In the meantime, this can make the sites sorcerers thus guard very dangerous, even for them.


      [sidebar]Optional Rule: Mooring
      As described in Lore of the Forsaken, Artificial spirits begin their existence entirely dependent on their physical counterparts. They are Fettered so closely that they are restrained across the Gauntlet, and must learn the ‘Unfetter’ Manifestation to ever become independent; many die instead, destroyed with their physical counterpart.
      Using this option, the ability sorcerers use to chain spirits to a specific object is not merely a Fetter they cannot release from, but a Mooring. Perhaps spiteful Artificials taught this technique originally, to inflict upon the other Choirs, or perhaps the sorcerers accidentally devised an exceedingly potent ritual. However it was learned, their technique designates the spirit’s Fetter as its defining physical counterpart, the individual it reflects.
      Even after the spirit is freed from the spell, unless it knows the ‘Unfetter’ Manifestation, it must remain within five yards of the object or being’s corresponding location. It has free use of its Numina and Influences in the Shadow, but any effect on the material plane must target its moor. The spirit also changes its nature in order to specifically reflect that individual object or being; assume that after a number of days equal to its maximum Essence pool, the spirit has all (and only) its moor’s distinguishing characteristics. Furthermore, when the moor is destroyed, the spirit loses one Rank (Traits may or may not alter, at Storyteller discretion); falling to zero because of this dissolves the spirit forever.
      Obviously this assumes that the sorcerer binds a spirit to an instance of its Influences, but should the Storyteller wish to make this option more disturbing, that doesn't have to be the case. Even if a spirit is bound to a wholly different object, it becomes Moored. Instead of becoming a perfect reflection, the spirit gains one dot of the corresponding Influence after a number of days equal to its max Essence pool. Jagglings may be able to cope with this fundamental change (though not always), but Gafflings are all but certain to become a magath.[/sidebar]



      New Condition: Guard Orders
      When having spirits - particularly uncooperative spirits - guard chambers and belongings on their behalf, sorcerers prefer to ensure that they actually follow through with the protection. The way they usually manage this is to make the spirit perceive threats to its charge as threats to itself.
      Effect: When intruders approach where or what the spirit is guarding, pain and fear shoot through the entity. It attacks on instinct and compulsion, as ruthlessly as it’s able. After several turns, it may roll Rank to stop itself, but its victims won’t likely be sympathetic at that point.
      Causing it: When setting the spirit to its duty, the sorcerer prepares a valuable offering of chimiage (preferably of Essence he stole from the spirit), and sets a simple booby trap to destroy it when intruders approach, placed just out of the spirit’s reach.
      Resolution: Once the trap is sprung. Observant intruders can spot the trap before it sets off and disable it, and the spirit may have Numina which could as well. With fragile enough chimiage, though, an effective trap is easy to make.



      Shards of Command
      This rare but easily created tool takes advantage of the system of debt in the Shadow, which allows spirits to learn additional Numina from one another at the cost of an additional Ban. The sorcerer takes a physical object, shatters it into two pieces under the right occult condition, and then embeds it within the chosen spirit. With the piece she keeps for herself, the sorcerer is able to compel obedience and, even better, is able to speak to the spirit at range.

      Traditionally, the Shard of Command is a mirror fragment which was broken in the light of the crescent moon, though other objects in different conditions are known to work: fishing hooks tied together then cut apart with a shell at low tide; a bone from a bull, cracked apart in a fire. Learning an appropriate technique requires an occult research roll which accumulates five successes, but costs no experience. After half of the charm is “given” to the spirit, the sorcerer needs only visibly show their half in order to compel obedience. A Willpower can be spent in order to communicate at range for the scene, which waives the need to brandish the charm for the spirit to obey. Mirror shards are preferred for the extra benefit of being able to see through the spirit’s eyes in the reflection, if it spends one Essence to share.

      Though simple and severely useful, this method has a few drawbacks tied into the reasons that it works. Firstly, if the spirit’s shard is ever removed, it becomes free from the arrangement. The spirit also is only compelled to obey direct commands which are actively achievable (meaning that passive commands, forbiddences, and similar don’t have to be obeyed, a fact few sorcerers are aware of), and the spirit is free to interpret or pursue orders however it wishes, within the letter of the command. This compulsion works because the owner of the shard’s other half is recognized as a superior spirit in a position of dominance; this also means that if the sorcerer’s shard is taken, someone else can take command - including the spirit itself. Finally is the drawback most sorcerers don’t even consider: what the spirit gets out of the arrangement. The shard embedded in them - a physical object - gives the spirit a lasting ‘Open’ condition, carried inside of itself, protecting it from Essence bleed and allowing it to Manifest at will. This unparalleled access to the physical in their ‘free time’ is one reason many spirits accept the arrangement with little hostility, and in fact sometimes share this ritual with enterprising young occultists.



      Improvised Rituals
      Like other ritualists who call upon the Shadow’s power, sorcerers can compel spirits to use their Influences on the sorcerer’s behalf using specially tailored rituals. [For more information, see the Blood Sorcery style Rituals by PenDragon or King’s Raven.] Unlike other ritualists, however, sorcerers don’t cultivate relationships with the spirits they work with, and they do not humiliate themselves with extravagant beseechment and costly service. They invoke the spirits more… directly.

      Sorcerers do not need to purchase a Rituals trait or Influences they can work with in order to perform rituals. Instead, sorcerers capture spirits and force them to do their bidding. This always requires the formal summoning and binding of a particular spirit (as above), but it means that sorcerers can perform great feats of spirit magic without ever studying, or cultivating relationships, or paying for that power; their only limitation is the spirit’s own abilities. While they have a spirit in captivity, sorcerers can use improvised rituals for free, so long as the effect is within the spirit’s Influences and it still has Essence to spend.

      As one might expect, this kind of treatment does earn the seething hostility of the spirits they treat this way… usually. Any spirit that escapes after such treatment is sure to be a life-long enemy of the sorcerer (which probably won’t be very long), and will share news of this kind of treatment throughout the local Shadow, making spirits resentful of the sorcerer long before they meet. Sorcerers can bribe or intimidate their victims into not acting against them, but many simply ensure that they never escape.

      Step 7: Determine the retribution the spirit will seek, if it gets the chance.

Of course, many sorcerers circumvent the fear of retribution entirely by never releasing the spirit, putting it into slumber, or destroying it completely while it is still in their mercy.

      Offering of another spirit: -4

      Favors promised to the spirit: -1 to -5

      Condition: Traumatic Bonding
      The spirit has been subjected to cycles of abuse and reward enough to confuse and disorient it, to the point that it considers the entire experience in captivity a beneficial affair; it regards the sorcerer well afterward and will be likely to respond to future summonings.
      Effect: the sorcerer gains +2 on future interactions with the spirit. Attempts to sway the spirit against the sorcerer take a 2 dice penalty (-2 on rolls, or 2 additional Doors).
      Causing it: Pay enough tribute after every ritual such that spirit’s Wrath is negative when it is set free.
      Resolution: The condition ends after [10-Rank] days, but can be refreshed with interactions in the interim. The spirit can be persuaded against the sorcerer at some difficulty. If the sorcerer harms the spirit with its Bane (binding circles do not count), this effect immediately ends.

          Gi Ten "Possessors of Power"

          Ritual: Second Shadow (••••)
          The oldest form of binding spirits to oneself is a perverse adaptation of werewolves’ Bind Spirit rite: the sorcerer turns himself into the binding circle by wrapping a barbed silver thread around one limb. This gives the entity the ‘Fettered’ condition with one important caveat: the sorcerer can use the spirit’s Essence. Access to this important resource gives the sorcerer access to several basic feats, namely bribing other spirits, activating Fetishes, and being able to Step Sideways at a locus. This has some drawbacks, though. Neither spirit nor sorcerer can use Numina, and spirits can only Influence their host. The spirit is also able to read the host’s thoughts and urge his actions, subjecting the sorcerer to constant invasion and pressure for a meager benefit.

          This ritual still gets passed around the sorcerer networks, though, probably as a test for enterprising young cultists; those subjected to a spirit’s manipulation might be easier for older cultists to dominate, or may remove a rival. Perhaps, given how simple it is to perform, the benefits outweigh the risks for resolute sorcerers without experience. Perhaps the consequences are rarely witnessed to be shared. Or perhaps this is one example of their enemies hacking sites and perpetuating false and dangerous rituals to destroy them from within.


          [sidebar]Optional Rule: Warping the Shadow
          Material beings and actions create resonance. This resonance is filtered through the Gauntlet to generate essence. A thicker gauntlet means that less essence is generated.* When physical beings cross into the Shadow, however, there is no buffer zone; their actions and emotion generate raw bursts of essence, flooding areas with new types of resonance, and sometimes generating spirits on the spot. Father Wolf drove humans out of the spirit world not because they were in danger, but because they could swiftly and dramatically alter the entire spiritscape. The sorcerers, who abuse spirits for their own material gains, when able to access the spirit world directly, set out to do so intentionally, carving spirit ecosystems to their liking with the same abandon as strip mining in the physical. For this, they earned their moniker as “the Defilers”.
          In games where action and consequence are a major theme, Storytellers may wish to use these same rules for werewolves and other characters as well. When, for example, the players use physical items to set fire to the Shadow, the spirits birthed can be an immediate, terrible threat to impress the importance of harmony and wisdom. However, storytellers can easily use this option for sorcerers (making them potent antagonists) but not their werewolf players; it may be the case that beings of half-spirit half-flesh (Uratha, Hosts, and Claimed) do not interrupt the balance between worlds, by virtue of belonging to both.
          *I base this off of descriptions of Barrens and Azlu, where strong Gauntlets lead to areas without spiritual reflections, but this is one interpretation of the setting, not to be taken as canon.[/sidebar]



          Ritual: Ride the Spirit (••••••)
          Prerequisite: Invocation (••••)
          This ritual works like Invocation; so much so, in fact, that spirits believe that it is. Like Invocation, when the Thaumaturge asks for a spirit's attention, they also offer the Possession Numen to one who answers, and the implication that they won't resist. Only when the spirit accepts and rides the caster is the ritual's true nature revealed.

          Spend a Willpower and roll [Presence + Persuasion] - [Resistance + Rank] every 10 minutes until 6 successes are earned. After 4 successes, if any roll fails, treat this as a successful casting of Invocation: the spirit arrives with the Possession Numen, and the Thaumaturge cannot reflexively resist. If at any point a dramatic failure occurs, the spirit is summoned, materialized, and aware of what was attempted.

          However, if successful, the spirit answers the summons and rides the caster; the caster remains in full control of their actions, and is able to use one Numen of the spirit's, spending their essence for it! This condition lasts for the scene.

          Of course, once the scene ends, the spirit is free to do whatever they want for this kind of infraction, but wise, or lucky, Thaumaturges only attempt this on weak spirits, and provide them excellent opportunities for Essence and Influence while they do. Fear of this unexpected ability and meager benefit out of it keep some spirits cowed.


          Ritual: Rite of Usurpation (•••••)
          To bind a spirit is an insult, yet sorcerers can work much greater offenses. With a spirit already bound the sorcerer takes a ritual knife, one that possesses elements of the spirit's bane, and sheers from the shade it's otherworldly might. This involves an extended [Presence + Resolve] roll, with a target number of the spirit's [Resistance + Rank] and an interval of one roll per hour. Upon completion of the rite the sorcerer strips the spirit of it's Rank and forges it into a Mantle (some piece of clothing or accessory).

          While worn this accessory provides the sorcerer with a number of benefits: he effectively gains the spirit's Rank, which grants Supernatural Tolerance equivalent to its dots, as well as a perceived position in the spiritual hierarchy, allowing him to command lesser spirits; he is also able to store and use Essence within himself and sense Essence around him, however the Mantle does not grant access to the spirit's Influences or Numina. The Mantle counts as a persistent supernatural effect and glows with otherworldly power to anyone who can perceive Twilight (in the "frequency" of spirits).

          This is an agonizing experience for a spirit: every day it is parted from it's Rank the spirit loses one dot of an Attribute or Influence. Once all it's Influences are gone the spirit dissolves, and the Mantle with it. Returning the spirit's Rank is not as simple as destroying the garment, indeed those attempting to do so will find that the substance is nearly invincible (some sorcerers use this to their advantage, making magical armor out of the spiritual reinforcement). Only by returning the Mantle to the spirit directly can its power be restored.
