                            Originally posted by iamspoonman View Post
                            I've been reading up more about the Pure from 1e to get some more information as my players are really compelled by them and I'm getting a bit confused. So Forsaken argue that the Pure should not be killed because "The People do not Murder the People", but then in the 1e source The Pure, there is the Oath of the Wolf which specifically omits this interpretation of that rule, allowing the Pure to unabashedly murder the Forsaken. So then why are there any forsaken left? They don't have the awesome spirit totems of the Pure, they spend all their time fighting against all the litany of antagonists introduced, and in general there are just fewer Forsaken. There's also references in 2e to 'skirmishes' with the Pure. If the Pure see no issue in killing the Forsaken, why are these skirmishes not resulting in deaths all the time? And how do the Forsaken continue to justify their believe in the Oath of the Moon protecting the pure?
                            First, as you can see, their Oath doesn't allow them to unabashedly murder the Forsaken. The Pure, p33. 'In seeing the Forsaken as lesser, the Pure theoretically believe it’s perfectly acceptable to murder the Forsaken. Not just to kill in the heat of battle — but to outright execute them. (Note that this still constitutes a Harmony violation. The Oath to Urfarah and sins against nature are not in accord.) In fact, some grudgingly accept that murdering Forsaken is the only way that Pangaea and Father Wolf will be allowed to return. Still, killing Forsaken rattles many Pure, whether or not they admit it out loud.'

                            Second, every werewolf is naturally borne with an auspice. It would be incredibly difficult to wipe out the Forsaken just by that basis, because if you kill every Forsaken in North America, there's going to be two going through their First Change the next day, and three more elsewhere across the world. It's impossible to tell when someone might change, who might change, and be there for every change that occurs before they become Forsaken.

                            Third, the Forsaken have been decimated. There's something called the Brethren War, just before the 'modern day' in first edition where the Pure have wiped out most of North America's Forsaken. That was the situation the players were starting in. But in a number of other places it explains that the Brethren War is a two-sided, cyclical thing. At some point the Pure gets too comfortable, and starts weakening themselves with infighting, the Fire-Touched never leaving the Hisil, the Predator Kings never leaving the wilds, and the Ivory Claws too arrogant to see anything happening to them. Which is when the Forsaken, organized and having to train and fight tooth and claw just to survive against a superior number, cuts them down. Then the Forsaken get fat and happy, telling their pups the Pure are just an old threat they've already taken care of and nothing to worry about anymore. Maybe the Blood Talons are going to point out that the Pure are gaining numbers, angrily, in the dark corners of the world where the Forsaken don't abide, but the Forsaken have other things to deal with. And the cycle continues.

                            Fourth, while this existed in first edition I don't think it's been as big an issue as it is now. There's other factions. Ghost Wolves can have lodges and faux protectorates, strange groups like the Cull and Lodge of the Field exist and would rabidly defend their right to in the face of the Pure coming to convert them for not having abandoned their auspice. And the Bale Hounds are always there to corrupt and sabotage any endeavor either side pursues.

                            Fifth and finally, for this post, though I'm sure there's other reasons, it's just a matter of organization. It would be ridiculously hard to root out a group like the Forsaken, or the Pure across the world in their entirety. You would have to know where every one of the enemy is, while they have the capability to escape to other worlds and use magic to conceal themselves. These are both groups that have existed for thousands of years, being hunted by each other, spirits, and other supernatural beings. They're all very good hunters, but they're all very good at surviving, too. And tribes maintain some intra-tribal communication, but not usually to that a high level of organization, no one really has a database of peoples names and packs and totems. Once you get past Packs and Lodges, everyone is pretty much scattered and unaligned in mindset with others beyond what brought them into the tribe.

                            I know that's a lot of questions, but I'm just so confused. At this point I really want my Pure to be a bit more sympathetic, more of the "different interpretation" that is so common in nWoD than the unrelatable "evil guys". What do you guys think of having the Pure have the same debate about their oath of the wolf as to whether it applies to Forsaken? Thanks for your input, the forum and community here are truly amazing!
                            I think it would be hard to justify, as is, Pure arguing that they shouldn't kill the Forsaken, full stop. Some people might just need to get killed. It would be a good point for people to bring up that the Pure are created via recruitment, however, 'Why should we kill them when we can convert them?' is a valid argument.
