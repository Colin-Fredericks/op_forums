                    I think the problem with Zap's statement is not that the vast majority of romance literature (in the USA) is bought by women. That's fact (though a quick side rant, it is a culturally reinforced piece of bullshit because it attempts to enforce a gendered divide by putting "male" consumption of erotic material in the category of porn and "female" consumption of erotic material into romance literature; thereby completely skewing any relevance this might have on if there's any real gendered difference in the amount consumption of erotica and reinforcing negative gendered stereotypes).

                    The problem is then generalizing that back onto what all women like (" If women didn't like this kind of thing you would think things like 50 shades of grey wouldn't be bestsellers."). Just because most of the consumers of a product are women doesn't mean that most women like that product. All it means is that there's an audience amongst women than enjoy reading books that contain these elements.

                    As for the article in the OP:

                    I think the problem is that "werewolf" becomes a cover for not being responsible with "edgy" material. Rather than writing fiction that either (a) establishes a healthy power dynamic between a couple that includes the altered psychology of being a werewolf or (b) deals with the unhealthy power dynamic being romanticized as being ultimately unhealthy, there is a growing acceptance of just tagging a character as a werewolf and lazily ignoring having to deal with pesky things like characterization and relationship dynamics.

                    Basically, there is a crop of urban fantasy romance literature that uses "werewolf" to disguise abusive relationships. Is there an audience for that? Sure. But that audience would probably be just as happy to consume erotica that does a better job of dealing with these topics.
