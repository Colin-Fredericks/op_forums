    Idigam Chronicle themes and possibilities question

    02-22-2015, 12:10 PM
    So, admittedly not that familiar with WtF in general but would be interested in getting the Idigam Chronicles when it comes out to expand a bit on possible plays with the Werewolf theme. The thing is though that "spirit cops" theme doesn't sound that interesting to me personally especially since it, in a way, is already present in WtA with which I have plenty of experience. So I am asking you the fans of WtF here, and especially those who are familiar with both WtF and WtA, about the other themes especially those separating it from Apocalypse.

    I would be most interested in the possibility of portraying Lycanthropy as more of a Curse than a calling to become a spirit cop, or a warrior of Gaia/Wyrm as was the case with WtA, and Idigam Chronicle, or 2nd ed WtF did it by the way have a project name like Blood & Smoke had other than the Idigam Chronicle, seem to have at least some of that Curse thought present in that one can become Wolf Blooded through bite or encounter with a Werewolf but does this theme go further? Can one use this upcoming version of Werewolf to portray them as more Cursed lineages that struggle with their bestial sides and perhaps an instinct to hunt, rather than some legacy of spirit guardianship bestowed upon them. I guess, from the Idigam being front and center here as antagonists, that it would be hard to separate them completely from any spirit world stuff, a clear legacy of WtA, and focus completely on the Curse aspect at least without some serious re-writing of the whole setting. But keeping the spirit side could it be played as I suggested without too heavy modifications?

    And in general what other themes you feel are most important in it besides the Spirit Cop thing which comes up a lot, so much in fact that cop shows are recommended over Werewolf films as inspiration on other threads here.

    I am asking this because I would be interested to get a fresh look at the Werewolf mythos but considering how many good books Onyx Path is spitting out currently, and I still have a few that are already out that I should get as well, I need to pick and choose which ones to get and which ones to leave.

      Think less spirit cop, more half-spirit. Your inner wolf spirit longs to roam the shadow, to hunt and to claim a territory of its own. Despite your fleshy human body, you can't deny this instinct. Oh you can try, but eventually the wolf will find its way out, and when that happens it won't be pretty if you've denied it for so long. Those tragic "cursed" werewolves who massacre their own villiages are the ones who try to deny what they are. The only way to prevent that is to embrace the wolf. But be careful too not to lose yourself to it, lest you start to fade away and become not but a spirit, devoid of any thought save the hunt. It's about maintaining harmony within yourself. Yes, guarding your territory is part of that, but that makes you no more a spirit cop than a wolf is an animal cop for fending off other predators from its hunting grounds.

                                  The theme of the first edition was never "spirit cops". It was savage fury. I don't know how it's been run in other tables, but my characters had to witness an entire world that wanted to see them dead, skinned and eaten (and if you were very, very lucky, in that order). It's the outrage building up? How about when the lesser predators, which aren't normally a rival to you pick on your (oblivious) loved ones and wear their skin like sock puppets, sometimes devouring their brain or hearts in the process? How about when a far numerical superior fanatical, inhumane cult of your same species comes down howling for your blood for the alleged sins of your forebears, after you swore to at least two spirit Gods that you wouldn't murder your spiritual kin?

                                  Cops must answer to due process. The Forsaken only have to uphold a nebulous oath which they themselves will never reach an agreement over it's interpretation.

                                  Cops don't make the laws. Forsaken are the masters of their territories, if they manage to bend the spirit wilds to their wills.

                                  Sometimes perps slip thru the cracks on account of technicalities. Your prey won't have a lawyer ensuring a fair trial as it's thorn apart by your fangs and claws.

                                  I guess that the only question that really matters is... When will you rage?

                                          It's also about the horror of the First Change at first, an experience that usually ends with the werewolf tearing apart people close to him. After that, as werewolves begin to settle into their lives as Forsaken (if they're lucky enough - many aren't), there's plenty of horror in the dawning understanding of the worldview upon the world of Werewolf: the Forsaken is based - a nightmare of the real world in which the aphorism "might makes right", that's pretty much a reality in the world we live in, is taken to its logical conclusion; in which humans have no power and no promise of safety at any time, and in which this thing people call a "life" is just a product of incredible chance in the face of impossible adversity of powerful forces to which humans serve no purpose other than as discardable energy source and mobile transportation.

                                          And in addition to that, there's getting used to the werewolf condition, the rage, the territorial urges - towards people, even. You would think people have it bad when Shadow things take over. Werewolves take the way vampires objectify people to a different level, trading and fighting over wolf-blooded as breeding stock the way people would barter over dogs. And that's the protagonists of the setting.

                                          Once the PCs have made contact with the Pure - or maybe a lot sooner, all it takes it to meet Forsaken werewolves of certain lodges - the Storyteller gets to introduce the horror of mindsets as extreme as to put one's dogma over one's own life and that of other people. The Pure have a Plan, and there's nothing they wouldn't do to achieve it, and no sacrifice too great and no atrocity too revolting. And there are plenty of people like that in the real world, people who put their extreme beliefs ahead of their own existence - suicide bombers and the like; but that doesn't make the Pure's actions less of a mockery of the self-sacrifice of the Forsaken.

                                          There is a more direct sort of horror in Werewolf. I've never been hunted by my life before, but there have been a one or two situations in which people chased me through the streets, wanting to beat me up. And it's worse than being in a fistfight, because those are over soon. Being chased can go on and on .. and this is an immovable part of life as a Forsaken werewolf. Their enemies are legion. Werewolves don't get to retire from their life and say "screw this, I'm doing something else." The Hunt is all they will and can ever have.

                                          All that remains, then, is to introduce the Bale Hounds, werewolves sworn to the darkest of spirits - a pledge that should begin to make perfect sense and seem like the only logical option left, because who doesn't like to be on the winning team?

                                              Something else of note (while I'm thinking clearly and have more than 4 hours of sleep) is the religious ideologies of the tribes. The Tribes of the moon and the pure (and the Bale hounds for that matter) are separated less by blood and more by religious beliefs of the world. Everything can be a truth or a lie. All spirits lie. Every story starts with "this story is true." While the tribes as described in 1e have a decidedly apocalypse feel, they are fundamentally different. They are still tribes as tribes can be described, and that is not to say that there isn't still full blood lines of werewolves and wolf blooded, but the basis for their tribes come down to beliefs and personal mantras over blood lines and duties.
