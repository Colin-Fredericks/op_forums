                        What I like most about this write up is the synergy between their Renown, their Ban,and their Prey. Disregarding the fact that a spirit can't help their ban, let's work through this(and I admit, I might not be playing Uratha definitions of words). If all the world were more honorable, there wouldn't be a need a to hide weakness, since people would honorably pit strength against strength, or at least their own weakness against weakness. THe world isn't like that though, and so in order to make it through one must hide their weakness and allow no one to see it, all the while striving to set the example and be the better person. The most abominable thing to such a people, then, would be those who exploited weakness cruelly for their own benefit, and who does that the most? Werewolves, spirits, shartha, and humanity have nothing on those who have Ridden or Claimed people in their moments of weakness and took them for everything in that moment. It's an affront to the ideal, and woe to anyone who thinks they can violate Honor so blatantly. Of course, the danger with dealing with that is that you yourself could be Ridden or Claimed, so the only way to be safe is to never show weakness, at any time.

                        Least, that's how I see it. It's a little more noble and idealistic than I think the Storm Lords would see it, but then again there was a time we all wanted to be heroes, and we never let go of those times as easily as we think.

                                    That looks like a lot of "ifs" to me. Take this bit.

                                    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                    The Claimed and Ridden go to extremes in ways werewolves,spirits, sharthas, and humans can't even really fully imagine. It takes endurance on all fronts to withstand those extremes and still be able to 'strike' when it's all over.
                                    That's only true if the Claimed has the abilities to back that emotion. If the werewolf backs it into a corner and the worst it can do is fire a pistol...

                                    And a long and tiring ritual. That's only true if you're not comfortable with just ending it in a quick swipe of the claws.


                                    You're looking at the worst case scenario; the best case scenario is that a Werewolf sees a Claimed, recognises the physical transformation (or has a useful Gift) and ends it in a quick bite.

                                    And that's the crux of my point. You're not using anything inherent or common the Ridden/Claimed. You're relying on individuals who act in a certain way; but by that logic even Hunters could be an Endurance foe - they have the same exterminate and desperation, their core Risking Willpower mechanic depends on it - and a hidden mastermind who sends minions after the pack could require as much stake outs and investigation as any Claimed. You can make an individual of any splat fit any kind of antagonistic role.

                                    Compare the Ridden/Claimed to Mask slashers - yes Masks are a bit of a one note, but that one note makes high Stamina mechanically optimum for 1v1 combat. There's no point increasing your damage but you can get a lot of advantages from extra health since that lets you last long enough to gradually wear the Mask down; and of course it feels like Endurance to have a character get stabbed in the gut dozens of times but keep fighting until the Mask falls.


                                    But while it's a universal trait to Masks that high Stamina is more useful than high strength, there's no equivalent to Claimed. Nothing common to all of them that makes high Endurance the superior strategy. (And if there was; it's certainly not highlighted in the write up)

                                                            Something I like about the Storm Lords from the player's side of things is that there is such an untenable series of factors you need to meet to attain the 'ideal' situation and be the perfect stereotypical Imnir. You need to be cloaked in ice and cold? That will be tough if your game is set in Florida - except that you can interpret the dogged stamina to withstand the heat as being your way of following Winter Wolf. You need to be the stubborn, prickly one who goes it alone and expects others to follow? That has to be reinterpreted if you are playing pack beta or omega - or if you are playing the team's social wolf? Now you have to interpret moderating your team's emotions and being an effective, maybe even 'friendly' face to the outside. What if you want to be a ranged specialist, or a were which is clearly not in their prime, whether disfigured, injured, too young, too old? An imnir who has a history or who has failed before? Now, for you, showing no weakness is taking pride in withstanding the disappointment of others, being true to yourself despite others' ignorance of what it *really* means to be Imnir. In short, because not every game is going to make you play a cookie cutter, five dots in stamina composure resolve whatever, there is an enormous space for personal drama and struggle and horror in the wheelhouse of 'being at the top of my game' for the Imnir. Just my two cents, thought it might be helpful to the posters who keep asking about being pigeonholed into 'hunting ridden' or 'being cold' and feeling hobbled: if you feel bothered by that, it should be a clear message to your ST to help you see a more flexible and uncertain world that you can be a little different in.
