                                From everything I've read, it's a lot less, "experts are still arguing over," and a lot more, "experts are frustrated because social taboos are blocking research." What little evidence there is points to large scale interbreeding (not intermarriage, which implies both partners maintained their ethnic identify, as many Ainu were historically forced/coerced into giving up their culture so their children could be raised Wajin and persecution free) due to unique genotypes only found in the Ainu people showing up in people that identify as purely Wajin, but there's not enough sample size to push it forward; and it's hard to get a sample size when it amounts to, "take a test to prove you're a member of a persecuted minority." The experts all want to prove their various positions, but the mainstream society is very clearly much happier not knowing.

                                Keep in mind we're talking about ethnic groups that coexisted in the Japanese islands for thousands of years before concepts of racial purity among the Wajin took root and large scale ethnic and cultural erasure took place.

                                        Originally posted by Heavy Arms View Post
                                        I think there's a few factors at play here.

                                        From a genetic perspective, you can make a solid claim that most people in Japan are of aboriginal ancestry, with central Japan having the least diversity/highest proportion of mainland genes, and the north and south of Japan having the highest levels of genetically observable mixed ancestry. The number of people that actively identify as Ainu is incredibly small, but the number of people in Japan that have Ainu (or at least Jomon) ancestry is very large. To give a comparison to a country that has a bit less cultural baggage on being identified as being of mixed heritage (and thus has much better demographic data) in Mexico, only 7% of the population actively identify as members of one of the indigenous peoples, but 83% of the population are mixed Spanish/indigenous ethnic combinations. Saying that the majority of Mexican werewolves have indigenous ancestry is practically redundant.

                                        Much like Mexicans are a unique people born of the interbreeding and cultural sharing of indigenous populations with European colonists, the Japanese people (genetically, I'm aware that culturally this is a position many Japanese people reject) are a unique people born of the interbreeding of indigenous populations with colonists from the Korean peninsula. Japan has had a long history of aboriginal erasure, and taboo against mixed ancestry individuals, making it extremely hard to tell exactly how genetically mixed the population really is. It could very well be that the explanation is as simple as most werewolves in Japan of are Ainu descent because most people in the islands share that ancestry on some level.

                                        Tokyo is also one of the places with the largest Ainu revival communities outside of Hokkaido (which the text highlights). Besides all of the cultural issues they have to overcome in trying to reclaim their cultural identity, they also have to content with a completely unknown factor that most potential members were raised as purely Japanese with zero idea that they have relatively recent Ainu ancestors.

                                        To me, while my knowledge of all this is extremely academic, the idea mostly comes off as playing with Japanese cultural taboos. The Ainu being associated with werewolves (with people that seem to be completely ethnically Japanese being werewolves and getting dubbed Ainu for it being a thing) makes sense when you consider that the stereotypes of the Ainu by mainstream Japanese culture has a lot of overlap with how Uratha end up operating (the Ainu are stereotyped as being more "wild" and physical, as backwards hunters, and the whole "hairy Ainu" thing combined with the Dalu form).
                                        This is all pretty well-said. And closely lined up with my thinking on the matter.

                                        Also, I don't know how explicit I made it, but my thought was that there WERE other Japanese werewolves (I think I mention that in Dark Eras, in fact). But for some reason, they just... stopped happening. Like with literal Japanese Wolves, they stopped being a thing.

                                        I don't look at it as if the Ainu are "magical". Quite the opposite. People all over the world become Uratha. Japanese people not of Ainu heritage tend to not become Uratha. Ainu aren't significantly more likely to become Uratha - but when they're the only ones in the area that consistently do, it perhaps looks like it.
