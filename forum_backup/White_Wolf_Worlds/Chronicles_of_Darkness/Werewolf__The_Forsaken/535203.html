    2nd ed game play question

    06-01-2015, 12:48 AM
    Last week I set aside money and, with great excitement, made the jump to 2 ed. I have been getting ready to play in my first WtF game in nearly ten years. I have run many games, but no one else ever wanted to tackle being the GM in my group till now. I can't explain how excited I am to play a character I have had in my head for over five years.

    Looking at 2nd edition I really liked the changes, until I got to the gifts and facets. Don't get me wrong, I like the idea and many of them are great, but it looks like Irraka and Rahu have a huge leg up compared to everyone else. It appears that the Cunning and Purity facets are among the best for almost every list as I was looking. I understand that these two Auspices are the most battle centered but it is a little discouraging if you want to play something else. Almost every facet I was looking at tied to Wisdom matched a one or two dot gift in the original book.

    Most noticeably it appears that the shaman type of play that was available to Ithaeur has been eliminated completely. The Elemental and Weather gifts just do not have the same power that they previously had and now with the new limitation on the facets what you do have is based on what Renown you are working with, and again, Wisdom seems to get little help in combat situations.

    I know that the game is not all combat, but if you are playing a tabletop game then you know that even combat light sessions are going to have a focus on the fights, that is one of the reasons we play after all. My question for those who have been playing, how balanced does it come out when all is said and done?

    I have wanted to play a shaman caster Ithaeur for years but after looking at what this book has to offer I just don't see that it can be done anymore. I am also concerned after looking at Killer Instinct and the rest of the Full Moon's Gift set that if the Rahu in the party decides to do something, no one can stop her. Yeah, Rahu are suppose to be battle focused but that one dot just seems crazy.

    I am feeling really discouraged and was hoping some of you could give me insights on how the game play has been going in 2nd edition.

    ​Thanks!

      If you're looking to play a blaster mage Werewolf I think the book may disappoint you. I don't think that's a major part of the idea of what a werewolf can do in this game. If you do want to play a kind of Ithaeur Shaman, however, you will probably have to rely on the Tribal Affinities. Personally, I think the Insight and Knowledge Gifts are exceptionally useful. The Nature gifts are really cool for a Protector of the Wilds sort of Shaman. I'm not sure if Beast Ride works on human beings, though I don't think it specifically says no, and that would be a pretty cool effect.

      If you go Iron Master, the Technology gifts make for a cool Technomancer type character (and the Shaping gifts, especially Unmake, don't hurt).

      The Death Gift of course is fantastic across the board if you're going for that kind of character. Sort of a traditional Witch Doctor type Shaman. Very thematic. Absorbing damage from packmates. Getting answers from corpses. Ability to see into twilight and damage the undead. I think the Purity facet is the only one in that Gift that isn't exceptional.

      And I'm very surprised to hear you say that the Weather gifts aren't as strong. I would beg to differ. The 1E Weather gifts were useless until the 4th and 5th dots, and in my opinion the Elemental gift is really strong once you get around 4 and 5 renown, especially if you are creative enough. As strong as anything from 1E. You really do become a blaster mage at that point.

      Your argument about the Rahu is correct in some ways. No one is supposed to stop a Rahu physically except another Rahu. And maybe an Irraka who plays his cards right. But those are their roles. The Rahu is going to outshine his packmates when it comes down to hitting things hardest, but that's his area. Everyone has their area. Your Ithaeur is still a werewolf and the pack is working together, not competing against each other. And there are so many ways for the Ithaeur to shine. The Iron Master Ithaeur in my group already Unmade a sports car as our prey was escaping in it, dismantling it completely. Used Iron Slave to send a truck barreling through the police station to break our Irraka out of jail, and used a rite to erase all video evidence of a giant Uratha vs. Azlu brawl that happened (partially) in a Bank parking lot. If that isn't cool, I don't know what is.

      Also keep in mind that Purity is the Rahu's auspice renown, but it's not his alone. If you think the Purity renown is strongest, your Ithaeur, as a Shaman, will probably have more than a couple dots of renown there anyway.

                              I think it's neat how different people see different renown as 'the best,' and how they rate the power of assorted gifts.

                              The Elemental and Weather gifts just do not have the same power that they previously had and now with the new limitation on the facets what you do have is based on what Renown you are working with
                              First edition had more limitations, as you couldn't have a Gift worth more dots than your highest primary Renown. So you couldn't get those 4 and 5 dot gifts until you had your 4 and 5 dots in a primary Renown.

                              The Elemental Gifts are pretty much the same as they were, except as I pointed out, easier to get and with Catastrophe. The mechanics for the first edition gifts were really ambiguous, as it suggested you could use any element in any gifts spot, but it didn't offer a good example for what, for instance, the fire element version of 'Invoke the Wind's Wrath' was. Now they offer a straight Influence, allowing you to do your basic damage if you want, as well as many utilitarian usages. We've had at least one thread exploring the things one can do with the elemental gifts.

                              As for the Weather Gifts, you're right in that they aren't exactly, 'Hit a target and do extra damage,' they do carry the potential to essentially create natural disasters, and while I hate this term, it's great for debuffs. Hunt of Fire and Ice, the Wisdom facet, can just destroy people outside, and you can ensure people can't find a way inside with the Cunning facet. Repeated use of Heavens Unleashed can have very serious results. For these you can just look at actual natural disasters and see what happens, right now there's massive flooding in Texas and a tremendous heat wave in India.

                              I wanted to play a Stormlord more blaster type, but now I am really going to need to tweak what I go with.
                              Or you can just go Elemental. Until 4 dots in Renown you need a source for fire, like a lighter, which you can turn into a blast of dragon's breath. But at 4 and 5 you can breathe fire yourself. From there you could just get a fetish like the Storm Coil to give you Influence of electricity in the same way you have it over fire.

                              With the fourth Crescent Gift, as well, you can just take a spirit's influences.

                              Again, I didn't make myself very clear in that I feel that the way packs usually choose their Alpha as the strongest that they have a huge leg up.
                              That's not necessarily how they choose their alpha.

                                          Thing worth pointing out from last year's Gifts thread:

                                          Originally posted by Acrozatarim View Post
                                          The second is a more general note for the thread - I got rid of what we termed 'Killing You Gifts'. Things like Thunderbolt and Killing Frost - powers that involve the werewolf pointing and throwing magic at a target to kill them, mage-style - are generally gone. Instead, the new Facets that focus on making the werewolf better at killing make them superior at doing so up close and personal, with claws and teeth and weapons. A werewolf doesn't call lightning down to strike you with a Facet; a Facet gives a werewolf the ability to tear through steel like it was butter, or to hack you apart like a joint of meat with each strike crippling you in a horrific new fashion.

                                                          Originally posted by Face_Plont View Post
                                                          I still worry about that one dot Full Moon gift that gives 8 again for one essence. That just seems absurd.
                                                          First of all, while 8-again may look sexy, mathematically it's actually less beneficial than you might think. Second, compare it to the other 1 dot Moon Gift Facets, though.

                                                          Crescent Moon gives you + Wisdom on Empathy rolls against spirits for a scene and reveals a spirit's Ban or Bane for 1 Essence (both on an exceptional success). That gives you a tremendous amount of leverage against the spirit for social maneuvering, and a huge advantage in combat against it. Plus you can spend additional Essence to learn the Bans and Banes of any spirit that may be present in the scene. Note that this is the kind of information you would normally have to go on entire quests to learn.

                                                          Gibbous Moon gives your whole pack +1 damage to all Brawl and Weaponry attacks for several turns for 1 Essence. Depending on the size of the pack, that will most likely add up to more total damage than the Rahu's 8-again will add.

                                                          Half-Moon gives you flawless lie detection for a full scene for 1 Essence.

                                                          New Moon gives you the Rote quality on a sneak attack for 1 Essence. Unlike 8-again, Rote quality is amazing, borderline broken in combat.
