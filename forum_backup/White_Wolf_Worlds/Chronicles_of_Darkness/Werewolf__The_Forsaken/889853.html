    Lodge of Arms

    05-13-2016, 10:05 AM
    Hi. Trying to recreate the Lodge of Arms for one of my games (also lodge of prophecy and lodge of voices). Here's what I have so far.

    I feel like the Siskur Dah benefit is a little lackluster, but I want it to be more interesting than a single bonus to weaponry or something. I mostly stole it from a 1e fetish (Lucky's Brother). The Forging Arms rite just confers the 1e benefit of the Lodge of Arms (+3 to rolls with one weapon, +1 to all others of it's type), which I thought was a bit too powerful to use as the Blessing or Siskur Dah benefit, but I could be wrong. The fluff would stay the same as in 1e. Same totem, etc.

    Blessing: A Lodge member adds 5 dice rather than 3 when spending Willpower to augment an attack roll against another weapon user.

    Aspiration: To defeat a worthy opponent (Weaponry/Firearms user with equivalent dots in Skill)

    Ban: A Lodge member can never refuse a challenge or duel issued by a weapon user.

    Sacred Hunt: All Disarm maneuvers automatically fail against an Uratha under the effects of the Lodge of Arms’ Siskur Dah.

    Further, the weapons of an Uratha under this effect always find their way back to their owner. If the owner loses track of weapon in battle (generally by throwing it at someone or sticking it into an enemy and letting go), a knife may slip out of the wound somehow. Others overlook the weapon, and so it remains to be reclaimed by its owner. The more time the Uratha has spent with a weapon, the more powerful this effect becomes. A dedicated, treasured, or long-held weapon finds some way to leave the battlefield with its owner, no matter what. Someone steps on it without noticing, flipping it into a backpack that the weapon’s wielder later grabs as she leaves, or something even more improbable may happen.


    Lodge of Arms Rite: Bond of Steel (••••)

    This is the central rite of the Lodge of Arms, a rite which bonds a spirit to a weapon and both to the lodge member who will carry the weapon for the rest of his life.
    Performing the Rite:
    The rite only takes place after days of preparation. Once all the elements are in place — the weapon built, a spirit captured and bound and the werewolf’s own spiritual aspect exhausted by days of endurance — the rite begins. Under the command of the Ritemaster, the new initiate carries out repeated sets of practiced forms with the weapon. They can be anything from a series of guards, parries and attacks with a blade to different ﬁring positions and target shots with a gun. As the new weapon-holder works, the lodge members who built the weapon and helped trap the spirits chant the words of the ritual with increasing volume and speed. The new initiate is expected to match these changes by increasing the speed and intensity of his exercises. This continues until the initiate is at the very point of exhaustion. Then, just before the young werewolf collapses, the ritemaster speaks the words of binding in the spirit tongue, and the spirits of the werewolf and blade momentarily fuse, infusing each other with new strength. Each of the participants in the rite, bar the initiate, spends a point of Essence to achieve this.This experience is often traumatic for both the initiate and the participants, causing them to lose control and frenzy. For that reason, all the participants in the rite must be experienced lodge members who can easily restrain the new lodge members without doing any serious permanent harm. Then the rite ends and the werewolf’s new partnership begins.

    Action: Extended (20 successes; each roll represents 1 minute)

    Cost: 1 Essence

    Roll Results
    Dramatic Failure: All successes are lost. The rite fails; if the subject also failed her Dexterity + Performance roll (see below), she cannot be inducted into the Lodge of Arms. If subject is already a member, she loses her membership in the Lodge.

    Failure: No successes are gained.

    Success: Successes are gained toward the total. If the total successes equal 30 or more, the rite is completed. The subject is successfully initiated into the lodge, and gains the requisite bond with her weapon. A lodge member gains a +3 modiﬁer to any attack roll using that weapon and that weapon only. Additionally, a lodge member may treat her Firearms or Weaponry Skill as being one level higher for all weapons of the same type.

    Exceptional Success: Successes are gained toward the total. If the total successes equal 35 or more, the rite is completed with an exceptional performance. All participants, including the subject, regain any spent points of Willpower from the invigorating experience.

    Suggested Modiﬁers
    The rite’s subject rolls Dexterity + Performance during the course of the rite to represent her own level of performance. Each success gained on this roll adds one die to the ritemaster’s roll. If the roll is failed, the ritemaster instead loses two dice from her roll to perform the rite; if the subject dramatically fails this roll, the rite fails utterly and she is forbidden from joining the lodge.

      I see what you mean with the Siskur-Dah blessing. Maybe keep the disarm protection, but factor in some sort of benefit to resist abilities that would make them unable to use their weapon, like things that cause Arm Wrack or paralysis? That would probably get tricky, too.

      Adding a Renown to your Weapon roll seems a little easy. Maybe the weapon gains a bit of the blessing of the predator's jaw? Where it gains some extra Lethal depending on your form, and acts as a supernatural weapon that goes through normal abilities that downgrade Lethal? I like the idea of potentially going into gauru or even Kuruth and still not letting go of your sword.

      Also I think the Rite is a bit much. I get where you're going with it, and it's being honest to the original, but it's a fairly expensive rite you might only use once, and even then likely only on other werewolves.
