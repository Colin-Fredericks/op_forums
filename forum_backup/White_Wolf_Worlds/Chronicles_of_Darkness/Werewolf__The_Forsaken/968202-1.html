    War Against the Pure. There's your End Game book.

    Not every game's ending revolves around reaching some kind of elder/arch-mastery/gentry level of power. Promethean's case, its becoming human. In Demon, its usually achieving Hell and becoming free from Compromises and the God-Machine's attention. In Mummy? Its breaking free from the cycle of servant-and-god in some way - by becoming a force of Fate, more human-like, or something else (or firmly entrenching yourself as a servant). In changeling... well, you want to avoid the "end game."

    For Werewolf? Its re-establishing Pangaea. That involves rising up against other wolves, getting one of the Totems (or yourself) to become the new Wolf-Prime, and fixing the borders between the worlds. A more stable balance. This kind of story is really covered by the WatP book.

    Now, I'm not saying its a subject that can't be revisited and revised, or established on a smaller scale that an individual pack can achieve, but that's the heart of the matter. We don't need a book to cover alpha wolves, because they still do the same thing as regular wolves. They dont' develop alien mindsets (at least, no more alien than anyone else with low Harmony).

            It really isn't. War Against the Pure is specifically about war, whether it's just simple organized brutality, or the Silver Crusade, a massive sacred hunt. It has very, very little to do with personal development. And while 'not every game's ending revolves around reaching some elder/arch-mastery/gentry level of power', werewolf's clearly does, as we see from Lycaon-Ur, Dorj Tserendjav, the Storm Lord Abni-Gur, and even Urazakh-Angir back in first edition. What they did, what they are, isn't even remotely covered in War Against the Pure.

            Werewolves are shapeshifters and half spirit. Everything about them screams 'becoming something else.' And considering the way Werewolf, and second edition in general, has broken away from traditional World of Darkness growth, there's no reason that 'something else' should just be 'a werewolf with bigger dicepools.'

                            That's basically covered by the freeform nature of Influences and the power of Gifts being pretty solid all around. One thing you don't do is go the route of giving one splat the powers of another. I mean, Mage has its issues I don't agree with so I'd really rather not see them pulled into Werewolf.

                            The simple way I go about it is to reintroduce Milestone Gifts with a modified way Interlocks and Ciphers work in Demon. You have all 5 Facets cut into you and its expressed through Renown in a way that's uniquely Werewolf is what we currently have. The Milestone Gift would be a capstone requiring all 5 and showing how your bond with a particular resonance reshapes you or the world around you. In game I'd probably say you undertake a quest involving a Rank 6+ spirit to gain the power, but all in all it seems like an elegant way to make something powerful, but uniquely tied to the story the player is trying to tell about your character.

                            Mind you, that's specifically for Shadow Gifts. Moon Milestone Gifts likely go with empowering your Auspice Abilities spectacularly. Although I'm the sort that'd require you to learn all 3 Moon Gift Sets for your Auspice and have a Greater Lune pop down and get all up in your business about it.

                            Wolf Gifts could probably go the same route as Shadow Gifts, but likely much more toned down since you're basically doing a form of meditation to find the power within yourself.

                                                            Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                                            They'd probably change their minds once they realize they can abuse Pangaean hearts to create demenses greater than that of any created through soulstone.
                                                            Which would be hard to figure out, since they've been effectively ejected from the universe and no longer exist except in the form of remnants that offer no such special advantage. You could sell t-shirts at the Convocation, though. 'Our Ancestors Knew Of The Pillars Of The World, And All I Got Was A Demesne.'

                                                            On to more pertinent matters, while I appreciate some people may like the idea of gaining Awakening-level control over their Influence, I don't think it really fits werewolf very well even at a bodhisattva level. The only great advantage in being able to Pattern, Unravel or Make the Hunt would be to not have to use the Sacred Hunt rite, but at that point you're just switching it out for something else. You could tag other Influences to it depending on how you lean but something more like Vampire's Blood Sorcery, except with 'spirit magic' would make more sense.

                                                            I think a major difference in the book would be its focus on the pack, rather than the lone wolf. While I do believe a lone werewolf should be represented in such an endgame book, for playing the game you would want to focus on a pack. And in this you have a different approach than you do with vampires and mages, who make those last steps alone and occasionally over the corpses of former coteries and cabals. A werewolf with Primal Urge 10 is devastating with the rules given. How much moreso a pack of werewolves at 8-10, with a suitably powerful totem that likely swells with such power it will have to start stepping away from the world, or risk destroying it. What are the Wolf-blooded of this pack? What are the humans?
