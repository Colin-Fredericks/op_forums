    Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post
    Because its a lie of a choice, is giving then the choice but then there is only 1 option that is not gonna cripple them.
    Only in the same sense that putting 4 of your starting Arrtibute dots into Strength, 1 into Decterity, and leaving Stamina at 1 is "a lie of a choice". Distributing character creation freebie points is always a tradeoff between specialization and generalization. If you want to build that redundancy into your pack so that you are never without someone who can do Sacred Hunt, you do so at the cost of having a greater variety of Rites available to your pack. If you choose to diversify your Rites, you do so at the risk of the person or people with Sacred Hunt being injured, captured, or killed. I call that a meaningful choice, because there are benefits and drawbacks to both options. If you, personally, don't think diversifying is ever worth the risk, that's your prerogative. In personally glad the option is available, because I, personally, think it is almost always worth the risk. Different strokes.

                                                Let's be clear here. Siskur-Dah is an important part of the 2nd ed Uratha experience, but it's not essential. That's why it's not automatic.

                                                Narratively, there are many, many stories that can be told without the sacred hunt. Using Wyrd's example, you could lose the only ritemaster who knew the rite and explore the difficulties that befall the pack. You could play a group of young Uratha taken under the wings of an experienced pack, so the elders call the sacred hunt and lead the younger pack. You could have the pack all invest in an ancient mentor who calls the Siskur-Dah and points the pack at his enemies. Eventually the pack realises how they've been used and struggle to break their bonds and redeem themselves.

                                                None of these games are possible if Siskur-Dah is automatic to every character. If it was, you'd have to house rule to take it away. There's your character tax.

                                                But let's look at mechanics. If you go beyond your Primal Urge Hunt Time and don't have access to Siskur-Dah, all that happens is you lose a point of Essence per day and suffer a breaking point towards Flesh. That's it. You don't go insane, or wither away and lose health levels until you die. One point of Essence, one breaking point towards Flesh. That's practically nothing. Go eat a human for the Essence and woah, you've counter-balanced the breaking point as well. (Yes, I know breaking points aren't auto shift, but you get the point.)

                                                The point is, no matter how much Wyrd oversells the absolute dire need for Siskur-Dah by every werewolf, the penalties for not having it are so small that it's in no way a requirement. It's designed to be an irritant, but it's not insurmountable by any stretch of the imagination.

                                                Is it preferable for a pack to have access to the rite? Sure. Does it make a werewolf's life more difficult by not having it? Sure. That's drama. Drama makes the game.

                                                Indeed, the default assumption is that a pack will take Siskur-Dah with their free rite dots. It's even assumed several members of the pack will take it so they can benefit from various different hunt bonuses.

                                                But stop pretending werewolves can't live without Siskur-Dah.

                                                        This is one of the strangest threads I've seen in a while.

                                                        On Sacred Hunt as a rite rather than an innate power:
                                                        ​ - It's a rite because, metaphysically, in the setting, what the Sacred Hunt does is a rite.

                                                        On giving the Sacred Hunt rite for free:
                                                        - The game does that​. You start with 2 free dots, which you can spend on Sacred Hunt ​but you don't have to​. I find it bizarre that if we'd simply said 'you get the Sacred Hunt rite', we'd have avoided this entire issue but provided players with less flexibility and freedom for their character choices.

                                                        On making the Sacred Hunt a 1-dot rite:
                                                        ​ - It doesn't matter how many dots we made it, this argument would still be the same; the goalposts would just shift. Regardless, it's 2 dots because that's where what it does lies in the costing structure for rites.

                                                        On taking rites other than the Sacred Hunt with the free dots:
                                                        ​ - There is absolutely no issue with doing so in play or in setting. Seriously. I've run several games under the 2e ruleset and regularly had a mix of characters starting with the Sacred Hunt and those not. It works absolutely fine every time.

                                                        On the Sacred Hunt as a tax rite:
                                                        ​ - It's bloody well not. I spent a long time hewing out the admin rites from the game; Sacred Hunt not only ​does something meaningful​ but there are benefits to having multiple pack members with it; equally, not having it is not the end of the world. I'm not sure why there's such a focus on an ithaeur having it, given that an ithaeur is no better at it than anyone else. Regardless, if you're the one person in a pack who takes Sacred Hunt, you're not shouldering the 'rite tax' burden while your mates all get to pick cool rites. You're the one in the position of power​. You​ get to choose what the pack actually subjects to the Sacred Hunt and what isn't worthy. You​ get to push the focus onto your favoured prey, as it'll just be your Tribal benefit that the pack has access to.

                                                        ​And for characters who choose something else, that's absolutely fine. In-character as well as out of character. It's trivial to think up justifications as to why a pack would happily have a mix of knowledge.
