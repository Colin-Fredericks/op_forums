                    In terms of pure system and rules, I believe it's quite simply a better game than its first edition. There are things I don't like, but if you consider it in its entirety, it's better. Flaws in the rules of the first edition have been fixed. The power level of the protagonists is where it should be. So that's a big plus.

                    In terms of setting and its representation, it's different from its first edition, in ways I mostly don't like.

                    It's like this - the first edition of the game is a horror game. It's established right in the corebook that I have in front of me right now - the Forsaken are exactly that, Forsaken. There's a strong sense of desperation in the setting - the Forsaken have been outnumbered by the Pure ever since the Brethren War. The idigam are back. There's also plenty of elaboration of the downsides of being a werewolf - the rage, the many things werewolves are afraid of, especially about themselves.

                    The 2nd edition book doesn't spend too much time talking about that. The protagonists of the second edition don't have it that hard. There are gaps in the storytelling section of the book, a section that, in my opinion, should really address the downsides of the Forsaken condition, and, again in my opinion, fails to do so.

                    Examples:
                    The Iron Masters hunt people. Think about this for a moment. They. Hunt. People. I find that to be a fantastic, evocative, thematically appropriate idea! Except that the book doesn't spend any word count at all on elaborating on in, presenting it in any sort of context or how scary, frightening a notion this is.

                    Expanded packs - wolf-blooded and people can now be a part of packs. The reason I don't like it is because it destroys part of the sense of desperation and isolation of the setting. In the first edition, it's just you and the other werewolves you call a pack. There's noone else you can rely on. Human interaction is hard, people cannot ever understand what it's like to be Forsaken. This is part of the horror of the setting - you're alone, yet the Uratha must still cleave to the human, because you cannot help being drawn to the world you came from. In 2nd edition, there's less of a thematic space for this sort of dramatic tension, because you get to have people as part of your pack. The Forsaken are less Forsaken because of that.

                    I dislike the termination of the unihar as a concept for a similar reason - it decreases the Forsaken factor. In first edition, if you lie with your own kind, you beget a monster. If you lie with humans, your relationship is almost certainly doomed to fail. In second edition, all that is gone.

                    It's a matter of personal taste, obviously - but the implications of the unihar never bothered me. This is supposed to be a horror and drama game; and there's plenty of drama in the notion of people who cannot ever be together and intimate with the only people who are like them and who know what it's like.

                    And then there's the tone - I far too often catch myself thinking that 2nd edition presents its protagonists in a glorifying, celebratory light that didn't exist in the first edition. The book emphasizes the upsides of the werewolf condition in ways that make me uncomfortable at times. It shouldn't be great to be a werewolf. It shouldn't be great to experience the rage and the hunger for flesh and the desire to hunt. The first edition of the game doesn't suffer from this particular problem. Blood and Smoke, a game that I like better than the first edition of Vampire, really absolutely doesn't suffer from this particular problem.

                    It would be wrong to say that Werewolf: the Forsaken 2nd edition isn't well written, doesn't have great rules and isn't coherent in terms of its themes and mood. I must applaud the writers and the developer for their work - more Werewolf books are always good, and I hope there's more coming. Still, speaking for my own tastes, I prefer the setting of the first edition.

                                I think the horror element is still present in the setting, it's just not focused on in the writing as much.                                             You know Christian, given what I've read of 2e so far, I think you may have something of a point about the lack of gravitas on why the Uratha we play are called the Forsaken, and the over-glorification of the predator aspect. Maybe.

                                            This is, of course, highly subjective because.... horror is subjective. And in my opinion, there is a whole fantastic brand of horror in coming to the conclusion that the book, in many parts written semi-in character, glorifies what a normal person would look at as extremely disturbing. It doesn't lack horror because of its glorification. That glorification is what's horrifying.

                                            I have the impression what you might be describing is solely the horror from the perspective of an outsider. While a werewolf that just tore a person limb from limb would be loving life. And it's written as "So I just tore a person limb from limb. Goddamn I'm fucking alpha."

                                            Which is genuinely fucking terrifying. And that is actually a very well done form of "show, don't tell". The book doesn't speak to us about the horror. It shows it to us, by presenting the perspective of the werewolf. Instead of telling us what to feel, it allows us to make our own conclusions. In my opinion, it achieves its objectives quite nicely on that front.

                                            Now, of course, I'm not going to disagree that the book could have used more details and word count in certain aspects, such as each of the Prey, and overall situation of the Forsaken as often besieged from all sides by enemies. But it still does excellently with what it presents, and it manages to show everything vital to Werewolf The Forsaken in an appropriate manner. It's a tight and focused book that does what it set out to do, I would say.

                                                I have not finished it entirely, but here are some bullet points (in no particular order):
                                                • On the whole it seems like a significant improvement (coming from someone who is not an exceptionally big fan of WtF)
                                                • I am slightly disappointed by the drier writing style compared to VtR 2e. The prose in VtR 2e is a terrific joy to read and I often find myself going back to reread the first and second chapter just to enjoy it again. I do not expect to have this happen much with WtF 2e.
                                                • Related to the above: It sometimes seems to focus too much on the facts and too little on the feel, which seems odd considering how passionate Werewolves are.
                                                • Most of the new rules seem pretty sweet. With some others I would need some more time to mull them over.
                                                • New Gifts are actually cool and noteworthy.
                                                • Some contradictions/unclear points/mechanical hiccups are a bit jarring, like the detection immunity in Hishu raised in another thread.
                                                • Some very nice artwork, but somewhat uneven in later parts of the book (as has been said)


                                                As for Christian A's points raised above: As a bit of an outsider to WtF, I can certainly see where he is coming from. Things certainly do not feel as desperate and grim as in first edition. That being said, I am not sure that was not part of its Apocalypse DNA, which has now been purged. Unlike the Werewolves of Apocalypse, the Forsaken are not meant to eventually fail utterly. In all likelihood they will have to continue their hunt for all eternity (which might, in some way, be even worse), so there probably has to be some good with all the bad. Things like the extended pack make them seem more rooted in the World of Darkness as a whole.

                                                Of course, mood and theme are easily adjusted to one's particular tables, so it is an easy fix, whether one likes the new direction or not.
