    Outside of the lazy sidebar reinforcements of how bad and hateful etc (show, don't tell? They do more of the latter in that "but they're really nasty! Oooh oooh... be careful!" way). they are in The Pure, honestly their Tribal concepts make a whole lot of sense. The insular purity driven Ivory Claws, the feral human-hating Predator Kings, the Cult of Spirit (well in part) Fire Touched. I can readily see how they'd come about as a werewolf phenomenon in the game's context without even worrying about the Firstborn mythos. They seem like natural, extremist facets of how a werewolf culture might react when things go bad. Given they're half human and how lines get so readily and violently drawn there from philosophical and other outlooks, I like their inclusion, including as PCs.

    If they had a treatment, it should be on par with the Covenants in Blood and Smoke; otherwise they're just anecdotes without enough core content to make them very viable (the old Forsaken Core really didn't spend much time on them there either even as pure antagonists, no pun intended). If they're presented, there should be the basic tools to have them playable, otherwise they may as well be akin to Fallen Clan/Covenant listings, or a condensed section like the Jiang Shih reference to get -some- basic utility from them. Personally, I'd prefer a Covenant level treatment; if they're a major problem and a direct, common (if not most populous in North America) werewolf faction, they really should have a nice chunk of space put towards them. An overfocus on external threats via Idigam, Shartha, Spirits etc with a lack of it on them could pose problems all over again.

                        I'm pretty positive there will be approaches in Idigam Chronicles that will make me personally go "That, THAT, more of that" as a given; it's been a rare nWoD book lately that didn't. Then again, I'm not one for light paint strokes either; "But don't change it TOO much" is what crippled many of the earlier nWoD books, so my perspective is to go hog wild and see how far something can be taken. Blood and Smoke is like that; there's very little mechanically unchanged, shuffled around or re-addressed. Basic concepts are certainly still intact, but it's anything but a light brush stroke that was taken to it. The result? Many players who did not like or had no interest in Requiem praising it and being invested in the game.

                        If I have any real concerns right now for Idigam Chronicles, is that the brush strokes applied won't be heavy enough to draw in the people that otherwise were dissatisfied, -especially- with the often maligned statement of "It doesn't feel Werewolf enough". It's a common thing, and in part due to the presentation of werewolves having little to do with humans, which flies right in the face of popular culture notions; the very ones that draw people to the games to begin with. For people who don't want much changed... we have the old Forsaken already. We've got basic rules updates all over the forums. Hell, the folks that loved the W:tA nods (I didn't personally), the conversion book and W:tA 20th ed to cull from.

                        That's where I start to go "well shit...". Sometimes games work as presented for folks, sometimes they don't, it's unavoidable; but I'm very concerned that the changes we're getting are going to be too heavily predicated on concerns of alienating the old fan-base diehards instead of standing on it's own as just a damn good product, changes be damned. Call it a misplaced concern, as it may well be (and I acknowldge that); but after looking at some of the bulk of the more active posters (outside of myself, because I'm one stubborn fuck at times), the areas I've seen consistently presented as primary concern (too weak Gauru, cleaned up gifts, an intact renown system) would -not- in of themselves draw new players in to give the game another chance given my own experiences. Basically running Forsaken through a GMC stopgap... nothing would really change for the better in regards to drawing new players in, though it may satisfy some portions (and I emphasize, portions) of the existing fan base.

                        So lets sum it up this way: I'm really hoping it doesn't turn into a one step forward, another step back situation where the devs and writers hold back running wild in some areas with new edition because "it might alienate existing fans". If it's a quality, awesome product, it'll stand on its own, changes regardless. And to be blunt, the game could use more new fans. I don't see catering to people satisfied mostly with what is present as beneficial with a new edition if it holds back that running wild and testing of boundaries. Or brushing off their concerns when they're present throughout the community (I'm not saying you're doing this Strider, for clarity's sake). "Oh, but that wasn't present before, or is too big a change; I like it as it is overall, lets not fiddle with it heavily". I find few things more toxic to game development, and after 17 years of dealing with LARPS and TT's and rewrites and house rules and custom made this and that's among other things that I got tired of light touch approaches. Nothing is more disappointing than buying a new edition of a game, and getting very little new content, beyond some rules tweaks that could be summed up in a small document. It honestly makes some of us feel ripped off and conned that it just wasn't handed out as an errata or similar.

                        It's one things to keep them in mind, it's entirely another to work with them. There's a reason why there's actually something of a fairly strong divide among Forsaken fans on the game, and why consistent problems surface with new players who take a look at the game. I want that addressed far more than turning Gauru form into a damn tank, because that's honestly trivial by comparison. Make sense? My tone is likely entirely off here, or perhaps my meaning, but I'm not sure how better to articulate it. Keeping in mind of course, outside of being a stubborn fuck, I can be a cynical bastard too.

                                            I'd be very, very game for a fiction anthology myself; using a Classic WoD game as an example, the only reason I got into, and gave D:tF a chance was the novels for it. They were evocative, gave the game a lot of context and a means to look at what the game would look like in actual practice from a narrative stand point. I ended up loving D:tF as a result from that additional perspective.

                                            I fully agree with Darksider here; if possible an Anthology style collection of stories could go a long way to fleshing things out and making it easier to grasp. Sometimes a deeper exploration of the game's themes via that medium (rather than shorter chapter blurbs for the supplements, which I prefer short in the RPG books themselves) go a long, long way. I think it'd go a long way to help smoothing some of the rougher spots by seeing it in a narrative flow (the transition from Integrity to Harmony, things like that).

                                            Since I'm a critical bastard though, and taking the Strix Anthology (which I have and read through) as an example; it'd likely be best if it didn't exist in a game setting vacuum. The former was heavily predicated on being an existing fan of the game for the majority of the stories and knowing a lot of the ins and outs and nuances; as a new reader, a lot of this was lost on folks who didn't have that level of familiarity. If the anthology was more, I'm not sure the word I'm looking for is basic, but at least dealing with some more core-things, it could go a long way to drawing new players in along with ramping up additional interest in Idigam Chronicles. I'm a bit biased here, but I'd love to see a werewolf story or two by Greg Stolze (I've loved everything I've read by him regarding WW and Unknown Armies).

                                            I realize that such game novels aren't always popular (and to be blunt, it often has more to do with the quality of the writing than them being game setting novels; look at the popularity of the 40K and Shadowrun novels for a good example of how they can sell like hot cakes when consistent in quality), or even profitable, but... if there was room for it, I think quite a few people would gain a much clearer POV on W:tF with their inclusion.
