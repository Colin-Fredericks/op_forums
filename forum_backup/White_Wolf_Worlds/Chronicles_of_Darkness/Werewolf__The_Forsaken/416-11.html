    Originally posted by kaho View Post

    In lunacys case i think the argument of "in the dark ages was necessary" could be use it against it. In previous eras civilization were more spread and isolated so lets say you have a village of 30 people and a pack is doing their bussiness there. That pack would probably be careful as the tenet of the moon already enforce discretion and on top of that they have the protection of lunacy so to a pack to be discovered the should have to careless in front of the one human in said village with willpower high enough.
    I'm not advocating that it be kept as is. I think the willpower should be lowered or that there is more of a rolled aspect of it.

    And even disparate villages, and even more recent past still had levels of communication. In the past one Death Rage in a crowded area would be all it took for people to leave and talk about what they'd seen and where. Trade was a rather common and driving factor, and not isolated to faceless commerce like it is today. Armies frequently invaded different regions.

    Originally posted by kaho View Post
    I think that in previous era with the tenet of the moon + lunacy is a miracle modern world have any conception of a werewolf whatsoever. Vampires have masquerade enforcer because they lack an automatic failsafe as efficient as lunacy. Its a thing of internal logic of the setting, why enforce the furry masquerade (werewolves don have a name for the masquerade....why is that? ) as tenet when its magically protects itself.
    The thing is, every other group has more capabilities to enforce it other than "Oh if we go hide far enough in the woods, nobody will see us." Vampires have adept slavery and mind control mechanics, but they still have rules about not doing things in public. Werewolves DO NOT have these social talents so readily available, if you got rid of Lunacy, you would only have the rule. And that rule likely wouldn't get very far without the large scale social influence vampires exert over a city.

    Originally posted by kaho View Post
    I could buy on of the two extremes though. Either the furry masquerade is enforced because they lack lunacy or because of lunacy maintaining the furry masquerade is no longer necessary.
    I think BOTH are necessary, I just don't think Lunacy should be kept as is. It needs to be lessened so that conflict and memory are MORE likely to occur, I just don't think it should be thrown out entirely when, despite whatever you think werewolves are capable of, they don't have the large scale infrastructure capabilities by default to enforce a version of the masquerade like vampires do, nor the dubious lack of laws associated with conventional morality.

    Also you seem to be assuming that Harmony will remain as is.

                                            Well it could simply be handled by a new condition appropriate to constituting a breaking point for the human, I do think that Memory loss needs some place in the reaction. I feel that they need to have some mechanic associated with that, though I feel Lunacy as is now takes that too far. It is TOO difficult to overcome.

                                            I would think something more appropriate would be something that factors in additional bystanders, and is more actively rolled, something that is success or fail, rather than willpower based. Maybe composure or resolve alone, with a particular modifier.

                                            Because say if the breaking point occurs and the average human rolls, they're going to succeed and likely understand whats going on (Resolve+Composure+1-2). Its hard to just shoulder it on Integrity alone and expect it to simply be justified, just as its difficult to think that someone wouldn't have said something. I've always taken the sin primarily to mean "Don't tell anyone".

                                            Lunacy as is IS overkill, but I feel there needs to be something that fills much of the void and the integrity mechanics simply don't do that by themselves.

                                                Repeating this from the old forums; Give more attention to the non-hishu forms and the specie of the character.

                                                One of the things that has bothered me a lot when I'm reading book such as Tribes of the Moon, is that when you look at the NPC descriptions, none go beyond hishu. It is like what the character looks like in human form only matters and the other forms are just tools and how one looks in them doesn't factor into the game. And that to me feels like it is sidelining what the character actually is, the five forms are the characer and how they look in those forms should be noted because (obviously) the characters won't only be seen in their hishu. I do not mean that each of the five forms needs to be descriped, just hishu and urhan, so that the ST and the reader gets what the character looks in the between forms as well. Minor thing I know, but I think it adds that sense that the character is a wereWOLF, a shapeshifter and not a human shaman.

                                                Speaking of human shamans, some concepts in the books can as well seem a bit too distant from a werewolf. Written with the powers in mind, rather than where those powers come from. Example characters should feel like they are werewolves, not random magicians.
