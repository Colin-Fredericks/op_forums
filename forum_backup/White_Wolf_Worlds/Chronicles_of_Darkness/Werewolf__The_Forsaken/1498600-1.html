    Really, the big drawback of Garau is, as others have touched upon, it's complete lack of access to non-lethal options. If you need someone alive for some reason you're going to stick to one of the other forms, particularly Hishu and Dalu for tool access (rope, your choice of less lethal weapon, setting up some kind of trap...). Garau's purpose is to spread fear and death, it's either the finale of a hunt or a last ditch desperate attempt to scars others before taking another form and escape (and if you're using it for that something's go r horribly wrong).

    Note that 1e Garau was considered underpowered thanks to the Rage, which is why 2e upped it to ten seconds of murder (or at the absolute peak about a minute, but who actually hits Power Stat 10?). It's very intentionally not Crinos, and a smart werewolf defaults to Dalu or Urshal for most combat, but it's a useful tool for when you need to make the kill right now.

                So enemies are either pointless (no silver) or super deadly (with silver), there is no middle ground.
                Having played a fair bit of Forsaken 2e this was not my experience.

                Let's take for example the time our pack fought a pack of Pure. We caused the Pure pack to all death rage near the start of the combat (by murdering their totem). They all took Gauru form, and we mostly stayed in Dalu and Urshul form. We managed to wipe out the Pure pack without using any silver. How? By focusing on one enemy at a time. We used our gifts, positioning, and misdirection to make sure that most of the Pure were chasing after shadows and not able to bring their strength to bear. We then focused down one enemy before moving on to the next. A Gauru form Werewolf only heals their health track in lethal each turn - for most Werewolves this is going to be about 11 damage. Dealing 11 damage across 2-3 attacks in a turn is not very difficult. One thing we found useful was that you can make characters with lethal damage attack first in the round to fill up those 11 boxes. Any characters dealing bashing damage can then attack later in the same round and it will count as agg regardless. It took us about 2 rounds to take down each of the Pure using these tactics.

                Another tactic that was used against us by the ST on several occasions was grappling. A character who is built for grappling can get some very high dice pools, even compared to a Gauru. On more than one occasion when we took Gauru form or fell to kuruth, we ended up in a grapple which we lost, and the ST used the "Restrain" manoeuvre. As long as an enemy is able to maintain the grapple, this pretty much takes a Gauru out of the fight.

                Gauru are not invincible, and a well prepared enemy can deal with one pretty quickly. One of our pack nearly died in one of the first sessions because they got cocky about Gauru form and rounded a corner into 4 guys with automatic weapons. Smart Werewolf players will use all the tools at their disposal to separate, harry, and corner the prey. When they take Gauru form to make the kill the outcome should already be decided.

                                    While I wrote the combat essay because I've seen way too many threads that misunderstand combat in Chronicles to put up with it anymore, it is specifically the way some Forsaken fans just don't get it that drove me over the edge, and I think I'm actually grateful for the good excuse to thoroughly deconstruct what I commonly see wrong when it comes up.

                                    I do want that combat thread to be in the back of people's minds for this, but since it's a lot, I want to iterate two particular points here just for ground:

                                    1) No one gets into a fight just to have a fight, and the majority of the time, they don't want it to be a fight, so much as an overwhelming steamrolling action that would never switch into the system, or otherwise getting the fuck out alive.
                                    2) All of the above then becomes a matter of resource management, on all levels, and weighing the gamble of any and all expenditures against the net success.

                                    So with that said.

                                    Originally posted by Shadowdragon View Post
                                    I think my biggest problem with Gauru form is the healing. All a character has to do is shift up, clear all lethal damage (bashing is already something they can clear super fast), then shift back down.
                                    Gauru is the biggest reason things are perpetually misunderstood here in Forsakenland, and I keep seeing the argument about healing in particular, so let's break this down.

                                    1) Healing neither helps your achieve your goals nor allows you to delay someone else achieving your goals. Sure, it keeps up the basic survival instinct of anyone, but it's an imperfect trade-off even on that front, because of Death Rage, and we'll get into that in a bit.

                                    2) Your time in Gauru is possibly the most important resource you have for any strategic employment, because unlike some of the other resource where it can be easy to restore with creative or transgressive solutions, you have no way of extending your comfortable time in Gauru within a scene, and every turn not taking advantage of the way it turns things into shooting fish in a barrel (assuming you have made the barrel) is a turn that makes it bonfire where you need a welding torch, and not even on that burns anyone. Wasting those turns just to heal is not a good strategy, it's an indicator you've already fucking lost the battle and need to run the hell away, because.

                                    3) Any discussion about the healing that does not also factor in Rage is pretty well so much noise in the rain, because it's not thinking about how the action economy matters for matters of combat and violent solutions to problems. So, for a recap, shifting into Gauru, for any reason, means your next turn has a total of four possible actions, being A) kill the motherfucker in front of your, B) throw something to try and kill the motherfucker you see down the way (which in a grand majority of cases is just not going to be as efficient as a combo of C and A), C) move closer to a motherfucker you're about to kill, and D) shift down. All of those, with reflexive exceptions for D that a grand majority of the time is going to eat into your Essence resources wastefully (particularly if you're constantly shifting up and down in combat), are going to eat up your turn for doing things that are going to be conducive towards establishing your scenario control that lets you use Gauru as the shotgun for a barrel of fish, particularly because.

                                    4) Eating up your actions is already going to make you more of a target than you need to be, which is going to be important here in a bit, but making it worse is that, outside of the tightest of action economy management that still isn't all that helpful, everything about the way Rage emphasizes aggressive positioning is an overextension that puts you at greater risk unless you're in a position where it pays to just finish the follow through. It almost guarantees you're out in the open in some way when you DO manage to shift down, and with a whole turn for people to dump violence into you, that's not great.

                                    5) This one is subjective, but I would make the argument that any Storyteller who has players who shift into Gauru just to heal is well within their right to call for a Death Rage roll.

                                    Now, on that turn thing...

                                    It's impossible to keep damage on a werewolf unless it's aggravated.
                                    Not really.

                                    I think the thing that trips people up on this is the term "turn" and how it's used everywhere else versus in Chronicles, so to lay it out directly, Chronicles uses the term "turn" the same way other games might use "round". WHen you understand that, you get to see the problem-a round of people stabbing and/or shooting you (either decreasing your Defense every attack or never caring about it) racks up by the time it gets around to your turn, and frequently does so by converting around to Aggravated damage-and every point of Agg Damage makes that trip shorter. And because of that, it can be easy to fall into a trap of a sunk cost fallacy for survival purposes-because these people are stacking lethal into becoming aggravated damage, shifting down makes you more vulnerable to that lethal damage, so you have to stay in Gauru to survive, but in order to stay in that, you have to pursue aggressive action, which means you're likely to stay in a vulnerable location, which results in more damage. And smart opponents will get this and exploit to hell-and you don't get far in the world of Chronicles if you stay dumb.

                                    Gauru makes it easy to survive when you are breaking up the opposition in a way they'll prefer retreat against co-ordinated or overwhelming offense. It's a temptation to die outside of that circumstance.

                                    It creates the superman problem. Superman is impervious to all damage, so attacking him is pointless, unless you use the mcguffin (kryptonite/silver/whatever) which can put him down almost instantly.
                                    Heavy Arms has already done a good job deconstructing this, but I want to succinctly suspect that you have either never watched any of the Superman cartoons/shows/movies* ongoing, or you've managed to forget just how much Superman gets challenged in his own media all the god damn time, no Kryptonite required. I'm also going to suggest that you're making the same mistake with Superman that people often make with combat in Chronicles, in assuming the violence is for violence's own sake and not for the purpose of other purposes. The Uratha, by comparison, are much squishier. If the problem is that players cannot come up with enough conflicts to challenge their character, while Superman has the better part of a century being relevant because of all the ways he's been challenged, maybe the problem is where the players are focusing. There's a reason Zak "I dedicated Sucker Punch to my mom" Snyder was a terrible choice for DC movies.

                                    So enemies are either pointless (no silver) or super deadly (with silver), there is no middle ground.
                                    Or stabbed/shot a whole fucking bunch, which people are half as likely to do when someone erupts into a giant fuck you man-eating wolf as run the fuck away, and also, that run the fuck away instinct makes them less effective even in situation where people are not of the right mindset to kill a (unexpected) giant fuck you man-eating wolf, which is arguably a middle ground, but also-

                                    -any scenario that forces a player to consider Uratha as a viable answer just for survival is already a situation that is High Conflict that begs a question of no middle ground, because it should either be ....I dislike pointless, I'll suggest either negatively effortless, or super deadly. There is a mismanagement somewhere if this is not the case-and hey, we all learn, but by that, it pays to know where the fault lies.

                                    Werewolves in gauru form destroying everything around them?
                                    I'm obliged to point out how this hits on the problem with the physical violence of Primal Fear-you can Down and Dirty combat people you can fight as appropriate for Rage in Gauru, which is hilariously potent when players are able to group up people who make up a large number of tertiary damage actions, but kind of a waste when you're only using it to instantly take down one person.

                                    Actually they only have to attack an enemy, and only one that's still active. They never attack friends or innocents, and don't have to kill the enemy. If it acted more like a vampire's frenzy then maybe it would be ok.
                                    I think I've already done a good job addressing the way that first line is half the problem, so for the rest of it:

                                    The thing of the matter, regarding not attacking friends, innocents, and not even needing to kill an enemy, is that a lot of this is true when you've taken the time to make Gauru form usage the most efficient you can, but when you're using it irresponsibly and inefficiently, you increasingly turn it into a game of chicken with disaster. Again, if the opponent is hitting you hard enough to make using Gauru for survival a seemingly viable answer, then you're facing rounds where trying to win is just locking you into more and more damage and bleeding turns until you run into Death Rage, and then you run exactly into that problem.

                                    3-5 round being nothing? What game are you playing? I don't think I've ever had a combat go past 3-4 rounds. This isn't D&D, combat in CoD is fast. Simply evading and defending against a werewolf in gauru form for 5+ rounds is asking a hell of a lot, especially when you take some of the other bs werewolf combat gifts into account, and it makes for a long and boring combat if the enemy just dodges every round.
                                    I feel like you've arrived at conventional number of rounds from the opposite end of operation, and the thing that makes me think this is the assumption that CofD is fast. Combat in Chronicles can feel fast because actions are weighty and impactful, and the game encourages you to take out the majority of opposition outside of the combat subsystem-but that goes both ways, and ill-advised action can easily drag out a conflict. THe way Gauru limits your options for action makes it so that, unless you make it something that you can invest in and end a combat with, is pretty apparently easy to kite and manipulate around, and any opposition that has an exit strategy is likely to notice or take advantage of it. Even panicked opponents are likely to make someone relying on Gauru casually run into a prolonged game of attrition by scattering and panic attacking to build up damage, and any opponent who has experience with the Uratha can and will make long scale attrition a meaningful barrier of death to a pack's goals and interests. Combat in Chronicles is "fast" because it usually has things slant in favor of one side or the other, and the main incentive of those who don't have it is to leave, regroup, and have another go-but when it gets into the thick of it, Chronicles combat can be a rather long affair, particularly if the opposition knows how to make things work in their favor -and most of the Uratha's prey have had ages to learn.

                                    Maybe if the healing thing was toned down, or it cost essense every round the werewolf remained in gauru form, or a werewolf in gauru form had to all-out attack every round (no defense), or the duration was much lower, or it could only be used once per chapter and not per scene, or the gauru had to attack uncontrollably rather than being able to focus on only enemies, any of these things would make it so much more balanced. And I'm talking about balanced as in against the game in general and what other splats can do. I know CoD plays it loose when it comes to game balance, but there are some things that are just unfairly strong and/or completely unbalanced.
                                    Putting aside ideas for balance because I am suspicious of the lack of Chesterton's Fence going on, I feel like the need to frame Gauru's advantages in the context of Chronicles on the whole is rather revealing, because it points out an acknowledgement that Gauru is designed for the context of Forsaken and the sense that it somehow works there. Chronicles games have a wide disparity of power and forms-a crossover game can have people who throttle the heavens paired up with a guy who has a shotgun from that one time their cousin took them out shooting for their birthday- because the games are designed to work internally with itself first. There's a reason why crossover was held off as a major focus of the books for so long.

                                    Within the context of Forsaken, the Uratha's advantages, including Gauru, are weighted against opponents who have reasonable ways to challenge that toolset-the Claimed are vigorously tough and tend to have environmental advantages, the shartha play attrition better than anyone else, spirits are almost just as slippery, tend to have esoteric advantages that are hard to take advantage of, and have millenia of familiarity with the Uratha, other werewolf antagonists literally know the Forsaken's playbook. Humans are the most subject to an imbalance, but so much of their power is already rooted in the sociological power and impact, and the traits that make humanity the persistence predator they are in reality can come together to make their a very smart opponent against the Uratha when a storyteller is willing to remember those. Everything is appropriate for a game of Forsaken.

                                    Within the context of crossover, the overall imbalance is easily handled by remembering to pair threats to the scale of their impact on the larger picture of what's happening-it doesn't matter if one person can throttle the heavens and one just has a shotgun if someone needs to hold back the heavens while someone else stops the bad guys or gets the thing. The Avengers movies, for whatever else can be said of those, provide good examples of how to do that, particularly the first one where consideration was very heavily payed to the fact that one of them could fucking shoot out massive fuck you bolts of lightning but another one of them was a a spy more specialized for taking out small groups of opponents. In crosssover, the Forsaken are upper tier powerhouses, arguably the fourth place winner in the overall power scale behind mages, mummies, and demons, and as such planning for them means understanding them as controlling more of the scenario and tanking against significant threats to open up other actors to make the significant tipping action. But, as grouping them with those other ones points out, this is not a unique problem for the Forsaken, nor is it a deal breaker.

                                    There's a difference between being strong and being broken, and garu form is leaning pretty heavy on the later. I guess when it comes to dealing with werewolves I'll just have to give everyone kryptonite, sorry I mean silver weapons.
                                    The longest game I've ever run in Chronicle was a tier one game of Hunter: the Vigil, and I regard this as one of the most instructive experiences I've had with games, because figuring out tactics and strategies for how to fight monsters from a permanently uphill perspective means you really learn to think how handle problems. My players, two in number at the time, eventually took down a pack of werewolves without a single ounce of silver, and they did it by exploiting the limitations of the werewolves.

                                    Thinking through the problem, learning as much as you can, and planning around what you can is always going to be more lethal than a silver bullet.
