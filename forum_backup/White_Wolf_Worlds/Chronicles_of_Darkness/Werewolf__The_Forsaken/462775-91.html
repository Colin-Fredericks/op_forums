                Originally posted by guang View Post
                1. What is so "dark" about the Shadow? Seems like an innocuous enough ecosystem. What is it that makes spirits so desperate to leave, and so dangerous-feeling for visitors?
                Lack of Essence makes spirits want to leave. A source of anger is not always available, fires go out in the real world, seasons change, and sometimes spirits don't want to go dormant, sometimes they just want more, plus during the daytime Helios makes things oppressive and hard to go about your normal busniess. Visitors are a source of Essence, so it's like walking into a tiger den with a steak suit, alternatively you can be a new mode of travel as your soul is subdued and the spirit possesses you. As for what makes it 'dark' there's everything being an overt predator except the few things that aren't, which are manipulative enough to be slavers or just decide to wear you.

                2. It seems that there are swarms of spirits crossing into the physical realm, not counting riding and claiming and all that. Riders and Claimers have obviously crossed a line, becoming monstrous, but what about unattached spirits? Are they just hunted when they become annoying or when they trespass, or is there a line they too can cross in which they become something fundamentally unnatural that werewolves would feel gut-level revulsion for?
                Offhand I wouldn't say werewolves feel a gut-level revulsion to spirits outside of perhaps wound-spirits and maybe, maybe magath. Some spirits, even ones that Ride or Claim people, are left alone. But completely unattached spirits are unlikely, at least for a lengthy period of time, if only due to the fact that they need to attach themselves to something before they start bleeding Essence. If a spirit did decide to just jump across every day, eat some Essence, and jump back without actively harming people, it might point this out while werewolves are bearing down on it. Some might leave it alone, some might destroy it, some might make a bargain.

                Werewolves are individuals and have differing opinions about things and ways to handle them.

                                                            Originally posted by CoronaBlue View Post
                                                            Given that, I've got some questions relating to how my character does his duty:
                                                            No problem.

                                                            a. My werewolf is walking down the street, and approaches a spirit in Twilight (which means that the spirit is in the physical realm, but not manifested?);
                                                            Technically it's Manifested, just in Twilight Form. This is covered in the Manifestations section down on p286. Every spirit has the Twilight Form Manifestation.

                                                            can he just see it, regardless of form or senses being used?
                                                            Yes, unless it's hiding. P95, 'The werewolf does not need to focus in order to sense spirits in Twilight. Unless the spirit is intentionally trying to hide, Uratha sense spirits in Twilight as well as they would manifested.'

                                                            Can he do anything to it without it manifesting or some special ability being activated?
                                                            He could activate any sense abilities. An Ithaeur could use Shadow Gaze to learn the spirit's bane. A creature's bane will hurt them even in Twilight. Otherwise it's hard to hit things in Twilight, you generally need a special ability, yes.

                                                            Does the spirit instantly know he is a werewolf?[
                                                            Good question, I'd say no, offhand. Things in Twilight tend to notice when they're noticed, however, and that might get attention.

                                                            b. Same werewolf, same street, but this time he approaches a human who is being ridden (urged = influenced from across the Gauntlet, ridden = sort of possessed, but not fully claimed?); does he just know? If not, how does he go about verifying?
                                                            A Ridden has the spirit Fettered to someone or something, using the Fetter Manifestation. It's in Twilight in the real world and would be seen as the previous one, unless it made an attempt to hide. Urged seem to be a 'kind' of Ridden. The spirit is still outside, riding them. Claimed have them on the inside.

                                                            c. The werewolf approaches a werewolf/vampire/mage; does he just know what they are? Do the latter two know what he is?
                                                            Miles may vary. I'm pretty sure werewolves recognize werewolves. Vampires can be pretty distinctive unless they're using Blush of Life. They also tend to have a lot of identifiers a perceptive werewolf might note, for instance, their 'breath' smells of blood. But some werewolves might not know vampires exist and think they're some sort of zombie or revenant or host. I generally have werewolves notice vampires unless they go to great lengths to hide themselves, and vampires tend to notice werewolves as another predator but not necessarily what unless they have experience with werewolves. The vampire book says this on p92, 'When Kindred notice werewolves, they feel physiological responses they never otherwise experience, such as goose pimples along their arms, and chills down their backs. They feel the adrenaline surge in the pit of the stomach that humans associate with danger and tense excitement.' Mages look like normal people, I don't think they'd just recognize one off the bat. It's worth noting that there are Gifts werewolves have and spells Mages do that would let them identify or note something off about someone.

                                                            But you can run it with everyone recognizing everyone else or not recognizing everyone else without the game being broken.

                                                            a. Something is causing trouble in my werewolf's territory; how does he find out about it? Without gifts/rites, does he just have to patrol around and ask questions?
                                                            Yes. Gifts and rites help, but if you don't have any of those, you just have enhanced senses and socializing. If you're on better term with spirits, some of them would likely have noticed trouble being caused. Otherwise you'd look to the human world, with the help of the werewolf's immediate and extended pack.

                                                            b. The thing is an Azlu/Beshilu; how does the werewolf find out? These enemies being what they are, I don't imagine they will do anything overt unless they have no choice.
                                                            They can generally only stay riding a human-looking body for so long before it starts mutating into a spider-monster or rat-man. In that time if they encountered a werewolf the werewolf might think they were the walking dead, like a vampire. Presumably some experienced werewolves would recognize the scent of rats or other little things that could point towards it, like blood from an ear.

                                                            Azlu and Beshilu aren't stupid, but they are obsessed. They can't really help themselves when it comes to some things, similar to a werewolf and 'the hunt,' but taken up a notch. With that it's noting overly aggressive rats or deaths by spider bites, a fight or flight response, atmospheric changes like a thickening or hollowing of the Gauntlet.

                                                            Obviously there's Gifts that help with these. Scent of the Unnatural, for instance.
