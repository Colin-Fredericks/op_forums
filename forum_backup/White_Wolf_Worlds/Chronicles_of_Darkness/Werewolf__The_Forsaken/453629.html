    Another bunch of 2e questions after last night's game

    03-22-2015, 10:31 PM
    Fun night with a Siskur-Dah completed. Blood was spilled, and I even had a moment at the end of the hunt where I felt just a bit sick to my stomach at the brutality.
    The prey had no chance. He felt confident, comfortable driving his shiny black Accord through the intercity street at night, with his gun on the passenger side seat. He was panicked but cool when his car inexplicably and suddenly turned by itself from the road into a dark alley between a closed up car repair shop and a graffiti ridden car wash and shut off. He grabbed his gun and his stash and leaped out of the car. He didn't make it to the end of the alley. The gun was never fired. His only sound was a breathy cry of pain as he was dragged down off from the crumbling retaining wall.

    My favorite part was the ringing of an iphone as the only sound in the alley above the wet rending and munching of the prey. The phone changed from "Call Missed" to "1 new message" as things fade to black.

    This is much different than the werewolf game I ran before. It was gory and dramatic, fun times.

    That said, there were some bumps in the road. I wonder how other ST's handle some of these things.

    *Sensing Spirits: Does an Uratha know what kind of spirit it detects? Should I describe spirits and leave it to the wolf to interpret? Should I put a roll to it maybe? I decided to choose how easy it'd be to interpret the spirit, and describe appropriately based on that. So sometimes you might not exactly know what you're dealing with, you only know it's dark, fast and darting and has a smell of decay about it. Other times it might just be a gnarled and silent old tree spirit.

    *Loot in game - A character with 0 resource merit "found" a canvas bag with ten thousand dollars in it. This is a general WoD question, but after the "fetishes acquired through RP" being implied it leads me reconsider how to handle loot since looting is always kind of a thing when there's a dead body nearby. There's almost always going to be a wallet, a phone, a car, who knows what else of value? This call I found harder, but I decided to rule it: You have free access to use whatever your find during the scene, but must pay appropriate experience to keep it past that. I think that's pretty much what the old rules follow. Anyone else have an opinion on how to handle found loot?

    *The shadow is everywhere, and wolves can peek through anytime they want - A frustration in my game has been that I need to generate the Shadow on the spot when a wolf decides to peek through to the other side of the gauntlet. Does anyone else find the requirement to have two descriptions for every location prepared kind of onerous? Any suggestions on how to handle it other than "plan thoroughly" or "wing it"? (The first I don't have time to do, and the second is what I have been doing.)

              With respect to sensing and identifying spirits, I think a lot depends on whether you mean a werewolf just using their innate abilities, specific Gifts and Rites or learned and acquired knowledge represented by skills such as Occult. Most scenarios will require a some types of roll, and could provide both different and overlapping information.

              As to the found loot, I believe Satchel correctly cites the relevant, or at least analogous, point. You can use or employ all your found booty, including all disadvantages such as explaining to the patrol officer or detective why you're in possession of a dead man's phone or car, why you're on surveillance video using the man's credit cards to buy fertilizer and industrial equipment, or the unexplained deposits (and tax implications) of your sudden cash deposits. If you wish to retain these resources, particularly without annoying legal issues, you must purchase the relevant merit dots.

                      An easy way to handle resource usage in game without requiring every player to sink dots into it? Assume everyone has enough money to buy any moderate purchase they might need. Then use the following conditions / merits.

                      Big Score
                      You just got paid handsomely for a job well done, you found a briefcase full of money beside a suspicious dead body, or you just pulled off an impressive robbery. Now you've got a pile of money or an overflowing bank account that is practically begging to be spent somewhere.
                      Resolution: You may choose to shed this condition to make a single availability 5 purchase, a reasonable number of availability 4 or lower purchases, or use this money as soft leverage to improve an impression by two steps if they accept your money. You also regain a point of willpower from the catharsis of throwing money at a problem to make it go away.
                      Beat: n/a

                      Strapped (Persistent)
                      You emptied your bank account paying for car repairs after the accident, you had to bribe that slimy official to get the job done, or someone just wiped out your savings account. Now you're completely out of spare cash, and have to rely upon others to cover your expenses.
                      Resolution: You resolve this condition at the end of the story, or when you gain the Big Score condition.
                      Beat: Your character suffers a meaningful setback due to her poverty.

                      Wealthy (••••)
                      Effect: You wisely invested your money in the right companies, you were born into a wealthy family and command a meaningful inheritance, or you represent an organization that can afford to pay for your every need. In short, you are filthy stinking rich. Once per chapter you can give yourself the Big Score condition.
