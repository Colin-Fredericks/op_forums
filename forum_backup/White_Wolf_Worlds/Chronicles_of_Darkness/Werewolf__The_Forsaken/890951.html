              The rules for regeneration clearly state that when the damage the werewolf takes is healed, the tilts associated with these injuries are removed.

              Arm loss is a tilt, a variation of Arm Wrack caused by aggravated damage. Ergo, by recovering all of the aggravated damage that resulted in a lost arm, the werewolf does indeed recover from the lost arm.

                                  The idea would not be one a common wolf would agree to. Rather a single foolish or desperate wolf tempted with promises that'd solve their plight. Maybe it is a piece of knowledge they need to defeat an idiam. Then after an act of desperation the wolf agrees "An eye for an eye" and sells a single eye for the secret weakness of an idiiam. This secret pans out and truly was what the werewolf bartered for. Next time they are searching for some lost scrap of knowledge or fabled bane they can't help but wonder if the merchant might have it as they hit dead end after dead end with a deadline looming. Lo and behold even if he does not have it on him he can fetch it for a price and though the price is high just as it was before it is a fair enough trade. After a time what was once an act out of desperation could be a willing choice. He had never cheated you yet so you trust him. Everything he ever sold you was always as advertised so you trust him. He never asks for more than you can live without for a time so you trust him.

                                  Just once would a single merchant need to convince a wolf that it was worth it. Just once would one have to offer solution for flesh before hooking the desperate or the foolish. After all if your eyes regrew in less than a week could you put up with being blind temporarily if it saves your pack? Of course you would and that is exactly what the merchant hopes. The first time for noblest of causes. The second because it worked before and other options are drying up. Until they come and sell bit and pieces for trifles and trinkets to solve their every whim and trouble.

                                  This is meant to spell 'disaster waiting to happen' but because it worked last time the foolish or the desperate come to trust the man that wants to buy them piece by piece... when one can regrow limbs you have to slip mental horror into your physical horror.

                                          This story is true. Once there was a young werewolf, a pup, called Red-Path, because he left a trail of blood and bodies behind him. He was a warrior, and a good one, no doubt. Skilled as any pit fighter you'll ever see and twice as mean. Red-Path loved only one thing more than the heat and blood of battle: his wife. Red-Path never left her, you see, not like he should have. He kept her at arm's length, tried to do right by her, but the bloodlust, the hunt, it called him. Sooner or later, our most loved ones end up in the crossfire. Usually, that means your pack, pup. They can handle themselves. We're born to this life. It's harsh, definitely, but your pack-sister can take the punishment better than your wife, or mother, or daughter. Some of us try to hold one to what we knew. In Red-Path's case, it might have been the only thing keeping him from becoming a really ravenous monster.

                                          It wasn't to be. A fighter like Red, he makes lots of enemies. And one day this Azlu, a real nasty piece of work that Red's pack had just torn to shreds along with her nest, followed him home. Yeah, you can see where I'm going with this. It ... wasn't pretty. Red-Path goes off on a hunt, chasing down the other Azlu, but it was all a distraction by this fuckin' mean crone he'd gone and pissed off. And while Red-Path is away, she takes his old lady, right, and — no, no, not that way. At least if she'd eaten the poor woman's brain it would have been over quick. No, she starts by terrorizing the poor human, then she bites her, see, and puts her in a coma. She merges with her in that really fucking unholy way they do, and then she spins this big web over everything Red-Path cherishes and waits. Just waits. He comes home to check on his old lady, gets caught in the webs, and down she comes, wearing the face of his wife on the spider's bloated body. Well, Red-Path goes berserk, as you might expect, and they rip each other to shreds. She wanted to take everything from him, the spinner crone did, but she hadn't counted on just how angry he'd get. Red-Path killed her, without his pack. And he kept ripping at the pieces until all the floorboards were soaked in so much blood you couldn't tell whose it was: his, the Azlu's, his wife's, what. Just blood, fur, shards of spider armor everywhere. The whole house came down. He had nothin' left.

                                          Red-Path didn't have much lucidity left in him after that. But what he did have he used to try to get her back. Now normally we mourn, we sing our wolf-dirge, we maybe hunt down the shartha for some good old-fashioned tooth-and-claw revenge. Not Red-Path. He'd been wounded, but it wasn't venom or spider-fangs that got him deepest. And he wasn't about to let go. Not yet. So he had heard of these real strange things, right, these ... these fae, like the ones that dance in the faerie rings and finish an old man's shoes for him, that kind of shit. 'Course, they're real dark, just like a lot of the old tales. Not this cartoon movie crap, but I mean the kinds of things you don't bargain with unless you know what you're doin' — and if you know what you're doin', you don't bargain with them. It's just askin' for trouble. But old Red-Path is fearless. So he goes to this sort of market they have, with goblins and trinkets and fuckin' straw-men and house-gulping tornadoes and everything else you could imagine. Every fairy tale come to life right there. Most of us just stay away from that shit. Spirits, hell people are weird enough without adding in even weirder things that we know less about.

                                          Red-Path goes to this market and he looks for someone who can grant him a wish. Anyone or anything that can bring back his beloved. Of course, he offers up any price in return, which is where he went wrong. Damn right he found a taker. Some old goblin or something, I don't know, who wanted a piece of him for every piece of her restored. So he gives up an arm. Naturally, he grows it back. He gives up a leg. His eye. Then the other. Then it gets down to the fuckin' nitty-gritty: teeth, hair, nails. But at last his patron has rebuilt his wife from all these pieces. She's not yet human, though, right? Not aware. Like a zombie, or some kind of soulless shell. She needs a heart, to live again. To be human. He realizes he can't give that without risking death, but he has this brilliant idea. No price is too high, right? So he rips out his own heart there on the spot. Now, normally, even we would have trouble with that. You might survive, if you're quick enough on the mend, but it's risky. So Red-Path takes on Gauru form to heal up faster. Seems like a good idea, right?

                                          Problem is, it worked. All of it. She gets his heart, and she comes to life right there in the market. Unfortunately, she sees this (literally!) heartless monster standing before her where her husband was, so what does she do? She runs screaming in terror. And Red-Path, overcome with all this rage and now without a heart, without any mercy, gives in to the instinct to chase. He comes to with his heart regenerated but covered in his wife's blood, having killed her a second time with his own claws. Yeah, I know. Fuckin' tragic, is what it is. So anyway, the story goes that Red-Path ripped out his own heart again right there and gave it to the goblin vendor, and let himself bleed out and die.

                                          But somewhere, in a market full of impossible oddities and broken dreams, there's a jar with a heart in it. The heart still beats to this day. And that, pup, is why we don't sell our fucking body parts to creatures of the night. Remember Red-Path's tale, young one, and remember that sometimes it's best to just let go of something, lest you rip out your own heart trying to keep it close.
