    Personally, I'd encourage groups to run it whichever way they choose, as long as they're consistent. Check with your Storyteller about which one the group uses.

    Now I'm not saying this as a cop out - I have reasons.

    1) Werewolf fiction generally falls into two camps.

    In the first camp, when the rampaging werewolf comes to town and the protagonist gets infected, he avoids falling into werewolf rage because of the threat it brings to those he loves, but eventually realises that the greater threat comes from the antagonist werewolf, and he surrenders to the rage knowing that he'll tear into the enemy, and it will end in the death of one of them. Often, once he's torn the throat from the antagonist, he turns on his family, who tearfully kill him to protect themselves.

    In the second camp, the protagonist avoids the rage because he will join the antagonist and they'll both rampage. Eventually his sorrowful family kill them both. (They might just try to kill the antagonist, hoping that it ends the curse and saves the protagonist.)

    I'd like to see both stories possible in WtF, depending on what the group wants. I don't want to turn either preference aside because of 'one true way'-ism, and I want to open the field for groups to explore the themes captured by both.

    2) Like I said before, Stew likes the emergent aspect of this, and according to Acrozatarim, had this view in earlier development discussions. I've already asked him about adding some words to The Pack to explore this. He said no to that, because it deserves the words to explore it properly. So, what I'd propose in a future book is exploring both possible interpretations - which game themes each explores, when you'd use each one, how to play them, how they change the relationship between tribes, how they change the relationship between Pure/Forsaken.

    Watch this space.

    Writer. Developer. World of Darkness | Chronicles of Darkness | The Trinity Continuum

            Those just all sound like results I would not have the party wake up to. Sure, by a strict reading of the rules those are the only likely results, but the way I see it, once the characters black out, what happens during that lost time is more a matter of story than rules.
