                                    I get the feeling that perhaps there's some confusion between roles...

                                    A line developer isn't a necessity for a game. It's a good thing to have, don't get me wrong, but many games have gone multiple years without them. A line developer pitches ideas along with the writers, and generally has a sense of the line as a whole and where things fit. Each book has a developer, who writes an outline, hires freelancers, and makes the best book they can. While line developers usually — but not always* — end up being the book developers for the books in their line. It is not just possible but common for lines to have books produced by different developers, under the guiding hands of Rich and Rose, without a line developer.

                                    I didn't announce this earlier because I was and am committed to finishing the books I had in progress, and it'd cause more problems than it solved. Hell, I was still developing The Pack. I'm absolutely certain that if I'd said something at the time, we would see threads like this worrying about the status of that book, despite me being clear that I was going to finish it. We've got new books on the way,

                                    New books are coming. They'll likely come faster because they've got developers who can give them the time and attention that they deserve. Rose oversees all Chronicles of Darkness books, so it's not like we don't have continuity between books. This is a good time for Werewolf: The Forsaken.

                                    * For example: Bill Bridges was the developer for W20: Kinfolk even as I was still W20 line developer.

                                                            Originally posted by Second Chances View Post

                                                            Or Shunned by the Moon could have a production cycle similar to Enemy Action, which has gone from Frist Drafts to Development in 3 months and could easily be out within 6 months of us first knowing about it.

                                                            You're panicking Wyrd, you need to chill out.

                                                            Seriously. Some of the comments here also make me think that there's some confusion about what a "developer" does.

                                                            I developed Mage books for a while, but I was never the line developer. I was a developer (insofar as I knew the process) who developed some Mage books (because I'd worked pretty extensively on the line). Joe Carriker, Bill Bridges, and Stephen DiPesa also developed Mage books during the same rough period.

                                                            This has also happened with Werewolf, Vampire, and "blue books." The smaller lines have had more consistent devs (I've done almost all of the development for Promethean, but that's as much to do with how little there's been as anything else). None of us actually work for OPP, we're hired on a freelance basis, so saying that anyone is a "line developer" is true only as far as Rich and Rose wants to keep hiring a particular developer for projects in the same line. Rich could decide tomorrow that he doesn't like the cut of my jib and hire someone else for the next Promethean book (there's gonna be another one, right, Rich? Please?), and while that would be sad, it's also totally above-board.

                                                            Similarly, if another such book comes around and I say "you know what, I'd love to but I'm snowed under developing Chill and writing Timepeekers", Rich would hire someone else to dev said book, and that would be fine.
