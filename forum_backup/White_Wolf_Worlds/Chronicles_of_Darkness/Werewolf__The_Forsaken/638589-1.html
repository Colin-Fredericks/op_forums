                Originally posted by 2ptTakrill View Post
                You can just rule that sunlight doesn't affect her in the Shadow, then focus large segments of your story on nighttime or in the Shadow. You shouldn't have to change major game mechanics to make the game playable if you want to avoid the mess. As for clan I would go with Daeva, that way your vampire fills a slot that most wolves never will unless they make it their character's focus.
                It isn't a mess because it isn't a major game mechanic. From a strictly mechanic standpoint damage from sunlight have almost no effect on the game, and almost no other rule reference it. The sole exceptions are the effects of Humanity and Blood Potency on said damage, but given how common it is expected in a vampire game for this to be an issue, I'd say those traits will hardly be thrown from balance because sunlight damage is gone.

                Again, it is a major theme in Vampire, and should be considered with caution. But really, the game doesn't suffer any mechanical setback for sunlight damage's removal, and the chronicle isn't about vampires to begin with. It can still be interesting to let the sun hurt the vampire, but it is pretty easy to get rid of this if it gets in the way of the actual themes of he chronicle.

                Remember that nothing here is about the official setting, about Requiem, or about other games. Its about this guy's chronicle.

                            I know I'm in the minority on this but to me the only interesting part of vampires is the weaknesses and how do you overcome them. I'm too old to enjoy playing fourteen year old fantasies of being the meanest and badest mofo in the room, or bagging the hottest girl with my wicked wiles, I want to overcome obstacles big and small. In my first requiem chronicle (I'm currently running the second) my players nearly lost all my willingness to run it when I threw a simple sunlight challenge at them, totally prepared to go very easy on any solutions they came up with, and the response I got was a "that won't be a problem", followed by making it clear they really didn't want to worry about it. That's the reason we have ended up playing more werewolf and mage than vampire.

                                        They can play as whatever they want. I was saying that in my experience, when a group gets together to play X, and one person just has to play Y for whatever reason, the concessions required to accommodate Y means X's fun suffers.

                                        As I said before, I don't believe anyone here has said that there's a right way to play. The OP said the group was trying to consider all the complications a vampire in a werewolf pack would bring. We've not even scratched the surface, but ultimately, which aspects impact play will be decided by the group. The spectrum runs from playing a vampire by the (Requiem) book and taking on board all the vampire condition and society crap that comes with that, to playing something with Forsaken rules that the group calls a vampire but has none of the actual nWoD connotations or baggage, and requires work from the ST to decide how the not-werewolf uses werewolf rules to demonstrate its specific condition. How many complications (the OP's word) come into that depends where on the spectrum the group wants the vampire to occupy.

                                        As a sidenote, few things about nWoD vampires pisses werewolves off faster than Ordo Dracul and their parasitic drive to drain Wyrm's Nests (read: loci), so good luck with that. (Caveat - I'm assuming OD still like doing this in 2nd edition. I've not delved as deeply into the Ordo's 2nd ed drives.)
