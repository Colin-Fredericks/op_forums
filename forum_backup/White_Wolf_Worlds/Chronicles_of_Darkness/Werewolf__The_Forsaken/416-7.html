                    Okay, in regards to the idea about the pack causing an increase in Honorary Spirit Rank, this is the simplest idea I've come up with so far:

                    First, you count up how many werewolves of each Rank you have in your pack. Whichever Rank has the most is the baseline. In the case of a tie, use the higher of the two. The base would be 3, adjusted down one for the two HSR 2's, then back up one for the HSR 4, so it still balances out at 3.                                     To Kaho (and this is going to be very long) regarding some Totem ideas:

                                    Totems were brought up on the old forum thread. I'm not a fan on their necessity for a pack to be, well a pack whatsoever. An alien spirit with it's own agenda... one you're almost by necessity going to have kicking around in the material world to "help" you out... that's what is necessary?. Talk about about the pot calling the kettle black when it comes to Forsaken pointing the finger at the Pure for allowing spirits to pull shit in the material world; Forsaken do it all the time. They aren't constantly watching it, nothing is stopping it form using it's own Influences to promote the resonance it wants to harvest Essence, and given the ban (that's a tickler me: the ban does what for the spirit? It should be a task that generates it Essence to give it more incentive to stick around, in general)... but this is a good idea?

                                    Yeah, no. Cutting that out entirely with the revisions being tinkered with (which I'll be posting soon, got pretty busy and am trying to hash out a preliminary Predatory Aura as to give one of the Instinct, well... some context in what it does for y'all). Pack Bond as a merit to reflect the connection to ones pack and the benefits, and Totem just being a spirit one managed to convince to work in service to you or... vice versa for benefits on both's end. Most certainly -not- built by the PCs entirely to cherry pick a quick buff via metagame (it's a NPC for christ-sakes with it's own agenda... no), and using actual merit costs to reflect it's benefits instead of the current system. If you want it to do more than alter your Essence to give you it's "Gifts" in return for doing a ban/task that keeps it sustained... you buy things like, I dunno, Mentor and similar.

                                    We're also looking at what an individual pack member gets is equal to the 1-5 rating they take in the Totem; some people reap more from the Totem than others, favoured or otherwise, but this can be a double edged sword especially with potent ones. A spirit doesn't stop being like, and acting like a spirit just because of the Totem bond, but I can see using the new conditions for Ephemeral entities such as Open as part of the totem package with some tinkering (something I've been thinking about after looking at my Co-STs and my own initial ideas some months back)

                                    That's what I'm going with regardless of where Idigam Chronicles goes; complete with those who bite off more than they can chew with powerful totems starting to "urge" you more an more in line with it's desires. For those of a Rank more than you or your pack can contend with, say hello to fighting with it's desires attempting to override your own; additional incentive as to why some Pure (Fire Touched, I'm looking at you) really get looked upon negatively by the Forsaken above and beyond the other factors; the tendency of some Pure to take or to be, well, taken by powerful Totems may have more insidious consequences beyond letting it run roughshod in the material realm.

                                    This will give a reason why many Packs -if- they take a Totem prefer weaker and more manageable lower ranked ones; they're easier to keep in check and far less likely to start influencing you from the connection of Essence you made with it. Even among the Forsaken in this system... you occasionally run into packs or individuals that have been entirely suborned by a powerful spirit, making them exceedingly dangerous problems to the Forsaken mindset; Balehounds with the Maeljin, Maelintents and Wound spirits become just the tip of the iceberg as other spirits may cause the same. The plus side is powerful Totems are likely to give you more... but it's still looking after its -own- best interests like any spirit, not yours except when they overlap. Rather than a hard and fast Rank cap outside of those funky crusades with powerful Elunim, anyone can take a weaker or more potent one. There's just issues when you take a stronger bond than your effective Spirit Rank with potent spirits; extra reward, but with drawbacks. Pure have a bit less in that their effective Spirit Rank (socially) is considered higher... but unfortunately as per The Pure and elsewhere, often potent spirits actively seek them out to make these pacts to get more of a foothold in the material world. In turn you see Pure influenced by spirits more often when it comes to powerful totems in more ways than one.

                                    The other issue I had that it was basically a PC made NPC that just... stuck around in Twilight to help your ass out for what was invested as part of the package, and that it was mobile without Essence Bleed seemingly (yet you never paid into it to get Essence directly, especially given many totem bans made little sense for essence generation); some won't like this or want to use it, but we're keeping Totem mobility tied to Rites rather than having one just offsetting essence bleed; it's not your tag-along buddy by default, and primarily -it- will be what guards the packs Loci if they have one, rather than having them stuck at home to defend it all the time. Normally what will happen is that it will anchor to something or slumber (with the right numina, rites or it travelling in the shadow) if being moved; we're treating Totem spirits being brought to other Loci as a form of warfare, and it's ongoing help treated more like Mentor and similar Merits rather than part and parcel.

                                    After years of using Totem as it was, it's been nothing but a headache as presented. No more tag-along-buddy. It's a spirit; not your loving slave effectively getting very little out of the bargain. I want more to the agreements made with the Totem. Sure a werewolf pack may force a weaker one into service, or one fearing it will be consumed by other spirits might offer it's services to a pack to save it's own ass but I want it more of a two-way street and more dangerous.

                                    Entirely cutting out the Totem granting you Essence as well; it's -the- precious commodity to spirits; they're not going to just give it (where's it getting it anyhow if the pack isn't letting it run roughshod outside of what the pack does to maintain it in the material world?) to a bunch of werewolves beyond what it takes to establish the pact. I can see more intelligent and potent spirits -possibly- offering it, but it's not out of the goodness of their "heart"; it's because they want to twist the resonance of the werewolves own essence bit by bit more in order to what they want. If you want to tie your Essence and Resonance up to connect with a Spirit, odds are it's going to influence you in ways above and beyond buffs. I've always wanted this more reinforced and the choice of which totem, and even -if- a totem is a good idea touched on more.

                                    The same is holding true for Elunim for those with Auspices under this idea, as well as Tribal Totems; they're all spirits in the end. They still follow the tendencies of spirits. They don't have purely your best interests in mind. The Pure can at times point at the Forsaken with their connections to Lunes and go "How is what you're doing any real different beyond giving the Bitch Mother and her Choirs more of a monopoly on the Shadow and the Material World? And -why- is that a good idea you numb-nuts, if you truly know what they're like? Driving packs mad, a Celestine noted for duplicity, inconsistency and madness? And you point the finger at us when we shun those things?!" with just enough conviction to cast doubt on how 'good' a position the Forsaken really are in with Luna. Chosen favourites to some. Duped slaves to others.

                                    My Co-ST is working on these ideas at the moment with me chiming in. I'm hoping to get some completed chunks posted pretty soon; I was going to post the Instincts flat out until I realized how much they tied into heavy overhauls elsewhere, so I'd rather have a cleaner rough draft to pop up before flinging it in your collective direction.

                                        Eh, I'm kinda iffy on more convoluted systems like that. Honestly, I'm thinking of just taking the highest ranking member's effective spirit rank (if using the Primal Urge systems to determine it, because getting high is rare and costly) and adding +1 for being part of a bonded pack. You're not going to get honorary ranked 4's and 5 Werewolves with any consistency; those are high, HIGH Primal Urge types under this idea, legends really. If using the idea that being two ranks higher makes your natural attacks like banes to spirits, it's still keeping things reasonable even if you do get an honorary rank 5 Werewolf in your pack; you're still just getting the benefits on rank 4 and less spirits, and that's in a rare case hunting alongside a true supernatural paragon among werewolves. In most cases your packs effective rank is going to sit from 2 (if you go by the notion their honorary rank is at the bottom for harsher chronicles) to 3 more commonly (going by the idea they start around effective rank 2), with 4, 5 and the very, very rare 6 being seen much less given Primal Urge Costs (even old style Renown Costs for that matter).

                                        That Rank 5 is considered scary as shit; he's effectively backed by his adopted half-flesh "choir". Spirits likely see them with the pack bond as more or less a singular choir or entity as far as ranking goes, and would be hard pressed not to take the threat seriously even if the individual only has effective Rank 1s or 2s at his side. Numbers are always the great equalizer. They won't be tearing the near-god Rank 5's a new asshole as easily, but even those very, very potent spirits sure have to consider if just butting heads with a pack with members that potent is worth the while.

                                        This would make starting packs who default around rank 2 treatment individually consistently very dangerous threats to rank 1 spirits. That said, I'm honestly more of a fan of werewolf claws and teeth being able to directly rip away Essence from spirits than the aggravated damage/Bane thing (or both). Discorporating them is all fine and well easily; I like the idea that a pack can have members attack and tear away or consume it's Essence without esoteric powers while others go in to rip apart it's Corpus more personally, but using the tentative idea presented in GMC this still works well too. It's something I'm using; hunting spirits for essence being part of the package, rather than requiring a Rite.

                                                    Originally posted by Thorbes View Post
                                                    What if the Totem is not an alien spirit with its own agenda and is literally "the Spirit of the Pack". Spirits get created by all kind of things, maybe the ritual bond of the pack creates an spirit that represents that packs interests and theme.

                                                    Influence: My Pack?
                                                    Definitely another option, but not one I'm too sure I'd go with. Too much "And then we get a big buff with little consequences, with something that comes out of nowhere like a deus ex machinae to hand us goodies, but little reason to pose an issue." I suppose if it could outgrow the pack and have it's own agendas, or if it was something made as a guardian/watchdog spun out of essence temporarily via a Rite it'd be worth the while (in fact that would be an interesting rite; Summon the Guardian or whatever, though I wouldn't be surprised if something like it exists in some book already with W:tF that I'm not remembering). Otherwise it pushes heavily into the territory of "we just got a spirit that acts as a Retainer, but does much much more than a Retainer, and it's only real drawback is we gotta do some task to keep it around and not falling under general spirit mechanics and assumptions". I suppose my problem there is that it's too much like the existing totem mechanic while being even more contrived.

                                                    What happens when it gets destroyed? Infected by the resonant essence of something else from feeding on the wrong types of spirits/essence? What happens if an Idigam reshapes it? Bound into a Talen or Fetish? What about when the pack disbands or loses members or gains them? There's some potential there for quite a few stories, but... I dunno. Something about it doesn't rub me right. It's a spirit; a good chunk of what spirits do and why along with a chunk of their mechanics shouldn't get tossed or glossed over. It's effectively a "werewolf spirit", without being a two werewolf mating consequence. Why not just seek out a wolf or a pack spirit at that point? How common is this to happen?

                                                    Personal tastes obviously, but it reminds me of some super sentai-esque "and then we create some avatar of our friendship and camaraderie to do battle for us" in a not-so-good way, complete with cliche words of comfort and wisdom now and then and handing out goodies. Ugh.

                                                    Blame my insomniac mind for that one. Honestly, I'd rather not blunt the impact of spirits with packs just springing them into existence by sheer existence as a pack. A "spirit of whatever we needed" seems extremely contrived if there's too much direct control over it's creation and end result. A packs actions creating a spirit (and likely not what they specifically cultivated to a T) that later takes an interest on them as an Essence source? Sure. But some innate, nebulous (very nebulous going by the existing W:tF books) tie to them, or reliance and utterly subservient or with only the packs best interests in mind? Eh... a spirit so specific and tied to a material extension (the werewolves) that way would default to being very low ranked by dint of W:tF's explanation. This seems more the territory of exceptions, or at least, extraordinary actions to me rather than a default assumption (though with room for it to possibly occur).

                                                    This is one of the areas where I was looking at Geist Krewes for some inspiration some time back, and a way to show how packs grow into Lodges (or potentially other Tribes) as they expand and gain a powerful spiritual patron. It could definitely work, but I think there would need to be quite a few considerations to keep it from going into the land of "I've got an NPC at my beck and call unless this -one- thing is not done who acts more like a favourable Deus Ex Machinae rather than a character itself that just so happens to likely trump both the Retainer and the Mentor merits in what it does".

                                                    Some idle musings; I'm running on fumes and being horribly overtired (and rambling), but that's what initially popped to mind; I can definitely see some ways to work with it and keep it interesting, but I really don't think packs should get a spirit "get out of spirit jail free card and get a sycophantic buddy spirit" by their very nature alone. There should be some room to question whether it is the pack itself making them using this option, or if they're simply drawing the attention of a pre-existing spirit that the packs trends, habits and actions resonate with. It should also get folks wondering about the actual nature of Tribal Totems and Lodge Totems if this occurs with any frequency. The more I think about it, the less predisposed I am against it, but I feel it should not be made concrete, rather than a possibility of that being what may occur if that makes any sense. *Tired chin rubs*.

                                                            So I'll toss my 2 cents in. First of all, I'm going to echo the sentiment of more emphasis on the wolf nature. Forsaken has never really grabbed me, because in my mind I feel like werewolf should have the potential for personal horror out of any game line. But in practice it always ends up being the most pulpy and focused on external horror. That's my experience, anyway. I mean, monstrous spirit abominations are horrifying in their own way, but it's all very focused on the monsters "out there" when what I want out of a werewolf game is to focus on the monster within. You have an eight foot raging wolf monster inside of you just waiting for a chance to rip its way out of your skin and kill something, how do you deal with that? Do you suppress it and hope it never finds its way out? Or do you embrace it and let the wolf run free in hopes you'll find some measure of balance between your dual natures? And all the while, you're still a human with a family and friends and bills to pay, how do you reconcile the two worlds? That's what I feel Werewolf should be about in theory and friends of mine who love Forsaken tell me that's a big part of it. But every time I play, I never see that side of it. I hate to use the "fuzzy spirit cops" cliche, because I know that's an oversimplification and doesn't represent the game well. Besides, I do very much like all of the spirit stuff. I just don't want the personal horror of never knowing when you might turn into a giant wolfbeast and rip someone you care about to shreds to be overshadowed by it. On the setting end of things... More of the Rocky Mountains I'm from Denver and I like having a local setting, so more on werewolves in my home town is always good.
