    Originally posted by Cinder View Post
    Just ask to the Exalted forum about 2nd Edition and its contributions about the setting. They'll give you enough proof to support this thesis until the end of times (does not help that it was not perfect even on the mechanics side, but people down there burst into flames when you talk about some of the fluff of 2nd Edition)
    You don't even have to go as far as that. Pick a gameline in Chronicles and look where the conversations are.

    As a minor example, Electroshock Therapy was this big ol' overpowered cheese thing in Promethean the Created. I literally never heard of it until some point in Second Edition's development. Wanna know what I did hear about? Transhumanism in Promethean.

    Mechanics are cool, and having good mechanics is integral to making a game setting work-but that's the catch, people play in the setting using the mechanics, at least in games like this. These are setting first games-and if you compare them to games that are, ideologically, more "people play with the mechanics using a setting", they sell better.

    I do want to reiterate a addendum to the earlier post, because I'm setting up pieces to start working on my own game-a lot of what I've said does get blurred with the initial creation of a thing, because mechanical and setting bedrock is a thing to build and it can be hard to really build a good game at that point, mechanically speaking, particularly since you really have to marry it to the setting. But as time goes on and the mechanical sense of things is figured out enough to be that bedrock, setting becomes way harder.

                        Originally posted by Second Chances View Post
                        And I love the unique and decidedly not generic settings. They show me ways that I can create something unique, they hone in on themes, and they feel like they are part of living, breathing world.

                        I think we just have very different tastes.
                        Its a matter of time and place to me. Personally i believe the first city book of a line (or in the case of the old core, the example city) should be the most generic one. The sorta-tag line of NWoD was that is a tool box and each city didnt need to conform to form but one, as dm, need to deeply understand the norm before changing it. See how and why each piece fit and understand them before start messing with it.

                        Saying this changeling city is different/original because the summer court dominates all year around doesnt have the same weight if i dont understand how and why a city normally function beyond the blurb and white room of the setting description in the book.

                        Past the first city book then sure, go nuts. Make a city which its king/prince is decided by lottery.

                        On the same vein on time and place, should all the dark eras books had been release around 2012 when all the lines were more or less done. The main 3 had all the mechanics need, same as hunter and changeling. Then I would love 3, 4 or even 5 Dark Eras book. Because they supplements to add to robust main lines.

                        Just taking forsaken, one had the Pure and their gifts. Plethora of gifts, Rites and lodges. Lunar gifts, alternative lunar gifts and a System for Antagonist (if the spirits still were clunky). It was a complete line and the Dark eras then would have been the cream and cherry on top of an good pie. But now it feels that the cream and cherry is going into a half baked pie.

                        Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                        Mechanics are cool, and having good mechanics is integral to making a game setting work-but that's the catch, people play in the setting using the mechanics, at least in games like this. These are setting first games-and if you compare them to games that are, ideologically, more "people play with the mechanics using a setting", they sell better.
                        I will point out that there is a difference between universal applicable fluff and fluff of limited use. For example, as we are in werewolf lets keep on werewolf, the tactic and life of the pure is pretty universal. How tribes teach and convince newly change uratha to join the forsaken is pretty universal. How spirit teach gifts and why, is pretty universal.

                        Dark eras book and location blurb arent, of all the times presented, which one as a DM i am gonna use? The crossovers one present the question of "do i wanna put X splat in my game?" and even those that are uni-splat, the question remains of whether i wanna run this splat game in this time and place? Because how Uratha society function in say chicago during the prohibition era would really not affect what is going with my game is Seattle 2005.

                        Its not even a question of fluff vs Mechanics. Its more of question of a fluff with a very specific use vs mechanics.

                        Also the comparison with pathfinder is pretty dishonest. Yes, wod is general like to thinks is very rules light (even thought NWoD has as many rules as 3.5 by its end) but i am not talking about incredible specific mechanics like "specific rules to blow a building using c4 that allows me to calculate the amount needed by the pound" or "a whole system to represent the different schools of Kendo" but instead. Forsaken is a game about hunting 6 preys, why are we missing rules for 2 and half of them? Is not even creating said rules, just updating them to 2nd edition. The argument of "wod isnt about mechanics" doesnt really fly on this because this mechanics existed in 1st

                        Originally posted by tasti man LH View Post
                        And that even if you did want more mechanics, because of the toolbox nature of CofD, the game actively encourages players and STs to come up with stuff themselves?
                        Not really the toolbox approach was more on the setting side. "No more metaplot to "limit you"" they said, each city and clan/tribe/etc can be compose of whatever you want.....because we couldnt do that before.....for some reason.

                        Mechanically it isnt that much of a toolbox. If it was we would have gotten a chapter on how to create gifts, which we dont have. Or a chapter on how to create Host which i argue is needed.
