    Thinking of replacing tribes with packs as werewolves primary social units

    03-27-2016, 01:30 PM
    I've been creating a werewolf game and I've found I'd prefer to focus on the packs themselves and the interaction with other packs. None of the tribes really clicked with me. That added with a difficulty in understanding the circumstances on multi tribal pack led me to simply getting rid of tribes altogether.
    I'm pretty confidant on making things work together fluff wise but i need some help with the mechanic side of things.

    What should i do with:
    - The tribal renown
    Do i simply ignore the free point of renown usually awarded for tribes
    Or
    Do i make a sort of pack renown. Which could influence the interaction between packs.

    - The tribal gifts
    Do I let the players choose 3 gifts that could be associated with their pack totem
    Or
    Do i let the player choose 1 gift. With the possibility of getting more with exp expenditure (perhaps reflecting the pack totem getting other spirits allied that would teach gift of the chosen category)

    - The favored enemy (this is the crux of my problem with tribes as i much prefer the favored enemy to be at pack level and reflecting experience and recurring enemies within a pack territory)
    I'm thinking of having the favored enemy be a merit that a pack can buy with exp (a merit not unlike the fighting style format giving bonuses that would range from 1 to 5)
    First, the favored enemy chosen would have to be much more precise (ex: just beshilu instead of host).
    Also for the bonuses i'd see things like 9-again for rolls to determine if the favored enemy is involved here. Or free specialties in the favored enemy (perhaps letting players choose if the take the specialty on occult, investigation, intimidate, etc)
    I'd appreciate more help on constructing that merit better.

      I would posit that packs are already the primary social units in Forsaken. Tribes are less like formal organizations and more like... Err... All of the werewolves that share a certain favored prey. If you've got a real hate-on for Shartha, you might ask your packmates if they know any special techniques for fighting them, and they'd direct you to the nearest allied Hunter in Darkness, who might be willing to teach you the tricks passed down from Black Wolf, if you're willing to accept Black Wolf's Ban.

      All that said, I don't want to come into a thread just to argue why you shouldn't hack the setting the way you want to hack it, so here's my advice on how to remove Tribe from Forsaken if you're of a mind to do so.

      What I would do is make Auspice as important to Uratha as Tribe is to the Pure. Give each Auspice a 4th Gift and two secondary Renown. Characters start with one dot in their Auspice's primary Renown, one dot in one of their Auspice's secondary Renown, and one dot in any Renown (can't raise any Renown to 3 at this point), two Facets from Gifts favored by their Auspice, a Moon Gift at dots equal to their Auspice primary Renown, and a Facet of a Wolf Gift if they only have one dot in their Moon Gift.

      As for Favored Prey, I would let individual characters pick theirs, so the pack isn't all stuck with the same Favored Prey. Maybe just pick one of the five and gain the Ban associated with that Prey.

If you've got a real hate-on for Shartha, you might ask your packmates if they know any special techniques for fighting them, and they'd direct you to the nearest allied Hunter in Darkness, who might be willing to teach you the tricks passed down from Black Wolf, if you're willing to accept Black Wolf's Ban.
                  I'd agree that Pack is definitely the primary social unit, but I think there is an argument to be made against Tribes. Especially if one wants to play up the territorial nature of wolfpacks, giving the PCs the ability to just run off and find another Hunter in Darkness pretty severely undermines territorial themes. Similarly if one wants to run without the sense of a global, unified Uratha community Tribes have basically got to go. All-in-all, though it's a bigger upfront investment by the storyteller, I think Packs and Lodges can provide everything Tribes do with more color and local flavor than tribes can offer (just look at Changeling, throwing away Seasonal Courts is, IMO, one of the best decisions of 2e).
                  Anyway, to the topic at hand here's what I do:

                  Renown: Packs get a Pack Renown, just as Tribes get a Tribal one. No noticeable mechanical changes required.

                  Gifts: One favored Gift, associated with the Pack Totem. Lodges can provide another two, assuming that pack is okay with joining a regional coalition. Either way, just drop the exp differences between favored and unfavored Gifts, the hunting requirements for learning new Shadow Gifts are limit enough.

                  Favored Enemy: I would have said just make it a pack thing, but I like your merit approach. I'll maybe put some thought to that.

                                                          I tend to regard tribes as orders of initiation, rather than meaningful social units. A tribe, as I'd run it, is the lineage of who found you and initiated you into the People. Sometimes you get a choice, sometimes you don't. Still, you get the benefit of your Firstborn's lore and alliances so long as you honor your vows, which is a powerful currency to carry you past your initiation.

                                                          Multi-tribal packs seem like the most sensible route to me, because it means fewer vulnerabilities. A pack without a Bone Shadow can barely hunt spirits, while a pack with no Iron Master has to tread carefully around urban prey.

                                                          However, for a pack-based splat replacement, i would probably retool the krewe mechanics of Geist. I never really liked WtF 1e's take on tribes, and was always looking at ways of adapting that system to replace it.
