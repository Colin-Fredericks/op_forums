              (For the record, this is my first foray back into the forums in at least a year, so I haven't seen a lot of the discussions you're referencing yet.)

              I think the impression of Beast as overpowered probably comes from a small handful of things:

              * They get the ability to recognize other splats as an inherent power, and can kiiiiinda con other splats into thinking they're one as well.
              * Their Skeleton Key power to doorkick their way into another splat's special dimension.
              * Combat applicability of a few specific powers.
              * Battlefield control via manifestation of part or all of their Lair.

              In a knowledge-is-power sense, the Beasts initially seem to have a colossal advantage over the other splats, but the lack of a central 'secret society' of any kind means that mostly they don't know the first thing about the critter they've run into other than the fact it isn't a vanilla human.

              I haven't sat down and actually done extensive number crunching on the subject, but my impression from having read 95ish % of the text is that Beasts are about on equal footing with their cousins in most ways. Pretty much like all of the splats, they have specific situations where they're at a massive advantage against most potential enemies. A combat specced giant in hand to hand combat with an unarmed foe, for instance, will have a serious strength advantage and a kraken will have a huge upper hand in a grappling contest but there's also things that they simply can't do. In a head-to-head with a social-specialized Vampire they're at a huge disadvantage simply because they lack any sort of mind control powers. In terms of post-combat recovery the Uratha make Beasts look like chemo patients by comparison.

              * Vampires can control minds and turn invisible (among other things, obv.). Beasts can't. Edit: I stand corrected, they actually CAN turn invisible.

              * Werewolves regenerate from virtually all injuries at staggering speeds. Beasts don't. Even their high speed healing power is like molasses by comparison, and active healing of anything but bashing damage is relatively costly. Edit Two: aaaand apparently I'm mis-remembering the Werewolf regeneration rules by a bit. Mea Culpa.

              * Beasts don't inherently take aggravated damage from much of anything BUT if they get tagged by a Hero virtually anything might develop the ability to cripple them.

              * Mages can conjure roaring columns of lightning and fire from the skies or boil you alive with a Word. Beasts can breathe fire, which they cannot subsequently control.

              * A Beast can (sometimes) manipulate a battlefield to their advantage by leveraging their Lair, but a Geist using the Boneyard powers has similar capabilities with more flexibility and fewer prerequisites.

              * Beasts' Nightmare powers sound potentially unbalancing, but you have to remember that they're really only an unopposed check against a normal person.


              TL;DR: I'm pretty sure people are reacting more to the combination of Shiny New Powerz and excellent flavor text than they are to the actual concrete mechanics of the game.

                      I stand corrected (and edited my initial post accordingly). I stand behind the general thrust of the post though. Any given critter has advantages and disadvantages in specific situations.

                      As to your earlier point as to whether a crossover specific game should be more interested in balance: There's an actual section in the Beast book labeled "Game Balance?" which (heavily paraphrased) essentially says that the objective potential power balance doesn't and shouldn't matter. It contends that the important thing is that you don't have one or more players feeling slighted because someone decided to be a jerk and powergame over the top of their character concept. Need to balance against a particular NPC threat? Chances are, they have numbers and organization in their favor. Need to worry about PVP balance for some obnoxious reason or other? That's up to the PCs to figure out. If there's one jerk in the group who likes to grab the most powerful option and bully the other characters then it's up to the Storyteller to contain their BS or (preferably) kick them from the game and replace them with someone who isn't a complete prick.

                      I tend to agree with the author. Trying to balance every game mechanic against every other is not only futile, it pretty much misses the point of telling a crossover story IMO.

                                      Vampires are social predators and control freaks.
                                      Werewolves are hunters and masters of their territory.
                                      Mages are academics and scientists, creatures of intellect and the mind.
                                      Prometheans are (post-apocalytic-type) survivors.
                                      Changelings are illusionists, wish/dream granters, and abusers.
                                      Hunters are batman, average people doing the best humanity can.
                                      Bound are, well, I want to say street-gang-like, dealing with death and curses.
                                      Mummies are youngsters growing old and their bodies failing them, but getting wiser
                                      Demons are stealthy spy types.
                                      Beasts... haven't finished the book, so I can't say.

                                      Anyways, my point is that every supernatural has its strong point (and, ironically, its also their weak points). In those specific area, the supernatural in question reigns supreme. I don't care how many dots in Mind a mage has, he really can't beat vampires with their powers of mind control. The mage will never be better at territory control or hunting than a werewolf, illusions/dreams as a 'ling, etc. Mages need to study and prepare to be as good in a field; while they can do a lot, they're not too hot without that preparation or specific knowledge.

                                      And so it goes for everyone. Vampire versus werewolf. Demon versus changeling. You name it. If we can play to one group's strengths but not another's, then the one we're playing to generally will win.

                                      So, when people are comparing Beasts to werewolves, they're seeing the wolves having to play in the Beast's strengths. Not surprisingly, since Beast the game is written from that perspective. In a hunt? The wolves are far better off. Always will be.
