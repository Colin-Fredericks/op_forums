    My wife and child have been battling bouts of pneumoniæ and I've been particularly busy taking care of everyone and making sure they don't drive each other crazy. Otherwise I like to believe I'd have jumped on this thread sooner. I often find myself trying to explain things rather than sell them, I'm lousy at selling ideas, but the Bale Hounds are my favorites so here we go.

    The Bale Hounds offer opportunity to explore evil in its many forms. The antagonists of the other games, outside of Belial's Brood, really avoid a 'pure evil' label. They tend to have human aspects, and be sympathetic, or at the least understandable. Even the Pure, for all that some people hate them, are just on the other side of an argument about what's right and who's wrong. No one's really 'dedicated to evil,' but the Bale Hounds and the Brood.

    Through the Ninth Tribe, one of my favorite ideas, you get to explore what evil is when backed up by the powers of a werewolf. A Bale Hound of the Ninth Tribe is a demon incarnate, not bound by limits of ephemeral form or occult pacts and demonological stricture. These are the big bad wolves, the ones described in the write-ups of the various Maeljin in Blasphemies. They're corrupters, spreading their vice and indulging in it when they can and reveling in it. Big fan of Soulless Wolf, myself, and I have some headcanon based on some of the connections between his write-up and terms in Geist.

    The other big selling point is the pacts. These guys are the most common in the books, which says something considering how little 'text time' the Bale Hounds get. They're werewolves who wanted something, and were willing to make a deal to get it. Olivia Citysmith wanted to enact a grand plan, Feng Shui'ing her city of Chicago to maximize its Essence potential, and made a deal with a Maeljin to live long enough to do it. Adelita Sundancer saw the enemies of her people all around, and knew they were going to lose, and that loss would mean death. She didn't want to be a part of that, so she found a Maeltinet and made a deal to survive. 'She fell to darkness not out of hate but out of pragmatism and fear.' Moriarty of the Black Moon eXtreme found himself bleeding to death, his quest to wipe out the vampires of Denver doomed to never get off its feet, when he was offered a deal to become something other than roadkill. These are the more 'human' werewolves. The People make mistakes, the People can be weak, the People are hungry for something, power, renown, love. The Maeljin can give it to them. These Bale Hounds can be anyone. They don't have to be ravening psychopaths or cold-blooded killers, they're just someone who wanted something more and were corrupted by price they paid.

            In my one and only Forsaken chronicle so far one of the earliest antagonists I introduced was a Bale Hound. I introduced him as a respected elder in the greater Forsaken community who was supportive of this new pack, even had one player take dots in Mentor with him filling the role. They talked to him, sought advice from him, really had a chance to get to know him, even as they hunted the mysterious individual messing with the wound in their region. When they finally fought him he had a face and a name. On the other hand Randal 'Runs to Ground', the Pure hunter who harassed them in their own territory, tracking them, luring them into ambushes, summoning spirits to raid their locus and even fire bombing their 'Den' while they were in it...Randal really pissed them off and they took greater pleasure (and showed no mercy) in ripping his head off, but he was still just a faceless threat who only had a name because he had a reputation. In the end he was just another Boss Battle, one of many.

                        Originally posted by Miriander View Post
                        I never liked Bale Hounds, I always felt that the niche they inhabit is already filled with Pure and Ghost Wolves. I see them as a bit of a relic from older edition, those are W:tF Black Spiral Dancers influenced by 1ed Vampire: the Requiem VII. Compared to other Forsaken enemies, they seem nearly comical and like the concept was beaten to death long, long time ago.
                        Its not easy to do a demonic splat and not have them come off as comedic EVULZ. However, it is possible. It all comes down to how its run. Just take a look at the Seers and Belial's Brood books. Both are roughly analogous to Bale Hounds, and they're done in a fairly believable fashion that's actually fairly attractive in a game.

                        Another thing you have to keep in mind is that the concept of what a Bale Hound is and does has kind of evolved with the release of books. One of which was Blasphemies, and another of which was Inferno. And they're going to change again with the shift of focus away from universal Vices / sins in 2e. There's no such thing as universal evil in the CofD anymore. At least, that's not the impression I have (unless you count the Abyss). So, while there's still a place for the Bale Hounds, its one that simply cannot fit the same cartoon evil you suggest.

                        Of course, my personal problem with the Bale Hounds is figuring out what I'm going to do with them when I remove the Inferno from my 2e cityscape. I've redesigned my game to only include vamps, wolves, mages, 'lings, and mortal-GMC. I can fill the humanistic corruptor roll with werewolves, but I feel like, without that Infernal crossover feeling, there's just something missing from the Hounds.

                        So, while they don't fit every game, I do think there's room for them in a lot of games, which is all that matters, really.

                                                Bale Hounds were a relatively major part of my longest Werewolf Chronicle back in 1e.

                                                They came in three flavours.

                                                The first was the PCs mentors. The campaign began when a savage Pure attack wiped out the older half of the pack, the NPCs who had been looking after the fledgling werewolf PCs. These NPCs had become Bale Hounds due to the influence of an insane Lune who had been corrupted by Baalphegor. She had convinced the pack that they had to 'fight fire with fire' and turn to darker paths in order to help keep an ancient horror under the city restrained; she'd been set to this task ages ago but had been tainted by it as the centuries turned. The Bale Hounds kept their activities secret, but as the PCs began to dig into the pack's history they slowly uncovered various discrepancies and weird elements of what had been going on. The Pure had targeted the pack because of a particular totem that had discovered the Lune's nature. In the end, the PCs discovered that a particularly tricksy Bale Hound loose in the city, one with amazing capabilities of concealment and masking, was actually the NPC Irraka from the pack who had escaped the purge.


                                                The road to the end here involved the Lune getting free again and, before the PCs eventually discovered her corrupted nature, she managed to convince two pack members to start down the same dark path that led to the earlier generation becoming Bale Hounds. Fortunately they discovered the truth soon enough before she convinced them to do the REALLY horrific stuff for power. In the end, the mad Lune went down insane and fighting, still convinced SHE was the good one following Luna's orders and that the Lunes that were being sent to take her out were the REAL betrayers. The Bale Hound Irraka met his end down the scope of the Iron Master sniper when he attempted to protect his mistress, finally forced out of the shadows and into combat.



                                                The second were the Baalphegori Bale Hounds at work attempting to release the horror under the city, the Devouring Spiral - a spirit-god that had been killed but was too powerful to actually die, once a spirit called Echoes of the Past who represented learning from history but who had been warped by the Maeljin into a horrifying abcess wedged part way into the Underworld. These Baalphegori were manipulating the cannibal-cult of 10,000 Leaves, collecting parts of the hostile timeline's pages and using the cultists as sacrifices and meals full of hunger-Essence. They attempted to free the Devouring Spiral but mostly got stamped out and rooted out by the Forsaken.

                                                Finally was Spirals of Bright Agony, an antagonist who, if I remember, actually died well before the Chronicle began but still had a major impact on it. He was the insane, genius Bale Hound of Psuelak who had terrorised the city a generation ago, whose schemes were still unwinding and causing havoc, and who was the cause of the most powerful Forsaken in the area having been exiled from the city and hence unable to intervene much in what was going on. Pseulak had its own plans for the Devouring Spiral, and it was Spirals of Bright Agony who had orchestrated those plans. The PCs kept encountering the legacy of his actions.




                                                So for me Bale Hounds work as the Enemy Within, the Corruption, and the Uncertainty. Bale Hounds cause mistrust; they leave schemes that must be unravelled and unpicked; and they provide horrible werewolf cults that can follow different ethos and mythos to the Forsaken and the Pure. Now, in 2e, I rather like to push the latter part away from the Bale Hounds alone; my games now feature bizarre heretic or blasphemous cults that would have been Bale Hounds in 1e but don't actually have anything to do with the Maeljin.

                                                In fact, I rather like the idea of broadening Bale Hounds out as a concept on precisely that basis - all the heretics and blasphemers and weird, unrelated cults and mythos all under the same heading as far as Pure and Forsaken are concerned. Some are the classic Bale Hounds following the Maeljin, but so are any number of witch-cults, totemic vessels of gibbering alien abominations and the like.

                                                - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
