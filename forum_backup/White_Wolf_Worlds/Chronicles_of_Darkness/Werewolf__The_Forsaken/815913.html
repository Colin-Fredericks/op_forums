    [Sell me on] The Bale Hounds

    01-25-2016, 02:06 AM
    What's this? Doesn't Bunyip already know this game? Has the world gone mad? Cats and dogs living together in peace and harmony aaaarghhhh!!

    Ahem. Sorry, back to being serious.

    A lot of people really like the Bale Hounds. Some like them more than that (wyrdhamster, I'm looking at you).

    Various recent threads have had people saying how much they're looking forward to 2E Bale Hounds, and I'm curious to better understand this.

    Obviously, I'm a fan of both flavours of Werewolf - you don't need to sell me on that. But, I've never seen Bale Hounds as that interesting. With Pure and Claimed and Spirits and other Forsaken and everything else in the CofD my werewolves have had enough on their plate without me spending too much time thinking about Bale Hounds.

    But 2E is here, and now I'm thinking different flavours of werewolves. I'm thinking Pure, I'm thinking other things.

    What interests you about Bale Hounds? What do you like/love? What do you think they should look like in 2E?

    I know you're out there. Sell me on them.

    Caveat: This shouldn't in any way be interpreted as Bale Hounds being on the current writing calendar. I just think about 'what next?' for both Forsaken and Apocalypse probably more than I should

      I think the most interesting aspect of the Bale Hounds is the paranoia they can create.

      The Pure are nuanced ideological antagonists, but they're not really good for subverting the PC's views. They proselytize too overtly, even if some of them can do so with amazing skill at making the Uratha question their allegiances. You're never going to find out you've being targeted for conversion to the Pure after it is too late to turn back.

      The other antagonists in the game aren't really going to even try for this sort of thing.

      The Bale Hounds though, can really run a long game on the Uratha and slowly draw them in without outing themselves. You can never 100% know if one's trying to convert you, or something else, in service of their master.

          I like them as an opponent that unify Forsaken and Pure.
          I like them as explorations of how Forsaken view embodiments of human "sin" in werewolf form.
          I like them as corrupting influences and "the enemy within" hook.
          I like them as agents of non techgnostic "demons".
          I liked the presentation options of individual, lodge and 9th Tribe. The 9th Tribe option was my preferred option.

          As an ST I like having non PC splats to work with, the Pure aren't that anymore. I'm a fan of the Maeljin and want to see them developed into something substantial, even if they are a holdover from Apocalypse they aren't the same beings in CoD. I'd like to see them get their own Blue Book treatment that is more substantive than what they got in Inferno that can be used across multiple game lines. I'd be happy if they were part of the reason for the existence of the God Machine and it's angels, one of it's functions being that it helps keep them from infecting the system. That doesn't even have to be the reason the GM exists, just a fortunate side effect of its existence. Don't need stats, these are entities no pack is going to beat down or hunt to destruction, but I'd like to see motivations and methods explored, in relation to the larger CoD universe. They aren't defeated by direct confrontation but by countering their schemes, ala Lovecraft's Mythos entities.

              I want to get back to this, but I don't have time right now and I'd need to reread some stuff on Bale Hounds. For the moment, I leave you with one of those comparisons they teach you how to do on the SATs:

              Forsaken:Pure:Bale Hounds::Pentacle:Seers:Scelesti. Or maybe you'd prefer Traditions:Technocracy:Nephandi.

              The Pure are focused on their, well, purity. They are werewolves among werewolves that seek to avenge Father Wolf and bring back a primeval global Hunting Ground. They are the extremes of both the savagery of Flesh and and weirdness of Spirit, and their ideology borders on alien in a very distinctly spirtual way. Even the ones who are slaves serve the great spirits of the Shadow.

              The Bale Hounds are the Other. They seek out and serve things that by all rights either should not exist in the Shadow or make the shadow infinitely worse. They're corruptive, and insidious, and somewhat alien in a truly non-werewolf way. In a way, they were the original out-of-context problem that the Uratha have to deal with.

              The human version would be...think about the horror that comes with coming face to face with a full-on neo Nazi skinhead. You probably disagree fundamentally with their ideology, and in some ways they embody the worst of being human...but they are human. It's like looking in a mirror. Contrast that with looking at something that's at least somewhat fundamentally other that you can't understand, something intent on spreading that inhumanity. The Bale Hounds are the werewolf version of that.

              Or to paraphrase a discussion from Exalted, the Forsaken and the Pure are two sides of the same coin. Bale Hounds are paper money.

                          That... doesn't entirely seem like a fair comparison. It seems like it would be more useful to consider what the Maeljin add that the Idigam don't.

                          For me, the Maeljin add familiarity. For all their horrors, they represent emotional concepts we know from instinct and experience. You can understand (even if you don't condone) how someone might get caught up with an entity of destructive lust.

                          The Idigam represent the unknowable. They're terrifying because what you think you recognize as patterns or methods in them are are protean and slippery. Defeating them isn't simply difficult because of their immense power, but because they are alien to the already alien spirit world.

                                  Let me add my two cents:

                                  For me, Bale Hounds are the true antagonists of Werewolf The Forsaken. I will try to explain how they fit in my stories by comparing them to the struggles a mortal human might face.

                                  Imagine you are a farmer that lives with your family, all of you isolated somewhere in the mountains. You have animals and vegetables, both helping you sustain your family. Wild beasts sometimes kill your live stock, and you have to deal with them. Insects destroy your crops, and you apply pest control.

                                  There is another family whose farm borders yours. Sometimes you help them, sometimes they help you, they are OK neighbors and you trust them to a degree.

                                  On the other side of the mountain, there is another family of farmers, one that has been hating you for generations because your grand grand father had feud with theirs. Your relationship with them is tense at best, and you advise your children to stay away from their land.

                                  And, every now and then, really bad weather hits your land, leaving a trail of chaotic destruction that you need to repair and prepare for if you want to survive.

                                  One night, you find a lone stranger near-dead by the river. You help and nurse him, treating him like family, and after sometime everyone loves him. You feel joy when your daughter announces their marriage, and you help him build another wooden-house near yours. Time passes, they have children, and he ends up leading the family as you grow old and tired. You feel safer now, you think everything is gona be OK. Until one day he convinces you to go for a weekend of fishing, and as drowns you, he tells you that he will sell your entire land to a mining company, that he has done the same to all your neighbors, and your family will think that it was what you wanted.
                                  In this scenario, the Family is your pack, the farm is your territory, animals are the humans living there, and plants are the spirits. Wild beasts are the ocasional humans that go out of control, and insects are spirits that need to be erradicated and pest control is the change in ressonance. The first neighbors are another Forsaken pack, and the other family are the Pure that hate you. The bad weather are the Indigam, forces of nature that you cannont predict realiably and most of the time can only repair the damage caused. Well, the Bale Hound is the last guy...

                                              Disclaimer: WtF is not one of my primary gameline loves, so consider what I say from that perspective.

                                              I have an unhealthy fondness for the concepts of Temptation and agents of Corruption. As others have said, a Pure is almost always going to be obvious to you and the servants of the Idigam often too alien. But your best friend could be a Bale Hound and you would not know it. He listens, he usually gives good advice. And maybe one day you are in a serious bind but he has "friends", powerful ones that can help you, if you but do one tiny, itty favour for them. And why not? The Pure are ideological enemies and the Idigam are bizarre, but Bale Hounds and the things they serve just want you to give in. To take what you really want, -Nay!- deserve! Why bother with all this crap when you know things are not really going to get better? Just take a trip to a Wound to see how fucked everything is.

                                              Some other quick points (many have already been raised):
                                              • They give a quick and easy reason for Forsaken and Pure to work together without involving the Strange and Bizarre
                                              • They allow for introduction of werewolves with powers that are outside the general wheelhouse of regular Gifts, presenting surprising challenges while keeping it in a werewolf package
                                              • They expand the mythology in interesting ways

                                              Lastly, from a more holistic viewpoint, they make the Chronicles of Darkness seem more coherent. Demonic entities are a thing all shadow-kin encounter and it is not just vampires who can align with them (i.e. Belial's Brood) or mages who make pacts with them. Werewolves too can form major groups around a common setting element.

                                                  Here's the thing about Bale Hounds - they almost require the presence of Wounds and the Maeljin. The maeljin and wounds, much like the entire Inferno thing, seem to have gained less importance as Virtues / Vices changed from being the objective good/evil vibes, and now represent other things. Yes, comparisons to Belial's Brood, Scelesti, and Loyalists stand out. These are the werewolves who've sold themselves to "demons" and other beings of incredible evil. In many ways, they're the new Black Spiral Dancers. But they're kind of limited, again by that whole Wound thing. Wounds are pretty much almost restricted, by nature, to cities as well.

                                                  Wait, you say. This is supposed to be a thread about SELLING the Bale Hounds. Not bashing them. Go somewhere else, you hater.

                                                  But wait, I say. I'm just setting the stage. I have yet begun to sell. Ahem. Now, lets get into the meat of the matter.


                                                  Wounds are pretty much the Infernally tainted areas of the Hisil. And, with all things Inferno, we're looking at the embodiments of human foils and failings. Think about that for a moment. The Pure represent werewolves who want to exalt the Shadow over the Flesh. The Forsaken want to balance the worlds. The Bale Hounds... want to exalt the Flesh over the Shadow. They're the embodiment human antipathy to the world around them, seeing it as a wasted thing only good for living in it yourself. They represent the threat that none of the other sacred prey do, save humanity itself. Its seeing the dangers of the Flesh maginified several fold, and seeing it in your own people.

                                                  The Pure are good antagonists because they serve as one kind of mirror. The Bale Hounds serve as a mirror for the other extreme.

                                                      Since I really like the Bale Hounds, I hope I can play, too.

                                                      To answer this question, I can sum it up in one word: familiarity. The Bale Hounds aren't an outside force that seeks to eradicate the Tribes of the Moon (or, to a lesser extent, the Pure). They can be anyone - your brother, your friend, your packmate. Think about any long-term friendship you've had. You know that person well - you can probably finish sentences for them. You've seen their triumphs, and, more pertinent to this discussion, you've seen their failures. A Bale Hound is like that - he's not from "that pack down the street," he's been in your pack for years. He knows every pack member's foibles, their worries, and their fears. And when the time is right, he pulls the strings that keep the pack together. Tugs them as far as they'll go and beyond, until they snap, and the pack tears itself apart in blood and viscera.

                                                      The idigam can't do that because no matter how much they study and plot and plan, they're still an outsider.

                                                      The Bale Hounds are the most dangerous prey for one simple reason: they're your friend.
