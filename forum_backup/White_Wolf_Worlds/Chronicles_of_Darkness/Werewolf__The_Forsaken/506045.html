    What Happens When You Run for Only Two Players?

    05-06-2015, 05:04 PM
    One of the things that I have trouble with in White Wolf/Onyx Path games and storytelling/roleplaying games in general is that they always seem to be designed to be played by a large group of people.
    Almost every game I know has a variety of specific roles for players to take and usually there are quite a lot of them.

    Now, my entire troupe is comprised of two players and myself as the storyteller.
    Werewolf seems to assume there are five players at the table.

    QUESTIONS: How does the game accommodate a smaller number of players than usual? Can two werewolves even form a pack numbering just themselves?
    What are the difficulties in running a chronicle just for two players and how can I overcome them?

    I was thinking of granting extra dots at character creation since, I’m sorry, but an arrangement of 5/4/3 in attributes and 11/7/4 in skills is extremely limiting, especially in a system that rests on a set target number of 8 for all dice rolls and eats away at dice pools with fluctuating modifiers, encouraging specialized roles, min-maxing and characters limited to being good at only one or two things – limitations that I think may be understandable in groups of 4+ players, but with only two characters, it’s exacerbated.
    I was thinking of upping those allocations to at least 6/5/4 and 12/8/5, and granting an extra dot in Totem each since the players will be already stretched thin in the Merit department as is.

          The first part of the Werewolf Chronicler's Guide gives some advice for running 'Lone Wolf' games.

          There's two primary ways of doing it. One, you offer a bunch of NPCs to flesh out a pack. Or two, you just have a lone wolf. Since a pack doesn't have to be werewolves right now, you can allow for the other wolf-blooded and human or Other members of the pack to help out with the totem and allow for 'time off' from being a werewolf and letting the player explore stories from other points of view.

          That said, Werewolf is based around packs for a reason. It can be a combat-heavy game, and if you're Storytelling that you're going to have to balance out the antagonists by making them weaker or less numerous so that they can be dealt with by a single character. A smart werewolf can overcome greater odds by being, obviously, smart about it. Researching a targets bane before hunting them can go a great way to making a lopsided fight more even, researching bans can cripple spirits and other things and lead them into traps.

          I think you do get a different experience with games like that. There is much less discussion between players at the table about what to do or pondering what something is. Combat tends to be faster. You don't have to develop and tie together storylines for a table full of people. So you'd definitely want to take advantage of that and weave the players background into whatever story you have going for them.

          I really don't think more stats is that necessary. You might just give them extra starting experience to let them build their own paragon werewolf. You could give a single player 5 totem points and keep wolf-blooded the same and it will not be that big a deal in terms of 'balance.'

                  Being as I play games with just my wife sometimes, I really wanted to comment on this thread. Elfive and nofather have put forth some great suggestions, though.
                  Werewolf is tons of fun, and pack is a very important element in it. To get that feel with only two players, having them play multiple characters or having there be NPCs in the pack are both great ways to go. You could also take a middle ground on this approach and have two to three other wolves in the pack that you control when everyone is together, but get taken over by the players when the pack splits up.

                  The other thing to do is work the story of just two wolves. I mentioned this in the long discussion of how Siskur-Dah works, but every auspice and tribe is a tool, and stories about not having the right tools can be just as interesting as stories about having them. Let your people specialize, then talk to them as people, as players. If they're down for stories where their weaknesses are really called out, then run stories against the grain they've built. Otherwise, play it to make them look awesome.
                  Say they make combat monsters, which is easy to do in WtF. It's the "you've got a hammer so every problem looks like a nail" scenario. If they want a story of trouble, them throw political intrigue at them. If they'd have more fun being awesome, then throw things that need to die at them.
                  Another angle on the not-the-right-tool story is the swallow-your-pride story. At some point, they're going to have to ask for help. Finding the right friend to help get the job done can be a fun to play out. Hell, that's half the point of social stories.

                  My last note for now is this: if you feel like extra stats will make the game more fun, give them extra stats. If too many negative modifiers are making the game no fun, stop giving them out, even if the book says you're supposed to. Or give out lighter ones. Its a game. Having fun is the point. If the rules are getting in the way of that, they explicitly say to start ignoring them.

                          I solo game (as in I'm the only player and storyteller) and although I usually play a couple of characters, I've never set up a pack with more than 3 werewolves. I feel like it takes too much admin to run more than that many characters.

                          My advice for a small pack would be for each player to have a primary and secondary role, this allows them to cover more possible circumstances without spreading themselves too thin. Also, with 2ed pack structure it's expected that you will have wolf-blooded or even human pack members to help out. You can let them fill in some of the other roles (some appropriate Tells can really help here), and if the limelight shifts into their area of specialty, let the players take over playing them.

                          A troupe playing style could also help out. Have each player create a werewolf and 2 wolf blooded (for example), at any given time, each player will play one character, but they can choose from the 3 they've created. If it's siskur-dah, most likely everybody takes their werewolf, but maybe one of the wolf-blooded is an expert social manipulator, maybe one is a historian or occultist, maybe one is an expert hacker. There are times where the furry killing machine isn't actually the best guy to have on your team.

                          There's a lot of possibilities with a small group and the advantage is that you can cater to exactly what you players want as you don't have to many play styles and tastes to deal with. A lot of people have offered awesome advice and ideas here, talk it over with your players and you can come up with a great chronicle.

                                  Like Terraben it usually winds up just being me an my wife these days. Everyone else has moved on or done something else with their lives.

                                  So for one werewolf + wolf blooded companion I try to keep the storyline geared for a 1 wolf scenario. Yeah that means keeping something like the idigam down, or not involving a pure "rape the land" festival (for now) but that's ok. The goal is for your players to have fun, not to kick them in the junk about how alone they are.

                                  In tribes of the moon there's a rumor about a blood talon who runs an entire city solo, and massacres any who step into his territory. So canon wise I'd say its doable (he's probably primal urge 5 though >.>). It is ofcourse ok to give seemingly insurmountable odds, but more so you can see how they handle a situation. My wife handled taking out her werewolf hunters pretty brilliantly, even involving combat by her lonesome with her partner. She's an Iron master cop in the lodge of the shield (contacts yo) and her wolf blooded is her partner. So they use cop tactics for everything in the hunt. Stake out the hunters, investigate their lair, get the drop on them with their standard weaponry. As an officer she has pretty decent gear, and her terrible capabilities as a cop are covered up by the lodge. Does the lodge really work that way in 2e? I have no idea, but she likes it so there we are.

                                  Point is, it's completely doable, just gotta find ways to work it with 2 players or less.

                                              I think a small group is great for Werewolf.
                                              Solo-playing too.
                                              In our Wolfblloded Chronicle, (soon-to-be-Werewolf) the player wanted a big pack.
                                              Lots of central NPC's.
                                              This rooted player's interest in the pack and all things related to her newfound family.

                                              We even made a system where the player can access some of the npc's strengths in-game as a merit.
                                              Some of the pack members are better in action-scenarios for example, Others have a better insight into the beings of spirits.
                                              They would gladly lend this knowledge to the player.
                                              It became a variation on the Mentor / totem merit, but less focus on mentoring and more on giving assist to what she already was doing, there and then.
                                              She could only pick one bonus at a time (and only for as long as she was wolf-blooded).
                                              As the merit grows she can add more bonuses to matters at hand as long as the pack member were around her.
                                              I think we called it "Tagalong".

                                              In other games we made a house rule called "assuming control", where players could take control of one or more pack members in combat if they were comfortable with it.
                                              Sometimes it worked, but if players could not properly read the roles of the pack members on the sheet (sending the Ithaeur charging against a predator king, when he was cut off from the pack might not be so healthy).

                                              I have been flirting with the idea of letting the player control others in the pack for their own stories.
                                              This would let the player see something from a very different perspective.
                                              Something to ponder.
