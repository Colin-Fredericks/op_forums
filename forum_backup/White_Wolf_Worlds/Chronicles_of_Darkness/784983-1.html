    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
    So, finally, we get the rules for making Investigation scenarios to run in Chronicles of Darkness corebook, getting the Clues and all. Only, I feel a bit confused by them. From mine reading it sounds like this:

    1. Character are doing some actions to get Clues.
    2. They get Clue with some elements.
    3, They can spend elements to get more Clues.
    4. When they are having enough Clues - voila, mystery solved! Characters get some epiphany and now have whole story. With one brillant light of understanding.

    The problem I have is in part of 2 and 3 I think - Where is "we get Facts A, and B - so we are going for Fact C" part of it?

    How to run, for example, Abyssal Intruder research by this system? How could this all look like in story?
    1. You need to define the event that prompts the investigation, that affords the characters with motivation (and Aspirations) to start digging.

    2. The onus on the how and what they wish to learn are on the players. Some might want to perform social maneuvering to be allowed to enter the censor's personal library, for example. It depends on resources.

    3. They spend elements to facilitate getting new clues, yes. If, in the example above, they get a clue from the library as to the next location to feature the intrusion or phenomenon, the story is moving forward.

    4. That should be at the climax. Even if it is confronting the antagonist with him making the confession whilst holding someone dear to the character's hostage.

But next sentence it says you can "guess" the Truth, but with incomplete Clues, giving complications - but instead giving mechanics, it's just state complications, not how characters do this to "get the Truth".

                As re-reading whole subsystem, I suspect that you can Uncover the Truth with less than all Clues by some Investigation (Skill) roll, but I'm not sure here.
                I think the intention is actually that you do need to have the minimum number of clues to uncover the truth, but the complications arise based on how many elements you have left by the time you get all of those clues. Based on this quote from page 79:
                Once the characters reach the required number of Clues, they’re able to Uncover the Truth if that’s their goal. They can do so without any additional rolls if they spend a number of Clue elements (divided however they like) equal to the required Clues for the investigation.
                I also like the risk/reward dynamic that this interpretation creates. You can spend Elements to help you get more Clues (or to help on other rolls related to the Clue), but doing so means that you might have to face future consequences when you finally uncover the truth, unless you make up for it by finding some clues with extra elements.
