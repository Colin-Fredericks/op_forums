            Put it this way, you have a job that gives you money in excess of what you need to live at your current pace. That's Resources. You have a wad of a few hundred dollars that you stole from a bank in your pocket. That's the Cash equipment. You have a nice car collection. That's it's own Equipment. You have a respected position in a company that allows you to call favours and influence policy. That's Status. You take a pay cut, you lose Resources. You get XP, you decide you want to use that XP to learn Firearms skill. You don't have a gun, but you also don't have the Resources anymore to buy it, so you give up your Cash for it. You still have your Status though, so you can make sure that Jeffreys the Janitor looks the other way when he finds you mishandling documents in another department, because you're a respected higher up and can get him fired. These are all separate ideas.

            So you have the disposable wealth that you get in a constant basis, that's Resources. You have disposable wealth you got from a one time deal, that's Cash. You have non-disposable wealth that's it's own Equipment.

                Originally posted by OsuNeko View Post
                And what if character does not have this Merit but only his Status dots? or you wont to say that character with Status Merit have authority to pressure?
                A character with high Status in an organization that would pay well, but no dots in Resources would have an income that is consistent, but not disposable. They make lots of money, but it all goes to paying for expenses. If economic pressure causes them to lose that job, they might lose the Status dots representing it (which would be refunded with Sanctity of Merits). They would also lose their source of income, and potentially anything they owned that relied on that income. For example, if they lost their house, they would lose their Housing Equipment and potentially any Safe Place dots invested in securing it (the latter being refunded with Sanctity of Merits). If they lost their car, they would lose the Vehicle.

                                        The answer is a bit more complicated than that. A player doesn't have to "back up" their character's Status with Resources, because Resources represents income that is both consistent and disposable, and it is entirely possible for a character to have high standing in a well-paying job and still not have disposable income, if all of their income is going towards paying off recurring expenses. Heck, a player doesn't even have to "back up" their character's important CEO job with Status dots, because Status represents power within the organization, and it's entirely possible for a CEO to delegate all of his responsibilities to other employees and have no real power. But if the question is, "should a player back up his character's stuff with Merits?" then the answer is, "it depends." It depends on the character's story and what mechanical advantages the player wants to get out of it.

                                                        The Resource merit doesn't represent wither or not your character owns a million dollar Ferrari or has the ability to save up for one in the future, instead it represents wither or not your character has the means to buy a car on short notice, right now and in cash.
                                                        It's an abstract system that is only meant to effect your character's available resources during a play session, not what he may gain during the long empty spaces in between.
                                                        On a related note I seem to remember reading somewhere that you can temporarily boost your effective Resource dots by taking a condition that temporarily reduces them afterwards, representing your character having leveraged himself financially and the time it takes to climb out of the red.
                                                        This wouldn't help a character with zero dots in Resources, if he wants that car he is going to have to steal it (or borrow it, exchange favors for it or use a different merit to achieve it).
                                                        All of that in-between stuff is for the player and story teller to work out, outside of the rules. It just shouldn't be available any time the character discovers he needs something right now.
