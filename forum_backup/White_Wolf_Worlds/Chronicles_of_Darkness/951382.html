    Ephemeral beings' Influence ; How does it work?

    08-15-2016, 07:59 PM
    I've been playing CofD for ages now it seems. But our group have for the most part played Requiem and Ephemeral beings ain't a big deal there. And the ones I've used sure has had some interesting Influence purview but I've never really used them. The vagueness if what they are capable off is just a bit too arbitrary in my mind. But now we've started a mage chronicle and ephemeral beings are way more important in Mage then in Vampire. And one of my players is portraying an Thyrsus Shaman and another is portraying a Mastigos Psychonaut. Though they are not capable of summoning spirits or goetia yet, it will come. So i need to get a better understanding of what you can actually do with influence, both in a broad sense but also in a more defined sense with practical examples. I don't want to be a arbitrary judge at the gaming table so some rules consistency would help here.

    So to help you out, here comes some examples of Influences spirits in my chronicle have, but im not sure actually what they can do.

    Influence: Comradery 2

    Influence: Wisdom 3

    Influence: justice 2

    Influence: Fairness 2

    Influence: Cold 5

    Influence: indoctrination 3


    So for example what can you concretely do with these? And how "much" would you gain so to speak. Mages spells have potency for example if you want to increase a skill roll with +2, thats a potency 2 spell. What would be the equivalence of potency for Influence?

      Have you looked at the chart on pg. 256? Influence 1 can act like Perfecting, Compelling, Unveiling and Knowing, Influence 2 is basically like Ruling, Influence 3 is like Weaving and Patterning, Influence 4 is like Making and Influence 5 is like Making with Advanced Scale and the option to make effects Lasting. Obviously, ephemeral entities have no respect for the actual Practices, but combining the descriptions of how Practices function with the description of Influences might give you some help. One thing to note, ephemeral entities have no ability to reduce or destroy things that fall under the purview of their Influence. That would run entirely contrary to their nature after all. That's how I try to grok it anyway.

                          A spirit might Influence an emotion tied with a character's Vice or Virtue to give it a full Willpower replenishment instead of a single dot, but I think such situations would be rare. You would only be adding dots in skills if said spirit was associated with that skill, like the God of Blades bestowing a Strengthen blessing on a swordsman. Attributes too. But at that point you have to deal with Duration if you don't want to get abstract with it.

                          Similar to the Defense thing below I'd probably have changing it to an Extreme Environment be based on a number of successes, limited by Rank. A Rank 2 cold spirit isn't going to have the power to freeze an entire room.

                          Giving out Conditions is probably a good way to represent certain Effects, since that's what Conditions are.

                          Take for instance Strengthen, one use of it is to give someone under its purview extra Defense. There is a lot of difference between +1, +1 per success and + rank or anything else.

                          I just have a problem going from abstracts to mechanics in this case.
                          I'd be tempted to let it add Successes to Defense to a max of spirit's Rank. Maybe more if there's an Exceptional Success or a Numina that allows one to spend extra Essence.

                          For combat, you're going to be working more with Numina than Influences. But you can look at how they work, mechanically, to judge how you think Influences should.

                          I checked in Mage, got the updated version and everything and it didn't really say that the different levels of Influence offer powers similar to the Arcana. But it's probably a good way to estimate whether something is doable or not.

                                  This is actually described a bit further on pg. 258. As total •'s of Influence == Rank, Rank is already a limiter. So let's take a Göetic demon (I'm old-school, and imo Göetia is the name of the practice, not the spirits it produces) with Rank 3 and Influence: Lust. The entity can Strengthen existing Lust for up to 1 hour/success, paying 1 or 2 Essence (depending on Duration desired), and rolling Power + Finesse vs. Resolve/Composure + Supernatural Tolerance. Success would open 1 Door of the target (or alternatively modify the subject's Impression upward by a stage per net success). However, this cannot induce Lust, only strengthen it where it already exists.

                                  This same entity could warp Love into carnal Lust (or change the target of the subject's existing Lust) for up to 10 mins/success for the cost of 2 Essence and by winning that same Contested roll, or change Fear into Lust for 1 min/success for 3 Essence by doing likewise. These effects would correspond to an Influence Condition and an Influence Tilt, respectively.

                                  Now let's turn this into a concrete example. A Guardian of the Veil needs to convince a mark to leave a party with her in order to take the mark's place at work tomorrow in a Seer-controlled company, but the subject's macho body-builder boyfriend is keeping everyone away from her. In order to keep her Signature from being read on the mark by the Seers, she decides to have her Lust demon help her out. Lust manifests as an extremely attractive woman who distracts the man, using an instance of it's Influence: Lust. The first usage is to Strengthen the lust that the macho body-builder naturally feels towards its Manifested form.

                                  The Mage capitalizes on the opportunity provided by the distraction to chat up the mark, who she knows from her recon previously had a serious relationship with her ex flat-mate. She commands Lust to use its Influence again, but this time to Manipulate the target of the mark's lust onto the Mage herself, and then to Strengthen that Lust to make her mundane efforts at picking up the mark easier.
