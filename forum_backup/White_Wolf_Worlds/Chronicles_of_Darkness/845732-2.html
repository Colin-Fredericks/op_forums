    You've got it just right on Contracts. Use the XP value as the means of judging their Asset rating. Forcing them to use just the Catches is certainly an interesting way to produce an atmosphere for the Beneficiary, that's for sure. Alternatively, if you don't want the Pact to bite quite as much, you could give them the Goblin Vow Merit with the Pact, enabling the player to redefine the terms of their agreement whenever it is most convenient.

    In terms of recurring/repeating transactions, I don't see why not. Performing constant tasks is definitely an aspect of a Sacrifice, with the Assets being the restored Willpower/Integrity dot. The Conditional Factor of that Asset would be "whenever they lose a Willpower/Integrity dot" and would translate to N+1. When Terms N+1 run out, and they gain all their Benefits, they are free- they no longer have to perform the perpetual Sacrifice. Making them subject to a Sanction is a good idea, but I would hesitate to make the total value of the Sanctions in terms of just Willpower and Integrity more than a reversal of ten restored dots each, because that would tear the character immediately to the lowest level of Soul Loss and make them pretty close to unplayable. Sanctions are more interesting when they become more than just a loss of power, but when they force other kinds of losses- such as, for example, a quest to make amends.

    It's also worth mentioning that Sanctions are a form of mitigation on the part of the Patron, so they can keep piling on the Sacrifices. A Patron who makes this deal will utterly own the Beneficiary, mind, body, and soul- probably literally. The only way to keep it up is to match the N+1 Benefits with N+1 Sacrifices, which could be perpetual tasks, payments of absurd amounts of money, giving the Patron a house, essentially becoming their slave, etc, etc, etc. Sure, you have an inviolate soul, but that's because it's held in the grasp of a tyrannical god-like creature. Actually... that's a really interesting character idea right there.

                I'm thinking of codifying the rules for Hedge Sorcerers (Sorcerer being a gender neutral term here - Hedge Mage, Hedge Witch, etc would also be valid). A couple of ideas:
                • Standardizing or streamlining the method for "summoning" Patrons to get you more powers.
                • Creating mechanical advantages and disadvantages for the sources of your power - "buying" a Contract off a hobgoblin might mean an easier deal, but your source of power is at risk, while contracting with a force of reality or the Wyrd itself (by any name), might net you inviolate power, but cost you dearly.
                  • I'm thinking of just automatically granting the higher Powers mitigation in line with the Just That Good reasoning.


                Plot/Chronicle Idea(s):
                Playing a group of Hedge Sorcerers that are part of some organization. Their oaths to the organization are part of an inherent Pact pre-established with the founder of the organization (Swearing of the Oath and carrying out of duties opts members into the Pact). Maybe model the organization off of Mystery Cult Initiation (granting Goblin Vow at the higher levels)? Or maybe as a Cult with Second Sight and/or Patron?

                Antagonist is a powerful Hedge Sorcerer that has managed to secure a source of Glamor to fuel their abilities (meaning they can just bypass the Catch requirements and use their powers whenever). PCs need to destroy or co-opt their source of power.

                (General Pact based) Antagonist is immortal/invulnerable/powerful through a series of Pacts with various Patrons - they are currently unassailable. The PCs need to track down and eliminate the Antagonist's Patrons (thus ending the Pacts by cutting out the source of power). Kind of like various "kill the sub-bosses before taking on the final boss" games.

                                        Vent0 To answer your questions summoning a Patron to make additional Pacts could be modeled with the existing Research and Summoning rules for Ephemeral Beings. Determine a number of successes dependent on relative Supernatural Resistance level (compared to Rank) and how public or private an individual they are. Public reduces the threshold of success, private increases it.

                                        The potential fragility of Pacts is actually baked in to the system, as each Pact needs a marker and should it be destroyed the Pact is null and void. However, using the Hobgoblin as an example, killing the Hobgoblin without destroying the marker (even if it's an abstract) might not necessarily end the Pact as the Hobgoblin is simply teaching the Contracts, not providing the power for it. Killing a Ghoul's Regnant doesn't remove their knowledge of Disciplines. Destroying an Ephemeral Being providing you with power would probably void the Pact, however. Bargaining with a cosmic being directly means greater power- seeing as you should judge the limits of a certain Patron -and that inherently encourages equal levels of Sacrifices.

                                        In regards to Supernatural Resistance, there's a Merit in the Hurt Locker spoilers that handles that. It's actually called Supernatural Resistance and is rated from 1-5, granting all the usual effects to the purchaser. It is available to everyone who has at least one dot of Supernatural Merits. If nothing else, your Hedge Sorcerers should get the ability to make more than one Oath. Gaining the ability to store Essence/Mana/Glamour etc would be a Greater Benefit (+3), unless you want to model it with a Merit. Skin Thief has the Essence Pool sub-trait, rated 1-5, and it's a common homebrew Sleepwalker Merit and Wolf-Blooded Tell. A Glamour pool Merit could be drawn up rather easily with similar results.

                                        Replenishing Essence/Vitae/Mana/Glamour is easy enough. I'd grant it as a one-time Benefit with a number of points equal to the equivalent XP of the rating, and the concurrent Sacrifice being suffering Lethal Damage, performing an equivalent task, or gaining a particular Condition. If you want to improve an existing pool permanently, suffer a Persistent Condition of some kind. Something like that is present in Mage 1e with Mana Investiture Pacts bring made with Acamoth and such. It's easy enough to draw up a Pact basically meaning Deed for Fuel. And in regards to Changeling, I believe Requiem for Regina mentions something named Glamour Fonts that exist where the border between Ironside and Arcadia is thin. I assume they're something like Loci for Faerie, but it doesn't go into greater detail.

                                        In regards to your organization of Hedge Sorcerers, why don't you post additional ideas in the Cults thread and we can flesh it out there? I'm always happy to test out a system.
