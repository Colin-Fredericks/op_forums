    The Predator movies

    To varying degrees they do capture the idea of a group of armed, well-trained individuals that have to deal with something utterly inhuman, with superior technology and abilities, and how woefully unprepared they are. And how even for a group of soldier meatheads, the unknown monster(s) in the jungles can be utterly terrifying.

    The third movie, Predators, even has an example of a hunter (Lawrence Fishburne's character) that's become so utterly paranoid and survivalist that he's willing to kill off the first human contact that he's seen in years, just to take his stuff.

    On a similar note, Aliens. Just because you have the biggest and most amount of guns doesn't mean you are any more prepared to deal with the creatures in the night.

    EDIT: Also, you could characterize the Predator aliens as being Slashers if you so wish: unstoppable killing machines that actually are inhuman.

                            Dracula is in many ways more a Hunter novel than a Vampire one.

                            The Inspector McLean novels of James Oswald are about a Glasgow police inspector trying not to believe he's taken up the Vigil, and how his life intersects with people who know better.

                            The Exorcism of Emily Rose is about a lawyer's first brush with the maybe-supernatural, and the question of whether she'll keep looking at the dark.

                            Weirdly perhaps, Pretty Little Liars. They're not facing anything supernatural - you could characterise some of their enemies as Genius slashers, but others are just mundane criminals. But they're a small group targeted by forces only they can see, and which they have to keep secret for the safety of the people around them. It shows both resourcefulness and mistakes as they investigate, is full of secrets and sins on all sides, has a shady mental hospital at the centre of everything, and shows the slow disintegration of the heroines' lives and how hard it is for them to maintain relationships with people outside their "cell." It also has a more strictly supernatural spinoff, but that didn't last long (and I haven't seen it).

                            The Bletchley Circle is a great model for a hunter cell.

                                    If other tabletop games are allowed, then I can't praise the new standalone Delta Green enough. It's about a semi-sanctioned conspiracy within the American federal government (primarily law enforcement, intelligence, and military, though every branch can technically be in on it) fighting the forces of the Cthulhu Mythos, but it leans incredibly hard into the grit of keeping up the Vigil. Not only is focused on the high lethality and intense cost to mental health that most Cthulhu games features, it also mechanically codifies your important interpersonal relationships and has optional rules for trying to maintain your cover job as well; agents are almost certainly locked into a spiral of burning bridges with the people they care about as they're forced to lie about what they've seen and done. It's intense as hell, and is exactly what I want out of my Hunter games.

                                                            Despite the protagonist being a wizard and bumping shoulders with the supernaturally powerful, Dresden Files is a good resource as many times it shows ordinary humans fighting monsters. From Special Investigations to The Knights of The Cross to Marcone's crime syndicate, it can really display the power mortals have when they band together. Many of the older Anthology shows reminiscent of the midnight society, goosebumps despite its leaning to a younger audience is still about mortals fighting the supernatural.
