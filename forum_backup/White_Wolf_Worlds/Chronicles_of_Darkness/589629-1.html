        Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
        Yeah, you can justify pretty much any attribute/skill spread if you try hard enough.
        I prefer to think of it as using extreme spreads to justify really different stories. Sarcasm asides, so long as the backstory suggests an interesting story arc and unique consequences, the extremes put forth are just as fine a character concept as any other one. There's no wrong way to do this sort of thing. I mean, solidifying it a little bit-hang on a second.

        Originally posted by Leetsepeak View Post

        And there, the question is answered!

        Some combination of Ben Stiller in Dodgeball and Vin Diesel in the Fast & Furious?
        Yeah, there we go. Imagine someone with on-screen Vin Diesel intensity doing Dodgeball. People get crazy passionate about things, and that can lead to a lot of depreciation of other things in life. Sure, the guy's scary and intense to watch on the court, and that makes him amazing in a fight as well, but he's super quiet, and when he talks, it's all awkward as hell. Dude's even more at the top of his game in unlife, but since he has to work at night, this creates problems. What happens when the blessing becomes a curse, when his old way of life is no longer viable? You've taken a beast of a man in his hyperspecialization and taken him out of his context, and he can't go back in. What's more, his ability is passive in a lot of ways. Sure, he can throw knives as well as balls, so he's not bad as an enforcer, but the dude was in for the game, and this sort of thing...well, good question time. Did Dodgeball become passionless for him, so the transition is smooth? Does that bother him? Does that make him wonder what he was really living for? If he was still in it for the fun of the game, then an enforcer's role is not going to gel with him. It was okay when dodgeball was the only thing he needed in life, but now he needs to reach out to someone even if he's going to try and return to his old ways and get away with it, and that mean living beyond his sport. Can he do it? Will he keep himself as he tries?

        So you have a character who's just had everything ripped out from under him and now is wondering where to go with himself, and if he even can go anywhere else. Is he already set to surrender to the Beast, just a slight shove away from sliding down the blood-slick ramp of his humanity? Or can he find something new to live for, or even defy the world and bring his passion into the Society of the Night?

        Much like a white-room scenario is useless without context, base assumptions about attributes-as-character don't mean much until we consider them in the right context. Can White Room Combat happen in a game? Sure. Shouldn't be the norm, because that's boring, but maybe it goes Shonen Jump style at the end and all that matters is what the character brings to the table. Can stories be used to justify having awesome attributes and nothing else? Sure, but it cuts both ways-attributes can suggest a dynamic story if one's willing to tear it apart.

        Broad readings. You can do a lot with them.

        That's why the answer to the OP's question is "Go try this in a game and see if it happens." Because in here, it's all just icons on a screen.

        EDIT: Can we get a movie of this? Vin Diesel as Dodgeball Ben Stiller trying to control his thirst for blood and live beyond himself while bringing the game of dodgeball into the Society of Night? I'd pay to see that.

                            It helps that there's a lot to do other than 'I attack the target.'                                             Originally posted by Maina View Post

                                            I don't have problems with 2e combat, but I just haven't found this to be true at all. My group had maybe one or two people (out of three players) gain one or two Willpower between them over a dozen sessions.

                                            Mask/Dirge require particularly specific circumstances, usually to some extreme degree, for you to see any Willpower. This usually means derailing the campaign specifically for each player to have a chance to regain Willpower.

                                            Touchstones (I vaguely recall them being an alternate method) require players specifically go out of their way to derail things and meet up with their Touchstone to gain Willpower that way.

                                            I've basically had to rely on timeskips to refresh people to full, because people have to derail the campaign to get so much as a point back. That only really even works because I have maybe one fight every four or so sessions, so people aren't spending a lot of it.
                                            We've talked about this before, and I don't mean to pick at it, but you shouldn't have to do anything extreme to get a single Willpower point from your Mask or Dirge. As the book says, err on the side of giving the point.

                                            For Mask, you just have to overcome some small hurdle in keeping up your front. Are you a Guru? Get a point by giving advice that sounds good, or by propping up your authority on a subject when it's called into question.

                                            Dirge isn't meant to actually be harder, but may be difficult if you consider doing a scene for one or two players to be derailing the chronicle. The default mode of play for Vampire involves characters pursuing their own goals (in addition to the coterie's goals) and living their own (so-called) lives. So they get their own spotlight scenes. That said, err on the side of giving points out. If a character is a Monster and kicks the crap out of someone, give the player a point.

                                            For a game that's more action-procedural, you can make up archetypes to suit team roles, and give out Willpower for fulfilling those. For example, use the archetypes from the Leverage RPG: Grifter, Hacker, Hitter, Mastermind, Thief.

                                            Touchstones can also be woven into the procedural part of a chronicle. On Forever Knight, we see a lot of Natalie because it's a cop show and she's the one doing the autopsies. On Angel, Angel interacts with Kate because they're solving the same cases, and later with Eve because she's his link to the Senior Partners. (Cordelia, a full member of Angel's team, would also be an easy Touchstone, but she's probably a PC.) Kindred: The Embraced is basically the story of all of the PCs who insisted on taking Frank as a Touchstone. Laura is Carmilla's Touchstone, and that's easy because they live together.

                                            An easy way to inject more Willpower into a game is to hand it out in addition to Beats. (I often do this instead of Beats for one-shots.) Player hits a character's Aspiration? Give them a point of Willpower on top of their Beat.

                                            You can also give out extra Willpower the way you might give out extra Beats. Player has a good roleplay moment? Take a point.

                                            Another option is to give vampires a point of Willpower for sleep, like humans.

                                            Finally, you can refresh Willpower at the beginning of game sessions, rather than at the usual intervals. Whether this works or not depends on how many scenes your sessions run and how much combat you're doing. Generally, if you run moderate-to-long and/or fighty, giving players Willpower at the start of a session works well.

                                                Originally posted by Maina View Post
                                                Mask/Dirge require particularly specific circumstances, usually to some extreme degree, for you to see any Willpower. This usually means derailing the campaign specifically for each player to have a chance to regain Willpower.
                                                Something I think a lot of people miss is that for the example Masques and Dirges, the actions listed are explicitly called out as sample actions that regain Willpower. They're not all you regain Willpower from. For Masques and Dirges you get Willpower for:

                                                Originally posted by Vampire: The Requiem 2nd edition

                                                Any time a vampire overcomes a small hurdle in defense of
                                                her Mask, she gains a point of Willpower. When committing
                                                atrocious or existentially risky acts in defense of her Mask, she
                                                regains all her spent Willpower points.


                                                Any time a vampire withdraws from his outside life in
                                                defense of his truer self, he gains a point of Willpower. When
                                                committing terrible, damning acts in defense of his personal
                                                identity, he regains all his spent Willpower points.
                                                These activities should be some of the main meat of the campaign and a lot of what your stories revolve around. If they're not...well, the game is sort of designed around it.

                                                I think a lot of folks run into problems with Willpower in Requiem because their campaigns focus around fundementally different things than what the book assumes they do. If that's the case, you're going to want to switch up Willpower gaining methods so that players get rewarded for particpating in the activities you see as being the focus of the game.


                                                Originally posted by Maina View Post
                                                Touchstones (I vaguely recall them being an alternate method) require players specifically go out of their way to derail things and meet up with their Touchstone to gain Willpower that way.
                                                Again, if you're finding Touchstones are derailing things, you might want to consider taking them out and replacing them with something else. If you don't find that interacting with Touchstones is a pretty natural part of what you want to focus your game on, ripping them out is probably smart and replacing them with something else. Like, if instead of focusing on humanity and the interplay because your character's vampire life and the human life they're leaving behind, instead you focus on vampire politics, maybe replace Touchstones with like "Alliegences" or something like that and have characters refocus by getting in touch with their vampire political loyalties. If you run an Underworld-style game where folks are going around doing like missions against the enemies of vampirekind, you can replace Touchstones with "Missions" or something, and have characters do tasks to forward their mission in order to get the same benefits from connecting with Touchstones, etc.

                                                    As a rule of thumb for all the 2nd edition WoD:

                                                    Give willpower almost every scene. Give lots of willpower every awesome scene.

                                                    Mask, Dirge, Blood, Bone, Vice, it doesn't matter. In the end, what the game intends to do is to set a tone for the characters behavior, giving the player a quirk to interpret. Every time he does so, he should recover a single willpower point, and to do so should be easy enough to be accomplished almost allways. It isn't something to derail the game, it is something to set the tone of how the character plays out in any scene without stepping out of it.

                                                    The full willpower reward is a little trickier, but sums up to be the Higher Calling of the character: when the character achieves to FOCUS an important scene upon his Calling, he deserves to refresh all willpower.

                                                    Vampire just add a little twist or two. Once by turning both Quirk and Higher Calling into the same thing. Second by turning the quirk a little harder to come by, but just a little, so you can introduce the interaction with Touchstones as a personal moment, even if inside another scene.

                                                    You can twist those rules however you want if they're not working. If you can't find a rule of your liking, just give a willpower point for each character at the end of each scene, and a full recover now and them when they are low on willpower and need to get ready to kick some serious ass, by combat or otherwise.

                                                        If i may make a suggestion to anyone who thinks Defence is too high? Have the formula be [Lowest of Dex + Wits] + [Athletics/2] + Celerity. In other words, only add half of Athletics (round up) instead of all of it. That has the effect of capping Defence at 8 instead of 10 for mortals and 13 instead of 15 for Vampires. It's not a big difference, really just a small tweak, but one that i think makes the balance of close quarters combat feel a lot better on the whole.
