    Making Geister more important (Wraithfying Geist)

    11-22-2013, 05:00 AM
    Geister are creatures somewhere between ghosts and gods, yes? They're also intrinsically metaphysically removed from the world of the living - unlike ghosts, they have no way to exist in the Flesh or even in Twilight.

    That's where mortals come in.

    Both a dead mortal and a Geist have much to gain from the Bargain - the mortal gets a second chance at life. The Geist gets to exist in the mortal world at all.

    The Bargain isn't a singular event, but a constant process. Geister have their own agendas, goals and things they need to achieve in the mortal world. Sometimes these goals even overlap with those of the mortal. Occasionally the Geist can convince the mortal to do things for him. Other times, both mortal and Geist struggle over command of the body both share.

    The Geist is the source of power keeping the fused identity of the Sin-Eater alive. If the mortal is weak, it can offer additional strength and protection. There's always a price, though, and it's the constant negotiation between human and Geist that defines the Sin-Eater condition.

    This is my attempt to bring a little Wraith into the Geist game. The Geist is the little voice in the Sin-Eater's head that tells him to do very bad things. Being part gods, Geister desire for things to happen that metaphysically resonate with their being. Being part ghosts, the things they need to happen are usually focused on negative things. A Geist that was created during an act of treason wants to further the cause of treason in the world. Some Geister just want to stab people in the eyes. Others might have cults of death that they care about, that they would see protected and taken care of.

    In this particular setting hack for Geist: the Sin-Eaters, the Geist takes on an active role in the game, similar to the Shadow in Wraith. Geister are capable of being in constant communion with their hosts, taunting them, offering them advice and trying to make deals.

    So how does it work?
    (I'm deliberately not using system terms here, because I don't use Storytelling to run Geist.)

    The Sin-Eater regains energy by completing tasks that the Geist wants him to do. These are always things that are thematically appropriate for gods of death, and that cause trouble (story hooks) in the long or short term - hurting people, causing mayhem, etc.

    The Sin-Eater also regains energy in exchange for Shadow points. Shadow points represent the influence the Geist has over the human. Accrue enough Shadow points and the Geist can try to take over the body for a time, enabling him to do the things he wants.

    Thoughts?

                                  If the PC fulfils her Geist's desires or uncovers/resolves aspects of the past you earn points that can be spent to avoid any conflicts of wills.                                                   *happens by, sipping a cooling tot of tea*

                                                  Hmph. Oh I see....*picking up the thread and perusing it quietly: or not so:* Oh interesting....and this....oh my....and that?.....I see.

                                                  *places it nicely back where he found it, smoothing out a few crinkles n'wrinkles; then shrugs, a soft smirk of mirth forming.*

                                                  Feasible. Though many of the things you've written here, are already fundamentals of the game as it exists. (Though clearly freshened, and given a nice coat of primer by your words herein.) I liken it to Bengal Tiger; where I am an Earl Grey fan, personally.

                                                  I have but, two suggestions: regarding the nature of how your Geists would be Shadows/Wraiths in this particular murder-scene:

                                                  1. If you are playing with either Very experienced players who like things extra challenging, or Newbies who have never played the game, perhaps keeping the Geists a secret primarily could be an interesting tryst to explore. Perhaps the character(s) die, and then return with no memory of what happens. (Story-wise, perhaps they wake up in the hospital, or they wake up lying on the ground where they had been struck down from this poor, poor, mortal coil.) They continue the game as a Mortal game, but have the occasional "Haunting" experience; and a distant voice (the Geist, getting itself situated, perhaps; like an unfamiliar bottom in a new, comfy chair!) that whispers secrets about Death and the power it can have in and over Life. Eventually the Geist may reveal itself, and the powers it can perform for the characters become more manual, and under their own control. (Rather than having to "Ask the Dark Voice to do it for them.") At some hair-raising point, they realize that a few of the things the voice has been asking them to do, (I would personally lead up from small things, until they're in over their head: maybe even do the old Shadow thing where the Geist occasionally takes control during certain circumstances; or at special times of day/night.) doesn't really sit right with them. Lay about long-story-arcs that compel them to -of course- go for the easy power grabs, but then slowly tighten the noose, and make them realize when it's (fairly, now.) too late that they have Bargained into something truly, truly frightening.
