    Here's the rough idea for the Boogeyman.

    Boogeyman

    “Don’t you recognize me?”

    Some people go beyond simply being ignored or missed by society. A few realize that they might as well be invisible to anyone and everyone. They can slip past cameras, avoid the best lookouts, mock any attempts by guards to stop them in their tracks. Whether through a preternatural skill at disguise or being able to vanish into thin air, they are the literal stuff of nightmares: A Boogeyman.

    Boogeyman slashers have gone beyond caring about being noticed by the wider world. They tried that, and it didn’t work. Now their only goal is terror, being the shadows in the night that mothers tell stories about to keep their children behaved in their beds. They’re the things that teenagers in lovers lanes across the country whisper as the reason they don’t want to be out after dark. The Boogeyman is practically a force of nature more than a man, choosing when to be seen and when to vanish after the kill.

    Finding a Boogeyman is extremely rare, and most hunters would consider the prospect of going after such a killer terrifying to consider. It’s one thing to face down a killer who you’d have trouble noticing, and a utterly terrifying concept of facing down a human being that you can’t see coming until it’s too late. Even other slashers find a Boogeyman terrifying, since even they aren’t safe from the invisible killer lurking in their own hunting grounds. Once a Boogeyman decides he’s taken claim over an area, no one is truly safe until someone puts him down, a feat easier said than done.

    What is a Boogeyman then? Are they still truly human? Some of their abilities might seem like the gift to shapeshift, but others claim that a Boogeyman is actually able to phase in and out of physical form, almost like a ghost. Some hunters have tried to capture them, only for their targets to apparently walk straight through the walls holding them and continue their murders. Few records on them even exist for the conspiracies, but all share a common theme.

    To the rest of the world, they might as well not exist.

    Background: No one just becomes a Boogeyman, at least not often. Something just happens to chameleons who realize that even their murders aren’t bringing them any closer to the rest of humanity. What they become attached to is murder, the only part of their lives that still brings them close to other human beings. How they leave humanity behind isn’t yet known, but they simply seem to phase out compared to other humans.

    Boogeyman can be said to actively exist in a state of something close to what occultists call “Twilight”. They can seemingly slip in and out of locked rooms, avoid the gaze of watchful guards and police, appear and vanish at will to further torment their victims. Being part of humanity is fully off the table. The Boogeyman thrives off the terror of their kills, and makes a point to never let them feel alone until the light leaves their eyes.

    Inspiration: Nathaniel Teager (The X-Files);

    Talent: Not Part of Your World

    Frailty: Lingering Humanity

    VASCU Case File: “Johnny J. Johnson”

        Okay, here's what I've got for the intro right now. Consider the lists incomplete until you guys figure on what groups do and don't count as cults.

        Introduction

        The Candle At Both Ends

        The Vigil is massive in scope. Vampires have their small fiefdoms, werewolves have their territories, fairies their freeholds. Hunters are everywhere you can look. From the frozen wastes of Ny-Ålesund in Svalbard to the scordhing heat of Stovepipe Wells, California, there are hunters who know that the Vigil is a lifelong commitment. The only way out is in a box, one way or the other.

        It takes many forms. The Vigil is a cell of street bums in London taking the fight to a vampire that was trying to suck a woman dry. It’s a ship’s captain telling the crew to ram the monster rising from the waters in front of them, glad he planned the route for just the right time. It can be seen in lone cells standing up to powerful inquisitors to protect innocent witches from being tortured, and in criminal enterprises running protection rackets to keep the monsters in line. There is no “typical” Vigil, any more than there’s a “typical” hunter.

        Compacts rise from ancient ages to carry on their duties. Conspiracies are birthed in the modern age as fellowships unite against the darkness. Cults and cabals tread a fine line between savior and sinner, as the Vigil grows and evolves with time. The only constant is the Vigil itself.

        Light Up the World serves as a further expansion of the concept of the Vigil, from fresh-faced conspiracies to salty compacts to dark cabals and vile cults.

        Chapter 1: Compacts explores several new compacts introduced.
        • The DiFillipo Family: American mafioso who find that the Vigil is just good business.
        • Bureau de Circonstances Extraordinaire: France’s governmental hunters who hide the dark secrets of their pasts deeply within their halls.
        • The Final Girl’s Club: Survivors of slasher attacks who banded together to protect others from the predations of others.
        • The Icelos Institute: Psychotherapists and counselors concerned with the supernatural effects on people’s minds.
        • The Reality Hackers: Computer experts and savants who believe they’ve found the source code to reality itself.
        • Schutzegruppe 14: Germany’s occult experts, purposefully divided to prevent evil from rearing up again.
        • Păzitors: Eastern European clergy who survived under communism to carry on their holy fight.
        • The Brotherhood of All Worlds: Leftovers of the Summer of Love who believe that the world can be saved with good vibes all around.
        • The Parole Gang: Ex-cons and parolees who believe they must continue to repay their debt to society.
        • Société d’Hadès: Parisian explorers of the secret routes that lead to the Underworld.
        • Llannerch Athrawon: Welsh keepers of mythic traditions attempting to channel dangerous heroes on a more righteous path.
        • SRO: An insurance company specializing in the risks presented by the Vigil.
        • MI18: England’s agents involved in policing the world’s super-scientific risks.


        Chapter 2: Conspiracies looks into the wider world of the conspiracies that see themselves at the top of the Vigil’s food chain.
        • Project: AMMUT: A secret United Nations force targeting the long-undead remnants of mankind’s dark past.
        • Hotel Mascaron: Anti-fairy spies and agents working to save mankind from eternal servitude.
        • The Athenic Church: Worshippers of the Greek goddess of wisdom that consider it their mission to preserve their faith.
        • The Disillusioned: Remnants of fallen power who take it on themselves to right past wrongs.
        • Raziel, Ltd.: Israeli weapons manufacturers that believe it is a holy duty to hunt down that which is born from Lilith
        • The Brotherhood Prizrakov: Divers into the Earth and far below it, conquering the underworlds for profit.
        • The Shih: Chinese mystics who seek balance in all things, even the Vigil.
        • KSI 21: The reinvigorated ghost of a Soviet program using spectral agents to gather information on Russia’s enemies.
        • The Carpenters: American civil servants bound together by the shared interest in keeping America stable and secure.
        • Haans-Chowdhury Productions: An Indian corporation that turns ancient secrets into modern weapons against the darkness.
        • Hakollel shen Hamerkava: Jewish mystics who have found the ability to create their own protectors.
        • Aki Ten: Japanese practitioners of Onmyodo, using spirits are their vassals

        Chapter 3: Cults and Cabals dives deep into organizations that tread a fine line in the Vigil’s candlelight. Are they hunters to seek out, or cults to drive away?
        • Cult of Moloch: Cultists who believe that no power can be gained without appropriate sacrifice.
        • The Fire-Touched: Masochistic cultists who believe they can gain power through their mystic brands.
        • The Knights-Errant: Questing seekers looking for what some claim is the ultimate artifact.
        • Bastet’s Beloved: Worshippers of the Egyptian goddess of cats, who returns their worship with protection.
        • Cage of Shadows: Followers of Lilith, believing that it is their duty to elevate their Mother to rule over Earth.
        • Bone Collectors: Scavengers of the Vigil, picking up the trash left behind by the fighting between hunters and monsters.
        • Heirs of the Crown: Demon-summoners, seeking the power of the unholy for their own gain.

        Chapter 4: Tools and Tricks looks at new endowments, professions, and slasher undertakings created to expand the Vigil even wider.

                    You asked the right person, I came up with the term Conventions are groups that aren't organisations in any shape or form, but a collection of individuals who due to some (usually supernatural) reason share a vigil. So for example Bastet's Beloved: They don't go out and recruit vets and zoo keepers then teach them cat magic from ye olde dusty tome of lore. Bastet herself is responsible for people around the world developing supernatural powers; and because she has specific criteria the people who get her powers share common traits; so apart from the fact most Beloved never meet another of their kind, they resemble a Compact.

                    I also came up with Circle/Cabal/Covenant for cults; but I was thinking they might work better for groups like The Athenic Church who don't fit the Hunter ideal of humans striking back against monsters (they take direct orders from a Goddess on a daily basis) but in terms of goals, behaviour, and integration into regular society resemble Hunters far more than monsters
                    • MI18: England’s agents involved in policing the world’s super-scientific risks (BTW, they're a Conspiracy)
                    • KSI 21: The reinvigorated ghost of a Soviet program using spectral agents to gather information on Russia’s enemies.
                    • Llannerch Athrawon: Masters of illusion and psychology who channel dangerous heroes on a more righteous path. (they're not actually Welsh; they're of no specific origin. The Welsh name is part of the pretending to be Merlin schtick)
                    • The Athenic Church: Worshippers of the Roman goddess of wisdom sent forth to spread their faith.
                    • Hakollel shen Hamerkava: Jewish mystics who wield and protect the secret names of God.
                    • Bastet’s Beloved: Cat lovers blessed and protected by Bastet.
                    • The Barkers Street Irregulars: Genius detectives struggling with personality disorders.
                    • The Reality Hackers: Computer scientists and theologians who debug errors in reality.
                    • The Order of St. Anthony: Battered but not broken by the Soviets, the Eastern Orthodox Church stands against the darkness.
                    • The Staff of Janus: The guardians at the gate between Earth and elsewhere.
                    • Ride to Damascus: Master storytellers re-imagining the old tales of Beasts and Heroes.
                    • Hearth of Hestia: Housewives who protect their homes through love for their family.

                                            Hmm, I can do an alternate Scourge based on the "art"/control idea. Something like a Collector. While a Minder attempts to raise their victims in a deluded attempt to make them better and (presumably) leaving to make the world a "better place", Collectors are solely selfish and have no desire to really protect or rear their victims; instead, Collectors are more interested in developing their collection, leaving their victims in a state of pure helplessness because that is the ultimate control. Their MO is lobotomizing their victims or otherwise looking for individuals who are more-or-less incapable of fighting back or objecting (coma patients for example). What they do is effectively playing with dolls that they have made.

                                            Talent...If I don't use a pastiche of the Master's The Hand that Feeds You, it'll probably be something like staging the bodies of their victims in such a way that it affects the viewers in an extreme way, even when the Collector cannot feasibly move them. They assist the Collector as if they were providing the Collector teamwork. They can also act as secondary actors for any Tactic the Collector uses. This doesn't mean that his victims are able to act on their own; if the Collector is otherwise incapacitated or incarcerated, their victims cannot drag the body or pick the lock by themselves, the Collector must initiate and the victims provide teamwork.

                                            The Frailty would most-likely be some sort of targeting tick; Collectors have a specific theme in their mind when they start their collection. One may want to turn their victim into dolls or playmates while another wants to recreate a family dinner. One may take to art and prefer to position their victim's body as if they were part of a painting or sculpture. Collectors are affected by the Fugue Persistent Condition with the trigger being either victims that fit into the theme the Collector desires or situations that invoke the themes. The effect of failing a Resolve + Composure roll for Fugue persists until the Collector is away from the situation or until the Collector or the trigger are attacked, otherwise they will remain in a daze observing the object of their delusions. They also value the safety of their collection over their own well-being for the most part, becoming severely enraged when their collection is tampered with, whether by the victims themselves or a third party.

                                            And for Influences, that would make Doctor X from The Cat Lady a dead ringer as well as Professor Henry Jarrod from House of Wax and possibly Sander Cohen from Bioshock.
