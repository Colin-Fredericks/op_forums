    I think I have a good idea for the Horror of my Namtaru Collector. He's pretty much going to be a non-humanoid Grey Alien, but strongly influenced by the more understandable critters of the Cthulhu Mythos (e.g. Mi-Go, Elder Things and Yithians). An air of otherworldly erudition juxtaposed to a repulsive exterior with virtually no familiar expressions of emotion to latch onto. Possibly lamia-shaped, bioluminescent and with several tool-like limbs. But scariest of all: totally clean and wearing actual clothes.

    Does that sound cool?

        I'd go with Eshmaki for the plant monster. The other one is just...wrong. (In a good way mind you) I don't quite get why it's Ugallu though.


        The fun part about creating a Beast is that you can look at them from a metaphorical concept, instead of a literal one. Remember, they're living nightmares, and nightmares are more heavily influenced by your subconscious than anything else. When I wrote the orphanage lady, I imagined her to be the unfortunate circumstances that swoop in out of nowhere and drop you into a situation you're uncomfortable being in. The children's parents die from tragic, unpredictable accidents. Maybe she caused a few, or maybe it was just bad luck. Either way, the children are scared, confused, and angry, and that's how she feeds.


        List of possible accidents:


        Car veering off into a ravine while driving at night.


        Rock slide during a hiking trip.


        Ropes snap while mountain climbing.


        Falling overboard during a cruise at sea


        Double homicide, killer didn't leave a trail.

                Anakim Tyrant: "What's with that look? Did you seriously think this was going to play out like your story books? You just come in here, swinging your magic sword and down goes the monster? I'm centuries old, you stupid son of a bitch, how many heroes do you think have tried that before you? A lot, and they had much better strategies than your Leroy Jenkins impersonation."

                The Beast hunts the most dangerous prey of all, Heroes. They are the horror that is stark reality, the moment of realization that a Hero is just a man fighting a creature that defies logic and science. It has several human life times worth of experience in battle, and its body surpasses human limitations. What hope does a mere mortal only a few decades old have against such a majestic creature as this? The realization that humans are not on the top of the food chain, that is who the Beast is, feeding on the shattered expectations and dreams of would be Heroes.


                The Beast's lair is a combination of a trophy room and a mass grave. Each chamber is dedicated to previous Heroes that sought to slay him. Various habitats and scenery littered with skeletal remains. Artifacts and weapons that were to be used against their killer now lay rusted and broken among the corpses. And in the center of the lair, an enormous battlefield, stands the Beast itself. A fell knight clad in demonic armor, waiting for its next challenger. Once the Hero approaches, they find the landscape to be formed out of human corpses piled up to form hills and mountainsides. The amount of blood leaking from them is enough to produce rivers and the sky is tinted orange as raging fires burn in the distance, attempting to clean away the seemingly endless mass of bodies.

                                    Today's Beast:

                                    The Horror is a huge gray owl, the size of a small house. The owl is weird, it almost looks like it's made of marble and ebony, with strange markings on its feathers. It stays calm most of the time, moving clumsily on its claws. When he finds that someone it's not telling the truth, he agitates and suddenly it's not clumsy anymore. In front of you you have a bloodthirsty raptor bird. The spots of the feathers now clearly appear for what they are: grotesque faces that laugh and spit cruel judgements, sometimes about those around and sometimes about past victims or unidentified misgiver.

                                    The Lair resembles a mixture between a modern tribunal and an amount of buildings from ancient Rome. It's deserted, if not for many bizarre stone statues that seem to move when you don't look at them. In the main halls, every single seat is occupied by this silent public.

                                    The character works as the sort of sleazy private eye you call to spy on your spouse. He's an Ugallu Predator that hunts the liars and the betrayers. Normally, he just scares them but, sometimes, he takes a finger from them. If they react really bad to being exposed, he takes a hand. Each prey is a new feather.

                                        I'm expecting royalties from anybody who uses my album cover idea.

                                        Namtaru Nemeses: The Warden, that's what they call her. A Beast who somehow obtained control over a maximum security prison facility. Her lair is a place where criminals go to disappear, a trap made of iron and concrete. Those who obtain a map of the complex or a bird's eye view will notice the prison is built in the shape of a gigantic spider web. In the realm of nightmares, The Warden's soul is a gigantic, iron spider. Her webs are woven from steel threads, cocooning her prey in chains. In the physical world, the inmates seem to lose their will to fight back, their spirits broken from years of incarceration.


                                        The truth, however, is much more sinister. While the inmates sleep, The Warden visits their cells in their dreams, sucking the life out of them like the spider that she is. Each time they wake up, they feel a little more empty, until the day when they just stop resisting, a spiritually empty husk of their former selves. A moral person might call it cruel and unusual punishment. But the only thing The Warden cares about is the governor keeps tossing insects into her web like a sadistic child; and to accomplish that, she needs to prove she can "reform" these dangerous criminals.

                                                    Anakim Tyrant: The twins do everything together. At no point has anybody seen them willingly part from each other. They are always within earshot of each other unless circumstances forcefully separate them. They are physically identical too, and they do that creepy shit where they speak in unison or finish each others sentences. Most people who know about the Beasts are probably aware they share the same Lair, but only those who have actually entered it know the truth. The twins don't merely share the same Lair, they share the same Soul. Their horror takes the form of a two headed giant, an Ettin standing 20 feet tall with skin as black as pitch and eyes glowing red.

                                                    Separation causes pain on their soul, and the twins will become incredibly violent in an effort to reunite with each other if they have to. They are the fear of being outnumbered, of having the odds stacked against you. Even the greatest warriors fall when presented with overwhelming numbers. If a Hero comes knocking, they will find not one Giant, but two; and they will have to face them simultaneously!

                                                        Makara Tyrant: "You can't handle the truth." "You don't want to do this." "Beauty is only skin deep." You have heard all these phrases before, but you never really took them into consideration, never really accepted the weight they carry until now. Until you looked beneath the surface and saw what they don't want you to know. Most people go through their whole lives in blissful denial, they reject what they don't understand and impose their own explanations and rationality upon the world just to keep themselves happy. They never consider that their lover is dangerously unstable until something causes them to snap. They expect their leaders to be the perfect role models and are always shocked when they abuse the power entrusted to them. But worst of all, they think they know everything about how the world works.


                                                        Mortals who care to look past this facade may claim the universe holds dark mysteries, but this too is a state of denial. The universe has no secrets, it offers knowledge freely to all who seek it. Humanity just doesn't want to seek it, because they're afraid of what they'll find. That is the horror of this Leviathan. It is truth, the real truth that humanity doesn't want to hear. Cold, pitiless and uncensored. It does not apologize, does not make excuses for itself. This is the way the world has always worked and it was your choice to step out of your comfort zone. If you can't handle what you learned, that's your fault, not his!


                                                        The Beast preys on those who's sight extends beyond their wisdom, those who want to learn the truth, but haven't prepared themselves for what they'll find. Its soul is a man almost completely obscured by clothing, who's face is hidden in shadow under their hat, glasses and scarf. A trench coat wraps around their body with gloved hands tucked into the pockets, or holding a clue to some obscure mystery. Its Lair is a library overflowing with stacks of books and paper littering the floor. At times, you can't even tell if you're actually walking on the ground level, or if the paper is stacked too high. Most of the text you find here is illegible, written in a script that your mind immediately shuns as being occult or extraterrestrial in origin. But if you search hard enough, you may come across a book, scroll, note or mural that contains information related to what you're seeking.

                                                        And then, when you inevitably start screaming "No, that's not true!" the Beast hears you, and he comes looking for who's causing such a raucous in his library.
