    Thaumium is like nMage's version of cMage's Primum - it's magic-resistant metal. It's an alloy of Oricalcum and Lunargent (IIRC) - those are two of the seven perfected metals.

    In Legacies: The Ancient, it's explained that you make Perfected Metal by shifting a quantity of metal into Twilight and back again dozens of times. We might change that in 2e (we're outlining the book that will include them right now).

    There's supposed to be seven different perfected metals, one for each of the classically-known metals: gold, silver, tin, lead, copper, mercury, and iron. L:TA explains that Thaumium is only one form of perfected alloy, and there's several different ones you can make with Matter Mastery.

    The actual materials aren't inherently magical, but have qualities above and beyond their impure versions

    Perfected Gold is Orichalcum - it's imperishable, conducts energy, impervious to acid.
    Perfected Iron is Siderite - it's memory-morphic, in that it doesn't break but *bends*, then reforms to its forged shape. It never dulls its edge, and can cut diamond.
    Perfected Mercury is Hermium, a superliquid - stir it and the circular current never stops. put it in a container and it will crawl up and out.
    Perfected Silver is Lunargent - highly malleable and chemically inert

    tin, lead, and copper are not described, but Perfected Lead might be Apeiron, the matter of Stygia Matter-based Supernal Entities are named after.

    And above them all, above Primum, is Adamas, the utterly indestructible, magic-resistant material Atlantean temples are made out of, which is made by a 7-dot spell Excising the capacity of a material to be damaged.

                    Regarding the lower depths. DaveB on Giant in the Playground:

                    Actual spoiler for Awakening 2e: The Lower Depths

                    "Places so far from the light they shouldn’t be able to exist, but do. Wormholes under a rotting log of Creation. The Lower Depths consist of countless realms, because the region lacks even the energy to contain a unitary reality. The Lower Depths are to the Fallen World what the Fallen is to the Supernal, and are therefore a Lie of the Lie.

                    Some mages believe that the petty symbols of the human psyche take on great importance in certain of the Depths, just as Supernal signs cast their shadows in the ordinary world.

                    Each Lower Depth is unable to retain a critical constituent of existence; it’s either absent or has been distorted into something hostile to ordinary existence. Sometimes the lack can be defined by an Arcanum. A Depth realm without Death might be choked with endless knots of imperishable flesh and plant matter. Other depths lack less definable principles, such as Shape, Identity and Righteousness. Some mages situate Hell here, because the entities known as akathartoi (“unclean ones”) closely resemble the demons of Abrahamic legend, feeding from human depravity. Mages have catalogued numerous Lower Depths, but only by interrogating and examining their inhabitants. No reliable source speaks of a mage returning from the Lower Depths, but it’s possible to summon an inhabitant upward. Their denizens find it difficult to manifest. They must satisfy signature hungers, and find it extremely difficult to take material form."

                    Canonical Lower Depths: The Inferno (lack and feed from human Vice), wherever it is Strix come (lack and feed from Vitae), Duat from Mummy (lack and feed from Sekhem). And those are three "Depths" relatively close to the Fallen World. Mage's gameline has included creatures like The Tutor, an immobile, hurts-to-look-at half-Materialized *thing* the Paternoster Ministry of Seers have imprisoned that rips the human capacity for individual identity away as a sting reflex, The Decay, a Lower Depths being that manifests as everything material crumbling as it eats Durability, and the Hounds of Annwn, vaguely canid (but entirely invisible, as they don't have a Pattern) beings that eat Patterns, used by a Left-Handed Legacy to build their own pocket world in the Lower Depths from the things the Hounds eat.

                            I was wondering: How is it with Gnosis dots and Arcana limitations in 2e? When I was thinking about it, I realized that if we go with the idea that getting a dot in an Arcanum is basically about learning an information (you have the option to pay for the dot with normal exp), the strength of your connection with the supernal shouldn't matter that much.

                            This is especially visible when you can have 2 mages - both have the same Arcana and mage 1 decides to learn a dot in Arcanum A. He does so and can't learn dot in Arcanum B because of the limit. The other mage learns dot in Arcanum B and can't learn the dot that mage 1 did. Why would the simple act of learning an information prevent those mages from learning the other one?

                            Now, I understand the need for some basic limit, so we don't have Gnosis 1 Masters running around, but that could be solved by, say, making the upper limit mage's Gnosis +2 for non-Archmasters. That would basically create the same kind of limit like 1e core for the first Arcana new mage gets and wouldn't require that half page long table that doesn't make much sense to me from a roleplaying perspective...

                            So, how does 2e deal with this?
