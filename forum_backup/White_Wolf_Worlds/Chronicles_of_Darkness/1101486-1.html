                Most important thing about crossover IMO comes from the BtP Core book.

                "Did you know that demons (or perhaps mummies) are
                the strongest things in the Chronicles of Darkness, objectively?
                Did you know that in a hypothetical space, a plain room with
                no other factors, a changeling can hand a vampire his ass on
                a platter? It doesn’t matter. Every one of the Chronicles of
                Darkness games is designed with different goals in mind, and
                absolutely none of those goals are “objectively equal power levels
                with every other game line.” You have to disavow yourself of this
                way of thinking early on, or you’re in for a lot of frustration.

                Some of the game lines have characters who are better in
                specific fields. Vampires, for example, have numerous social
                tools that can manipulate, fool, and persuade characters.
                Werewolf packs, on the other hand, are apex hunters, and can
                find, chase, and kill damned near anything. While exceptions
                exist, you don’t expect werewolves to outperform vampires in
                a social arena, and you can’t hope for vampires to hunt with
                the ferocity of werewolves.

                What isn’t okay, however, is making one player not have
                fun. You don’t win the game through tactical dominance; you
                win the game by telling cool stories and having fun. If someone
                isn’t having fun, you all lose. It’s important to address possible
                conflicts up front and be clear about them. If you have a player
                with a vampire character who wants to be a social maven, it
                might cause conflicts if the werewolf player decides to focus
                entirely on werewolf social powers, outshining the vampire in
                her expected area of expertise. These are certainly problems any
                group can come across, but they’re especially pronounced in
                crossover groups. You can cut these problems off at the pass by
                making your characters together and discussing what everyone
                wants their characters to be good at, bad at, and otherwise."

                Most important tip in regards to any crossover game. I've been running a CofD 2E crossover game since December with a decent chunk of homebrew for the other splats to 2E. The fact is the splats aren't going to be balanced. As long though as every person feels useful to the party and feels their themes are touched upon, you'll be fine . The advice above with specific themes is also good.

                        There's something none of you have touched upon and it's their ability to "feed", so to speak. For instance, feeding for a vampire is dangerous, unless you have blood bags/servants. But for a mage, it's a matter of course. So there's also that: in your average chronicle, your mage will often have his Mana full more easily than your vampire will have his blood points full. But at the same time, in a case of emergency, it's easier for the vampire to refill: there's litterally sources of blood points walking everywhere. And let's not talk about the difficulty for a demon to refill Aether...

                        That's something important in the case of Beasts. Sure, they have one of the most convoluted ways to refill Satiety. But at the same time, these guys can actually get STRONGER the lower they are on their satiety. So they are also one of the only splats that can actually go "to the ground" easily.

                            Of course, in a crossover game refueling may get much easier for many splats.                                 On another note, there are also side benefits to the splats that can be HUGE depending on your chronicle. We already talked about demons and their ability to lie and pass undetected and mages and their mage sight. But a Beast has its lair, which is a reaaaaally huge deal. It's basically an otherwordly place where your ennemies will have trouble tracking you, with the ability to open portals to pretty much anywhere (or close enough that it shouldn't matter) if the lair is big enough or your environnemental tilts common enough, and providing environnemental immunity to your brood members... that's huge. A changelin had the ability to passively detect magic and travel at an unbelievable pace through the hedge (which, in a worldwide chronicle, can be a huge deal).
