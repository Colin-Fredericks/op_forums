                    The precise wording of the bane is not how the character will be encountering the bane, which is instead that (barring high Humanity or vessels with high Stamina) attempting to nonfatally derive sustenance from blood that has been taken from characters without their knowledge and against their will requires taking enough from the vessel that the damage alone will have a measurable impact on their effectiveness in non-routine tasks of the sort that require a dice roll.

                    You can coerce the vessel into agreement by offering alternatives they would definitely not prefer — including "if you do not give me some of your blood I will kill you," in a pinch, or "if you don't let me suck your blood I'm not giving you the substance you're addicted to — and nothing stops you from employing Dominate or good old-fashioned gaslighting after the fact, but any situation where the character is not consciously giving up some amount of their blood to you, regardless of what they contextually believe about that offer/relinquishment, is one where you are not getting sustenance out of at least some of that blood. That means no sneaking sips from the bottom bunk, no hypnotizing strangers into presenting their necks, no grab-and-gulps in back alleys, no opportunistic licks at open wounds, and so on.

                    Experiments of interest may include seeing how such a vampire processes different types of stored or non-sapient blood — can animals dedicate their Vitae in the way humans and vampires can? does a human's permission apply retroactively to blood separated from their body before they made the offer? does mixing blood from willing and unwilling vessels affect the conversion rate? — but the basic intention of the bane is pretty clearly the same as why the action that triggers its effects is a Detachment trigger one dot below "Lying in defense of the Masquerade" and one dot above "Depriving another of consent with a Discipline" (and at the same level as "Urging another's behavior with a Discipline"). The semantics of how much the vessel knows about why you're asking for their blood are irrelevant — the complications of the fact that you cannot bypass the vessel's will without wasting Vitae are the important part in a game where difficult power dynamics and the psychological strain of secrecy are baked into the setting.

                        Your character takes no sustenance from the blood of the unwilling or unknowing.
                        ● Does the vessel know beforehand that you are taking blood from them? Being able to tell only mid-bite that that's what's happening is a useless definition of "knowing" here. Nobody would bother specifying that you can bite them but only if they can see that they are bleeding.
                        ● Is the vessel willing to give you that blood? It doesn't matter if it uses the word "consent" or not. This is what consent is, definitionally.
                        That's it.

                        Does the vessel need to know you are a vampire? No. Do you need to brief them on every little thing that's going to happen? No. If they know you're taking their blood, and you convince them to be okay with that, you get sustenance.
                        Can you bite who doesn't already know, and accept voluntarily, that you are going to drink their blood? No. You get no Vitae from doing this.

                        This bane makes it very hard to maintain complete secrecy as a vampire. Most vampires are not expected to maintain complete secrecy in gameplay, but if you want your character to maintain complete secrecy, probably do not give them this bane.

                        Is it possible for a vampire with this bane to obtain blood without revealing themselves as a vampire? It's a hassle, but yes. Just don't bite them. Get a syringe. Convince them you are a medical professional and you need to draw blood. They have to know you are taking their blood. You're not required to be honest about why. Fill some vials and chug it from the vials. Sustenance.
                        You can also convince people that this is some weird sex thing you're into and find somebody who is also into it. You don't have to be undead to drink someone's blood.

                        Much more commonly, a vampire with this bane is going to have a small number of mortals who know they are a vampire (but may not really know much of anything about vampires besides "they bite people and drink their blood") and who for whatever reason (bribes, blackmail, supernatural favors, the ecstasy of the Kiss, the Vinculum, ghouling, genuine concern for your wellbeing) are willing to let you drink from them. Make sure they don't tell anyone. Make sure nobody spies on them. As long as these things hold, you're good.

                        Vampire games are about things going wrong and what you'll do to deal with it.

                                Originally posted by Neos01 View Post
                                My understanding of the word unknowing was then, simply put, wrong
                                I wouldn't say it like that, but knowing your native language could help clear things up.

                                This is not by any means "Informed Consent", as this is a more specific concept, and one with ethical and legal repercussions that give it a reasonably clear definition. And at least in Portuguese I can guarantee it exists and has this same definition.

                                An "Informed Consent" is Informed about the circumstances, causes and developments of the act. Being directly asked to state your full name isn't asking Informed Consent, for that the person asking should also state their reasons, how they plan to use this information and what they plan to do with it afterwards. There's a lot of details we may discuss about how thorough or detailed such information should be, but it must be given and must not induce wrong conclusions by falsehood or selective omission.

                                So, Informed Consent would mean at least telling the victim you drink blood for sustenance (circumstance and cause), you'll drain such amount from them (developments, as they must know how this may let them debilitated), and after that the blood will be processed in your body and mixed with previous victim's, rendering it useless for most known uses of blood. You may also have to answer follow-up questions. That is Informed Consent.

                                What we said is, not only in at least some languages but also in several relevant contexts, just consent. If we're talking "consent on taking blood", absent the core information of "taking blood" it isn't consent to begin with.

                                Yet the way you worded isn't strange, but this is more worrying than justifying. The scenario you described is extremely common all around the world IRL and is a common argument for predators to throw uncertainty on the level of "consent" in the attack. Most women know situations like that very well, frequently by having been there as victims. This isn't just clear, it's a familiar but worrisome argument from someone who isn't trying to find the limits of the rule, but to blatantly talk their way out of it.

                                Of course, given the setting, a character trying this is perfectly well within the theme. But it's not a reasonable rules question for someone who isn't already prone to very problematic ways of thinking consent.

                                                    Originally posted by Alphari View Post
                                                    Were there rules / mechanics or story containing information about bloodlines and banes? Homerule?
                                                    The mechanics for bloodlines in general are as printed in the corebook on page 98. Night Horrors: Spilled Blood contains a number of bloodlines as examples, and there are some old blog posts with earlier examples cut from the corebook for space.

                                                    Kindreds can create bloodlines, and those bloodlines can become a clan (in theory). Some clans even states they were a bloodline once. I can't find anything about changing bloodline or evolving it.
                                                    This is for much the same reason as there are no mechanics in Werewolf for a lodge becoming a tribe — it's a plot device that happens rarely enough and on a large enough timescale that the particulars (beyond reaching a fuzzy threshold of scope of membership) are going to vary by individual group.

                                                    Banes. Kindreds could get those to mitigate some trauma. Yet there is no clues if it is possible to change or remove/heal such spiritual trauma. They have time, mysteries, diablerie (elders eat mortal souls), powers to physically transform or travel astral... yet, nothing to heal banes or humanity.
                                                    You restore Humanity by spending Experiences to buy Humanity dots. Banes are permanent changes to the character, whether gaining your Clan Bane when you first hit Humanity 6, elders getting an Elder Clan Bane when they first hit Humanity 3, or developing more unique Banes from Detachment.

                                                    The heretical Coil of Ziva from Secrets of the Covenants concerns itself with mitigating the effects of Banes not covered by the Coil of the Ascendant, but it's by definition not going to be your Mystery Coil and therefore even if you're allowed to learn it you'd need to climb the ranks in the Ordo to advance it to the cap.
