    Perfected Material Ideas

    03-30-2017, 09:23 AM
    I recently started in a game and am playing someone trying to make it into the fashion field. So my ST, CrownedSun , had linked me to the preview of perfected materials since that sort of thing suits me. I like playing crafters, and there are imperial brocades that use threads of precious metals.

    Anyways, what really caught my eye was alkahest or perfected water. It dissolves things, which could be incredibly useful. The first thing I thought of was this could let you potentially merge materials in ways that wouldn't happen naturally by dissolving them then evaporating the water (since I assume that's possible with effort). Say a sheet of lapis lazuli infused silk, where the particles of stone are entangled up in the proteins of the silk. But you could take it further... what about metal and say... wood.

    I feel like the potential here is pretty significant, and while I've focused on water, I've only started scratching the surface. What ideas do other people have?

    Oh, and I had the vague idea that you could perfect plants and other living materials, to serve as part of decorating a ritual space. But that was more a side thought.

                                  This... does not work that way. To make a superalloy out of titanium and aluminium you need to melt them together and cool them in a very specific way so that you get two differend mixed crystals of materials bonded together. Dissolving does kind of the oposite, it breaks every bonding of materials appart and leaves very tiny part up to single atoms or molecules floating around in the solvent. So if you mix titanium and aluminium in Alkahest and vaporize it afterwards, all you would get is very fine aluminium and titanium powder.

                                  Same goes for silk or minerals. If you put silk or a diamond into a perfect solvent, all you would get in the end is carbon powder. What you could do is make extremely fine powders out of raw elements like gold or platinum and then use a simple mater spell to weave these into a silk thread for example. And that silk could likely be perfected in any other way.

                                                          The thing to remember is that, with water, the speed at which the state change takes place matters. There's a reason why cryopreservation of cells is done by freezing them very quickly (along with other things such as DMSO, but I digress). The reason is that crystal formation is prevented except at the smallest possible way. There's ice, but the crystals are SMALL. By comparison, if you slowly freeze water, the ice crystals will be much larger.

                                                          Proteins are regularly dissolved in water. We can produce silk like polymers already through techniques, though thinking about it, doing what I'm talking about with silk might require similar, specialized equipment to produce threads, but we're talking a sheet and the polymers are going to be already prone to forming a polymer (though it might require enzymes to catalyze the reaction). I've worked with proteins a LOT in research labs. Polymers less so, but I know more than the basics.

                                                          Lastly, you can have long strands of biological material dissolved in water. That's what DNA is, it's not a short molecule, but a very long one. And the labs I worked in were dealing with both DNA and long protein molecules, cut to single genes, but they're still very tiny on some scales... just not as tiny as singular atoms or simple sugars.
