    Is there going to be an endgame book for Demon?

    01-31-2014, 05:59 AM
    Now, one thing that kind of bothered me in nWoD is that while they have given us what we should expect in Mage endgame, (Imperial mystries) other splats did not exactly have gotten that kind of treatment. (There's a Changeling endgame book, Equinox Road, although the relative scale of those books kinda differ very much) -correct me if I'm wrong though., I kind of feel that at least in terms of the endgame, mages did get some special treatment on that. I don't play all that much endgame-games, and one of the reason is that. Although WoD games were never really designed for crossovers, I love to do it anyway, and when I want to run a high-powered crossover games, there are far too less materials out of Mage and maybe Changeling.

    I think, though, Demon the Descent is a great game to have a book to cover the endgame, since while it is awesome as it is, it's never really independent from God Machine Chronicle, and GMC is a subject on which something I really want an endgame. So, is there going to be a book on demonic endgame, covering hell, really terrifying arch-demons, and actually cosmic battles against God Machine?


    One more question though. What do you think the Demon is on a splat power scale? I haven't played all that much demon, but while I think demon is really powerful on a low XP, the lategame demons are far far less impressive. I'm probably very wrong though. But let me know what you think.

      A few thoughts. First, I think of the God Machine Chronicle book as the equivalent to Inferno, Book of Spirits or Book of the Dead. A general book for dealing with demons, the spirit world, the underworld, and now the God Machine.

      Secondly, Demon is part of nWoD 2.0. The only game we have to compare it to is Vampire, and even then, its debatable in terms of "power." Going Loud to destroy a burner cover is a very potent ability when it comes to white room theorycraft, and having a high Cover as a resistance stat is pretty good. But both of those have their own major drawbacks that come up in play - vampires don't have to worry about angels spotting them and swarming them.

      Three, an end game book is likely, though given how "Hell" is personal to every demon, we're more likely to see general guidelines instead of an actual sub-game for reaching that state. That said, I also imagine we'll be getting rules for playing the half-connected Exiled beings - connected, yet still independent, demons. I think these are the equivalent to oWoD DtF Earthbound.

      Five. Mage did get special treatment on the whole tier 4 game thing; the reasons aren't worth speculating (or, in my case, worrying) over. Playing a True Fae got a small section in what amounted to an ST guide book for hacking the game. Vampire got a Primacy game in the Requiem Chronciler's Guide, though few people know of it. I suspect the latter two will be how most game lines handle it.

          Couple things:
          1) Promethean's had a defined endgame since the corebook, Vampire doesn't strictly have an endgame that's not "stop being a vampire" or "fade to black," Werewolf's endgamey bits are split across at least three different books, Geist was quite plainly a one-book line at design, and Mummy's literally not done with its planned run yet.
          2) Meanwhile, Changeling teased the outcome of high-Wyrd and low Clarity repeatedly over several books and had material to draw on from earlier books concerning Eldritch Entitlements and the manifold domains of Arcadia. Mage had archmagic as a thing that was around since the corebook and a sizable chunk of the material in Imperial Mysteries was consolidating or building on specific bits from other books, like how the sample Tetrarch is the owner of a powerful Artifact from Seers, and on top of that it was one of the Big Three lines, a distinction that's marginally less significant after the shift to PoD but nevertheless worth remembering.
          3) Demon, as with Mummy, is still coming out, but much of the material you'd expect to find in an endgame book seems likely to already be included in either the Player's Guide or the Storyteller's Guide (Ciphers, in-depth looks at facilities, hacking the God-Machine, etc).
          4) "Splat power scale" is kind of irrelevant at the moment when only half of the established gamelines are definitely going to be running on a different system with according updates, and especially when, of those lines, only half of them are actually out.

                      Originally posted by torete View Post
                      Now, I know that mages are supposed to do everything, but I thought Demons and especially Vampires were supposed to be more 'social' bunch?
                      I wouldn't call demons a social bunch, really. Tempters? Absolutely. But your default demon is going to be a spy-like. The basic demon powers they all share come down to swapping Covers, and various "Spoofing" abilities to avoid detection. They're awesome at lying and hiding things, but I don't know if I would call super subterfuge powers enough to be a "social bunch." Demon form stuff, for instance, isn't very good at social boons. Pacts are social, but only a handful of embeds/exploits are what we consider social in return. Even then, there's a number of tricks demons have to be rather tricky.


                      As for vampires? Mind 4 to open all doors... versus Dominate 1 which negates the need for doors in the first place. Or Majesty, with its insane social conditions. Either of these disciplines, at low levels, could likely get a chief of police to hold a press conference and admit to murder; something that opening Doors cannot do at all. Vampires come with the addictive Kiss and blood bonds, two powerful social tools.


                      All in all, Mind 4 sounds to be a bit on the weak side, comparatively.
