    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    I don't know why; but whenever I hear that claim, I find myself thinking of the white-room game balance mentality.

    If you're referring to the ability of Sleepers to effectively hunt the Awakened, as emphaszied by Dave, it's certainly more than the typical "white room" template vs. template combat comparison (which I loathe with the heat of a thousand suns!).

    It's not too difficult to acknowledge very broad basic power discrepancies among certain groups or in-setting roadblocks. Awakened mages and Sleeper Hunters are both human, except the former have significant powers, abilities and advantages, with no inherent non-human weakeness (e.g., sunlight, silver, etc). Even a newly Awakened mage is "protected" by the Quiescence and has significant and versatile powers that should present a serious challenge to even a small group of highly-experienced, combat-trained Sleepers. This is expressly acknowledged by the Mage developer and is an intentional part of the nWOD setting. Lower Tier Hunters are supposed to be underdogs, and some prey is more difficult than others.

    The desire by you and others for some canon Tactics or other mundane means to offer basic Sleeper Hunters a real fighting chance against the Awakened only emphasizes the point.

    Of course, we can get into some real "white room" discussions like who would win a bar fight, a Blood Talon Rahu or an Obrimos Adamantine Arrow?

                                        Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
                                        That might be true, but one of mages greatest strengths comes from their ability to potentially no sell a lot of methods of attack.
                                        In 1E, my Perfected Adept stood a better than even chance of winning a toe-to-toe fight with a werewolf, even without time to prepare in advance. In 2E, Gauru form is a terrible, terrible thing to face. If a mage has time, sure, she can make something happen. If you're just going from "zero to sixty" in a bar fight situation, unless the mage is preloaded with spells, the werewolf is likely going to win. The huge trait bonuses, fast weapons (teeth and claws have +3 Initiative), massive amounts of damage, and ability to shrug off harm are hard to overcome. The Obrimos, if skilled in Life, might theoretically use a spell to trigger a submission response or some other instinctive reaction, but probably won't be able to muster up enough direct damage with Forces or Prime or whatever to get it done before the werewolf eviscerates her. Then you must also contend with the fact that the more you hurt the werewolf, the more you risk Death Rage—at which point some options, like mental influence, become much more difficult.

                                        Many factors come into play in this situation: experience level, luck of the rolls, and so on, but by default, a werewolf is no pushover in a straight fight. In 1E, various supernatural creatures like mages, vampires, and changelings could specialize in physical prowess and at least stand a good chance against a werewolf. In Second Edition, werewolves a lot more dangerous physically, and Gauru is nasty. And the thing is, the werewolf can do it reflexively if she has any kind of decent Harmony. So while the mage is spending a turn trying desperately to get some kind of spell active (but can activate Mage Armor reflexively), the werewolf has transformed into a much faster, much stronger form, with a good Initiative bonus for her unarmed attacks. If she grapples, which she can do with her teeth, she really puts the pressure on the mage, who will find it very difficult to escape.

                                        Smart mages, even those skilled in battle, don't go picking fights with lycanthropes. At least not in an arena that favors the werewolf.

                                                    It is true that Forces could make it difficult to approach the mage, although the mage has little time to make it happen. That's part of the problem. The werewolf shifts and is up in your face right now. You need a spell to work, which means the factors must be a certain way lest you get caught up in your own magic. Sometimes you must account for the size of your opponent, you won't have time to use many Yantras, etc., so not working with an optimal dice pool. But yes, certainly you could use Control Gravity or Turn Momentum to slow down or disorient your foe. Those spells won't let you win on their own, but they at least give you some breathing room. The problem is that you might not win Initiative, either.

                                                    A completely average werewolf (Str 2, Dex 2, Sta 2, Wits 2, Com 2) has Initiative 8 while using teeth/claws, not counting Merits or other abilities. The average mage has an Initiative of 4-5, say, but even a fast mage is still looking at 6-7. So the werewolf has the better chance of going first, can reflexively shift into Gauru and pounce on you, and once you're grappled, Control Gravity or Turn Momentum or the like aren't going to help you much. Then that werewolf (a Blood Talon Rahu) is hitting you next turn with 8-again on her vicious attacks (that probably have major bonus damage), plus other Gifts available to that werewolf can throw a monkey wrench into the mage's plans. Unchained might actually let you escape those spells, she can cripple your Defense, or maybe hit you with Incite Fury and leave you berserk, unable to properly cast spells as you fly into a single-minded rage. She might stun you or knock you down, costing you your action, then set upon you while you're down.

                                                    A werewolf is highly dangerous in a fight just at the drop of a hat. She needs almost no time to become a dangerous killer. The mage might thwart her with a single spell, if that spell works as intended and is cast in time. Otherwise, the mage must spend at least a turn or two getting some spell active that will even up the odds. By that time she's probably crippled or in such a disadvantageous position that it's impossible to regain momentum.

                                                            Oh boy..., I was just trying to inject some levity in the thread when I raised the werewolf vs. mage bar fight.

                                                            "White room (white bar?)" fights simply don't account for the innumerable variables in combat even attempting to emulate real circumstances. For instance, are the characters armed with fetishes, imbued items or mundane weaponry, can the environment be used offensively or defensively, is the protection of patrons or other innocents a priority, is back-up or other allies available, or just plain luck. The abilities provided by XP progression are also not necessarily equivalent among different templates, and truly min/max combat characters would be so usually specialized if translated to reality as to be ridiculous.

                                                            However, if I was to engage in such a foolish discussion, which of course I would never do, I would suggest the Obrimos simply levitate the werewolf up and as far away as possible, and then take all the time or actions she deemed necessary. Last I checked, there were exceedingly few werewolf abilities that permitted flight. If that didn't work, mages above a certain medium XP level have the fairly unique ability to cause significant aggravated damage at a distance, This should be employed immediately and with abandon.

                                                            Most importantly, no Wise mage of any Path, Order or Legacy should get into a bar fight with any lycanthrope without sufficient preparation (I believe mages learn this in their first day of Arrow training). There are more than ample uses of Forces, Prime and other Arcanum to get the hell out of Dodge quickly and easily, and then later carefully and patiently prepare to put down that rabid and mangy excuse for a canine at a time, place and manner of her choosing. It should not be thought of as a "retreat," so much as a temporary and limited "strategic withdrawal."

                                                            But, as I stated earlier, I would never engage in such pointless and unproductive conjecture...

                                                            We should be discussing far more important things, like why Dave is sadly all wrong and obviously delirious after finalizing the Mage 2e opus, and how Mage Arcadia is really the same place as Changeling Arcadia...


                                                            Edit: Just to keep with the point of the thread, no human Sleeper Hunter would stand a chance against either the mage or werewolf in that hypothetical bar fight. In fact, anyone with a lick of sense should avoid that bar entirely!
