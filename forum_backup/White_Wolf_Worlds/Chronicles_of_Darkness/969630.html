    Can other Great Apes become Awakened?

    09-14-2016, 11:32 PM
    I was watching The Flash, and it features Grodd as a psychic Gorilla, its kind of scary to see what something we treated as lesser than us turning into something greater than us.

    So with most settings that make humans special or attribute mystical powers to humans, I wonder why not Bonobos or Orangutans? Why not Chimpanzees or Gorillas? Humans are apes. It causes me to wonder how that factors into a setting where Awakened lived in a non-history as spiritually superior beings. Are apes just shells of a fallen world and the evolved humans was the best spot for our mystical souls? Or is being Awakened not necessarily dependent on a particular species?

                          Indeed. And not simply learn sign language but it creatively, demonstrating empathy and theory of mind. Washoe, a signing chimpanzee, has demonstrated a capacity to understand the role of a recipient in communication; slowing her signing when communicating with new zoo keepers to make it easier for them to understand. Likewise, she's used the sign for tear to convey sympathy and sadness, despite crying not being something chimpanzees are physically capable of doing. That suggests empathy and an understanding of relational language, if not outright symbolism.

                          Most interesting of all, gorillas typically fail the mirror test - a common test of cognitive capacity to recognize oneself as separate from one's environment - but Koko, the famed signing gorilla, passed it.

                          And that's all without going into the more esoteric stuff, like the suggestions that chimpanzees develop religious behaviour towards large bodies of water.

                              If language is the only thing that separates non-human animals from Awakening, a strong case is probably with the more chatty of the cephalopod animals.

                              Of course, from the very human-centric attempts to define language, one of the key factors in what makes human communication "language" is the features beyond communication. A good example is the ability to creatively construct new words for arbitrary reasons. For example, making up a new word just to fit a rhyming scheme in a poem. We have never observed such behaviors in any other animals; even other apes we've taught to speak a mutually intelligible communication system.

                              That also fits in with Mage fairly well. The first instance of an Imago of a spell is very much an extension of that same concept: the ability to creatively apply parts of symbols into a unique expression that did not exist before.
