    Using automatic successes replacing exploding dices?

    09-25-2016, 08:08 PM
    Hello everyone.

    Im running a werewolf game and my players arent happy with how the exploding dice mechanic works.

    More often than not the pcs would get a 10, get happy with a reroll and then get dissapointed when it doesnt reach success.

    On the other side of the spectrum so far 3 games in i have had many situations when a goon with 4 dices keep exploding dices 2 or 3 times until a pc died (i let them burn a WP to survive)

    The group comes from playing OWoD 20 in which a 10 again was replaced with an automatic success (meaning if you roll a 10 it counted as 2 successes instead of 1) and one of the player suggested me that house rule.

    While owod and cod are very similar they still got differences. Has anyone try this? Or see a huge problem i am not seeing?

    The idea would be that any ability that allows for exploding dice like 8-again, 9-again or 10-again would be replace with counting as 2 success trading the posibility of "infinity rerolls" for 2 sure successes when triggered. This would not apply to rote action rerolls.

    Thank you

      It would make combat deadlier.

      In your examples, for instance. Your group gets a 10, and gets an extra success. But a goon with 4 dices gets 2 10s, getting 4 successes. Add Weapon Bonus on top of that.

      Keeping in mind probability is the same for NPCs and PCs, you should probably avoid using groups of NPCs as antagonists.

              Yeah, I don't think it would change most things prominently, but it could affect combat.

              It does increase your chances of a dramatic success, but since you need five successes for that, it's not going to unbalance things too much.

              As nofather said, combat is the real point of contention. CoD combat is pretty damn deadly (particularly after the 2E rules changes), and I could see that making things even more dangerous. Eight health seems to be the player average, meaning five damage is when you start to see combat penalties, and eight is when your character is unconscious (and has eight minutes left until death). Two 10s suddenly turns from a quarter of a character's health pool and a moderate chance for more to half incapacitation.

              Maybe make automatic successes one of the options players can take for willpower expenditure?

              More likely, I'd just tell my players to stop being babies. Luck is the nature of the game. If it wasn't a factor, we'd just compare stat values. Sometimes the enemy pulls a hat trick. Sometimes you do. And most of the time, confirmation bias ensures you that the former outweighs the latter. Luckily, the new rules are designed to reward failure with experience. These games aren't about winning, they're about the highs and lows, and the more rare huge successes are, the more rewarding they are.

Scale accordingly.

                                          A 10-again die has an EV of 1/3. Getting +2 on 10 has an EV of 0.4
                                          A 10&9-again die has an EV of 0.3*(10/8) = 0.375. Getting +2 on 9 or 10 has an EV of 0.5
                                          A 10&9&8-again die has an EV of 0.3*(10/7) = 0.429. Getting +2 on 8 or 9 or 10 has an EV of 0.6

                                          Personally I'd do it as: if you were rolling a 10-again or 9-again, then you get one automatic success per three die you roll. If you were rolling a 8-again, then one auto success per 2.5 dice you roll (i.e. 2 auto successes if rolling five dice etc.)
