    Classes of Magic [Subsystem]

    10-06-2023, 08:25 AM
    So for quite a while now I have been thinking about how to adapt certain D&D class ideas that I had in mind into a system. Obviously, I considered D&D as a system, but I had too many variations that I wanted to examine and making 20 levels of a class sounded like a headache to me (and I also have some sour taste in my mouth from D&D lately so also that). As such, I invested a lot of time to conceptually trying to work around it, almost considering to make my own system (or use a very flexible one as a base, such as Savage Worlds), when it occurred to me that I do know of a system that allows a flexible use of magic and which probably could be modified to give the aesthetics that I want without dealing too much with the system. So yeah, I decided to modify M:tAw by adding a Kith-like splat to Mage based around D&D classes- Calls.

    Basically, Calls are certain wrappings you add on the Mage template during character creation, which encourages magic to be used in certain manners by enforcing a set of Conditions which are gained by increasing Gnosis (currently up to 5, because let's avoid "epic" levels for now), meant to "replicate" the "level benefits" in D&D Classes. The Gnosis Conditions include an additional way to gain Mana, a Condition which increase Paradox chances ion certain occasions, and two more Conditions to influence how the Awakened casts magic. All the Conditions which don't grant Mana grant Arcane Beats instead, like Paradox Conditions. Additionally, Calls have a special Act of Hubris for the Medium Wisdom range (meant, again, to shape behaviour), and their own Magical Symbolism, including 3 additional Rote Skills and Call Tools. In case the Call rote Skills overlap with their Order Rote Skills, the character gains a Specialty in that skill for free.

    While the Calls are meant to coexist alongside Paths and Orders and not to replace them, they do east a bit of territory from both sides, especially parts of the Paths as they take certain nicknames (such as Witch, Warlock of Thaumaturge) and use them for something else. As it is a fan project with a certain goal I don't mind it, but in case it is a problem I suggest just taking it into account that when you had another significant splat into the system, it is going to step on some toes. Obviously, suggesting on refining those Calls will be welcome.

    Anyway, while Calls are meant to represent some modifications to the template and different views for what is "magic" in the context of the game, there are still some "ground rules" which Calls need to obey, imo, to keep the themes and integrity of the game. This is what I called the "Postulates of Magic", which are presented below together with the first Call that I wrote- the Rogue (there are currently around 38 different Calls, but I hope that some will fall out as I will discover that they have too much of overlap and can't stand on their own). Hope you'll like it!

    The Postulates

    Magic Exists

    The existence of magic is a fundamental fact of the universe. While many cultures, orders and cults have tried to define it through numerous ways or even deny it, the underlying fact is that magic is an active force and an elemental part of reality, serving as the “power of creation”.

    As Above, So Below

    Magic can not be understood, defined or formulated, yet it can only be activated through understanding, definition and formulation. Different sects have different approaches, and those approaches are the ones which allow magic to be actualized- a Fallen understanding serving as a channel to a Supernal truth. As such, the unexplained is being explained, even though falsely, and that false understanding is what guides towards Ascension.

    All Lies are Equal

    As magic transcends understanding, all approaches to magic are equally false. None is “closer” to truth as all provide different paths to truth. None of them sins towards magic as magic has no preference and no morals. By claiming to be more superior than the others, they actively undermine their own truth, becoming false in the process- yet one must commit to a certain Lie and claim it to be true, or magic will not be able to be actualized.

    The Ancients Failed

    Once, magic was free. Now, the world is Fallen. The ancients which once guided the world through magic have failed their faithful, either destroying the old world or rising to become tyrannical gods. This, however, never happened- there was never a utopian world of magic which had fallen from grace, there was never a paradise from which humans were banished. Yet it is a living fact within magic that there were ancients, and that they are now gone, forever lost. Each society defines the “lost utopia” according to their own terms, be it Atlantis, Eden, Lemuria or whatever fits their own interpretation of magic.

    Hubris Demands Compensation

    Acts of hubris sin against the very nature of reality and the foundation of magic. No methodology can escape this demand, for this is the punishment which fell upon the ancients, forming a law upon the heavens. Any careless use of magic would end up with twisting, if not shattering, the very nature of reality, allowing it to be corrupted and become less true and more false. This act is what is sometimes called a “Paradox” - a true state forced upon a false reality and vice versa - yet different groups have their own terms to explain the failure of magic when it is used without the proper wisdom.
    ​
    Rogue

    Magic is Serendipitous

    Magic is not learned- it occurs, drawn to the Awakened who are favoured by the Powers that Be. The Awakening is not a part of calculated mechanisation of the gods, it is a stochastic process, where the people in the right place and the right time are simply lucky enough to stumble into a Mystery Play, and are lucky enough to survive it as well. Some people win the lottery, others win the genetic lottery- it makes sense that some souls win the Supernal lottery as well. And those who are lucky enough to Awaken just keep luck drawn towards them. It is just like evolution- the lucky survive, and the more they survive the lickeir they will be.


    Rogues generally don’t view magic as an analytical or mystical process- magic just happens, and it generally serves in favour of the rogue unless there are other powers in play. As such, the magic of the rogues is rarely a vulgar one- it almost always emerges as part of reality, disrupting the fabric of the Fallen as little as possible. Oh, sure, rogues often have their small ceremonies and superstitions- knock on wood, spit when a black cat passes, wear your lucky shirt and so forth, but those are just methods to draw luck. When they negotiate, they just know the right words that will allow them to open the road to their target;s heart. When they are getting shot, the bullets accidently ricochet and start a fireball that consumes their enemies. When the dead claw their way from the grave with desire for vengeance, the rogue just happens to throw salt upon them, and somehow it is enough to banish them back to the hungry Underworld. Magic is an instinct, an hunch, and should be understood the same way.


    While being lucky is obviously beneficial, it is hubris to think there is no cost for one’s fortune. Rogues who often abuse their luck start to draw the ire of the gods, as they put themselves into troubles out of faith they will be saved. While sometimes they just die as their luck fails them, much worse events take place- luck is invoked in their favour when by all chance, it should be impossible for them to succeed. Their demand for fortune overrides reality itself, and by doing so, reality twists and breaks and the gods rage, demanding compensation. That’s when the Abyss bursts into the world- balancing the luck of the rogue with much needed misfortune.

    Gnosis Conditions

    Bountiful Luck

    Rogues dance on the strings of fate, allowing them to find enlightenment at pure chance. Once per chapter, when making a role related to their Call Rote Skills and achieving an Exceptional Success, they can choose to “liquify” their luck into Mana, gaining an amount of Mana equal to their number of successes in the roll instead of the regular effect of an Exceptional Success. The role will still be treated as a Success in whatever it was meant to achieve.
    Source: Adopting the Rogue Call

    Out of Pattern

    Rogues view magic as happy coincidences bestowed upon them through randomness and chance, and trying to enforce that act through force disrupts their delicate symbolism. When casting magic in a way which visually violates the natural laws, add 2 dice to their Paradox roll.
    Source: adopting the Rogue Call
    Arcane Beat: the character avoids solving a problem using magic at a significant cost in order to avoid using it vulgarly.

    Sly Hands

    Rogues put their lives in the hands of chance, betting on lives on a daily basis. When failing in a roll which involves a Speciality, the Rogue may choose to take a Dramatic Failure instead of a regular Failure as normal- yet by doing so, they may choose to gain an Arcane Beat instead of a regular Beast.
    Source: reaching Gnosis 3 as a Rogue
    Arcane Beat: as described

    Chained by Events

    Rogues are so used to relying on their luck that they become imprints of it- they must follow a set of rules, or otherwise they’ll fall from the Pattern and become forsaken by the luck that carries them. Choose a certain obligation, equivalent for the Ban of a Rank 3 ephemeral entity. If the rogue is to violate their Ban, their Gnosis is considered to be 2 dots lower for all purposes besides Mana capacity (but does affect Mana per turn) until the end of the Scene.
    Source: reaching Gnosis 5 as a Rogue
    Arcane Beat: following the Ban at the cost of causing significant damage to your goals or allies.

    Act of Hubris

    Wisdom 4-7, Medium / Understanding

    Rogues are tied to the natural flow of the universe, and that understanding could lead them to gain further intuition about what might happen. Disrupting an event which should naturally happen as understood through prophecy, dedicated knowledge or common sense, is considered an Act of Hubris on their part. Whether it is stopping a cat from devouring a pigeon, convincing a person to turn away from their true love or turning a poor person to rich one, when performed through the acts of magic (or when it is known to be destined through the acts of magic), those acts are nothing more than a hubris.

    Magic Symbolism: Taking a Chance

    Rogues always lurk between the cracks in reality, being favoured by fortune as they put themselves in peril. As such, rogues are always driven to situations where it is all or nothing, gambling on their lives to either win it all or fall to the Abyss. However, that doesn’t mean that they are fools- they always make sure that luck is in their favour, drawing it through their personal rituals and charms, as well as maintaining that they are skillful enough at getting out of trouble as they are in getting into them, just to make sure they have a way out if and when luck will fail them.

    Call Tools: Superstitions, lucky charms, dice and games

    Call Rote Skills: Enigmas, Larceny, Socialise

    Philosophy of Magic

    On the Paths

    Acanthus: it is good to truly understand what “luck” means, but you should be careful to not try and control it as much.

    Mastigos: making a friend from a foe is the greatest skill you’ll have in your arsenal- just charm them with a wink and a smile and they are yours.

    Moros: it is always good to have a card in your sleeve- such as an “infinite gold” cheat code or having a friendly ghost whispering in your ear.

    Obrimos: we are favoured by magic itself. The gods have blessed us, and they are on our side when all hell breaks loose.

    Thyrsus: surviving a fatal wound or gaining the favour of an ancient good are all privileges granted to us, and which we shouldn’t abuse.

    One the Orders

    Adamantine Arrow: war is nothing but a game, and there is no better test for your luck than surviving a battle.

    Guardians of the Veil: providence must be cherished- use it carelessly, and it may run out, and then the Abyss will come to catch you and no one will hear you scream.

    Mysterium: knowledge is power- you shouldn’t take a bet before making sure you stack the deck in your favour.

    Silver Ladder: luck has blessed us with many gifts, which we should use at the benefit of those less fortunate than us.

    Free Council: everyone has their own methods to draw luck and ward against misfortune. We should learn from them, instead of focusing on ourselves.

    Seers of the Throne: luck is a gift of the Exarch, meant for their servants- and as they grant it to those worthy, they can easily also take it away.

    Tremere: luck should be at the hands of the worthy- and as such, it should be taken from those misusing it.

    Scelesti: the Abyss is not the misfortune incarnated- it is true providence, and infinity of possibilities. Drink deep from it and prosper.

    Banishers: luck grants us so much, yet some dare to demand more than they deserve, and burn the world in the process. This hubris must be punished.

    On the Arcana

    Death: death always accepts gambles.

    Fate: always in the right place and the right time.

    Forces: all that you need to start an inferno is a spark.

    Life: the human body can achieve great wonders when wired correctly.

    Matter: with the right set of tools everything can be shaped.

    Mind: say the right words and you can easily open the door to your enemy’s heart.

    Prime: solve the equations, make sure to not divide by zero, and follow the maths.

    Space: just follow your intuition and the world will bend for your will.

    Spirit: even a lesser god can’t withstand flattery

    Time: what is prophecy if not remembering but backwards?

    Abyss: the gap between the lucky and the unlucky

          Before making a new Condition, you should always ask yourself:

          How does the gameplay experience benefit from this being a named Condition with a resolution or beat trigger, instead of just being a general mechanic, keeping in mind that the Condition system best functions when a minimal number of simple and flexible Conditions are in play at a given time, each immediately relevant to what is going on in the story, and each impelling player and character behavior towards interesting and genre-appropriate narrative events and plot beats?

          (You may ask: "Don't most of the Conditions in the books fail this test?"
          Yes, they do.)

          I'm not sure what use the Postulates are, as they seesaw between simply restating the Awakening setting premise and replacing parts of it with an overbroad shrug. They seem self-contradictory (if all lies are equal, how are there living facts within magic defining the lost Time Before? if no teaching is a sin for magic knows not sin, why is there then a Postulate all about the sins magic always knows?), and not in a way where the contradictions express something flavorful and complex.

          I like the effects of the Rogue's first two or three Conditions alright, though you may have gathered my feeling on making them actually Conditions rather than just "if you are a Rogue, apply this Paradox modifier and get Mana from this situation." The Mana gain does seem a bit too high. Remember that under normal conditions, an exceptional success will return a minimum of five Mana if it's just equal to successes. The third or fourth Condition seems to me getting into overly defined territory. This is a "kith," a more defined subset of Path "style" meant to layer on top of Path and Order. Don't make it too complicated and detailed, your character already has a lot of other stuff going on. Cut down to one or two simple but impactful changes to the character's approach to magic and to life.

          The Rogue's Act of Hubris seems like a recipe for arguments over what does and does not count as something that "should happen." If you can't turn a poor person into a rich person because if a person is poor then they're definitionally supposed to be poor (shades of The Secret and prosperity gospel)... what can you do? Taken to its extreme conclusion, this logic suggests that everything is already as it should be and you just can't use magic. That is extreme, but... where's the line?

          Apologies if the feedback is presented as too harsh, I'm very low on sleep right now and spilling words dimly.

                      First, just to make sure- your criticism is very much welcomed and the feedback doesn't feel too harsh. Constructive criticism is always a good thing, and this is why I publish those things.

                      As for the comments themselves-

                      Originally posted by Stupid Loserman View Post
                      I'm not sure what use the Postulates are, as they seesaw between simply restating the Awakening setting premise and replacing parts of it with an overbroad shrug. They seem self-contradictory (if all lies are equal, how are there living facts within magic defining the lost Time Before? if no teaching is a sin for magic knows not sin, why is there then a Postulate all about the sins magic always knows?), and not in a way where the contradictions express something flavorful and complex.
                      Basically, the Postulates are not some system or in-game terms or something like that. They were made as my own "design guidelines"- things to keep in mind when I write those Calls down. The reason is that at as things stand, I was not sure how diverged the interpretations of the Calls about magic are going to be, so I needed to keep something in mind. It should be noted that I have initially considered Calls as replacements for Orders (you might see some remnants of it in the writeup), so while I did the mental link between the Postulates and the outcome it might be less clear for others. Basically, they should represent something that all Calls agree upon, and that there is no Call that violates it. It needs to be seen how relevant they eventually will be, which depends on how far am I going to diverge from the :"canon" perception.

                      Just to make sure we are all on the same page, here is what the Postulates are meant to express:

                      - Magic exists (pretty basic)
                      - All Calls are equal in their perception of magic ("lies" mean "Calls" in this regards. It means that no Call is the true "master of magic" or is somehow "closer" to understand magic than the others- so no "Wizards are the true masters of magic" kind of thing)
                      - There was a World Before which have been retroactively removed from the world (Exarch shenanigans)
                      - fuck around and you'll get Paradox (so no Call can have a feature to ignore Paradox).

                      So yeah, I don't think that are too weird, and potentially are not needed, but again it is just to make sure I "stay in lines" when writing those down.

                      I like the effects of the Rogue's first two or three Conditions alright, though you may have gathered my feeling on making them actually Conditions rather than just "if you are a Rogue, apply this Paradox modifier and get Mana from this situation." The Mana gain does seem a bit too high. Remember that under normal conditions, an exceptional success will return a minimum of five Mana if it's just equal to successes. The third or fourth Condition seems to me getting into overly defined territory. This is a "kith," a more defined subset of Path "style" meant to layer on top of Path and Order. Don't make it too complicated and detailed, your character already has a lot of other stuff going on. Cut down to one or two simple but impactful changes to the character's approach to magic and to life.
                      Like, the main question I have here is "are there too many features, or is it just their mechanization as Conditions"? Because If it is the later, I personally don't have an issue with it but I also don't have a strong attachment for not calling them "Conditions". I explained my rational for why they were made so, but I can, like, replace the Act of Hubris with Chained by Events (I wasn't sure about it anyway), make Bountiful Luck into a non-Condition mechanic (just give it some fancy name), and so just reman with 2 Gnostic Conditions. This both reduce the amount of moving parts and reduce the number of Conditions to 2, which I still want them to be as such as it does serves my goal as explained.

                      We could, however, make one Condition into Persistent (gained at Gnosis 3) and change the other into Temporary one, but it would require some thought about how to redesign them (probably Out of Pattern is the Persistent one and Sly Hands is the temporary one, but I need to consider how to gain it).

                      Again, thanks for the feedback, and feel free to give more comments both about those subjects or in the future

                          Bountiful Luck is pretty interesting. Ironically, it does not gain much benefit from Conditions like Inspired, which is something Fate magic could grant. It can be powerful with the right setup, but the once per chapter limit prevents it from being abused.

                          Regarding Out of Pattern, I am concerned that in extreme situations, where the mage needs to throw everything at a problem, it might be too detrimental. When facing a dangerous spirit, escaping a peril in the Underworld or dealing with guardians of a piece of Infrastructure, natural law is not a primary concern when everything would be abnormal to a Sleeper. Since the objective is to incentivize certain behaviour patterns, I think including an exception for non-mundane situations would be helpful.

                          Sly Hands is pretty cool mechanically, but the name seems to suggest a smooth criminal, a precise conman. The mechanics, however, are about the moment a twist of fate happens, landing a Rogue in some hot water. I think the name should reflect that. Curiously, if this is used often by players, it effectively becomes the inverse of Reliable Talent.

                          Chained by Events feels like it errs a bit on the side of punishment. You either operate under a Ban and take significant setbacks, or your Gnosis takes a penalty. I feel like the Arcane Beat is not a counterbalance to losing dice on your power stat, since it affects Clashes of Wills, spell control, mana per turn limit and spellcasting ability.

                          The Call itself feels strongly Acanthus by design, which makes the new Act of Hubris feel off. Acanthus are all about taking charge of their own fate, embracing Choice & Consequence, rather than keeping their head down and be pulled along by the currents of fate. Similarly, Rogues navigate outside the law, using superlative skill and wit to sneak in a win. So I imagine its supernatural archtype would have an emphasis on defying fate, on finding ways to trick prophecy.

                          Hopefully that proved helpful, I think this is a pretty cool concept.

I feel like the Arcane Beat is not a counterbalance to losing dice on your power stat, since it affects Clashes of Wills, spell control, mana per turn limit and spellcasting ability.

                                      The Call itself feels strongly Acanthus by design, which makes the new Act of Hubris feel off. Acanthus are all about taking charge of their own fate, embracing Choice & Consequence, rather than keeping their head down and be pulled along by the currents of fate. Similarly, Rogues navigate outside the law, using superlative skill and wit to sneak in a win. So I imagine its supernatural archtype would have an emphasis on defying fate, on finding ways to trick prophecy.
                                      As I mentioned, I considered to move Chained by Events into an Act of Hubris, and I currently consider cutting the additional Act of hubris all together (I think that there is enough material for a Call even without it), so I may just cut it altogether.

                                      Also, just to clarify (which I think it also answers FallenEco), while Rogues do rely on luck, they are not meant to be directly tied to the Acanthus (that is, any Path could adopt any Call- there might be more Acanthus who adopt this Call, but nothing mechanical is meant to limit it in such a way). Also, I am still going back and forth about the ability to adopt a Call post-Awakening, and sometimes even consider rules for changing a Call later in game. Still rough thoughts, nothing set.

                                                  Next Call is now up- again, feel free to share criticism:

                                                  Fighter

                                                  Magic Forge the Soul

                                                  In the eyes of the fighter, magic is only as strong as the vessel which channels it. A healthy mind in a healthy body leads to a healthy soul- that’s the essence. While the Awakened can move mountains and command the gods, magic always takes its toll- and if the soul is weak, the furnace of magic will consume it, shattering it into pieces. If the soul is strong, it will be forged to its ideal form, actualising the Awakened Will without falling into the pitfalls of hubris.

                                                  As such, fighters always care about their physical form and mental state- while some fighters might take it to the extreme, most seek the perfected form which would lead to perfected will and perfected magic. While some of the Awakened use spells in order to augment their bodies, for the fighters this is commonly being viewed as “cheating”- you merely adjust the body, you do not truly forge it, and as such you become dependent on magic which would bring to your downfall. A true Awakened does not need magic in order to solve their problems- it merely comes as an expression of their own enlightenment, reached through hard work and effort. Only after the body and the mind are being properly trained to their human maximum, then magic could truly offer to transcend their physical limitations, crossing the edge between a human and a god- for only the strongest and flawless blades would not shatter in the inferno which is Ascension.

                                                  In order to measure the purity of the soul, however, it must be tested. Fighters as such commonly do so by putting themselves into conflicts- going through both mundane and supernatural dangers, striving through mental and physical hardships as one. Participating in the Duel Arcana, fighting against lesser gods, testing their skills in competitions and working all night without any sleep- all of those forge the soul of the fighter, allowing it to draw upon greater magic. As they view their body as the vessel of magic, they often imbue themselves with supernatural capabilities, either weaving the magic into their own Patterns or combine it with their movements and strikes. Yet they must always make sure to never become dependent on it- for this is how the Abyss pollutes the body, mind and soul, turning the paragon of excellence into a crippled husk.

                                                  Libation: Blood of the Enemy

                                                  Fighters draw their worth from their antithesis, finding enlightenment in proper adversary. Once per chapter, when fighting a “fair fight” against an enemy and causing the target at least one point of lethal damage, the fighter can exchange that one point of damage in order to gain an amount of Mana equal to their Gnosis. Generally, a “fair fight” means not using traps, ambushes, poisons and other forms of deceit, as well as using weapons of “proper level” against the threat- such as mundane weapons against mortals and magic against spirits.

                                                  Gnostic Conditions

                                                  Living Vessel (Persistent)

                                                  Fighters do not view themselves as users of magic, but as capacitors who contain it within them. For them, magic must be channelled through their own bodies as a vessel- or otherwise, the Abyss will lash out as they corrupt their own souls with hubris. When casting a spell that does not act through the fighter’s body or tools directly (such as throwing a lightning bolt instead of channelling it through a fist or sword), add 2 dice to the Paradox roll.

                                                  Source: adopting the Fighter Call

                                                  Arcane Beat: every time you put yourself in a significant hindrance or risk due to the casting limitation, gain an Arcane Beast

                                                  Soul Blade

                                                  Fighters bond with their dedicated weapons, weaving them into their own Patterns. While under this Condition, the character gains a +1 for all rolls using the weapon that triggered the Condition and -2 when using other weapons or ritual tools until the end of the scene.

                                                  Source: activating Blood of the Enemy

                                                  Resolution: either defeating or being defeated by the chosen enemy.

                                                  Arcane Beat: defeat the enemy, either by killing them or receive a surrender.

                                                  Act of Hubris: Rusting the Blade

                                                  Wisdom 4-7, Medium / Understanding

                                                  Fighters believe that magic is not a tool- it is a blessing bestowed on those who are perfected, and avoiding to test the blade leads to its rust, and as such to hubris. Refusing a fair challenge when proposed to them by another, be it a duel, a game or a competition, is considered as an Act of Hubris for fighters.

                                                  Magic Symbolism: Tools of War

                                                  Fighters are made for battle- their bodies are forged, their minds are sharpened, their souls bear the scars of their conflict. Each fighter is a living weapon meant to bring ruin upon their enemies while protecting whatever they hold dear. As such, the fighters express their existence as a vessel of war through the tools of war themselves, channelling their magic with their bound weapons, proof of their victories and their very bodies, which are deadly weapons by themselves.

                                                  Call Tools: Weapons and armour, martial arts, trophies won in battle

                                                  Call Route Skills: Athletics, Warfare, Weaponry

                                                  Philosophy of Magic

                                                  On the Paths

                                                  Acanthus: always follow your oaths and the agreements you made with both allies and enemies, for those who break them draw the ire of the gods.

                                                  Mastigos: first break the mind of the enemy, then break their body. Those who can’t withstand the first test are not worthy of the second.

                                                  Moros: always pay respect to the dead, even if they were your enemies, for all are equal in death. Otherwise, the dead will demand their due.

                                                  Obrimos: a proper vessel of the divine can hold the power of the gods in their hands- yet to be consumed with destruction will destroy you as well.

                                                  Thyrsus: to truly forge one’s body and mind, they must leave the comfort of civilization behind. Learn the ways of tooth and claw before you dare to lift a sword.

                                                  On the Orders

                                                  Adamantine Arrow: always fight with honour and respect your rival. This is what turns simple violence into a battle, and distinguishes men from beasts.

                                                  Guardians of the Veil: the soul is purified through the cycles of reincarnation- and those who have flawed souls should return to the cycle so they may be forged anew.

                                                  Mysterium: one must know their enemy if they are to defeat them. Understand their weakness, plan your strategy, and when they are the least prepared- attack.

                                                  Silver Ladder: to be a true leader of the people, you must represent their values- be at your most excellence, and others will follow your footsteps.

                                                  Free Council: humanity has forged countless tools of war and countless ways to destroy itself. Learn from their mistakes so you won’t do the same.

                                                  Spirit: a god can be killed if you have the right weapon.

                                                      So while I made some advancements on this projects, I always kept feeling like I'm hitting my head against a wall and that I somehow overcomplicate things. That feeling just made it very hard for me to make any advances in this project, as I kept feeling like there must be a simpler way to get what I want, and that there are too many moving parts in this splat. After giving it some thought, I eventually came to the conclusion that I really was overcomplicating this- I wanted Calls to be a behavioral system that shapes magic, and as such I really should have just made the Calls into what they needed to be- a Condition which grants Arcane Beats. As such, I've ditched most of the other features (those probably could be better adapted as Merits which require the Call Condition in order to be bought), as well keeping the Magic Symbolism and Call Tools (which seems to be a pretty standard part of Mage splats). However, instead of granting 3 Route Skills (which, again, overlap with Order), the character instead chooses a single free Specialty among the three Call Skills which are presented. This give some variety within the Calls and I think is a somewhat more elegant solution than the current system. As it is a much simpler solution, it also helped me to write up all of my "Tier 1 Warrior Calls" (where Tier 1 refers to "standard D&D classes" concepts). I am yet to write a bit of fluff in regards to a "Awakening to the Call", so currently it is mostly mechanics. Please let me know what you think!

                                                      Rogue: Magic is Serendipitous

                                                      Rogue (Persistent)

                                                      You are a rogue- master of trickery and mischief who is carried by luck. As a rogue, you view magic as a providence brought from fate, the gods, the universe or whatever name you give to the Powers that Be. As such, your spells must also flow with the course of luck, for disrupting it might summon the Abyss. When casting a spell, if your magic is actualized as something which could be explained as coincidence or happy occurrence (such as a lightning striking the enemy during rain or a small fortune being found on the floor), you gain 2 free Reach for the spell. However, if the spell is cast in a manner which is vulgar and obviously magical, you suffer from a +2 modifier for the Paradox roll.

                                                      Arcane Beat: whenever you manage to use trickery against your enemy and succeed, you gain an Arcane Beat.

                                                      Magic Symbolism: Luck

                                                      Fighter (Persistent)

As a fighter, you see magic as being only as strong as the vessel which bears it- your body, and as such the excellence of the body is required in order to reach the greatest levels of magic, and that the body must serve as the spell’s perfect vessel. When casting a spell which is channelled through your body, weapons or armour (as as enhancing the strength of a punch or cloaking a sword with flame), you gain 2 free Reach for the spell. However, when casting a spell without using the body as a vessel, you suffer from a +2 modifier for the Paradox roll.

                                                      Arcane Beat: whenever you solve a problem without magic when it could have been done easily otherwise while putting yourself or your allies at risk, you gain an Arcane Beat.

                                                      Magic Symbolism: Tools of War

                                                      Monk (Persistent)

                                                      You are a monk- mystical warrior trained in emulating the Supernal through your body and soul. As a monk, you view magic as a result of spiritual enlightenment and freedom from physical constraints, which can only be achieved through meditation, discipline and rigid training, meant to embody aspects of the everyday world within their body and soul. Choose a certain aspect of the world- this could be an animal, weather, trade, location, element and so forth. This aspect serves as your Martial Art Style. While casting spells that resonate with your Style, you gain 2 free Reach. When casting a spell which violates that style, however, you suffer from a +2 to the Paradox roll.

                                                      Magic Symbolism: World Apart

                                                      Ranger (Persistent)

                                                      You are a ranger- a master of the hunt and stalker of the wilderness. As a ranger, you view magic as part of the environment- the world itself is filled with magic, and has a will of its own which must not be defied. As such, your magic must fit the world around you, or otherwise your magic will pollute the world with the foul touch of the Abyss. Whenever you cast a spell which fits or utilises the local environment (such as the use of local flora and fauna, the current state of lighting or weather or objects which are locally present), you gain 2 free Reach. However, if your spells outright defies the local environment or tries to violently change its properties, you suffer from a +2 modifier to the Paradox roll.

                                                      Arcane Beat: whenever you manage you bring down a foe by exploiting their weakness or your understanding of the environment, you gain an Arcane Beat

                                                      Magic Symbolism: the Great Hunt

                                                      Call Tools: traps, vehicles or tools which ease movement

                                                      Call Specialities: Drive, Ride, Stealth


                                                      Barbarian: Magic Unbound

                                                      Barbarian (Persistent)

                                                      You are a barbarian- a force of destruction embodied in human form. As a barbarian, you believe that the mind, or the ego, is the barrier which blocks the natural use of magic, and that by killing your own sense of self and delving into your instincts and raw impulses you can become one with the world and actualise true magic. As such, magic must be an untamed force that is born out of raw passion, while trying to bind it through words and logic you sin against its natural state, allowing the Abyss to enter the world. Whenever you cast a spell that aligns with either your Vice or Virtue, you gain 2 free Reach. However, if the use of the spell outright violates those Anchors, you suffer from a +2 to the Paradox roll.

As such, all acts of magic which serve those ideals are honourable, while those who violate them are punished by the wrath of the Abyss. Choose a certain code of conduct, made out of three clauses derived from your Virtue. Whenever your spell is meant to advance those values or behaviour in the world, gain 2 Reach for free. However, if your spell’s use breaks that code or disrupts those values or their progression in the world, you suffer from a +2 modifier to the Paradox roll.

                                                      Arcane Beat: whenever you make a significant sacrifice in the name of your ideals, you gain an Arcane Beat
