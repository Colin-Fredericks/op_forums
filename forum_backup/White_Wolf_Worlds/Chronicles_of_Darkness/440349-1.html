                Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                I was thinking more of a kidnapping slaver; but without the whole "take them to another country to avoid the law" because Disquiet effects everyone and thus slavery becomes normalized.

                Basically I'm going by Disney's Pinocchio because that's the only version I've seen and the True Fae who're very... unsubtle about the fact you're their slave.
                That has... Certain disturbing implications regarding the effects on the supernatural landscape. What would happen to the Shadow in a region affected by high levels of Pinocchian Disquiet? And a prevailing might-makes-right philosophy where people are taking slaves left and right, with the resulting desperation from the enslaved... Hmm. I'll have to open my copy of Inferno when I get home, but it seems like "spirits of oppression" plus "tormented souls" plus "the downtrodden wanting power to overthrow their masters" is a fine recipe for creating/attracting a couple different types of demon.

                And that's BEFORE adding in the fact that the Pinocchio is likely to be one of the slaves, which means you're probably going to have a Wasteland develop on top of all this.

                So then the Hedge-alike develops and the twisted faux-Fae (can we call them Bogeymen? Is that name taken already?) emerge to punish "wrongdoers," which there'll be no shortage of at this point.

                Meanwhile, the stories developing from all this are so riveting that the True Fae start to take notice...

                The story almost writes itself, doesn't it? The Gentry start grabbing people and leave Fetches in their place, which gives the Pinocchio plenty of raw material for attempting to create another of his kind. All he needs is a fresh human heart. PER ATTEMPT.

                Honestly, a Pinocchio Lineage would make a GREAT source of antagonists under this model. After their Progenitor's experiences, they figured out that they could roll into a remote town, let their Disquiet turn it into a haven for slavers, then keep their heads down while the Wasteland develops and the Bogeymen slaughter their masters. Then, in the aftermath, they become the saviors of the surviving former slaves, whom they lead to settle in another town. But their Disquiet is still in full effect, so the whole thing starts over.

                Meanwhile, they get plenty of corpses to use for parts, and even if the Fae don't show up, there's always the possibility that there's already a Fetch present.

                                                Just spitballing possibilities here. I'm thinking the Bogeymen enforce a twisted, heavily ironic form of lex talionis. Doing unto you as you have done unto others.

                                                And hey, maybe they don't leave the slaves alone so much as just not abducting them. But, you are guilty of such-and-such a crime, here's your punishment. And you did this other thing, so here's your retribution. And they're very expansive in what they consider an infraction.

                                                There doesn't even have to be what we'd consider consistency to the system.                                                             Marionette Wastelands

                                                            The Pinocchio Prometheans belong to the element of wood, and their connection to the Hedge is undeniable. When they linger in an area, it takes on the attributes of that realm, first superficially and then metaphysically, before finally opening Trods through which Bogeymen emerge.

                                                            Stage Two — Plants and fungi grow at an impossible rate. Weeds choke the lawns, tree roots rupture sidewalks from beneath, and black mold renders brand-new homes uninhabitable.

                                                            Stage Three — Children are plagued by nightmares of Bogeymen in their closets and under their beds. Buildings collapse, their walls torn apart and replaced by fungi and trees in a bizarre sort of fossilization; residents without any other recourse may just shrug their shoulders and move back in.

                                                            Stage Four — Trods appear everywhere and the Bogeymen (Hobgoblins contracted to the Blue Faery) show up to enforce the most horrific form of ironic, Bronze Age-esque pseudo-justice imaginable; nearly everyone is "guilty" of some offense for which the punishment is both extreme and horrific.
