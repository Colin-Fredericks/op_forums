            As Ophions are antagonists I'd err on the side of uncertainty. Part of the elder god's monstrousness resides in how totally alien they are, even their desires are indetermineable. The real question is how to spin them into A) a different form of alien from the God-Machine and B) an alienness that seems approachable by players looking to walk the road less traveled (e.g. Divining into divine madness). That second one is a toughy, but I'd venture a good jumping point is unconscious impulse and an implicit desire to bend the world into a very specific, but extremely alien shape (this especially is a departure from the G-M). For players this process goes: "take your most gonzo idea of how the world should work, now keep expanding and refining it for five millennia."

            To the point of becoming a Leviathan, I agree that there is little need to differentiate their inheritance from that of Uratha. Where the difference should be pronounced is in the handling of that inheritance, and the culture that rises around it (which are already very different from Forsaken). What Leviathan 2e could draw from WtF2 is having some alternatives to direct blood relation. Off the top of my head this could include:
            1. Rosemary's baby style children who were born through occult ritual, channeling a specific strain.
            2. Victim's cursed/processed/devoured and shat out(literally or figuratively) by power Leviathans (similar to how Stigmatics & Wolf-Blooded may arise from exposure).
            3. People who've come into possession of some terrible ancient artifact that begins to bend their very being into something new and alien.
            I'm sure I can think of a few more with time.

                Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                So here's a question for everyone. They can commit atrocities without feeling empathy, regret, or even satsifaction at the completion of their goals.

                In short; they're like Lovecraft's monsters. They don't kill you because it benifits them. They kill you because you're so insigificant they have no reason not to step on you.

                The question is; how do you describe that from the inside? Why do Ophions do anything at all if they have no feelings? I don't really want to make them another God Machine following their programming.
                Well, if they do something on a regular basis and show logical behavior, they aren't truly emotionless. Living beings need emotion to assign a priority of decisions and to internally judge their options. Psychological fact; truly emotionally-void people show an inability to come to decisions, even what they'll order for lunch, easily.

                I'd say an Ophion lacks any degree of empathy or even the abstract concept that other beings are real. To them, every other entity is simply a Thing I Use To Do Things or a Thing That Stops Me From Doing Things. Think a Real Time Strategy fan with his/her computer-generated army; on a conscious and subconscious level, the army does not actually exist to the player, they're just bits of code that look like people. Thus, the kind of human wave tactics that would be utterly horrifying in real life are interpreted by the player as having the moral context of shuffling army men or pieces on a chessboard around, and their deaths affect the player about as much as losing a piece to the unharmed and non-hostile opponent.

                I imagine an Ophion is the same way, but without the human opponent-and with no capacity to actually care for the cult except as resources. While an RTS can have a good story that makes the player care whether the individual characters in the army live or die, an Ophion never develops that, and never can. Any cultist conferring with them is, at best, just another mission briefing.

                That, and PenDragon's idea of an alien image of what the world should be. Becoming an Ophion should come off as losing your ability to care. By contrast, getting away from becoming an Ophion should come off as painful, as walled-off altruism comes crashing back, sort of like an eye in a dark room having a bright light suddenly switched on; the pain quickly vanishes, and you're probably better off by the end of it, but losing your detachment hurts.

                Also, yeah, seems in-theme to have Calling events. Particularly because Leviathans are terrified of dying out, and giving out plot hooks to have a genuine chance of actually increasing the overall population of the Wicked Tribe and for a solitary Leviathan to create a friend out of loneliness doesn't seem life a net loss. You are Cthulhu, it makes sense to also be able to Call, even if it's hard and inexact.

                                                    I'd like to make another push for Ego as the divinity tracking stat. Sure it doesn't fit the Freudian definition of the word, but other more common uses of the word (as in Egotistic, Egoism, and Egocentric) are an ideal fit. Other possible terms: Humility, Hauteur, or going a little foreign Ahamkara. (That last one requires explanation, but seems a good fit.)

                                                    As for tracking how bestial a Leviathan has become I'm still quite fond of Sapience. It has a ring to it that evokes Homo sapiens, which, at low levels of Sapience, a Leviathan would be far from. Other possible terms: Rationality, Temperance, and going into the Quaran Nafs (which again requires explanation).

                                                    We could also dip into Aristotle, notably his works on the concept of Ethos. Where he posited Ethos had three parts: Phronesis, Arete, and Eunoia. Phronesis could be a good term for a Leviathan's bestiality, as low Phronesis would dictate someone without prudence or practical wisdom. Eunoia refers to a persons receptivity and good-will between himself and his audience, which is ideal for a Divinity track (though tragically it would also be a descending track).
