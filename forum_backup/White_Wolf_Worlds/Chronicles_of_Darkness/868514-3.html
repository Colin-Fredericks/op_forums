        So, I shared some of my own thoughts on Neolithic vampires in (appropriately enough) the Vampires in the Neolithic thread, particularly that I liked the idea of the ancestors of the various clans being very different creatures that gradually grew more similar to one another. Along those lines, I thought I might start throwing out some ideas about ancient vampires that are more along those lines.

        So, first off, here's my first crack at a sort of "Proto-Nosferatu":

        Nightmares

        Neither immaterial ghosts not entirely things of this world, Nightmares are rarely seen or heard, but are nevertheless known by the fear and sickness that they spread. It is said that they are unclean spirits cast up upon the shores of Ocean Beyond Life, and that they come up from caves and deep wells to prey upon the living. They rarely venture far from the dark holes from which they emerge, for they cannot abide the light of the sun and so go about only at night.

        The Nightmare template consists of the following:
        • Face of Horror: Nightmares are frightfully hideous when their true form is revealed. They are said to most commonly resemble rotten and waterlogged human corpses, but they always bear some deformity, like a twisted limb (sometimes in excess of the normal number), a vestigial tail, or a mouth full of jagged and broken fangs, which marks them as not entirely human in origin. A nightmare can never disguise its monstrousness through any mundane means, and is incapable of normal social interaction with humans under most circumstances.
        • Hunt Unseen: Nightmares are naturally invisible to mortal perception and concealed even from the mystic senses possessed by the Wise. Those among the Wise who are learned in such matters believe that this is an effect of the creatures’ supernatural hideousness; that mortal senses and even the mind’s eye naturally turn away from such horror without registering anything more than a nameless unease. Attempts to penetrate this invisibility using supernatural means trigger a Clash of Wills. A Nightmare can deliberately reveal itself to mortal senses, but this takes a conscious act of will on its part.
        • Unearthly Form: Nightmares are only partly beings of this world, and are neither living in any ordinary sense of the term nor entirely made up of physical matter. Outside of extraordinary circumstances (at the Storyteller’s discretion), Nightmares suffer no damage from sources of harm that would normally inflict bashing damage, and suffer only bashing damage from sources that would normally deal lethal damage. They suffer Aggravated Damage from fire and sunlight as described in Vampire: the Requiem 2nd Edition. In addition, Nightmares are preternaturally light, weighing no more than a large cat or small dog in spite of their size and strength. They leave no footprints in firm ground and only the faintest traces even on snow or soft mud, and they can stand on top of sleeping victims without harming or waking them.
        • Predatory Senses: Nightmares have exceptional night vision and generally keen senses. They automatically gain the benefits of the Acute Senses merit and never suffer any die penalties from darkness. They do not, however, have any particular affinity for or special senses related to blood.
        • Breath-Drinking: Nightmares feed upon the breath of the living, and may only feed from a sleeping or unconscious victims. Although it inflicts damage and the Drained condition as described in Vampire: The Requiem 2nd Edition, the act of feeding does not itself pose a risk of waking the victim. Victims of a Nightmare’s feeding invariably suffer surreal and terrifying dreams which are sometimes recognizable to the Wise as visions of horrors from the Ocean Beyond Life. These dreams, along with the pain and weakness caused by the Nightmare’s feeding, are often the only signs of the creature plaguing the community.
        • Born of Darkness: Nightmares cannot embrace, nor can they create Ghouls or Revenants. They seem to arise spontaneously from the deep, dark places of the world. Each comes into the world with a Humanity between 3 and 5 and memories of once having been human, but these memories are often distorted and incomplete, and blended with nightmarish visions of a hellish underworld. No Nightmare can remember its death, if indeed it ever did suffer a mortal death, and none can adequately explain how it came to be in its present state. They do not begin play with any touchstones by default, but may gain them by taking the Touchstone merit at character creation or in the course of play.
        • Vampiric Traits: Nightmares begin play with one dot of Potency, which they may increase through the normal means, although they are incapable of performing Diablerie. They may spend vitae to heal or activate Physical Intensity as described in Vampire: The Requem 2nd Edition. They possess the Predatory Aura, but may only evoke the Monstrous Beast when Lashing Out or fighting another vampire’s Predatory Aura. They suffer the normal consequences of damage, including Torpor. In addition to their basic abilities, Nightmares may purchase the Atrocious, Haven, Touchstone, and Unsettling Gaze merits, and the Celerity, Nightmare, Potence, and Resilience Disciplines, as well as appropriate Devotions.

                                            The Purified in the Sundered World

                                            Sometimes accidents happen. That's what the purified are, accidents. In the Neolithic, there is only one way for a mortal to become immortal. No magic rite or ritual can delay the onset of age. There is no special potion to kill you and then restore your heartbeat. There is no philosopher stone from which springs the elixir of life. To become Purified three things must happen. First, you must have some skill with the occult. Second, you must travel into the Shadow. Finally, and most importantly, while in the Shadow you must be devoured by a Pangaean. Naturally, only a very few who meet these requirements ever come back as the Purified, but those who do are imbued with a fraction of the power of the mighty Pangaean that consumed them.

                                            As occult knowledge is required to become Purified, and hedge magic aids the process, The People who do become Purified are often drawn from the ranks of those who associate with The Wise or from the Wolf-born. Typically, this means that most Purified are potters or hunters.

                                            The People have no name for the Purified. The chances of meeting even one of the Purified is extremely small to begin with, and even those individuals who encounter them mistake them for the Wise. Some of the Wise are aware of their existence, although there is no standard nomenclature for the Purified yet, so a variety of names like The Returned, Spirit-Cousins, and Clay-Shapers are used. In the First Tongue, the Purified are known as the Asilal-thakh, or "Distant Wanderers". Wolf-born will occasionally go on a hunt for Urfarah, hoping to that Mother Wolf's hungry jaws offer them a different path to power than that of the Uratha. Most die horrible, mundane deaths and many Purified never find Father Wolf, instead returning with another Pangaean as their arcane totem.

                                            Creating a Neolithic Purified Character

                                            When creating a Neolithic Purified Character, the normal Purified template in Immortals is amended as follows:
                                            • In addition to having access to Numina and Siddhi, the Purified have access to a single Arcana, which is chosen at character creation. This Arcana is always the highest Arcana possessed by the Pangaean that created them. The Purified may purchase spells from that Arcana on a dot-for-dot basis, just as the Proximi of later eras do. The Purified use Chi in place of Gnosis, Integrity in place of Wisdom, and may spend Essence instead of Mana when casting spells.
                                            • The Purified's Unseen Sense applies the Pangaean that created the Purified and to any evidence of their passage, even if that evidence is entirely mundane in nature. The Purified have a deep spiritual connection to their progenitors, so they know if they have crossed through the fields where Bull was grazing yesterday, or if they pass over the site of Fox's demise.
                                            What has yet to come:

                                            Just as the worlds of the Wise and the Urathra are destined to be thrown into upheaval by the death of Wolf, the Purified will be dramatically impacted as well. With the fall of Pangaea and the rise of the Gauntlet, the Asilal-thakh find their souls split in half. They have the ability to draw upon both the Shadow and the Supernal; the latter connection is primarily mediated through their connection with the Pangaeans. Through the magic of sympathy, even a dead Pangaean can sustain a Purified's magic, largely thanks to the existence of the Omphalos Stones. The Gauntlet seals many Pangaeans away, however, cutting off the Purified's contact with their spiritual anchors. Many Purified cope the same way that the remaining Pangaeans do, by shedding their connection to the Arcana. Others trade immortality for power, gaining access to a second Arcana and becoming the first Proximi. A rare few continue to strike a balance between the spiritual and Supernal, but they all but disappear when the Vinca culture is finally destroyed.

                                            *******

                                            A pretty simple adaptation, but I really like it. I'm sure there are things that we can pick apart and improve, but I think this fits nicely into the weirdness niche of the Sundered World without being too over the top in terms of power. There are some fun implications and seeds that I thought up of while writing. In particular, I'd like to know what happens to the Wolf-born who was purified by Bull when the Gudthabak come calling?

                                                    Yes and no.

                                                    Yes, the power of the Arisen is ancient, lost, and arguably (not really arguable, actually) dwindling. They are cursed to not only be servants but they're cursed to be immortal. The world will only grow increasingly alien as they struggle to adapt. The art of relic creation is lost, the number of Shuankhsen only increases, the power mortal sorcerers wield diminishes, and people forsake the divine entirely. Despite their everlasting nature they're doomed.

                                                    But no, because underneath it all is a sense of timelessness. In every civilization for literally all of history the Deathless see the branches grown from the Nameless Empire's deep roots. Every myth hearkens back to theirs. The magic of Utterances is classic, Biblical myth kind of stuff that doesn't grow old. Death ever weighs heavy on the mortal mind, influencing them to join death cults no matter which age they dwell.

                                                    Sekhem is the lifeblood of the material world. It's been there since the first woman laid eyes on her child, and since a son set his dying mother to rest. Death is timeless. Lifeless things abound in the Sundered World.

                                                    Somewhere in all that is a story to be told, whether or not our protagonists are Mummies
