                                Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
                                They're sort of spirit-y incorporeal entities of hunting from Arcadia that fae grab, force into physical form (which they hate) and send them out to collect changelings. They are sort of immortal, near perfect shapechangers and make for rather.... different antagonists compared to most foes.
                                Giving some context to this:

                                In 1e, the True Fae vied against one another for Titles, massive Contracts with the Wyrd that allow them to manifest in Arcadia without risking their very being, through narrative conflicts called Legends, with the stakes generally being the Titles they used to engage in a given contest.

                                Typically, a Fae can only be challenged to Legend combat by the Fae who holds the claim to that Title, initially by being the one to describe that Fae in a fitting manner. The most dangerous exception to this restriction is if one of the Fae in a Legend falters in their description; the resulting "Dread Gap" allows any Fae to seize control of the narrative and either claim the prize for victory or fail without incurring personal loss.

                                In 2e, a freehold is founded upon a Bulwark, a ritual based on a story familiar to that part of the world which empowers the Courts with Approaches that effectively restrict the region from access by any Fae who cannot abide by their rules. If this sounds familiar, it's because it's really easy to describe a freehold as an artificial scarecrow-Fae. The Bulwark significantly narrows the range of viable invaders as long as it holds up.

                                The Huntsmen, then, are the Fae's tools for trying to force a Dread Gap by breaking the Bulwark while they're on their primary duty of retrieving a wayward changeling who's grown in power and status. Their main targets are almost invariably changelings who've built up a couple Approaches, which means they're more expensive to try to bring in in the long run, so they sow discord among the Courts by targeting the least of their nobility and leaning on that best of intimidations, "It's them and theirs or you and yours."

                                The suite of powers and tools they have at their disposal make the fact that they work on a time limit very fortunate for the Lost.
