        Probably too late to the party but here's my impressions. I'm aware of your style on your posts and want to add to the conversation how I'd treat each hurdle you found so far. I'll continue to address your following posts in this matter to help you grasp the system better.

        Re: I'm under the impression Charlaquin is mistaken. An individual bonuses and/or penalties has a cap of +5/-5 (barring supernatural mitigating circumstances, but you're a mortal here). If you somehow manage to stack 2 bonuses, say, a +4 suit for the red carpet to appear sharp before the cameras and burning willpower for the +3 bonus on that but you haven't slept because of heavy coke abuse in the last 24 hours (-1), then you'll roll Presence+Socialize at +6 in order to become the People's magazine cover. Trying to surf a tsunami while there's a typhoon going on would be Dexterity+Athletics at -10 (-5 from wild waters, -5 from the uprooting trees wind velocity).

        Re: Contested actions tie: Roll again. Eventually someone's gonna win, even if that specific turn grappling each other was spent with them locking knuckles and grunting while facing one another in a dramatic fashion without anyone getting the upper hand, yet.

        Re: Time. I personally love saying "Alright people, let's finish this chapter." When we've played a lot and the climax is starting to build.

        Re: It's called a beat instead of silly D&Desque XPs as in "story beats", an exercise in screen-writting. They mark the dramatic moments within scripts. Which is why 5 beats means 1 experience: you keep upping the ante into a climatic experience. Language is powerful.

        Re: No hard answers - nothing says you have to outright inquire, out of character which clues they're getting... just how they're looking for them. That should inform what to give them.

        Can your players solve everything by having your characters passively go about collecting clues? Yes, if you don't introduce complications you can. That's how the horror happens, that's how the drama splits forth, that's how they earn beats and experience.

        Re: Librarian example: Then your characters, in desperation at the oblivious librarian do something drastic: such as taking her hostage. Drama and beats galore. Or someone's savvy enough to social maneuver that without a hitch, but I wouldn't give the guy time to take the librarian out on a date to get a better impression before the Horror strikes. Or maybe the Librarian was the Horror and that's why she was so adamant. Go nuts.

        Re: “You're not paid enough to risk your life for this.” Could be heavy leverage (Presence or Strength+Intimidation). Just score 1 success whilst penalized by his 4 doors and he'll back away.

        Social maneuvering is specifically tailored at long term discussions. Like making a pro-life minister realize that sometimes a woman needs to have an abortion (73% of people agree with this, sometimes there's mitigating circumstances according to John Oliver).

        Re: Stacy failing at getting the last door with a threat at gunpoint and waiting a week to threaten him again (forcing doors and accruing no successes). Stacy now takes a breaking point for the threat and can shot the idiot in the leg at a bonus on however much damage she pulled, and another breaking point. And she gets I don't know, like 3 beats and 2 conditions for more beats after all is said and done.

        Maybe she scores 5 successes. Add 2 from the gun and she hit the femoral artery. He's going to die unless somebody provides expert medical care within turns. Maybe she doesn't cares, and just grabs the key to the private section and goes for the book. Maybe she does that before trying to desperately re-arrange the murder-scene somehow. The drama is flowing, the players are having cold sweat and the beats are mounting. People is having fun.

                            I've been a bit busy but let's get back to this. I also have my physical copy now, by the way. This will be a shorter read.


                            BUILDING EQUIPMENT

                            It's interesting that what I thought was going to be a crafting system also includes other forms of “creations,” like organizations and plans.

                            Ah, so it's supposed to be a McGyver system of sorts, where you “jury rig” together stuff while under duress or where you just create simpler stuff. When it asks if I have “dots in the relevant Skill equal to the equipment bonus blahdiblah,” does it refer to the Skill which the equipment boosts or the Skill I use to craft the object?

                            The actual crafting action seems straightforward. You get a penalty depending on the equipment bonus and then you roll and hope you're lucky. If you're crafting in combat, it takes a number of turns equal to the equipment bonus.

                            The good thing about the examples of play is that they remind you of rules that you easily forget as you read. For example, that you earn Beats for resolving Conditions. The example for crafting a plan was interesting. You can boost your pools fairly easily by coming prepared (and not fucking up).


                            FICTION: YEVGENY

                            What is this? Google translate? This must be the worst written text I have read in a while. Did Onyx Path pay for this?


                            BLACK THREADS: STORYTELLING

                            “Some groups find it ideal to stick with one Storyteller throughout the course of a chronicle ...” That's the way I'm playing and the perspective I'll have when reading this. Any other way doesn't fit my particular playstyle or the group I'm playing with.

                            I have some ideas of the kind of a campaign I'd run, but I'm withholding any decisions until I've read all of the book. I also want to take a look at the God-Machine Chronicle to see what that is about.

                            Most of the listed Storytelling Responsibilities are natural because of how long I've been roleplaying, but it's good that they exist. Let's see if there is any new advice that I could benefit from right away.

                            Provide Space to Play: We have two spaces that we play in. Most of the time we're playing over at a friend's place, but occassionally we're at mine. I have a better setup for managing atmospheric music and such, but my friend has a bit more space.

                            Food and Drink: We usually eat lunch together before we play, then buy some snacks for the game. Then we play for a couple of hours (usually 5-6 hours). When we don't have time to eat before the game, we usually take a break a few hours in to order pizza.

                            Establishing Setting and Theme: I think all players know fairly well what the others are interested in. We usually leave this to the Storyteller and then run with what he comes up with.

                            Interesting Characters: Every player is certaintly different when it comes to this. I rarely play combat monkey and usually plays either the sneaky type, the smart type or the social type. One of my players always play chaotic neutral and his characters tend to be cruel even when I want to have a more heroic group (I have to compromise with him a lot). Another player can play any type of character concept, but is often fairly passive and laid back. He can take some clever initiatives, but he often lets other players shine. He seems to enjoy playing the silent strong guy who stands next to the social character and looks menacing. Another of my players is very hyperactive and likes to take the spotlight. He doesn't care about optimization and usually creates very poorly made characters because he has an idea in his head and thinks it'd be fun, even if it doesn't work in practice.

                            Cheerlead for the Player Characters: I agree with this. I never go out of my way to punish my players and I often give more than I take. One of my players coined the phrase “CIFA moment” though. It came up some years ago when Spartacus was popular on TV and Batiatus had his quote about “cocks in fucking ass.” The CIFA moments are moments when I've taken all of my players hope and they think that they will be screwed over.

                            Providing Regalia: I suck at this. I mostly ST Exalted where it's easy for players to jump ropes with the storythread. The regalia is usually some kind of important letter or a symbol that's supposed to mean something. I would like to do this more often.

                            The Spines are all solid advice. It also gave some inspiration. Still not talking about any potential stories I'll run though.

                            I find that there aren't too much for me to comment on here. Most of it are solid advice. Other things don't fit my prefered way of Storytelling. The Exquisite Corpse thing doesn't interest me at all.

                            I don't have much experience with Storytelling horror. I've done some supernatural noir back in the day, and I've had grotesque and suspenseful elements in other games, mainly fantasy ones. Though I've played in horror games, I haven't really aimed to ST them, and this will be an interesting experience. If I can create a tense atmosphere for my players remain to be seen.


                            I'm going to continue with Missing Persons: Creating Your Setting next time.

                                    MISSING PERSONS: CHOOSE YOUR SETTING

                                    I understand that it's a really good tool for new Storytellers but I'm having some difficulties being interested at I start reading this. Thinking objectively, I do think it gives solid advice to new Storytellers and it's almost a walkthrough to how you can set up your first game.

                                    The methods where the Storyteller and the player design the game together doesn't appeal to me on either side of the table. When I'm Storyteller, I want to present the setting for my players. When I'm a player, I want to immerse myself in a world where I don't know what lurks around the corner. I blatantly ignore any suggestions in this chapter that doesn't fit my own playstyle---but I appreciate that it's there for others.

                                    The suggestions for how to create this “missing person” could be applied to pretty much any NPC though. It presents some questions you could ask in order to better define the character which is a good way to add depth to NPCs. The part about Putting it on Paper is a good way to create NPCs that are somehow tied to player backgrounds. I do allow my players to design NPCs during the chargen process---I just don't want them to narrate the world around themselves in game. I want their focus to be on their own characters in a situation presented to them.

                                    As I was reading through Implications, a thought occurred to me that these “missing person” rules can be used to present a situation where the person itself isn't missing per se, but the mystery itself needs to be uncovered. Your character wakes up in a rundown apartment covered in your boyfriend's blood and with his lifeless body next to you (boyfriend = missing person). You only remember a few vague details and you don't know if he was killed by you or someone else. Because everything implies that you're guilty, you dispose of the body and don't call the police. The mystery of “finding the missing person” is then played out as you “finding the clues that eventually lead to the conclusion of what happened to him.” The paranoia itself could be the horror of this story. Perhaps you gain breaking points when seeing cameras and police cars? Maybe you assault an innocent person because you think he's been watching you. You'll get breaking points if you see him without acting on it (because it adds to the paranoia) and you get breaking points for assaulting him and learning that he's innocent. I could truly torture my players with this. Hopefully in a good way.

                                        Initiative isn't actually determined by a dice pool, it is an odd man out in the system. You roll a single d10, then add the Initiative Modifier to the value rolled, and then that sum is the character's base initiative for the encounter. So if you roll a 4 on the die and your initiative is +7, you've got initiative 11. You then apply whatever penalty for weapons drawn, so if you draw a -1 initiative knife on the first turn, then from the second turn on you're acting at initiative 10.

                                        Defense is not rolled unless you're Dodging. Defense 7 is indeed a lot. Don't forget the ability to spend Willpower on dice. Violence in CofD basically expects you to bleed Willpower as much as you can.
