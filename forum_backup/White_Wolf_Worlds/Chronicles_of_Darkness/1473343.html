    Conspiracy Theory: VASCU

    01-26-2022, 01:21 PM
    So last time, we talked about how the general distrust towards the American federal government comes from its security agencies doing some shady things across history- and while TFV may not be one of those ('cause VAMPIRES), VASCU, undoubtly, is. After all, VASCU is outright stated to be a product of MKUltra, where the CIA have performed illegal human experiments- and in the case of VASCU, those experiments ended up awakening the psychic abilities (or at least, something very similar to them) at those subjected to the treatment now known as the Wintergreen Process. Considering their origin, VASCU really makes sense as a Core Conspiracy- like the original six, it too have its roots in real life conspiracy theories, about forbidden attempts by the American Governments to unlock psychic abilities, clairvoyant spies and police consultants, and other such tropes that fall right into VASCU's alley- at least, they fell. Before they became private.

    Now, this is, I believe, one of the most controversial parts about 2e- never mind the whole Infernal Dread Power mess or how the book tells you "those are not the monsters from other lines" while other parts do not agree with it, or the AKD and the Ashwood Abbey getting the short end of the stick and getting replaced by new organizations while they are left only with short writeups, or even the whole thing about adding Bygones to the setting without actually giving any example for them. Even the Barrette Commission getting shafted and becoming part of TFV didn't got as much attention as this- VASCU, the once federal agency meant to protect the world from the horror of supernatural murderers, has now became a private agency, PIs and mercenaries meant to capture supernatural criminals around the globe and bring them to justice worldwide. Something which... got mixed feelings.

    Now, one of the arguments against the change was screaming THIS IS METAPLOT at this change- and this is not entirely wrong. Sure, metaplot got some very bad rep from WoD and CofD didn't really do metaplot (except for some very strange case- Tokyo, for example, was meant to be a metaplot experiment), but this is also not exactly what Hunter did. Sure, it did provided plot points to connect between changes in the organizations as we knew them to their current forms, but that's more of a byproduct and a comfortable design choice. The main point that 2e wanted to deliver was that in the world of Hunter, nothing is constant. Everything is on the brink of change- this is very clear in the splat writeups, which work really hard to show the level of instability and fear of compromise in all organizations. Change is a running theme in the book, but some people just chose strange hills to die on- VASCU was such a example, but another, lesser known example was how the Lucifuge had a change from their Endowment from being able to pay the lethal damage price from others to only themselves- something which was reverted in the final draft to "this was always the case". Which is weird, considering that the MM have the same section about how their Endowments changed their nature through the centuries. I really think that the fandom simply latches on changes only if they can make memes out of them- MM Benedictions? Not a meme. Self Cutting Lucifuge? meme. Null Mysteriis have a strange leadership crises based on C&C? Not a meme. nuVASCU? meme. Heck, EVIL METAPLOT is by itself a meme, so yeah this whole mess does make sense- people love memes, and once a good one catches on it becomes really hard to stop it.

    Now, unlike certain changes which happened between the preview and final book, VASCU remained unchanged- something which made a lot of VASCU fans uncomfortable because unlike the Lucifuge, the "meme value" of the so called nuVASCU was only a part of the dislike towards the change. The core of the dislike was that people liked the Psychic Police that VASCU had. I mean, really, even compared to other conspiracies, VASCU was relatively innocent- there was little to work with in terms of strange or dark dealings. I mean, sure, there was the Lansing Facility were monsters were contained and the strange thing about the Hall of Mirrors, but other than that... yeah, nothing much. The Director of Operations got some attention for working all the time- something which people decided to take as literal and somehow have made a extrapolation that he was a God Machine angel in disguise (which is impressive considering it was before the GMC), but the fact was that the conspiracy was still without too many dark aspects, and they were severely hindered by having to actually find evidence and put monsters into trial like normal citizens instead of just shooting them in the darkness- something which no other conspiracy had to do, and this concept of hunting monste4rs using law abiding strategies was what most of VASCU's fans found interesting.

    2e changed it- a lot of what made VASCU, well, VASCU, was changed in the new writeup. While VASCU were stated to still follow the rules of the countries in which they operate, and still searching to collect evidence in order to put supernatural murderers on trial, the lose of Lansing and them being beholden to the federal law have robbed a lot of their fans the wrong way. The Director, the only point of something being potentially strange in VASCU, was quickly resolved as "another quick change in leadership" before the FBI dismantled the organization- when a anonymous benefactor came and saved the organization. Dr. Wintergreen was mentioned as a plot point, which was obvious- but the tension between VASCU and the FBI has very much escalated- while the FBI may hire them as consultants, the fact that they are stated as "mercenaries" in the writeup gave people the impression that now they are just another conspiracy that kills monsters, as while slashers and other "human" murderers are put on trial, it is stated that in general "monsters don't count"- which means that things like putting vampires and werewolves in jail is no longer part of VASCU's current form. While the new version did allowed a much easier way to implement VASCU all around the world, unlike the former version which was very much USA locked (with some exception of working through the UK's branch of the Society of 12 Keys), it looked like the common fan would have preferred VASCU being a part of the Interpol than a private organization. While I personally don't have much of a issue of VASCU being private, there is indeed some lose with them no longer trying to bring monsters to trial- even though I do appreciate that it is much easier to implement them outside of the US, considering how local is the FBI (and no, the Society of 12 Keys does not grant them same international freedom that other conspiracies has- it helps them to work in the UK, sure, but nothing like the ability of the MM, Cheiron or TFV to deploy hunters around the globe).

    Still, whether you like it or not, this is the current form of VASCU- and for this thread, however, is gives some very interesting perspective. We know what VASCU once were all about and what they currently are, and by tying the threads which connect the two together we can, to a certain degree, get what truly makes VASCU what it is, and what truly is part of the agenda behind it- and what was simply a artifact of them being a governmental agency. After all, Lansing and Suicide Squads are a cool concept which their previous incarnation provided- but now that they were removed from the FBI it is likely that those things are still around- probably under the control of VALKYRIE or some other agency and/or cult. However, VASCU still tries to bring slashers to trial- but they don't put the same effort with other, more horrifying monsters (like honestly, putting a vampire in jail sounds like a much greater headache than it's worth). The same also applies to the Director of Operations- they may have done everything to keep the conspiracy running by themselves, which indeed caught some attention, but the fact they were removed like a GoT character makes you think that perhaps they were not as important as you thought. In the end, the common themes which remained are tracking down killers, psychic abilities, Dr. Wintergreen and whatever weird thing is going with the Hall of Mirrors.

    Or is it?

    Now, the thing that we need to remember that while the "Director is a Angel" theory came long before we knew anything about the God Machine angels, there is indeed some resemblance between VASCU and the God Machine. The development of psychic abilities, the Cold War and human experimentation in leadership sounds A LOT like something which the God Machine will do, and suddenly dismantling a organization which is considered to be a asset because they were "too successful" and "not modern enough" and them being "relic of the Cold War". I mean, really, FBI? Really? They were so good that you started to distrust them because they used psychic abilities and not modern data analysis? That's the same level of bull**** that one of my players in my D&D game said when he refused resurrection ("I don't want to be revived because I'm afraid that if I'll move on I won't be able to taste anything". You find the sense here? No? Great, because there isn't). Now, this could be easily explained with the standard "interdepartmentional rivalry", and while it could make some sense, loosing the asset of the Wintergreen Process and their psychic abilities sounds like something very stupid to do. Again, humans are stupid- abut I really think that there is something else between the lines, something which is far, far stranger...

    The Hall of Mirrors.

    Now, we all know that the Hall of Mirrors is strange- as some have pointed out, the "only strange" point in VASCU. You know, besides Lansing, psychic abilities and all that jazz. This was seen as the only factor which shows that VASCU are not in control- that there is some secret behind the scenes, even though what exactly its meaning beyond being in a shared astral world which connects all VASCU hunters was unclear. A hint for this part, however, came during Dark Eras- when in Beneath the Skin, the Skinthief/Demon Crossover Aztec setting, we got a God Machine Cult dedicated for Huitzilopochtli which had a very, very similar ability- the power to move their physical existence into a place between dimensions, through which they can see the world like a "hall of mirrors". A cult which was very dedicated for understanding the cycles and foresee the future, gather information and analyze the calendar. A cult which, when you reach a certain level, allows you to develop psychic abilities. A cult which sounds somewhat like VASCU.

    Got you again! You thought that VASCU were only around 1890 at best if you include the Society of 12 Keys. Of course their true origin is a Aztec God Machine cult. Have you noticed what is going on those threads?

    Now, by taking the fact that VASCU may be related to the God Machine, some things do start to make sense- for example, let's say that VASCU was indeed shepherded by a angel, with the Director of Operations being such an angel. What if that angel were to Fall? That would mean some very bad news for VASCU- as once the project is compromised, the God Machine may decide to remove that whole organization in order to "minimize damage". After it, it probably doesn't know what made the angel to fall, and it could very well be that it was related to something in VASCU.. or perhaps in the Wintergreen Process? Something made the God Machine to view VASCU from a asset to a threat, and as such it worked its bureaucratic mechanisms in order to get rid of that liability. That should have been the end of VASCU- but it wasn't someone unknown managed to save the conspiracy. Someone with strong ties to it, apparently- someone who invested a lot of time and effort in those agents, and didn't want them gone, not yet.

    The Director of Operations.

    Here is the thing- I think that the Director being removed as a afterthought in the writeup is meant as a misdirection. We are supposed to think that after all of those theories, they weren't actually important- but then, the Director would make the most sense as the person who would keep VASCU going after being dismantled. Was he the Fallen Angel who was the reason that the conspiracy was erased? Is he a new demon which supports the conspiracy? Or is he just a Exile- a angel which keeps doing what it knows because the God Machine removed VASCU without updating their protocols? All options could work- but it really feels like the Director was a angel turned demon, and that this Fall was what motivated the God Machine to try to remove VASCU- which is weird, because in the end, the God Machine have only did half of the work. VASCU is still around, under some demon/Exile, still doing some biological research geared towards the awakening of psychic abilities and still putting slashers in jail. They weren't completely destroyed- yet if they are indeed a threat to the God Machine's calculations, then why are they still around?

    The writeup suggests it is Dr. Wintergreen.

    Now, originally I thought that the mysterious patron of the conspiracy may be Wintergreen herself- and it may be true. Yet I think that the Director makes more sense, with Wintergreen being the Red Herring. However, we do know that Wintergreen is still around, and that she vanished mysteriously after she was filling unheard. Now, it is very, very likely that Wintergreen wasn't very young when she helped creating the process when awakens psychic abilities in agents- so now she is probably at her 50's at best, probably older. While she may have some developments in the research, it seems strange that the FBI would keep VASCU behind just in order to capture a old doctor which lived underground for years. Heck, it is even stranger that a conspiracy of some among the best investigators in the world couldn't have found Wintergreen after all this time. When you think about it, Wintergreen is a strangely appropriate name- not a common surname as far as I can tell. Actually, I haven't found references for people having such a surname. What I did found was that Wintergreen is a family of plants known to be made into medicinal tea and oil- something which makes sense for a doctor specialized in using chemical to unlock the mysteries of the mind. Is it a case of someone following the meaning of their own name?

    Or is it just a Cover?

    Now, let's see what we can make out of those sharp shards, shall we?

    We have a ancient Aztec cult which, after coming in contact with the God Machine, have gained access to a strange liminal place known as the Hall of Mirrors- and it should be noted that the God Machine does seem to have other examples of messing up with the "mirror world". That cult gets wrecked with the fall of the Aztecs, but its secrets survive. Centuries later, the Society of 12 Keys is being formed in the UK, managing to stop Jack the Ripper's cult of personality (well, partially- the Abbey is still around after all, but at least those parts which were favorable of his actions. More of a proto Hunt Club than anything else). Let's fast forward to the Cold War, and you have the American Society being melded into the RCU, getting wrecked tragically by a slasher cannibal family and the remnants getting moved to Project MKUltra. Now things become interesting- as suddenly, a genius doctor called Barbara Wintergreen comes to the notice of the FBI and helps producing the Wintergreen Process- yet she objects of using it so quickly. She vanish during October 1978, a time in which there was a partial solar eclipse- and we need to remember that (a) the original cult was a sun cult and (b) that the Hall of Mirrors is an actual place into which cultists once vanished without trace. Along this whole process, we have the Director of Operations pushing towards VASCU being operational, yet getting replaced somewhat repeatedly, until the last one just doing everything in order to keep the conspiracy on the field. That's not enough, the FBI dismantles the organization, and someone anonymous comes and basically buys the conspiracy to keep it going under a private name. That's all known.

    Let's add the God Machine into the history and see what we get.

    Let's say, for a second that Wintergreen was a angel, which had a simple mission- create a regulation process to eliminate unwanted chaos factor. Now, this is important because slashers are a walking and talking chaos factor- they kill people who should not be killed, disrupting the calculation of the God Machine. However, eliminating them entirely is not efficient- the God Machine finds it more efficient to put a leash on those killing machine than just killing them, which means that the angel has a mission of creating a mechanism meant to control slasher activity instead of outright stopping it. That comes by unearthing the ability of the human brain to develop information processing capabilities by relating it to the obscure Hall of Mirrors, a strange realm which allows the mind to connect to all threads of information. Wintergreen provides the Process to the Director, and VASCU starts going- but it moves too quickly for Wintergreen's opinion. Perhaps she is a obsessive angel which wants the Process to be perfected. Perhaps she is upset with the God Machine moving against her recommendation, taking over her project before it is finished. It could be that she grew attached to VASCU's agents and actually cares for them. It could be something in the Process which increase the likelihood of angels exposed to the Hall of Mirrors to Fall. Whatever it may be, Wintergreen Falls and by using the solar eclipse's interaction with ancient Infrastructures leaves to the Hall of Mirrors to hide from reality. Meanwhile, a new angel takes over, replacing the Director of Operations- yet for whatever reason angels supervising VASCU keeps on falling. After a while, the Process starts to be viewed more as liability than as a asset, and when the FBI develops methods which can replace VASCU the conspiracy is viewed as unnecessary and is being dismantled. However, the last Director is very, very attached to the conspiracy, and as such decided to keep it going using the guise of his Cover. Now that VASCU is operational yet outside of the God Machine's control, it becomes a asset once again- it can stop slashers when needed without the danger of making angels to Fall, as they don't need a angel supervisor anymore. Is it a gambit? Yes, but one which the God Machine can tolerate. The demon may try to weaponize the God Machine against the slashers it wants to use, of course, but as the God Machine monitors VASCU it can keep an eye on that demon, use VASCU as needed- and send his own slashers when they do too many problems. And Wintergreen? Now, that's a demon it would like to find and tear apart.

    On the other hand, you may feel that there are too many demons in this scenario- and you may be right. The solution is very simply- make Wintergreen human. Actually nothing in the process demands that Wintergreen will be a demon- and as she is a character with much more depth than the Directors, it would make sense that if someone is human, it is her. Nothing really changes by having her a human- only that she uncovered the secrets of the Aztec on her own (with perhaps some help from the Director pointing her in the right direction and potentially stigmatize her), when it was the mission of the original Director to create the said mechanism instead of Wintergreen's. Now, this all work nice and well until the God Machine the God Machine starts seeing Wintergreen's objections as a liability. Wintergreen, who got tired of being ignored and VASCU moving against her recommendations, senses the threat of getting "removed" by the God Machine due to her connection to the Hall of Mirrors. She used the solar eclipse to escape to the Hall, from which she watches upon the conspiracy- and perhaps becoming the catalyst which makes the Directors to malfunction and Fall. This is something that the God Machine is willing to tolerate to a certain point- VASCU's control over the chaos factor of slashers compensate on the angelic Fall rate caused by it, until the point where the regular FBI tech is able to replace them. At that point, it is time to eliminate VASCU- but again, the latest Director (which managed to flee) comes into contact with Wintergreen which warns them, and together they help turning VASCU private. Again, this way VASCU's benefits once again compensate for the risks, even though the God Machine now use its controlled slashers in order to "regulate the regulation". VASCU are now more free than before- but the God Machine still wants to put an end to Wintergreen, even though it can't find her as long as she hides within the Hall.

    Now, trapped between a God which ahs forsaken them and a demon which can't let go of them, VASCU need to try and work towards their goal- a world without murder. Far more free than before, VASCU are found exactly where Wintergreen would have wanted them, a group no longer trapped in the mechanisms of the God Machine, and where the demon is very much influenced by her. She works through the Hall of Mirrors, constantly watching and following the threads of information which help VASCU to regulate slasher activity- and now that there is a slasher epidemic, something which Wintergreen probably blames the God Machine for, their work is needed more than ever. The thing is that the Hall of Mirrors is not only the liminal places between spaces, it is also the liminal place between everything. All information flows through the Hall, allowing Wintergreen to pull the strings and help the conspiracy end the concept of murder in the world. That could be done, ironically, by connecting slashers to the same Hall- by connecting all world into the Hall. That's important because as slashers lack any soul, they are naturally found outside of the Astral's collective might- but the Hall is different, as it does not requires souls, only connections. Wire the whole world through self reflections and eliminate the concept of murder from the world- or at least, put a leash upon murderers- and that's it. Game over. That's of course something that the God Machine would very much dislike- murder is a method which it finds very efficient for many cases, and as such it would invest all it can in getting Wintergreen out of the Hall and take over VASCU once more, but simply destroying VASCU will not stop Wintergreen. She would still be in the Hall, using her own agency of demons which fell to her whispers to rebuild the organization once more. Let justice be done even though the sky may fall, as they say. The only thing she needs to do to achieve her goal is complete the pieces, connect the world, and use the power of the Sun as a catalyst.

    A Black Sun's Map, if you'll will.

    And that's it! We finished all Core Eight. Hope you enjoyed that ride, and now we can move to the next stage- supplementary conspiracies.
