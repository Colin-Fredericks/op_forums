                                            Blood on the Border

                                            p.252

                                            Ciudad Juárez, Mexico, is a city of industry with 1.5 million residents and hundreds of maquiladoras pumping out goods for the vast market of the United States just across the Rio Bravo (called Rio Grande by the gringos)
                                            "Maquiladoras"? I'm all for conveying local color, and using the correct vocabulary, but "maquiladoras" is definitely not part of English yet. Either it needs to be defined in the text (like they did with Rio Bravo/Grande), or in a footnote.

                                            (Even after looking up the term, I'm not clear on whether they're "legit" factories, sweat shops, or something worse)

                                            A city of dark mystery, with thousands disappeared and killed by unknown assailants.
                                            While I've seen "disappeared" used to describe quietly killing someone, then hiding the body, it disrupts the flow here. Probably the best fix would be a rewrite, perhaps along the lines of

                                            "A city of dark mystery, with thousands "disappeared" or outright killed by unknown assailants."

                                            or

                                            "A city of dark mystery, where thousands disappear or are killed by unknown assailants."

                                            A city of violence, temporarily quiescent as the government abandons its war on the cartels and the victors lick their wounds
                                            Might want a comma after "cartels".

                                            Juárez holds both promise and danger for Beasts as well.
                                            Not wild about the "as well". Switching back from italics provides a natural break in the flow of the text, rendering "as well" almost dangling. I suggest cutting it, or adding a subordinate clause at the beginning to provide a stronger connection to the last line.

                                            On the one hand, it seems to offer a vast smorgasbord of opportunities to satisfy their Hungers with little chance of being caught.
                                            I suggest dropping "seems to", particularly since you're presenting this as the "good" side of relocating to the city, counterbalancing all the competition (and dangers).

                                            The city is rife with property crime, providing many opportunities for Collectors and the other Hungers
                                            'Collectors" and "the other Hungers" seem mismatched.

                                            and a pair of twin Heroes are turning the tables on Beasts lured to the city.
                                            This is nitpicky, but "are turning" is kind of passive, particularly for a threat. If you have a moment, consider going with something a bit more dynamic, a la "twin Heroes already stalk the city", or something along those lines.

                                            Although the area around Ciudad Juárez has been inhabited for thousands of years by bands of hunter-gatherers and later by corn-growing agriculturalists, the history of the city itself began in 1659, when Mission Nuestra Seora de Guadalupe was established by the Franciscan order to proselytize to the local native population.
                                            Borderline run-on sentence. You need a comma after "hunter-gatherers", at least. And I strongly recommend breaking it into two sentences, with a period after "1659". One option would be:

                                            "Although the area around Ciudad Juárez has been inhabited for thousands of years by bands of hunter-gatherers, and later by corn-growing agriculturalists, the history of the city itself began in 1659. That year, Franciscan monks established Mission Nuestra Seora de Guadalupe to proselytize to the local native population."

                                            Aside from a few ambiguous petroglyphs, the first evidence of Beasts in Paso del Norte as Juárez was then known dates from this era. A written account of a Spanish brood headed toward Santa Fe to investigate reports of vampires among the Pueblo peoples mentions passing through the town in 1678. The Pueblo Revolt of 1680 then sent colonial and pro-Spanish Indian refugees flooding into the outpost.
                                            As the supernatural history of the area, this should probably be its own paragraph, rather than fused with the early (mundane) history.

                                            Also not wild about the "headed toward Santa Fe to investigate reports of vampires among the Pueblo peoples " bit. Its an interesting tidbit, but its almost a separate thought shoehorned into the sentence. Consider cutting it, or using parenthesis or hyphens to set it off.

                                            Paso del Norte passed through the Mexican War of Independence of the early 19th century relatively unscathed, but shifts in the river had a lasting impact, putting several local communities on the north side of the Rio Bravo’s new course.
                                            Not wild about "of the early 19th century". First, "century" should be capitalized. Second . . . it makes it sound like there have been multiple Mexican Wars of Independence.

                                            The Texas Revolution too had little effect on Juárez, despite the new Republic’s claim of the Rio Bravo as its southern border.
                                            Need commas around "too
