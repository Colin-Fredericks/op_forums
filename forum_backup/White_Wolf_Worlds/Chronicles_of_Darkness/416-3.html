                    I don't see why Lunacy needs to be removed, on the opposite side of that coin. Just because in modern times people do not believe in such things and dismiss anyone who says otherwise, does not mean it has always been the case. While a vampire can ghoul the right people, has a bent towards slavery and mind control, werewolves have more physical bents and influence with spirits.                                     Clarification's sake does push me to say, I want them adjusted into merits for Renown vs their current iteration and mechanics. The very notion I don't take issue with; the current implementation I do. Lunacy though? No. I really find it both overblown and detrimental. I honestly believe a Status-like, or Mantle-like (or lets say, GMC Cult-like) merit fits the bill a bit more effectively, and it certainly doesn't require a full game rewrite, but honestly? I'm already doing a heavy overhaul with my Co-ST. I'm not going to short-change a game's potential because it'd be more work and may require some drastic shifts. Not going far enough was the precise problem W:tF and V:tR had in it's former iteration; I'd be heavily disappointed if they dialed things back and didn't take heart from the Chronicler's Guide and go a bit wild with the ideas they've tinkered with.

                                    For the record Rahuman, some of what you'd like to see I do agree with. I've been going with the notion that buffing the Gauru form's accelerated healing to cover 1 L a round makes it worth while to take in other situations (and makes those in prolonged frenzies scarier) without the need of Essence expenditure. Especially when it comes to a blow that might knock them unconscious, the prior roll to see if they Death Rage has a bit more of a survival-based oomph to do that even if you're lacking Essence. That and it makes Death Rage a more dangerous proposition; not only for those in your way, but those around you in general; taking you down by physical force hoping to "tire you out of the form" is rather difficult when you're raging, healing 1L or 1B a turn, getting back up right pissed off and still having some Essence to spare for other things.

                                    Given many of the powers we're working on for Instincts are Reflexive... it means Gauru form most certainly has an arsenal of abilities it can use and that's more of what I wanted to see on my end in general. When you talk, Rahuman about buffing of Gifts, and cleaning them up, I'm in full agreement there, it's why many of the Instincts I've been tinkering with emphatically -can- be used in Gauru form, and in Death Rage (for good or ill). I'm going rather divergent there, some of the Instincts are revisions to old Gifts while others have no correlation. "Gifts" are being shifted into what you gain primarily from hunting down and consuming spirits; converted Numina, as well as the occasional blessings one gets from Totems and spirit pacts (which have their own pitfalls that makes hunting and consuming spirits the more common Forsaken approach).

                                    For me it's been far less the forms being problematic (though I've done something perhaps drastic in the eyes of others with Dalu and Urshul) in their stats and capabilities, and more of being hamstringed in what you can do in some of them. I'm still iffy on 9-agains (let alone 8-agains) in that form; that's more something I'd prefer to see as part of a Death Rage/Gauru Form fighting style, taking to heart we're getting a frenzy based one for the vampires, I think it's been -long- in coming that there are some fighting styles that give you options for these in combat. Same with the highest of Wits or Dex; with GMC Defense I don't think it's necessary at all save perhaps as part of a Combat Merit rather than an innate.

                                    What I do want to see shifted overall though, is Gauru form's utility in hunting; right now it's a bit iffy in how it's presented (despite a nice sensory bonus), especially with Death Rage's description. I'm not a fan of frenzy states that make you "stupid"; with the change to Vampires, I think that's a much cleaner way of presenting it. Focused, vicious targeted acts that can spin even more wildly out of control when things go sour in the pursuit. I'm probably not articulating this too clearly, but the basic idea I want is that Death Rage is the hunter killer instinct gone out of control, rather than say, being a spinning spazzing Taz in presentation that sometimes comes to mind. Other individuals most certainly can run afoul of it or make themselves targets (and things going more protracted and sour as a result from provocations), but it's more of a description shift if that makes sense. I see adopting Gauru form and Death Rage as much a survival instinct as a bloody killing juggernaut, I'd like to see a bit more tweaking to emphasize that more than portion we currently have.

                                    With that in mind... I've introduced a rule where Werewolves with 0 Essence that enter Death Rage -will- attack and consume others that are viable for Essence some time ago (though they may make attempts to steer it's course some; nobody wants to eat a packmate, and yep... I do put other werewolves as viable sources in opposition to the current rules). Being spiritually 'hungry' is a dangerous, dangerous state for temptation alone; spirits just don't appear on hand to keep you fed, they're tough and tenacious compared to the much more ubiquitous humans and we heavily revised Loci so they no longer act as essence batteries, which means you get Essence from hunting and eating, not much else. Raging on an empty spiritual belly is like a spirit -desperate- for any Essence it can get; it's going to get ugly. Toss in a system where addiction to human/wolf flesh is quite possible (similar to Vitae Addiction in mechanical approach), and sometimes you get those classic werewolves that just can't help themselves, made worse if they Death Rage. They're dangerous to those around them. They run the risk of becoming more dangerous, or falling into the classic werewolf man-eater trope more readily than the after-thought it is now.

                                    That's something I -really- want to see. Essence farmers can go into the recycle bin. If you Death Rage, people are quite possibly going to get eaten, and you're going to be left with the mess on every front left after. And yes; I go by the notion that you do get some Werewolves out there who specifically hunt, kill and eat other werewolves, going looney tunes into their spirit-nature and the whole "like eats like" they have going. Werewolves banding together as a pack is more than just instinct; it's survival. The Pure, rogue 'man-eaters' and low-harmony cannibals, spirits and more are all out there. You're on the menu as a new werewolf. Band together to survive, because you're going to have to be one cunning, tenacious and vicious fuck to survive on your own, and odds are without the help of the pack, you're going to do some ugly things to get Essence.

                                    I don't need a mind-wiping, debuffing built in power of doom to reflect a common sense thing of "Oh shit, I better run, it's big, it's huge and it's coming right for me." The only thing I've found it adds is 1) less concern for the Veil, less precautions and less thought in general on the part of PCs and NPCs regarding taking alternate forms and it (aka laziness), and 2) making human antagonists unless their build with ridiculously high Composure and Resolve ratings pretty damn irrelevant. I got utterly tired of it being a built in consequence dodge. It hasn't added anything for me, at all. Roses suggestion on the werewolf Predatory making others feel like prey (and in turn inflicting a condition on somewhat that reinforced that, potentially with options including madness for another iteration, or the whole animal magnetism thing as the third) works for me. It requires active effort. Lunacy as is is an afterthought.

                                    I've used it for a while (and that's changing in the next two months in my game) using the rolled system in War Against the Pure for the alternate shape shifter equivalent as a place mat for the last few years, and it's -helped- and I find it better, but I still find that a switch to a more targeted Predatory Aura (and definitely cranking it down in overall potency or group affecting capacity) would do best. Something where you could separate the weak and scared from the 'herd' from a brief panic, put them in general more to flight than fight etc. rather than a passive "You're just fucked if you're anyone but the most exceptional of people." So I suppose it's less "Lunacy is gone" and more "what it does and how it's implemented is shifted away from it's current incarnation".

                                    Also, apologies for the massive posts, doubly so if they seem confrontational at all, I'm addressing a lot of things at once, some which haven't came up in this discussion (yet, but inevitably do). Quite a bit of what I want to see changed is due to many prior experiences reinforcing something I found just didn't work for me for a host of factors. I like to get everything out of my head before I get distracted and forget it. Hopefully most of that made sense. Obviously I'm going to have some very different ideas and wants from Idigam chronicles than some folks, but man... if you've got the opportunity to overhaul a game, nothing bugs me more than tip-toeing and not going wild with it. It's a new edition; it shouldn't be held back due to prior supplements. I can safely say I've liked Claire's ideas consistently however, so here's hoping we see more.

                                        Sometimes I think that Werewolf would have been, or make more use of, the subtitle "The Legendary." Why? Admittedly this is more of my interpretation of Renown than anything, but it does seem like Renown is a formal codification of a werewolf's personal legend among the spirits. A werewolf preforms feats of Cunning, why he must be Clever...and when he spirits recognize that, he becomes supernaturally clever and is able to apply that to his powers or whatever use the new system makes of Renown. After all, werewolves have spiritual qualities, and spirits can be interpreted to be essentially living meanings.

                                        Basically, werewolves are living legends and Renown is what they're legendary for. Now, the thing about legends is that they're exaggerations as well, lies that have become folk-truths. This could play into what Talum was talking about earlier on how werewolves should fear the mobs and the emphases on Pack vs Lone wolves, and how that silly "Legendary" Idea could apply.

                                        Basically, a werewolf would have to decide who's legend to promote. Should a Werewolf sacrifice the potential of his deeds to the pack, take up Gifts or whatever that makes the pack's legend as a whole stronger? He is personally weaker and is in more danger if caught by his enemies alone, but his pack as a whole benefits and they are more potent as a result. Or should a werewolf be selfish, and advance only his own legend and become the monster he has played himself up as? His pack, if any, would resent him, but he would mutate to become the played up super strong monster that lurks in many posters mind as the "ideal werewolf". Hell, this divide could also play into the Tribes with the Ivory Claws and Storm Lords preferring the personal approach, Hunters in Darkness and Fire Touched prefering the pack, and so on.

                                        TLDR, I suppose the point of this rambling is that I'd like to see Renown being played up as personal significance for the PC, and for Werewolf1.9 (2.9?) to play up the choice between the self or the pack. Focus too much on the pack, and you'll all be vulnerable to being split up and picked off. Focus too much on yourself, and you'll be alone when the mob comes to deal with you.

                                        ANYWAY, moving on from this pointless rambling, I do hope the werewolf writers have played a little of Magic: the Gathering's Innistrad block. The werewolves there were pretty rad and fun, and I think a good example of how I hope the Idigam Werewolvs should be: Tough, certainly capable of tearing shit up, but vulnerable without some support from each other or spells and still have the need to be cautious of their enemies.

                                                            Regarding Renown. I like it, although I am aware there are some mechanical issues but Renown is not a stat that is as broken as it seems some people think it is.
