    I think the differences are far less fundamental than that. The Orders have much more in common with each other than they do anyone else. They're both trying to build a better Awakened society, they're both concerned with the treatment of Sleepers, they both want to spread access and awareness of magic and the Supernal in a way that make the other Orders nervous, they both strongly oppose the Seers. They differ on tactics and philosophy for achieving the same ends, but their practical objectives are generally aligned. When push comes to shove they're much more likely to support each other than not, especially in moments of actual crisis. And then go back to bitterly arguing philosophy over which approach is actually better once the moment has passed.

                                                        I think the key problem is that this...

                                                        Originally posted by WHW View Post
                                                        If you decide to stop paying taxes, your government will put you into jail, and you may not decide to stop being a citizen and still live there.
                                                        Consilium will tax you and expect you to obey it's law...but it's up to you if you want to subscribe. You join wanting benefits, and in exchange you are expected to follow Consiliums guidance.
                                                        ... is way too modern in its mode of thought.

                                                        Not all governments work that way or have worked that way. The Consilium is not a modern government. It's an ancient thing; perhaps as old as agriculture. The Assembly is descended from this system, in the same way modern parliaments are ultimately descended from things and folkmoots.

                                                        The Consilium is more like a... Well, like a folkmoot, really; you even have dueling (holmgang) as a means of dispute resolution. These sorts of gatherings were more democratic than Consilia are, but ultimately the principle is the same: If you're a part of a community, you attend the folkmoots, have a say in the law, and, if you have a dispute with a fellow, the moot resolves it through a legal channel.

                                                        If you're not part of that community, though, you're an outlaw. That means that the law doesn't bind you, but nor does it protect you, and others are free to do to you as they see fit.

                                                        That's how most small communities have had their law function pretty much since forever. That's how governments of that nature have functioned, often with a chieftain or king being responsible for organizing and holding (and, at times, enforcing) the law.

                                                        Consilia are similar. They exist as a dispute resolution mechanism for members but largely ignore outsiders. They will get involved for certain crimes and offenses - letting a murderer wander free is bad form - but otherwise they don't really care to get involved.

                                                        Hence, I don't really see why a Consilia isn't a government. It's just not a modern one.
