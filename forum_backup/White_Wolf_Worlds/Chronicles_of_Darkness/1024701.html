    Sadism, Psychopathy and parochial altruism

    12-31-2016, 06:56 AM
    God day every one! It's again me with ideas of questionable quality.
    At the down of new year i will star steampank chronicle on homebrew lore but with CofD mechanics. And players already present to me their mostly evilish characters. This led me to question, can characters resist breaking point not because of their will (hight enough Composure+Resolve), not because of circumstance (selfdefense or achieving virtue), but because of their physiology? Here I propose to discuss in an amateur manner mental disorders and their effects on the mechanics. And i am not read yet Beast The Primordial.
    IMO, Integrity perfectly represent phycology of normal people (in game terms). But sadists and psychopaths not exactly mentally healthy people. But they can behave. And they can safer. Their disorder lay within empathy and social interaction.
    Most inborn psychopaths can have high Integrity (unlike normal people with low Integrity with obtained psychopathy). They can reflect their action. And understand where they action place mistreat. They can analyze emotions, but cant understand them. There they don't give a fuck for harming people or be harmed by others.
    With sadists matter more complicated. They can understand emotions but they retrieve joy for harming others. Even when they understand that's amoral. And they will continue to do this as long as it does not put them at risk. There we say "just use original rules!". But sadist does not safer degenerative condition from Integrity loss. They don't become more insane, they become more corrupt (from their addiction to harm).
    And third in row but not last in meaning group - We Are. In our common nature embedded behavior of group rivalry. In social psychology this phenomenon is called parochial altruism. In short terms we more love who include in "our" group and hate the most who disclude "our" group. We fill less empathy for those with whom we associate less ourselves. And safer the most when see how our most loved has been harmed.

    And there three merits i can propose.

    SADIST (** or ***)
    Prerequisites: Only on character creation, Resolve *** (sadist should have nerves for reaching his desire), Vice related to harming people
    Effect: on two dot version character does not safer from degenerative condition from Integrity loss. On three dot version selfdefens action grant autosuccess on breaking point.
    Drawback: character obtain Addicted Condition (Vice) with all the attendant.

    Psychopath (*****).
    Prerequisites: Only on character creation, Composure *** (psychopath should not give a fuck for anything)
    Effect: character never safer breaking point from social interaction (harming, killing, abuse - whatever what include interaction with humans)
    Drawback: every fail on empathy considered as dramatic fail.

    Third merit goes well with hate group system (from Hurt Locker). It's perfectly show how behave people with such ideology... But does not grant anything for saving your integrity.

    Philosophy (Style * to let us come up with more!)
    Prerequisites: Status * or more in any hate group, Composure * or ** (a person should not be able to resist the influence of the crowd to him), Wits * or ** (should not be aware of his misbehavior).
    * Our / Alien: for each dot in merit character get bonus on Integrity roll when harming an alien person and penalize Integrity rall when harm our person
    *** Holy War!: when engaging in combat where obviously known enemy affiliation character can spend willpower to gain automatic success on each breaking point within battle. Character safer only one condition for all breaking point occurred within battle.
    Drawback: Storyteller should write down (or memorize it another way) each misdid maded by character in which it was possible to escape responsibility because of this merit, and remind each one of this with maximum (for max dot in merit) penalty on Integrity loss when character lost prerequisites.

      Merits are supposed to be boons foremost, with their drawbacks being consequences of overextending yourself. Are these boons to your character? Or anybody? Really?

      Putting aside how questionable statting up stuff like this is, I'd say sadism can already be represented by tweaking existing mechanics. Take Vice and Virtue, for example. Normally, I'd say take Sadistic as a Vice and be done with it. If you go further, you can have Sadistic as a Virtue. Now, mortals are supposed to have redeeming and positive qualities as Virtues by the rules, but a little adjustment to the base template will be okay it seems you are already approaching this whole thing in the "Those people must have something fundamentally wrong with them" perspective (one that I strongly disagree with, but just rolling with it for the moment). Go further and have them be immune to the universal breaking points of inflicting harm on others. This quality need not be inherent, and can be acquired.

      For the Merit, I have no idea why sadists would have high Resolve. The Resolve Attribute represents your patience and perseverance, and the ability to think straight in stress. Maybe the sadist develops that as he continues to inflict harm on others in a steady way, but I don't believe sadists are inherently strong-willed that others. By chance, did you put that in for the "cool" and "badass" factor that gets applied to criminals so often in pop culture?

      The same line of logic above applies to the Psychopath Merit above and its Composure requirement. Composure represents your ability to keep calm in stress and emotionally adjust to changes in the environment. Even heartless killers get upset when their plans go wrong and get caught, and feel fear when faced with their own pain. And besides, "psychopathy" isn't even a singular phenomenon, unlike sadism which can be roughened up with sandpaper to be fit in anti-social behavior.

      Lastly, parochial altruism I see more apt as a series of dice modifiers on breaking point rolls that interact with group psychology rather than a Merit.

      And once again, in case I haven't made it clear, I really don't think these are suit to be represented mechanically. CofD got rid of Derangements from its 1E version for a damn good reason.

                  There are some major flaws in your understanding of sadism, which is understandable. It's not something I've seen popular media address well ever, but being a sadist doesn't mean somebody lacks empathy, or that harming other people never causes them emotional distress. It's a kink, and like any other it's situational and context dependent and varies from person to person. I'm a sadist, and in most circumstances seeing other people in pain is not something that I enjoy, quite the opposite; it bothers me a lot. It took me a long time to accept that it was a thing I wanted in any circumstance at all. The first time I started having sadistic thoughts about a friend it left me hating myself so much that I wanted to kill myself. I didn't understand how I could want to harm somebody who was so kind to me and to other people in general. The answer is I didn't, and I don't. I want to make someone suffer in the moment, in way that leaves them with good memories instead of trauma. Before I understood S&M it didn't occur to me that was possible or made any sense but it absolutely is and does. There's no more of a contradiction in that than there is in saying I enjoying moderately strenuous hikes in good weather but would be miserable doing a 13 mile barefoot slog in the pouring rain.

                  If you want mechanics to represent somebody who really isn't bothered by the suffering of others breaking points already give you pretty significant leeway to determine what bothers your character enough to roll, and where to assign modifiers. The chart in the core calls itself out as a suggestion, you can always adjust the numbers to suit your game. Beyond that, Hunter has a system for replacing normal breaking points with more character specific compulsions. It would need a little updating but the general idea should work fine.

                          Many have brought up sadism, but I am going to address psychopathy for a moment. Psychopathy is, first and foremost, a genetic quirk. Some people are born with less empathy for others and a desire for power. These people aren't killers, child molesters, or any of that. Perhaps they will become lawyers or police, or any number of other positions. They contribute to society.

                          Now, psycho killers that we are familiar with from media are another matter. They are the result of a combination of the above genetic quirk combined with (ususally childhood) trauma/abuse. In game terms, they already have low Integrity, broken people with psychological issues they have trouble dealing with. Often, killing is the result of trying to gain some control over themselves and the world around them, a way to release built up stress and frustration and gain catharsis.

                          Anyways, the point is that psychopathy, barring trauma, is little different from a normal person. Psycho killers have mental trama and broken minds, reflected by virtually no Integrity, and should have no special considerations in merit form.

                              "Deviation" is a lot more complicated than simply "normal" or "sick". There are several ways in which I'm not normal (by which I mean I fall into groups representing 2% or less of the population), but they're mostly not things that are immediately obvious. Some of them impair my daily functioning, like a learning disability that often leads me to forget important items when I leave the house to go somewhere, among other organizational difficulties. Some of them don't directly cause me problems but mean I can't intuitively understand the way the majority of people think, like being on the ace spectrum. Some of them are just weird quirks that don't seem to do anything, like growing up cis-male but discovering at 17 that I also have one partially formed ovary and thus technically fall within the intersex umbrella. Speaking of which, intersex people aren't any less capable of empathy or remorse than the general population.

                              To answer your questions about Beast and mechanics in general though: Human characters with a propensity for murder have always been represented with low integrity/morality. Slashers in first edition had Morality 4 or less, and Beast's primary antagonists typically mirror this with low integrity scores. Not because all Heroes have low integrity, but because low integrity Heroes are vastly more likely to go out killing. First edition Morality did have the idea that characters can reach a point where certain Sins no longer bother them, and you could always go that route and ignore the recommendation that murder always be a breaking point.

                              Beasts themselves handle things very differently. They lack an integrity score, and what they have in its place is Satiety, which is not subject to the same rules. Beasts can be as violent, cruel, and depraved as they wish, and doing so tends to increase their Satiety. Beast flips the sanity/morality bar concept; it rewards Beasts for being cruel by filling their hunger bar, and leaves the punishment aspect to the enemies that kind of behavior inevitably creates and attracts, and to what the players and characters are willing to live with in pursuit of a fix. All told, Beast is an excellent game to explore sadism and amorality, either as characters struggling to find healthy outlets for violent tendencies, or just as an outlet for the players themselves. I get that, sometimes you want to play terrible fictional people, because you can do all that stuff and not worry about consequences since the victims only exist in the group's imaginations.
