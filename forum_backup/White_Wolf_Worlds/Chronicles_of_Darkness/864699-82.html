        I am trying to incorporate elements from the hurt locker in my game but I have trouble understanding how tracking ammo would work, I understand what capacity is and how the new system works but when it comes to the part of acquiring reloads it says that they last for the scene they are acquired or the next, what does this mean? ammo magically disappear after it has been acquired?
        Basically, yes.
        The intention is that procuring extra reloads is something you do when you expect combat rather than having to care about longterm stockkeeping (for PCs) & bookkeeping (for players). If there's no combat, then no need to bring extra ammo.
        When it works, it provides a bit of a cinematic feel. It's the protagonists having their preparation montage before a fight. Or in a less action hero-type game, it just connects the supply run with the reason for the supply run in a good narrative fashion.
        But in a game it can be difficult to know when combat will happen, or even whether it'll be combat at all. If they stock up on ammo but then manage to talk the antagonist down or incapacitate them without using weapons, then that ammo is simply wasted. And if the PCs know they'll be attacked but not when, they either have to go shopping every other scene or be (relatively) unprepared even though they should know better.

        Quick note: It's only the extra ammo that disappears. The weapons themselves automatically refresh the ammo they can hold each scene.

            I understood it as:

            If the scene in which you’re acquiring extra reloads is the same scene in which you’re using them (ie the gunfight scene), you expect your acquired reloads to be all spent in this scene.

            If the scene in which you’re acquiring extra reloads is the scene that will be immediately followed by the scene in which you’ll actually them, you expect to keep your acquired reloads in that following gunfight scene, because, well of course so because that’s why you were resupplying yourself in the first place.

            So ultimately, extra reloads you get last for one or two scenes. Sorta like the “Power Charge” or “Focus” special moves common in JRPGs.

                            See also the Guns Don't Work That Way sidebar. The Hurt Locker system is written under the assumption that you aren't tracking ammo count and magazines individually, so counts are abstracted and you have "enough" magazines in stock, with extra reloads being "more than enough." If you and your player are tracking magazines down to the shot already, you can probably omit the Capacity system except as a rough guideline for how much burst fire depletes a magazine.

                                            Remind him that to stay sharp he needs to practice a lot, and that maintenance and accidents (like wet ammo) have costs. With that you can simply assume that he's procuring extra ammo regularly and roll with it as implied, letting him carry his four magazines.

                                            This is abstracting the game. The actual number of magazines makes little difference, and in real life you would really be subject to a number of circumstances that would make this number fluctuate. His real concern is to be over-prepared, and the rule covers that. Just assume it being used whenever you think it's adequate instead of making it a too involved process and it will run smooth with the same dramatic effect of his paranoia.

                                            After all, if you're worried enough to be sure to always be with four magazines, you're probably also always verifying them, reassuring yourself they'll be there and work properly, even using up or throwing away one you think isn't reliable anymore, or just stockpiling at home far more than four. The idea is that the same rule covers all of those, so you both have more freedom describing the form and consequences of this behavior.

                                            If at some point he tires of the constant application of the procuring rule... well, the character is probably tiring from the effort to always be with four magazines in his person, too. This is a lot, not always easy to carry, and certainly not always well received where you go.
