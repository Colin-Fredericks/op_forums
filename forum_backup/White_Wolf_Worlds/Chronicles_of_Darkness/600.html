    Blood Sorcery Style Ceremonies

    11-01-2013, 06:45 PM
    Ceremonies

    To all but the Bound death is a mystery, and even the Bound know little about what awaits a Ghost who passes on. Man fears the unknown, and to face his fear of death itself man created mythologies to explain what happens next and ceremony to give a feeling of control when faced with the inevitable. It is not magic, the ceremony is to help the friends and family find closure in the face of death itself.

    It is not magic, the ceremony is to help the friends and family find closure in the face of death itself. Any ocultist could explain why these ceremonies cannot serve as a practical form of magic, and yet they do serve. Somehow, in defiance of all occult logic the Bound can take scraps of funerary rites; those long abandoned in sands of time, those still practised, even those that never existed outside of some cheep paperback. They can blend them together without any thought to their underlying symbolism or occult theory and somehow the results will work as well as any genuine magic.

    Ceremonies is rated new dots * 6, when a Sin-Eater earns a new dot of ceremonies they get a free dot in any arcana, and learn a ceremony from the arcana they increased.

    Sidebar – Ceremonies Glossary

    Ritual Trait: Ceremonies


    Magic User: N/A

    Theme: Arcana

    Brought Ritual: Learned Ceremony

    Improvised Ritual: Ceremony

    Spell: Ceremony


    Sidebar – Ceremony Cheat Sheet
    Step One: The player chooses the Spell effects and applies additions. (OOC)

    Step Two: The Sin-Eater designs the ritual actions (IC)

    Step Three: The players and Storyteller decides if the ceremony gets any bonuses for style (OOC)

    Step Four: The Sin-Eater pays any costs and performs the ceremony (IC)

    Step Five: The ceremony takes effect.


    A Matter of Style

    Within their area of speciality, the Bound are free from most practical limits. With few genuine occult principles behind their ceremonies the Bound can usually do whatever they like. Ceremonies are a riot of artistic expression and shameless theft, Sin-Eaters' grab aspects of every funerary tradition they think is cool and mix them together to create ceremonies that are interesting and express their beliefs or style. The Bound's culture is a dynamic one, with a constant flow of new ideas, traditions, and artistic critiques.

    Within a Krewe the rules change slightly; it is expected that a Krewe will make some declarative statement about metaphysics. They will explain what they believe ghosts are, and how the magic ghosts and Sin-Eaters functions. Few Bound believe their ideas to have much in the way of factual truth, most are happy enough if they don't contradict anything obvious (that is, they don't contradict anything in the Geist: the Sin-Eaters core rulebook) and even a Krewe formed entirely of Necromancers would probably be quick to admit that their metaphysics is a work in progress.

    The real purpose of a Krewe's metaphysics (again, apart from that Krewe led by Necromancers) is not to be accurate, but to be different to the other Krewes. A Krewe's metaphysics is one of many ways that a Krewe defines itself, forges a sense of group identity, and raise a flag pole that other Krews can see and (hopefully) respect. Members of Krewe creates ceremonies that fit logically into their Metaphysics, not because they believe it's correct or because they get much practical benefits, but as a mark of belonging. A sign of affiliation.

    This tendency becomes more pronounced in larger Krewes. If there's just three members, well everyone knows you anyway. If there's thirty members, visible signs of loyalty are vital to rise in status and earn respect from your peers. As well as growing in cultural importance, a larger Krewe's metaphysics also grows in mystical importance. As a Krewe's internal culture becomes richer and more pronounced, it starts to bleed into the Krewe's channel. The metaphysics becomes real, but only for members of that Krewe. A Sin-Eater from a large Krewe which believes that Ghosts feed upon emotions will feel something supping at herself when she's around Ghosts. When she looks at mortals who spend time around ghosts she will see the signs, whether with human Empathy or a more mystical perception.

    Most Sin-Eaters believe the effects to be purely internal. It's their perceptions, their magic, which has changed. The mortal's emotions aren't actually being eaten by ghosts (but Krewe's members might). Some Sin-Eaters theorise that they may be somehow altering reality through the power of belief, their Krewe's channel or some other reason. They are looked at with suspicion by the other bound. It's a small step from thinking you can change reality to thinking you should, and no one likes hanging out with Sacrosact Sin-Eaters.


    Arcana

    Sin-Eaters know of three arcana, each of which covers different aspects of death and magic. No Sin-Eater can have more dots in an arcana than they have in ceremonies. The three arcana refer not to genuine occult classifications, but merely to practice at performing different effects. Arcana cost new dots * 5

    Oculomancy (Wits): Oculomancy can reveal information and look across vast distances. Turned upon people Oculomancy can unlock strange senses and altered states of consciousness. However Oculomancy is limited, and can only work on people, the dead, Anchors, Mementos and emotions.

    Necromancy (Manipulation) Necromancy can communicate with the dead, or control them. With Necromancy a Sin-Eater can summon or bind ghosts, raise corpses to walk again and do it's bidding, or protect the living by banishing the hungry dead. Necromancy can even work with death itself.

    Entropomancy (Dexterity) In time all things die, all objects wear away. Entropomancy can be used to destroy or curse, weathering people and things away under the ravages of time, or raw deathly energies.


    When a Ceremony uses multiple Arcana use the lowest Attribute to determine the dicepool.

    Improvised and Learned Ceremonies

    Sin-Eaters don't need to sit down and carefully plan out a ceremony. With their innate connection to the Underworld and the frankly flexible nature of ceremony they can usually improvise something. Yet it is often more convenient to memorise a useful ceremony. Learning a new ceremony costs dots * 2, or just dots if the Sin-Eater has a teacher or a written description of a ceremony.

    Learning a new ceremony that does the exact same thing as a ceremony the Sin-Eater already knows (probably because the old one has gone out of style) only costs 1 experience point, if the Sin-Eater has a teacher or written instructions than it is free.

    Step One: Rhythm

    Before the Sin-Eater can perform a ceremony the player must first determine the effects they wish to produce, which Arcana it falls under and choose which additions will apply.

    Area of Effect

    Area of Effect is used for ceremony that have power over a circular region around a specified spot or a cubic volume.

    Radius | Defined Volume | Target Number
    1-yard radius | 5 cubic yards | None (basic success)
    2-yard radius | 10 cubic yards | +1 success
    4-yard radius | 20 cubic yards | +2 successes
    8-yard radius | 40 cubic yards | +3 successes
    16-yard radius| 80 cubic yards | +4 successes*
    end{tabular}

    * Affecting larger areas is more difficult. Add an additional two successes to the target number per x2 radius or volume.

    Duration

    Duration is used for ceremony effects that must persist longer than a single turn. Note that wounds and other lingering consequences of the ceremony persist even after the Duration ends.

    Duration | Target Number
    One turn | None (basic success)
    Sin-Eater's Ceremonies turns | +1 success
    One scene | +2 successes
    One day | +3 successes
    One month | +4 successes*
    end{tabular}

    * Add an additional success to the target number per month of additional duration.

    Eternal Duration
    Life always ends, death can last forever. All Sin-Eaters know this and Ceremonies reflect this. For +5 successes a Sin-Eater can make the effects of a Ceremony last forever, but only in certain circumstances.

    To qualify the Ceremony must either affect a dead being, or affect inanimate matter to grant it properties that exclusively relate to affecting dead beings: Such as enchanting a sword to cuts ghost, turning an object into a Ghost's Anchor or Fetter. Dedicating Momento's qualifies.

    Sin-Eaters count as alive for the purposes of this rule; as a guideline when something is ambitious ask yourself if it can die of old age.


    Number of Targets

    Number of Targets is used for ceremonial effects that must be applied equally to separate individuals, objects or areas.

    Number of Targets | Target Number
    One | None (basic success)
    Two | +1 success
    Four | +2 successes
    Eight | +3 successes
    Sixteen | +4 successes*

    * Add an additional success to the target number per x2 targets.


    Potency

    Potency represents the strength of the ceremony; it is used whenever the ceremony has an incremental effect. Ceremonies that add or subtract dice from dice pools use Potency to determine the dice by which to modify the pools. Ceremonies that inflict damage use Potency to determine how severe the wounds are.

    Potency | Target Number
    1 | Basic success
    2 | +1 success
    3 | +2 successes
    4 | +3 successes
    5 | +4 successes*

    * Add an additional success to the target number per die or wound.

      Range

      Range is used whenever the Bound wishes to affect a target further away than what she can touch. “Touch”- range ceremonies require the Sin-Eater to make physical contact with the target. In combat, this requires the sorcerer to touch the subject with her body or a ritual impliment (see “Touching an Opponent”, page 157 of The World of Darkness) before the Duration of the ritual ends. The ceremony then takes effect against the target. To affect a target outside the Sin-Eaters line of sight the Sin-Eater must know exactly where it is. This usually requires targets to be immobile.

      Range | Target Number
      Touch | Basic success
      Line of sight | +1 success
      One mile | +2 successes
      Two miles | +3 successes
      Three miles | +4 successes*

      * Add an additional success to the target number per additional mile of range.

      Size
      Size is used to affect very large targets; a basic success is sufficient to perform a ceremony with power over a subject of size 20 or less. If multiple subjects are targeted by the ceremony, the size of the largest one is used for this factor.


      Size | Target Number
      20 | None (basic success)
      21-30 | +1 Success
      31-40 | +2 Success
      41-50 | +3 Success
      51-60 | +4 Success*

      *Add an additional success to the target number per additional 10 size.


      Step Two: Music

      The second step is the most important, the Sin-Eater (and the player) must actually design the ceremony and choose how it must be completed. If the Sin-Eater is performing a learned ceremony then no thought is required, it will be the same as last time. If the Sin-Eater is performing an improvised ceremony then more thought will have to be put into it. At a bare minimum the Sin-Eater will have to do something; they can't just chant mystic gibberish and hope, it has to actually be a ceremony. If the Sin-Eater clearly hasn't put any thought into it or is thinking in terms of practicality rather than style then impose a -3 penalty. A ceremony should include some ritualistic actions that can plausibly lead to the desired outcome. On the other hand a well thought ceremony can provide a bonus, give +1 for each of the following:

      • The ceremony makes sense according to the Krewe’s metaphysics (even if it’s obviously wrong by any other standard. Note that Factions and Conspiracies have additional reasons to follow their own Metaphysics)
      • The ceremony incorporates elements of funerary traditions or occultism (even if they’re wildly out of context, a commonly accepted mistake, or stolen from a work of fiction)
      • The ceremony inspires appropriate emotions in onlookers or participants.
      • A hypothetical group of average Sin-Eaters would agree that The ceremony is stylish or badass.
      • The ceremony has that je ne sais quoi.

      Example: Louis wants to help a Ghost communicate with the living, so he lights a big cigar and blows smoke. Soon the Ghost appears as a human shaped cloud of smoke and starts talking. Louis’ Krewe believes that Ghosts are permanently hungry for the pleasure of the flesh, and so by enjoying a good cigar in it’s presence he fed the ghost, making it strong enough to manifest. In addition Louis is a big man who looks good in his usual pinstripe suits, smoking a cigar makes him look kinda badass. He gets +2.

      Louis uses his ceremony enough to make it worth his while to buy it as a learned ceremony. Later on he teaches it to Kevin. Kevin is a small weedy guy who’s Krewe has a completely different set of Metaphysics. So when Kevin performs the exact same ceremony he doesn’t get the bonuses.

      Over time styles change, Krewes get different ideas about metaphysics, and what appears badass for ayoung man might look tacky or immature when that same guy performs it ten years later. The bonuses are measured at the point of use and can vary for the same Ceremony, even the same Learned Ceremony. Sin-Eaters have a dynamic vibrant culture behind their ceremonies with new ideas sweeping constantly through and updating or replacing old styles. You have to keep with the times.


      Step Three: I've got My Man

      Now that the player and the character both know what they want to do and how to do it, it's time to perform the ceremony. All ceremony are an extended action with a Dicepool of Psyche + Attribute. Improvised ceremony cost half the highest Arcana (rounding up) in Plasm, Learned ceremonies are free. Both usually require some small amount of equipment.

      Over the following turns the Sin-Eater will perform their ceremony, turning something that was supposed to provide nothing more than catharsis into genuine magic. When the Sin-Eater has one Success per dot of the highest Arcana plus any Successes required by modifiers then the ceremony takes effect, but remember that a ceremony can never accrue more successes than the dicepool being rolled.

      Ceremonies can be performed as a teamwork action, all participants must of course be able to perform Ceremonies. The Plasm cost, should there be one, needs to only be paid by the primary actor, in addition the modifiers for style, Haunts and mortal participants only apply to the primary actor. If a Sin-Eater lacks the requisite arcana, they may still participate but only roll Psyche.


      Time Per Roll

      The time per roll depends on the highest Arcana involved in the ceremony.
      •: 1 turn
      ••: 1 minuet
      •••: 5 minuets
      ••••: 15 minuets
      •••••: 30 minuets

      Other Modifiers
      • The ceremony has style: +1 to +5 (see Step Two: Rhythm)
      • The ceremony is performed in a Haunt: +Haunt Fluidity
      • The ceremony resonates with a Key that the Sin-Eater possesses: +2
      • A Sin-Eater incorporates her Keystone into the ceremony, and the Ceremony resonates with the Keystone’s Threshold: +3 (not calmative with the +2 for having a Key)
      • A Sin-Eater wears a Deathmask while performing the ceremony, and the Ceremony resonates with the Deathmask’s Threshold: +1 (remember, only one Memento can apply a bonus to a ceremony)
      • Mortal participants join in the ceremony:
      ◦ Three to five mortals: +1 die
      ◦ Six to ten mortals: +2 dice
      ◦ Eleven+ mortals: +3 dice



      Krewe Metaphysics

      As a Krewe grows in size it's culture strengthens and reinforces itself, with profound effects upon the Krewe's channel to the Underworld. The Ceremonies performed by the Krewe change, gaining new possibilities but also new limitations.

      Faction Metaphysics (OO or OOOO)
      Faction Metaphysics is a Supernatural Benefit

      The Krewe's Channel has grown to the point it has started to change the Krewe's Ceremonies. At the Two dot version the Krewe gains one dot of faction additions, at the four dot version the Krewe gains two dots of faction additions, but also one dot of limitations.

      Faction additions can provide new ceremony additions, additional sources of bonus dice, and can tweak the limits of the Arcana in some small way.

      Conspiracy Metaphysics (OO or OOOO)
      Conspiracy Metaphysics is a Supernatural Benefit

      The Krewe's channel has grown even further and has a greater effect on the Krewe's Ceremonies. At two dots the Krewe gains three dots that can be spent on conspiracy or faction additions, but must take a dot of limitations. At four dots they gain an extra two dots of additions, and another dot of limitations.

      Conspiracy additions can provide powerful new additions or dicepool bonuses, and more. A conspiracy addition could provide a new way of performing ceremonies or unlock new Arcana.

      It is possible to buy Conspiracy Metaphysics without Faction Metaphysics, this would imply that the lower levels are either too generic or too esoteric to have a practical effect on the Krewe's powers.

      Limitations
      Just as a Krewes metaphysics broadens the Krewe's power it also limits it in certain ways. A Krewe that believes ghosts to be psychic echoes couldn't use a Ceremony built on the assumption that ghosts are part of a soul, as described in Egyptian mythology. Or rather they could, but they'd be knowingly working outside their own metaphysics. If they want to remain within their own metaphysics they will have to limit themselves to what their metaphysics says should be possible. A lot of this is style, the Sin-Eater would have to encorporate Egyptian (or whatever) motifs into their ceremony. Sometimes there is also practical limitations.

      Limitations typically enforce some additional cost, ceremony prerequisites or reduce the dicepool in certain situations.

          Using another Metaphysic
          Sin-Eater culture being what it is, it is not that rare for a Sin-Eater to use a ceremony that breaks his own Metaphysics. You can't apply your benefits without your limitations, but you can ignore both if it's convenient. At the first Tier a Sin-Eater can use ceremonies from any metaphysic she wants without penalty. In addition if she has access to a teacher or a codex from a faction or conspiracy, she can spend exp on learned ceremonies that will have all the benefits and limitations of that Krewe's metaphysics. Krewes with such advanced metaphysics often guard their knowledge jealously.

          Factions are not much different. Their members can perform improvised ceremonies within their own metaphysics, but they can also perform improvised ceremonies outside their metaphysics. In essence a member of a faction can choose if their additions and limitations apply to any given ceremony. If the Bound has a teacher or a codex, they can buy and benefit from learned ceremonies from other Krewes that takes advantage of both their unique additions and suffers their limitations. To a Faction, metaphysics is an important way to mark someone as “one of us”. So even though there's no mystical reason to follow the Faction's style, it is still a good idea to suffer the limitations for anyone who wishes to rise in status.

          Conspiracies however are completely bound by their metaphysics. Every ceremony, improvised or learned, must come from within their own metaphysics. A conspiracy can never ignore their own limitations or be tutored on Ceremonies using another Krewe's additions. However being able to perform improvised ceremonies with conspiracy additions is more than adequate compensation.

          Sample Faction Additions

          New Factor: Voodoo doll Sympathy (O). “A representation of an object is the object itself”. A hand crafted doll of the target provides a sympathy modifier. The more accurate the doll is (more accurate is a larger penatly to dex + craft) the lower the Sympathy penalty.

          New Factor: Great Works Sympathy (O). “We will all die, but our works we can live on and perhaps find immortality”. You can use people's accomplishments (paintings, books, even completing that big deal at work) for Sympathy to them.

          New Modifier: Positive Feng Shui (O). “Objects absorb emotions, even if it's not strong and pure enough to become a Momento it can still help your Ceremonies”. You get extra dice for performing a Ceremony in an appropriate resonance.

          New Modifier: Skilled Ceremonies (O). “The principles are simple, if you can understand a ghost's feelings then you can control it”. Choose a speciality in one of the Arcana, this should be roughly as narrow as a skill speciality. When the speciality applies, you may replace the Attribute with a Skill (chosen when you take this addition). Skilled Ceremonies may be taken multiple times for additional specialities.

          Sample Conspiracy Factors

          New Methodology: Device Maigc (OO - OOOO). “Ghosts are just electromagnetic phenomena, and the nice thing about science. It's repeatable”. Instead of rolling to perform a Ceremony the Sin-Eater can roll to create a device. When the device is turned on at a later point the effects occur, as though a ceremony has just been performed to completion. Any costs (from Limitations, or form turning an improvised Ceremony into a device) must be paid at the point of use. For an extra dot the devices can be made reusable or usable by ordinary Mortals (they however, must be members of the Krewe and only the Bound can actually create the devices). For two extra dots both additional benefits apply.

          New Arcana: Biomancy (OOO). “Life and death are a cycle, when we die our body nourishes the earth and from our flesh new life grows”. The Krewe can perform rituals that encourage growth, heal the sick and enhance the body.

          Sample Limitations

          New perquisite: Bulky Equipment (O). “Ghosts are semi-aware electromagnetic phenomena. You're not going to shift one without a lot of volts!” Every Ceremony needs at least twice it's highest Dot in size points of equipment. Whether that's altars and ritual sacraments, or jury rigged proton packs.

          New perquisite: Sacrifice (O). “When something dies it opens a door to the Underworld, we call our power through that open door”. You must kill a living creature with a size equal or greater to half the highest dots in the Ceremony.

          Increased prerequisite: Plasm (O). “On the great cycle of reincarnation nothing can truly be created or destroyed. All power comes from somewhere, and ours comes from Plasm”. Improvised rituals now cost 1 Plasm per point of the highest Arcana. Learned rituals cost half the highest Arcana (rounding up) in Plasm.

          New modifier: Negative Feng Shui (O). “Objects absorb emotions, even if it's not strong and pure enough to become a Momento it can still screw up your Ceremonies”. You get penalties for performing in a place with an inappropriate resonance for your ceremony.

          New perquisite: Possession (OO). “The power of a ceremony comes from the Geist and the Geist alone”. The Sin-Eater cannot perform ceremonies, they must cede control of their body to the Geist who will hopefully perform the ceremony. Convincing the Geist requires a roll of Synergy (which may be waved or an automatic failure if it the ceremony is favourable or against the Geist's personality), on a failure the Sin-Eater may make a contested manipulation + persuasion roll to cut some kind of deal with the Geist.

          Ceremony Errata
          Some existing Ceremonies are special cases or need additional explanation under the new system:

          Pass On (O)

          When a ghost resolves all it's anchors it Passes On. When a ghost's anchors are physically destroyed before they can be resolved the ghost is dragged to the Underworld. This causes an interesting problem for Sin-Eaters who Advocate for ghosts. If a ghost is trapped in the Underworld it's anchors can still be resolved, that is the ghost's murderer could be brought to justice (or whatever is needed) but since the ghost is unaware and cannot experience the necessary emotional catharsis it remains in the Underworld.

          A Sin-Eater could painstakingly try to bring the ghost back to the land of the living and show the ghost that justice has been done, but what if it was done 200 years ago and everyone involved is now dead?

          This is where the ceremony Pass On comes into play. The Sin-Eater combines their knowledge of how the anchor was resolved with the lingering mystical connection between the ghost and their anchors to create a full sensory experience. If the anchors actually have been resolved this experience provides the necessary catharsis for a ghost to pass on.

          Pass On is one of the genuine occult mysteries found among ceremonies. It's dead simple to perform, but curiously it doesn’t fit into any existing arcana. Perhaps because emotional catharsis and helping people move on is so core to the purpose of funeral ceremonies since before Sin-Eaters ever got their hands on them. Perhaps because it's so heavily tied to the mysterious process of passing on. Any Sin-Eater can use Pass On, regardless of which arcana they don't have.

          Krewe Binding (O)

          Krewe Binding is at the heart of Sin-Eater culture. Any group of Sin-Eaters could simply decide to work together but forming a Krewe is more, it's a mystical process that combines their powers to create something stronger than sum of it's parts.

          By that definition it might be possible to perform a Krewe binding with powerful Necromancy, in their own way Sin-Eaters are undead. However the truth is that any Sin-Eater can learn to perform a Krewe Binding ceremony, no matter which arcana they have or do not have. No one is sure why, the process of creating a Krewe and nearly everything about a Krewe's channel remains a mystery.

          One Last Song (O) and Ease the Path (OOO)

          One Last Song and Ease the Path are both ceremonies that do the same thing. They completely heal the target, allowing someone to live life to the full for one hour and one month respectively. Then afterwords the beneficiary will die, claimed by whatever Threshold had already marked them.
          This reveals the truth of the ceremonies, they are not blessings upon the living. They are bargains with death itself, they send death away with the promise that it can return unhindered at it's appropriate time. Therefore both Ceremonies fall under Necromancy.

          Mending the Mortal Coil (OOOO)
          Mending the Mortal Coil is a simple ceremony that removes points of damage from the target's health track. This doesn’t fit under any of the three Arcana. The reason is that Mending the Mortal Coil originates from a long gone conspiracy of Sin-Eaters who's name has been lost to history. Generations of Sin-Eaters have passed the ceremony down to their successors and many Sin-Eaters consider it a sacred duty to teach the ceremony to the next generation and ensure that the Bound never lose the ability to heal.

          (Necromancy can heal the dead, including Sin-Eaters but the Bound aren't going to abandon their ability to heal the living people they care about any time soon)

              sidebar - A Note of Terminology

              A medium is anyone who has a psychic ability to communicate with the other side. However not all mediums are able to perform ceremonies, the ones who can are set apart by a psychic link to the Underworld itself, rather than “merely” possessing second sight. In this section the word medium will exclusively refer to those who are able to perform ceremonies.

              Mediums

              Since the very beginning of humanity, life and death went hand in hand. Ghosts have always been found side by side with the living, Anchored to the places they lived in life. Is it any surprise then that mankind has adapted to the presence of the dead? That a rare gifted few stand on both sides of the divide. These people are Mediums, and death is both their blessing and their curse.

              Mediums are a diverse bunch, the only common thread is a psychic connection to death itself. For some this defines them, they call themselves “special” or “freak” and feel apart from humanity. For others it's one part of a greater whole, they have their family, their job, and yes they talk to Ghosts. Other mediums deny their abilities, they might try to medicate them away or maybe they're just really good at ignoring the Ghosts. Unfortunately quite a few mediums have received treatment from well meaning psychiatrists or parents who didn't realise the ghosts were real. A lot of mediums are somewhat morbid or just uncomfortably casual with the idea of death, but then just as many aren't. Either because they had it socialised out of them, or because they're just not like that.


              Becoming A Medium

              Mediums are born. The psychic link to the Underworld is an innate trait, some people have it and most people don't.

              Character creation

              The Medium template consists of 1 dot in ceremonies, one dot in two arcana of your choice and three dots worth of learned ceremonies.

              A Medium also has an innate ability to see Ghosts, even if they remain in Twilight. This Deathsight is always on and Ghosts are always aware that a medium can see them, mediums who wish for a little privacy may disable their Deathsight for a scene by rolling Resolve + Composure. A medium can also enhance their Deathsight by spending one Willpower and rolling Resolve + Composure: Success allows them to communicate with any ghost as though it had the Ghost Speech Numina (but will not help if the Ghost is unwilling or too mad to talk clearly), an exceptional success also gives +2 to all social rolls with Ghosts for a scene.

              While Mediums come from all walks of life, they do need some abilities to cope with a life surrounded by the dead. Most have a dot or two of Occult, even if it's only Wikipedia or paperbacks from the occult section of a high street bookstore, most Mediums have read at least something to try and understand what they are. Composure is also often quite high in order to ignore ghosts when reacting to an invisible person would result in trouble. Many Mediums are quite skilled socially. Some favour Persuasion and Empathy to understand a ghost and resolve it's issues, or at least cut a compromise so it will leave you alone. Others use Subterfuge for the same reason, placating a ghost with empty promises. A sadly large proportion of Mediums developed Subterfuge on the therapists couch, it was that or institutionalisation, while other professional mediums use Socialise to schmooze for clients.

              Mediums can end up with any Merits they want, not all enter the lifestyle beyond what is necessary to get by. Those who embrace their abilities are likely to end up with Social Merits. People able to communicate between the living and the dead are rare, and one who is successful can make good money and grateful Allies and Contacts among both the living and the dead. Many Mediums intentionally seek out a ghost Retainer, an invisible ally can be no end of help. Others end up with a Mentor, Ghosts have been a while for a round and have a wealth of experience. while with so many frauds studying with a genuine older medium is a great way to learn what's real and what's not.

              Concepts: Seance running medium, one man travelling psychic stage show, person who just wants a normal life, purveyor of “post life insurance”, elderly gravedigger who knows more than he lets on, orphan raised by ghosts, goth, perky goth, funeral director, detective with unusual advantages, criminal with a dead partner.


              Sidebar - Second Sight

              If you own a copy of Second Sight then you have a little more flexibility when creating mediums. Instead of Deathsight the player has up to four additional merit dots that can be spent on any mediumist merits or Unseen Sense, providing they apply to Underworld related phenomena. These may be supplemented with regular merit dots if the player can't afford all the Mediumist Merits they want. Incidentally mediums gain a one dot discount on Unseen Sense.

              Deathsight attracting the attention of ghosts instead of inflicting a permanent Derangement is an intentional change.


              Parlour Tricks

              Mediums can perform ceremonies by virtue of their psychic link to the Underworld, but this link is not as strong as Sin-Eater's connection. A Medium can only used learned ceremonies, they cannot improvise, and rolls an Attribute alone: They have no Psyche and their link isn't strong enough to benefit from style. In addition a medium must spend one point of Willpower before each ceremony to focus her psychic link. Like Sin-Eaters Mediums can perform ceremonies as a teamwork action, but every medium must spend one Willpower.

              Mediums can join a Krewe, though they cannot Found a Krewe or inherent the mantle of a Founder without becoming a Sin-Eater. Being part of a Krewe will affect her ceremony in the exact same way it affects members of a Sin-Eater and any Temporal benefits will, of course, apply as usual. Some Krewes respect Mediums for their abilities and recruit them as agents to fulfil their duties, or expand their size and influence. Other Krewes see mediums as useful followers but unworthy to learn the Krewe's true secrets. At the extreme some Krewes consider it blasphemy for the Living to ever have such close ties to the dead. Unsurprisingly the first sort have the easiest time recruiting mediums, but a silver tongue or a threat can recruit mediums into a Krewe against their best interests.


              Mediums and the Underworld

              Not all Mediums have any innate ability to access the Underworld, but some do. Gateseekers (see the Geist: the Sin-Eaters SAS Through The Ebon Gate) can use their innate psychic abilities to track down avernian gates. Other mediums possess the Gatekeeper merit. Of course, if a medium doesn’t have any innate way to work with avernian gates they can use ceremony instead. Videomancy can be used to uncover a avernian gates key, and at it's apex Necromancy can even create new gates. Inside the Underworld mediums generally have to rely on ceremonies or mundane skills. A medium is more comfortable in the land of the dead than a mortal, but not quite as comfortable as a Sin-Eater; which is still pretty uncomfortable.

                  Necromancers

                  Ancestor worship is one of the oldest forms of spirituality known to mankind. Death is a mystery, perhaps the first great mystery to face humanity. Is it any wonder than that those who were drawn to mysteries: Scholars and priests, visionaries and madmen. Is it any wonder that since the beginning and even today they seek out the mysteries of death.

                  As necromancer's draw their power from study and lore they tend to have some common traits. Most necromancers are well off, or at least comfortable enough to be able to dedicate significant time to studies that are unlikely to pay the bills. Many are also educated, not because their magic depends on formal education, but because they're the sort of people who value learning and because it helps them fit in with other necromancers. And yes, because sometimes you have to read a text in Latin.

                  Most necromancers are also somewhat insular, or at least very good at keeping secrets from their friends. As a practice necromancy has a blemished reputation, and is traditionally associated with wickedness. Most necromancers keep it to themselves, it's not that hard if you take basic precautions.


                  Becoming a Necromancer

                  Necromancer's are trained. With enough study anyone can become a necromancer if they get hold of a genuine textbook or are accepted by a tutor.


                  Character Creation

                  The Necromancer template consists of 1 dot in ceremonies, one dot in two arcana of your choice and three dots worth of learned ceremonies. A Necromancer also gets a free dot in the occult skill and a free occult speciality relating to their mastery of death magic.

                  As a scholarly pursuit a necromancer will at the very least be quite versed in occult lore. Academics is often just as high, on a practical level it's useful for finding one's way around a large library. Many Necromancers will also have both a high intelligence and resolve, intelligence to learn the secrets of death and resolve to keep focused on years of study. Resolve, and composure, both help a necromancer stand firm in the face of fear, for in the study of death a necromancer will see much to be afraid of.

                  For merits languages are common, and needed to read ancient texts in languages like Latin or ancient Egyptian. So is resources to buy expensive books and have the free time to read them. Many necromancers also have a good danger sense or more rarely, an unseen sense. Since most necromancer's can't see ghosts (without the benefit of a spell) many develop good instincts which keep them safe from the vengeful dead. An unscrupulous necromancer may have a wide verity of retainers; ghosts forced into servitude. A more studious necromancer may have a mentor, a teacher or an ancient ghost he's called up to study from in exchange for some creature comforts. The natural immunity merit is surprisingly common, even if necromancers don't have a balanced diet and exercise they spend enough time around corpses to toughen up their immune system. Of all the Merits usually reserved to Sin-Eaters, a Haunt is the only one commonly sought by necromancers. Both as somewhere to safely hide the tools of the trade, and as a tool of the trade, a
                  good Haunt is invaluable.

                  Concepts: Practitioner of the dark arts, post-human trafficker, servant of the grim reaper, professional psychopomp, rather unusual but well respected historian, stereotypical nerd studying “necrology”, desperate seeker of immortality or true resurrection, crime boss, mortician, priest of the ancestors, underworld explorer, wannabe god.


                  The Art of Necromancy

                  A Necromancer cannot perform ceremonies as the Sin-Eaters know them. Without the Bound's link to death a Necromancer cannot make those travesties of poor occult research function as practical magic. However the magic a Necromancer can perform can be represented using the same mechanics with a few differences.

                  Firstly the fundamentals. A Necromancer have the Ceremony trait, though it is called Necromancy. Necromancers also can't improvise spells, every one must be learned before they can be performed, consequently Necromancers do not need to buy Arcana. This does mean that Necromancers find it easier to combine different Arcana in a single spell.

                  Finally Necromancers have different dicepools to Sin-Eaters: They roll Occult + Intelligence for Oculomancy, Occult + Resolve for Entropomancy and Occult + Presence for Necromancy. Necromancers don't use those names, if a necromancer uses Latin, it's usually correct Latin.

                  A Necromancer's spells always cost something to activate, the default is half the highest Arcana's worth of plasm (round up). To acquire Plasm a necromancer can either go to a Haunt, or they can use a trick similar to a Sin-Eater's ability to consume a Ghost (G:tSE p 176). Roll Occult + the Necromancy Arcana. Necromancers cannot store Plasm within their bodies, instead they craft special “soul jars” to hold Plasm.

                  A necromancer can join a Krewe, but this has no practical benefit to their abilities. In fact it can have the opposite effect as the Krewes metaphysics begin to disrupt the Necromancer's own powers: If a necromancer joins a conspiracy and that conspiracy's metaphysics are incompatible with the necromancer’s genuine lore (it probably will be) then the necromancer cannot perform any magic until they leave.


                  Necromancers and the Underworld

                  Necromancers do not have any innate ability to enter the underworld, their motives to venture into that dark realm are no more, but no less, than any other man. Their magic can of course help with access: Videomancy can find an avernian gate or uncover it's key. A master of the necromancy arcana could even create a gate for their own use.

                  Sidebar - Variant Costs

                  While other practices may get more students, few can claim a heritage as old as necromancy. Some of the variants practised around the world and across the ages performed their magic with different payments. Some of which may include: Sacrifices (each size point of the sacrifice is worth 1 or 2 plasm, depending on the variant), Memorabilia (one Charm per spell), blood (each point of lethal damage is worth one plasm), a Vampire bloodline known as the Giovanni use Vitae (each point is worth one Plasm).

                  Sidebar - Dark Bargains

                  Necromancers are often portrayed as willing to make dark bargains, usually for magical power. The easiest way to represent this aspect is to turn to strange gods of death: Deathlords, Horsemen and other, stranger, chthonian things. The necromancy Arcana could be used to summon such beings, though if they dislike being called from the depths doing so may be fatal for even the most knowledgeable necromancer (and if not, it still can't be called easy); necessitating the acquisition of sacrifices to placate it or some obscure weakness that just might give the necromancer a chance of binding a god to it's service.

                  A second possibility is to turn to demons. After all, is Hell not an afterlife? The main advantage of using demons occurs when a necromancer is an antagonist to Sin-Eaters (or mediums). If you extend a necromancer's powers to cover demons (or let them sideline, libraries can cover more than one topic) you have an antagonist who is both relevant to the players due to a shared focus on death, but who also wields strange powers outside their usual frame of reference. A fitting antagonist. More information on the legions of hell can be found in WoD Inferno.
