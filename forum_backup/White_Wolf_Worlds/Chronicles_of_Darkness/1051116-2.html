                Trivia time.

                This was actually briefly referenced in the original God-Machine supplement, but it looks like it didn't make it into CofD. In drama (particularly in Stanislavski's system and method acting), a beat is a unit of action -- a character has a realization, or changes her tactics in regard to what she wants. In short, it's a change or a turn in the script.

                Whether they needed to call them that is, of course, subjective; personally, I like the way it ties things back to theatre.

                Even more trivial: they're called beats as a metaphor for the script-as-music...or because of a particularly thick Russian accent trying to say the word "bit" in English

                    CHASES

                    It seems pretty straight forward at the start. I realize quickly that chases don't just refer to vehicle chases, but can be applied to any kind of action that involves this kind of contested action. For example, tracking someone through the wood while that someone is actively trying to mislead the tracker. Chase! Trying to get the information from the target's database while the corporation's hackers are trying to track your location. Chase! Finishing your report on the corrupt politician before your rival reporter finishes his. Chase!

                    I enjoy the examples with different edges providing chase modifiers. This is a chart I appreciate because it gives a good idea on how to use modifiers. Not sure about Seizing the Edge. Seems like that can be handled better than the provided example. I'd probably do something like seizing the edge through exceptional successes instead of playing guessing game for some reason unrelated to the action.


                    THE SOCIAL CHASE

                    Told you it could be used for other stuff. It even gives an example to use it for Social Manuevering. Some social situations for sure. Interrogation, for example.


                    VIOLENCE

                    Yay! Finally!

                    The mention of the Beaten Down Tilt is a neat addition.

                    I must admit that the title “Down and Dirty Combat” immediately grabbed my attention.

                    “This system resolves the entire fight in a single roll.”

                    (O_O)

                    I think it's good that this rule exists but I'd probably only use it if I believe the fight would be trivial or uninteresting for the players.

                    It's interesting how it mentions that you almost are required to use Willpower to win fights. I guess it makes sense if the system really is as gritty as it seems, especially for poor mortals. Further paragraphs remind me to keep mooks weak. It makes sense for a gritty game. I imagine that I can easily buff some enemies if I want to give my players and even more difficult experience.

                    The text continues to be fairly straightforward when it describes Initiative. It says that weapons have Initiative penalties. I guess I'll learn more about that when I see actual listed weapons. And the modifiers cap at 5 like normal.

                    Delaying Actions and Surprise are straightforward as well. Hmm. So all close combat attacks use Strength and all distanced attacks use Dexterity. But the ranged combat using Firearms ignores the opponent's Defense. As of right now, it seems like all you need to hit a target with a gun is a single success. Shouldn't be too difficult when you get three dice from Willpower.

                    Is there some kind of soak to mitigate damage?

                    So as long as you haven't done your action yet, you can choose to dodge (if you have the highest initiative, then does that apply to the previous round in between your actions?). You double Defense, roll them as dice and apply them later on as modifiers to the attack roll. Since this is an element of chance, it can give more successes than your normal Defense, but it's unlikely to do so. It feels like quite an unreliable gamble unless there are other factors in play.

                    Hmm, so you can actually apply a sort of Defense against Firearms if they are up in your face? Seems like a fairly good defense too since you add the weapon's Size + 1 to it.

                    I like the Combat Summary Chart. That's something I'd want to print out.

                    When mentioning Shooting into Close Combat, it says that the shooter gets penalties depending on the number of people she wants to avoid (with a higher penalty if someone is grappled). This penalty applies to the attack against the enemy, right? Does failure mean that she actually hits any of the targets she wants to avoid? Or is that really not an issue unless you botch?

                    I don't really understand what Human Shields mean when they say that “unlike normal cover, the victim takes all of the damage from the attack”. Does that mean that the victim is always damaged if you shoot, or can you actually succeed at shooting only the enemy target? What about if you get an exceptional success? Oh, and it mentioned “armor” so I guess that answered my question about soak.

                    Wait! I think I found the answer to this: Specified Targets. If my target has taken my wife hostage and I don't trust that there are any other safe ways than to make a shot, he will be considered in cover. My wife has Stamina 2 and Armor 1 which penalizes my attack by 3. Now if I would shoot, then my wife will also suffer the damage. But if I specify that the target is the bad guy's head, it says that I have a -3 penalty. This should allow me to shoot the bad guy in the head without harming my wife despite her being his hostage. I don't think the penalty would cap at 5 for this though, so it's probably at -6 … which is a difficult shot.

                    Tilts are mentioned. I ordered a bunch of Tilt Cards from DrivethruRPG the other day without knowing what they were. Now I do. I'm glad I ordered them.

                    Armor definitely seems like the life-saver here when you can't dodge firearms.


                    INJURY AND HEALING

                    You roll Stamina each round to remain conscious when your rightmost health box has been checked with bashing damage. However, is your Stamina penalized after every attempt or does it stay the same?


                    SOURCES OF HARM

                    I don't have much to say about this. It seemed straightforward.


                    VEHICLES

                    This is also fairly straightforward. Seeing all of the different modifiers is a bit intimidating, but not in a way that takes away from the experience. It makes sense for what it is and it contains enough meat to allow for creativity.


                    EQUIPMENT

                    The Availability and Procurement paragraph notes some good guidelines on how to deal with obtaining equipment.


                    I noticed how I got a little distracted while reading The Build Equipment Action which made it difficult to absorb the information. I'm going to take a break because of that and get back to this section the next time.

                                        Dodge replaces any non-reflexive actions for that round. If you're at the top of initiative, then you can Dodge for this round, regardless of what happened last round, but only if you take no other standard action with your turn.                                                             Ugh. Social Maneuvering. I don't have time to dissect your post right now, so I'm going to give a really quick response rather than write (another; see signature) essay, which may fall afoul of your "my understanding changes through the course of this post" shtick.

                                                            Originally posted by Ekorren View Post
                                                            My initial impressions are good. The text emphasises that social manuevering isn't mind control, which is good. However, the mention of “Doors” is another addition to the terminology that now at initial impression seems to be the “Clues” of the social system. So … I want to convince the Guard that he should let me pass without interference. The storyteller determines that the Guard has Resolve or Composure 3, which gives him three “doors”. He also has an aspiration to become employee of the month, which adds a fourth door.

                                                            So I need to present him with four different arguments for him to let me through? “You're not paid enough to risk your life for this.” (Door 1 unlocked). “I promise to treat you to coffee if you let me through.” (Door 2 unlocked). Ehm … “I'm going to find your children and shoot them too if you don't let me pass!” (Door 3 unlocked). Damnit, most people would have broken by now. “Eh! Could you let me pass, please?” (Door 4 unlocked) Woohoo!
                                                            ARGH. There is so much wrong with this interpretation already. Here's the thing. Social Maneuvering is not a replacement for persuasion, intimidation, or any other straight-up talk-to-the-guy. If you want to convince a guard to let you past, you shouldn't use Social Maneuvering. There's no time, unless this is step 2 of a 14 step process for heist preparation. (Which, y'know, is cool. Watch Ocean's Eleven and how they acquire the technician's badge. Make sure you rewind and see how they find out his personal information.)

                                                            ElvesofZion has already said it better than I have time to, but just to reiterate: I would advise against using Investigation or Social Maneuvering for anything fast. Do other stuff in between relevant rolls: that's what they're good for. Unless your task makes sense in that context, I would, again, advise against using those two systems because they're built around having time.

                                                            And as Charlaquin implied, this isn't really your fault. Most people seem to make this mistake with the system. I'd really like to see them take another pass at explaining it and expanding it at some point because, as is, it's clearly really confusing for most people.

                                                            So... no. You shouldn't present the guard with four arguments. (Actually, if you're going that route, you'd better have a 6th and 7th because each of these is a roll, which might fail.) First, you should improve the guard's impression of you. You should make an Empathy roll, or read his mind to discover his Vice, or whatever. Considering Impressions is important! Because without it, you don't understand the time aspect of the system. You breezed right over this fundamental bit and it hurts your ability to understand what's going on.

                                                            Then, you figure out a general plan of attack. Maybe you "accidentally" bump into him at the bar. I... look, guards are hard, because the correct story-thing to do with a guard is to one-shot him and move on. Mooks exist to make the hero look competent, not to present a challenge. Digressing. But you should be using what you learn at each successive Door as a prelude to the next. Oh, you chat him up at the bar and learn he's got kids. Then maybe you help him get a present for his kid's birthday coming up. Maybe it's a bike, so you tell him of a good bike trail. Or you find out he wants to be employee of the month and you give him some tips on how to move up the corporate ladder. I... he's a guard! Guards don't--. Agh.

                                                            Again, you open Doors by rolling stat+skill. If you're rolling the exact same combination every time then, as a player, you're pretty one-trick and one-dimensional. Hell, show him some fancy karate moves with some Presence+Brawl or something, at least, I don't know.

                                                            I call the Integrity-analogue the "subjective stat".
