    What Stories Can You Tell With Nomadic And Explorer Vampires?

    06-11-2016, 01:47 PM
    From what I've seen, most Chronicles focus on a single city or another area of similar size, and the interplay between the prominent Covenants there. Since much of the game's appeal comes from drama in the classical sense, I think a Chronicle focused on your coterie that's always on the move wouldn't need a ton of adjustments. After all, the main conflict of holding onto your Humanity would remain.

    However, there are several logistical obstacles to that sort of alternate play style. Avoiding travel during the day, finding a relatively stable source of Vitae, reinventing your identity and forging documents every so often, periodically adapting to new domains with different rules and so on.

    So, my questions are the following:

    1) What new spins on the game can you do with this set up? I don't mean anything groundbreaking. Just a change of pace.

    2) What could lead a coterie to adopt this life style? What could motivate them to settle down and go their separate ways?

    3) Which solutions are available to the aforementioned logistical obstacles?

    I feel like this play style lends itself very well to cross over with Changeling and some parts of Mage. The former deal with crises of identity as a norm and they can offer different perspectives on the meaning of Humanity and Home. They have many tricks up their sleeves that facilitate the whole travel process. A changeling could help transport the coterie through the Hedge, Tokens, Fae Mounts and maybe even a Mobile Hollow. They could also buy fake documents and other important items from goblin markets.

    As for Mage themes, maybe one of the coterie's members is a member of the Ordo who's fascinated by Mysteries. Maybe this vampire and the mage are looking for the same occult knowledge, or they might seek different answers but have complementary skills. Alternatively, one of the vampires could be a Carthian and the Mage a Libertine. They might become allies in a wide-eyed plan to change the status quo. In any case, the Mage could also help with the logistics, such as cutting down on the difficulties of travel with Space and Time.

    Last but not least, a Beast might join them by offering the usual benefits, such as a Lair they can use as secret base. The Lair might have the Find A Vein Merit. The Skeleton Key would be super useful for travel and secrecy as well. I think the easiest Hunger for this group to handle would be Hoard, particularly if it's for occult knowledge. Or maybe Hunger for Power, so that the Beast can Feed by coercing people into welcoming and helping the coterie.

    If you are not fond of combining this play style with a lot of cross over, no problem. If you have any tips that focus on how vampires could do this without involving other splats a lot, feel free to share them.

    Bye.

      First edition saw this and had a book about it, Nomads. It was all about the pitfalls, reasons and story hooks you could pull. If you're interested you might check it out, a lot of the mechanics have changed but the non-mechanical sections are solid and some things could be easily updated.

      1) What new spins on the game can you do with this set up? I don't mean anything groundbreaking. Just a change of pace.
      Nomadic and explorer seems like two different things for me, though there's some amoutn of overlap.

      A nomad is a drifter, they're not settling in one place for any significant length of time. An easy hook to go with is 'why.' Are they being hunted, perhaps the hunters who killed their sire, or a blood hunt? Perhaps they're actually under a curse, something like Disquiet that causes other vampires to start turning against them if they're too long, though you don't need a magical reason for this, vampire domains tend to be insular and it's not unheard of for a vampire to have an abrasive personality. One of the characters in my last vampire game went seeking after her perhaps-brother from when she was alive, who went missing at the end of the game. She was under the Vinculum so her feelings on the matter were skewed. The player is now storytelling our werewolf game and the character has popped up looking for clues to where he is and who might have caused him to disappear.

      An explorer is seeking something. The clan books are from the points of view of different explorer vampires. Some are traveling across the world, some are barely venturing out of their city, but they're seeking others of their clan to find out the histories. Some are extorted into it, others are just doing it to sate their curiousity. In the Belial's Brood book, the introductory fiction has a vampire sent to investigate the history of the covenant so she and her superior can frame them for some political sneakiness they're trying to pull off back home. There's enough legends and ancient mysteries to occupy a vampire's entire unlife. Maybe they're the sole member they know of a strange bloodline and seek answers. Maybe they're looking for remnants of their covenant, long turned to dust and ash but who likely left some evidence of their existence. Maybe they're trying to find out who the Nameless is.

      2) What could lead a coterie to adopt this life style? What could motivate them to settle down and go their separate ways?
      Some of this was obviously answered in the previous response. But there's always the people who, even as a human, felt the wanderlust. Anti-social or just enjoying meeting new people, these things could carry on into their Unlife. They can still be linked to others, maybe their sire wants them to explore the world and what they are, or they're going to see the last of their living family and friends before abandoning themselves fully into the All Night's society.

      Finding a reason to settle down involves working off the reason for their wandering to begin with. If they've been traveling because they were tormented by guilt about who they drank dry after their Embrace, coming to terms with that could give them a reason to settle down. If they were unaccepted elsewhere, all it might take is a warm welcome. This kind of thing can be very easy or very hard, depending on the circumstances. One might be willing to give up their nomadic lifestyle if they're just offered a nice position, others might never be able to really settle down.

      3) Which solutions are available to the aforementioned logistical obstacles?

      The Nomads book has a few merits and devotions and probably sorcery. Just off the top of my head there's one that summons a sort of spiritual familiar to watch over you while you sleep.

      In this case, a ghoul is really going to be your best asset. There's a movie, unfortunately named Forsaken, about a group of vampires who travel the roads with their modern Renfield stand-in. They basically sleep in the trunk while he drives and obeys the laws so no one will pull him over and open the trunk. Requiem ghouls will likely have some powers to help with this, as well. If you don't like standard modes of travel, the Protean Discipline that allows you to merge with the earth is a good one for dealing with sunlight. Odds are if you're traveling by vehicle you're going to come upon some shelter, be it a motel or a gas station. Any of these things can offer sustenance and shelter for daysleep. In the aforementioned movie, drifters and other travelers seemed to be the primary source of food.

      To be sure, it's a riskier way of doing things. It would be hard to be confident if you don't know where you can hide out for the day or find yourself running low on Vitae.

      If you are not fond of combining this play style with a lot of cross over, no problem. If you have any tips that focus on how vampires could do this without involving other splats a lot, feel free to share them.
      There's a lot of support for this style of play, though Chronicles is written in a style that doesn't suggest much travel. Danse Macabre has the nomadic biker gang, the Brides of Dracula, as an entire covenant. The Circle of the Crone has vampires following the Hero archetype, who have reason to give in to wanderlust, travel and overcome obstacles. The Invictus and Prima Invicta are both likely to have couriers and diplomats for whom travel is their life. Dragons may even see it as an opportunity to study change, and adherents of the Lance are occasionally archeologists, seeking evidence to support their beliefs and further Theban rituals. I imagine there's more than that in the various covenant books.

      In one of our games the Storyteller deemed the sun in the Hedge didn't count as the real sun, which made it a very tempting place for the few vampires we met, though I don't believe it was part of the setting as written. The Book of Spirits and Book of the Dead both detail alternate worlds one might travel in. I believe the sun in the Shadow is considered real sun, perhaps moreso in second edition, but the Underworld has no sun so if one can find their way through the tunnels you can have an Underdark-like story.

      In the vampire games I ran there was some travel but they took advantage of their contacts in covenants and most of their obstacles came from running into the weird politics going on in the other domains.

              One other option might be to integrate the Gallows Post Covenant into the Modern Era. They were first introduced in the Broken Covenants section of VtR 2E, but got a true Covenant write up in the Dark Eras section Requiem for Regina.

                          I was in a nomadic campaign back in the masquerade days. What we did was have one person tell a story as ST, then they would swap to another player and just keep going through. Since each story was basically a new stand alone story, it lends itself well to this type of play. I think the current ST got xp equal to whoever got the least amount, that would need to be slightly tweaked with the beat system. When I ran it my character mostly stood behind some one else and glared at whoever they were talking to.

                          It was pretty violent, I had to narrate driving away from a place engulfed in flames so many times. Basically the group would encounter weirdness, investigate and smash it. I think they went to Mexico City for some meeting/party with the rest of the Sabbat and got pulled into the politics there since they were just passing through. I even set up a bar scene ala Near Dark, but that bar got burned down.

                          So basically its encounter weird stuff, deal with it and then drop in and do some favors for someone. Throw in some interactions with just normal people.

                          There are a lot of nomadic or small town bloodlines you can mine from 1st edition Requiem. There is those aging hillbilly types, those gangrels that set up a place on the road to lure people in, the Brujah and that one poorly defined bloodline from Lancea Sanctum that are wanderers. There are probably quite a few I am missing (who were those travel circus folks again?)

                          As for why, off the top of my head: A vampire was framed for killing his sire and is on the run. A vampire did kill her sire and is on the run. A vampire can't stand living under a Prince's rule. A vampire has a vision to take to the road to meet their destiny. A vampire's child just died of old age and they can't stand to see that happen to the rest of their kids. Spent decades following the Grateful Dead and now just doesn't feel right staying in the same place. Under a mystical compulsion to keep moving. OD- find mystic stuff, LS/CoC- spread the word, IV-failed their regent/lord and is on the road to atone, Carthian - form a network/spread the message. There are probably dozens of reasons to do it.

                          The biggest issue I see is the touchstone system, especially the willpower regaining part, this may need to be looked at.
