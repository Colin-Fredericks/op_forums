    OK, since a change of topic would probably be a good thing at this point, and I've finally gotten around to reading the chapter, let's change topics and talk about some errors of misrepresentation actually present:

    Perhaps the most egregious error is the claim that the Protestant Reformation was directly caused by the Witch Hunts of Europe... In fact, as written in the chapter currently, it is heavily implied as the sole reason for both the Protestant Reformation and Henry VIII forming the Church of England.

    This is ignoring so many factors within the Catholic Church that brought about its own fragmentation and downfall that it's honestly quite astonishing. It's calling a symptom of the disease the cause of the disease, itself. The Catholic Church before the reforms of the (ironically named) Counter-Reformation/Council of Trent routinely engaged in practices that would be seen as naked corruption by modern eyes. The Church openly extorted bribes from the nobility, or coerced massive concessions of land under pain of Excommunication. (And that was basically the equivalent of handing everyone in the world who hated you a license to freely murder you or conquer your lands.) The Church would openly accept money in exchange for making a nobleman the arch-bishop in charge of some of the lands those noblemen likely donated to the church under coercion. In fact, it worked out just fine for them, since, while bishoprics were not hereditary, the money was, and the Church could happily just keep accepting bribes for every successive generation of de facto hereditary lords being given the cloth. They didn't stop at just selling rank, for that matter: They traded holy relics of the saints (things like their finger bones dug up from their graves) for cash. Naturally, there were plenty of con men in the clergy, willing to claim any random pauper's hand was a saint's holy remnants on Earth, and that just having that severed finger would TOTALLY cure you of any disease. If it festers and you get a new disease, that just means you need to buy MORE fingers!

    The Pope rewrote how the heavens work by Papal decree, inventing Purgatory wholecloth, just so that he could charge noblemen bribe money to go to Heaven, instead. That is, overtly trading material wealth for Heaven. You know, the exact thing Simon Magus was supposedly such an evil sorcerer for attempting to perform. These purchases of indulgences were THE single largest listed reason why the Reformation revolted against the Church.

    It's not as though there weren't reformist movements before The Reformation, the Waldesians existed centuries prior, (as did the Lollards and Fratecelli,) which protested the Catholic Church largely as having become materialistic and corrupt and for their efforts, largely were the "pagans" and "witches" that were put to the stake in the earlier witch hunts. (In fact, they likely were the original cause of the widespread belief in witchcraft in the first place, as they helped the church to suppress dissent over their flagrant corruption to declare dissent in league with Satan. That said, the divergence from history on this point for the purpose of setting up ahistoric nWoD factions is an overt divergence from history, so is understandable.) The Franciscans only avoided the same fate by gaining Papal recognition, yet from then on, were a source of constant internal dissent over the materialism of the Church in that era, arguing for ascetic lifestyles for the clergy.

    The reason Martin Luther caused a continent-rending schism when Waldo did not was because the Church of the 15th Century was even more flagrantly corrupt and power-hungry. Look up Pope Alexander VI and the children he had by his Papal mistress, whom he overtly schemed to put into power over Italy through excommunication of all local rivals, giving Church funds to military conquest over Catholic faithful lands, or repeatedly re-marrying his daughter (and killing her husbands or declaring them impotent and annulling their marriage so she could be "reused" as a political token for marriages). Machiavelli wrote The Prince essentially as a resume to the Pope's son, to prove he was ruthless enough to be an adviser to someone as ruthless as the child of the Pope. The name Borgia is synonymous with evil within the Papacy, and is a FAR greater direct cause of the Reformation.

    And it's not like the Papacy itself was alone in critically undermining the moral authority of the Papacy. For much of the Late Renaissance, France overtly owned the Papacy. The Knights Templar were declared heretics and their wealth looted (by the state, not the church) because the King of France could not repay his debts to the bankers of the Church. Oh, and while I'm on the topic, banking was usury, a heresy punishable by death, (the reasons for which are a giant page-long rant in and of itself...) which meant that the Church would kill any business rivals in their pursuit of placing others in debt to them. This was, of course, AFTER the period where the Papacy would excommunicate the would-be successors to the Holy Roman Empire, only to have the Emperor march into the Vatican, execute the Pope, and set up his own puppet Pope who would ensure he would be coronated. Even that was when there weren't TWO people claiming to be Pope, as was often the case, just to help ensure that there was always confusion and an undermining of the legitimacy of the Papacy. (Often rival puppet Popes of France and the Holy Roman Empire.)

    All this is to say there were KIND OF A LOT OF VERY GOOD REASONS WHY PEOPLE WOULD BE UPSET WITH PAPAL RULE, not just one single random thing that happened.

    In fact, the largest, most distinct, most iconic era of the Witch Hunting craze in Europe didn't occur until around 1580, six decades after the Protestant Reformation. Most historians list the Protestant Reformation as a cause of the Witch Hunts, not the other way around.

    Henry VIII simply took advantage of the already massive discontent, and took the chance to use the chaos of the Reformation to break off from the Papacy to get his marriage to a relative of the Holy Roman Emperor (who currently exerted the heaviest influence on the Papacy) annulled.

    In fact, you could pretty much sum up the 16th and 17th Centuries as "The Protestant Reformation happened, and everyone started murdering each other constantly for 200 straight years, to the point of nearly halving the population of what is now Germany, and all the social orders of the Middle Ages fell apart in the bloodbath, and the witch hunts were just another excuse to kill your neighbor before they killed you," and be done with it. (Although, to add even more confusion to the issue, it's also believed that Lyme Disease, a disease from the New World that caused seizures and was heretofore unknown was treated as demonic possession or evidence of witchcraft, and may have directly led to a lot of the hysteria in Europe that occurred in the decades immediately following some of the earlier round trips to the New World.)

    Speaking in a much broader sense about the whole of the arc of Western Civilization, this was the start of a new era. Following the collapse of the Great Empires in the West, especially Rome, monotheistic religion dominated Europe. This was the collapse of The Church that could hold such a powerful sway over the hearts and minds of populace that they could keep any other true centralized empires from forming, and radically altered how commerce was conducted for 1500 years. This was the collapse of that system into the free-for-all of nations striving for global empires based upon trade and the exploitation of foreign peoples and lands through the slave trade that the Church had previously kept abolished. It was a fundamental change in the social order, and like most fundamental changes in social order, came with a LOT of bloodshed, fear, and paranoia. Again, the witch trials were just a symptom of a MUCH larger movement going on behind the scenes, the grasping paranoia of a populace who was seeing monumental changes to the fundamental structure of their worldview on a scale so massive they couldn't comprehend what was happening, or where it was going, and as such grasped onto easy explanations. That is, rather than the social discord being brought upon by the changing of an epoch, where the social contracts they depended upon were being torn up from beneath their feet, ancestral rights being denied them, their being forced to move from everyone-knows-everyone villages to faceless cities to feed the upcoming Industrial Revolution, or the decay of the feudal societies or church structure that had governed their ancestors lives, or the new diseases ravaging their families being the result of the changes in the world they couldn't understand, much less control, it was easier to just blame that one guy that didn't fit in for being a witch.

    Blaming it all on one book or just treating witch hysteria as though it spawned ex nihilo does a disservice to the historical context that these chapters are built upon. After all, it was these religious and cultural divisions that actually caused the Puritans to want to set up a colony outside of England, and separate from the (Anglican) Virginia Colony. (Only to then persecute each other as soon as they were on their own, and fragment further and further until towns were primed to tear themselves apart.)

    To go further, I don't think you can really talk about the Puritans without also talking at least a little about English Civil War that broke out as a direct consequence of the Protestant Reformation, Splitting of the Church of England, and the various conservative and radical religious groups that fought for control of England that would lead to a Puritan movement being oppressed in England itself in the first place. Puritan fundamentalism is a direct rejection of the more materialist and conservative stance many, especially of the landed gentry wished to see enshrined in the new Church of England, since the status quo ensured their continued wealth and power. It informs their hatred of displays of wealth, gatherings for purposes of entertainment, etc.

            Ok, I've been thinking about Native Hunters for the Doubting Souls era, and here are a few ideas.


            -Obviously Les Mysteres would exist in the region. The Algonquin concept of the Manitou fits the Mysteres quite well - as does the usage of French. This is far from a universal representation of Native hunters though.

            -I don't have a name yet, but the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederation) should have a more institutional Hunter Conspiracy. They can trace their history back to Hiawatha uniting the five peoples, and laying down the Great Law against dark magic, cannibalism, and human sacrifice. I'm thinking this Conspiracy will wield Relics, having been in the business long before Aegis Kai Doru ever stepped foot in the Americas. Their sample relic list would of course be heavy with Iroquois cultural evocation (the thunder-blasting chert of He-no, evil-repelling False Faces, and skill-granting wampum patterns) and trophies from monsters of their lore (like the literal stone-coats of the Thunenhyarhen, or the captured and bound disembodied hand of the Oniate).

            Think of this group as a bit of a cross between Aegis Kai Doru (relic wielders & collectors against dark sorcery) and Task Force VALKYRIE (defenders of the state... and their relics serve as the eras version of Advanced Armory in their way) - as opposed to the Manitou serving Les Mysteres.

            - Again I don't have a name yet, but as the Algonquin women are traditionally responsible for the disposal of the dead, I thought an all female Compact dedicated to putting the walking dead to rest would be appropriate.


            Together with Les Voyageurs, that's 2 Compacts and 2 Conspiracies for Native Hunters in "New England"... I'll tinker with a few farther afield and see what I can come up with (Choctaw Conspiracy gifted with prophetic info from the sun? Navajo "singers" who actively seek hozoji with the supernatural, seeking them out in an effort for harmonious coeexistence?)

                                                            So here's my own preliminary list of Hunter groups I'm using in the era...

                                                            Tier 2
                                                            - Brotherhood in the Night (Puritan Compact that in its New England form is mostly like an era's predecessor to the Long Night; its cousins back in England though are more Leveler/Digger oriented and look more like the Union than the Long Night)
                                                            - Lightbringers (the "Enlightenment" in full swing back in Europe, these empiricists are straight up predecessors to Null Mysteriis)
                                                            - Ordre de Saint Gabriel (knightly order loyal to the throne of the Sun King; probably Tier 3 in scale, but lacks the power of a supernatural Endowment)
                                                            - Sequttôi (Wampanog Compact dedicated to mourning those past and laying them to rest)
                                                            - Scarlet Watch
                                                            - Les Voyaguers

                                                            Tier 3
                                                            - Acheron Shipping & Trading Guild
                                                            - Lucifuge
                                                            - Malleus Maleficarum
                                                            - Les Mysteres (major upswing in members since the plague earlier this century, as more Native hunters in the region give themselves wholly over to the Spirits out of desperation)
                                                            - Order of the Knights of Saint George (they're still operating in the name of the Church of England, but the instability of English governance with the Civil War, the Protectorate, the Restoration, and the Glorious Revolution means they don't answer to the crown anymore for their own protection - their majesties William & Mary do not know they exist)
                                                            - Sons of Sosondowah (a bit further inland than the English settlers currently extend, but the power of the Iroquois League is a great enough that they feel their presence none the less; these warriors of the Haudenosaunee fight to uphold the Great Law's principles against dark magic, human sacrifice, and cannibalism)



                                                            Aegis Kai Doru is still strongly rooted in what was once Byzantine territories, as extended further by the Coptic and Orthodox Churches. They don't yet have a strong presence in Western Europe or through that the New World at all.

                                                            The Ascending Ones have some presence in Spanish and Portuguese territories into the New World (having had an Iberian presence since the days of Al-Andalus) but none yet in New England.
