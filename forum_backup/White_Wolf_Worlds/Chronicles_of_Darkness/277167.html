    Game Idea: "You're Not My High Priest!"

    10-10-2014, 09:44 AM
    Silly-sounding title, I know, but I think the idea has merit.

    Basically, the characters are awakened by somebody claiming to be a member of one of their cults, presumably high-ranking. They dispatch the Arisen to obtain a heavily-guarded Relic, though this is just a ruse. Secretly, the "high priest" wants them for <some sort of ulterior motive involving the destruction of a lot of property>.

    That's where the general premise ends. More specific would be stuff like the summoner's motives, investigating how the mummies all managed to be stolen from their cults (if they actually were), why did the summoner need >1 Arisen. Was the intent to make the Arisen lose face with their guilds? Was one of the party responsible for this, and the high priest was acting on orders from a previous Descent?

    I'm trying to come up with a good pitch for some players who haven't done Mummy before at all. My idea is "Somebody awakens you for ulterior, sinister reasons." Naturally, at first the players wouldn't know that.

    Basically just brainstorming. Any ideas?

      If your players are ok with not starting with a Cult, you could easily have a situation in which they all once belonged to the same Cult, but were later abandoned. The false priest was just lucky enough to stumble across an entire cache of Mummies in one place. The more disparate the group, the harder it's going to be to justify all of them being in the one place for this to take place. Although if one member of the group is actually behind it all, well, that certainly provides an excuse. It's probably both the easiest explanation for this scenario, and one of the most entertaining.

      Rather than raising them for a bit of relic hunting, why wouldn't the false priest just raise them with the purpose he desires? Then the entire group has a Judge-enforced Purpose that they must follow, or else lose Sekhem. Bonus points if it's a historical setting (you could run this as prelude to a modern campaign, if you want). If you set it in a time/place of turmoil, like modern Iraq/Syria, China in pretty much the entire first half of the 20th century, the French Revolution, or something similar, it's pretty easy to justify both multiple mummies and thoroughly human motivations for violent purposes. I remember an idea floating around somewhere about a Nazi occultist raising a Mummy to help defend Germany, to name one suggestion following a similar theme to your ideas.

          Well, presumably, our Sinister Summoner fears retribution from the Arisen if they discover what his intentions were after the fact. Once you're finished with your Task, you immediately lose a Sekhem and are free to do whatever you want for the rest of your Descent, correct?* I remember reading an Actual Play where after the Arisen destroyed a drug den for his summoner, he turned around and burned the summoner away for wasting his time.

          And if the true goal was by one of the party members seeking something heretical, it definitely doesn't want the rest of the meret to catch wind of it, I'd imagine.

          But that's just reasoning for why a Sinister Summoner would want to keep their motives secret.

          I do like the idea of a legit high priest being coerced into summoning the mummies to help some sort of historical villain group, like the Nazis. Honest, mixing Nazis and Mummies just strikes me as a golden adventure hook.

                                              Heh, I actually had a sinister summoner storyline. In this case, the high priest was the legit heirophant of the main mummy NPC. However, said priest resented the arisen and the hard life hes had to lead, so hes decided to turn the arisen on each other in hopes to destroy them all. To that end, he has his own Cult that has infiltrated the cults of the Nomarchs and has been directing the cults against each other. To add a little personal torture to his successor, he summoned his Mummy lover and commanded the mummy to kill him. Sekhem driven by Purpose, things got messed up pretty quickly (imagine five nights at freddies, but with a mummy and brainwashed cultists).
