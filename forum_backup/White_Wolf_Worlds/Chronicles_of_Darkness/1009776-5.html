        Originally posted by CatDoom View Post
        I have to wonder why mages aren't more aware of/interested in Arcadia. Mages have been sticking their noses where they don't belong since at least the dawn of this iteration of human history, and yet it remains a huge blind spot for them. I mean, mages know that nobody who goes for a swim in the Ocean Orouboros ever comes back, presumably because more than a few mages over the centuries have *tried it*, and yet they still seem blissfully unaware of the Gentry and their realms.
        Odds are they have, but it is a pretty varied place. One mage can spend their entire life studying Arcadia only to find that they've only explored one realm. The Arcadia of Changeling is not a single unified area similar like the Hisil. It's more like the thousands of Places That Aren't in the Hisil.

        From there it would get harder to prove anything, as the Gentry vary from one to the next. Certainly the basics are known, it's not the Supernal Arcadia, it's a place of contracts and powerful alien beings, it has a warping affect on people, even mages, who stay there too long. Iron acts as a bane. That much could probably be taught by any mage who seems like they've been around and are well-studied. But even there, if you were a beginning mage who had no ties to Fate and didn't know or care about changelings and had never met one, is it something you would seek to learn about, compared to something more relevant?

        Then there's the geographical and chronological problem. Arcadia doesn't neatly overlap with the world the way the Shadow does. I think that may have been refined for the Hedge in second edition, with it reacting to pain or fear or something, but in first edition Arcadia didn't seem to correlate nicely with the real world. You could be taken by the Raptor in Chains in New York and find yourself a running hare next to what once was an Asian soldier stolen from the world four thousand years ago. The mages in Washington, at least those interested in the Shadow, likely know of the major spirit courts there, including the spirits of the city. But there's no 'Washington DC' Gentry that were limited to the city, though I guess there could be, and any other Gentry could pop up.

        Plus not as easy to really get to and explore.

        It's probably one of the more well known 'other planes' though. Less so than the Underworld and the Shadow but more than Duat.

        I'd expect we'll see some of the more well-spread beliefs about Arcadia when the Crossover book comes out. Until then, I believe the book Rites of Spring might details the general belief mages hold about Arcadia, or at least changelings. Maybe Equinox Road.

                                        Originally posted by MCN View Post
                                        Strix can make one to their realm for both strix to leave and at least vampires to freely enter.
                                        The Strix can make a portal. Faerie has literal stable roads running between it and Earth. These things are not interchangeable.

                                        In a game that's supposed to have an uncertain cosmology and full of mysteries, I really find such hard definitions that are being pushed to be... overly forced.
                                        The way entrances to Lower Depths operate is described in Mage's section on Irises. It's not even remotely descriptively comparable to how a Hedge gate works.

                                        The fact that many Lower Depths are defined by outright missing the qualities that allow certain Arcana to function does little to quell the sense that some people are pushing "Faerie is a Lower Depth" as part of the Changeling fandom's ongoing baggage with Mage, particularly since "Awakened magic doesn't work at all in the Hedge" is a misreading I have seen multiple times.

                                        To use another mage example, it feels like Left-Handed. Something is "lower depths" because some powers-that-be have decided to arbitrarily label it such, because it meets some semi-flexible criteria.
                                        "Left-Handed" is a partially politicized term for a general umbrella of harmful practices relating to the use of Awakened magic. A Lower Depth is a place that is so far removed from Phenomenal reality that parts of the latter start to fail around gates to the former and these failures are lesser symptoms of the place's inhospitability to conventional existence.

                                        The Fae literally cannot make worlds that are uniformly inhospitable to visitors, and the Fae are Faerie. Their need to enforce narrative upon themselves and their surroundings is not interchangeable with the consumptive hunger common to the Depths.
