    Originally posted by Ashenrogue View Post
    Once again, this is mostly going to depend on exact arcana and build (as per the usual). We know that mage armor is reflexive in 2e and has different effects based off of the one being used. We also know that at least the Time shield specifically messes around with initiative, and that the Death shield downgrades damage types. Long story short, it depends heavily on the mage. I also think you're underestimating how quickly a mage can turn lethal. Spells don't need anything higher than a single success nowadays, and while you likely aren't going to be able to get an extremely high number of factors piled into a spell at the drop of a hat, you usually don't need to. A mage only needs a single turn to mind control you, a single turn to create an unpassable barrier, a single turn to cause any number of status effects, conditions, or tilts, etc. While mages are nearly unstoppable with prep, they're nothing to sneeze at even when caught off guard.
    Trust me: I've been playing Mage: The Awakening since its release, I have worked on and extensively played most of the 2E game lines (including Mage and Werewolf), and I have right in front of me the latest developed copy of the 2E rules. I'm not underestimating anything. As well, remember we're talking about an Obrimos of the Adamantine Arrow here, so not guaranteed to have Death, Mind, Time or the like, or have them at high levels. Ultimately, Mind is one of the best bets for stopping the fight before it starts, which I'd call a "win," but the question was about Blood Talon Rahu vs. Adamantine Arrow Obrimos.

    Spells need only a single success to take effect at all the configured factors, yes, but you have to take everything into account. Like Withstand, or the chosen opponent's ability to shrug off harm like nobody's business. Various Mage Armors can help, definitely—Death is good, although you're still taking damage, even if it's downgraded, and Time helps you avoid getting hit. Casting a mind control spell might help you out...for a turn or two, unless you can get it with more advanced factors, but that means greater difficulty. If you put so much effort into increasing its duration, you still have to worry about Potency, lest it have no effect at all on the rampaging beast. Even my character in a 2E game has failed spellcasting rolls with 6-7 dice before, when the dice gods were vengeful.

    This is what I mean. Much of the mage's ability depends on multiple factors. It's possible that one spell with one casting roll could propel her to victory. It's not always likely, though. Conversely, the werewolf is a reliable death-dealing machine. She attacks fast, she hits hard, she keeps on coming, and she has tremendous advantages up close (grappling and status-inflicting effects, especially). The Rahu's magic helps make her a better killer. She's less versatile than our hypothetical mage, but trades that versatility for special focus in killing up close and personal. The mage is going to lose if she has to go hand-to-hand with the werewolf, and has one turn at most to change the nature of the fight such that she doesn't have to duke it out in a straight fight. That's not a whole lot of margin for error. The werewolf doesn't have to work very hard to be super-deadly in battle. It's pretty much all reflexive stuff that she can do right from the outset. The mage doesn't have as many inherent advantages.

    If I wasn't so busy trying to finish another project tonight, I'd stat up a couple characters and roll out a "bar fight" scene, just because it's fun.

                                                    Originally posted by branford View Post
                                                    If any major template could make a Resolve + Composure roll, it's mages. I would also imagine that a number of spells or Arcana should be able to force a Clash of Wills against Incite Fury.
                                                    Yes, Resolve + Composure is a good roll for most mages. However, she has to make that every turn or else attack in a blind rage. I'm sure a great many spells could help lift the Berserk Condition (Clear Thoughts comes to Mind), but that's also another action you're taking aside from attacking, while the werewolf is focusing her attention on attacking.

The mage is fighting an uphill battle here against a foe with a lot of inherent advantages for this sort of fight. She's definitely better off if she manages to get away, now has some idea of what she's up against, and prepare for a future conflict.
