                                                  Considering I have been chopping one away at the recipes in the Apocalypse Cookbook recently, I can tell you exactly why we need more Cookbooks, particularly for Chronicles.

                                                  1) Cooking is fun! Having a book of recipes in general is a fun way to get on your feet and begin learning it if you haven't done it, and as you do it you start getting into it, you get the joy of experimenting and trying new things-I'm already coming up with ideas on how to do better than Damper Bread for the Bunyip, which brings me around to my next argument...

                                                  2) Much like music, prop pieces, and many other things, cooking a recipe tailored to a gaming session is a a great way to establish a mood for games, and having books that helped to build on the pre-existing in game packages of moods in the forms of y-splats or z-splats or individual cities or whatever make gives you a pre-made toolkit for inspiration in case you don't want to come up with a individual dishes for session, instead pairing them up based on what you have. I have a recipe I'm working on(Human Hearts and Poison Apples) that I know, once I get it right, I cannot conceive of trying to pitch Changeling The Lost without serving it alongside it, and considering the duel themes of Paris, I think my projects after that will be figuring out dishes for each of the Rosiere Courts.

                                                  3) It's a great human layer to add to any supernatural society you want. The Recipes in the Apocalypse Cookbook were more nuanced and interesting approaches to each of the tribes that made them feel more real, far less cartoonish, and more human-and the Chronicles of Darkness is even more deeply rooted in the sense of the human, even when it's in rejection of it. By taking a franchise that is all about that place in between and the transition from one to other and giving it a very human place to show a face in, it allows for further richness and contextualization of those characters.

                                                  Take, for instance, the jab at Beasts. Sure, the big thing for them is that they eat the shock that they generate by acting against humanity...but they still need to eat regular food too. But on top of that, the way they live their lives as Beasts surely colors their culinary tastes and relates their Legends and nightmares with their Lives and connections. Imagine how two different Giants approach a main course, communicating intense, overpowering flavours that never the less are enjoyable to have wash over you, to communicate hopelessness, and the ways that changes from places to place, person to person. How do varying Nemesis play around with saltiness, bitterness, sourness, and spicyness when trying to slyly communicate things to their prey, and what do their choices say when the only person they're serving is themselves? Questions like these humanize one of the most contentious splats in fandom and at the same time allow their themes to be reinforced in things we get, that we're intrinsically familiar with, all while giving an angle for something different for your night.

                                                  4) The Chronicles world is rife with opportunity for cool recipe ideas, even as it's wider spread forms embrace a powerful homogeny. It has both strange niches and strong ideals that weave in and out of a wide variety of cultural contexts and momentary moods and philosophical viewpoints that can be powerfully expressed in food. Consider how a certain someone's problems with reconciling how the Diamond and Free Council approach culture could possibly be answer by showing how the Silver Ladder and the Free Council approach an Indian recipe, for example. There's room and ability and divergence to both re-tell and re-examine the stories we're familiar with and make really cool food.

                                                  Yeah, no, more cookbooks, please, and particularly let's get started on Chronicles based dishes. In fact, I'll be openly disappointed if Changeling the Lost's kickstarter does not have a stretch goal for a cookbook.
