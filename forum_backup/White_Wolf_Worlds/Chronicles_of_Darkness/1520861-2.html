                                    Looking at the blue book, summoning and warding/binding require specific knowledge of the thing you're trying to summon or drive away. When Summoning you need to get the conditions (and Conditions) right to allow the thing to manifest, whether that means getting a specific ghost's Anchor or causing your summoning area to become Resonant with a particular spirit.
                                    Abjuration looks more general though, since it works the same without regard for specific Bans or Banes. Warding/Binding combines Abjuration with the specific bane of an entity to protect a specified are, so that's also specific to particular entities, or at least to entities with the same Bane, since the book calls out low-Rank ghosts having a Bane of salt quite often.

                                            As far as the blue book is concerned, the actual ritual matters way less than knowing and using the bans and banes and summoning conditions of the entity you're interacting with.
                                            Based on the blue book and interpreting the Mummy sorcery mechanics strictly I'd say that Abjuration in general could be one Open Rite, but summoning and warding/binding would count as open rites per unique set of banes and summoning conditions. And "classic exorcism" is called out as a combination of Binding to keep an entity in place, Abjuration to provide a spiritual kick, and using Bans and Banes together as much as possible.
                                            Spirits likely have different enough conditions that I'd say every spirit is unique, but IIRC every ghost has their body as an anchor, so I could see an argument for "summoning any ghost with their bones" could count as an Open Rite if you wanted it to, and enough ghosts have salt as a bane that "warding a ghost away with salt" could also be pretty generally useful. If you want to summon a particular ghost out of the Underworld, though, I'd probably make that an Open Rite per individual ghost, similar to summoning particular fiends out of Duat.

                                            My reading of the Sorcerer rules is that you only use up a Rite slot with an Open Rite if you choose to Master it. So if you're summoning and banishing lots of ghosts or spirits that are just hanging around in Nefer-Khertet/twilight then I think you just do that with the blue book mechanics, minimal as they are, and treat it as an unmastered open rite, and save the Mastery for striking up a relationship with a particular ghost or spirit that you summon on a regular basis.
                                            So given that Sorcerers aren't really supposed to know a ton of Rites to begin with and that anyone can do an Open Rite, and that you're supposed to do the the rite the exact same way every time and the name of the being you're summoning is probably part of it, I personally would err on the side of making each type of interaction with a specific entity it's own Open Rite, and just using the blue book rules for them when applicable. If a sorcerer wanted to Master a particular rite, that's kind of like spending xp on an Ally, where they're deepening a relationship with a particular ghost or spirit.
                                            That's just me, though. If you want Sorcerers in your game to benefit as much as possible from Open Rite Mastery you can interpret the mechanics as liberally as you like.
