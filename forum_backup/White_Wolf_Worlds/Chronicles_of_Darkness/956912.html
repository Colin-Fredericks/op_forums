                                              As a child, I was extremely tiny, always. Lack of growth hormone meant that without treatment, all of my development would be delayed or not happen at all, including puberty. But as I said, I started treatment at four or five. I don't remember very many details from that time. I remember having like three or four blood tests every few months in the beginning, and my mother having to hold me down. I cried, a lot, and to this day (from that and the nightly injections) I have an extreme phobia of blood and of medical procedures involving needles. I remember the blood tests being longer than like. Blood tests you typically have as adults. It's possible my mind is just stretching them out, but I don't think so, because I specifically remember my mother bringing movies for me to watch. Like movies plural. So I'm pretty sure the blood tests went on longer than is typical. I've also read that when they're initially trying to detect those hormone levels, it's difficult because they secrete at one period during the day, so it's possible they extracted blood over a long period to compare the levels throughout the day.

                                              Anyway. It got so bad that I would cry and lock myself in rooms to avoid getting my shots, when I was maybe six or seven. My mother said we could stop taking them, and then I found out she used to come into my bedroom at night and give them to me while I was asleep, while also using a numbing spray.

                                              I first grew breasts at eight years old, and that brought about terrible street harassment from grown men. Keep in mind, at this point, I was still extremely short and I looked eight years old, or younger. At this point, my parents would still sit me in a stroller and sneak me into theme parks or pay a younger child's rate. I'm not mentioning this for people to start saying THEY don't prey sexually on children, so please, no one do that. I'm just telling you about my experiences. Thanks.

                                              Moving on. I first menstruated at ten years old, and my periods were always extremely terrible. I mean, like, the pain was so bad that I couldn't leave the bed for a week at a time, and if I had to go to school, I would stay at the nurse all day every day. This went on until I went on birth control for the pain, which I didn't do until university.

                                              The medicine was also extremely expensive, by the way, so I was fortunate that my family was pretty wealthy. I don't remember how old I was, but I remember at a point that our insurance wanted to stop covering the injectibles. My endocrinologist and my father (he's a lawyer) had this long battle with the insurance company, and they kept paying for it, but it's hard to have to travel with medicine that has to be kept cold. Needles on planes, all of this stuff. Most hotels provided fridges for free because it was a medical necessity.

                                              Now, I'll be twenty-three years old in September. I'm 4'9" and I weigh 82 lbs. People will sometimes still think I'm extremely young if their eyes just glaze over me or I'm with my parents. My baby sister is six years younger than me, and she's taller than I am. People will frequently say shit to me like, "I know you're disabled, but you're like a model anyway". Don't say that shit to people. That's harmful as hell.

                                              I still consider myself disabled. There are bit things and small things. Small thing--most cars where the passenger seat only turns on the airbag if a child's not sitting there decide it by weight, and I apparently don't weigh enough for the airbag to turn on, so that's kind of dangerous.

                                              But yeah. I know I started rambling by the end. Feel free to ask any questions.

                                              Personally, I think it should be a Condition, not a Merit, but I guess that also depends on whether or not you're talking about an actual disability like what I have, or just an adult who's kind of short.
