                    That's one aspect of them, and some are certainly exactly that, but I feel there is a strong element of spirituality in the Ordo Dracul still. First, basic research, while it can be aimed a certain way, is fairly unpredictable. The main goal is knowledge and you can't be certain what you're going to find. The goal of the Ordo Dracul isn't knowledge itself, it's transcendence. Knowledge, acquired though scientific methods, is just a means to an end. At the core, there is the idea, which you could call a belief or faith, that there is something beyond the vampiric condition that is attainable (be it gaining back one's humanity for the most idealistic of them, or just a vague notion of a better, more efficient predator, or some kind of demigod) and that the research has to be aimed that way.

                    I'm waiting to see what secrets of the covenants say about the 2ed version, but in 1ed, you had lots of very philosophical practices like following the dragon's tail, which I saw as some sort of way of meditating on the impact your actions have on the world. Plus, you have the division into "sworns", and the master/disciple dynamic that suggests an underlying philosophy. Honestly, the feeling I'm getting about the Ordo Dracul is more like Middle Age/Renaissance Alchemists looking for the philosopher's stone. From afar, it looks like it could be kind of scientific, but there's lots of weird ways of thinking, linking names to potential real effects, what a certain element looks like and what you think it could be used for depending on it... including the potential for the entire research to be a form of inner journey, with the elements, chemicals, and surgeries being nothing more than allegorical means of unlocking things within yourself.

                                                Speaking to the original question, I really think it entirely comes down to nerd subculture having an antagonistic view of organized religion and Christianity that makes something Christian-adjacent like the Lancea unappealing. On a basic level, they look like Vampire Christians, and everybody hates Christians, so they don't want to be like that. On a deeper level, there's a lot of organizational principles of faith-based organizations that work on the communal level like churches that, for whatever reason, does not seem to jive with the mindset of a lot of Vampire players, which IMO is a shame, because I think the Sanctified offer the most fertile ground for exploring what being a vampire might do to religious beliefs, or how someone seeking answers might be driven into the church. My kingdom for someone who wants to play a character eager to participate in church politics for reform reasons in a given domain.

                                                On the other hand, the Circle has things which are aesthetically appealing to a lot of vampire players: They're blood witches, pagans, counter-culture types with a supposedly fierce focus on individualism and a way of personalizing the mythology of a character. Fondly remember the Humanity you lose and live it up as a goddess of the night kind of thing. Which is very viscerally appealing to a lot of vampire players, I think. The key, in my view, is that it's appealing to players but it's worth thinking about how initially appealing it might be to characters. The Circle is very nakedly different and unfamiliar and inhumane in a lot of ways that might be harder for a regular person turned neonate to appreciate, so I don't think they're as easy a sell ideologically. I can imagine that a lot of characters would be like Loki in the Chicago domain, in that they sit on the edge of the Circle, because their laissez faire way of doing things appeals, but they might be afraid of diving deeper.

                                                I think exploring how the Sanctified support young Kindred and the transformation from scared person afraid of the night into an Anointed dark creature of God could be really interesting if a player had an interest in pursuing it. Most people I've played with have not really, so I suppose it's a thing I aspire to do.

You can be a follower of nearly any religion you want under the Circle of the Crone, and they seem to be much more lenient than the nigh impossible standards of fundamentalist Christianity.
