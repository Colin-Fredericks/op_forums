    Sorry for the double post, but I have some preliminary stuff on the Neolithic Beasts amongst the Vinca that I wanted to get out.

    *****

    Begotten Sons and Daughters

    Fear was the first language. Before words or even the relatively complex gesture-based vocabulary of the great apes, there was fear. Terror was the best teacher, and the lesson it taught was survival. Screams in the darkness reminded our earliest ancestors that it was best not to look into it, that staying together was the best bet for our continued existence. By the time of the Sundered World, the Primordial Dream is already fairly removed from general humanity, the development of language creating a buffer that pushed it up against the Anima Mundi. There are no recorded legends from this time, no stories of the monsters that haunted their nightmares, but fear of the dragon has always preceded the existence of one. Chambers in the Primordial Dream are born from the trauma of lost Integrity inflicted by a supernatural being, but Horrors and the Hearts of Lairs are born when time, fear, and storytelling allows that trauma to warp, to aggrandize, to teach. That hunter, trader, or lost wanderer made it back to their people and spread the story, and it changed in the telling. The Horrors wait just outside the light of the campfire, and the negative reinforcement of the terror and trauma of a few pulls the community closer together.

    By the time of the Sundered World, it had been nearly 193 000 years since our earliest relatives were born. 193 000 years to encounter Spirits, Restless Dead, and even the gods of the Border Marches. 193 000 years for Horrors to fester and grow. The Horrors are legion, waiting to feast, slumbering in the womb-egg-homes of their Hearts, and they are needed. Mother is calling, because the People are forgetting. They are forgetting fear, they are forgetting that simplest, first lesson. They are forgetting that there is a reason they are afraid of the dark. They look at their villages and hearth fires, they listen to the Wise, they gaze upon their fields, and they think they are safe. They have appeased the spirits of river and flame, they deal with the spirits of field and forest, they beat back the spirits of hunger and disease. They would wander out into the darkness, and they would see what lives there. The People would be devoured by what is waiting.

    Mother speaks: Teach them. Show them fear. Remind them of what they have forgotten. Make them wise.

    Here Be Dragons

    In the Sundered World, the Primordial Dream isn't quite so restricted and festering, the Bright Dream hasn't expanded far enough to crush it up against the Soul of the World. Communities and populations are small, still in keeping within the limits of how our brains define 'personhood'. We are equipped to handle about 150-200 people as actual living breathing human beings, and anything beyond that effectively becomes background noise. As populations expand, the Primordial Dream becomes cluttered by this static, to the point where only the Children and those who dream deep can even hear it anymore. Not in this age.

    In this age, the Dream is like a clear still pool with horribly dark depths- dreamers can see how deep it goes, the Horrors can find them with much less trouble. Because of this, it becomes far, far easier for one of the Begotten to feed. Any Beast character may feed by Inflicting Nightmares (Beast: The Primordial pg. 99) at any Satiety level voluntarily after successfully meditating, not just involuntarily while asleep when Ravenous or Starving. (Though that still happens) As a result, Neolithic Beasts tend to feed by Inflicting Nightmares while at High Satiety, and indulge their Hungers physically at Low Satiety. Those Begotten who still live in or around their communities tend to feed by Inflicting Nightmares, but are almost invariably driven away by Heroes or the Wise eventually. Most Neolithic Beasts have likely spent some time in the wilderness, fasting down to Ravenous or Starving as they make their way to a new community. Established Children, and especially Apexes or Beasts Incarnate, will often live near, but outside, a village of some kind- their powerful Lairs expanding their hunting grounds in the Dream.

    Because the Bright Dream is so relatively small in this era, and the Primordial Dream so expansive, the Horrors are farther reaching- counting the Temenos in general (outside of specific, human-related, Realms) as part of the Hive. What this translates to is the Begotten being able to take the form of their Horror outside of the Lair while wandering the Dream. The Forest-Wise will often stumble across the Children while roaming the far reaches of the Dream, and some even bargain with them for knowledge of how to sing to the Navel Stone and enter the Soul of the World.

    Children of the Gods

    The Border Marches are a hunter's paradise, and that is no different for one of the Begotten. Like any other otherworld, they may take up Lair Chambers here, and the Hunting Bonus conferred by Pangaea itself makes a fine Lair Trait (Depths 1-2 being Minor and Depths 3-4 being Major). Beasts can open Primordial Pathways to the Border Marches just as they may open them to anywhere else, though this is generally unnecessary as you can effectively walk your way into the hunter's paradise if you intend to. The Hunting Bonus may be used normally, and confers its dice bonus to rolls to use Nightmares, but not the dice qualities (such as 9-again or rote action); dice qualities still benefit Atavisms.

    Ruins of the Time Before also make fascinating Lair Chambers, especially since a Beast is naturally immune to their Lair Traits. Being immune to Broken Time could render a Beast far more durable against the magics of the Sky-Wise, just as being able to call it down could make them a terrible scourge upon humanity.

    The Begotten have an interesting relationship to the Pangaeans, especially since fear of them birthed so many Horrors over time. Many of the Children have Horrors that may strongly resemble a given Pangaean god, and such Beasts often consider the god in question their 'parent' or at the very least a consort of the Dark Mother. It is extremely easy to pick up Chambers in the wake of a god's visit to the Flesh, or gain Satiety through invoking holy terror, taking advantage of a Horror resembling a god could lead to offending said god. Pangaeans are more than an equal to the Begotten, and the last thing a Beast wants is an offended deity storming into their Lair and tearing it apart.

    The Broad Dreamers- Neolithic Heroes

    When the Dream was quiet and not filled with the chaos of millennia of myths and legends, Heroes were not affected by a constant stream of ideas telling them that their one and only duty was to calm the tempest within the Temenos by killing Beasts, they dreamed broad, but because of that they could read the passage of the Dream as surely as the Sky-Wise could account for the weather. A Hero was someone for the community to rally around, someone who could see the far-reaching view of the Primordial Dream and interpret the purpose of the nightmares. While the same things attracted the attention of Heroes, they knew better than to believe it was all about them. But should the Hero be unable to determine the lesson hidden within the nightmare, they still had a course of action left to them- seek out the Beast and ask them. The Hero was the interpreter of wisdom, but the Beast is the keeper of it, and while Heroes and Beasts would likely still never work together, their ideas on fear and its purpose very different, they could at the very least respect each other. The Beast knows fear in all its many flavours, they seek out their Kin to better understand it, and the Hero could depend on the Beast to know some obscure piece of lore that not even the Wise could uncover. Should all else fail, or a local Beast fall to the temptation of indulging its Hunger wantonly, the Hero could slay the Beast, becoming more powerful and more capable of defending their communities.

    Those Heroes who vainly believe that they are somehow better, those driven to obsessively hunt Beasts, still exist, but they are likely not accepted into the community the way that those Heroes who interpret nightmares are. These early precursors to Heracles, Beowulf, and others are often hunters and men of the woods- dangerous wandering travellers who are likely amongst the first to discover the arts of war.

            *****

            One Big Screwed Up Family

            The Begotten don’t yet identify themselves by Family, because they lack the mythic and folkloric framework to understand it. Most of the Children identify themselves as Kin based on the appearance of their Horrors, their Atavisms, and their Birthrights.

            Hunger Management

            A Beast in the Sundered World can effectively gorge themselves to their heart’s content. Capable of Inflicting Nightmares whenever they desire, establishing Family Ties with Uratha goes a long way to being able to benefit from Family Dinner whenever they want. Following along with mortal hunters is of similar benefit. Simply skulking around the Border Marches for long enough gives them exceptional opportunity to witness hunts and feedings. As a result, the Begotten of this era are far more liberal with using Satiety, making them comparatively more dangerous.

            Mother’s Watching

            In the modern day, many of the Children have had a brush with the presence of the Dark Mother at least once in their lives. In the Sundered World, where gods walk astride the earth and the Dream is a still dark pool, she is even more present in the lives of her Children. She is a dark presence, far more vast and terrible than even the greatest of Horrors, she is a whispering voice calling them into the warm embrace of the Dream, she is love and acceptance like they’ve never known, but she’s also a nightmare to nightmares, a horror beyond anything. You can see her, sometimes, writhing just beneath the surface of the Dream- down where even Beasts fear to tread. She is Primal Fear, cold and eternal, fierce and ravenous even by the standards of Beasts. The Dark Mother can be merciless. Like Urfarah, the Firstborn, and the Pangaean gods, she occasionally involves herself with her Children in a visceral and tangible way- and the Begotten shudder in ecstasy and horror in remembrance of those fleeting occasions.

            Inheritance

            All of the Inheritances are still present in the Sundered World, but it is worth acknowledging that in this time, where populations are smaller and more isolated and legends of mythic monsters spread like wildfire because the People are dead certain of the existence of monsters, that becoming a Beast Incarnate is much easier in this time. Some villages have a local Beast, and when that Child of the Dark Mother eventually spawns a Legend, they find themselves increasingly blessed by her power, capable of adopting the Beast-Shape.

            Aftershocks

            When the Border Marches collapse into the Gauntlet, the effects are felt across the otherworlds. This last bastion of primal wilderness- a crossroads between Flesh, Shadow, Depths, and Dream -was what enabled the Horrors to roam freely. When the Gauntlet rises, Beasts find they are unable to maintain the form of their Horror outside their Lair. They cannot feed by Inflicting Nightmares. Their ability to manage their Hunger suffers a dramatic blow. Beasts who found themselves able to survive with a light touch and minimal suffering are driven to indulge their Hungers, suddenly plaguing communities they used to have at least cordial relationships with; some unable to live with their situation fall to the Merger. Heroes, left without other options, kill as many Beasts as they can, gathering up Gifts. Their Integrity suffers, and many become wandering monster hunters, killing every Begotten in their path. This chaotic time will eventually lead to the stories that clutter the Dream and taint future Heroes.
