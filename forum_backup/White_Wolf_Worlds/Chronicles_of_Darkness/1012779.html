    Adding More Carrots to the Stew; or, Emphasizing the Roles of Orders

    12-05-2016, 05:32 PM
    So, after writing up my post in the Guardians thread, I figured I might as well have some fun with the idea here a little.

    Mage's design philosophy works way more with carrot than stick. I like this philosophy. I like encouraging action through reward, especially if the action leads to dramatic and interesting behaviours.

    So, my plan here is to produce a merit and Condition or three to try and encourage players to fulfill the roles that their Orders ordain. Not every Order needs these - at the very least, the Egregore's access to Advanced Libraries whenever and wherever a mystagogue is means they already have benefits here - but some could definitely benefit from it.

    ---

    The Adamantine Arrow - Oaths of Service

    The ideal Adamantine Arrow's words and actions and soul are one and the same. Though deception is a valid tactic, when an Arrow swears an oath and gives their word, they are bound to it, mind, body, and soul. It is by remaining proud and unbroken that an Arrow may fly straight and true.

    Oath of Service - ● to ●●●●●
    Prerequisites:     Adamantine Arrow Status ●, Gnosis equal or greater to the number of dots in this merit.

    The Arrow has, through secret Order initiations and sufficient meditation, practice, and endurance of ritual purification, learned how to bind their mind, body, and soul towards purpose. When an Arrow wishes, they may swear an Oath. While there are no formal requirements for any ritual attached to this merit (more than one Oath has been sworn in the heat of battle), most Arrows choose to make their vows relatively formal; swearing them in front of at least one other Awakened, often with a hand upon a symbolically appropriate object (like the tomb of an Adamant Sage, or the sword with which they intend to do the deed).

    As soon as the Oath is made, the Arrow gains the Oathbound Condition for the terms set.

    Drawback: The Oath is its own advantage and drawback. An Arrow may only have one Oath per dot in this merit.

    New Condition - Oathbound (Persistent)

    The Arrow has sworn an Oath. The terms of this Oath might be flowery but the intent should be summed up in a single sentence ("Defend the Hierarch from all enemies", "Kill the one who slew my master", "Never accept help for your injuries", etc.). Oaths are usually sworn towards ideals, not people (with those sworn towards a particular person usually sworn to their title or station, not the person themselves).

    If an Arrow ever breaks the terms of their Oath, either by failing or betraying their duties, or simply neglecting them for more than a week, they suffer a -2 to all magical actions (mundane ones are unaffected) as their mind, body, and soul move out of alignment. This penalty is cumulative with other broken Oaths, up to usual penalty maximums. It abates as soon as the Arrow makes some significant gesture of contrition or a major effort towards achieving their goal.

    If an Arrow upholds their Oaths, however, an Oath may act as an Order tool Yantra in any spell working to uphold or further the Oath in question.

    Beat: The Arrow gains an A-Beat any time they put aside an obligation, risk a betrayal, put themselves in harms way, or otherwise act to uphold their Oath in the face of significant personal cost or risk of harm.

    Resolution: The Oath ends only when an Arrow completes their duty or that duty becomes impossible. If an Arrow swears an Oath to a figure, that person may release them from their Oath if desired (though an Arrow may refuse this freedom and often does).

    ---

    Sidebar: Ending Oaths?

    The wording here is deliberately set that Arrows cannot renege on their Oaths. An Oath is not something you can just agree to act on when desirable or convenient; it is a vow marked into your very soul. Such things are not easily undone.

    In practice, Oaths may be unmade by a Master of Fate, though most Arrows consider such an act to be as blasphemous as removing a Mage's soul. Likewise, stories persist of Arrows who, recognizing the error of their ways, used a new Oath to cancel out an old one. Such tales usually carry cautionary warnings that they often drive the one who swears the Oath mad.

    In your games, though, your players may find that maintaining a certain Oath just isn't fun anymore. If that happens, don't be afraid to exploit loopholes in the Oath or let it "evolve" by allowing the player to swear a new Oath expanding on the old one. Fun comes before mythic significance.

    ---

    This is the first part. Coming up next: Guardian Dossiers and Thearch Commands.

      Guardians of the Veil - Secret Dossiers

      The Guardians of the Veil are both the answer to "Quis custodiet ipsos custodes?" and the cause for the question. An Order of spies, infiltrators, and shadowy agents, all watching their fellow Awakened for signs of moral flaw, the Guardians of the Veil do their work by keeping abridged of what other sorcerers are up to.

      To this end, the Guardians of the Veil have developed techniques for maintaining mental maps of the Awakened they are responsible for watching over. This information is shared with the Caucus and they, in turn, have information shared with them. The result is that a Guardian can maintain a sizeable mental dossier on the Awakened of their Consilium and beyond.

      Dossier - ● to ●●●●●
      Prerequisites: Guardians of the Veil Status ●, Trained Memory (GotV, pg 46) or Eidetic Memory

      You maintain a dossier; a collection of mental documents on the Awakened in your region. This might take many forms but word association codes and memory palaces are common enough methods to be considered the norm. Many Guardians incorporate details into poems.

      Having a dossier allows the Mage to make appropriate rolls (Int + Wits or Composure) to recall details about who a given Mage is, even if they've never met before, as the Mage remembers some details from their information network. They add their Dossier dots as a bonus to this roll.

      In addition to general information, every Guardian who maintains a dossier also has deeper details. Once per chapter per dot of Dossier, the Guardian in question may gain the Informed Condition about a given Mage or local Caucus, representing the deep insights the Mage has. Other Awakened are often surprised and disturbed when a Guardian they've never met can recite their home address.

      Drawback: Your Dossier requires up-keep by both yourself and others. Your character must faithfully both maintain good standing with their Caucus and contribute information to their superiors' own Dossiers. Falling out of favour can mean losing dots from this merit, or even losing it entirely.

      ---

      Sidebar: How Much Is Too Much?

      Guardians of the Veil are not omniscient. While they might be creepily informed of a character's habits, if the Guardians really did know what everyone was up to at all times, there'd not be much of a game, now, would there? The information gleaned by rolls or Conditions derived from this merit should give Guardian players a chance to feel like a spy who has dirt on just about everyone, and to reward them for using it.

      In other words, let information from this provide new angles or leverage, but don't solve the whole mystery with it unless it's just a side thing, y'know?

      Also, not all knowledge is equal nor are all Mages equally paranoid. Feel free to apply a penalty when targeting some Awakened who are especially private or whose natures are Occluded.

          The Silver Ladder - Supreme Command

          It is the role of the thearch to lead. While it would be wrong to say the form does not matter, thearchs take charge. They are spiritual guides, moral teachers, judges, heads of state; those who take on the thankless burden of leadership and become the head that guides the body politic.

          Heavy is the head that wears the crown but it does wear it and it is a crown. Thearchs who take on a role of leadership find themselves empowered by such things. Their own souls find it easier to climb the Ladder of Being every time they help another to take that next step.

          Command - ●●
          Prerequisites: Silver Ladder Status ●, Inspiring ●●●

          Those who take up the Silver Mantle find respite and resolve in reaching out to others. It is when they inspire others that they themselves feel inspired; it is when others look to them for leadership that the thearch feels a certainty in their soul that they are a leader. This cyclical empowerment gives thearchs the strength they need to lead by example in moments of crisis.

          Once per chapter, when a thearch with this merit uses Inspiring, they may draw Supernal inspiration from their own words as well. If the Mage succeeds in inspiring or guiding others, they gain the Inspired Condition as well towards the same action, which, when resolved, provides an A-Beat.

          If the Condition makes no sense for the thearch (the Mage inspiring others to take an action the Mage themselves cannot, say), they simply regain a point of Willpower and receive an A-Beat.

                          While I appreciate that it'd be great to give the Arrow some more potency with their Oaths, these merits are already XP sinks. Having to buy multiple oaths could get painful for a player. Still, if you want...

                          ---

                          Soul-felt Oath - ● to ●●
                          Prerequisites: Must be sworn to at least one Oath

                          Not all Oaths are created equal. In an ideal, pure, Supernally-inspired World, this would not be the case, but in the Fallen World, some things are more defining than others; more resonant with the Awakened soul. An Arrow whose Shadow Name's symbolism bonds more strongly with an Oath, or who has a greater sympathy to its target, or who has simply held an Oath for long enough finds it more resonant with their soul; a thing which is easier to uphold and harder to break.

                          When taking this merit, choose an Oath the character possesses. For every dot in this merit, the yantra bonus that Oath provides increases by +1. In addition, the penalty for violating that Oath deepens by -1.

                          This merit may be taken multiple times, but only once (up to the maximum number of dots) for a given Oath. If the Oath is fulfilled, the character may regain the XP spent on this merit, as per Sanctity of Merits, or transfer it to another Oath (including one freshly sworn on completion of the old one; many Arrows will, on finishing a grand quest, often swear to the next one pretty quickly).

                          ---

                          Sidebar: Oaths In Conflict

                          Sometimes, Oaths come into conflict. Generally speaking, this actually isn't an issue; most Oaths are in some way complementary, and as such rarely will Oaths themselves conflict. It does still happen, though. An Arrow detective who has sworn to never drop a case until it is solved, and an Oath of fealty to the Hierarch, faces a conundrum when said Hierarch orders them to drop a case for political reasons.

                          If the Arrow breaks one Oath to obey the other, penalties should apply as usual, rules-as-written. However, STs are advised to be lenient: Unless the situation that arises is a truly make-or-break character moment, when the PC must decide what matters most, try not to force false dilemmas. In the above scenario, maybe consider letting the Arrow be creative. Their loyalty to the Hierarch might require them to ignore their order in favour of upholding the Lex, for example, for they are loyal to the position and not the person. This can feel unsatisfying, since it's bending the rules a bit, but needs must.

                          Ideally, a good ST avoids engineering scenarios where Oaths themselves conflict. The Oath itself should bring the Arrow into conflict, not having two Oaths that run counter to one another. As ever, player-ST cooperation to keep this from happening is invaluable.

                          As an optional rule, though, Soul-felt Oaths might be considered more "important" to an Arrow's sense of self than lesser ones. Violations of lesser Oaths to serve a more powerful Soul-felt Oath may not count as violations, so long as no other alternative exists. If the Arrow could have taken a third course of action and violated neither Oath, they suffer the consequences.

                          ---

                          I might boost Command a bit, though, thinking on it; either cutting the cost to one dot or improving the bonus somewhat. Maybe replace Inspired with Steadfast or something. I'm still having to think on it.
