    So You've Captured a Seer, Now What?

    07-23-2016, 12:18 AM
    If a pentacle cabal captures a seer what should they do?

    The answer probably depends on what the circumstances were so I'll break it down by categories.

    It was an accident: The cabal cast a sleep spell (or other incapacitating spell) on a large area for an unrelated purpose and discover they accidentally caught a seer.

    They were provoked: A seer attacked them and they won.

    It was a misunderstanding: The cabal was embedded in a fight between sleepers for some reason, and incapacitated a seer who was on the other side.

    Side question: if you realize there is another mage (pentacle or seer) on an opposing side in a battle mostly consisting of sleepers what do you do.

    I'm curious what people think because I am planning an unusual chronicle where these situations might come up a few times.

      I don't think it's all that unusual. Your players have to be creative in the ways they handle these situations. That's part of the horror. I have a story of my PCs unexpectedly and horrifically torturing a Sleeper for info on a mission they had undertaken. A Sleeper who didn't know anything at all (not that the other way would have excused it).

      Mages are not superheroes. This is a horror game. Don't get rid of it. Embrace it. Start fights with a lot of Sleepers around, make it so that they can't default to magic with so many Reaches.

      (And the answer to the first question depends a lot on the context, so I'm afraid I don't have an answer for you there.

      But embrace the horror. That's my advice.)

              I hate to say it, but how a cabal reacts to a Seer should depend on context. You've given us some, but there's more required.

              Something to remember as a background context is that there is theory and practice and the two are distinctly different. In theory, Seers and the Pentacle are in a state of constant war with one another and enemies to the end; the only good Seer is a dead Seer. A good Atlantean (or libertine) gets as much information and as many resources out of a Seer captive as possible before disposing of them in a timely, efficient, and clean manner, free of sadism and cruelty.

              Practice isn't theory, though. The war between the two sides is very real and shaped by circumstance. How a Seer is captured generally matters less than the context they were captured in, but not entirely.

              If the local Atlanteans are more powerful than the local Seers to a significant degree (i.e. outnumbering the Seers 10 to 1 and with far more powerful elders), the Atlanteans may have carte blanche to do with the Seer captive as they see fit. They're an enemy of the Awakened Nation and not a part of the local Consilium, so the local law probably says you can hang them from a meat hook if you want to. Every Seer is a potential murderer and possibly an in-fact murderer. Torture is still frowned upon when done without purpose, but, in the end, they're a literal insurgent faction; few people are going to rake you over the coals and fewer still are going to investigate in the first place.

              If the Seers are more potent, though, there's political nuance to be considered. Any act taken against the Seers will have consequences. If you took a captive as a part of an ongoing campaign, that's fine; it probably falls under the same category as above, though you may be expected to share what you find with the Consilium at large. If you took them captive as a part of a dispute known and recognized between two specific sub-groups of each faction, you'll be expected to act with honour and decorum; fighting only to get what the dispute was about. If it's to the death, it's to the death, but if it isn't, killing them will be bad for you. There might even be formal agreements specifically for sorting out this sort of thing between the local Pylon(s) and Consilium.

              And if the Seers are more powerful, well... Capturing someone is bad for you and killing them's worse. Being noticed at all is a bad thing. Then it's more of a hostage situation; releasing them only - only - when you have Fate-bound oaths, sworn on their part in the name of the Exarchs, to let you be. Or, if you're the insurgent faction now, you use them to drive as many long knives into as many Seers as possible, because, no matter what you do, a bunch are about to be turned on you. Get gone.

              And then you have the personal context. What do the characters want? And who is this Seer to them? Young characters are probably the most eager to put a dagger in them for the Atlantean cause but the least likely to do so. Older characters, who recognize political nuance, are more likely to have lost friends, maybe even family, to the Seer cause and seen just what the Seers are really like. The more experienced they are, the more likely they are to realize killing is a bad idea, but also the more likely they are to want to do so.

              So, tl;dr: What will the political repercussions be of any given action and how much do the characters want to make the captive bleed?

                              I think he was hitting at something different;
                              Consider it like this - for many Mages, belonging to one of the sects is like...well...belonging to a club, religion, whatever organization; you either are one of them by following their rules, or you don't and you are not. If you are a sinner, you might get removed from the society, or be refused certain services (example: certain politicians might be refused holy communion if they publicly support something considered to be a sin).
                              So if you are a Seer, a Nameless, or an Apostate? We are not obliged to extend our services to you, we don't have to protect you, and if you are one of us, you may say "I'm not anymore" and lose all of your benefits.

                              Silver Ladder doesn't think like that.
                              They consider all Awakened to be One Nation. Nation has certain obligations towards it's citizens. Even if they don't pay taxes, try to become independent, or murder people, they are still citizens of that nation, and that nation has to respect these rights, these obligations. From Nation's point of view, you can't quit being a citizen by yelling loudly, and even if you do, as it's member there are things it simply cannot stop, even if you don't want them.
                              So for a Adamantine Arrow, that Seer is an outsider, someone who isn't one of us, and thus can be punched, even if we don't punch others of our kind.
                              For a Silver Ladder, that Seer is still one of ours, and we still have our obligations towards him.*

                              This is probably why Consilia don't devolve into tyrannical oligarchies based on pure Strong Beats The Weak.

                              EDIT
                              So, a very simplified and shallow read, but:
                              For Arrows, Seers are rivals. Rivals are to be defeated.
                              For Guardians, Seers are dangerous neighbours. These are to be kept in check and outsmarted, sometimes - cooperated with.
                              For Mysterium...Iit's too complicated.
                              For Free Council, they are enemies. Not rivals. Enemies. Enemies are to be DESTROYED.
                              For Silver Ladder, they are misguided faction. They are to be integrated.

                              *This is, of course, sneaky. They assume that, by default, you are One Nation, and thus, have to follow One Nation's Laws. So for Silver Ladder? Every Mage should follow Lex Magica, and they are not allowed to disagree. A Ladder-Centric Consilium would probably enforce it's laws even on those who aren't members, because they decided that all are members.
