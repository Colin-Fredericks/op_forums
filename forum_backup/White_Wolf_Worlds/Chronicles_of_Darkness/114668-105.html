    I'd say your first priority in deciding what should be the case is what you feel would work best for your game. If you want the cabals to be interacting in some way or another as regards local Mysteries or resources, make them a part of a Consilium. If you want to encourage more of a feeling of isolation, don't have a Consilium at all. It's such a small group to begin with, everyone knows one another, and there wouldn't really be much of a need for a Consilium to resolve disputes.

    If the big Mystery of the region affects everything along the coast, that'd be more incentive to have a Consilium. One of my games is set in Oregon and there is a state-wide Mystery called the Great Western Whorl that makes state-wide cooperation pretty important (if I'd had the Silver Ladder book when I started the game, I'd have simply made Convocations in the state reasonably common but live and learn). However, if the Awakened there don't need to discuss Mysteries - maybe the guys up the coast are spying on a Supernal Verge, while those way down the coast are chasing swamp vampires - then there's no need for a Consilium; the two groups might as well not exist from the perspective of the rest.

    Another possibility is that there's simply an agreement for each cabal to have a leader or representative meet to discuss things and have a round-table rather than a Consilium. If every cabal has at least one thearch member in it, this process would be decidedly easier to produce.

                                    (EDIT: I wrote up some houserules for expanding the Duel Arcane in response to this, feel free to check them out: http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...l-arcane-rules )

                                    Originally posted by rwknoll View Post
                                    Bump? Maybe it's not a simple question? :/
                                    If you're reading this DaveB, I and others would love to see some clarification of the Duel Arcane when the Mage FAQ comes out! Until then, here's an effortpost about my interpretation of it. (now that it's no longer a conflict of interest for me to answer)

                                    First:
                                    A dueling character may not use Yantras or spend Reach beyond that she gains for Arcana mastery, and must still account for spell factors and resistance.
                                    The conceit of the Duel Arcane in 2E is that you're (dramatically and poetically) modeling a specific spell effect which you are capable of actually casting, it's not like the 1E Duel where it's a totally abstract display of power. I believe the intent of this sentence here is that you should look to a spell which you could actually cast, spend 1 Reach on Instant casting, and then maybe 1 Reach on sensory range if necessary? You could also assume that you're casting as an aimed spell, but that may be a moot point because you aren't actually completing the spell and so you wouldn't need to make a "throwing" roll. I suppose it depends on how far the attacks/defenses are going? Depending on how you read it, it could be that you need to fully cast and complete the effect, it just doesn't actually have a mechanical impact because it's purely in the Supernal World.

                                    Possibility 1: it's as simple as Attribute+Arcanum for all attacks and defenses.
                                    Possibility 2: you account for Reach as if actually casting the spell you're using, which might require a Gnosis+Athletics/Firearms-Defense roll for your Attack to make contact if you're not devoting Reach to Sensory Range. If the spell effect you want to recreate is Withstood, you may need to take dicepool penalties on your Attack or Defense as usual to raise Potency, and the Attack/Defense fails if Potency isn't high enough.

                                    Possibility 2 would raise some issues because, since the spells aren't actually taking effect, there would be no real reason not to use 1 or 2 dot spells that aren't Withstood. But that might just be a flavor issue.

                                    ---

                                    Second: I think your initial read is correct, there is no target number or Defense or Withstand rating or anything like that, only the other mage choosing to Defend on their turn. The way the rules are written suggest to me that you only add extra successes as dice on your next roll (whether Attack or Defense) if you Attacked. Fortune favors the bold.

                                    Third: I don't see why you couldn't spend WP in the Duel. Usually the rules will explicitly state when you can't spend WP on a roll, such as for Wisdom degeneration, so I assume it's possible by default. I think it's a feature, not a bug, that the Duel favors high Resolve+Composure. It's no different from a gunfight favoring high Dexterity+Firearms, except that it requires less Skill specialization and so is open to more characters.

                                    Fourth: Again, I don't think the Duel is different from any other roll that has exceptional success/dramatic fail and ensuing Conditions. Tilts and Conditions usually make combat and other challenges more exciting and dynamic.

                                    ---

                                    Having said all that, when you look at the rules, there's an emergent strategy for the Duel that isn't really called out explicitly.

                                    When you make an attack roll and fail, you can't use that Arcanum to attack again until the Door in question opens. This implies that it's possible to put your opponent in check by blocking all of their attacks until they run out of Arcana to use. If they can't use their Arcana to attack, they either have to Negotiate and offer a concession (probably a big one since they have almost no leverage) to open your current Door, or Defend until you're placed in the same position as they are.

                                    That, along with the possibility of imposing Tilts (a magical version of Arm Wrack, for instance), would seem to make the Duel more dynamic.
