            Well known is a tricky concept. Even with a TV show for them, what the public sees isn't necessarily most of their reality, unless you believe that TV shows are accurate depictions of reality. By the way, distinct TV shows can have wildly different takes on the same group.

            Originally posted by nofather
            But the activity is, outside of some exceptions, pretty much all the same. Hunting and killing supernaturals. If supernaturals are open to the public, they can call cops like the next person, providing cops will respond to them.
            To be precise: I think the activity of hunting supernaturals would be not only well known, but actually obvious to expect. Indeed, it would have at first its greatest surge of inductees of all time. A world of known supernatural will be a world where everyone have the most basic knowledge needed to make the decision to take the Vigil, so yes, everyone with the will to do so will act upon it, and you can expect a lot of people in this.

            But what isn't so simple are the groups themselves. First of all, because they are criminals, almost all of them. They are institutions of humans, so they are just common people that joined those groups and accepted to do their things. And them we have a long list of crimes. The simpler ones are just illegal watchers, doing police job, and sometimes executions, without state consent. The Conspiracies are all in dirtier mud. Cheiron Group does illegal research, Task Force Valkyrie is a paramilitary group leeching the resources of the Pentagon, and so on and so forth. Certainly some of them can get the upper hand on some scenarios, but they have simply too much to explain to simply go out.

            AND, of course... you can simply ignore your previous history, get your buddies and pretend that you are only now going in for the Vigil. But that isn't the same, because to go out in public means to make a lot of compromises, and those compromises depend on a lot of things. It isn't anymore just your intention toward the unknown. It is now also your intention toward the known and toward the society and its expectations. TFV would probably be dismantled because it is too much a liability, but it would be relatively simple to create internal procedures to absorb both its agents, its archives and its assets into the Pentagon proper and them simply create another, public, Task Force. But them, this new TF for dealing with the supernatural would have a change in policy to account for the new expectations created by the state of things. The end result will still be a Task Force in the US military with the resources to deal with supernatural threats. But how they perform this, or even their new definition of "supernatural threat" can be wildly distinct from the original TFV. And maybe the original TFV remains in the shadow precisely to keep their ways.

            Imagine this for each group of Hunters. Maybe the Union just become a political party who advocate for laws to protect against the supernatural. Maybe two, with extremists now having reason to left the main group (or a minority of non-extremists doing so). The Cheiron Group would have to find ways to legalize its discoveries quickly, before they lose the monopoly over supernatural research. Actually it works for supernatural groups, too. What changes about the Hunters is that with other splats you have one reception for the groups and other for the splat itself. For Hunters there are no "splat itself", so only the destinies of the groups matter.

            Originally posted by nofather
            To be fair, I didn't say easier time, I said well received.
            Ok, got it. I'm not sure the "main" groups aren't able to be as well received, though. I just think about some Storm Lords breaking a deal with Discovery for half a dozen shows. The Iron Masters would probably have an easier time making contact with human institutions, anyway, from the mob to the police, with Hollywood in the middle. For Uratha, I do think the Forsaken would have an easier time and be more well received, because they're more probable to simply keep to their human lives and from there carve their niche.

            Now that I thought about it, I think that who would really have better acceptance among human society would be the Ghost Wolves. And for Mages, the local minor Orders, not Seers nor Pentacle to be fair.

            Originally posted by malonkey1
            Well, you could hide the magic parts from non-magical end-users. Forces-Mind-enhanced servers, Fetish-based security systems, voluntary Embrace/Ghouling for wealthy clients, Changeling Pacts for beauty and strength in exchange for cold, hard cash, etc.
            Yes, welcome to the Sixth World, the setting of Shadowrun. Sooner or latter the supernatural will be integrated, and them the lines between supernatural societies among themselves and with human institutions will blur even more. The Seers can actually lose more in philosophical and religious terms as common (and ruthless) Corporations are more efficient at their game: profit, and hire people.

            Originally posted by Vent0
            Changelings still need to worry about the Gentry, and mortal support isn't really going to make that much difference.
            Can't be so sure, but indeed it raises a lot of questions. The Changelings are maybe the most probable to be accepted and helped by society, because they're first and foremost victims. And the mortal institutions are likely to throw a lot of their time and resources into the problem of the Gentry. Those are real threats, beings from another plane invading the world to take away its people. So, even if at first the mortal institutions don't have the means to protect them, to deal with the Gentry is probably going to be a high priority mission for most of them.

            Originally posted by malonkey1
            To be honest, in a Revealed world, I don't think it would be too hard to get support for an all-out war on the Gentry (as hilariously one-sided as that would be in the Fair Folk's favor). They're nearly invisible to the Gentry, hard as nails, and have a pretty wide bag of tricks, so if anyone is equipped to run recovery in Arcadia, it would be the Prometheans.
            Maybe it isn't even as one-sided. First because the Fair Folk have as a rule that they can't overwhelm in a conflict. As veiled as it is, they need to always set an achievable winning condition for the other side. Second because with this unveiled world, information will spread. So each time someone finds a weapon against the Gentry, everyone knows it. Research teams will be able to actually make the statistical numbers and define among the suspicions of the Changelings what really can be true about the True Fae, or at least point a more sensible line of investigation. Not that humanity is always a cooperative bunch, but in such a case it is probable to become an effort of almost all of Earth institutions in cooperative research for means to defend our world.

            Just don't think the Courts are going to be like religions. Their primary purpose is to offer protection against the Gentry, and it is likely to be still the same. They could become the nexuses of the numerous Task Teams over the world committed to fight the "Gentry Threat". An internal culture is already a common element on any organization, and they will still retain theirs, but with the new developments, it is impossible to know ahead of time how they will change internally. But overall they will be institutions for protection, research and fight against the Threats from the Gentry and their related Realms.

                                                            It would be impossible to control. At least bribes have a trail of money and services. A vampire just has to be in a room with someone in order to compromise them. A mage doesn't even require that, and a vampire with certain disciplines can get away with it as well. The governments of the world would be effectively powerless in the case of supernaturals going public until an arrangement was made for them that, ideally, prompted the supernaturals into going with the flow as much as possible. They could tack new laws but nothing could realistically enforce them except other supernaturals, and that's not something that would be guaranteed.

                                                            Most of our laws work as a deterrent. If you get caught, you could get fined, you could be sent to jail and have your normal life ruined. But most supernaturals are already living lives that force them to avoid a normal life and hide from the law.. If you threaten a vampire with prison, or the Final Death, what's to keep them from just saying 'screw it,' turning on obfuscate and living like they did before the Masquerade fell, hidden and in the shadows? A werewolf can just find a new territory or escape into the Shadow until the heat dies down.

                                                            I'm sure some governments could get away with something to pacify some of the supernaturals, like offering vampires 'All Access' passes to prisoners if, in exchange, they performed services for the government.
