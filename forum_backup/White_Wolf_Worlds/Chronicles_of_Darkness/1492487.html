    Alternative Integrity traits for splats

    09-07-2022, 02:40 AM
    I was wondering how changing the Integrity-like traits of splats, by switching them with those from other splats or creating new ones, would affect the themes and focus of respective game lines.
    I think some combinations may actually make sense and even have interesting results.

    I'll start :
    Demon: Memory (aka Programming?)

    What if the Fall wasn't a specific event, but a gradual experience? Just as Memory is, among other things, a measure of a mummy's sense of identity and free will against being a slave to the Judge's will, Demons too may struggle to gradually letting go of the God Machine 's chains, with the risk of relapsing

      Hmm...

      Vampire — Cover: Don't let people know you are an undead predator. Twist is that 'people' includes yourself. Fake it till you make it.

      Demon — Pilgrimage: Pave your own road to Hell with good intentions. Experience the full human existence, with all its zeniths and nadirs.

      Promethean — Stability: You are unborn, incomplete, unstable. Ground your fires with hope for tomorrow, and occasionally venting out some steam.

      Deviant — Clarity: It happened, and you did it. Don't let Them tell otherwise. Take control of your own past to seize the future, this time on your terms.

      Changeling — Memory: Remember what life was like, before it all went widdershins. Remember yourself so that you become yours, not Theirs.

      Mummy — Wisdom: Reflect upon the cruelty you and your masters inflict on the world. Lament, repent, and take responsibility.

      Mage — Satiety: Feast upon the world's Mysteries and lose yourself in trance. Fast until your Obsessions take a life on their own to push you to the next Mystery.

      Beast — Synergy: Cooperate with your Horror Within such that you may better understand, and eventually shape, the stories you tell and hear about yourself.

      Geist — Harmony: Stay too dead, and you risk being stuck. Stay too alive, and you risk being powerless. Always remember you are both.

      Werewolf — Humanity: The Wolf must Hunt, but the Man must Hold. Hold on to what you held dear, even if the Hunt washes it away.

          Humanity-Arguably all of them, but if we had to get picky, Promethean, Changeling, Beast, Hunter, and Mage are probably the games where this'd be the most useful and interesting. The laziest answer is Promethean, but that doesn't make it inappropriate.

          Harmony-Any splat that deals with a more overt duality benefits from this, so arguably Geist, Changeling, Beast are easy correlations on that front. Interesting cases could be made for Mage and Promethean as well, depending on how you read their journey. Still, Geist is the appropriate (if easy) mark, so.

          Wisdom-Just about any splat that has to deal with power as a mechanical theme warrants it, so Demon and Mummy are easy picks. It also does a good job reflecting the personal responsibility any Beast has over the managing of their developing lair and the consequences of not keeping a firm grip on it, so there's that. Interesting things also happen with Werewolf and Hunter, but because it's not only appropriate but interesting, I think Beast is the clear winner on this one.

          Pilgrimage-Pilgrimage is a good stat for anyone with a larger scale goal consideration, or otherwise inverts the spending argument by starting low and ideally trending high. Changeling, Demon, Geist, and Mummy all fit in that model pretty well. There's an interesting take of Deviant as well that could come out of that, particularly with some Light of the Sun context. Arguably, Geist is the one where reaching the end through growth matters most, but since I'm trying to keep a complete shuffle, Demon is the more interesting one of the remaining.

          Clarity-"Honey, where are my keys/pants?" Anyhow, Mage's relationship to vision makes that a superficially easy pick, but actually engaging with the deeper ideas....Hunter and Deviant also make for interesting relationships. Mummy also plays nicely. There's a really wild version of Beast that emerges from this take, but out of those, I think Mummy's relationship with the Judges and everything behind and around that makes this the winner.

          Coded Integrity-Arguably, the more "human" the splat, the more appropriate. Mage is easy in that regards, Vampire and Geist are also considerations, as is Deviant. There's something interesting in Changeling also engaging with it. That said, Mage engaging with something like the Code and then falling away from it is the most interesting, I think.

          Synergy-See the overt duality argument, and be unsurprised to see Werewolf, Changeling, and Beast here, Vampire too, now that I dwell on it. Mummy to, at an odd angle. We'll go lazy and say Werewolf.

          Memory-Memory plays well when the sands of time should be a thing to consider, so Vampire and Demon play nice here. Changeling too, particularly since it can more immediately play into the relevances of their day to day life. Geist can get interesting with this via the lens of death. Finally, there's a whole section in a potential Chronicler's Guide for a Beast with Memory. Honestly though, there's something delicious for the fairy tale to interact with Memory, and it has thematics that can play nicely with what Clarity was doing, so I think I'd have to give this to Changeling.

          Cover-It's all about who's interested in drawing up lines and veils and masks and distance. Changeling and Hunter come to mind, as does Deviant. Vampire could easily translate it over into the Mask and Dirge dichotomy. Geist has some tangential relationship via Hide From Reapers, but that's a little weak. Of these, though, Deviant's probably the one that most benefits from having multiple of the same meter, and the way Variations can easily degrade those makes a neat tie in.

          Satiety/Kinship-Arguably another "all of them case", but the combo of balancing hunger and close relationships is so obviously an ur-sample for Conviction and Loyalty to draw from that Deviant stands out. Werewolf and Mage also have some easy draws. Same with Hunter. Vampire is also a great place ot see how this archetypal take works-in fact, so much so, I'mma give it to Vampire.

          Stability-Just about anything that calls for constant stressors works here, so Hunter, Changeling, and Promethean are all easy picks here. Werewolf and Geist also have interesting consequences here as well. Still, Hunter probably the one that gets the most out of more directly interacting with their psychological state as damage, so here we are.

          Obviously, about half of this is sincere, if short, consideration, and the other half is really lazy spitballing.

              My take from this is actually that not only any splat could work with any of them, if some requiring more work or lending itself to more specific themes, but that actually making adaptations so any splat may work under any Integrity-like stat has another benefit: it makes for an easier time defining a theme for your chronicle while keeping it open to mixed splats (as either PCs or NPCs).

              Tying the stat to the chronicle means that you have a solid core theme, and you can welcome different splats as they add their views to the same theme instead of despite the theme. Everyone uses Humanity and you have a game about the need to stay connected to the species you don't belong to anymore. Everyone uses Wisdom and you have a game about how distinct ways to power still hold the same basic ethical questions on using it. Everyone uses Satiety and you have indeed a game about monsters forming kinship over the common experience of living with unnatural needs, even hunters.

                                          Originally posted by moonwhisper View Post
                                          * Honestly I don't see how Changelings and Deviants tracking psychological deterioration as health boxes instead of the usual Integrity-like dots benefits in terms of game themes. Sometimes it feels like 2e just added more complexity for the sake of it.
                                          Smaller problems that can escalate to larger problems. Mild Clarity damage can be cleared all at once through interaction with a Touchstone where severe damage is closer to actual dot-loss in other games, and Stability is built to have problems get more and more severe instead of relying on the equivalent of a dramatic failure to get the sort of issues it already models as new or worsened Scars, to say nothing of how having a variable maximum rating prioritizes pursuit of certain Traits in a way that simply buying up Integrity dots wouldn't.

                                          The two splats whose Integrity analogue is a damage track are also the two for which loss of rating is most common; Satiety and Harmony come the closest, and the former doesn't degrade that fast unless you're actively burning it up, while the latter tilts itself toward one end or the other. People already have game-psych issues with having to spend Experiences to raise Integrity in the games where it's there to be lost down to a stable level for your playstyle, and a game where casual pings to your rating are not disastrous benefits more from a framework where you don't have to weigh minor damage as "I've lost a dot in a trait in this system where losing dots in traits is a pretty big deal."

                                                  To add to what Satchel said, I find the 'damage track' model of things more "complexity that actually adds to the game," than, "complexity for complexity's sake."

                                                  Humanity is.. really more complicated than it needs to be. The entire system is just a reason to roll dice that, over time, basically don't matter compared to player intentions. If you want to play a high Humanity vampire, or you want to play a vampire slowly descending to monstrosity, or a vampire that goes down to the brink and then struggles back up, the math of the system is easy enough to manipulate that the vast majority of the time you'll hit the character arc you want your PC to go through. The odds of rolling so poorly that you hit Humanity 0 are very low, and the number of Beats you earn along the way can easily pay for any Humanity dots you want to buy and possibly have Beats left over. Well before this thread I've posited that Satiety is actually a much better model for vampires and the five Condition stages can take the place of the effects of various levels of Humanity if written correctly.

                                                  You can, quite easily (it's even in the VtR 1e Chroniclers Guide) simply Humanity a lot without losing anything in the end result. You only have to deal with the subjectivity of play experience (such as Humanity being rolled making it feel riskier than it really is).

                                                  I'll speak to Clarity as I have way more experience with it. What the "Integrity as Health" model does, for the cost of some complexity, is allow a lot more variability to the immediate mental state of changelings without having to shift their "rating" around a lot (as happens with Harmony and Satiety as Satchel noted). Having a "mental health" track lets you have an idea of how much stress your changeling can endure, while treating threats to their stability as damage lets bounce around with bonuses/penalties as it comes and goes. While there's certainly some discussion to be had around the specific math of CtL 2e in this regard, the results are a lot more elastic: your character can get bent and stretched a lot under stress, but return to normal fairly quickly, but there's always the risk of getting stretch too far and snapping.

                                                      I have an entire home for which this is a central change for everything major supernatural type in my Chronicles. This homebrew is listed as Frailty Chronicles in my signature.

                                                      Each dot in what I call Stability Advantage (Short hand generic for listing Integrity, Wisdom, Clarity, Humanity Harmony, and Synthesis) represents a relationship with an NPC which gives mechanical benefits and is susceptible to being damaged as the PC acts monstrously and gains Atrocity. I have a whole system how maintenance of these relationships both keep the character sane and helps them avoid scenes and conditions where those that hunt monsters threaten there existence. It plays especially well with the masquerade themes, and avoiding huntsman themes, but overall I feel makes everything better. For werewolves and sin-eaters they have two 5 point Stability Advantage, one represents relationships with humans and the other minor spirits/ghost which represents aspects of the supernatural ecology of their territory (respective to the type). Thus having high harmony/synthesis is good, as it means being tied strongly to both sides of your nature, while low mean you are either untethered to one or both, with adverse effects for being low in either.

                                                      I have found giving these abstract stats a face and voice, and an innate meaning advantage makes the players value them more, and that the NPC are a pool for which two or more separate PCs can have different relationships with the same individual interlink PCs and can create good drama dynamically.
