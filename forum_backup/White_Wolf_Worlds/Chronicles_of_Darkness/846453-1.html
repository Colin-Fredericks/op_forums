        33) You found one of those old-timey messages in a bottle. Judging from the words and conditions of the paper, it's at least decades old. The contents of the message paint a pretty weird log of a ship captain, but something about some of the offhand references strikes you as really odd. It's only later that you realize the captain was talking about historical events... that turned out differently from the history you are familiar with. Did they simply go crazy?

        +) And it seems that was only the first message. Each tide seems to leave another portion of the log in your care, and together the tale they paint gets weirder and weirder. Its clear now that whatever events happened around the captains time turned out differently, or at least the captain thinks they did.

        ++) And now the message are seeming to follow you. You went to vacation miles away and you still keep coming across the bottles. You even went inland one day and found one in a bucket of water.

        +++) The last one left a map. It points to an island nearby. The only problem is - there is no island there, from your experience or any maps of the area. So why is it you seem to see lights coming from the direction the island is located on the map? And why does it seem like the island is getting closer on the map each day you check?

        34) You find yourself becalmed in a fog one morning. GPS is going haywire and your can't see the sky, let alone the sun or stars. Then the fog starts to clear... and you see a cave ceiling above you. Then an old sail boat pulls up along side you out of nowhere and someone claiming to be "the" Admiral asks what are you doing on the "Ocean of Fragments"?

                    35) The rip is hungry. The lifeguards talk about it from time to time. The beach should have honestly been closed down years ago, but some vested interests keep it open. Swimmers caught by the rip disappear out to see in moments. Every now and again the lifeguards save a swimmer in time, but there's video footage of the lifeguards that show that they seem to drag their heels, take too long to prepare, so when they get out they find nothing but calm blue water and a body face down. All the bad press has seen visitors to the beach drop sharply in the last few years, but someone keeps trying to drum up interest, get people out there with competitions or events. Someone is trying to feed the rip.

                    36) Six dead humpback whales washed ashore last night. They all had the same injuries. Their eyes were burned out, tongues severed, and a long cut from head to tail along their belly. Marine biologists are baffled as to what could have inflicted the wounds. A few retired police are telling anyone who will listen that those were the very same injuries left on victims of that serial killer who was around in '72.

                    37) The cruise ship blew into town in a whirlwind of music and high spending tourists. There was apparently a staff shortage on board, and a whole bevvy of of young people were happy to grab a chance at a job for a few months and escape their town's ailing job market. Thing is, it's been weeks now, and no one's heard anything from the ones who left. And if you look real close at pictures of the tourists themselves, it's a bit odd how they all have these flaps of skin barely visible behind their ears. Someone suggested "gills" in the pub the other night, and the whole place went quiet. Sure would have explained the smell.

                    38) It seemed like it happened overnight. All of a sudden every lobster pot was being hauled up filled with these ugly skittering critters. Relics of a lost age, from before the birth of humans or lizards or walking things. They've been lurking in their buckets for the last few days, too weird to consider killing for food. The state's environmental agency's been contacted, but no one's arrived yet. The way the creatures move and act is starting to give people the creeps. They're huddled together, but not for safety. It looks like they're planning something.

                    39) The crew of a tanker have been adrift since the engine died two days ago. The ship's engineers haven't been able to get it started again. They've had intermittent radio contact, enough to let the closest port know where they are, but no one's come out to them yet. That's fine, supplies are plentiful, current is basically nonexistent, and the weather has been mild. There does seem to be an awful lot of kelp building up around the ship though, and it looks like it's rising straight up from the ocean bed. The way it moves at night looks like something large is coming to the surface beneath it.

                    40) The Ranger is a navy vessel about to cross the equator for the first time since taking on some fresh recruits. The shellbacks have been busy getting ready for a big crossing the line ceremony, something Ranger's crew have always taken very seriously. The polliwogs have mostly been shut out of the preparations, but a few were conscripted to haul some big crates up to the main deck. They caught glimpses of the preparations, and boy, it sure seems like it's going to be one helluva ceremony. There are weird grinning masks made of black wood everywhere, and Neptune's trident is so big and old they could have sworn it was the real thing. Captain seemed pretty excited too when he thanked them, and he must have grabbed some contact lenses 'cos his eyes looked kinda... fishy.

                        41) A Japanese whaler has been found adrift in the Southern Ocean. It is coated with ice, preserving the bizarre scene on its decks. A massive creature is frozen in its death throes, its body resembling nothing so much as a specimen of Labidiaster Annulatus the size of a school bus. Around the dead starfish are the ship's crew, many of them clearly crushed or strangled by its arms. Even stranger, the ship is quickly identified as a vessel still at port back in Japan. The crew members are in a similar position, many of them with the ship, others are people known to be alive and well who could never have set foot on the ship. To top it off, it appears every piece of electronics on the ship has been set to the year 2018. And as the ice thaws and the great starfish's body is unfurled, the salvage crew notice its wounds are beginning to close.

                        42) The mangrove swamp is changing. As the tide levels rise with each passing year, scientists are finding that the local flora is adapting in unusual ways. The shallow water ecosystem is thriving, with underwater cameras showing a great number of unusual adaptations among the species there. Bulbous growths are common in plants and animals alike, and the delicate limbs of the increasingly mobile mangroves twist together into arches and spires that put more than a few in mind of architecture. It takes a while for a detailed analysis to be performed on these rapid changes, but when the results come back they are startling. It is as if the entire swamp has been altered on a genetic level, and the DNA of the living things there no longer match any earthly creature.

                        43) A street gang in Polynesia, total nobodies on the world stage, have suddenly begun to expand their operations globally. In a matter of weeks they have grown from backstreet thugs to masters of international shipping, and no one is able to explain where the money or influence came from. A criminal empire is coalescing out of nowhere, connecting ports in every country across the Pacific. Drugs, firearms, and illicit goods are pouring through these shipping lanes and law enforcement in every country is powerless to stop it. The best they have been able to do is disrupt a handful of the smaller shipments. A small case of pearls has been found buried within the shipping containers of each of these shipments.

                        44) White worms are showing up along the waterline at the beach. There are thousands of them in the wet sand, just beneath the surface. Besides wriggling between the toes of beachgoers and generally grossing everyone out, they also appear to be having a major impact on the local fish population. Those that eat them, and many local species apparently consider them a delicacy, begin to grow larger and slower, almost corpulent. They in turn are eaten by larger fish, who begin to demonstrate the same behaviour. A few fishermen have caught some massive specimens in the last few days, but after cutting them open have discovered worms living in the fish's tissue. Citizens are being urged not to eat any fish caught in the local area.

                                    45) The song of Broken Cliff is a muted choir, a gentle susurrus of the wind through its caverns and the waves breaking far below. Campers and tourists often find themselves falling asleep while seated near the cliffs, lulled into somnolence by the sound. They usually dismiss the vivid dreams of drowning that follow when they wake, gasping for air. Those who stay on the cusp of sleep experience a sensation alike to the water closing just over their head, a creeping darkness on the edge of vision as they struggle to stay on the surface of consciousness. Of course, this is all the product of active imaginations and proximity to the oceans. The song is just the wind.

                                    Just don't sleep too long.

                                    46) Michael Koppton found the most beautiful shell in the entire ocean, just walking along the beach. It was a stunning thing, a curling spiral of vibrant colours that shimmered in the light. Then his wife stabbed him to death, and she was caught with only the shell in her permission. Then the shell vanished from the evidence lock-up, and the arresting officers disappeared without a trace. When the shell resurfaces it was lying by the body of a coast guard officer with a broken neck, aboard a patrol boat he had taken out without permission.

                                    47) The sand island offshore is only a few years old. A change in local currents built it up in a matter of months. The local bird population has avoided it completely, even though it would make the perfect nesting ground. They won't even fly directly over it. And to be fair, they're not the only ones who get an uneasy feeling from the island. The way the sand moves, forming mounds or subsiding into little pits whenever a back is turned, is disconcerting. The presence of quicksand on the island has been confirmed, and the currents around the island make it dangerous to swimmers. Local officials are advising people stay clear of it, and that all shipping give it a wide berth.

                                            50) Glowing waves shouldn't be supernatural. Phosphorescent phytoplankton aren't even that uncommon in these waters, although they're usually only seen when the waves break. Tonight though, tonight the entire ocean is alight. From horizon to horizon, as far as can be seen around the ship, the water ripples white and blue beneath the crescent moon. And then the shadow comes, larger than the ship, swallowing the light as it closes in on the helpless vessel.

                                            51) You should have cleaned the gash on your arm before this. Reef cuts are always dangerous. But you forgot, and now the wound is definitely infected. You can see little flecks inside it, yellow and white and red, that kind of remind you of the coral you were diving near when the cut happened. When you go near the tap to wash it out, however, the wound starts to hurt. It gets worse the closer you come to fresh water, until the pain is too much and you stumble away. But the sea, well, the closer you come to that the better you feel. One little soak wouldn't hurt, would it?

                                            52) You drowned, and then you woke up. Now the sea is with you, the tides coursing through your brain, waves crashing just behind your eyes. It is old, older than any dry thing, the primeval ocean from the depths of time. And it knows things, for it has seen all that there has been and will be here until life on this planet runs out. You're not sure if the things it is showing you will make you a prophet or a harbinger of doom, but the waves cannot be denied and the truth of future tides must be shared.

                                                53) You've heard of St. Elmo's Fire. It's meant to be a kind of pale 'fire' that dances on the masts of ships in storms. It's not meant to burn on the surface of the sea on a calm night. Or underneath it. You saw the first flame three nights ago. Just one, maybe a few hundred yards away. The second night, there were dozens and they were at most a hundred yards away. Yesterday there were too many to count, bobbing on the waves only feet from the hull. As the sun goes down, the flames start to flicker into life. One catches on the aft deck. The wood beneath it takes on a golden metallic sheen.

                                                    54) The local government still hasn't worked out where the order to dredge the bay came from. The matter is currently undergoing a quiet investigation, but it didn't stop the crew from working every day. In fact, there were even reports of them working through the night. The company that employed the men is a total unknown, phone calls to the number listed as their head office go on hold to the sound of something like whale song and are never picked up. The men themselves were impossible to interview. They never left the dredge. A few attempts to go out to the vessel have been turned back by a voice on the radio broadcasting a warning, saying that it is too dangerous to approach. This voice refused to be drawn in conversation, it just repeated its message until the investigators turned back.

                                                    And then, one morning, the dredge was gone. It couldn't be found in any port in the area. As far as government records show, the work wasn't even paid for. Perhaps they found what they were looking for?

                                                    55) The military was conducting a series of exercises in the area. It was when they attempted a simulated beach landing that everything went wrong. A squall came out of nowhere. Live rounds were fired. Several units claimed they were attacked by unknown assailants. Six soldiers died, another fourteen were injured. The incidents were ultimately cataloged as friendly fire, and no one has been back to the beach since. They say if you stand on a boat offshore when the sun is just right, you can see the shapes of soldiers dying on that beach. If you're unlucky, you might even see what killed them.

                                                        56) A thick grey foam washed in with the tide, coating swimmers at the beach with a sticky grey film. Those covered in felt nauseous and fled the beach, lifeguards closed the beach. The next day they were all back at high tide, plunging into the water despite the protests of the lifeguards on duty. And when the foam came back, it looked like the swimmers started drinking it.

                                                        57) There's been a shipping container on every freighter for the last six months marked with a strange triangular symbol. It gets waved straight through by customs officials and stored in a warehouse at the end of the dock. And there they've been sitting, untouched, ever since. There was a break-in a few weeks back though, and every week since a different petty criminal has shown up floating in the harbour in front of the warehouse. It's happened every Sunday since, like clockwork.

                                                        58) Has anyone ever been up to the fish farm just round the head? I was passing by the other day and saw a couple of workers knocking off at the end of the day. No one I recognised from town though, which got me thinking. There's only a few hundred of us living in this place. So who is it breeding the fish up there? And does anyone actually know what they're breeding?
