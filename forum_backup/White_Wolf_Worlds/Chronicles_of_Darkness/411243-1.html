    Information supporting the middle part of a hunter chronicle. What sort of effect are hunters having on the world around them? How are they coping with their growing experience and knowledge weighed against their degrading morality?

    A hunter endgame, or various endgame scenarios, with a page or two discussing how to properly finish a hunter chronicle. It doesn't have to be on a victory on a global scale (though information on that possibility would be welcome). But a possibility of campaign ending victory (bittersweet as it may be) or resolution (as intensely personal as it might be) would be most welcome.

        Total Overhaul of the Tactics system. I think it's needlessly complicated, overly burdened with prerequisites and a lot of "Wait, what? Why?" decisions. Tactical fatigue needs to go. Requiring multiple people needs to go. Bonuses for arbitrarily selected compacts and conspiracies need to go. A tactic, ideally, is either a permission to let an extended action do something it normally could not (a working exorcism, for example), guarantees a particular outcome or side effect occurs/does not occur (for example, "Controlled Immolation: Fires started by the hunters are assumed to be controlled...") or otherwise modifies the stakes of teamwork/extended action rolls, rather than affecting them directly. "Disappear" is a signal from players to ST they don't want to be captured. I would see them more like group controlled Conditions than anything else, with an optional "Take a beat to have a Tactical Failure - Disappear means someone is followed or captured, etc."

        Someone needs to think about the Aegis Kai Doru. I think some kind of Endowment relating to forging artifacts of legend makes more sense. I mean, in theory "Relic" is already a merit available to regular World of Darkness characters and any of the conspiracies might come across something.

        The Compact/Conspiracy divide needs to die. A build-your-own conspiracy system could scale up from regional groups without an endowment or institutional longevity, to the really powerful conspiracies that might have more than one Endowment. Make the system more flexible and interesting. We'll be building our own Courts in Changeling 2e; lets build our own conspiracies in Hunter 2e.

                        I want a redefinition of the tiers: in 1e, it’s an uncomfortable conflation of “how well organized are the hunters?” and “what style of game are you playing?” The assumption is that if you’re playing an isolated cell, you’re also playing “hunters as regular mortals in over their heads”; if you’re playing with compacts, you’re also playing “hunters as competent mortals who hold their own”; and if you’re playing with conspiracies, you’re also playing “hunters who dabble in the supernatural and blur the line between human and monster”.

                        In 2e, I’d like these tight couplings of concepts to be considerably loosened, ranging from talking about the different play styles and the mechanics that support them independently of the different organizational tiers to recasting some existing Conspiracies as Compacts and vice versa (without any change as to whether or not they offer supernatural Endowments) to providing advice on how to use various orgs in chronicles that are set up with a different playstyle than the one for which the org was originally conceived.

                        On the first point, there are a number of ideas from previous books (mainly Mirrors and Strange Dead Love) that I’d like to see introduced into Hunter as optional rules, in much the same way that 1e presented the Code as an optional rule: for “competent mortals”, I wouldn’t mind an optional system of “edges” that can be acquired through Practical Experience ranging from skill tricks to trait boosts and maybe even additional uses for willpower; for “borderline supernaturals”, I’d like to see GMC-style refinements of Conviction and Forbidden Knowledge (also tied explicitly to Practical Experience; and in the latter case, presented in the context of Dread Powers). The key would be to clearly section them off as playstyle-specific rules that are optional even when the requisite playstyle is in effect, and to stress repeatedly that if you don’t like any of these rules options, you don’t have to use them.

                        In effect, Hunter is three “games” in one: it can be used as if it were a “blue book supplement” that introduces new organizations and tools for mortal protagonists to take advantage of; it can be used to portray a “special breed” of mortals who have been set apart from your average denizen of the World of Darkness by the fact that they’ve encountered the supernatural and not turned away; or it can be used to handle mortals who fight the monsters using every tool they can get their hands on, even at the risk of becoming monsters themselves. I’d like to see this three-way conceptual division made more explicit.

                        My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness

                                                    Could we please ditch the numeric labels for the Tiers in 2e? Instead of talking “Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3”, go with GMC’s “Local, Regional, Global” terminology — and revise the Conspiracies so that they are Global, or demote them to Regional status.

                                                    Just as importantly, decouple them from individual power level. Here, Tier 1, 2, and 3 might translate to, say, Gritty, Cinematic, and Empowered. There would still be a loose coupling here:

                                                    • isolated cells would tend to be featured in Local chronicles and would tend to feature Gritty protagonists
                                                    • Compacts would tend to be featured in Regional chronicles and would tend to feature Cinematic protagonists
                                                    • Conspiracies would tend to be featured in Global chronicles and would tend to feature Empowered protagonists

                                                    But those would only be tendencies: you could easily have a Regional chronicle featuring an independent cell of Empowered hunters, or a Global chronicle featuring Gritty hunters who belong to Compacts.

                                                    Incidentally, 1e effectively only has two “personal power” Tiers: if you’re not Empowered, you’re mostly Gritty with a touch of Cinematic. This, too, is a conflation that I want to see ditched: let those who like playinng Hunter at the Gritty tier go purely Gritty, without any “cheats” to make life easier on them (not even Risking Willpower); and let those who like playing Hunter at the Cinematic tier go beyond what Hunter 1e allowed hunters without supernatural resources to do, into the realm of Strange Dead Love’s Heroic Mortals.
