        Okay, let's actually address the issue of the question to the best of our ability, keeping in mind that I am not even a medical science amateur and am just reckoning this with the best "writer trying to create versimilitude" instincts I've got. Spoilers, the back-of-the-napkin answer is "a scale ranging from 'barely better than most humans' to 'what if werewolves could actually chill for most of their lives' depending on how good your healthcare is". I'll try to remember a too long, didn't read at the end of things.

        So What Does Dying, and Living a Long Time, Look Like?

        First thing first, getting an idea of what the best humans can do without magic to get an idea of what we're trying to deal with. Fortunately, Satchel has a breakdown of the records for humanity already written for me, so I'm just going to steal that.

        Originally posted by Satchel
        Oldest person to ever live reached 122, with the second and third oldest being 119. Fifth through tenth place is all varying elaborations on 117. Number 1 was notably in good health but almost blind and deaf and died of unspecified causes, while Number 2 had had surgery for two different cancers and died of unspecified causes about half a year after she'd been said to have been feeling ill. Number 3 was similarly healthy but frail and had bad hearing and eyesight and died of natural causes. Number 4 also died of natural causes at 118 (a month away from 119), was blind and wheelchair-bound, and was the oldest person to survive COVID.
        I'm also going to put in a general idea that the general idea of living a good long life is keeping physically fit, having a good lifestyle ranging from food to living spaces and decent work/life balance, and I'm going to keep in mind that the biggest issue, and the one modern science doesn't quite yet have an answer for, is telomere senescence, where effectively the the parts of your DNA designed to protect your most useful DNA get chopped off until finally your DNA is subject to degradation, a subject my keen, academically rooted mind got all of it's information from that most reputable of sources, Mathew Patrick's Film Theory on Why Logan was actually dying in Logan. Truly I am a scholar.

        To round that all out, with our extremes and what was physiologically the state of them out there, let's also set the averages we to counter those exceptions with. I'm going to be a lazy American and use the US averages, with men getting about 74.8 years and men getting 80.2 years, and then averaging the two of them out to 77.5 years, which will be roughly useful when we get account to trying to guesstimate numbers.

        A random thing of note is that, while magic can definitely shortcut a lot of particular problems, modern science is more reliable for the purposes of keeping alive as much as possible by simple virtue of us not having to argue about an effect being Lasting or not, and so broadly we are expecting magic to be used to resolve problems after all reasonable exploration of sciences are done, because we don't want the Awakened longevity to be resting on a house made up of spell that can be counterspelled-rather the opposite, we want that to be as a minimal as possible.

        The final thing here is that we are keeping in mind here that Death is part the metaphysical constant, that outright trying to avoid dying marks you an aberration in the weft and weave of reality, and that therefore there is some kind of line where in a person trying to live as long as possible without trying to avoid dying that can be met. After a bit of research and arbitrary line drawing on my part, we're figuring something of a base idea that every 60 years, any metaphysical force attempting to fit that criteria faces a Withstand of +1. You could probably map this out to the actual rule standards in the game where in you assume each Stamina point, under the assumption of best possible care, comes out to 60, but then you'd basically have to flip the Withstand mechanic on it's head and also reach some wonky conclusions, and this is all just back of the napkin shit anyways, so basically, every 60 years introduce +1 Withstand to Spells intending to extend life without breaching into immortality, meaning that at some point even archmages need to start planning out their permanent retirement.

        I'm going to playing fast and loose with all of this, so apologies in advance.

        What The Awakened Bring to Bear Against Lifespans

        Okay, with arbitrary base goalposts and muddy ideas of things to address laid out, let's address the things that being a mage contributes to, starting with just the base template features and thing you'd have on the Newly Awakened form of things, and what Arcanum can do for your livelihood and at what level of aptitude you'd start seeing those benefits. We're gonna also fudge some estimates and on what that might do for lifespans.

        Upfront and per my particular, idiom, I'm going to first point out that, while it's costly, every mage can do some regeneration for 3 Mana per bashing/lethal damage. That 3 Mana can come from scouring their pattern for their Attributes, which from there take a day per Attribute to heal, as opposed to the two days to heal lethal normally. On similar fronts, no mage comes out of Awakening without some kind of Mage Armor, which is good because mages have above average threats of harm to their life than the average individual. Broadly, it's hard to map out how much of a difference that can make in comparison to the normal (you're welcome to argue that Forces Armor means the Obrimos never haver to buy sunscreen or that it doesn't work that way, for example), but on the broad strokes, I'm willing to at least say that means mages on average at least definitely start some place of a longer span than the average of 77.5 from earlier. The random accidents and incidents of a normal person's life are just more handily dealt with as a mage.

        But okay, basic shit out out of the way, let's get into the Arcana, starting with the Gross Arcana. Life, obviously, is the most useful here, allowing you to identify from Life 1 the problems and issues you need to take care of to be as healthy and whole, both in the immediate and in the long term, and to be aware of various kinds of threats coming their way, and even resolve it. A Life Initiate could cleanse themselves of lingering alcohol traces after a night out on the town, and thus spare their liver and kidneys the work and damage of cleaning that up, and do the same with any other materials that enter their body that might be better served by immediate ejection, and in general also is able to be way more aware of their environments and possible threats to them. And Life from there just gets better and better, including possibly negating telomere senescence, which hypothetically, so long as your DNA isn't fucked with and you continue to get the materials you need to keep yourself physically rebuilt over time, could mean you live forever as far as the science go-and that's why I made it clear we're assuming a ceiling comes in somewhere unless you deliberately breach the world, so obviously good deal. (Also improved pattern restoration at 2)

        Time might not seem the most obvious Arcanum to ensure timespan length, even accounting for how it can hasten, slow, or even freeze physical processes and deterioration, but the really big thing is getting an actual idea of how and why your physicality changes, warps, and deteriorates-from being able to see if cancer and heart attacks are in your future and when to just being able to plan out for when you're going to be deficient on nutrients and vitamins. In short, if having a good health plan is vital for staying alive as long as possible, then being able to see your 7 day, two month, and ten year gains/losses is just really damn helpful. Willing to say you see the returns as an Initiate, if you're minded for it.

        Forces also doesn't seem as immediately useful, but like the jab about Forces Armor gets at, there's a lot of wear and tear offered by the sun and wind and just sheer friction that being able to nudge and manipulate does a lot of small scale aids per second that help keep you going longer, so again, a Forces Initiate with a mind for longevity will start seeing the differences.

        Matter is where we run into some questions-the body needs minerals and materials to maintain itself, but the line between Life's Purview and Matter's Purview becomes a question mark. The idea of Matter's role in supporting and enhancing medical supplies and body replacements as needed is undeniable-any bandage wrapped by a Moros is just going to be better in terms for closure AND flexibility than a bandage applied by a normal person, for example-but to really make the arguments for aid, I'd make the argument it's take a Disciple(3 Dot) of Matter to really start seeing how those things come fully into play. Your Arguments may vary. (later thought; See things like air and water filtration as one reason why Matter helps)

        Space probably has some law of sympathy manipulations that you can argue at least provides background adjustments for improving the general longevity of a given mage, but I have an easier time arguing how to use those sympathetic connections to physically tear a person apart rather than keeping them together, so I'm going to expose my ass and say Space only helps with longevity in niche circumstances and otherwise doesn't factor in.

        Shifting over into subtle Arcana, I need to highlight Spirit by virtue of really arguing autonomous sources of healthy living. From being able to adjust the resonance of their living and work spaces to better make them spaces for healthy living to having spiritual aid for physical and emotional health, and being able to rely on Loci, Resonance, and Spirits either tightly controlled or in good relations means that it opens up any given mage's spell control for other things. Like, yeah, I can easily argue a smart Initiate of Spirit would very quickly see long term benefits coming into play for them, easy, peasy.

        I probably should go over to Death at this point, but my brain is saying Fate, because basically being able to say things work out for your long term physical well-being and forget in a couple of forms already does a lot to basically mean Fate Initiates do a lot avoid wear and tear.

        Death is an odd one to discuss, because it doesn't so much as a contribute to any given mages livelyness so much as it helps with preserving them and working to keep entropic forces-but we've been making that argument for everything else so far, so I'm not above it. Ectoplasm can aid with the handling of toxins and poisons in the bloodstream (see Sin-Eaters for proof of that, but keep in mind Mages would ostensibly need to be more careful with applying that as a tool since they aren't a necrotic being like said Sin-Eaters), and some of the readings on things like Without a Trace can indicate it does a lot to reduce and minimize the small scale death we all experience to keep those things from leaving traces. After all the other Arcanum are considered, Death seems like it cleans up or delays all the other consequences. At a minimum, I'd think it's take at least 2 Dots to see how those spells could help with longevity, and I could make an argument that you'd really need 4 dots, but I wouldn't take it off the table just simply because this is the Arcanum that let your hold off the very thing we're trying to stretch out.

        I'm going to knock out Prime and Mind in the same here, because they're kind of minimal but at the same time contributive. Prime lets you adjust leylines and Resonance, so in the same way Spirit can let your create spaces that promotes healthiness, Prime at 3 Dots is enough to create environments that contiribute to your long well-being, and at a minimum of 2 helps you ward against supernatural contributions to your early demise. Your mental health contributes a lot to your physical wellbeing, and from having a good emotional balance to your life to just placeboing as needed to get through something, Mind contributes pretty interestingly to longevity. Those sort of tricks start as early as the second Dot, so.

        Okay, so translating that into something a little more approachable, let's lay them out by just how much more liveable each Path can be.
        • The Thyrsus are by and far away the best for living the longest, giving them the most power and control over their own health and wellbeing, and having a lot of really solid self-support for their mental health, control over their living and work spaces, and in having active aids for their health, even before being able to effectively being able ot build their own ephemeral healer support spirit. I can easily believe newer mages can easily attain 120 lifespan with minimal concerns, and master/adept Thyrsus likely can rival a werewolf in their own right with a half life of 95 and a total lifespan anywhere from 180 to 200, provided they aren't *just* relying on their magic to do it.
        • The Acanthus occupy my next best for long lives, but keeping the character notion just to their Arcana, that means the best of them likely get to very comfortable versions of 120 years of life, and I can see easy arguments pushing that out to 140-150. Newer mages might see the US Average swing up to 90, increasing the odds they do become masters or adepts who can earn that.
        • The Obrimos deal with enough of the things that contribute to the wear and tear that can over time cap your life that I feel comfortable saying they also can probably more comfortable reach 90 on their own, but I also don't see them able to win against the pull of entropy that gets them the boost other mages might. 120 on their own is in the realm of improbable, all other factors asides.
        • The Moros have so many questions marks in how you can use Matter and Death to prolong your life without going into lichdom that I can see arguments that they only, at best, ensure they get to 80, and arguments that they could easily hold death off till 120, depending on the read. For my part, I'm going to say they're likely to obtain the 80-90 range on average, with 120 lifespans for Moros mostly undeniably being the result of the fact they really can afford the healthcare in mortal sciences. Or Lichdom.
        • It feels appropriate to say that the path with the least interest in raw physicality is the one who sees the least returns on increasing their lifespan purely on their own, and so I reckon the Mastigos are the ones who likely see a change in their lifespan of only 80 to 85, depending on their mastery
        Now mind you, that's a mage by themselves, and not accounting for the interplay of arcanum. The standard for becoming a Archmage being at least an adeptitude with three Arcana can reveal some of the holes and limits to what it take to have a really comprehensive grasp on your own longevity. Hell, it doesn't even account for the biggest factor.....

        Sectarian Healthcare And How Society Lets You Live Longer

        The nice thing of being a social creature in a social world is that, no, you don't have to do it by yourself, and be a bleeding master of the universe in order to do it. In fact, I just wanna lay out that this is one of those reasons why the Sects are so appealing in the first place. A Solitaire has to bargain for help as an outsider from anyone for anything they can't do, and even a member of a Nameless Order has to contend with the fact that, unless said Nameless Order has issues of healthiness and longeivty as a major or related part of the Mysteries they deal with, the Thyrsus in their group probably has better things to do than to play doctor. Meanwhile, the Orders and Ministries of the Sect are large enough to have mages who likely to just commit themselves to those Mysteries and functions in the wings, and also likely has ins with the mortal institutions that you can just go to and get the standard help you would otherwise need. Sure, in the case of the Seers, getting regular medical treatment and evaluations is basically creating a laundry list of ways for people to get at you, but that's what the bribery and blackmail is for, so it all pans out.

        Of course, the *downside* to that is, like with job-offered insurance, you either have to be meeting business standards for your organization, or you need to go above and beyond. Getting magically backed assistance with longevity is basically a good recipe for owing a lot of favors across the board.

        But, with that asides, you can basically lower the bar of entry for those longer lifespans the more invested they are into their organizations, and really well supported Masters and Adepts can possibly push that boundary even further.

        Archmages need a particular mention. Dynamism, Entities, and Excision introduce a lot of materials that allow you do all the more of everything without violating our parameters, and while the Ententes don't offer the same social support network the Sects do (remember, every Archmage is effectively in conflict with every other Archmage, Ententes only suggest reasons that might abate in circumstances, predominantly for the purposes of sharing information, not directly aid most of the time), any given Archmage's ability to sweep any mage into their own shit means they've effectively got the same support as anyone in a Sect.

        Okay, this has gone on too long, let's wrap this shit up.

        Okay, So What's My Conclusions?

        Let's get right down into it:
        • The majority of non-Sectarian mages are likely only going to see about a 5-15 year life increase, with the Acanthus reliably going up to 120, and the Thyrsus being the most likely to get to 200.
        • Sectarian mages are more likely, barring violent intrusions, to reach 120, with the Acanthus pushing up to 150, and the Thyrsus being the mostly likely to live up to somewhere between 200-300 years.
        • Archmages could believably push 500 and stretch out to 700.
        I dunno, I was more interested in laying down something for people to actually work with and argue about. My math and expectations are pretty well open to being wrong, I reckon, but I've got the thought this isn't a terrible launching point for actually getting into answering the thread, rather than having diatribes about Ascension or whatever. Do with this what you will.
