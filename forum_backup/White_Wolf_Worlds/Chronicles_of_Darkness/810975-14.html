                Originally posted by Dave Brookshaw View Post
                Spell Control always existed, and was always called that. People are getting confused about the (entirely seperate) Spell Tolerance, which was in 1e and isn't in 2e.
                That may at least partially be because 1e Spell Tolerance was the one where you could go past the limit at the cost of bad stuff happening (penalties to all actions as your Pattern is overloaded with spells, IIRC), whereas Spell Control was an absolute hard cap you could never exceed. 2e Spell Control lets you get past it, but there are downsides, much like how 1e Spell Tolerance worked. 2e Spell Control doesn't really resemble 1e Spell Control in that sense.

                (That being said, I like 2e Spell Control a lot better than 1e Spell Tolerance, and I doubt we'll see many Mages blowing 4+ points of Reach past their Spell Control considering the sheer amount and level of badness that could result in).

                        I don't generally think tiers are very useful in ChroD. As other people have said, each game is built with a different template for success. Demon is about espionage, Werewolf about the hunt, Vampire about social politics. This lends them all unique standards for "success" in the same way that default success standards might be combat in D&D, investigation and psychological steeliness in Call of Cthulhu, and wetworks in Shadowrun. You wouldn't rank CoC characters according to who's best at slaying dragons or D&D characters according to who's best at maintaining their sanity (except perhaps in a Ravenloft game).

                        But since we've already been going strong with the D&D comparison, here's a potential thought exercise. If default, middle-of-the-road characters from each of the core books were playing a gam of Dungeons and Dragons, what class would they represent?

                                        I'll assume our characters are up with the times and playing 5E, and I'll also assume this is about what characters would choose to play rather than what they'd adapt directly into. Avoiding dual-classing while I'm at it.

                                        Vampire: Sorcerer? I'm not sure what would best fit them, but that would give them a nice spread of activate-at-will powers that simulate their breadth of disciplines.
                                        Werewolf: Ranger, what with the capacity to hunt and harry prey and take advantage of the environment around them to their benefit. Barbarian would also be appealing, I think.
                                        Mage: Wizard. Access to all the spells you could ever want, but you have to prepare in advance for the challenges ahead.
                                        Changeling: Bard. Buff your pals, disadvantage your opponents with song, dance, and poetry!
                                        Mummy: Warlock. Limitless power granted to you by a profane entity.
                                        Hunter: Paladin or cleric for tier three hunters. Plain jane warrior for the first two tiers.
                                        Demon: Thief, dashing to an assassin specialization as quick as their feet will carry them there.
