                        Also, it technically doesn't contradict canon in any way, since it's basically meant to take the state of the ChroD universe from an out of universe perspective (separate settings inside one meta-setting that occasionally intersect) and find a way to make it true from an in-universe perspective.

                        For example, part of it is to provide a way that Mages and the God-Machine can exist in the same setting while having relatively little interaction with each other.

                        The "crossover mangrove" is sort of the reverse, being an out of universe perspective on an in universe truth, that the supernatural tends to be somewhat subjective and shaped a lot by collective perception. So, for example, this theory allows that a Mage could learn about Changeling Arcadia, come to any of the many theories that have been established as to their connection, and have any of them be true, as they form as a metaphysical connecting device that allows the Mage to exist in both worlds.

                        In a similar way to how Mages can manifest in other worlds as an umbrella for any sort of mortal magician, Horrors can potentially be any of the kinds of creature from the other worlds, but they are rarely put under the kind of magical scrutiny in the base world that could firmly establish if they are, say, a Hobgoblin or a Spirit or a Stigmatic Cryptid. Generally, intense magical scrutiny is the best mechanism of collapsing the wave function of ambiguity and creating a bridge between two reality layers. However, in some cases, ambiguity still remains due to the perception factor. A Mage could encounter the Hedge and become connected to the World of Thorns, believe it to be an Emanation Realm, or believe Hobs to be Supernal beings created by this emanation, and they could be entirely right regardless of whether or not that's the "actual" explanation, because their perception of them is close enough to the truth that it forms a valid reality bridge. On the flip side, a Demon might perceive a Mage as an unusually powerful Stigmatic "hacking" the Machine, and this would be "true" from the perspective of the Demon layer, just as the Mage's perception of the Machine as an aggregate of control symbols created by the Fall would be true on their side.

                        And the reason why magic powers that depend on certain supernatural forces that may not be present in every layer work in every layer is because a person caught between two layers does not actually travel to another place but projects themself onto it, or becomes a metaphysical anchor subject to the "rules" of both. So a Mage's magic works even on a layer with no Supernal realms, because the Mage is still based in the World of Mana no matter what other layers they become connected to. This is where crossover theories come into play: people caught between layers must establish one to anchor themselves in place and not slide back completely into their own layers, and the bridge formed by a crossover theory permits magical phenomena from one layer to effect others.

                        Also, the beauty of this system is that it requires no rules and mechanical changes and basically means that reality itself follows the tips from crossovers written in the books.

                        Another idea- the Astral permeates and connects all the layers. This is why Beasts are connected to other monsters: because they are born from one of the few supernatural phenomena that is naturally equally tied to each world.

                                Well, glad you like this one!

                                And yeah, the other cluster I mentioned in the first post was meant to be the Old World of Darkness, and the one they tried to contact is Unknown Armies. I actually drew a "map" of the multiverse, with the ChroD cluster in the middle, and several others off in the distance. My idea is that the distance between a universe cluster depends on thematic and content differences. So ChroD is fairly close to UA, and close enough to oWoD that there are "lines" connecting them that could allow them to intersect. The oWoD cluster, additionally, has faint lines connecting it to the Exalted cluster, while both worlds of darkness are in the same multiversal "neighborhood" as the Anne Rice cluster, the creepypasta cluster, the Call of Cthulhu cluster, and so on. And if you were in the Unknown Armies cluster, you'd have an easier time getting to the Illuminatus Trilogy cluster or the Tim Powers cluster than you would getting to the Star Wars cluster.

                                I'd personally not have the oWoD universes be part of the ChroD layer knot, but that's how canon works- you pick which one you want. I already assume that Mage and Promethean as they exist in my universe knot are different from the way they exist in other peoples' universe knots, for example, because there are no actual universe knots and all of this exists solely in our minds.

                                To establish some terminology, I'm thinking each layer has its own Paradigm (dominant supernatural methodology) and Primacy (powerful background supernatural string-pullers). Example Paradigms would be the Spirit World, the Supernal, the Wyrd, the Divine Fire, and so on, while Primacies would be super powerful spirits, Exarchs and other Supernal beings, the Gentry, the Principle, the Judges, and Machine, and so on.

                                        I think it's worth noting that the original World of Darkness included something very much like this, both in Demon: the Fallen (where it mentioned that the World of Darkness used to be composed of multiple layers of reality tightly coupled together, with each representing a different take on matters) and in the Time of Judgment supplement Mage (where it was referred to as the Fractured Cosmos, and posited a similar scenario but with much looser bindings between the different variants of reality).

                                        It's something I like where the original World of Darkness is concerned, as the various gamelines had metaphysics that sometimes clashed violently (though it's possible that One World of Darkness will address this issue). I'm not so fond of using it with the Chronicles of Darkness, because by and large most gamelines don't have or need dominance of their paradigms: for instance, the Divine Fire, animism, and the Wyrd all coexist without any of them being the ultimate underpinnings of reality. The Supernal is the only exception to this, and even it isn't so much a dominant paradigm that everything else is subservient to as it's the index of reality: e.g., it isn't that the Wyrd is a manifestation of the Supernal as it is that the Supernal describes the Wyrd. Without the various metaphysics competing with each other for the role of dominant paradigm, there are no clashes between them in need of resolution. The main exception to this is Hunter: the Vigil, which as written allows mortals to do things that other gamelines consider to be impossible. I don't know if that will still be true once HtV2e comes out, though.

                                        In fact, I suspect that a major motivation behind the upcoming Crossover Chronicles will be to identify apparent clashes between the lines and iron them out so that you can have a single, consistent ChroD setting that includes every gameline.

                                        That said, I do use something like this for the Chronicles of Darkness: but rather than the dividing line being gameline-based, I have it based on ChroD's toolbox nature. That is, you have a single ChroD "core reality", and then a bunch of customized variants emanating out from it, each corresponding to a particular Storyteller's take on the ChroD. For instance, my default ChroD setting doesn't have vampires or Beasts in it, because I have little interest in either of those gamelines.
