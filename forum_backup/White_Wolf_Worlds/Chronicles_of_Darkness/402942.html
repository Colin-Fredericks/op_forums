    Werewolf inspiration

    02-12-2015, 12:10 AM
    Stupid question. I still can't wrap my head around how to make werewolves distinct. I think this is partly due to the book over-emphasizing the common wolfyness of all Uratha, and partly due to my general unfamiliarity with werewolves, perhaps. I can't see a way to play up the animalistic and rage-filled aspects of their character sufficiently while avoiding turning them into caricatures and/or making them all way too similar.

    I get the pack dynamic fairly and have plenty of fictional and non-fictional sources to look to for examples there. Same for the Shadow.

    My problem is that I can't think of any media to look to that portray werewolves similar enough to the Uratha to be useful, the problem being that most werewolf stories are about solitary and, more importantly, uncontrolled lycanthropes. The only possible exception to this I can think of is Teen Wolf, which i admit, I haven't seen yet. I also thought perhaps Being Human might work but (A) it's still basically the story of one werewolf, and he's still at least somewhat cursed and (B( I watched a couple of episodes of the American version and it kinda soured me on the whole franchise (especially since i know they used the same plots.)

    Any other suggestions?

      Teen wolf is ok for it in a spirit cop sort of way. The book recommends cop dramas, which works for the characters... but perhaps maybe something like Jack Nickleson in "wolf", Mr. Jones as a film is a good example of the kind of shit you deal with in the Hisil. American werewolf in london and ginger snaps are both good films for first change wolves dealing with rage frenzy. What about "Wolves" which just went for sale on the playstation store? Fairly good violent film, featuring inter uratha violence.

      For books you could read something like Anne Rice wolf gift, while not really being similar to the Uratha has inter pack play and a sort of world building you may be looking for. Dog soldiers is a pretty good example of pack workings, all be it more of a hunter film example. American Werewolf in Paris has this whole werewolf cult thing going on.

      The Uratha, as they appear to be in 2nd edition, are more in line with the horror stories of the past, while still keeping the game themes as they were. What might help you with the Uratha is the Rage (players guide) Lore of the forsaken (good for cultural stuffs but not really necessary at all) and hunting grounds the rockies. It gives a clear example of Uratha activities within their respective regions. Tribes of the moon and The Pure will cement what each particular tribe of werewolves mean/how they act, though you can supplement the former with "the Rage".

      Not sure if this helped any... you'll be hard pressed to find films that show werewolves as the Uratha are... you can find films and activities similar to what the Uratha do, but not enough with werewolves.

                          Werewolves have fundamentally human emotions, albeit with greater than life passions. They don't have crushes, theirs is love at first sight. They don't get offended, they will plot how they'll kill you and make it look like a suicide, or coerce you to take your own life at their own terms. They don't have protegees, they have cubs, which will be protected with fang and talon.

                          The way those emotions are magnified by their spiritual passions informs how they approach their lives as several hunts. Seeking out one's loved one, despite the fact that you're a forsaken creature with alien entities that would love nothing than to wear your sweetheart like a sock puppet would keep any Uratha up at night, making sure the Spirit World isn't restless. The process of having someone take their own life, after coercing him to make amends for their slight and set their matters in order would be arduous and completely ethical for most Bone Shadows. And finally, mentoring the next generation of Uratha, passing down the traditions that have lasted for millenia is ingrained in their very being.

                                              This is for first edition. You might want to wait until second edition comes out before you decide to get anything.

                                              An important thing to remember is that they aren't rage-filled people until they've hit some Death Rage triggers, are nearing their auspice moon, or are using their gauru form. Your average werewolves aren't Bruce Banner, going to turn into a rampaging hulk because someone made fun of their hair. That's just the more easily angered people to begin with, or werewolves who are low on harmony. Primal Urge did it too, bringing a large penalty to any social rolls.

                                              You're free to play them as people with anger issues, but that's just a narrow facet of Uratha as a whole. When it comes down to it, aside from what Ephsy has already mentioned, they're normal if usually passionate people. But some are going to be cool and collected, some are going to be temperamental hot heads, some are going to be nearly disassociative in their interactions, and some just don't care. The signature characters in the core book range from a descendant of railroad barons who runs his protectorate like he runs his international corporation, a confident woman from a family that own a lot of defense contractors for the US Military, a guy from the suburbs trying to finish their psychology degree, to a biker with a heart who doesn't like drunk drivers on his road, the veteran who loves to let loose, and an old bigot who's a patriarch of his clan.

                                              I think the Shield is a good example of werewolves. Vic Mackey's Strike Force are cops, the rest of the department may as well be a Protectorate. Mackey has a skewed view of things but when it comes to crime he's more about control than elimination. Getting rid of drug dealers will just cause more trouble down the road as other forces move to fill the vacuum left in their absence, so let some dealers do their work, and feel free to take a little off the top. Even the gangs could be compared to packs. The problem with this is it ends to focus on very street level kind of things, which is good, but as we've discussed elsewhere can lead people to thinking all werewolves are bikers and gangbangers.
