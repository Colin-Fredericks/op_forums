                    Originally posted by xenoterracide View Post
                    What would a mage need to use to make "Poison Ivy" vines attack from nothing or a seed? I ended up improving a character, and trying to map it back to something. For the game I just used the rules for the snare dread power.

                    Life 3 seems most appropriate. The 3 dot spells are the first spells that allow you to directly harm other mages, and they seem to be most appropriate here. At the 3 dot level, every Arcanum has a "you deal [potency] amount of bashing damage" spell (except for Fate, but Fate's spell can piggy-back on another Arcanum), with +1 reach upgrading it to lethal damage. At the 4 dot level, all Arcana have a "you deal [potency] amount of lethal damage" spell, with +1 reach allowing you to upgrade it to aggravated damage. Game-wise I'm fine with this, because of the Wisdom degeneration it entails, but sometimes it's hard to imagine exactly what happens. For example, it's easy to imagine a 4 dot Mind spell causing lethal damage to anyone who looked at the mage wrong (aneurysm, stroke, straight Phineas Gage level brain damage), but Mind-based aggravated damage is a whole other story altogether. You can't really write about glowing-green scum covered swathes of neurons, because that's just instant death no matter how you look at it. There's no real 'up' from lethal-level strokes. Once you've had a stroke, you're fucking fried, you're done. For the Life Arcanum the Lethal -> Aggravated level makes sense, but for Mind (and a fortiori most other Arcana) it does not.

                                    I only have the Mage 2nd edition core rulebook, so if this is contradicted by the Chronicles of Darkness rulebook I wouldn't know, but: degloving is something that can happen simply by the mechanical procedures of something a hand could come into contact with, so it would be lethal damage by definition. In the rulebook, aggravated damage is described as either inflicted by supernatural beings or sources, or nuclear power. Degloving usually happens either because of the intervention of a machine or due to high-pressure mineral oil, so it wouldn't be aggravated in either case.
