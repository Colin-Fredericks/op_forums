                As I figure out a campaign for my players, I've contemplated the whole 'lack of antagonists' thing in the book and - my take on it - is thus:

                There is an overlooked antagonist that most STs miss entirely, or houserule out of existance for fear that they may take some of the control of the players characters out of the hands of the players. The Geists themselves. Sin Eaters - by their very nature - are literally sharing their heads with a super powerful batshit crazy super ghost hybrid. If you want to run a Geist game, their interaction and demands on their hosts should be a major motivation in game. Whether your ghost makes them want to avenge rape victims, or finally find proof that the illuminati is covering up archeological digs that prove Napoleon was in fact an alien, the Geists should be the biggest motivation for the players to act.

                I want a splat book on Geists themselves, showcasing the different ways they manipulate their hosts and cause mayham.

                                            I've been writing up a system overhaul for Geist, and I looked into both how the Beast is handled in Blood & Smoke and how the Possessed are handled to figure out how to work with the Geist conflicting with the Sin-eater. In the end, I went with giving the Geist a dice pool based on the Sin-eaters Psyche rating. I posted it somewhere, but I can't remember where, so I'll add it to the end of this post.

                                            You can also get alot of mileage out of Mages and Thaumaturges abusing ghosts, and the Geists (if not the Sin-Eater themselves) not being alright with that.
                                            One of the main problems is that there's not really any reason Sin-eaters would care about that. Going through Geist, a lot of the suggested plotlines only really work if the players are "good" people. If they're antagonistic, or even if they're just neutral, there are very few reasons for them to get involved.

                                            Geist does have antagonists. A lot of them. It just doesn't give much reason for you to care.
                                            An Abmortal could be a great antagonist, but they don't have anywhere near the same narrative punch as, say, the Strix. When a Strix shows up in a city, shit goes down. It's an "and then it started raining" to any bad situation.

                                            They throw Cities into chaos. But Geist? Doesn't have that. No reason for them to care about anything. It's always a bad sign when the setting has no drive beyond the goodness of people's hearts. And I think that might be one reason that some people criticize Geist as a game in which there are no consequences.
                                            Interaction with the Geist
                                            The Bargain fuses Sin-eater and Geist together in unison, but they’re still separate entities even if they possess the same space. Sometimes their goals and desires conflict, and they might struggle against each other. The Geist is for the most part a passenger, but even a back seat driver can make a grab for the wheel sometimes. A Sin-eater can allow their Geist to take control, if they choose, forgoing any contest. The Geist controls the physical body and the Sin-eater gains an additional Aspiration directed towards what the Geist wants to accomplish. Once they accomplish it, the Geist relinquishes control.
                                            Sometimes a Sin-eater doesn’twant to give up their body, though. Either out of personal possession--it isher body, after all--and sometimes because the goal of the Geist conflicts with the Sin-eater’s convictions or own goals. Geists can often be dangerous when left unchained. If a Sin-eater wants to struggle against the Geist, they perform a sort of Abjuration against themselves. The Sin-eater rolls Stamina + Composure to keep control, while the Storyteller rolls based on Psyche for the Geist.
                                            Psyche Rating Dice to Struggle
                                            1 to 3 4
                                            4 to 5 5
                                            6 to 8 7
                                            9 to 10 9
                                            A Sin-eater can spend a point of Willpower to resist the Geist, only instead of providing +3 on the roll, it staves the Geist off for another turn. During this turn, the Sin-eater physically strains, growling, exerting control over their body. The roll for the turn is held off. When a Sin-eater finally stops spending Willpower (either out of choice or because they simply can’t) then when the roll finally happens, the Sin-eater gains +1 for each point turn spent holding it off.
                                            If the Sin-eater wins, the Geist is pushed back and calms down (metaphorically, at least. It won’t attempt to take over for the rest of the scene, but it might still grumble and groan) as the Sin-eater retains control over their own body. If the Geist wins, they take control, as described above.

                                                            Referencing the frenzy systems in other games is in no way equivalent to the geist. In other games, experiencing primal/emotional triggers can drive you into fear and rage - this is a fine system, it works for them, and if there was something Just Like That in geist, but fit to its themes, I'd probably support it. But that is very different from a mechanic where the geist can take over and use the character's body for its own goals. In its worst cases of abuse, that can turn into "player wants to do [this]; gm wants player to do [that] - roll for possession". Forcinng a character to act out the unrestrained fantasies of a Death archetype and then sticking the player with the consequences is a very hostile mechanic, and can quickly lead to extremely frustrating gameplay. It might be an experience some players want - and Inferno exists to supply it. There are better ways to reinforce geists themes. The social conditions, I think, is the best one I've heard so far. There was also another one some time ago in a different thread where you could bribe the geist with something similar to its archetype or manipulate it by appealing to its human remnants, which also works with the themes better. Discussions about giving the relationship with the geist mechanical teeth should start at Those points - not with possession.
