                Just responding in brief...

                Saintly was indeed an awful choice of words. In a place like the World of Darkness, that has "crapsack" written into the very fabric of reality, people with that level of idealism and incorruptibility would be incredibly rare, if not unheard of. That was a huge reason why people were throwing around the "Mary Sue" label.

                Nobody is disagreeing with the idea that Reason is a Good Thing. What is disagreeable is that Reason would be an especially potent force in the World of Darkness. There's an excellent quote in one of the Mummy books, can't recall which one, about the fact that the Cursed laugh bitterly when someone talks about humanity's progress; having lived since the Bronze Age, they can blend into society almost perfectly, because even though they have seen humanity's transition through thousands of years, witnessing the rise and fall of empires, nothing. Has. CHANGED. Not where it matters. THAT is how awful the World of Darkness is, where slaves from the early Bronze Age claim there's nothing different after all those thousands of years.

                I can support the idea of a group dedicated to the Power of Reason... with their Flame as a fragile, vulnerable thing that must be carefully guarded and tended, watched over for thousands of years and slowly fueled in hopes of one day chasing away the stormy darkness.

                What I cannot support is the idea of a group where the Flame of Reason burns so strongly that it's inevitable that it will overturn everything that makes the World of Darkness the world that it is. It's just too unthematic. It's the difference between Princesses, who do mesh so well with the World of Darkness, and Sailor Moon, which doesn't.

                The statements you are making are making Marduk seem less annoying, but still not really selling me on it at all.

                Honestly, I think that moving the "revolution of the idealists against the old men" from "near future" to "recent past" was probably not a good move. Or an outright mistake. In large part because I cannot see that working out well for the idealists at all. Seriously, you mean to tell me that the Old Men never once decided to add in all sorts of boobytraps and backdoors to keep this sort of thing from happening? If I was one of the Old Men, I would make it that being able to use any of the Mardukian technology requires a special implant - which pulls double-duty as a brain-incinerating self-destruct device, just in case I need to protect myself from my own pawns.

                Seriously, this is something all of the long-living supernatural splats repeat: you do not get old unless you are a paranoid sneaky bastard.

                I'm not saying that the Idealists couldn't have won, just that it should have cost them a hell of a lot of lives, technology, stockpiles, resources, knowledge and... well, everything. The Marduk Society should be a conspiracy on the brink, in no better shape than the Leviathans in terms of "presence". They just have the confidence that they "are destined" to rise from the ashes like a phoenix. both because of their idealism and their ideology.

                Also, yes, I know the Wake was always on in 1e. I thought it was a pretty stupid mechanic back then too.

Vampires are burnt by sunlight and fire, and thematic reference is made to fire and sunlight as the Curse of Purity (something along those lines) in Vampire 2e, but humanity isn't any purer than usual, and mortal hunters don't wield the Powers of Purity against vampires -- despite the fact that "defilement vs. humanity" is a deeply embedded Vampire theme. Likewise, the vampires' Nemeses aren't Pure-Hearted Mortals, but demon owls who want them to sacrifice their humanity.

                                We appear to be getting into personal politics. Personally, I do believe that science et al are, in general, a force for good in a overwhelmingly apathetic universe -- but the thing is that you've built your understanding of the setting on your own personal assumptions, i.e., that humanity's better nature will inevitably overcome its baser urges and that the id has been squashed by modern society.                                 Reason, science, civilization et al. sometimes allow us to rise above our base instincts.                                 Personally, at this point, I'd go back to 1e's "a civil war is on the horizon" -- it gives players more freedom, acknowledges that science is imperfect and that people don't stop rationalizing the choices their ids make just because they own cars and computers (while still allowing for a degree of idealism), and lets us keep the mermaid flesh thing around, which -- let's be honest -- is just cool.

By reducing Marduk to a pure metaphor, you dilute its value to players -- a grayer organization, one willing to take moral risks, offers more options.
                                    The point about diluting the value to the player is particularly important. Marduk as it stands is basically a beat-stick. A whole lot of story potential from 1e has been lost.
                                    Basically, you're taking the themes too far, to the point where they cease to be interesting.

                                    Originally posted by CalvaryKeter View Post
                                    Personally, at this point, I'd go back to 1e's "a civil war is on the horizon" -- it gives players more freedom, acknowledges that science is imperfect and that people don't stop rationalizing the choices their ids make just because they own cars and computers (while still allowing for a degree of idealism), and lets us keep the mermaid flesh thing around, which -- let's be honest -- is just cool.
                                    I'd propose having Marduk caught up in a new conflict, based around the "and then what?" question.
                                    Off the top of my head, I'd see two major questions resulting in four major factions.

                                    Questions
                                    What to do about the Leviathans
                                    -Kill 'em. They are inherintly monstrous, with no redeeming value.
                                    -Save 'em: Leviathans are victims of their own nature, and through the power of progress, we can find a way to redeem them.
                                    --Wake disrupting tech might not exist now, but that is not to say it could never exist.
                                    What to do with their advanced tech.
                                    -Keep it to ourselves. The world isn't ready.
                                    -Give it to the world. The world will never be ready if we don't.

                                    Factions
                                    -Helping Hand: Give the tech, and the techniques by which the tech was developed, to the world. Use our moral high ground to save the Leviathans from themselves.
                                    -Surgeons: Give the tech to the world, mandkind will ultimately better themselves. Eliminate the Leviathans. They are offer no redeeming value, and are a cancer that holds us back.
                                    -Shepherds: Keep the tech, save the Leviathans. The world lacks the judgement to use our secrets without hurting themselves. We should use the tech to uplift them to the point where we are ready, and that includes the Leviathans.
                                    -Guardians: Keep the tech, eliminate the Leviathans. Mankind isn't ready for our secrets, but we can help them along by eliminating at least one of the things preying on them and holding them back.


                                    On another note, someone suggested Leviathans having access to anti-tech powers. I think there is potential there, but instead of having advanced tools break, I think having the Leviathan attack the human aspect would be more appropriate. Instead of having the Leviathan make a gun jam, instead they make the user forget everything they know about gun safety.

                                            I cannot understate just how accurate Almarak and CavalryKeter are in their posts. Metaphors are powerful and all, and the WoD does use them, but even so, there's such a thing as going too far - and Marduk Society, as it is, goes too far.

                                            The World of Darkness goes by several core assumptions that one HAS to maintain, or else you don't have a World of Darkness game anymore. You have something else using the Story Teller System.

                                            The first: mortals control their own fate on the grand scheme, there's no "grand conspiracies" running everything major in history like some conspiracy buff's take on the Illuminati. This is an assumption Marduk is dangerously close to overthrowing.

                                            The second: power at a price. You never get something for nothing in the World of Darkness. Supernatural powers always come at a drastic price, whether that's in the form of actual banes, as in PC splats, or in moral or thematic corruption, as in the case of every Hunter conspiracy ever - from Cheiron Group's mutilation of body, mind and soul with its thaumatechnology to the Malleus Malleficarium being built on a lie by an ambitious ghoul to the Lucifuge being the children of demons. This is an assumption Marduk has forfeited, unless one counts the whole "Godtech is strengthened by Virtuosity" thing.

                                            The third: innocence is the first casualty. The more idealistic you are, the less powerful you are. If you would hold yourself back from the shadows, you inherently weaken yourself. This assumption is the primary thing that Marduk is breaking.

                                            The author of Princess: The Hopeful actually said this a couple of pages back - around page 14. Princess: The Hopeful is an idealistic, hopeful game, but it's undercut by several notions of gritty realism and cynicism, mostly that the fundamental assumption of Princess is that Princesses have the deck stacked against them and it's very uncertain if they will ever succeed. This is good, this keeps them both in line with their core focus - Hope Springs Eternal, Hope Is Strength - but at the same time it recognizes and respects the three core assumptions of the World of Darkness. It's why the game has earned respect for being a Magical Girl Game that actually fits the World of Darkness.

                                            The Marduk Society does none of that. It would be a terrible excuse for a game if the Marduk Society was actually supposed to be the player faction - and they're not! This is supposed to be Leviathan: The Tempest, the game of playing as primeval aquatic Lovecraftian monster. The Marduk Society are supposed to be the game's antagonists... and when your antagonists are portrayed as being all around better and cooler and more interesting than your protagonists, something has gone wrong somewhere.
