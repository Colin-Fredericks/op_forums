        "Well, children, gather around and allow me to tell you a little story..."

        Once upon a time, a younger, more precocious (AKA a year younger) Reaper Verse set off to start a fan splat known as Shadow: The Perverse. Given that he had written a few pages in a fit of mania and sudden inspiration, the style was overall clunky but showed promise. Unfortunately, life happened and prevented him from continuing his work in a consistent pattern, thus cursing the fan splat to the depths of his Documents folder.

        In all seriousness, I wanted to approach the World of Darkness splats by writing a creature that was deliberately patterned off of Eastern culture, focusing on Taoism and the idea of Yin and Yang as well as the myriad creatures and the theoretical person we as humans believe others view us as. Mainly, Shadows were creatures of hate and self-loathing that were spontaneously born whenever a human had a personal crisis. They were essentially a negative version of humanity that lived in a land that did not exist known as The Labyrinth. They had no reason to exist but did and were envious of their mortal counterparts for having some reason to live their own lives.

        What I did not post were my current thoughts about Shadows, mainly that, because they are creatures that should not exist, they are constantly pained by a void in their soul, seeking to fill that void by feeding on human misery. If they didn't, they would be reconsumed by the void that exists beyond the material realm and The Labyrinth.

        In a nutshell, Kingdom Hearts' Nobodies, Silent Hill's gallery of iconic monsters, and Persona's Shadows all wrapped into one neat little package. Pity that I gave up for a bit. At least now I can scavenge the cutting room floor bits and continue with some insight. :V

                                            I still have the Word document and the original post is floating around on page 7-9. The main mechanic that the Shadows had was the ability to summon The Fog, a localized phenomenon that brings a section of the Labyrinth into the material world, causing physics to go all wacky and invoke strange, arbitrary rules into the world (such as preventing people from crossing passed stop signs or caution tape, opening locked doors by presenting innocuous items, and messing around with GPS and compasses), which I feel would be a selling point for the game.

                                            An idea that I'm still working through is the backstory for why the Labyrinth is, specifically the titanic lords, known as The Guardians, that once existed and essentially controlled the first Shadows. The main thing about them is that they are based on the Ba Gua, eight representations of reality, and that Shadows came to be when a Guardian known as the Brood Mother released them from a crevace in the land.

                                            Something that was brought up in the thread was the idea of controlling lesser Shadows, which developed into the idea of Preta, hungry spirits of Buddhism that suffer. I played around with this idea and came up with their counterpart, Butterflies that are meant to lead the Shadows in their journey to continue existing.

                                            Things that I didn't write up is their unique Splat stats. I wanted to use Ying and Yang in some manner; after reading through Blood and Smoke, I feel like they would work as their respective Virtue and Vice. Shadow Integrity would be something like Existence, Identity, or Purpose, meant to represent a Shadow's tie to reality; willingly abandoning things that keep them grounded to reality slowly tears at their soul until the Void consumes them, wiping them from existence.

                                            Basically, the entire idea was to play around with the idea of how the most hated aspects of individuals would behave if they were given life. Eventually, it slowly mutated into an introspective on the meaning of being "real" and what it means to have no actual purpose in reality.

But sometimes, the Enkindling produces the opposite effect, creating a mortal who will try, at any costs, to kill the dragon. These mortals are called Slayers, and they are the bane of a dragon's existence. Fate works on the Slayer's side, aiding their strikes and protecting them from harm.
