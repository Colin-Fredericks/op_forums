                                So here are your translations, Phersus, fresh off the belt:

                                Wild Sea:

                                1) Meaning "barbaric sea" or "monstrous sea" — a dangerous or unforgiving sea: ursufgutha makaf
                                ursufgutha-makaf
                                ursufguthamakaf

                                2) Meaning "untamed sea" or "natural sea" — a sea as it is found in the wilderness:
                                i'ilth makaf
                                i'ilth-makaf
                                i'ilthakaf


                                Deep Waters:
                                bhurudh illu
                                bhurudh-illu
                                bhrudhillu


                                Soul Glass:
                                zi ansak
                                zi-ansak
                                zinsak


                                Moon Soul:
                                iduth zi
                                iduth-zi
                                iduthzi


                                Rock Fortress:
                                dhuunum na
                                dhuunum-na
                                dhuunuma


                                Ancestral:
                                affag (yes, this is really how it's translated, it's one of those "unfortunate words")


                                Seawall/Groyne:
                                niksuhumsagarfath


                                Devourers:
                                agrih (agrihim if a spirit and plural, agrim if singular and a spirit)


                                Fire-Possesed:
                                izilu (if a Ridden)
                                iziduhu (if they are creatures that inherently have fire as a trait of theirs)
                                izag (if they use or own some sort of fire or flame)


                                Jewel In the Crown:
                                ninzukhinaha
                                ninzukh-ina-aha
                                ninzukh ina aha


                                I will go into detail as to what these mean later. As for why I have given three versions of certain words, it's a trait I noticed in First Tongue that was lost in Sumerian — hyphens, spaces and affixes/suffixes all determine a word's relation to the nature of something. For eexample, i'ilth makaf means "a sea in the wild", i'ilth-makaf means "a sea that is untamed (but that's not necessarily it's whole nature)", and i'ilthakaf means "a sea that is inherently wild and feral".

                                And, straight from the devs as translations for "Protectorate":

                                Gi-Uru: A place under protection; another name for a Protectorate.
                                Zahhiri: Also a name for a Protectorate, but carries a symbolic, more militaristic connontation.

                                    And before I forget:

                                    nuharul: Thing of no place (lit. "something inherently not of any world or place of existence") — a strong insult somewhere between "bastard" and "piece of shit" in terms of severity, but HIGHLY insulting to use on something high-ranking like a spirit; a harsher, more impolite term for a Magath.

                                    muthrahabalthu: Spirit fight — usually meaning a worthless fight between spirits. It literally means "mote challenge", and like urdurhabalthu and wolf cubs, it refers to a senseles feud that's more reserved for motes trying to pick each other off.

                                    "Lal ku nukuk!": "Show me your teeth!" — werewolf version of, "Come at me, bro!"

                                    "Duzag lal nuku!": "Show yourself to me!" — werewolf version of, "Come at me, bro!" when dealing with spirits

                                                    In recognition of the removal of unihar from 2e canon, I have decided to take the term and give it a new meaning for use in 2e games. Anyone can feel free to use it, devs included:

                                                    unihar: One of the absolute worst insults ranking up there with "shiniduth", a unihar is a foul name for an illegitimate, unwanted or otherwise looked-down upon child or pack member. It has a meaning similar to the original meaning of "bastard", but is far worse — it comes from an ancient superstition that two Uratha mating would produce a vile, horrible monster that would leave the mother infertile and devour the rest of the pack. Said superstition is believed to originate from the Ivory Claws (who once used it to scare their tribemates away from certain partners), but it has spread throughout both sides and is considered unacceptable in werewolf society. To call someone a unihar is to call them a monster for ever being born, a potential liability and a nuharul all at once, and people will look at you like you're insane if you use it in affectionate terms.

                                                    And if you call a spirit a unihar, run. Just run. It means something truly, truly terrible to those of the Hisil, to the point where you ST is justified in siccing a horde of otherwise-peaceful nature spirits on you if you use it on them. So horrible is the meaning and implication, the spirits have made sure it's lost to time, and hearing the word can send them into a frenzy.
