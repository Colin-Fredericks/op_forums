                                                06-20-2016, 02:40 AM
                                                The Last Dance Motley settles into a nice weekend, busy as always but also exciting. Lori has a moment of elation (and mild fear), Calamity takes on a task, Jacob helps an unlikely friend in need, and Insincere Marguerite struggles with her loss of confidence. Speaking of that Darkling, her story did not really take place on camera, as these stories tend to not, but takes place at the end of the session.

                                                Gawain’s player had to miss, but this should not be a pattern. They should be back next time.

                                                Session LVIII

                                                Lorelei’s Diary

                                                Okay, so my mood has vacillated wildly over the past day or so, due to circumstances mostly beyond my control. So if this entry seems a little chaotic or disjointed, that would be why. Fair warning.

                                                (Why do I consistently write these journal entries as though I expect someone to read them? I don’t know. It would be a colossal embarrassment to find out that all of my deepest darkest secrets were known to someone else… so why do I persist in taking a conversational tone in what is essentially a private reflection? Maybe it’s the drama queen in me, plotting my life out in a series of monologues and dialogues. Maybe as a Changeling, I find it easier to think of my life as a story to be told. Or maybe I’m just internally lonely and a little weird. Who can say?)

                                                So the last time I wrote anything in here, Gawain and I were preparing to head over to Monica’s house to see if she needed any healing (and to see if she was holding a grudge). Unfortunately, Gawain was called away unexpectedly at the last moment, and was unable to join me. I’m not sure what came up; he didn’t say. In any case, I was prepared to head over to Monica’s place all by my lonesome when another idea struck me.

                                                I’d noticed that Marguerite had been acting sort of down lately. It was almost like she’d lost her confidence on some level (if you can believe that). I’d thought about asking her about it, but I didn’t want to call attention to it and embarrass her. Considering that my own actions earlier this week probably contributed to her funk, I thought it would be nice if I could do something to draw her out a bit.

                                                I asked her if she would mind accompanying me to Monica’s house. “Gawain can’t make it after all, and I’d really feel better if I had someone I can trust along, just in case Monica’s still holding a grudge,” I said, injecting just a bit of nervous tension into my voice. I didn’t really believe that Monica would do anything to hurt me, of course… but you can never tell when Changelings are concerned. Some of us have odd triggers in our subconscious.

                                                Marguerite happily agreed to accompany me, and the two of us sped across town in my ‘vette. She was awfully touchy-feely on the way over there, and I think she appreciated both the opportunity to spend time with me and the minor vote of confidence I gave her. When we got to Monica’s place, Dominika answered the door with a glare. “You’ve got a lot of nerve, showing up here after what your girlfriend did to mine last night,” she said with no small amount of venom.

                                                I sighed softly. “I’m not here to fight, Dominika,” I assured her. “I just wanted to see Monica, to make sure she’s all right.”

                                                Dominika’s stony look didn’t soften. “She’s fine,” she almost spat out. “I took care of her.”

                                                I smiled. “I’m glad,” I said softly. “Look, can I talk with the two of you for just a few minutes, please? I’d like to make sure this doesn’t go any further…”

                                                Dominika looked like she was prepared to reply with something suitably scathing, but Monica’s voice called softly from deeper inside. “Just let her in, Dominika. We need to talk this out anyway.”

                                                Dominika grudgingly allowed the two of us to enter, and we found Monika in the living room. She offered us seats, and we sat down to discuss the previous evening’s fisticuffs like civilized people.

                                                It was remarkably civil in the end, actually. Monica apologized for making a scene at the Dream House, and seemed really embarrassed by the whole situation. I told her (and Dominika) that I understood why she was upset by Halley’s actions, but that we at the Dream House have to work to reestablish our status as a haven in the wake of the Chalcedony incident.

                                                The rest of the conversation mostly consisted of Monica reliving her fight with D with something like fondness. Dominika and I exchanged long-suffering glances while she went on about the destruction, and I think the Caribbean beauty started to calm down and relax a bit at that point. I made sure to mention that Monica had given as well as she got, and that I had spent a considerable amount of time tending to D’s wounds the previous evening. I think Dominika was sympathetic, having gone through something similar herself.

                                                “It wasn’t as even as all that,” Monica admitted. “De la Rocha has gotten a lot stronger recently. She could never have put me through a wall before, no matter how good she was.”

                                                I nodded. “We’ve all developed a few new tricks recently,” I replied, “I just hope D’s abilities don’t get her into trouble.”

                                                Yeah. What a laugh that turned out to be!

                                                I’m getting ahead of myself. After Marguerite and I said our goodbyes, we hurried back to the Dream House. I confess that I might have been speeding a little in the hope that I could make it home before Joan and Andromeda dropped by. I had promised Joan that I’d keep Andromeda company while she was off on Summer Court business today, and then I’d been called away to deal with the Monica situation, and I didn’t want Joan to think that I’d forgotten about my promise, or that I was avoiding her or anything like that.

                                                We got back to the house about five minutes before Joan and Andromeda arrived. I greeted them at the door, and Andromeda’s face lit up when she saw me. She gave me a big hug and thanked me for agreeing to spend the day with her. After an almost painful moment of consideration, Joan stepped forward and offered me a chaste embrace as well. It was the first time she’d been openly affectionate with me in front of others, and that didn’t escape my notice.

                                                “Thank you, Lorelei,” she said softly.

                                                “You know you only had to ask, sweetie,” I said warmly. “Spending time with Andromeda isn’t a chore; it’s a privilege.”

                                                Joan gave me a genuine smile and a nod, then turned to go. “I hope this business will not take long. At the very least, I shall see you tonight at the show.” Then she took her leave.

                                                Andromeda, Marguerite and I made our way upstairs to where Julia was once more painting in the attic. Andromeda revealed to us that she and Joan are officially engaged now, but that they’re keeping it a secret from the rest of the Freehold for several reasons. At first, it was because Joan had taken a lot of flak for having a “blind spot” in Andromeda, but now… well, Andromeda very sweetly said that she didn’t want to make the announcement now because she didn’t want to steal Marguerite and Julia’s thunder. I think Julia was touched by this sentiment.

                                                There was also some mention of the curious momentum that seems to accompany engagements. It’s been remarked that once a single engagement is announced within a circle of friends, it seems like others inevitably follow. Joan and Andromeda also don’t want to seem like they’re just getting on the marriage bandwagon. There was a fair amount of giggling among us as we resolved not to mention this strange phenomenon around D.

                                                One thing that’s probably worth mentioning is that Andromeda kept calling Joan “Lindsay.” I can only assume that this was her name before she was Taken. Since it’s not widely known, I can also assume that Joan doesn’t want people finding that out. I think the others understand this as well, but maybe I should talk with them just to be on the safe side.

                                                Andromeda was pretty upset that she had let it slip, and as an act of contrition she told us all that her own former name had been Rebecca. In the interest of full disclosure, I told her that my name had once been Juliette (and told her that she could share that with Joan if she wants to). She smiled and expressed the opinion that it was a beautiful name.

                                                Before I get to the other big news, I wanted to mention that there were a few times when Andromeda’s melancholy showed through… mostly in regards to the Table. She seemed almost desperate to find a way to use it safely, so that she could help more people like she helped Joan and Jolie. My motley-mates and I tried to oppose this line of thought gently but firmly, as none of us want to risk Andromeda’s well-being again, but she was adamant about researching the possibility. Though I’m sure Joan is aware of her fiancé’s feelings on this subject, maybe I should mention this conversation to her just the same.

                                                Okay, so shortly after Jacob joined us in the attic, Andromeda got a phone call. It was one of those infuriating situations where you know someone is talking about something very important, but you’re only getting half of the conversation, and your brain tries to make up likely scenarios about what’s really going on. Add to that the fact that Andromeda kept glancing over at me and saying things like “yes, she’s fine,” and “no, I don’t think she’s heard yet,” and you can understand why I was a big tense ball of curiosity after a few short moments.

                                                “What’s going on?” I interrupted when Andromeda paused.

                                                She shrugged. “Joan says that de la Rocha might be in some sort of trouble with the Summer Court,” she said after a moment.

                                                My heart plummeted. “Is she okay?” I asked.

                                                “She’s not hurt,” Andromeda hastened to reassure me.

                                                “What happened?” I asked.

                                                Andromeda looked sheepish. “She… she punched Viscount Augustine in the face,” she admitted after a moment.

                                                I know I blinked a couple of times at that. “She… what?” I asked.

                                                “And menaced him with a church pew,” Andromeda added. I’m not sure, but I think I spotted a glimmer of amusement in her dark eyes.

                                                I sputtered for a moment, then regained my composure. “Is that Joan? Can I talk to her?” I asked frantically.

                                                It was Joan, and I could talk to her, as it turned out. Andromeda handed me her phone, and I held it up to my ear with trembling hands. “Joan, what’s going on? Is she all right? Does she need my help?”

                                                Joan’s voice cut across my increasingly hysterical questions with quiet assurance. “Calm down, Lorelei,” she said softly, “de la Rocha is fine. She stopped by the church during her lunch break so that she could join the discussion regarding our Court’s stance on the Day and Night courtiers within the Freehold. The discussion became rather heated. As your motley includes a Night Courtier, De la Rocha felt it necessary to remind Viscount Augustine – with some vigor – that the Dream House is meant to be a place of sanctuary for all changelings. The Viscount implied… he said…” Her voice faltered for the first time, and I thought I detected a hint of anger there.

                                                “What is it, sweetie?” I asked, trying to remain calm.

                                                Joan’s voice was tight when she continued. “He said that the Last Dance motley advertises the Dream House as a sanctuary mostly so that you will continue to enjoy a wide selection of lovers,” she finished after a moment. “De la Rocha took issue with this opinion, and struck the King of Summer in retaliation. She then proceeded to pick up a full-sized pew – which had previously been bolted to the floor – and threaten to ‘beat the bitch out of him,’ as I believe she put it.” By the end of this sentence, the anger had been replaced by tacit approval.

                                                “Oh shit,” I said in response.

                                                “Indeed,” Joan agreed.

                                                “What are they going to do to her?” I asked once I’d wiped the tears out of the corners of my eyes.

                                                Joan thought for a moment. “I do not know. She is officially being detained at the church for now, though I doubt anyone would try to stop her if she decided to leave. There will have to be some punitive action, of course… she struck the monarch. But Augustine himself has already admitted that his insult was unwarranted, so I doubt her punishment will be too severe.”

                                                I sniffed with relief. “Is she able to talk? Can I speak with her?” I asked (a bit redundantly).

                                                Joan agreed, and a few moments later, my honey’s voice came to me over the phone line, sounding chipper and incredibly pleased with herself. “Hey, babe,” she said brightly.

                                                “Don’t you ‘hey babe’ me, missy,” I started in, my fear making me a bit sharp. “What the hell was that all about?”

                                                D’s voice faltered. “He said…”

                                                “I know what he said,” I interrupted. “They’re just [u]words/[u], D. I can take it. I’m a big girl. I don’t want you getting in trouble with your Court because…”

                                                This time she interrupted me. “He as good as called you a whore, Lori,” she said defiantly. “Nobody says that about you. Nobody disrespects you. Not where I can hear them.”

                                                I let out a shuddering sigh. This wasn’t how I’d wanted the conversation to go. “Can I start over?” I asked with a quaver.

                                                “Sure,” D said, sounding a little confused.

                                                “Thank you,” I said fervently. “Thank you so much for looking out for me like that. You are the best girlfriend ever, and I am lucky to have a knight in shining armor like you to take care of me. I’m sorry I snapped at you before. I just get so worried about you sometimes. You… you punched the King, D. The monarch of your own Court. I don’t want you to get in trouble over a little insult. I’m not worth that.”

                                                “You are to me,” she said after a moment, “and you always will be, and nothing anybody says is ever going to change that.”

                                                I was crying by that point, and I didn’t care that the rest of the motley and Andromeda were staring at me from across the room. “My God, I didn’t think it was possible for me to be any more in love with you,” I whispered into the phone.

                                                “I feel the same way,” she replied, “but, y’know, I can’t get too mushy or emotional right now. I’m kinda under a microscope at the moment.”

                                                “Are they going to keep you there?” I asked.

                                                “They might try,” she said with a grin I could hear.

                                                “Be serious,” I admonished.

                                                “I don’t think so,” she replied. “The Viscount has already apologized for what he said, and admitted that he was out of line. They’ll probably give me a slap on the wrist at worst. But my lunch hour is almost up, so they’ll have to let me go soon.”

                                                “You’ll be back for the show tonight?” I asked.

                                                “Wouldn’t miss it,” she promised.

                                                “Good,” I said. “Don’t make any plans for later on tonight. I intend to show you my gratitude in a variety of ways.” I let that hang in the air for a few moments as I moved as far away from the others as I could, turned away, and covered my mouth. “Te amo, Maria,” I whispered into the phone.

                                                “Te amo, Juliette,” she replied, “siempre.” And then she was gone. Joan and I exchanged pleasantries for a few minutes, and then I handed the phone back to Andromeda and walked back to the motley in a daze.

                                                Of course I had to explain the situation to the rest of them. Though I was still worried about D and about whatever punishment the Summer Court might level against her (Summer Court punishments can be pretty severe… at least according to Ace), I couldn’t suppress a little shudder of glee every time I thought about how D is willing to fight for me. She punched a king. Because he insulted me. It’s… I don’t think the word “flattering” really covers it.

                                                So I futzed around for the rest of the afternoon, pretending to get ready for the show but really just bouncing back and forth between anxiety and elation over D’s assault on one of the most powerful people in the Freehold. Joan stopped by a little before we officially opened the doors for the show to check on Andromeda, and pulled me aside for a private chat.

                                                After a brief kiss hello, she put a hand on my shoulder. “I wanted to let you know that the penalty levied against de la Rocha was not as bad as it could have been. She is prohibited from participating in Court functions for one month.”

                                                I sighed with relief. “That really was just a slap on the wrist, wasn’t it?” I asked.

                                                Joan nodded. “Even so, de la Rocha almost declined to accept it. She demanded that Viscount Augustine apologize to you personally for his slight upon your character.”

                                                That gave me pause. I mean, it’s nice that D’s concerned about my honor or whatever, but, well… it seems like maybe this is a little much. On the few occasions where I’ve met Viscount Augustine, I thought we’d gotten along pretty well. I admit that it stung when I heard what he’d said about me, but I don’t want to embarrass him or anything like that. He’s supposed to stop by some time this weekend. I hope I can think of some way to make it less… awkward before then.

                                                In any case, D strutted into the Dream House shortly before the show, still looking like the cat that ate the… you know what, I’m gonna drop that metaphor before it gets too weird. I ran up to her – probably making a complete fool of myself in front of our patrons in the process – threw my arms around her and covered her face in kisses. “My hero,” I whispered when I came up for air.

                                                “Damn, babe,” she said with a smirk, “I should punch Augustine more often!”

                                                “Don’t joke,” I admonished gently. “D, what you did for me today was incredibly sweet, and I am thrilled beyond measure to know that you’re looking out for me like that, but it wasn’t very politically savvy. You have to be careful now.”

                                                She shrugged. “It’ll blow over. We’re the Summer Court, remember? We’re all about anger. You think this is the first time someone’s lost their temper with the monarch?”

                                                I shuddered as I remembered the stories we’ve heard about Lucien Torrez and his “disagreement” with the previous Summer King, but I let it pass. “I’ve got to head backstage to make sure everything is set for the show,” I told her. “I’ll find you in the audience after my number. And like I said… don’t make any other plans. You’re mine tonight.”

                                                For once she didn’t wince at the possessive tone, she just tipped me a wink, kissed me quickly, and whispered “knock ‘em dead tonight, babe.”

                                                I’ve been wanting to do a sort of soulful blues-rock number for a while now, and had looked into performing Bob Seger’s “Turn the Page,” but my backup singers kept dissolving into giggles whenever I sang the line about “all the same old clichés, is that a woman or a man.” I guess that line is pretty silly when applied to someone with my physique. In checking out Mr. Seger’s subsequent career, however, I came across a rollicking number called “Old Time Rock and Roll.” It was actually released a couple of weeks before I was Taken, but I’d never heard it. Since it’s about nostalgia and good old-fashioned rock music (both near and dear to my heart), I decided to give it a whirl. The crowd really seemed to enjoy it, though I’m sure the very short skirt and fishnets helped.

                                                I changed into something (slightly) more sedate after my number and worked the crowd for a while. Halley was there, looking miserable but safe. I tried my best to comfort her. She still thinks that Monica and Dominika are making a colossal mistake by dating each other, and I think she really believes that (it’s not just jealousy on her part). I sympathized with her, but repeated my opinion that sometimes people have to make their own mistakes.

                                                Molly Nuit was also there, and much to my surprise she looked like she was getting totally wasted. I don’t know how many of those Black Widow drinks she had last night, but I know it was alot. I gave Jacob a questioning glance, and he assured me that he had cut her off when it became apparent that she was overindulging. He’d also called a cab for her to make sure she made it home safely. He explained to the motley the next morning that Molly had been feeling a bit lonely lately, and that her unique situation (having been completely remade by Master Darke less than a year ago) left her unprepared to relate to other people. We all agreed to do what we could to help her, of course.

                                                After working the crowd for a while (making sure to speak with Joan and Andromeda and Flores), I made my way to D’s table. I climbed into her lap and put my head on her shoulder. Throwing Gloria an apologetic look, I said “I hope you don’t mind if I monopolize her tonight.”

                                                Gloria chuckled. “She’s your girlfriend, Lori. You don’t have to justify anything to me.”

                                                        I was reminded of a discussion that was held before on this thread on what our main heroes would be if they were in Changeling: The Dreaming, rather than The Lost. It got me thinking on what they would be second edition of The Lost, and it lead to me compiling the list below. The Kiths are pretty much accurate to how they are now, given that in second edition a Kith represents the role the Changeling led (effectively replacing Seeming there), but Seemings would probably be a bit different.

                                                        From what I remember, there was a discussion based on this topic before, but I wanted to throw my two cents to the thread before moving on to something more pertinent.

                                                        Gawain would be a Fairest, given that he took charge and risked himself to help save the dreamer Gawain as well as Calamity. He could also be a Grimm, given the emphasis he has on having played the role of Gawain and the Grimm's role-based nature, but Grimms kinda confuse me so don't quote me on that. :-p

                                                        Lorelei would be a Wizened; though she had help from her friends, it was her skill with her voice and her choice of songs that allowed her to convince her Gentry to allow her to leave.

                                                        Ace would be an Elemental or a Beast; it's hard to say which because him biting his Keeper could be taken as him giving in to his snake-like nature (an Elemental) or defying the rules in order to help someone (Beast).

                                                        Calamity would be a Beast; while she did betray others in Snowglobe (a Darkling's method), it was her lashing out and breaking the rules that allowed her to escape. Betrayal was all but bred into those within Snowglobe.

                                                        Jacob would be a Darkling; it isn't a typical Darkling escape, but Rime's decision to not do as his Lady wished, and the general reaction Rook had, shows a clear betrayal. He could also be am Ogre, given all the abuse he had to endure before he escaped, but Ogres are usually those who plan their escapes from what I've read.

                                                        Insincere Marguerite would be a Fairest, ironically; she was released into the world, but she wanted to expand her potential, to become strong like Mama Lion and be the greatest thief, which is pretty much Fairest 101 in second edition.

                                                        Ironically, De La Rocha would likely still be a Darkling given how she pretty much chased Twitch out of the Hedge, following how Darklings need a betrayal to escape.

                                                        Julia would probably be a Wizened; it's hard to say since aside from her painting her way through the Hedge, and thus using her skills to their fullest, I don't think we've read much about her escape.
