    A Krewe Based on the Ulgan

    12-16-2013, 08:45 AM
    I had occasion to be reading Promethean last night, including the section on Ulgan. And this paragraph jumped out at me about its creator:

    "Erlik was the first of the Kara Kam — the black shamans who traffic with the realm of the spirits. He taught the Kara Kam how to cross over the wall between the material world and the spirit realm. First, they had to become aware of the spirit world. Each underwent a visionary experience before they came into their powers, each dreaming that Erlik’s spirit servants dragged them over the threshold and tore them to pieces before putting them back together. When they awoke from this dream, the Kara Kam had the power to speak with the spirits and to touch them."

    Now where it says spirits, Promethean is referring to Hisil spirits, this paragraph came right after one saying that this Erlik fellow had been sent to the land of the dead. Anyway, I was reminded of this paragraph from Geist:

    "Every single one of the Bound manifested some sort of supernatural connection with the energy of death before the Bargain ... sometimes there's an event that triggers it perhaps an echo or a precursor to the Bargain that binds them to a geist, or maybe an encounter with some supernatural force)..."

    Implication: "Majorly fucked with by a ghost or geist" is enough to qualify as the necessary connection.

    Ergo: This could easily make for a krewe. But a krewe driven first and foremost by the geists, including those that are not currently bound to a host. Those to become Bound are called upon by being haunted enough to tie them to the energy of the Underworld, and then their death orchestrated - the offer of the Bargain is accompanied by an ordeal, or at least the perception of one, making the position of Sin-Eater something that's earned, not just taken to avoid death. Obviously, this is a pattern that's somewhat dependent on being supported by mortal mythology, but mortal mythology talks about death quite a lot - and somewhere back in a past even the geists don't really remember were common seeds that gave rise to both human and krewe legends of Erlik and his servants.

    Mythology:

    Creation Myth: Tengri, the Sky-Father, saw a piece of earth with a face like those of the gods, and chose to make man. But his creation, Erlik, was full of madness and violence, and had to be driven away from the world into the lands of the dead. Then Tengri tried again, and created Ulgan, and from Ulgan came all the people who live in the world today. But the children of Erlik lived on, and in time were joined by those of the race of Ulgan whose lives failed them. Hearing of the living world from these dead children of Ulgan, the children of Erlik wanted to experience it for themselves - and learned how to cut a space into living men that could be filled with them - allowing them to live in the world of substance, whilst granting those they joined with the power to reach those who had gone before them. (Other than that, the krewe's mythology would probably stay close to other Siberian legends).

    Metaphysics: Humans are the children of Ulgan, distinguished best by their deeds and other marks on the world. Those deeds and marks live on after their bodies die: the same but not quite the same. Geists are the children of Erlik, brothers and sisters to humanity. Some of each race have the spiritual wherewithal to reach between worlds - to them falls the responsibility to care for their tribes and ancestors. Members of the krewe believe that every geist has one bone in its body which is material, and this has relaced the corresponding bone in their own body.

    Aspects: Geists who join with Bound are themselves the equivalents of shamans, and tasked by Erlik with finding their counterparts among the children of Ulgan. Their aspects reflect this: they mirror embodiments of different responsibilities (and Archetypes to some extent), but don't claim the kinds of specific identity or changes to identity that some krewe-bound geists do.

    Joining:

    It should be noted that this krewe which I haven't come up with a name for yet) very rarely recruits Sin-Eaters from outside - instead, it orchestrates the death and Bargain of a viable host, and recruits them as they die and return - the geist in this equation may be an existing member who has lost their partner, or an unfettered geist who is recruited, and goes through an intitiation of its own at the same time of the Bargain. Since the kinds of person that gets chosen tends to be one who would accept the line about this being a vocation, and the geist is altered into an embodiment of that vocation, the result is a higher Synergy than might be expected from a Bargain that's largely imposed by force.

    Should a Sin-Eater seek them out with a view to joining, he wouldn't necessarily be turned away - though it would have to be both the Sin-Eater and his geist who wanted in. Joining would be accompanied by an initiation ritual during which she and her geist would be symbolically separated and reunited. (It would also involve killing him and letting him revive - given the circumstances, this might be exempt from the usual penalty to Synergy this entails - or it may not, and leave recruited Sin-Eaters disadvantaged and regarded as evidence for why the usual method is better).

    Ethos:

    Ban: Only shamans are qualified to deal with the spirit world; everyone else should be prevented from doing so - unless their interest signals that they might make a good shaman of course. Some things are too important to life to be violated: betrayal of kin, betrayal of hospitality, that sort of thing; everyone has to abide by this, but when shamans break these rules the consequences are spiritual as well as social, so they apply all the harder. While it's perfectly proper for shamans to profit from their work, and cultivate a fearsome enough reputation to profit well, they can't actively exploit those who've sought their aid.

    Duty: They're shamans; their duties differ little from those you'd expect of shamans. They act as guides to the dead, leading them to the Underworld. They dispel hauntings and combat other spiritual menaces to the best of their ability. They deal with spiritual afflicions of those around them.

    Destiny and Bane: I'm not sure what these would be; they're a group that's rooted in the way things are (as they see it). Similarly, I'm not sure of an eschatological element to Siberian mythology that would make a good parallel in the krewe's ethos.

    Benefits:

    Tier 1: Deathsoul Intitiation is a common one, and opting into the Ethos Commitment is almost universal. Pooled Merits is only rarely applied - most members work alone, and take payment from clients rather than each other - that's less true in modern times, as nomadic lifestyles have become rarer.

    Tier 2: Key Initiation (Stigmatic, unless the group is Tier 3, in which case Shamanic). Krewe Status is likely to remain an informal affair.

    Tier 3: If the krewe has reached the point of being considered Tier 3, then a new Shamanic key will have been forged, which extends a Sin-Eater's powers into the world of spirits. Mythic Aspect is unlikely, as the founders are long dead, and the geists take on types of identity rather than claiming to be the founders' geists returned (even if they are). Mythic Factors is pretty common though. Merit Mastery would apply to Allies, Mentor, and Retainer (which might at high levels be ghostly or spiritual allies).

    Shamanic Key:

    If the krewe has advanced to the point where it can make this, then the general shape of what it can do is as follows:

    Shamanic Boneyard: The user can perceive loci, verges, and spirits of the Hisil within the area of the boneyard; with more difficulty, this may also detect Ridden, Hosts, or Werewolves. It wouldn't convey any ability to see into the Hisil itself.

    Shamanic Caul: The use unbinds his spirit from his body, allowing it to project into Twilight, and at high levels into the Hisil if performed at a locus. He also gains the ability to physically interact with spirits.

    Shamanic Curse: The user can cage up a target's actions by laying taboos and bans on them.

    Shamanic Marionette: The user gains authority over the spirits of the Hisil, being able to communicate with them, bind them, and banish them. At higher levels, this extends to the Ridden, including at least some chance of ridding Urged (though not Claimed) of their tormenter.

    Shamanic Oracle: The user can see between worlds, and identify anything touched by the forces of the Hisil, including any Resonance of an area. Scrutinising a spirit would reveal its type and possibly its ban.

    Shamanic Pit: There are especially dark and destructive spirits in the Shadow Realm, sometimes going by the name of Maeljin; the user can find these spirits, and damage the spiritual fabric of the Hisil and the Earth to make it suitable for their influence (note that almost any actions of this kind will violate the Ethos Ban, as well as getting you messily torn apart by werewolves, as often as is required to get it to take).

    Shamanic Rage: The user can pour the ephemeral substance of the Hisil into a living target, or tear it out of a spirit - either does plenty of harm.

    Shamanic Shroud: The user gains additional protection against the powers of spirits. Maybe also Ridden. Probably not werewolves.

      *Toddles in*

      One suggestion? Shamanistic Caul? Your listed power is nearly identical to the Elemental Oracle, though without it explained in further detail, I'm not entirely sure whether you're making a power with less risk in it's use, or just keeping with your theme. (Interesting ideas though, surely.) Might I suggest, since the over-arcing idea behind The Caul is to invite the Geist more intimately into your person, and change his form, (rather than removing aspects of it; in your case, a soul.) that rather than remove his soul for venturing outward, you can change different aspects (according to level, of course) of your person into a Spirit of sorts? Primevil Caul would have some excellent ideas for the power-scaling I'm thinking of. Another idea might be that your form changes to be the perfect recepticle for Spirit Posession; where The Caul allows you to make a sort of "Secondary Bargain" with local Spirits; giving them the gift of the Flesh, in exchange for other-wordly abilities; which scale upward with your command of the Caul. (I.E- You can handle "bigger" spirits, as your command of the Caul increases) The inherent risk of course, would be that the Spirit might break the Bargain you made with it, and try to take full control. (You'll notice that many, if not most of the Manifestations listed are really double-edged swords in their use.)

      My other thought was your Synergy note; about how Sin Eaters in this krewe might start off with higher Synergies than most Sin Eaters. You're already colouring outside the lines, so there's no need to compare at this point. (No offense: seriously.) But rather, if you are going to compare, perhaps saying that Sin Eaters that join and run in this Krewe have much better likelihood to INCREASE their Synergy than most. (As you'll note in the Synergy section, on average, many of the Bound actually drop in Synergy a few degrees, before reconciling with their Geist.) If you have Sin Eater's who are properly prepared for the Bargain as it is, then they're much more likely to be accepting of the alien behavior a Geist can exhibit.

      Just some thoughts. Cheerios!
