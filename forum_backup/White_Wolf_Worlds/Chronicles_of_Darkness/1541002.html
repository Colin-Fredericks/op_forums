    Pre-Existent Souls and Moonchild Operations

    08-25-2024, 05:27 PM
    Following are a series of thoughts regarding the Arcana involved in incarnating a soul. For the sake of discussion, two processes are primarily discussed. The first is that of attaching a soul to a natural pregnancy, whether that be the mage's own pregnancy or that of a target. The second is that of incarnating a soul into a living construct that has been manifested ahead of time using other Arcana. There are of course, even more esoteric processes that come to mind, like giving souls to machine intelligences, but I have largely limited my thoughts on the matter to those two modes.

    Right off the bat, it would seem like the Arcanum involved in incarnation itself would be Death. The 1e write-up mentioned how Death attracted the soul into matter, and Death as a whole has generally been depicted as the Arcanum that deals with souls. It seems like Death 4 would be required to permanently affix a soul to a Pattern, whether that be the Pattern of an unborn child or that of a human fashioned through the use of the Life Arcanum. I cannot really see Death 5 being involved, given how no Making is involved, but I can also see why some Storytellers would want that level of proficiency in the Arcanum being required.

    A significant question, though, is whether or not use of the Mind Arcanum would also be required if one desired a fully functional human being to manifest. In the case of a natural pregnancy, it seems like it would not be necessary, but I can see an artificial human requiring the application of the Mind Arcanum to demonstrate sapience.

    Of course, constructing a living body would probably be Life 5, given the use of Making.

    What are your thoughts on the matter?

                              The Practice of Dynamics, the first Imperial Practice archmasters get to grips with, is used to create spells that dynamically alter themselves in response to different conditions, vary in effects based on who encounters them, and otherwise update themselves without Awakened oversight. The Practice of Dominions, the last Imperial Practice that is both well-defined in its effects and confirmed to exist, is the one archmasters use to create subsouls like ochemata or Chantries from their own souls.

                              The soul the Awakened work with is canonically made up of the five Subtle Arcana plus some special sauce that the Tremere attribute to the Sixth and Seventh Watchtowers; Mummy likewise describes the soul as being the product of binding the five powers together, with the undifferentiated stuff of manifest existence both differentiating into those powers and no longer holding them together once the soul disappears upon death without magical assistance.

                                  I have a vague recollection that Imperial Mysteries posited the means of creating a soul ex nihilo as an enduring mystery even for archmasters. I think that can hold up even if one thinks it should be within the capacity of even the least of the Imperial Practices, given the conceit of quintessences; the exclusivity is the kind of thing that can be used to maintain many examples of spells that could be theoretically cast but as yet none of the handful of archmasters has managed to either divine or acquire the components necessary to actually bring about.

                                  Although I'll admit, it is a bit funny how the game treats the soul as a bit sacrosanct in that regard, given how it's otherwise a bit irreverent on the topic. Sure, the soul is integral to a person being fully functional, but in the same way a vital organ can be (including the fact that it can be replaced with one that came from another). But it's not positioned as the truest and most essential aspect or form of a person, and the very notion of an afterlife is still an open question. As it is, the most concrete thing that comes out of obstacles for mages to create one is creating complications and moral quandaries for resurrection, conjuring entire new humans, and dealing with the aftermath of Reapers (or anybody who might latch on to the idea that rendering a mage soulless is the most sure way to disable their powers).

                                                      I think that mages not knowing whether or not souls can be created or not was only established in a sidebar in the 2e core rulebook. There was little on the topic in 1e, apart from the idea that souls fell into the Fallen World from the Supernal World. Some of the text seemed to imply that souls had some point of origination, but the book did not come to close to making a definitive statement on the topic. The one thing that was pretty solid was the soul has to have some form of pre-existence in the Supernal World, as it was trapped in the Fallen World after the Abyss reared its head. Of course, the construct which the game refers to by the term soul would not really align all of the ideas of incarnation magic that have been discussed in this thread. Perhaps Mind would be the key factor then, given its ties to identity? Other than that, it still seems like it is possible that the soul would be a relevant factor in a working that is modelled after a classic Moonchild operation or the like. According to the Chronicles of Darkness core rulebook, you need a soul in order to utilize Supernatural Merits, and obviously you need a soul in order to Awaken, even if you can retain your magic should your soul get stolen. As an aside, I almost feel like such a working could be used as flavor for a Proximi Dynasty or something.
