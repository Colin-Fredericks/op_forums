                Originally posted by ShadowcatX View Post
                Again, this is incorrect, aggravated damage is tracked separately from lethal/bashing, so 3 Agg and 2 lethal is different than 5 Agg in theory (though perhaps not in practice). You can argue that the lack of a difference in practice is important, but I would counter that it was future proofing from the designers that just never ended up mattering.
                It literally isn't. The closest this assertion gets to being correct is that in 1e the corebook says that Structure lost to aggravated damage can't be repaired except through supernatural powers, which 1) refers to aggravated damage as a thing supernatural creatures can deal, 2) has not been reproduced in any iteration of 2e's presentation of the mechanics, and 3) is never referenced, alluded to, or relied upon by any subsequent mechanic involving damaging objects thereafter.

                The way weapon damage works changed between editions, but we can go all the way back to Armory, where mechanics for explosives first showed up, for comparison:

                In 1e, damaging explosives all benefit from the Extra Successes rule on attacks (usually equal to the explosive's Force rating) and deal bashing or lethal damage; they apply their Force rating as automatic damage to anyone who isn't hit by a direct attack in with the weapon in the primary blast area and as a dicepool (which also benefits from any 8-Again or 9-Again rule the weapon applies) against anyone in the secondary radius. They can have the Armor Piercing quality, which ignores points of Armor and Durability the same even in an edition where Armor didn't do the same thing to attacks as Durability did; Thermite and White Phosphorus have AP qualities or 8 and 3, respectively, and making plastique explosives in shaped-charge form reduces their blast area but applies that reduction in yards as levels of AP. Stationary explosives that are not thrown or launched do not have a "Damage" rating that adds bonus dice to their attack roll (only having the extra successes the explosion applies), and instead resolve whether they hit using the systems for traps to determine whether they apply their damage or not.

                in 2e, damaging explosives apply their Damage rating as a type of damage determined by how close the subject is to the explosion — aggravated damage at ground zero (touching or within reach of it), lethal within the primary blast area, and bashing within the secondary blast area; they also roll their Force rating as an attack dicepool for which Defense does not usually apply against all subjects, adding successes on the roll to the damage dealt as well as applying the Damage rating again on any successful hit, applying the same type of damage by proximity as Damage does. They can have the Armor Piercing quality, which now pretty directly works the same for Armor as for Durability (the main distinctions not altering the fact that Armor Piercing attacks ignore their rating in Durability); Thermite and White Phosphorus still have the same AP ratings they did in 1e, despite the change in their damage numbers to account for weapon modifiers applying extra successes instead of extra dice. Objects downgrade points of damage from an explosion equal to their Durability by one level, from aggravated to lethal, lethal to bashing, and bashing to nothing; Hurt Locker says that leftover aggravated damage is not subject to Durability, which Hunter 2e reproduces and Deviant does not; presumably due to reader confusion about the definition of the term in Hurt Locker, the sidebar in Hunter clarifies "ground zero" as "a smaller area in the explosive's immediate vicinity," while Deviant's section more directly calls it out as "touching the explosive or within reach of it." Deviant is likewise the only place where it is specified that thrown explosives like grenades put the explosive within range of the primary blast radius on a successful attack roll, with an exceptional success being required for it to put the target at ground zero. Placed explosives are not given special rules.

                So basically, despite the edition shift, to all appearances explosives still work mostly the same after accounting for the change in how weapon damage works, and in fact explosives are more damaging in 2e even without the assumption that they functionally ignore Durability within the primary blast area. Setting aside for a moment the question of aggravated damage, I feel like if the intention was that being outside of ground zero of a grenade hit was enough to break most equipment on your person (dealing damage in excess of the object's Durability when most objects have Durability of 3 at most) the books would have said something.

                                                        Originally posted by JJBooklord42 View Post
                                                        Hi, this might be a strange thing to bring up, but could someone tell me how modifiers actually work, because I have a horrible feeling that a lot of people, including myself, might be doing it very wrong

                                                        this is the rule from DtR and CtL

                                                        Modifiers: Bonuses add the indicated number of dice; penalties remove them. Unless otherwise specified, modifiers never exceed +/–5. Add all bonuses before subtracting penalties.​
                                                        That's the idea, though the ambiguity of whether "modifiers" means "total modifiers" or "individual modifiers" is where a lot of the confusion seems to live; my understanding is that the intention is for you not to be able to get a non-elective Dramatic Failure on a roll whose base pool you have more than five dice in nor more than double your pool past the same threshold — rolling fifteen dice, which gives you a roughly 50% chance of rolling an Exceptional Success, is understandably meant to be the product of extremely beneficial circumstances to extremely talented and well-trained people when supernatural enhancements aren't involved.

                                                        Supporting this read is the fact that Mage frames penalties for casting with additional spell factors as deviating from "the normal -5 penalty cap to dice pools" while not providing the same exception to bonuses from Yantras, which are explicitly capped at a net positive modifier of +5 after penalties are applied. Where people get iffy about that is the fact that in certain circumstances it's very easy to reach the point where certain circumstantial bonuses just end up going to waste, e.g. spending three Vitae to enhance a roll with Physical Intensity in Requiem, assuming the intention conveyed by the example case in the 1e corebook carries forward into 2e, would result in only five dice being added instead of six from the two per Vitae, or any changeling with Acute Senses getting a circumstantial bonus higher than [5-Wyrd], or a Paradox risk roll being composed entirely of modifiers.

                                                        Presumably a later iteration or edition of the system will be fully unambiguous about it, but for the moment the intention does seem to be that the total modifiers don't exceed five dice up or down from your base pool.
