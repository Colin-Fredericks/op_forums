    I think one of the main concepts about mage is that it is.. magical!! And the fact is that mage is a really messy and big giant concept to envelop in one book. U can literally play anything any genre story or plot u want to set up for your players.

    Do u want a bloody plotting game of thrones style that will make even elder vampires tremble in fear? Try to cook something up with mastigos and acanthus manipulating minD souls and time to achieve their objectives. An acanthus can set up plans to overgrown entire cabasl and cities when the fate wheel starts spinning and mastigos can eradicate minds creating puppets like elder vampires.

    Do u want extreme shootouts bloody gore and violence on the street with cabals killing each other on sight? U can do that. Cabals will probably start wars over artifacts books or spell rotes now.

    Do u want a Terrifying spy game where u canT trust nobody and u become a paranoid member of a creepy sect that is ready to kill and hide even the most infamous secrets from human and awakened society? Join the guardians.


    Do want an all out war and special forces operation feeling ? Join the adamantine arrow. A veteran arrow cabal can easily hunt down a pack of werewolves or eradicate a coterie if they need to.

    Do u simply want to wander around in wonder exploring magic mistery and wonders and try to solve enigma puzzles or riddles set up by gods spirits and weird creatures? Explore the dream world and spirit world, ghosts hexes.. hidden curses and occult societies ? I think the misterium and the entire mage society is for u.


    U can play mage like a true detective story, a doctor who epic saga or a game of thrones gritty and backstabbing plot.

    Or u cán throw everything out of the window and play an Indiana Jones style chronicle where ur players crawl in jungles and Tibetan monasteris looking for Atlantidean artifacts and the location of the watchtowers.

    Is that enough?

        Similarly unsure as to OP's exact question too, but I can offer some insight as to why I started running 2e. Its mix of the Matrix and Hogwarts is stylish enough in itself, but really, Mage is about the horror of knowledge. Acquiring knowledge means you can do *anything*, but it comes with it fears unto itself. Knowledge that there are monsters constantly trying to get in, that reality is malleable and broken, that everyone is watched all the time by uncaring gods, that ordinary people are regularly abused but never have any idea about it. That knowledge confers extraordinary power, but it comes with it a complete disintegration of the world as you know it. Consider, as one simple example, the Mastigos. Progressing in their Ruling Arcana means becoming increasingly aware that our minds are playthings of magic, and that objective distance is a fable. What exactly does that *do* to one's sense of self is a fascinating question in its own right. What even is a "person", to one that now knows thoughts and memories and being in a discrete position are all rubbish lies they can twist at will? Brief glimmer of my feeling but there you are.

                        Copying a thing I wrote elsewhere:
                        "M:tAw is about the moment when the veil is torn back, when you understand the higher Truths of the universe in a thunderbolt of insight, and you realize that this understanding, this gnosis means you can do things you couldn't before. This understanding is singularly addictive: once you've tasted that power, you're compelled to seek out more of it, searching for things that do not make sense and tearing away at them until you unlock their secrets and feel the rush of power that comes from a broader and deeper understanding in those universal Truths. But of course, it isn't all sunshine and rainbows on the road to godhood: for some reason, something is holding you back, and it has always held you back. There's an intelligence that tells you that you shouldn't want to know, called Exarch, and there's a maliciousness that punishes you when you reach too far, called Paradox. But despite these things, you are on the Path to godhood, and damn the consequences that happen along the way."


                        The core of Mage 2e is in three mechanics: Gnosis, Obsession, and Wisdom. After that, you need to understand the Reach and Paradox mechanics.

                        In terms of setting, you need to know what the Lie is, but other than that... "Weird shit is everywhere. Messing around with it has consequences."

                            What I really like about Mage in comparison to the other major splats is that your character is human, without being a helpless human, and you can play them however you want. They can be as depraved as the most blood encrusted elder, has violent as a Rahu on roids, or you can play them as a straight up paladin and saint, they can be super sleuths, superspies or Chuck Norris. The sin of pride leaves far more room for variation than rage or blood lust. You can also play whatever flavor of chronicle you like, they aren't tied down by territory, dietary needs or the lethal rays of the sun, but they can also claim a territory and fight for power in Machiavellian courts. You can play Cthullu, DnD or the Bourne Identity, whatever you feel like without having to rewrite fundamental parts of the setting. YOU CAN EVEN PLAY THE HERO!

                                    Mage is a game about secret truths, obsession and the corrupting effects of wielding god-like power.

                                    Once upon a time, you dived into the rabbit hole and came out different. You are Awakened now, and see the mundane world for what it is. A fabrication, an illusion. A Lie. The world you once thought you knew is like a cardboard cutout of people and places now, and the weird and supernatural drip and leak at the edges of perception. There is no escape from the nagging sensations the occult world feeds you. And if you look, if you really LOOK, the world unravels before your eyes and the truths of Reality might burn your retina, etching visions of power into your brain.

                                    The Awakening does not just grant a passive understanding of the world, though. For every time you push at the edges of perception, reality bends and twists according to your will. Suddenly, you no longer need to wait for a taxi, you align the threads of causality to bring one to your doorstep. You don't need to wait for the police to catch a criminal, you know where he's hiding and see the spirits around his bolt hole, waiting for the right words to feast on him. You don't need to wait and resign yourself and grieve for the inevitable death: you can do something about it, and the price to pay for it is not out of reach. When the time comes for you to make a decision about all of these, will you choose what is Wise? Will you choose what is right? Will you be able to live with yourself knowing what you had to do to make it to the end?

                                    That's the sort of game Mage is. Choose wisely.

                                                    A Mage could, in theory, sit quietly around for the rest of their life, never using their magic and not really being any different from any other human. That might seem like a strange selling point, but for me it's the key difference between playing Mage and anything else in the Chronicles of Darkness. The reason a Mage goes on adventures is because they choose to go out there and interact with the world. Their motivation isn't some alien hunger for blood like vampires or strange spiritual nature like werewolves. Their motivations are either curiosity for the noble mages or a lust for power for the more selfish. Either way those are very easy motivations to connect with as a player and they tend to give you very active and self-directed characters.

                                                    As a storyteller you don't need to craft complex political justifications about how the group can work together or try to manipulate everyone into going on the quest. All you need to do with a group of Mages is give them a mystery. If you say "By the way, there's a rumour that there's a small town out in the countryside where every single flower growing turns black under the light of the full moon" the players will usually answer "When's the next flight out there and can I get a ticket?"

                                                    One of my Mage games I ran one week without any particular plot in mind so the players loaded up the wikipedia page of a nearby town and looked up the interesting and mysterious places there which they decided to proactively investigate and see if any of them had a capital M mystery involved.

                                                    Just be aware that Mages are very very good at solving mysteries. A single spell could unravel the plot of most detective novels straight away, sometimes they don't even need a spell when just Magesight on its own gives you a lot of information. Designing mysteries for Mages is a bit different from designing a mystery for anyone else and it can take a bit of practice to get the hang of it.
