    How would Fables work in the WoD setting?

    10-15-2016, 11:28 AM
    As of late, I've been delving into the absolutely fantastic comic known as Fables and have been drawing quite a few parallels with WoD gamelines.

    After all, both feature isolated communities of supernatural beings who live beneath the noses of the mundane world engaging in twisted power games of monumental consequence.

    For the uninitiated, the Fables setting can be surmised as such:

    Every character and creature from popular stories and tales of old are real, brought to life by centuries of belief, renown, and veneration. Initially, they existed in separate worlds apart from both the mundane and from each other; free to live out their stories time and time again, but this was not to last.

    In time, a dark empire headed by a mysterious being known as the Adversary arose and rolled over kingdom upon kingdom like an unstoppable wave. Soon, even the most powerful of Fables were forced to flee before the coming horde or die beneath its trampling feet. A great exodus of Fables carried tiny bands of refugees to the one place the Adversary hadn't reached; the mundane world.
    Now, they are forced to live in secrecy amongst away from the prying eyes of the "Mundies" (Fable slang for the non-magical) for fear that discovery would bring yet another wave crashing down upon their heads, only this time, with nowhere left to run.

    Trapped between the rock of the encroaching Adversary, whose incursions grow more and more bold by the day, and the hard place of a dreary mundane world, the Fables have nothing left to do but wait; forming tightly knit but fragile communities as they prepare for the coming war.

    My question posed to you is this; how would this setting function as a World of Darkness gameline, both mechanically and in terms of lore?

                                          Anyway, so back to a topic we've been skirting around; why do they take the form of characters out of stories?

                                          dxanders gave us some reasoning, but if our Fables aren't going to be True Fae we need something else.

                                          What if the True Fae organized some kind of massive play with their changelings? Only rather than have them merely act out human stories, they were forced to quite literally become them, both physically and mentally.

                                          Initially, they would only tell stories in closed loops, but as the Fae grew bored of the same tales over and over, they'd start to mix and match different parts; why not have Dorothy visit Wonderland for a change? This would explain why kingdoms in the Homeland were once isolated dimensions and eventually fused together over time.

                                          Eventually I imagine the Fae would want to get in on the action personally, and this is where everything starts to go wrong.
                                          If you've ever seen the movie Westworld I'd think of the situation similarly to that one; every day the actors are reset from any mental or physical damage so that they don't start to realize that they're repeating the same story.
                                          But there's only so much that can be repressed, and as the Fae started to get more twisted with them, stepping in to derail plots and send everything spiraling down into madness the trauma would start to leave imprints that wouldn't fade away.

                                          Thus, the "Adversary" is the True Fae themselves. The Fables are convinced that the massive sets the True Fae built for them to act out stories on are truly their ancestral homelands and the Fae who are now stepping in to take active roles in the story are some malevolent alien force come to drive them out.
