                                    Originally posted by Oukranos View Post
                                    Where the Acolytes have blood sorcery, Where the Sanctified have inquisitors and crusaders, Where the Carthians have overwhelming dice modifiers, the Invictus have numbers. The Acolytes can afford to throw a single powerful thing at something. The sanctified can afford to throw two skilled warriors blessed by theban at something. The carthians can outwit anything with scathing dice. The Invictus though, they can afford to throw twenty moderately skilled bodies at the issue and continue to do so relatively indefinitely. The Invictus strength has always been force of numbers. Individually weak, but strong as a community.
                                    I would like to see this concept mechanized more directly. The Carthians get an ability to "summon" a group of nameless thugs, for example, to intimidate or curb dance a low level rival. That seems more about numbers and backup ready minions / friends.

                                    I also think there should be more universal mechanics for Oathbreakers, rather than the smaller sub-mechanics woven into the individual oaths. The on-the-ground network of the Estate would be a great counter to the mystical-philosophical Carthian Law, for example. You break an Oath, and you get the Oathbreaker Condition. Cue that condition to things like Hard Motherfucker - you don't have to kill a Carthian, you can kill anyone with the Oathbreaker condition to be brought into the gang. You could even have the condition scale like Tempted and inflict on-the-ground network penalties:

                                    Condition: Oath Breaker
                                    • Mechanics: You just gave the finger to the Estate. Maybe they pushed you too far. Maybe they asked you for something too big. Maybe you just got tired of doing things for that shithead notary and his weasel-words. Whatever the reason you broke the Oath doesn't really matter. What matters is you got caught breaking it and now the Estate is coming down on you like a hammer. If you have any of the following Merits, put a line through your last dot for each of them. You cannot access that dot until this condition is resolved. Merits: Allies, Contacts, Resources, Herd, Invictus Status. This condition stacks if you break multiple Oaths, each instance must be resolved separately.
                                    • Beat: Some Merits (Ex: Oath of the Hard Motherfucker) allow Estate members to target Oathbreakers for payback. Take a beat whenever you get hit with that kind of payback.
                                    • Resolution: Fulfill the equivalent of a major boon to the Invictus member with the highest City Status.
                                    Anything that mechanizes that feeling of a syndicate / network / organization beyond just leaving it to roleplay / drama. You could use the condition for all sorts of payback Merits: Estate Enforcer - get a damage bonus against Oathbreakers. Fool Me Once - Gain an initiative bonus against Oathbreakers, or you can never be surprised in combat by an Oathbreaker. And that's just gang-style combat ideas.

                                                04-23-2015, 05:49 PM
                                                Assorted Playtesting observations if these are still useful:

                                                Crowdsourcing (CS)

                                                General Feelings: The action has some limited applications. The suppression effect means that unless you're getting something you can't normally get, it will usually be better to go for alternate means of gaining equivalent bonuses (Social Maneuvering, Boon Exchange, etc). Two points where it did seem useful - giving the bump to 6+ dots, and pressing tasks where the coterie / individual is a few points short of solving a problem. Though the latter is slightly hampered by the time cost of the action itself.

                                                Specific Question: Dots gifted from one Kindred to another are suppressed for the remainder of the story. This does NOT seem to apply to the receiver. So - My character with 5 Dots in Allies acts as the hub. Coterie-mate with 3 Dots in CS gains three successes on the CS roll. Three other Kindred can now gift one dot of allies to me each. After the massive 8-dot Ally maneuver which triggers a state-wide recall election for the governor, the three Kindred who gave me ally dots lose them. But do I? As the text references only the "shifts" disappear, my interpretation was the hub character should not lose them.

                                                Information Network (IN)

                                                General Feelings: Each contact grants a focused skill specialty relating to the details of the contact in question. The clause we got stuck on was the "applicable only on information gained from the contact." This caused two effects: Either live conversations with the contact would be drawn out as the player tried to get as much information as possible before walking into a situation, or play would get broken during scenes as the player reached out to the contact as "new" information was needed on the ground. To address that flow, we just made it much more abstract in nature - as long as it was something your contact would reasonably have given you information on, we assumed it happened offscreen. A a slight balance, we didn't let the other specialty-enhancing merits piggyback off these (Interdisciplinary, etc).

                                                Invested (I)

                                                General Feelings: We didn't think the 'Merits for Favors' clause would see much traction, since trading merits for favors is pretty much the local Invictus shtick and you didn't need a merit to do it. However, it ended up being more an offscreen hook for the player:

                                                "I need Contacts: Street to start looking for that runaway."
                                                "These three Invictus characters would be known to have that. Who would you approach and what would you offer?"

                                                Player than made an offer which was reasonable, deal was done. Didn't take much on-screen negotiation, and I think by allowing the player to choose the favor that was done, there was more connection to it. A stepping stone for further relationship between that character/NPC. Unless there was literally no character I could think of with the type of sought Merit, I always tried to make it a non-Generic character re: the favor.

                                                Also, we discovered this Merit works well for someone leaving the Invictus on good terms and keeping connections with old allies / family.

                                                Noblesse Oblige (NO)

                                                General Feelings: We immediately ditched the City Status requirement for this so as to open it up to low status positions (Hound, etc) and to Clan (Priscus) and Covenant (Varied/Many). The three dot cost seemed rather steep for basically swapping out one touchstone for another. YMMV, but positions are a little more fluid for us, so you were subjecting yourself to a more vulnerable touchstone, and not actually gaining much of a mechanical advantage. We decided that rather than replacing the Touchstone outright, you would anchor this Merit to an existing Touchstone. You would then gain any benefits from that Touchstone by either interacting with the Touchstone as normal, or by interacting / fulfilling the duties of your office. This led to thematic joining in some fashion, either the anchor Touchstone represented an aspect of the office, or interactions with the touchstone became flavored with themes of the office. And then finally, we ruled that loss of the position just meant you could no longer access the benefits, though we decided that using Sanctity of Merits to get these dots back would be a Humanity 3: Two Dice breaking point as the character admits to themselves that they failed. Alternately, they could leave the dots on the sheet in belief that they would earn / wrest the position back but in the meantime got no benefits.

                                                Social Engineering (SE)

                                                General Feelings: Merit is very good. Learning banes and destroying havens are massive advantages against an enemy, let alone an ally. The social bonus / penalty to a single roll is definitely the weakest option, and was only taken when 4+ successes had been rolled by the base rules.

                                                Specific Questions: There was some discussion on if a single option could be taken more than once with multiple successes. Could you just learn three different banes/conditions on the target, get 30 minutes in the haven, or get (+3) to a social roll on the target? We decided yes, you could stack in that fashion.

                                                Abuse Issues: The issue almost immediately got abused over the "characters may only be the target of SE once per story." While we immediately quashed using SE on oneself, a tactical use would be using SE on an ally for no or little effect (-1 to your next intimidate roll!), thus immunizing them from further SE effects from enemies or the like. I quashed that as well, but I feel the rule might be better served with a user-based limitation. Something like: "SE can be used a number of times per story equal to your Invictus Status, and not on the same target more than once." This would mirror a lot of other X per Story/Session mechanics, while still being extremely valuable, and giving it a bit of buff against the steal/mimic Covenant powers from other groups, while also avoiding the case where Elder Eversecret auto-failed just because Neonate Nosykid got there first. I also gave PCs a beat whenever SE caused them a legit setback.

                                                Oath of the Model Prisoner (OMP)

                                                General Feelings: The timing and adjudication of this is too vague. In theory it isn't available to characters without Experience Points. In theory it has to be sworn in the presence of a Notary, etc. More definition is needed on the difference between "intent" and action. We decided that this is an Oath that can be sworn in any domain where a Notary is active and has made a public declaration regarding the oath (specific words of surrender, a specific flavor ritual, etc.) Thereafter anyone who knows the oath details may swear it. If they don't have enough XP on hand, they can suppress skills or merits relating to Combat to balance it out in a pledge of peacefulness. Once the captivity / Oath ends, they get their traits back.

                                                Specific Questions:

                                                Trigger timing - when would the damage happen in the following scenario?

                                                Invictus captured by Carthians.
                                                Invictus prisoner pledges the Oath.
                                                Carthians meet and vote on whether to kill or free the prisoner, one at a time.
                                                Vote is tallied, execution carries.
                                                Carthian captors begin marching to the kindred jail intending the execution.
                                                Carthian captors engage the Invictus Prisoner (initiative) so as to perform the execution.
                                                Carthian captors begin inflicting damage on the Prisoner on each of their combat turns.

                                                Regardless of where the damage starts, would the damage happen every turn one of the above valid moments is hit, or only once per essentially extended action?

                                                How do you handle multiple or changing captors thematically? Does the Oath automatically target whoever reasonably has access to the Prisoner, or does the Oath need to be resworn with each new transfer of power? Example - the above Carthians vote for freedom and release the Invictus Prisoner over to the Circle Sheriff. Is the Sheriff now bound by the restrictions of the Oath, or does the Oath have to be re-sworn actively? If it's auto - it becomes more versatile for torpid / staked prisoners.

                                                Abuse Issues:

                                                The captors cannot harm the oathbound else they suffer the willpower/health loss - but no mention if they can allow or orchestrate for others to do so. If they can simply look the other way, then effectiveness of the Oath is massively curtailed.

                                                The Prince-Slayer Gambit: Invictus Prisoner swears the Oath to Invictus Captors. A gang-squad of every Invictus with Resilience (or who don't mind taking some aggravated damage) attacks the Prisoner. Carthian Prince suffers one lethal per attack. He knows exactly why he's suffering the damage, but does that include where the action is taking place?



                                                Will have more later.

                                                    Continued Playtesting observations:

                                                    Oath of the Refugee (OR)

                                                    General Feelings: Cool concept, but since most games involve a specific/single Domain, it doesn't see much play with PCs.

                                                    Abuse Issues: The ultimate way to abandon the Invictus. Swear this Oath, leave the Covenant and City (You're gonna lose the Invictus Status anyway) and your old Unconquered friends have just as much trouble finding you as anyone else.

                                                    Oath of the Righteous Kill (ORK)

                                                    General Feelings: Very useful merit, though it does feel like there's a missed opportunity here to let the Sanctified compliment the Estate with this Merit. In any case, there were a few areas that needed clarification:

                                                    Specific Questions: Is it purely the Merit-holder's prerogative to allow the Oath to go off? Meaning, does the action in question actually have to be a threat to the Invictus / Masquerade? Or do you just need to convince the Merit-holder it is? (via Social Maneuvering, Disciplines, etc.)

                                                    What exactly does the clause 'She checks for Detachment as if she committed the crime herself' intend? Does that mean that the breaking point mystically transfers to the Merit-holder at (+1) die, regardless of the Merit-holder's current Humanity or Banes? Or does the Merit Holder get to ignore any breaking points entirely if they wouldn't normally be an issue, either being above her Humanity rating or because she's taken a bane for that sin? We did the latter, making low-humanity bane-riddled confessors the best confessors - though that did feel a little clashing with the theme of encouraging sympathy / empathy. Felt better to have the Elder talk about duty, service and the meaninglessness of human weakness, let alone humans themselves, in the protection of the Masquerade.

                                                    Unclear on how specific or broad the "action" has to be. Might be a good place for a narrative example to give some guidance, especially concerning if multiple sins are committed in the action. Let's say that to defend the Masquerade, a mid-level Invictus is sent to kidnap a mortal, determine if the mortal has told anyone else, then track down anyone they may have told, in each case either erasing memories or killing the mortal in question. Do the two Oathbound need to make a laundry list of possible sins? (Impassioned violence, removing consent with a discipline, premeditated murder, to name the obvious.) Or should the Oath auto-trigger on the next instance of a sin in pursuit of the action, and then need to be resworn?

                                                    Oath of the Safe Word (OSW)

                                                    General Feelings: Cool thematic idea, but practically the risks are too big. At the end of the day, you're paying 2xp on an Oath which gives you in the best case a 5xp Social Merit. So - a 3xp freebie. Then there is the Willpower exchange. This is very tricky thing to adjudicate between a PC/NPC as the Storyteller. How much Willpower does a reasonable NPC allow to be taken per story? How many should the NPC take in return? Does the loss of willpower actually register in a sensory way for either party? I may know when I'm low on Willpower, but will the other Oathbound? And then there is the death-knell risk. If the paralyzation clause were only available to the to Oathbound, it's already steep. But open the door to the fact that one of the two could be forced/leveraged by others to trigger the paralyzation, and you not only need to keep the other person happy, you need to keep it all a secret.

                                                    Abuse Issues: Why would an Elder not hedge her bets with a Neonate by using entombed command (Do not break this oath without warning me first.) Or using any other number of ways to assuage an inferior kindred? As above, very risky. And if the neonate / inferior doesn't plan on utilizing the kill-buff as part of the process, other Oaths are more effective at both the benefits and the consequences. Oath of Fealty for a simple one. Oath of Action is very popular - get access to a (potentially) 5-dot discipline, perform a trivial service or let a month pass, and inflict some damage on the Elder as some final payback.

                                                    Oath of the True Knight (OTK)

                                                    General Feelings: This is the scary one. An armor bonus, immunity to Vinculums, and discipline defense. All good stuff. Some clarifications:

                                                    Specific Questions: The Oathtaker cannot be subject to Vinculum or other individual Oaths. First - does that mean a full 3-step Vinculum, or any step at all (which brings to mind more the Oath of the Virginal Knight, if the Oathbound cannot have ingested any blood previously).

                                                    Second - Ingested Kindred blood turns to ash in the True Knight's mouth. How does a Knight who can only consume Kindred vitae survive this? I would normally think by Oaths of Fealty or Penance so they can get in on the mystical transfers of vitae - but those require swearing an Oath to an individual, which seems to be prohibited as well for the True Knight. Oath of Abstinence doesn't seem like a viable choice either for a combat-focused role. Or - does the Knight only need to be free of individual Oaths at the time of the swearing, but after becoming a True Knight can swear individual Oaths as needed?

                                                    Alright, I hope any of this helps. I love the Invictus and am excited to see all this come out. Thanks again for the open dev opportunities.

                                                        I was under the assumption that if you are Invictus, somewhere along the line you get some oath or power that allows you to be a better lord of a domain than the next guy. I have read all the oaths and merits here and people's responses. I could not find a good ability/power that allows you to control things. Most of these powers seem excellent things for the low-mid tier kindred and helps them to be better organizers and contribute to the society they are in over all, which is great, as low level invictus was really in need of some love.
                                                        But I think the Invictus elders need something that is actually useful to control domains, similar to the discipline Tenure in Invictus covenant book. Without the ability to control and limit what is going on in their domains, Invictus is less of a good choice for elders and actual domain lords than almost any other covenant. Oaths that prevent people from doing things you dont want are extremely weak in my opinion except for dealing damage which can easily be healed since the oath breaker can easily prepare for this beforehand (ie. break the oath at a time of his choosing). There is also the added benefit of realizing someone broke the oath and that is as good as it gets.
                                                        My interpretation is that this 'new invictus' mentality is join up when you are new, reap whatever benefits you can get(which is not all that great, but a stepping stone if no other covenant has you), then eventually join up with another covenant including carthians which grants you more powerful abilities and actually the ability to control your domain properly.
                                                        One argument posted above was that the invictus has numbers, and thats its advantage. It is really hard to imagine in reality what is actually granting these numbers. I find it hard to believe it is the multitudes of free or easy to access social merits that allow invictus to hold such great numbers. I would assume if i get 10 players at a table, invictus would have trouble to get the majority of members if the actual benefits of the covenant was presented.
                                                        As it currently stands the dragon with the voivode coil and the more-powerful-than-god-machine carthian law is easily out performing the powers invictus has at its disposal to control a domain. I would assume a prince or domain lord would rarely need additional merits, or oaths that can be broken left and right with no real penalties, the other covenants simply make better domain lords and princes. I dont know how much crunch the fluff can handle. But it looks less than convincing to me that an actual powerhouse would bother with tons of oaths and dozens of merits when other options are irresistable, unstoppable and simply more powerful.
                                                        I think these powers are very cool, if some of them require a bit extra attention (game time wise) to get full benefits out of, and I actually think a covenants powers should be similar to this. I just think without a way to counter the carthian law it is pointless to invest into them. I would assume it would require a similar effort to manage votes for a powerful law to pass, as it is to manage tons of oaths to hold a domain. And law is unbreakable.
                                                        In spirit of open development (and a bit humor) and to give a suggestion let me give a simple example, this follows the same swearing oath by oneself logic:

                                                        I Am the Hard Motherfucker (Oath of Sovereignty)
                                                        Prerequisite: Invictus Status 4, Blood Potency 5 etc. etc.

                                                        This is an oath sworn by oneself. Once you swear it you are a hard motherfucker. Combine all your vassal's city status. The total is deducted form any carthian law status accumulation attempts on you (reduce the total carthian status collected by the lynch mob). If you gather enough status points more than the total carthian status bearing cathians in the city, the law no longer applies to you. You can grant this immunity to others if they swear an oath of service to you. If you have vassals who have vassals, count them as well. Ie. a prince gets the regent's as well as regent's vassals and tenants for his total.
                                                        For any other blanket city leveling power, count all your vassals relevant power stats. Apply the same procedure. If the theoretical power uses primal-urge of 8 werewolves, then count your vassal's blood potency to counter it. This only applies to blanket city crippling powers (ie. werewolf trying to flood the whole city, archmage trying to incinerate your city etc. if you have such cosmic stuff in your campaigns, if you like archmages incinerating cities, dont use this last part, hopefully it shouldn't be necessary anyways).
