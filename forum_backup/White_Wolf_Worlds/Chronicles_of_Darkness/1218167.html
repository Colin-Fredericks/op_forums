    Slowing down humanity loss without trivializing it?

    05-13-2018, 06:56 AM
    I don't like how a lot of Vampire games I run have people spend 95% of their experience only on Disciplines and the like. I feel it makes them just grow too fast to keep a long-term game going at the slower pace of growth that I want. So for my next campaign, I'm only going to allow buying Discipline dots and Blood Potency (and a few other things like that probably) with Blood Beats, like the optional twist from the 2E core VtR book, though tweaking the things that give blood beats a little bit. That way more skills and fun merits my players tend to skip "because Disciplines" will get used, too, which is nice.

    I'll also limit max dots in any Discipline to 2 + BP.

    However, it'll be slowing down progression quite a bit and I plan to have a rather long campaign, and I feel there might be issues with Humanity. I already feel it's usually too meteoric a descent, and I worry that they'll all hit like Humanity 4 while still being at the "fledgling new member of vampire society" level, which isn't what I want.

    Any of you ever done something like this and have a good house rule to slow down Humanity loss a bit, without actually making the PCs go "Oh, I'll probably not lose any Humanity here, so might as well cut loose!"

    Some random unformed ideas I've had:
    - Give low BP Kindred a bonus to Humanity rolls against high level Humanity sins. Say a BP 1 gets +3 to Humanity 7 and higher sins, +2 to 6 and +1 to 5. These bonuses drop by 1 for each BP after that. The risk is still there so it shouldn't factor too heavily into decision making, but if you're not unlucky it does slow a lot.
    - Some sort of house rule where Humanity tests aren't something you roll, but rather something that is rolled against you. The Humanity check has a bigger dicepool the worse the transgression, and you lose 1 Humanity if it scores X hits against you. The amount of hits doesn't refresh until the next day, so if 7 hits are needed and you get 4 hits against you, the next tests within the same night only need to generate 3 more. Could tweak the numbers so that losing Humanity from 1 high level sin is unlikely yet possible, but repeated abuse is likely to lead to it.
    - In addition to either of the two above, could impose some harsher conditions on those who only barely succeed at a humanity test, so that even when you make it it temporarily really inconveniences you, giving another reason to tread carefully around Humanity. The conditions wouldn't negatively affect what you're currently doing of course, as they'd be flavoured around the type of sin, because else using vampiric abilities at all could become a bit moot.

      In general, my first response to, "I want to slow down progression," is to adjust the Beats:Experience ratio up from 5:1 to something like 7:1, or even 8:1 if you're adding in Blood Beats too.

      This just slows everything down in advancement by increasing the cost to advance without taking away the feeling of getting rewards for doing things. It's a very simple way to alter the speed of advancement without messing with any other bits in the game.

      I'm not sure if you should be addressing the, "everyone buys Disciplines only," thing with house rules though. I don't really see it as a problem with the rules (Disciplines should be attractive things you want to buy and all), but something with expectation setting. Disciplines aren't "I win" buttons. It's worth examining your games as the ST to see if you've been allowing Disciplines to solve everything, and then accidentally encouraging people to not bother with investing in other traits, since that's something you can fix without messing with other stuff simply by how you construct the setting your characters are working in. Obviously there's also the important, "talk to your players," thing.

      Additionally, it's good to model. Showcase NPCs (helpful and antagonistic) that have a more balanced spread of Traits to help show why Disciplines aren't the only thing to buy.

      Humanity... I'm not so sure about because I'm not sure why your saying it plummets so fast. The 2e Humanity system is actually pretty forgiving with just your default Touchstone's +2 bonus. Maintaining 7 isn't easy without spending XP, but Humanity 5-6 is pretty easy to keep for awhile until you're doing some pretty inhuman stuff on the regular.

          Hmm, in my experience players have been getting down to 5 Humanity pretty fast at least, even without doing inhuman stuff often. Of course, with dice involved it can always be a big coincidence.

          I might be exaggerating a bit when I say Disciplines are the only thing getting bought, but they're noticeably high up there at least. And it's always been like that no matter who STs across every campaign I've been part of, so at that point I think it's at least a little baked into the system. Disciplines tend to get rushed to a respectable level, then people start shopping around for other things (some of them, at least). But I feel that for a night-to-night game, that just tends to lead to an increase in vampiric power that seems too fast for the setting and go against the feel of the game a bit. It's always going to end up like that even with slower progression (that is, vampiric progression, since with the addition of blood beats the total beats the players would be getting would actually be higher, it's just that a smaller part can be spent on vampiric progression), but even a little bit can go a long way.

          Ultimately, I'm aware that a multi-pronged approach is best, and running a highly political game, I have plenty of ways to show that your 5 dots of Resilience aren't better than 5 dots of Allies, and my players already know what expectations I have since I've talked to them about it. That's not really the bit I need advice on, I want my game's progression rules to be internally consistent with everything else I'm doing, hence the needs outlined in my opening post.

              Here's a thing that works with my troupe at my table. We share a vision about disciplines being equated to age and power, not exp. By which i mean, we all share the idea it would be extremely rare and weird to find out that a neonate had a 5 dot discipline. a fourth dot in a discipline would be rare and on a character that's really narrow and specialized in that regard. So we don't have that problem with neonates, even PCs, running around with 4-5 in their disciplines, so that's a start.
              The other thing though is part of the actual game theme and design goals: Disciplines should be cheap. I remember Rose saying something in the line of "see how cheap it is to be a monster". So there's that, disciplines are powerful, and cheap, but they are also a fine reminder about your lack of humanity now. It's part of the personal horror experience.
              One thing if you still feel that every exp goes into disciplines, is probably that they are too good at your table, like a work around skill checks or a easy cop out from hard situations at large. If you need to open the door quietly and not leaving marks, no amount of Vigor is going to help you. If the Nosferatu Primogen is prone to lashing out to everyone that seems to good to be true, Awe is probably going to make his impression of you far worse then it was, and no amount of Dominate is going to save your skin when the Prince finds out you dominated her.

              But if it's still a problem and you want to change it up, i agree that Blood Beats might be the way to tweak it. Mage basically does this with their Arcane Beats. Maybe lowering the BP and Discipline costs by one, and giving a few more options for getting Blood Beats, but not allowing normal Beats to purchase those powers.

              On the Humanity part, I'm with Heavy Arms here, in 2ed it's pretty normal to stabilize at 4-6 unless you're regularly doing really inhumane shit. (And if you plan to, get a bane). To be fair, PCs are usually way to involved around the shit fan to keep humanity at 7, or six for long. But then again exp is a thing and so are Banes, and not going out to actually kill people and being a complete inhuman dick. Players that work new solutions around problems usually keep their humanity higher, instead of killing the masquerade breach, bribe him for example. Don't go hunting in a nightclub with only one blood point left, get yourself out in the woods, because you know you'll probably not keep yourself from Frenzy.
              T
              he most basic solution i could come up with is to grant everyone a free dot of the Touchstone merit, so now everyone has another die on degeneration rolls as well as new npcs and story hooks to hang around with in game. That slows the degeneration a fair bit as well as get more interesting things into the game.

                          If you just want to draw it out, you might make the Humanity breaking points take time, rather than being instant roll-and-answer. Roll at the time, give them the appropriate Condition if they've failed. But don't have them lose the Humanity until that Condition is resolved. Get them used to playing those Conditions.

                          Alternatively you might 'make' them take banes, where in response to losing their first Humanity they automatically scar up that part of the soul and become immune to further losses from it, in exchange getting some vampiric quirk.

                                  I like to give players something when I'm running a campaign, in my current vampire game I gave them a group haven from their Sire but anything that the group think of as theirs is good. I suggest having a relatively infiuencal NPC who values Humanity and an understanding of proper technique (ie. skills) who can serve as a mentor/contact/quest giver for the party if they keep him happy (the way many video games make the good alignment path better in terms of party members and player homes). Also I allow players to take the Professional Training merit from CofD which helps make their go to skills a bit better and cheaper when the reach On the Job Training (so of course they now max PT out before trying to up anything else). Finally don't be afraid to point out merits that require skills at high levels on find ways to inflict penalties to dice pools as a way to incentivises buying them up.

                                      One change I would suggest is having "Protecting your Masquerade" bestow a bonus rather than a penalty.                                       The latter argument might imply Requiem should impose a penalty, but the former applies even more strongly.
