        More on point though, Ogres might deal with brutality, but they aren't stupid by any stretch. A lot of ogres and giants from folklore and mythology are actually rather cunning, to say nothing about trolls. Changeling Ogres in particular don't have a curse that says "you must be straightforward". An Ogre can emerge from beating the shit out of a Keeper as he goes to bed or even goes under anasthesia on the suggestion of the Ogre themselves. What defines them is brutality and violence, but that definition does not include simple-minded, slow, or dumb. In fact, Ogres might be the best at dealing with the Huntsmen because they understand the balance of approaches better, and know when to be clever, sneaky, and under-handed, and when to charge in and just break some skulls. They can play and break the game.

                    Here is a bit more of my Homebrew about the Huntsmen,

                    Originally posted by Homebrew of Pale_Crusader
                    Huntsmen, Atrocity, and Attention
                    Huntsmen enter the physical world and murder. These murders are an Atrocity, but are also extremely necessary for the Huntsman to even exist in the mortal world, not unlike how an accident over feeding during a vampire gaining its vitae even when it was very careful is still an Atrocity. The Huntsman as a corporeal being without a Stability Advantage it automatically gains a Permanent Atrocity from this act, and also has a capped Cover-Up of 1. They suffer an increase in severity their Cold Wrought Iron bane, gain a Personal Bane based on the nature of the murder, and this places the Huntsman immediately on a tightrope of having the maximum Attention it can have for its Cover-Up. The common knowledge of mundane world norms it gains from the murder lets it be aware of the in-world consequences of gaining the attention of authorities or monster hunters. Each time the huntsman is reformed in Arcadia without understanding of the mortal world, due to bargains struck time immemorial by the True Fae, it is considered a new creature by the forces that govern cosmic justice and it sheds its Permanent Atrocity from its past visits and its reforming isn't prevented by banes. Part of this bargain for this fae chicanery requires the Huntsmen loses all understanding of the mortal world, so it must murder again and put itself right back onto that tightrope to accomplish its mission. It likely that the Huntsman's Personal Banes will be different each time it reforms and hunts anew. The balance of Attention and Cover-Up does drastically limit the huntsmen’s pursuit of the changeling, making them eschew tactics they would net them greater than one Temporarily Atrocity if at all possible, and if they do something that would gain them another Permanent Atrocity it is almost always in the scene immediately prior to or during their end-game. This still leaves a lot of treachery, stealth and guile available to these heartless hunters, and they should not be underestimated.

                                        Can the Huntsmen kill the True Fae who summon them, or conspire against the True Fae so that another will take that risk? Sure, they persist as immaterial entities in a sort of slumber when not on the Hunt, so another True Fae can still summon them. Unless an Arcadian law forbids them from doing that, whether subtly or forcibly, it sounds like something a deeply frustrated Huntsman might one day consider.

                                        Are the True Fae the only ones who can summon Huntsmen? One of the required ingredients is a True Fae's breath, which a clever changeling might steal or persuade into being given. Stealing or binding immaterial/abstract things is only fae, after all. If it is possible for other beings to summon Huntsmen, is the template of the Huntsman altered?

                                        What prevents Huntsmen from giving or "losing" their Arcadian weapons to perform an assisted suicide (other than their conflicted enjoyment of the Hunt)? Do the True Fae subject them to some nightmarish torture if they discover such "cowardice"?

                                            I discussed the Huntsmen with my group, and one came up with a pretty interesting concept. What if a given Huntsman decides the only way to ever be immune to being sent on Hunts is to destroy reality/humanity? Granted, this wouldn't be a likely goal to actually achieve, but it'd sort of expand the Huntsman in question into a more dangerous antagonist. They'd delay and stall their hunts, killing more humans than necessary, blackmail Mages into widening this Abyss they think Huntsmen come from, damage Infrastructures, anything they can take down before finalizing the Hunt. Granted, they'd need to figure out HOW to damage reality, but.. that'd be even scarier. Goes from "Im coming to get you", to "I will do irreparable damage to your city, its people, and the things you love.. and THEN Im coming to get you":
