      I think the video game your referring to was Masquerade, not requiem. Maybe. Either way.

      Vampire is a game about backstabbing - but, like many games, only if you make it such. Think game-of-thrones and you've got yourself a vampire game - except instead of playing mortal people in a strange, hostile world, you are playing a secret collection of people with the power to control others with their very blood, monsters who prey on the weak by their very nature. You are playing a creature that was once a human, and has to come to terms with the fact that it is not - and is now a part of a community of sharks who make eternity interesting by playing russian roulette with lives.

      So... Pretty much its a game about playing a bad assed vampire, meeting all sorts of characters, dealing with political intrigue and generally having a good time as you roll some dice.

      There are a lot of styles of play too. You can play a game entirely based around cutthroat politics, but you can also play a game where you span the centuries, all trying to tell a story told over the coarse of millenia.

      That said, if your having an issue with the game, it might not be the game - but the people you are playing with. Because the game itself is a toolkit for people to have fun - it's not a strict regiment that must be played in a way that ostracizes certain players trying to figure the game out

          I don't care much at all for playing the game about undead politics, FWIW.

          For me, Requiem is a game about intense personal drama, about exploring how normal people like you and me react to extraordinary circumstances, and how they either fight tooth and nail to resist their own monstrosity, or tumble down into it despite their best intentions not to. How does someone handled being turned into a creature of violence? How would they cope to the apparent loss of their own soul, and the shift away from being recognizably human? What bizarre coping mechanisms would the dead create to deal with the weight of immortality and their own alien hungers? That's a rich narrative vein that makes for powerful stories, and that's before factoring in metaphors - for addiction, for toxic relationships, for other things - that vampires easily represent.

          So is it "fun" in the same way an action movie or a classic high fantasy adventure game is? No, but I'm not always (in fact, almost never) looking for that.

              If your group is dead set on interpreting to a narrow definition of "fun" as "being badass and kicking ass," then frankly no amount of advice from us will persuade them. Think about why people read tragedies and find meaning in them. Think about why the main characters in fiction sometimes fail, instead of being promised of success in everything they do. Requiem is a lot like that.

              Except that this too is still shallow. You can be a badass master of the night, mingling with every manner of people from every sphere in the world, fending off the Prince and his lackeys in a grand political intrigue. It's just that the game firmly reminds you that your actions do have consequences. And the game doesn't force you to avoid those consequences like the plague. You can take them head on, refusing to go down silently.

                  I mean, I don't honestly think Requiem is mutually incompatible with vampire badassery that doesn't necessarily find much when it delves deep beneath the surface of a character, either. I enjoy it when personal drama and art emerge from the interactions within the game, and I've rolled self-loathing, alienated educated Catholics doomed to a theologically untenable damnation, but I've also rolled opportunistic lunatics hiding behind a thin visage of human sentiment, with "do a fun homicide" on their list of aspirations. The best games meet somewhere in the middle.

If they, say, have a strong vision of what they think Vampire is, and you don't see where the part you were looking forward to comes in, or if it's vice-versa, talk it out and see if you can find where the Venn circles overlap.

                          Well, sure, the core of Requiem is the long slow loss of everything except the few things the PC's will hold onto and thus make eternal-but that's a really long time frame.

                          THere's time, in that long, slow sequel, to attend festivals and pageantries. To love and lust and drink deep. To stumble down the street puking up blood because what even the hell was that dude smoking while your friends laugh at you and trudge to pick you up. To watch movies and riff on the twenty foot pretty-boy angsting it up. To play a game of tag across rooftops. To eat a god damned hotdog (that you will subsequently puke up, but hey, you can taste it).

                          Requiem is a dark game that deals with inevitability-but that loss loses meaning if you do nothing but lose. Characters will cope, move on, change, justify, twist and turn, and do things as they seek to live with themselves-and it's important to know that they can, will, and should live.

                          Live long enough and life loses it's savor, sure. It can become too much of a thing-being a person is hard, after all, and one can only carry so much before something inevitably crumbles-and vampires pick up some of the heaviest shit. But if the vampire of a player reaches that point and finds that they have not managed to hold onto something other than blood-slicked misery, they missed the point. This game is not being dead-this game is dying the long way around, with all the weird, zany, fun, heroic, and sometimes poignant items such a bucket list includes.

                              There are two main points that define Vampire the Requiem: the aforementioned Requiem, and the Danse Macabre.

                              The Requiem is the tragic part of Vampire. It's the usually slow, sometimes fast, but always inevitable (or so they say) degradation of the self until all that you were and everything that makes you you fades away until all that's left is the hunger. It's where you get pathos. It's where the character becomes sympathetic and relatable. It's when your character realizes what they're becoming and how they chose to handle it. It's often sad, often tragic, but it's the part that often matters the most. In other words it's the down beats of the story, and gives the character's substance.

                              Then there's the Danse Macabre. The part of Vampire that gazes into the abyss and laughs. The part that says "better get this done tonight, because tomorrow may never come." It's where the fun comes into play. It's what brings life into the Covenants. It's why Kindred get up each night and live the f$&k out of the night until the dawn. It's where vampires make love, war, political intrigue, unearth forbidden secrets, and paint the town red. It's also when they burn, when they're reduced to ash. No slow death in the Game of Princes, you either win or you die for good. These are the up beats of the story, and make the setting itself come to unholy life.

                              Hope that helps. ^_^

                                      On a less overall-thematic-point that relies on Arc's personal preferences, my two main arguments for what makes this game fun in a more immediate sense-Chapter One of Requiem Second Edition.

                                      The Sexy, Brutal Appeal of Power, or the Clans- Requiem Second Edition went a long way to make it's character options strange, powerful, and damned sexy. There's a blood-slick style to the lot of them, and it's got a simple elegance to it's power appeal.

                                      Be the one who controls the eyes as the Daeva, and lace the crowd with murderous intent as you make a few jokes, melt hearts with just a wink, and stand as their deity, stronger and faster and so-all-encompassingly important than they ever will be.

                                      Howl obscenities at the monsters as the Gangrel-they don't even fucking know you wear all their faces. Run with wolves, choke a soul with the smoke of your corpse, and smile with teeth too wide as the asshole realizes he didn't smash your face in-you'll survive the end of worlds, and ravage anything that thinks otherwise.

                                      Laugh from the shadows as they miss your Cheshire smile as the Mehket-and then poison them with those nasty little sins they thought were safe. You know every lie anyone's ever told, who they'd hurt the most, who could be saved by the truth, and know that they'll never find you, too busy chasing the voice you play in their head.

                                      Challenge nightmares to blinking contests and leave them sobbing as the Nosferatu-you're every ghost of vengeance they hoped to leave buried, the blood of maggots squirming out of their closets, their beds, their eyes as you break their minds and bodies, never more than the boogieman in the shadows their parents lied to them about.

                                      Rule the court and make the idiots dance as the Ventrue-you wear your right to conquer and lead like armor, lead sliding off you like rain, your words infecting beasts simple and evolved, making the rats feast on the cats and the rich and powerful to dress themselves in shit against their will, all in service to your inherent right to reign.

                                      The Sweet Joy of Transgression, or the Covenants- You're not human, and sure-humanity has a lot of important things you should try to hold on to.There's a richness to their life that you might appreciate more now that you're dead. But let's be honest-there's a thrill to breaking the taboos. You're not human, so why should you be wholly bound to them?

                                      There are swine in the towers, and the Carthian Revolution is going to rip them from their leather-wrapped chairs and toss them out the windows. Set the world on fire and build brave new worlds in the ashes, Your were so powerless against whatever the ruling class was before, but now? Now you can unmake them with everything that empowers them.

                                      Those fuckers who keep trying to upstage you? Fuck them! The Invictus not only implores you to kick those parasites in the face, but rewards you for it. And if the idiot above you isn't gripping the ladder tightly enough? Well, if you can rip them off, there's a spot open for you friend. Let them try and climb back up-you know where they hide their bodies.

                                      There's always some fucks who can't abide, who go too far, who make you and yours look tame-except not, cause you're God's Monster in the Lancea Et Sanctum, and you can call down his unholy wrath to punish the assholes who violate the boundaries, who dare to tread upon your sacred path. Justice is mine, sayeth the chorus of teeth.

                                      Dance and hunt and fuck and kill with the Circle of the Crone and live the life of the monster in ecstasy and abandon, your life more beautiful and vibrant now that you've let go. Dive into the trance of living, commune with gods, and come back knowing that you are one of them.

                                      Fuck Nature and Fuck God-The Ordo Dracul knows there's more in this world than curses and bloodlust, and they'll dive into the darkest, most eldritch places to turn themselves inside out, ripping the weakness out of them and returning, transcendent in their actualization of "Whatever doesn't kill you makes you stranger".

                                      Combine those two with a human element, of finding ways to connect to the simple, important things that make being human worth it, and you have a recipe for a fun time.

                                                          I'm not a fan of Vampire, really. There are some things that appeal to me but a lot doesn't. I never quite know what to do with it, and I think some of that stems from 'undead politics' being uninteresting.

                                                          However, take a look at what's behind the politics - what's fueling it? Politics are for the monsters - for the Kindred arguing over who gets to suck blood from which night club and no you don't have permission to drink there, only I can. It's a juxtaposition. When you're young and newly embraced you struggle to retain humanity as more and more of it is harder to hold on to. When your friends and family forget about you - when they die, and you keep going - you just don't give a fuck anymore. Your life has been ruined, stolen from you, and I'm sorry but I have to drink blood every night or I stop existing, what do you want from me?

                                                          Covenants are a mask. It's all a play acted out in mimicry of what you were a century ago. It's not about fitting in or 'doing what you have to do.' It's about giving up. You can't win this fight. You've lost already. It's about surrendering to the Beast.

                                                          Vampire is a game about this descent.

                                                          Mummy is about starting there and trying to remember your long lost humanity.
