                            Eh, I think it's fine for the Mage gameline to treat Sleepers as powerless (I almost said "irrelevant" here, but that wouldn't have been accurate at all). That's not what many people, including myself, want. But trying to please everyone isn't a terribly good idea when designing games. The current team's vision for Mage is that the Awakened's relationship with Sleepers is entirely one-sided in terms of power and influence. So all the stories that are told in Mage will start from this assumption. I just acknowledge it and stay away from Mage.

                            Having said that, I think a thing to remember is that Hunter: the Vigil is not like other gamelines when it comes to crossover. In many if not most Mage games, the existence and exact nature of vampires or mummies is largely irrelevant. They might exist, or they might not. If they exist, they might work like their respective books say, or they might not. It's not going to be hugely important to a cabal of mages trying to deal with an Abyssal incursion, hunting Atlantean mysteries or whatever else mages get up to. The same applies in reverse - vampires dealing with Danse Macabre politics aren't going to consider mortal willworkers important, unless they happen to be involved. But regular mortal humans? They're always there, and they always end up on the receiving end of supernatural fuckery. And not all of them are willing to take it sitting down. Thus, hunters. They're a much more consistent presence in the setting than any given supernatural.

                                Second and especially first are problem - I understand hunting mages should not be walk in the park, but making Sleepers Hunters to be "fucked over", all the time, is problem.                                 They are a problem if you are running crossover, hunter vs mage, yes. But that's pretty standard when it comes to crossover (and hardly restricted to Hunter), indeed as crossover problems go it's a pretty easy fix. I proposed a fix a few pages back (Integrity loss allows the Hunter to remember the supernal even that caused it), things that also come to mind include:
                                • Quiessence sets in only after the fact, meaning hunters can deal with the magic in the present and only later (e.g. after that story ends) do they suffer memory loss.
                                • The Code can provide some degree of protection against Quiessence. I know Dave said that wasn't the case, but frankly, when developer intent gets in the way of the game you want to play, you kick that intent in the face and carry on.
                                • Remove Quiessence entirely and come up with some new reason Mages keep hidden. For instance, perhaps traumatic exposure to the supernal dramatically increases the chance of the victim awakening Mad or as a Banisher. Or disbelief makes spells go violently haywire. Or magic incites in people a disquiet like effect, driving them to hate the Mage. Any of those would leave the Awakened community reticent to reveal their art.
                                Frankly though, Quiessence is in my mind hardly the biggest hurdle for a Hunter. That would be the sheer flexibility of supernal magic, and the fact that the Mage can probably, in a turn or two, devise some form of fuck you countermeasure for the Hunters. And lets not forget, that even without Quiessence Mages can still cast spells covertly, and are well incentivized to do so (Disbelief, sleeper Integrity damage, and Wisdom loss). Meaning that even the removal of quiessence will not necessarily make Mages easier to detect.

                                Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                                I really would like to hear what Sleepers can use to confront Awakened without going with Endowments - what Tier 1 group could use to make ambush on mage? To investigate?
                                Sleepers can confront Mages in much the same way they confront any other supernatural: find their weakness and exploit it. In a Mages case that could mean jumping them in a dark alleyway (as weakness in unpreparedness is among their greatest faults). Similarly blowing up the building they are in, or sniping them when they walk out the front door are both highly viable. Or it could mean finding their sleeper family and holding them hostage, or figuring out the Mages objective and putting obstacles in it's way. It could also, for the less homicidal players, mean talking to the Mage and coming to some form of pact or agreement. Or perhaps converting one of the Mages acolytes to their cause, or interrupting the Mage mid summoning and letting his unbound monster tear him limb from limb. Players can be inventive when you give them some strings to follow.

                                As for investigating, that's a little harder. They'd have to rely on tertiary evidence which, frankly, is probably all the evidence there will ever be. Mages have ample reason to keep a low profile, and one who is being especially vulgar will attract more attention that just the hunters. Attention, I might add, that the hunters might discover and make use of. Basically to fight mages hunters have to delve into the themes of Mage itself. They become investigators, looking for secrets and signs of the Mages passing while trying to find the proper leverage to get the upper hand. Or, maybe not, maybe they just put a bomb under his car and blow the fuck out of him when he goes to work. Different strokes.

If you escape the encounter covered in burns you don't need to be able to remember that the mage was chucking fireballs. And it doesn't make them forget the mage or their actions, which you don't seem to be getting. It makes them reconstruct the memory of magic into something else. You still remember the person, you still have some misconstrued approximation of what they did to you (unless what they did was so out there there was literally no way you could think of it as anything else, which I feel would be fairly rare). The Sleeping Curse does notprevent you from attacking anyone. What the Sleeping Curse does is, after the fight is over, makes the Hunter forget that the mage used magic. He doesn't forget that the mage tried to fight back or did anything in that period of time, or even that the mage was there. He just misremembers what exactly it was the mage did during the conflict.

                                                        A Hunter might not remember exactly why he has burn marks, or why his ear drums are busted, or why his partners twitching like he went and hugged an electric fence but he remembers the guy they were after was there and is still pretty sure all of it was his fault.
