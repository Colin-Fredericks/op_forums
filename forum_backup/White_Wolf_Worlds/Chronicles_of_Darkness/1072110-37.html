        Don't worry, Duskage, I still try to keep the Hunter perspective. That said, I want the book to seem like it could believably fit in the same setting, which is why I try to make sure Nobles and the Darkness are not too different from how they are described in the main book. For example, before that there was a discussion on how it seems weird when you know everything about Princess that Hunters could make a Princess go Dethroned by accident; I think that can pass because we don't know the whole story, and the Hunters themselve don't know everything about how Princesses dethrone, so it's alright to keep it. On the other hand, if I went to give Princesses and Darkspawns ability they couldn't possibly have nor be mistaken for having, that would be problematic.

        Admittedly, King Raven has difficulty seeing the book with a Hunter perspective (something he admits himself), but given he is better-versed in Princess, he can't be blamed for that^^


        As for the Cold Guard and the Magisters... yeah, pretty much. I think they are both distinct interesting foils to the Nobles; the Cold Guard are cold, pragmatic, close-minded but well-intentioned extremists. The Magisters, meanwhile, are trying to BE Nobles and envious of their powers, but end up as a grotesque, twisted parody of the thing (And I love them for it, in a love to hate kind of way), because they lack what truly make Nobles: the very virtue they reject.

                    West Philadelphia

                    Aside from the current stirrings of war with the fairies, most of West Philadelphia is relatively calm compared to the rest of the city east of the Schuylkill. Unfortunately, this peace may not last very long.

                    Dr. Douglas Palmer, Null Mysteriis Zoologist

                    “Everyone forgets that the animal kingdom is just as vulnerable as the rest of us.”

                    Dr. Palmer has been working at the Philadelphia Zoo for ten years now, occasionally leaving to make an expedition or conference with his close friend, Dr. Malcom Yee. Palmer didn’t learn about the stranger things in the shadows until three years ago however, when the zoo was in danger of losing Tony, their Southern White Rhinoceros. Tony was supposedly sick, but none of the vets could figure out what the problem was. Determined to uncover the cause, Palmer camped out that night with Yee, hoping his friend could help him figure out just was wrong with Tony. That was when the thing slithered over the walls of the enclosure.

                    Malcom guided Palmer to ORAS, and from there Palmer has taken it upon himself to both guard the zoo from the predations of monsters trying to use the zoo’s animals as resources and to study what he might come across. He found that for years, the zoo had been a veritable candy store for the unknown in Philadelphia. Gorilla blood, lion fur, lizard scales, resources for paranormal “recipes” capable of doing anything from imbuing a man with the sprinting speed of a cheetah to venom of a cottonmouth. The zoo had been lucky though, the animals were too precious to outright kill. Still, Palmer took up his protection of the zoo, and has been consulted by ORAS on several cryptozoological finds since Yee’s disappearance.

                    Worrying incidents have Palmer on edge now. Someone has been trying to break into the zoo, he finds the evidence each morning when he has his daily talks with the morning staff. Scratch marks, locks jimmied, and gouges in the landscaping outside several exhibits. The animals are all healthy however, none are showing any odd or adverse symptoms that could indicate a paranormal incident in the making. Still, Palmer is worried, and has considered the possibility that someone is through with the zoo being under hunter control.

                    Description: Palmer is just over 40, but his work has kept him healthy enough; sometimes you need to wrestle the animal down when it gets riled for it’s own good. His hair is lightly salted, and a gleaming enthusiasm for the animals always keeps his eyes lit brightly.

                    Storytelling Hints: Palmer’s role in the Vigil is one few hunters remember to care about. Of course, this worries him greatly, since he wonders just how long it’s been since someone realized that creatures of the darkness were stalking his zoo and the animals under his charge. He doesn’t know who or what is trying to get at the animals, but he’s also curious as to what’s stopping them. They’re clearly able to enter the zoo, but why aren’t the animals being harmed yet?

                                    As I understand it, the Cold Guard's basic premise is that magic and the supernatural are inherently evil; the better and more innocent magic looks, the worse it really is, and the more it needs to be wiped out lest it deceive the unwary. They're foils to Princesses because they cast doubt on the whole Hopeful mythos, and the assumption that the Light is beneficial - especially since they know a lot about that mythos. (And, you know, it's actually true in P:tH that Dark creatures are drawn to the Light-touched. The Cold Guard's view would be hard to refute in-setting. We know that Nests feed on human evil because P:tH's rules say so, but the Nobles can't be sure.) It has nothing to do with idealism vs. pragmatism or short-term vs. long-term thinking.

                                    I don't know of any other hunter group that takes that view exactly, and makes a point of hunting supernaturals who are trying to help humans.

                                    Incidentally, I think the Cold Guard's Endowment is over-powerful for what it costs - Endowments are priced as Merits, IIRC? I'd split Ice Dam into three: a Merit that gives Supernatural Tolerance equal to the dots bought, a Merit that adds to abjuration and exorcism dice pools, and a Merit that forces a Clash of Wills to activate supernatural powers. The second and third would have the first as a prerequisite.

                                            Originally posted by Michael Brazier View Post
                                            They're foils to Princesses because they cast doubt on the whole Hopeful mythos, and the assumption that the Light is beneficial - especially since they know a lot about that mythos.
                                            That's the thing, I don't think they cast doubt upon the Hopeful mythos. Imagine a hypothetical debate between a Cold Guard and a Princess.

                                            Cold Guard: Magic is evil.
                                            Princess: No it's not.
                                            Cold Guard, pulling out a gun: Yes it is.

                                            That's not an argument that would make either the Princess or (more importantly) the player think twice about magic. What's worse, it leads to a flat empty conflict with no underlying substance. When the biggest magic haters in Hunter Cannon (the Malleus Maleficarum) fight Mage hunters there's tons of deeper philosophical debates that can underlay the conflict. Hubris vs humility before god. Awakened curiosity/obsession vs faith. Ect.

                                            That's what the Cold Guard need. Something that they can actually argue about to a Princess, preferably where both sides have a point. Better yet, something where a Cold Guard and a Princess can both see a suffering mortal and have a real argument about how to help them. That something should be the core of the Cold Guard. Not their hatred for magic.

                                            Originally posted by Michael Brazier View Post
                                            We know that Nests feed on human evil because P:tH's rules say so, but the Nobles can't be sure.
                                            Objection! The Charm Weight of Memory provides objective proof that acts of human evil cause nests. A Princess "has a vision of the most emotionally intense event the object was involved in within the Longevity period" and may learn "did the event cause the growth of a Tainted or Blessed area". (That's from your version)

                                            So Princesses have proof that human evil causes Tainted Places.

                                            (Besides, the best Hunter organisations are ones that have arguments that can make a player playing their prey's splat think, not just a charachter).

                                            Originally posted by Dusksage View Post

Compare to the Malleus Maleficarum; where regardless of if the Exarchs are real; regardless of wheather god is real, the MM Hunter and the Mage can still have an argument about solving mysteries vs faith.
