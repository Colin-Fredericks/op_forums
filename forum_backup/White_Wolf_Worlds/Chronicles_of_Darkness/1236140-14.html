    Originally posted by Jeremysbrain View Post
    A couple of questions I'm not entirely clear on:

    Do mummies have to be awakened in their tomb or in an emergency can a cult awaken them somewhere else? As long as they have an inheritor, and some vessels could they summon the mummy in a Walmart parking lot?

    If the mummy is risen in an inheritor or canoptic jar while his sahu still exists, can he be called back to his original sahu on his next descent?

    Can a mummy have multiple tombs with multiple sahu prepared for him?

    Does a mummy retain his memory rating at the end of henet, unlike a death-cycle resurrection?

    They can awaken from their corpse, from one of the four canopic jars containing one of their body organs, from a Talisman they created, or within the body of an individual invested with one of their pillars. It's easier to do in the tomb, but it will happen depending on which of the above is present at the Call.

    His sahu, if it has not previously been destroyed, would theoretically still exist, but the implication for rising in an inheritor is that the inheritor's body becomes the new sahu, rather than an additional one. I don't know that I've seen anything saying either way directly.

    There can be multiple tombs, and you can store the sahu, talisman, and canopic jars in one tomb, multiple tombs, or not in tombs at all.

    Memory is generally retained if you die and resurrect in the middle of a Descent. At the start of a Descent, what happens to your memory is determined in large part by what happens during the Arisen's experience in Duat. There are systems in place to determine the outcome, but it is partially up to the storyteller what benefits, or penalties, result from their experiences.

                                Originally posted by nothri View Post
                                Has this question been answered?- How does a cultist survive long enough to enter a mummy's tomb and perform the Call over their body when entering the mummy's tomb is supposed to trigger the monster to animate and kill the invader the moment they enter?
                                Entering a mummy's Tomb is a Sybaris trigger, which cultists are immune to; waking from a disturbed tomb is not guaranteed; the section on the Sarcophagus Text specifies that sometimes the cult simply doesn't survive the entry; and most of the text around waking from disturbance involves tomb robbers and/or the contents of the tomb actually being disturbed.

                                Also, you can perform the Call without being in the mummy's Tomb — there's a not-insubtantial bonus for doing it there, but you don't need to play with fire to quite that degree to wake up a mummy whose cult you belong to.

                                        It took me a while to find it, but I have a partial answer to your question. On the 84th page of the Corebook, it is stated that Vampires and Abmortals are treated as Immortals for the purpose of Investment. This means they can carry up to four Pillars. The Halifax setting has a Hollow Mekhet Inheritor as a notable character, described on page 295. Page 84 also implies that all major templates can be Invested, but doesn't clarify at which level, and also suggests that Pillars may interact with them in unpredictable ways.

                                                Originally posted by Demigod Beast View Post
                                                Is Shezmu a fiend? Or something else entirely. Indeed, the Duat section of the corebook mentions gods other than the Judges living there, but that they have no interest in the Arisen. What would you count these things as? Fiends? Inactive Judges?
                                                Stuff that's not coming outside in any context where that matters.

                                                The only thing out of Mummy's cosmology from that neck of the woods that isn't in an established mechanical category answers "is this a Supernal god, a demon, a creature of death, or something else?" with "Yes," and there's precious little reason to expect things that aren't Anpu to read any differently if they ever showed up above the topmost layer of Duat.

                                                The gods aren't fiends or Judges, they're gods. They're plot devices mechanically and their own thing narratively. What they do is make the world work the way it does (and occasionally the way it's not supposed to, when Sutek's place in their makeup asserts itself) and produce phenomena more relevant to the story than themselves.

                                                They're metaphors. This is very much a "Supernal beings don't usually appear in the world when they haven't had bodies fashioned for them by magic or occult confluence" sort of thing mixed with a "beings of godlike power tend to need circumstances that take entire stories to prepare to bring them out of their home dimensions." Ostensibly Shezmu being The Wine-Press Of The Gods means those of them who aren't Anpu or Re are just getting drunk on human suffering, and if they want to poke the twitching carcass they express themselves more subtly through the things they are.

                                                        That being said, I say feel free to treat any given Nameless God you feel inspired by from Egyptian Mythology as a Judge if you need to assign any powers or stats for any reason.
