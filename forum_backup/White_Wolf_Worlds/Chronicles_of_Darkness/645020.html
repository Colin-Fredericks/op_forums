    How do conspiracys survive?

    08-30-2015, 02:08 AM
    Something that's confused me. Let's say a conspiracy sets themselves up in a certain location. They spot a (werewolf, vampire, creature from the black lagoon, ect). And kill it. Then they find another one and kill it as well, then another, and another. And if somehow the hunters are still alive I would assume that the local (insert hunted creature here) population would start looking into the random deaths, eventually leading them to kill the hunters, or forcing the hunters to retreat and get back up. More hunters arrive and there is all out war. The conspiracy would likely win the first time, but then it would happen again, and again. Until they all died or the conspiracy becomes a compact as most of them die. So how exactly do they function when they have a "kill on sight" motto and organized supernaturals?

                  There is always the fact that the monsters being hunted are not an internationally organized body of like-minded and disciplined people. They are a fractured bunch that do not communicate well on anything more than a regional level and even at a regional level they are often wracked by internal politics that can get extremely bloody at times. What is more, their ability to act tends to be tied since they have to keep up at least some pretense of secrecy. Not just because of humanity, but also because of their own supernatural enemies. A large amount of deaths is not going to help keeping a low profile. Besides, there is always the arrogance in that they belief that the hunter groups are a great pawns to use against other monsters. Not to mention that the sane, well-adjusted monsters are unlikely to even draw the attention of the hunters. It are the insane and depraved amongst themselves who draw such attention, and if somebody else takes care of them, good riddance. Why do you think Hunters tend to think of the others as monsters? Those are the opponents they tend to face.

                  The conspiracies on the other hand are disciplined, organized and well trained to keep their secrets hidden. Most of their agents also know very little about the upper echelons of their organization and as such can betray very little when caught. The organization leadership likely has some backups hidden, and that is assuming they are not actually supernaturals themselves (which is certainly true for some of the conspiracies). I also doubt that they would stick to one town, trying to eradicate everything non-human. For one thing, there are not enough Hunters to do so and a well-adjusted monster stays below the radar. Hunters of a conspiracy tend to go to a place to investigate a specific case and leave when they have dealt with it. It are the lower tiered hunter groups that stick to one place, but those either end up disbanding, dead or as pawns eventually and if destroyed a new similar group rises later on with similar believes.

                          Conspiracies draw from hypothetical conspiracies in real life as a general principle, so they survive in similar ways- hiding among lots of numbers, lots of dummy set-ups that can be stepped up at any moment, striking when they have the game overwhelmingly stacked, and more or less just playing the paranoid game as best they can.

                          Something to consider is that we, as players and readers, know that the conspiracies have bases in Athens, Milan, Rome, etc., but the monsters don't, or at least, didn't know about for a long time. The Conspiracies kept up their veil of secrecy long enough to become established powers where they settled in, and decentralized enough that they can no longer just hit one place and win.

                          In fact, that's another thing, the supernatural aren't actually coordinated all that well. Sure, the Tribes exist around the world, but the focus for werewolves at the Pack level and the expectation is that local wolves deal with local problems. The Courts in 1E and the Covenants are shared ideologies and structures, but there's little actual communication between cities-and in 2E, Courts are now less global than they were, with each city now having a choice between traditional courts and new ones. Throngs are as much grouping as you get in Promethean, and that's not going to take down a Conspiracy anytime soon. Krewes generally also exist on the Throng/Pack level as Cults, and the second common structure for them, Factions, tend towards a small region. The Guilds are global affairs, with some decent structure too, but the Arisen have to weigh in their time value versus their duties, and often times the Conspiracies aren't large enough to merit actual attention within their timeframe. It falls to the cults to figure out if it's worth that, and most cults do't actually work with other cults to coordinate that sort of thing. Demonic Agencies are focused on their local region, and those are really more informational networks most of the time. Beasts might have the capacity to pull together broods(Including all of the former) on the right sort of scale to do it, but they consider a fair number of conspiracies as troublesome kin and often would need to invest in building up presence. Mages are the only ones who have the human tendency to look beyond their area and collaborate on the sort of scale that can handle conspiracies now, and they have better things to do.

                          The point is that the Conspiracies have stayed alive by staying quiet and sneaky and the right kind of populous enough throughout the ages long enough to become both powerful enough, wide enough, and connected enough to be able to square off against the more factitious monsters.

                                                      A conspiracy has an interest in keeping their Vigil a secret from the world. At most, they might expose an individual, not supernatural organizations. Rarely, would a conspiracy actually know how numerous a given group of supernaturals might be, so they are cautious. Mortals, if they knew the truth about supernaturals, could wage a war and the mortals would win against even a combined effort of all supernaturals. The cost in death to eliminate one group would be difficult to imagine. So even if KOS is the motto, it still falls short of all out war. It isn't worth it for all parties involved. A cell doesn't worry about the big picture. A compact is still too small to pull off anything so big. A conspiracy has leaders that know they will never "win" the war so they focus on battles.
