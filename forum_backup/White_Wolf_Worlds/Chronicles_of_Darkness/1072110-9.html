        Well, to give a bit of context: if you want your protagonists to be sympathetic and still claim that it's possible to "nobly kill" anything, let alone something that was basically irradiated into a sadistic mutant monster, in order to decrease the total about of evil in the world than you either need them to a) be either fanatical or deluded or b) write up said mutant monsters as so irredeemably evil that it might as well turn them into baby eating Nazi's. But that's sort of the buy-in for Princess the Hopeful though, it's a dark fantasy game at heart. Not a horror game. And that puts it at odds with all of the core CofD gamelines since even Inferno acknoledges that Demons on their own are shallow and empty characters, but their interactions with regular humans is what makes them interesting and gives depth to stories of faustian bargains and good intentions leading to a descent into hell.

        But I digress, I'd probably write the morality play section as sort of a primer before the supplement goes into the Nobility proper. Spend a few sections discussing how individual Hunters beliefs are effected and shaped by their experience with the Vigil, and how their personal interactions with the supernatural shapes their morality. Then slowly go larger in scale until you get to how Conspiracies like the Aegis Kai Doru develop their own mythology to justify their Hunting practices and how personal experiences can call into question those beliefs and how that could potentially be both good and bad for Hunters in the field. Something like that.

                Feel sorry about? Definitively no. In all cases my success would amount to mostly luck I'd imagine.

                Justified though...well that depends. If it was personal then yes. A Kindred setting fire to their shadowy tormentor or a Changeling kicking in the teeth of their Keeper can be very powerful scenes. But feeling justified about the destruction of another creature is a very dangerous spiral to fall down. Coincidently that very line of logic is primarily what Hunter the Vigil is about, but even then the game clearly realizes that this line of thinking is what transforms ordinary people into monsters.

                That downward spiral only tangentially exists in Princess. Any Noble can feel completely justified in slaying all the Darkspawn they want without consiquences. The only cause for pause involve collateral damage, or slaying people who are dark but not "fully tainted." It just...strikes me as sort of fiat to allow players to guilt free butcher semi-human creatures that are often metamorphosise against their own will. And that turns to contempt when said game's player character culture swears it's for their own good.

                I'm not the only one who sees this as kinda fucked up right? Maybe that's even the point? It just...it's kinda weird, really the only problem I have with the game. But then again, maybe that just how the genre is?

                    Trying to avoid turning this thread into a long morality debate, I got the impression that darkspawn are probably not meant to be common. The Nobles aren't meant to keep killing darkspawn, they're meant to make the world such that it stops spawning darkspawn. Killing darkspawn is meant to be a tragic case of having to do something terrible because no-one else stepped in when things could still be saved.

                    There's a reason that the faction whose defining purpose is "kill darkspawn" are one of the villains. If you're slaughtering darkspawn wholesale, then somethings gone wrong. What you should be doing is redeeming darkened, purifying tainted areas and making the world a place that doesn't spawn darkspawn.

                    At least, that's what I got and how I'd play it.

                                                    Potential opinions on the darkness from the other main Compacts:

                                                    Network Zero- As much as they'd hate to admit it, lurkers are really useful for the secret frequency. They're obviously supernatural, leave behind huge amounts of evidence and barely try to hide. Finding one in the area is usually cause to try and get as much footage as possible before someone smashes it.
                                                    This, however, leads to another thought- unlike most supernatural creatures, you can make a lurker. Not with complex rituals or rare cosmic coincidences, but just by hurting people. You can do it by accident. Of course, almost everyone in the compact finds the idea abhorrent...but the thought keeps coming up in the more extreme fringes. Final and undeniable proof of the supernatural, at the cost of one person's soul. Might that not be worth it?

                                                    Null Mysteris- Serious studies into the Darkness are running into problems. Not just because it seemingly lacks any kind of biological, chemical or energy-based vector- at this point the scientists of the compact are pretty used to that- but because of the ethical difficulties studying it. Pretty much any serious investigation into the outer darkness violates every experimental ethics guideline at best and is outright scientific atrocity at worst.
                                                    Among those members of the compact who know of the Darkness, two sides are forming. One argues it should be left for now- knowledge isn't worth your humanity. Others say that the need to study this potentially apocalyptic force justifies overriding some normal restraints. The debate is rapidly getting less academic, and if left to grow may soon spill out into the wider compact.

                                                    The Loyalists of Thule- The Loyalists hate the darkness. Lurkers, infiltrators, nests- they all need to go. They say this is because of its danger and evil, and that's certainly true. But also, with their knowledge of the occult, they are one of the few groups who know of the Darkened. The idea that the worst among might not have committed their crimes alone, then responsibility might lie in the hands of some shadowy alien force rather then the human heart- to the Indebted, it's both psychologically and ideologically horrific. Better to destroy the darkness before people get ideas.

                                                    Ageis Kai Doru- the Agies Kai Doru are worried by the darkness. They know how paradise fell, and it wasn't the outer darkness. Is it some dark force the mages of Atlantis drew on to power corrupt arts? Then why don't the tales of atlantis sound anything like the powers infiltrators wield?
                                                    Were the Mistress of Honey and Asterion children of the dark? Then why aren't shapeshifters affected by jade in any way, or darkspawn by silver?
                                                    Was the fall of atlantis not the first time mankind lost paradise? Then why don't any of the records of Atlantis mention that?
                                                    Practically, of course, it doesn't matter- they're monsters and must be stopped. But try as they might, the Guardians can't fit the Darkness into their mythology, and more and more, that worries them.

                                                    Ascending Ones- The ascending ones are frustrated by the Darkness. Their role as diplomats fail before them- even the smartest Darkspawn refuses to co-operate or bargain except as prelude to attack, and their presence brings chaos to both mundane and supernatural communities.
                                                    The easiest answer is to wipe them out, of course, and most ascending ones do. But on faction have started trying to capture them, in the hope of redeeming them. If they can take even these monsters and make them good- or at least reasonable- that would be a significant practical and moral accomplishment. If it leads to anything but slaughter on both sides, that is.

                                                    Mallus Malificeruim. They're demons. Demons from hell, send them back. Easy right? Well, no. Most demons, they need to tempt. They manipulate or lie or bribe, but they can't just make you sin. They can possess, yes, but that doesn't stain your soul. Not the Darkness. The Darkness just makes you a worse person, overrides your free will and makes you turn away from God. If some theories about what happens with darkspawn is correct it might even destroy your soul outright. The idea of the devil being able to do that is horrifying on an almost unimaginable level to the Hammer of Witches.
                                                    Naturally, the higher ups write long theological papers explaining why that isn't the case- the victim must have subconsciously listened to the darkness, or only the body was controlled, or the soul goes straight to hell- but even the possibility is devastating to Catholicism. A good reason to kill the whole thing now just in case.

                                                    TF:V- TF:V doesn't care a huge amount about the darkness- they're monsters, kill them, boom. Job done. But there's a secret. People in power- politicians, businessmen, generals- there is a lot of darkened among them. A few infiltrators, even. You don't reach those heights without doing something, and often it has consequences. And TF:V knows exactly who they are and how integral they are to the power structure of the nation.
                                                    Naturally, if this was revealed would be deeply damaging to the USA's authority, so the grunts aren't told about this. As long as they think the darkness is just monsters they can shoot that's great. Another devil dealt with by TF:V.

                                                        On the subject of saving Darkspawn. It's very intentionally impossible. Princess (at least Dream) is designed to be a toolbox style. If you want to play an all Troubadour game along the lines of Jem and the Holograms where the only antagonists are: mortal paparazzi, muckraking gossip magazines, and record company executives, you can do that. If you want to play a Sailor Moon inspired game where you fight your way through progressively more powerful Darkspawn every session then you can do that too.

                                                        In line with that design philosophy there has to be at least one antagonist type where violence is the unambiguously correct solution for players who want to play in the style of classical magical girl warriors, lots of magic lasers, no moral issues with using them. Thus, apart from Darkened and Dethroned, there's no curing Creatures of the Darkness.

                                                        Of course Hunters might not know that. (Though Hunters being more concerned about monster rights than Princesses does seem a tad athematic to me)
