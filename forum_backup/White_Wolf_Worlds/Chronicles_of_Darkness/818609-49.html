                I can kind of see that. My issue with Genius's Resourcs-based Wonder construction (other than the 1E Resources), was that it felt too simplistic, and it made the Wonders feel less personal as a result. I did leave the option in to just have components pop up as you go, but I can definitely see how it might bog down play, especially for hight-complexity Wonders.                 Maybe loosen the requirements, have one Core Component that holds it together and is hard to get, and the other components are just ones you can just pick up more easily?                 Finally, it appears that the only difference between Low and High Inspiration Genii is that the latter has more stable Wonders and can support more at a time. They're Wonders powers might still be identical. Is this intentional?
                Sort of. There's kind of been an issue of "do-everything Genii" in Genius, and I wanted to make it so you would be less able to to everything (for example, now you have to fit it into your Paradigm, and it's harder to make things you don't have a working knowledge of), but still able to play the Genius you want to play (Your character can start play with their weird psychic technique or whatnot, and they can develop more without having to progress super-far into things), without making advancing pointless.

                And while it's kind of only tangentially related, I'm considering the idea of making Inferences/Postulates/Whatever into a Condition you can get by working with a Field. So, in preparation for developing your formula for turning lead to gold, you can perform some studies with Alchemy to get the "Transmutation" Inference Condition, or you could study an open Avernian Gate you run across in order to store up a Condition in case you wanted to build a portal to the Underworld later. Resolving the Inference Condition reduces the complexity of a relevant Wonder, and offers an Inspired Beat.

                                                    EDIT: Here's the new text for Step 6, "Acquiring Components."
                                                    Step 6: Acquiring and Incorporating Components

                                                    Now that you’ve designed your Wonder, the hunt is on! You need to somehow get ahold of the Components. You can do this through trade, calling in favors, skullduggery, heroics, or simply taking it, and while you might be able to get them through favors, Allies, Contacts, Status, or sending a Retainer to retrieve it, there will always be at least one Linchpin component, one that’s integral to your Wonder and is too rare or difficult to acquire to simply buy it or send somebody else to get it.
                                                    System: Minor and Major Components

                                                    Most of the components for a Wonder are not too terribly difficult to get. Usually, they can be gotten by buying them, getting a Grant from status in your Fellowship, calling in a favor, or sending a Retainer to go get it (often an Igor, or occasionally a more intelligent Mane or Automaton.). These a Minor Components. Funtionally, they are goods with an Availability rating of 2 to 4 dots, and you can use Resources, Status or Contacts in a relevant group, Allies, or the rating of your Retainer to get it (and you may have to roll for it if you can’t get it). These are still necessary, but they’re easier to acquire. If you can’t get the Component this way, you can choose to acquire it like a Major Component (below), or you can try to redesign your Wonder to use a different component.
                                                    Alternately, there are also Major Components. These Components are rare enough, dangerous enough, or just difficult enough to get hold of that you can’t just call it in. You’re going to have to get it yourself. Every Wonder has at least one Major Component (called a Lynchpin). A Larva is also automatically a Major Component (you can’t just call up your biology professor and ask him to steal people’s eyes, now, can you?), and you can make a single Component a Major Component to apply 9-again to incorporating any Minor Components, as you’ve specificaly found the perfect part to tie the Wonder together. These aren’t more difficult to Incorporate, but they’re immensely more difficult to find. Getting a Major Component should be a miniature adventure in itself. Breaking into a government lab to steal their experimental processor, hunting through a dense jungle Bardo for an ancient Diamond, chasing down a damaged UFO to take its power core, and so on.
