    Your view on the World of Darkness?

    07-25-2016, 08:14 PM
    Since Chronicles of Darkness is quite modular setting, it's interesting how you see the world itself?
    As for me, I think that World of Darkness should be... you know... dark)) So, I see the following points^
    1) High lethality (like, we have no 'official' statistic, so in my headcanon it like 'among all nub-supernatural creatures only half of them survive first 5 years, and the next 5 years will survive only half of the first half' But THAN the statistic curve flattens - cuz victims turn to hunters.
    2) Social structure - a medieval (or gangster/cartel) mentality/ Like, small groups, strong unity inside (cuz you can't trust somebody except your bros and sis), strong xenophobia to outsiders (any stranger is an enemy or at very least suspicious - till prove otherwise). To guys from other splats, the same xenophobiaX2. Lot of cruel moments (Vendetta and mutual responsibility lead to such cute things like 'for fail of the one all his/her family and friends will be slayed - and their age, sex and pregnancy stage is irrelevant'). "Kill or be killed", "right of the power" and other principles work directly.
    The world operates according to the laws of dark crime drama

    So, how you see your WoD?

          Dark. Not as in hopeless, but as in creepy. Not grey, but dark shades of vibrant colors. There's lot of genuine happy and peaceful lives out there that aren't destroyed by evil monsters/fellow man. But even those feel or remember something creepy out there; not exactly watching and leering at them (those are usually the victims), but just... there. We pretend they're not but we don't go out on full moons 'just in case'. As the hyspanic saying goes 'non creo en brujas pero que las hay las hay'.

          Also limited settings. Usually humans and splat-less monsters (maybe even including splat-less versions of the usual guys), or splat-centric world with appropriate other monster conforming to it (mostly unexplainable or cthulian cryptids and mortal witches, warlocks and sorcerers).

                                          The personal horror of Chronicles is probably one of my favorite things about the game.

                                          I'm big on the general concept of the recognition of flaws and the acceptance of damning factors. Really, personal stories about coping with the terrible, learning to abandon them or transform them into the wonderful or find the healthiest way to go with it-those are the things I like. A character's external struggles are fun to watch and all, but they don't mean as much to me if I don't get the pleasure of knowing that they were once terrified of who they are or were or what they did, that they are angry at themselves or sad at themselves, how the world wounds them-how they'll do those things again and again, how it keeps happening-and how, these fights, these moments, are about them reclaiming themselves, transforming into more or greater, or even something worse but is wholly theirs.

                                          I don't like escapism. It has its' place, its' uses. But a crazy shoot-out lacks punch if someone's not descending into or arising out of the abyss. I want to know how the shoot-out is transforming them, not how many bodies are hitting the floor.

                                              It's a universe without cause or reason, the supernatural is there for the same reason the natural is, a complicated culmination of random events. Most understand this, and it scares them into clinging to some belief or another that makes sense of the world and promises some greater meaning.

                                              The supernatural is wildly prevalent, everyone's been touched by it, though in most cases it's nothing as obvious as an encounter with a vampire. More often it's something like Thomas Ligotti's Gas Station Carnivals, a lingering tension constantly itching at that reminder that the universe isn't how they try to think it is, isn't safe or even meant for them.

                                              Taking some cues from the old World of Darkness, it's our world but worse, just as its our world but more mysterious. The tension drives most people to extremes. Maybe they hide out, find some safe niche with people like themselves and never leave it, the whole group growing more and more bitter at the outsiders. Or maybe they act out, stealing money from their bosses, employees, friends, family, gang, cult. Some see ways to seize control of things and go after that. There's a heavy sense of desperation the closer you get to the heart of the cities, and the wilds a sort of eerie fraying, as the things people cling to are farther and farther away and the truth of the senselessness of the world becomes harder to deny.

                                              Like Lashet, I have the 'mortality' rate high, even among supernaturals, with most either dying or losing themselves to whatever their version of 0 Humanity is. Vampires sometimes just disappear, thought to have traveled to another city or fallen into torpor and are never heard from again. Werewolves who fall in holes in time or Shadow are thought to have died in battle against some unseen enemy. Among the supernaturals I try to play up the lack of knowledge about 'other places.' There's strange, scary tales about not-so-far-off lands. A city in the next state where the Circle follows a hideous Blood Moon that grants them the power to convert other vampires to its worship. A town of werewolves somewhere down the highway who serve a Firstborn no one's ever heard of that hunts Forsaken and Pure with them in the flesh.

                                                  I use the following quote to pitch the setting to any prospective players:
                                                  "What are you prepared to do to survive tonight and will you be able to live with it come morning?"

                                                  Everyone knows somethings up but they keep their heads down and try not to grab any unwanted attention. Life is scary enough without thinking about the paranormal. The vampire who tried to drain you in that alley was "just" a ordinary mugger, the witch who read your fate and said you'll die in 7 days was just a hack psychic. And the thing that took your infant son from his crib last night? What are you talking about you never had a son! Now take your medication and get some sleep.

                                                  Every part of the World of Darkness is an isolated state. Similar to a feudal kingdom. What's happening in New York is not happening in Tokyo. Large cities have factions of monsters politicking and sabotaging each other. Small towns usually only have a local pack of werewolves or a couple of vamps who hunt in the night. And in isolated rural areas things are completely unique. A cult in Loch Ness drowns sacrifices so they can get safe passage to the underworld. A group of werecoyotes cause car wrecks on the desert highways of Arizona as a practical joke only they get. And in the jungles of the Philipines, things called Tikbalangs jump down from the trees and grab passerby and bring them up to their nests. It's our world reflected through a fractured mirror, you still recognize it but the image is unfocused and distorted.

                                                  The stories are intensely personal. Few "save the world" stories here. Underground factions fight each other for supremacy but there is no one power controlling everything, there are a million tiny conspiracies all tripping over one another.

                                                  In general I see the setting as resembling contemporary shows like True Detective, Black Mirror, Utopia, and Hannibal.

                                                  For more urban settings I think of the works of Thomas Ligotti and Franz Kafka. For wilderness settings I think of the carnivorous horror of Laird Barron.

                                                  Basically the Chronicles of Darkness is one gaping maw that will swallow you and spit you back up as a monster.
