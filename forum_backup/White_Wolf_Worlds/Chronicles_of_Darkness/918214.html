    Let's Talk Cartoons

    06-20-2016, 04:16 PM
    Or, rather, cartoons that could be mined for ideas. I'm going to throw some out - I'll try to go for more obscure ones, because heaven only knows that you've probably seen the obvious ones.

    Mona the Vampire is a slice of my childhood... and probably a good look at what a light-hearted Innocents chronicle looks like. It's about a little girl who dresses up as a vampire and investigates mysteries which she gives all kinds of supernatural explanations for. The episodes flip-flop between how she sees things and what's "really" happening. I suggest it if you can still watch children's shows without spraining muscles from eye-rolling.

    Detentionaire is another good one to mine for ideas vis-a-vis an Innocents chronicle. The writers clearly had some fun with it, and it's well worth the watch. Let me tell you, the cyborg principal and live tatzlwurm mascot are the tip of the iceberg. It's really damn entertaining, is all I'm saying.

    The Maxx... Where to start? Well, say hello to the oneiros of Julie Winters. It's got bits and pieces for Changeling - though who the Changelings are is up for debate. Maxx pretty clearly feels like one of the Claimed, and Australia... "Not the Australia they teach you about in the cities - the Outback. No, the *real* Australia: the smoking caves, the gods who eat mountains, the flying dragos, the rivers of gold, the Leopard Queen taunting me, almost an enemy, beautiful and feral as the great beasts she runs with."

    If you haven't seen it, MTV has the whole thing on their website. I strongly suggest it.

    Cybersix is... I've not checked the inspiration list for Deviant, but I'd not be surprised if Cybersix was on there. It even has the angst . It's been a while since I watched it, but it was a fun ride, and my gut is telling me that, yes, it was very World of Darkness.

    Delta State: Stop me if you've heard this one before - four amnesiac psychics walk into a defensive war against voracious mental parasites from the deep human subconscious. Seriously, they have the Clairvoyance, Telepathy, Omen Sensitivity, and Object Reading merits, respectively, and have some merit that lets them enter the astral as a group. It's worth a watch, if only for the clever tricks for low-key psychics.

    So, anyone want to add to the list (I'm expecting to see someone suggest Gravity Falls or Over the Garden Wall. My hopes are a little lower for Monster Allergies. )

      I haven't watched these in a long time, so I'm relying on my memory and quick searches.

      Gargoyles: Not only is the atmosphere already excellent, the drama between the fascinating characters struck me as well-written even when I was too young to really know what that meant. I also remember fearing Xanatos a lot more than the Gargoyles, which is a testament to the former's cunning as an antagonist.

      Ben10: Despite being aimed at kids, the show had all sorts of fascinating ideas for aliens and assorted weirdness, and how they might adapt to life among humans. The Plumbers are a great inspirational material for Conspiracies. Some episodes have pretty dark stuff as well, but it never lost sight of the humor and the Rule Of Cool. I haven't seen the sequel series, so I can't recommend those. Ben himself could be seen as a Deviant, since he has an implanted gadget with strange and visually obvious powers that motivates several people and monsters to hunt him down.

      Godzilla: No, not the one with Godzooky. I'm talking about the one based on the 1998 American adaptation that got terrible reviews. It has a kick-ass intro, and some cool ideas for how the CoD might respond to kaiju threats and allies. The designs for the kaiju themselves are pretty great too.

      Batman The Animated Series: Seriously, I'm putting this one here as more of an "inb4". If you want some inspiration for a vigilante Hunter game, look no further.

      I didn't know you liked cartoons, Amechra. Do you happen to frequent /co/ and /tg/?

                  There was a short lived show that Disney had called Filmore that is a absolutely great source of inspiration for how to portray an interesting chase in an innocents game, with multiple foot chases per episode as a result of the series being a send up of 70s police procedurals starring a middle safety patrol. The show is hard to find though, there seems to some issue with the rights to some of the music used in the show that keeps Disney from releasing it on dvd or streaming.

                  Scooby Doo Mystery Incorporated is what happens when you take a tongue in cheek version of the classic scooby doo formula, add an ongoing plot that draws inspiration from horror genre sources ranging from classic horror movies to Babylonian mythology to HP Lovecraft in ways both serious and parodic with a big spoonful of interpersonal drama between the mystery inc gang for good measure. This is very much a darker and grittier scooby doo, to the point where the costumed villains actually try to kill the gang rather than limiting themselves to trying to scare. Perfect for the chronicles of darkness.

                      Spawn certainly has the dark imagery and mystery of the Chronicles of Darkness. It arguably takes it too far, you might be surprised they didn't run out of black ink, but the representation of mood and atmosphere is solid.

                      Aeon Flux could probably be a good inspiration for a particularly frenetic mortal game, Hunter with a large espionage component, or more likely Demon.

                      A lot of strides have been made in taking cartoons from a series of unconnected episodes to having episodes that are each important parts of a larger story, whether they move against the larger metaplot or show characters developing in response to previous episodes. It's not unheard of to build chronicles like seasons from tv shows. Dragon Magazine did an article about it long, long ago.
