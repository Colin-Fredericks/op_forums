    Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
    That, I think, is a slightly bias reading of the Acanthus. Acanthus don't trade their family for magic, they make a significant and unexpected life change (which may well be abandoning their family) and it triggers their mystery play.
    I prefer to think of it as an awesome reading

    Don't get me wrong. Everything I said is the reason why I like the Acanthus. I wouldn't want to be one, but I think that they're well written and their crazyness makes for both interesting Mage stories and interesting crossover with Changeling.

    But do remember that every Acanthus - actually I'll quote it:
    Before Awakening a Witch thought she was powerless, shacked by family, the state, poverty, unconfronted sins, addictions--the iron-plated road of a predictable life, where pain answers any deviation. Every Sleeper walks in lockstep with karma imposed by the Lie, but before they Awakened, Acanthus felt especially confined. They yearned to escape the way we all do after the hundredth blow from a bully, or the thousandth day at a desk.
    We're roleplayers. I'm sure we've all dreamed of giving up the 9-5 for a life of adventure; but if you actually feel that daily life is a shackle then something is wrong. Either your life is in a bad way, or your mentality is in a bad way. (In fairness, with the Exarchs around it may well be the life)

    Originally posted by PenDragon View Post
    Hedge Wizards are going to see the world through the lens of Wyrd Sorcery, a universe of interconnected narratives and dramatic turns. An Acanthus, whose insight into that structure far surpasses that of a Hedge Wizard, is going to look super-duper-dubious.
    Actually. I think an Acanthus would see Fate through the lense of symbology; which is an entirely different perspective to the universe of interconnected narratives and dramatic turns. Though I can't say how it differers on a practical level.

    Though what you said about Seers sounds like the Acanthus point of view - that's what a Seer symbolises; the platonic archetype of a seer if you will. Admittedly Changeling does use archetypes and tropes a lot (there's merits like ugly stepsister) so maybe I'm just going down a dead end here.

                            Well, you'd probably want to start with Cobblethought (Dream 5) as a base, as both of them do the same thing: conjure things into reality. You'd adjust the mechanics as needed to suit the changed thematics, then you'd either want to bump up the power level *just* a touch or bump it down a touch and lower it to a 4-dot contract. After that you'd want to add a drawback (say, maybe an opposing item or creature of roughly equal power comes along, too, or perhaps your life starts following an unhelpful narrative path. Swap in an appropriate Catch, and you're set.

                            As for making it inherited, this sort of power can't really be transferred by Pledge, rules-as-written. Granted, the Others cheat and your table is your table so you can change that as necessary. Perhaps there's a family where, somehow, everyone who marries in is fae-touched in some way - fetch-child, ensorcelled, or even the rare kid of one of the Lost - and who inherit strange powers as a result? Maybe they've got a hobgoblin parasite infestation. Maybe they're an otherwise-unknown aberration.
