                                            Originally posted by Diggs
                                            It is complicated so I might not be explaining it well. It has been the case from the beginning of Requiem that the werewolves in Requiem might or might not be the werewolves in Forsaken. You can stat werewolves using Requiem mechanics and some hand-waiving, which is the default situation unless you play a Requiem/Forsaken game. They are completely compatible so you can indeed play a combined game of Requiem and Forsaken, but that is not the default. They actually don't worry much about cross-over. They don't try to make vampires and werewolves balanced in any way. If you want to have both in your game by using both lines then you can balance them how you want. They leave it to a group that wants to do it. Vampires are balanced. Werewolves are balanced. Everything is balanced within its own line, but not balanced to other lines. It's hard to explain. Forsaken doesn't exist if you play Requiem unless you want it to exist. They write as if it does, not as if it must. They went for an interesting approach. It is a bit different than Classic.
                                            Tough it is easy for someone used to cWoD to use rules of one splat to represent other creatures, it isn't how the game is intended to work on nWoD. Lets just see Requiem 1st ed:

                                            Pages 114 and 115, about Supernatural Conflict. Through the entire text Disciplines are referred to exclusively as vampiric powers. The text talks about how other supernatural creatures resist to them, but it is fairly explicit in saying they may have or not equivalents to Blood Potency, but not BP itself. It talks about how a vampire resist to their powers with BP, but makes it clear such powers aren't Disciplines. And in the end explicitly says the rules about such creatures are "addressed in Werewolf: The Forsaken, Mage: The Awakening and other Storytelling games".

                                            I just searched for it through the entire book, and saw no reference to use of werewolves or other creatures by Requiem's rules. The book does assume such creatures exist, but it makes no other assumption about them. Instead, it focus on vampires, sure, and simply say to the ST to look for other lines for information on other creatures, no support to work them without the proper line whatsoever. Of course you can do this and of course you can also bring the rules of other lines and drop their own fluff entirely, but that ISN'T an assumption of the game or how it is intended to run. By the way, there are even canon cross-over setting, the city of Chicago IIRC, as far as canon go in nWoD.

                                            Originally posted by Diggs
                                            I can see how werewolves and mages might get in the way of a Requiem game. Shadow, Astral, all that weird stuff. You can strip that away, but for antagonists it might be easier to just use the tools in Requiem to build what you want. For player characters, you seem better off starting with something from another book than building a template from scratch. I think Skinchangers has werewolves of a sort.
                                            Personaly I think the places of power and other worlds of those games make for solid Dragon Nests for the Ordo Dracul. In fact, exploring the other lines and possible truths and myths among other supernaturals makes for a very interesting chronicle. The Ordo would want the knowledge, while the other four Covenants where look for ways to turn those things to their own side, or just act to suppress knowledge of them from vampiric society.

                                            I'm not saying there are anything wrong with ruling out some or all other splats from a given chronicle. I think it work much more in a chronicle-by-chronicle basis than as a general rule. That's why the setting is toolbox-like to begin with. But that said, more tools are more tools, more options of building blocks for your chronicle. Surely there are chronicles where I can pretty much see other splats stealing the spotlight from the one you intend to run, and so it is better to rule them out as they are. In others, the same presence of splats can sharpen the spotlight by contrast.

                                            By the way, werewolves are combat machines on 2nd ed, but far from so much more powerful. With the right build a vampire can overcome most werewolves in single combat, and group combat have so much variables that they hardly can be said on advantage in a fight between a pack and a coterie of the same size. The regeneration thing is only in a very limited in use Killing Form that easily leads them to frenzy. And their frenzy is far worse than a vampire one in power.
