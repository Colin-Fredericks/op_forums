    [Homebrew] Promethean Lineage: Skyggen

    07-13-2016, 01:42 AM
    Hi everyone, this is my first post on these forums, but I'm excited to share this idea I've been tinkering with for a while. Would love to hear feedback if anyone has any!

    What I've got here is a brief/not-so-brief introduction to the Lineage, along with its special Bestowment and rules for Disquiet, Wasteland and Torment. I'm still working on a special Mockery and a couple of Athanors to go along with this Lineage, which I may post when they're finished.

    This is written under 1e nWoD rules, since that's what I've got access to. I expect it would be easy enough to update to 2e rules... suggestions are welcome!
    SKYGGEN
    THE HEDGEBORN


    Basically, the story goes like this: A young scholar, travelling in some distant land, loses his shadow. It being olden times, he doesn’t give it too much thought until years later, when the shadow turns up at his doorstep, dressed up in clothes, flesh and all. The shadow had been on adventures, travelled the world, learned how to be a man himself. They become companions again, the scholar and his shadow, but this time the shadow’s in charge. Then there’s a princess, and in the end—you know what? Just read it yourself, it’s not that long.

    What ancient myths are to Ulgans and Nepri, fairy tales are to the Skygger. They are dreams and shadows bound to dead flesh, new life created from whimsical fantasies. A Skygge is born in the Hedge, that perilous expanse that separates dreams from the waking world. And like the Hedge, a Skygge is always between. Neither human nor monster, neither alive nor dead. It wears a stolen face as a mask, but underneath is just meat that rightly belongs in the ground.

    The Lineage takes its name from the Danish word meaning “The Shadow,” an unsubtle nod to the Hans Christian Andersen story which some believe is the first written record of one of the Hedgeborn. If that truly was the genesis of the Lineage, then it is among the youngest of the Promethean families, dating only to the mid-19th century. It is quite possible, however, that their history stretches back much further

    Rather than the classical bodily humours, Skygger are fueled by Glamour, the distilled essence of pure emotion. Thus, far more than other Prometheans, the Hedgeborn are creatures of varied emotions. Rather than being consumed by anger or sadness, they express a full range of feelings—if only on a superficial level. Shadows’ emotions are highly affected by the emotions of those around them, and they can also change in seconds if circumstances change. Skygger are attracted to social contact, but wary of crowds as too much emotional information can overwhelm their empathic natures. It is not uncommon for them to vacillate between the poles of pursuing connection with others and isolating from it.

    Among Prometheans, Skygger are considered capricious and variable, but also skittish and paranoid. They are always looking over their shoulder for some supernatural threat, or some mortal who recognizes the face they wear. They might flee the mortal world to the Hedge at the drop of a hat, then come back just as abruptly when the Thorns offer no solace.

    Like all the Created, Skygger struggle to find their place in the world, a task made more difficult by the fact that they wear the face of another person. They often travel far from the place of their creation, but in the age of social media and selfies, it seems no place is quite far enough to feel safe.

    Progenitor:
    Beyond the Hedge is another world, a realm of beauty and madness, called Arcadia. The rulers of this fantastic world are beings of terrible power called the Others, the Gentry or the True Fae. Among those few who are even aware of the existence of the Hedgeborn, it is generally believed that the creator of the Lineage was one of these otherworldly entities. It isn’t known which one, or why, or even whether it was on purpose. Some Shadows, and a few other supernatural beings who consider themselves experts on the Hedge, theorize that the creation of the Skyggen Lineage was an unexpected accident: Sometimes, for reasons truly understood by no one, the True Fae capture mortals and take them back to Arcadia. When they do, they often leave behind a replacement called a Fetch who wears the face of the kidnapped mortal. These Fetches are nothing more than soulless automata, cobbled together from leaves and junk found in the Hedge and held together by faerie magic. It is possible that the first Skygge was born when a True Fae attempted to create a Fetch using a fresh human corpse as the raw material. Somehow, this process tapped into the Divine Fire and created something much more than a simple puppet.

    The process of creating a new Skygge is unlike any other Promethean’s in two important ways: First, the generative act must be performed in the Hedge; and second, in addition to a complete corpse, it requires a significant contribution from a living mortal. The corpse that will become the new Skygge must be kept in the Hedge for at least twenty-four hours in order to absorb an appropriate amount of Glamour. Then it is dressed in the clothing of a living mortal, and the shadow of the mortal is made to fall on the would-be Promethean. The creator then sacrifices a dot of his own Azoth as usual, and the creation takes on the face of the living donor. At that point, the mortal’s part is over. A compassionate Shadow would guide the mortal safely back into his own world, or at least give him the courtesy of a quick and clean death. More callous creators might leave the mortal to wander, lost and naked, through the Hedge until his soul is ripped apart by the Thorns, or he is found by something even worse.

    Should the generative process go awry, creating one or more Pandorans, these broken beings share their creator’s ability to enter and leave the Hedge at their pleasure. In addition, they are immune to the soul-rending effects of the Hedge’s thorns and can travel without fear through the thickest brambles. Some Pandorans birthed in the Hedge remain there for the entirety of their existence, waiting in dormancy for a Skygge or other unlucky Promethean to pass by. Most, however, make their way quickly back to the mortal world to find more abundant sources of Azoth.

    Other Nicknames:
    Shadows, Doppelgängers.

    Appearance:
    A Skygge’s outward appearance always begins identical to the living mortal in whose shadow it was birthed. They tend to be a varied lot, generally chosen for being in the wrong place at the wrong time, rather than any particular trait or feature. Skygger often modify their appearance, however, with piercings, tattoos, and radical changes of hair cut and color. This is partly due to Skygger’s tendency to make impulsive changes on the spur of the moment, but it also helps to disguise them from those who might otherwise recognize the face that they wear.

    Disfigurement:
    When a Skygge’s disfigurement becomes visible, her outward, living face darkens and becomes harder to distinguish, before finally fading into transparency and revealing the partially decayed corpse underneath. Expressions and movements of the face appear only as wispy shadows over the dead visage. The body of a Skygge appears impossibly thin, almost two-dimensional. Most Hedgeborn smell of rotting wood and other plants.

    Humour:
    Glamour (Labile, Circumstantial)

    Element:
    Skygger are born from the shadows of mortals, and their element is Shadow. Shadows are the dark reflections of our selves, they can play tricks on the eyes and play games with the mind. Shadow is the element of deception and peril.

    Bestowment:
    Gate Key (See Below)

    Refinements:
    Perhaps more than any other Lineage, many members of the Skyggen practice Argentum (Magnum Opus). Their natural interest in the Hedge and the Fae frequently brings Skygger into contact with Changelings, who may find commonality in their separate but similar searches for humanity. Hedgeborn who embrace their changing, emotional nature may practice Mercurius, while those that favor the isolation of the Hedge sometimes follow the path of Cuprum.

    Character Creation:
    Social Attributes are favored by Skygger, who often need some charisma and finesse to navigate the complicated social worlds that both attract and frighten them. Many Hedgeborn are Jacks (and Jills) of all Trades, having a little knowledge and experience many areas represented by different Skills and Merits.
    Charity and Hope are common virtues for Shadows, whose empathic nature fosters such notions; alternatively, those who seek balance in their unstable lives may strive for Temperance. Being creatures of emotional lability, Skygger often lean toward what feels best in the moment. Thus, Lust and Gluttony are common vices for them.

    Concepts:
    Addictions counselor, carnival worker, children’s author, dancer, expatriate, thief.

    Bestowment:Gate Key
    The Fae magic that linked the first Shadows to the Pyros has left its mark on the Skyggen Lineage, and they share in the Faeries’ ability to enter and exit the Hedge at will.
    Cost: 1 Pyros
    Action: Instant
    Transmutation Cost:Azoth x 7

    Activating this Bestowment in the presence of a door, gate or other portal in the mortal world allows the Promethean to create an egress into the Hedge. Likewise, activing the Bestowment within the Hedge opens a doorway to the human world. The use of this Bestowment and travel within the Hedge follow closely the rules described for Changelings in Changeling: The Lost (pg 216-218), with the notable exception that the Promethean’s Azoth score is used in place of Wyrd wherever applicable (but see also the special rules for Prometheans in the Hedge on page 78 of Equinox Road).

    Though it is not part of their creation, other Prometheans can gain the Gate Key Bestowment as a Transmutation. Such a change does not duplicate the Skyggen disfigurement, but it does necessitate subtle alterations in the humours of the Created on a metaphysical level. To gain the Gate Key Bestowment, the Created must increase her level of Glamour. This increase makes the Promethean more imaginative, but also more prone to daydreaming and paranoia.

    Disquiet:
    The Disquiet engendered by a Promethean of the Skyggen lineage typically manifests first as a profound suspicion that the Promethean is someone who cannot be trusted, a deceiver. Mortals become guarded, not only with the Promethean herself, but with everyone in their lives. Any perceived irregularity, real or imagined, is viewed as a sign that another’s intentions are not what they seem. Families eat dinners in careful silence, neighbors peep at one another through the blinds, and business partnerships dissolve in baseless accusations. Ironically, those touched by this Disquiet become more deceptive and prone to lying themselves, trying to protect their secrets from those who might manipulate and exploit them. As the Disquiet nears its boiling point, individuals may be taken with fits of paranoiac delusion, the most common theme of which is the suspicion that friends and loved ones have been replaced by lookalikes. Eventually, the tension becomes unbearable and the community must either disintegrate or root out the ringleader of the deceivers: almost inescapably the Promethean herself. People don’t like being tricked, and in their desperation may avenge themselves in profoundly violent ways.

    Hedgeborn Wastelands
    The Skyggen’s element is shadow, representing not only deeper darkness but tricks of the eyes and the general sense of danger. When a Skygge’s wasteland manifests, the area becomes more hazardous and unreal.

    Stage Two – Structures cast longer, darker shadows than before. Weeds grow quickly wherever they can, knotting themselves into snags and tripping the unwary. Humans feel edgy and unsettled when entering the area.

    Stage Three – Stinging, cutting plants like nettle, rose and wild blackberry grow everywhere, pushing themselves through cracks in pavement and consuming gardens seemingly overnight. Wood splinters and steel fences flake, leaving everything sharp to the touch. Colours seem brighter in the daylight, but the shadows of night become all but impenetrable.

    Stage Four – Nothing in the area feels safe or predictable. Whenever a person or object enters your field of view, it’s as if they appeared out of nowhere. Laughter and strange music are carried on the breeze with no obvious source. Doors and gates open spontaneously into the Hedge, and those unfortunates who pass through are unlikely to be seen again.

    Hedgeborn Torment
    When a Skygge gives over to Torment, she loses all control over her mercurial feelings and responds with single-minded certainty to whatever emotional whim occurs to her. If she is startled, she will flee in terror; if irritated, she will lash out in violent rage. What might otherwise cause mild discomfort produces uncontrollable retching, and what might be a slight attraction becomes powerful lust. Furthermore, these reactions can change instantaneously if a new emotion is felt, and the Promethean can be easily influenced by the emotions of those around her. As a result of these clashing, overwhelming emotions, the Promethean suffers a -1 penalty to all Composure rolls. The initial period of Torment lasts for a number of hours equal to the Hedgeborn’s Azoth.

          Thanks for pointing that out! You're right that the books are pretty clear that only humans can channel the Divine Fire-- though it seems to me that they're a little bit ambivalent about it. Of the five main lineages, more than half are rooted in ancient mythology about gods. Two specifically list gods (Ishtar and Isis) as the demiurges, and a third (Ulgan) scrupulously avoids giving a demiurge at all, but notes that the progenitor was torn apart and put back together by spirits. You can always say that in reality these were all humans, and that the stories have just changed over time, but that's a little un-World of Darknessy for me.

          I also kind of question how much it really impacts on a Promethean story what the true nature of their Lineage's demiurge was, since within the context of the story the only one who can create a new Promethean is another Promethean-- but once again, you're right that the fluff is quite specific on the point. So if you want to play with that premise firmly in place, but want to try out the Skyggen lineage, then there is a simple and thematic answer for you: No one knows who the true demiurge was. The story, while compelling, is just a story...

          That's one way to do it, anyway. What do you think? Thank you for your comments!
