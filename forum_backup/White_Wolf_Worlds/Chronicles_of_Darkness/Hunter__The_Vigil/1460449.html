    Council of Bones- Issues and Thoughts

    09-19-2021, 03:22 PM
    So when the 2e Kickstarter launched, the manuscript have introduced us three new organizations- Nine Stars, SWORN and the Council of Bones. While I loved Nine Stars from the moment that I read them, I found out that I didn't really like the other two organizations. Now, I am the first person who says that no hunter group is for everyone and the reason that Hunt is so customizable is because of that reason, but the thing is that up to this point, I didn't really dislike any of the groups- oh, sure, there were some groups I barely cared about, like the Heritage House or the Illuminated Brotherhood, but lack of feelings is very different from actively not liking a certain splat- so it was a new experience for me, which may me ask myself why. One of the things I considered was that perhaps it is the result of a personal baggage- the Ashwood Abbey and the Aegis Kai Doru are among my favorite Hunter orgs, so it made sense I may have some lingering emotions which affects my judgement (yeah, self criticism is important when you criticize a product, and as I know I am an emotional person I know it may cloud my judgement), and as such I decided to take a step back, let time pass and see how I feel about the groups later. And when the final book came out, I felt like I was able to judge those groups from a cleaner point of view, and even felt like I can really enjoy them. For example, once I got that SWORN basically rose to fill the void left by the Keepers of the Weave and similar compacts, it made them much more grounded in the setting and me interested in the compact as a whole, and when I read the final writeup I really appreciated the tweaks they added to it- which may have been small, but really brought new light to the group, imo, and made them far more interesting and enjoyable.

    I still didn't like the Council, however.

    Here is the thing- from the first moment, the thing that annoyed me the most about the Council of Bones is that I should like them. I really like the medium shtick, and I'm a sucker for spiritualism based characters. Mediums are cool, and dancing on the liminal boundary between the living and the dead while using séance tools, Ouija boards and tarot cards just sounds awesome. I really like the Cappadocians from V:tM, and scholars of death is a very cool concept which I enjoy playing and writing about. So all that considered, I should find the Council of Bones cool and enjoyable. I should like them, and at some moments, I really thought that I did like them- but once I reread their writeup in the final book, I once again felt a strong sense of dislike. Which means that yes, I like their concept, and now, it is not about them taking the spot of one of my favorite conspiracies- because again, conceptually I do like the image I have for them in my head, but I just don't like them in the way they were written. That really annoyed me- not the writeup, but the fact that I don't know why I don't like it. It felt like I'm missing something, as there is something about that conspiracy that I can't put my finger on which makes me call the Council "bastards". And after giving it much thought, I came to an interesting conclusion.

    I thought I like the Council of Bones because I like mediums. However, the Council of Bones are not mediums. Any what do I mean by that?

    Here is the thing- aesthetically, the Council are mediums. They use spiritualism techniques. They speak with and for the dead. They commune with them to maintain the Vigil and protect innocent lives. That's all on the surface level- but when you examine their themes and how they actually uphold the Vigil, you quickly discover that there is a huge dissonance between the conspiracy as perceived aesthetically and what they actually are. There is a reason they are not related to the age of spiritualism, and instead for instances of massive deaths. While they may look like mediums, sound like medium and speak like mediums- they are not mediums, and what do I mean about that?

    Let's look on the three main issues which I identified on that subject.

    1) Use your abilities only for the Vigil: Now, that may look as a simple on, almost a trivial one. Good hunters shouldn't use their abilities for personal gain, right? The conspiracy doesn't give you superspeed just so you could win a marathon, right? However, when we think about it carefully, we quickly can see something interesting- that no group actually uphold such a rule. They just assume that their hunters will not abuse their abilities, which are, after all, very geared towards the Vigil anyway. The conspiracy relays on the loyalty and devotion of its own members and deals with issues when and if they arise, no need to outright spell that rule. And some conspiracies probably don't even care about it- Cheiron, for example, wouldn't give a damn about how you are using your witch eyes as long as you keep bringing profit to the company, for example. Conspiracies care about the job being done and trust both their ability to regulate their hunters and the personal responsibility of their members. However, The Council, however, states it as a rule- and for a good reason. Unlike most of the Endowments granted by conspiracies, Perispiritism can easily be abused by its users because mediums do not need to act in secrecy. A lot of people believe in mediums and go to consult them, and having actual spiritual abilities means that those hunters can operate in plain sight, something that no other conspiracy can (beside, maybe, the MM, but they are devoted to God and all of that, and there is something fishy about their Endowments). That means, however, that being able to use your abilities as mediums should be an advantage, providing money, resources, influence and reach. The Council could easily become a powerful organization, even more than it is already- but it chooses not to. They give up on their leverage in the name of a vague reason like the "Vigil". More than that, we should remember that for the Council, the Vigil is not about helping the dead commune with the living- their Vigil is about hunting down necromancers, the undead and those who abuse the dead, as well as murderers and monsters which bring suffering for ghosts. So the most basic thing that a medium should do- commune with the living in the name of departed loved ones, helping them to deal with grief and eventually move on- that is not part of the Vigil. Basically, this rule could mean, at least for the most devoted/zealot members, that the Council members are not allowed to be mediums, which should let you know immediately that there is something fishy about this organization. Also- note that they hunt those people and monsters which can manipulate or deal with the dead- sounds like trying to get a monopoly, isn't it?

    2) Death for those who use their abilities in the open: Now, while you may argue about the less part in case of the previous issue, this issue directly states that no, you are not allowed to be a medium. I mean, the whole part about mediumship is for those with which the medium commune for to actually believe they are speaking with the dead- and by being unable to use their Endowments before others it means that the Council members can't do such acts in favor of the living. In here, the conspiracy outright states that they care more about the dead than about the living, and that they are willing to kill their own members just to guarantee it. Sure, the text excuse it by them fearing to be viewed as "monsters/manipulated by monsters" by other hunters- but we are talking about 3rd Tier hunters. Of course other hunters may view you as a monster. That also pretty much kills the possibility for Council members working with other conspiracies- just imagine them saying to their cellmate "wait a second- i need you all to leave the room and give mem five minutes. No questions asked". That's crazy! Not to mention that killing your own valuable hunters just because they use their powers before others is crazy- unless you have something to hide, or think you have one. No other conspiracy kills their own members for such a reason, and almost all of them do their best to maintain their members, preferring to keep wild cards close than outright getting rid of a useful assets. From here we can get that the Council is very, very paranoid, very secretive and views their abilities as special- something we'll talk about in the next issue. Of course, we should also consider that as the Council deals with the dead on the daily basis, they may not view death as too much of a punishment as the hunter could still assist the conspiracy as a ghost- which only further enhance that the worldview for the Council is very, very messed up, being unable to understand the importance of life- or perhaps having a very unhealthy obsession with death.

    3) All other magic is tainted and corrupted: think about it for a second- just thing about it. It may not look like much of an issue, but when you consider is thoroughly, you can see that this point is as much of an issue with the Council being mediums as the previous two. Why? Because it means that the Council is afraid of the dark. Mediums generally do not view the supernatural as a scary thing- they seek to commune with it, understand it. They cloak themselves with the supernatural as they delve into its secrets, trying to figure the darkness as they are granted with eyes which can see through it. For mediums, the supernatural should be a part of the natural, and both should coexist one next to the other, and fear is only a matter of misunderstanding. And here, you have the Council of Bones, which believes that only THEIR abilities are pure and sacred and only THEIR powers are clean of taint. THEY are the chosen ones, THEY are the pure, and everything and everyone else is tainted and scary and should be avoided or removed. It is not just about monsters- the text explicitly mentions that they view the supernatural Endowments of other hunters as corrupted as well. There is a sense of arrogance in here, a sense of pride of being Chosen by Death- and with that supremacy, also fear, fear that everything else is corrupted, everything else needs to be cleansed, all other supernatural abilities which are not granted by the Mark of the Scythe are corrupted and vile and bring only evil to the world. While they are very much scholarly, the secrets they learn is not in order to understand or explain or accept the supernatural, it is because they fear it, and one should learn their own fears in order to fight them. THEY are special, and everyone else ARE the enemy.

    So yeah, what we get that while aesthetically the Council are mediums, the text tells us again and again that they are not, and even calls them arrogant and hypocrites in the ST section. Below their spiritualism surface, we gain a much darker and strange organization- one which constantly dies and is reborn as relation to death, one which exist for the dead and by the dead, one which views death as trivial and feel more comfortable among ghosts than among the living, one which does not seek to serve as a bridge between life and death, but one which seeks to keep their mysteries for themselves, valuing knowledge yet not sharing it and which constantly believe that only they are allowed to speak for the dead and only according to the terms of the conspiracy. Zeal, fear, devotion, supremacy- those are not the features of a medium. Those are the features of a priest- or an exorcist, but they are also not exorcists as they care about the dead and not the living, seeking to serve their interests in the name of the Vigil while attacking the fleshy beings which try to manipulate them. No, the Council members are not mediums, not exorcists.

    They are a death cult.

    The Elysian Mysteries. The Dionysian ones. Orphism. The Council of Bones shares much more with those strange mystery cults than it shares with modern spiritualism. They with to live alongside the dead, understanding death and explore the meaning of dying. They converse with ghosts and devote themselves for their mysteries, while keeping those two parts of their lives separated. They see death as not being a big deal, because they seek to serve Death itself- and they are chosen by it. They are CHOSEN. Special. Pure and unique. They are the true servants of Death, and those who defy Death's will should suffer for their crimes. All other hunters- they deal with corrupted abilities, because they have not been Marked. They have not been purified. Death is a matter of purification, not corruption, and the ghosts are the messengers of Death which speak truth which only the Council is worthy to hear. The Council is special, and is practicing the Vigil because it is the will of Death. They don't seek to connect the living and the dead nor do they actually work to keep them separated (as the ghosts are not the enemies of the conspiracy). They simply embrace Death, seeking purification and understanding of the mysteries through it. The Council of Bones is a zealot, devoted and paranoid death cult, which forever views itself as unique and special, being unable to die no matter how many times their enemies shatter them. When Death calls, they rise, and the dead will keep them as a anchor from falling into the depths of the Underworld.

    Now, does that understanding makes me like them? I'm not sure. The dissonance between the aesthetics and the truth below it still bothers me- but it is an intentional dissonance. Now, at the very least, I can understand the Council- which is much more than I had before thinking about it. I think that once I'll manage to replace the mental image I got from my expectations for them with what they actually are I may at least stop disliking them, but I am not sure if I would actively like them. Still, it is a progress for me, personally, and at the very least it would give me some time to reimagine the Council in the way they are intended to be, instead of the false impression that I had for them.

    And that's it! Those are my thoughts about the Council of Bones. Feel free to say if you agree or disagree with my analysis, and if you would like me to make similar posts for other organizations.

                                                  So In order to make sure that I give them a fair judgement, I've decided to give a quick reread to the material we have on the Council and check the fiction from Geist's Anthologies (which is not a lot but that's what we have), and it seems that one of the things which I apparently made up is that the text stated them hunting for necromancers- even though it does hint about it, as it states that the conspiracy forbids using the dead for personal gain and their section in the ST chapter. In fact, the later section really feels like a teaser for the Geist fiction, as it mentions them not always knowing with what they are dealing with (a.k.a Reapers), and both the OOC sources (fiction and ST) seem to delve a lot about how arrogant they are. They representative in the fiction, Professor Kelly, while not wanting to die, still gives a strong feeling that he (and the rest of the organization) don't really get why death is such a big deal, which support my view that the Council has some very unhealthy obsession with death and a twisted worldview which doesn't really give life the respect it deserves.

                                                  Another thing worth to mention is that, according to their own mythology, when Death gave the first hunter the Mark, it was done in favor of the dead- and that reinforce that the Council uphold the Vigil for the dead, not the living. While them hunting only those monsters which kill would make them look nice, they don't seem to do so because they want to protect innocent lives. They do so because monsters killing people generally produce ghosts, and those ghosts ask for vengeance, and the Council exists in order to act according to that wrath. It could be that their goal is to minimize the creation of ghosts (which would make sense), but it also means that their service as mediums is only in order to allow ghosts who died violently by the darkness to avenge their death and move on (or maybe they'll stick around to help the conspiracy. Or maybe they'll fall to the Underworld. So many options to doom a ghost).

                                                  It is also worth to be mentioned that as in the fiction the Council do not understand what a Reaper is, we can safely assume that they don't know a lot about the Underworld, and are very Twilight focused. From their point of view, it seems, Reapers are indeed targets for the hunt, being undead monsters- but we should also mention that Kelly does refer to Bound as "zombies", and as zombies show up in their Enemy section it is probable that they do hunt Bound (or at least, some of them), thinking of them as undead monsters. So overall, their goals is twofold- hunt those who create ghosts, and hunt the dead which are a danger to the world- and potentially necromancers, but that is more implicit than stated. They are also scholarly, old fashioned and seek after knowledge about the dead and death, which again fits the death cult themes.

                                                  So taking it into account, when treated as a death cult, we can gain a better understanding of their belief and way of life- the Council of Bones is a liminal organization, standing in the boundary between life and death. They are not here to help ghosts move on, and they do not seek to help the living move on with their grief after losing their loved ones. They are a one sided channel which exists to serve Death by upholding a Vigil for the dead, which is a unique concept I must admit. Monsters, in their view, is easily anyone who murderers and leaves ghosts behind, but where ghosts are all pathos and emotion and rage the Council are cold and rational and distant. They don't avenge the dead as burning angels of wrath, the simply act according is a very calm way, simply "balancing the scales" and paying Death his due. Their methodological actions seem to be a product of their view that they are the Chosen of Death, and as such they hunt because of duty to Death himself, not the dead (which is why some of them do hunt ghosts in general, and most will hunt ghosts who go out of control- or fuck up be dealing with Reapers). Again, they are very Twilight focused, which makes sense considering their theme of liminality and cycles. If I were to think about what they actually believe in, is that Death as entity is a judge, and they are his executioners, who follow the verdict with a calm righteousness and believing that their actions are just because they are Death's Chosen. So yeah, arrogance is indeed found deep within the organization.

                                                  That gives me a better mental image for the organization, which honestly makes me dislike them less- still would have preferred the writeup would lend itself more towards a mixed conspiracy game, but I work with what I have (like, yeah, I can change it for my games, but when you do you need to make sure you remember that it is a personal change so you won't end up into silly internet arguments where what you think the splat is about clashes with what they actual are)
