    Convention: Knights Errants, the Anchorism

    08-17-2014, 09:09 AM
    They call them Knights Errants, Bogatyr, and less flatteringly, Voluntourists.

    In every society there’s a place for them, for the scions of the rich and powerful to prove their worth, win glory, and perhaps swing a sharpened length of metal at somebody.

    In modern nights the concept of nobility and hereditary prestige isn’t what it used to be, so the rich and powerful, and these days - the middle classes, work even harder to give their family every possible advantage to succeed in life. Hereditary prestige isn’t what it used to be, but personal glory is worth even more. The right charity in a poorer country and daddy’s money can be a ticket to respect, self-development, and the university of your choice.

    The knights errant fall somewhere between a subculture and a stereotype. They’re not just voluntourists but voluntourists who talk about what they do in terms of chivalry, honour and obligation - at least when in front of the cameras. The whole concept is considered to be faintly ridiculous by many, harmless teenage posturing by some, and offensive to the people they’re supposedly helping by others.

    Despite all this, many governments and charities go out of their way to attract Knight Errants due to the wildly improbable successes they tend to achieve, and just as many discourage them for the bizarre chaos that often follows in their wake; for the Knights Errant are beloved by the Wyrd.

    Picture: A woman wearing jeans, a t-shirt, and a baseball cap with a small stars and stripes emblem sits at a cafe table. Before her is a plate of turkish food and a book marked “Red Crescent”. She’s reading a book labeled “tourist’s guide” and has a large backpack on the floor by her feet. A sword that looks ornate enough to have prophecies written about it rests against the backpack.

    The Enemy

    The Knights Errant aren’t an institution and cannot have institutional enemies. The closest thing they have would be poverty, disease and natural disasters. A Knight will travel across the world to volunteer.

    And yet fate doesn’t let it be that simple. Feudal vampire lords, plague spreading mad scientists, faerie dragons, faerie witches, actually most kinds of faeries. Uncomfortably often a Knight Errant finds himself fighting monsters with a sword he’s never swung before. Surprisingly, they often win.

    There’s a reason most knights never go back for a second trip, it’s the same reason some never stop.

    Hunters

    You were the school’s star baseball player and your life was set. One year volunteering in The Gambia then collage on a full sports scholarship. It was only later that you realised your school didn’t have a commemorative sword for athletic achievement to award you, after you saw a village chieftain's daughter being carried away by a faerie hag. Three years later, you’re still in pursuit and honestly, you prefer adventure to normal life.

    You spent most of your life living on a slowly diminishing trust fund and medieval fantasy. One day you snapped, sold up as much as you needed to buy a sword, armour and a horse, and went riding in no direction in particular. You can’t tell the difference between a gangbanger and a goblin, even when fate isn’t intervening, which is why the police are after you.

    Attitudes

    Most Knights Errants are Tourists.They don’t consider themselves to be knights, they just loosely drifted nearby. They bunked with another knight and got given a sword as a parting gift or they sent in an essay about chivalry to get funding. They’re ordinary people who travel, volunteer, and don’t even realise that the Wyrd has it’s eye upon them.
    Free Specialty: Socialise (Backpackers)

    In contrast you have the Lifers. They identify as knights, and try to get themselves a title. Many are amature swordsmen and equestrians; they also tend to have self-made heraldry and might go so far to speak an in annoying faux-medieval syntax. Despite this air of the ridiculous Lifers are almost unanimously sincere in their desire to help others and to uphold the ideals of chivalry.
    Free Specialty: Academics (Chivalry)

    Finally the Questors actually know that the Wyrd favours the Knights Errant, and they like it. Questors intend to ride the Wyrd’s favour to the adventure of a lifetime, and perhaps a sip from the holy grail.
    Free Specialty: Occult (Quests)

    Stereotypes

    The Wylde Society: That's the second artist who painted me as a knight, but both of them painted the exact same armour.

    The Office of the Lord Stewards: The Telegraph wanted to know why I'm volunteering abroad when we have problems at home. I told them you need a special licence for "knight themed charity work" who makes a law about thar?!

    Yuri's Group: I volunteered at a psychiatric hospital but I just ended up as the errand boy, every day it was fetching something else from the pharmacy and you would not believe how much trouble I had walking a few blocks.
    There were sword fights!

    Aegis Kai Doru: Do you have any idea who keeps stealing my swords? Never mind, I'm going to Paris soon. There's always something lying around in France.

    Status
    The Knights Errant are not an organisation, and they do not have formal Status. But they do have something similar. Knights who uphold chivalric ideals and successfully complete quests are instinctively trusted by their fellow Knights and receive blessings from the Wyrd. Being a successful charitable volunteer doesn’t matter, the Wyrd only cares about chivalry and questing.

    O: Wyrd Image (Questing Knight)

    OOO: Two dots in Striking Looks

    OOOOO: Three dots in Fame


    Endowment: Questing (O - OOOOO)

    The Knights Errant never realise they have an Endowment, but it's a powerful one. The Wyrd draws thick around the Knights, thick enough to turn teenagers into heroic knights.

    Knights errant may use Talecrafting, even if they don't realise it. Their dicepool is Questing alone and they may only Talecraft with the tropes of chivalric romance.

    Drawback: It is Talecrafting, which means cruel twists of fate abound. However the Knights Errant have no risk of addiction, humans are more grounded than faeries.

    Bonus Material: The Holy Grail
    A Knight Errant who spends a long career successfully helping others, upholding their knightly code and possesses the Virtuous Merit may find themselves on a grail quest.

    Each grail quest follows a three act structure. The first act is The Call: During The Call the Knight Errant will find the Wyrd’s hand upon her life becomes more extreme. Over a steady progression the world around her transforms itself, bit by bit, until it resembles something straight out of Chivalric Romance.

    By the end of The Call a knight may find himself travelling to help with an earthquake, only to arrive and find the Red Cross has been forced by circumstance to set up it’s headquarters in a medieval themed park, and has been handing out costumes to everyone whose clothing was lost; creating a bubble of medieval life that’s actually a lot more convincing than it should be.

    When The Call reaches its apex the Knight is informed that only the Holy Grail can solve the problem and sets out on a quest to retrieve it. The Wyrd leads them to the first guardian, who represents mundanity and self-doubt. The first guardian is often family, friend or a respected authority figure, and her job is to convince the knight that this entire situation is ridiculous. There is no grail and they hasn’t been proper knights since the medieval ages.

    The choice presented is real. The Knight can turn back, sacrificing all dots of Status (Knights Errant) and Questing to try and solve a mundane problem by mundane means, or they can press forward and enter the Hedge.

    The second stage of a Grail Quest is the Journey. They must travel through the Hedge to find the Grail, braving all the dangers of the Hedge on the way. Knights Errant have no more ability to navigate the Hedge than anyone else, but the Wyrd provides signs and guides. At the end of the Journey lies the second guardian, a powerful figure who must be bested with martial powers. Be it a dragon that must be slain or a fellow knight who must be defeated in a contest of skill.

    The third stage of a Grail Quest is the Challenge, a test of the knights honour and morality admitted by the third guardian. If he passes he will be permitted to take a sip from the grail.

    Grail Knights gain the following benefits:

    They replace their Integrity track with Morality.
    They may use Morality to resist supernatural effects, as a monster would with Potency.
    When acting to help others they may replace one component of the dicepool with Morality. For example, when racing on horseback to deliver medicine they may roll the highest of: Dexterity + Animal Ken, Morality + Animal Ken, Dexterity + Morality.
