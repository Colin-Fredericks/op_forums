      Take a Like just for having read Dream Catchers

      Sadly, there's not much more information about them. Glamourweaver came up with the idea but never wrote them, I put them in Dream Catchers because:
      1) I like putting in little references like that. (There's two more non-Dream Catchers Fae-Conspiracies hidden in the PDF; one is REALLY subtle)
      2) I felt that they'd make a good example, they know about Hedge navigation while being closer to a typical Hunter than the Wilde Society, and don't have a Hollow like the Sons that they could use to avoid the danger.


      Their proper name is The Staff of Janus, they're also known as The Gatekeepers.

      I think Glamourweaver described them as The Freemasons, if they were run by neo-pagans. A mixture of librarians, gardeners, academics and people with lots of money, they have power over time and doorways which they use to try and prevent travel between the worlds.

                  I'm not sure what to do about the similarity to the Knights of Saint George; perhaps their "cover" identity could be changed slightly? As I understand, the Masons evolved from actual trade guilds of masons (stonecutters/builders). You could put the Staff of Janus a little further back in this process, so that new members join what they think is an antiquarian gardening club/landscapers association (which would explain the number of gardeners and scholars in their ranks) before learning the truth.

                  Anyway, here is possible set of factions:
                  The Grain (like the grain of wood, not wheat): Their primary concern is simply closing doors that should never have been opened, followed by getting rid of the "intruders" who have already entered the world. Many of them believe that most monsters come from outside the world and that shutting all the gates into reality is the only way to stop the depredations of these creatures, whether by preventing further incursions or actual destroying the monsters by cutting them off from their home dimensions.
                  The Arch: This faction believes some doorways serve a function as part of the "architecture" of the universe. By closing one door you may make it easier to open another by weakening the walls of reality nearby. On the other hand, an artificial gate may also cause structural weakness. This group decides very carefully whether to close gates, safeguard them, or even open new ones in the belief that mishandling could eventually cause the whole edifice to collapse.
                  The Keys: This group, probably the smallest, believes that doorways exist to be used and merely attempt to decide who is worthy to use them. They also try to return things and people to their "proper" places, which means that it has the most infighting of the factions. Issues like whether changelings belong in Arcadia (being fae) or the mundane world (being originally human), or whether vampires should be sent to the Underworld with extreme prejudice are constant issues of debate.

                                                      I felt like writing, so here's the first half of their history. They'll need a second one describing how they came to be a freemason like society.

                                                      =================================

                                                      Magic is found at the thresholds. It arises at dawn and dusk, it is found at the border between civilisation and barbarism, the one who is neither male nor female but something in-between wields it.

                                                      Magic has all the danger of the threshold. It is familiar enough to tempt, but foreign enough to betray us. It is the time our world changes, and it is the door through which strangers enter for both good and for ill.

                                                      But doors have gates, and gates have gatekeepers.

                                                      The Gatekeepers began as a Roman mystery cult, merchants who travelled beyond the Empire and adopted the habits and ways of barbarians in the name of profit began to speak of wisdom that could only be seen by their unique point of view. They made their sacrifices to Janus, the god of transitions, gates, passages and time so that they might receive his blessing every time they walked through the gates. In time they began to incorporate rituals and beliefs from the foreigners they traded with, intentionally solidifying their position between Rome and the barbarians.

                                                      The original Staff of Janus lasted over a century without ever accepting the supernatural, that all came later. After Christianity came to Rome and the old mystery cults were outlawed. Most of the Staff willingly renounced their membership, their religion was a trader's network that had suddenly become unprofitable. What remained was an echo chamber of the most devout believers,

                                                      Now they could have fled beyond the Empire, they were rich merchants with the money to make a good life for themselves anywhere they pleased, but their theology was centred around being half way between Rome and foreigners. Unwilling to leave they instead began a ritual designed to split themselves into two people, one Roman, one foreign. With their animus stretched across continents they could blend into Christian Rome without abandoning their faith.

                                                      It didn't work, neither did their next attempt, but they kept at it. Raiding their collection of myths and rituals, and their wealth, they tried ritual after ritual until one of them worked. Just not how they hoped it would happen. Half of the high priest was thrown outside of the world entirely, something else entered the world.

                                                      Whatever else happened to the priest, one thing was clear. He now understood magic, he understood the doorways between the worlds that Janus kept, and he understood just how foolish he had been. Gathering his remaining cult he fled north, remaining ahead of Roman conquests teaching them of what the magic he learned, and the true way to honour Janus: By guarding doors like the one they had so inadvertently opened.

                                                          Since I can't think of a good second part to the history I'll do this next.

                                                          The Enemy

                                                          The Gatekeepers make one thing clear to every recruit. They are not heroes, they do not hunt monsters or rescue the innocent. They are gatekeepers, they guard the doors and peruse any illicit visitors. That's all they do.

                                                          Central to the Gatekeeper's ideology is the idea of liminality. The Gatekeeper's are human, but they are humans who have adopted ideas, rituals and practices from beings who dwell beyond the world. They stand on the border between humanity and the other. This unique perspective gives the Staff, and the Staff alone, the wisdom to govern the gates and with it, the right to own and profit from the gates.

                                                          Even if the Staff of Janus isn't as wise as they like to believe, they are not fools. They know that the creatures who use the gates are liminial beings in themselves. Changelings were once human before they were twisted, and still partly are. Werewolves can become a man or a wolf as they will. The Bound have passed through the gates and returned with something from the other side.

                                                          So how do the Gatekeepers justify their unique rights and privileges when others are clearly more liminal? The answer is the kind of self centred thinking common to most human organisations. We are humans, and this is humanities' world. No matter how liminal a supernatural being is, it belongs in it's own world. Providing the monsters understand that then the Gatekeepers will have no problem with them, and will even be willing to form alliances, there is nothing untoward about a border having a guard on both ends. The wealth and influence the Staff can call upon to offer sweeteners has led to some stable and successful alliances.

                                                          Of all the monsters it is the fae with whom the Gatekeepers have the longest history. Other monsters may travel between different realms, but none create new doors with the casual ease that faeries do. The Staff of Janus fears that if left unchecked the Fae could tear down the walls and let their world flood into ours, sowing madness and mayhem in it's wake. They wield both diplomacy and guns in their attempt to stem the fae's travel, but their belief that even Changelings should not be permitted to live upon Earth has made them few friends.

                                                          After the fae The Staff of Janus concerns itself with Werewolves and the Bound. Werewolves have a lot with in common with the staff, both believe that the borders between this world and others must be maintained and both believe they uniquely have the duty and right to protect and own the doors, so naturally they hate each other. The Bound are a diverse lot, and many could agree with the Staff's mission but very few will agree that they are supposed to live in the Underworld. Nobody wants to live in the Underworld. Still The Bound rarely create new doors so the Staff takes a less hard-line view, there is some room for compromise and The Bound are so very bribeable.


                                                          Structures

                                                          The majority of Gatekeepers form The Grain. With a gun they guard the doors and peruse intruders into the world. With a watering can and sheers they shape the landscape, drawing on the protective power of plants to lock and seal the gates into the world.
                                                          Free Speciality: Crafts (Gardening) or Occult (Plants)

                                                          When the time is right stewardship of a door passes from The Grain to The Key. For a limited time the gate is openend and, for the right sort, passage is permitted. The Key are diplomats who welcome guests and merchants who profit from trade, but they are also guards who keep out the wrong sort.
                                                          Free Speciality: Empathy (Liminality)

                                                          Supporting both The Grain and The Key is The Arch. Formed of investigators and scholars The Arch seeks out undiscovered gates and studies known doors. They assess the nature and danger of each, determining how much effort should be dedicated to keeping the door shut, and under what circumstances travel should be permitted.
                                                          Free Speciality: Investigation (Gates) or Occult (Gates)
