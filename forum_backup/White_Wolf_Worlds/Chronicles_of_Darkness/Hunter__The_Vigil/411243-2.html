    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
    Or maybe instead of Amateur, Professional, and Empowered, the terms should be Gritty, Cinematic, and Empowered. The point is, numeric terms aren’t descriptive and facilitate ambiguity; named Tiers can be much more clear about what they’re trying to describe.
    Trail of Cthulhu divides the game between Purist play (the supernatural will either kill you or drive you insane) and Pulp play (you can kill the supernatural with Tommy guns and dynamite). Hunter would benefit from a similar decision; are the player characters struggling against an overwhelming threat, or are they kicking in the door of the vampire's haven and burning it to death with homemade flamethrowers?

                I'm not sure that three-way split works. I could see a ragtag bunch of normal people still having storylines involving supernatural empowerment; given the various side-effects and consequences of supernatural powers, or how low-key they might be, I don't necessarily think "has powers/endowments/supernatural merits" is on the same axis as the level of institutional or organizational support they might have. The normal person with psychic visions compelling them to investigate the supernatural is a Tier 1 concept despite some degree of empowerment.

                There are really two tiers - one where the resources available to the group are literally the combined resources available to each individual hunter (as in, "just the cell") and one where there's organizational support and resources available to the group. Compact to Conspiracy is just a matter of degree.

                So I'd abandon Compacts/Tier 2 as a distinct tier, and just go with Tier 1 and Tier 3 (though you wouldn't still use those terms). The availability of Endowments and the like would be a "bolt-on" to either tier. Make room for Tier 1 hunters who have come across a Relic or a Tier 3 Conspiracy that lacks an Endowment.

                I do like the Regional/Global split for Conspiracies, but I think that could be reflected by a build-your-own-conspiracy system that measures "reach." And I wouldn't say either level mitigates or has less of a chance to act as a horror setting. Hunter works as an ongoing game, which does lend itself to allowing a certain degree of survival and success by the players, since otherwise they're dead at the end of the story, and it works this way at any tier. On that note, perhaps deeper systems or guidance for all the many things you can lose other than your life. The Vigil demands sacrifice, after all...

                        I notice that you keep talking about “tier” in terms of “how extensive are the resources the troupe has to draw upon?” That does seem to be the notion that 1e was using when it conceived of the Cell/Compact/Conspiracy split; but that’s not really the notion that GMC is going for with its Local/Regional/Global/Cosmic split: there, the focus is less on the protagonists’ resources and more on the scope of the chronicle’s consequences: to give an extreme example, you can easily have a lone(ly) troupe in a chronicle where the fate of the world hangs in the balance — that is, a Cell of hunters at the heart of a Cosmic chronicle.

                        Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                        I don't like the idea of a pure/pulp split. To me one of the cool things is that both exist side by side in the same setting. A group of factory workers struggle alone to keep a vampire from taking over their factory and squeezing the workers dry; but that same vampire fears the government blowing up his haven with attack helicopters.
                        That’s got nothing to do with “pure/pulp”; that’s a matter of “cell/compact” — as with Professor Phobos, you’re talking in terms of the troupe’s “reach”; and mixing different groups with different “reaches” in a single chronicle is entirely reasonable. I’m thinking of “gritty/cinematic” being on a separate axis from that; it has to do with whether the protagonists themselves are to be portrayed as ordinary people or as extraordinary people. I put “empowered” at the top of that axis, not because ordinary and exraordinary people can never have supernatural abilities (they can), but because “you are borderline supernatural” is the logical conclusion of that progression.

Tier 1 you're alone against the darkness. Tier 2 you have allies, resources and lore. A fighting chance against a real threat but no more than that. Tier 3, monsters fear you.
                        The folks at Onyx Path don’t see things that way, which is why they redefined Tiers in GMC. But the original concept that you outline here still works as the basis for the distinction between Cells, Compacts, and Conspiracies; and to that extent, I agree that it should be kept. But just as “do you have supernatural powers?” ought to be decoupled at least partially from “how much of a support network and resources do you have?”, the latter ought to be at least partially decoupled from “what is the chronicle’s scope?” — which is the essence of GMC-style Tiers.

                                    I very much like the local/regional/global/cosmic spread. I don't necessarily think we need an "Empowered/Not Empowered" bolt-on sort of modifier, because individual hunters in a group may or may not avail themselves of the various supernatural merits; they're already purchased from the same pool of XP and moderated by ST/group consensus anyway. A local group of Hunters, one of whom is a telekinetic, is neither in the "Empowered" category (because, say, the other four hunters don't have any) and not really in the "Depowered" category either.

                                    Some conspiracies just teach certain kinds of supernatural merits. I would favor allowing the conspiracy system the flexibility to have, say, a local group that has an Endowment (the genius loci of the creepy small town Whisper Lake guides its defenders to defend the residents; the Archangel Lesser Magellanic Cloud condensed into mortal form over this stretch of African Savannah and if you have lived off the soil, animals and water of the area for a year and a day some part of you is becomes part of the Archangel...) and a global (i.e., "Tier 3 Conspiracy") group that doesn't have one (NATO's Office of Special Contingencies is an international security service whose mission is to keep NATO forces and command structure free of supernatural influence; the Chasseurs sans Frontieres track down monsters and coordinate hunts across borders).

                                        Just to throw it in, but Trails of Cthulhu's Bookhounds of London had something a bit like this -- in addition to Pulp/Purist approaches, they also had three tones available for the game (Arabesque, which was sort of faerie tale, Technicolor, which was Hammer Horror-ish, and Sordid, which was basically about how scummy things could get) and discussed a bit how to tweak the game to support any variety. What was interesting was that they did this without any real mechanical rules or what-not. They just had a few pages discussing how the setting would look with each tone, about half a page of plothooks, and the dozen or so NPCs were presented with brief variations on the theme. So the sleepless book hunter was a heroin addict in Sordid and was cursed in Technicolor.

                                        So discussions and allowances of different tones doesn't have to be terribly complex or require enormous page counts.

I definitely want to see deeper and more nuanced takes on hunters who want to become supernatural.

                                                I think all of that would come together really well with a smattering of different example cities with very different vigils going on.

                                                    If there's something I want to see, it's honestly more to do with the paranoia of the different groups.

                                                    I like the idea of keeping 1st tier "bunch of guys got their shotguns and started shooting at the things they heard go bump in the night" being about ignorance and paranoia of the supernatural, but making the 3rd tier heavy hitters have a different theme.

                                                    Think of it like the difference between Alien and Aliens. In Alien, there's only one monster, but the nearly-helpless cast can't do much to stop even that, and it makes the single xenomorph build a creeping, paranoid tension. In Aliens, you have marines with miniguns mowing down dozens of the beasts like it's nothing, and it has this huge adrenal rush sense of an action hero movie more than a horror film. In fact, I watched Aliens with my parents repeatedly, along with Top Gun (fast-forwarding the boring talking scenes to get back to the cool jet planes), when I was just 4 or so years old because it was about tough marines blasting the acid out of a whole bunch of bad guys, WHOOO! The idea of it being scary just didn't even enter my mind.

                                                    If you're playing Ride of the Valkyries while your Task Force: VALKYRIE platoon drops in on a werewolf compound with Blackhawks and Apache helicopters firing silver-shrapnel launching TOW missiles and dual-wielding M-16s while shirtless (gotta show off that six-pack!) and wearing an American Flag bandana... you're not fooling anyone calling it a "horror game" anymore.

                                                    Rather, I'm actually against the notion of having completely segregated "compromised" and "uncompromised" conspiracies, the way Kings Raven suggested. I think it makes for the game balance to have each different conspiracy have its own little paranoia in its own little weird way. Rather than the whole of VALKYRIE being a vampire front, make a branch of it compromised, and have the players have to navigate the bureaucracy to isolate the infected cells... and find a way to get into a position where they can actually accuse or eliminate a superior officer without being branded traitor, or tipping the target off so you get liquidated, yourselves, first.

                                                    In fact, I think it's kind of the point of Hunter that the conspiracies are at least as morally murky as the monsters they are supposedly hunting. As written, the only group that's an unambiguous team of good-guys is VASCU. (And they're mostly there as the heroic antagonists of a game where you purposefully play unabashed pure evil characters, not primarily written to be played.) Having the option of playing "everything we do is perfectly morally justifiable!" Ashwood Abbey kind of misses the whole point of Ashkwood Abbey. There's a Hunter conspiracy based purely around being a group of cannibals trying to steal enlightenment from eating other people's brains!

                                                    The conflicting goals of the conspiracies make them interesting. Surely, a fight against the "monsters" of Cheiron can be as interesting as a fight against a vampire. Just because they're all humans doesn't mean they share goals, and when goals conflict between armed, paranoid people to whom violence is a way of life, then violence will prevail. If you're playing the Stonemasons, you have to watch out for Illuminati spies as well as the Witches.

                                                    The smaller tiers have little equipment or training or knowledge, but they are at least "pure" in their cause, while choosing the conspiracies means getting power and knowledge of your supposed enemy at the cost of losing knowledge of the trustworthiness of your allies, and total control of your destiny. You can have your Ride of the Valkyries moment, but in between comes the uncertainty of whether your superior officer is sucking down leech juice, and maybe you should frag this guy... or is that just what the witches WANT you to think?!
