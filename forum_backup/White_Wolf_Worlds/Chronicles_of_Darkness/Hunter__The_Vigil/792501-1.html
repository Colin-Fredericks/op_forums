    Originally posted by Stupid Loserman View Post
    Null Mysteriis doesn't need a conceptual shift, but it needs an attentive, science minded eye to look over its writeups, one that knows that science is a method, not the body of knowledge accumulated by the method. They shouldn't read like they rush into some science-fiction hypothesis to shoehorn their findings into before they have some solid foundation to ground it on. They should remember that "I don't know" is not a dirty word in science, and what's important isn't getting from no answer to an answer, but carefully chiselling at the edges of the unknown to make sure, however slowly the answer might have to come, it's one that fits the facts. A little pseudoscience presence is fine, both in the form of phenomena that are pseudoscience in the real world but real phenomena in the Chronicles of Darkness, and in the form of investigators who are a little kooky and don't quite have their reasoning straight. But the pseudoscientists need to feel like authentic pseudoscientists with systems based around foundational assumptions, not movie extras spouting a little bit of technobabble. A little more attention to what they use their findings for practically wouldn't hurt either, and is probably a good bridge to forge connections between the compact and other hunter cells and organizations.
    My god agreed! Science is a method, and given that the Chronicles of Darkness exist in a world where things follow very strict rules (even if they aren't the real rules of physics) Null Mysteriis should be able to figure them out better than anyone else, rather than fall back on pulpy nonsense.


    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    ???

    I missed a lot more than one.

    I like the Ahl Al-Jabal, though in 2E I would like to see some acknowledgement that they and the Ascending Ones (Knife of Paradise) are both Hashshashin. That is, the Ahl Al-Jabal are based on the actual historical Hashshashin while the Ascending Ones are based upon the myths about the Hashshashin.
    I'd be okay with that if it were something like "the myth of the Hashashin was partly inspired when the Ascending Ones were erroneously attributed to Ismailis of the Nizari State." I do not want to see them directly be Ismaili on more than the individual level. Ismailis have been levelled with the charge of being the descendants of "The First Terrorists" and drug lords because people believe in the stories of the Hashashin were true. No serious historical scholar believes that anymore, I have recently seen the idea called laughable in books and academic papers, and it is highly offensive to Ismailis. The Ahl Al-Jabal are an excellent example of how to respectfully and appropriately draw on those ideas in a non-offensive way, especially since the (false) idea of Ismaili hasish smokers is never alluded to. The Ascending Ones are supposed to date back to Egypt and have nothing that is uniquely Ismaili about them. Linking them without some major rewriting of the AO would be a step backwards (IMO, feel free to disagree).

                My god agreed!                 I agree that as written the NM theories can come across as obviously wrong and kinda silly. However; the real world runs on rules and people have trying to work them out for... well, since the beginnings of humanity probably, and we still don't have all the answers. Even in the traditionally defined era of the scientific method, it's been extremely hard to make progress. Hundreds of thousands of people have sunk their entire lives into advancing that body of knowledge only very slightly (and that's if they're lucky). That a small group such NM have made little progress is actually pretty realistic. Science is really hard.

                Also, the NM are going from a background of our modern materialist body of scientific knowledge, and then they encounter stuff that is totally out of left-field. There is literally no reason to suspect that there's an animistic Shadow to the world, and yet there it is. How do you even begin to theorise about it? That they end up flailing around trying desperately to make some sense of it within the frameworks they have shouldn't really be a surprise.

                I think there's an interesting tragedy to the group that gets lost by simply making them heroic scientists.
