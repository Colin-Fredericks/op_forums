    Originally posted by Aermas View Post
    No. Because we dare to dream better. We deny the limitations set to us. We were born to walk the earth, yet we deny those limits & created wings to fly. Every human achievement has been in denial of limits. We are stubborn creatures against entropy & when the Earth burns we will deny our extinction & continue on.

    Most of myths & heroes & stories are about denial, about standing up against something, about being the underdog etc.

    Maybe "denial" isn't the right word for it. There is probably a more coherent word, but I can't think of it off the top of my head. Desperation is only one form of denial. Fighting to impose your own beliefs on the world whether that be your victory in a fight or your survival for food. It is because you deny what is happening to you with every fiber of your being. You say denial is about slipping away but you are only seeing it in its negative aspect. Denial pushes you & sets you free, whether that is good or bad. It is an arrogant emotion but it lets us harness lightning & even come back from death only because we will it.

    & That is why humans can risk Willpower. Every other creature is fighting something inherent.
    A vampire is fighting it's beast
    A werewolf is fighting it's dual nature
    A geist is fighting it's spirit
    A mage is fighting the lie

    A hunter, a human, is fighting the world, & as time has proven, the world loses.

    Defiance sounds like the word you're looking for. I think in this situation the difference between Defiance and Desperation is more academic than not. Defiance is born out of desperation, because if we weren't desperate, we wouldn't need to defy things in the first place, or whatever. It's really not that simple, but for the purposes of the discussion I don't really think what you're suggesting is really all that different from this idea of desperation.

                                            Originally posted by amechra View Post

                                            Why can't it be both? To quote misremembered poetry, Alone, godless, an injured cat cures itself with its tongue.
                                            In a sense, it already is — inasmuch as it already is a power source that lets you alter the universe in real life. That is to say, metaphorically so. It lets you alter the universe in that being audacious sets you free to achieve effects nobody anticipated you could achieve, not even yourself. That's the level on which audacity is a power source in Hunter: the Vigil, the level that risking Willpower represents. It's not a metaphysical force.

                                            As to why it can't also be a metaphysical force? Sure it can, it's fiction, and if you enjoy that at your table, go for it.

                                            I wouldn't expect anybody to consider it an inbuilt feature of Hunter: the Vigil, though, because making this literal statement about the nWoD universe in turn makes an artistic statement about the nWoD that isn't to the thrust of the gamelines' themes. It says that "rah rah, fight the power" is a tangible energy, that you can achieve anything if you put your mind to it, and that the game is about that to the degree that it has rules and in-setting energies to enforce it. I'm all about affording mortal hunters all kinds of impact and agency through their grit and organization and everything they've sacrificed, but the horror atmosphere of Hunter in specific and the nWoD in general rests on the cornerstone that nothing is certain. That means, among other things, that sometimes good people give it everything they've got without holding back and fail.

                                            Once you're making artistic statements that connote against this, you're shifting the genre.

                                                    Personally, I'm not a fan of the conspiracies. I find they tend to shift the tone of a game too far from the 'fighting the darkness alone or near-alone because it's the right thing to do'. I'm not going to say they're unworkable, because it depends on the group/game/etc, but if I were to use them, it'd be in the form of something like:

                                                    A member of the cell got ripped up pretty bad in that last run at the wolf-people on the hill, too bad for the vet to patch up. So they had to drop him at the ER and spin a story about how they found him torn up on the side of the road. The other members of the cell can't hang around too much or it'd draw suspicion (and more suspicion is the last thing they need after that house fire last month, what with their car being seen fleeing the scene), but they spend as much time as they can afford to keep an eye out.

                                                    One day, the ripped-up member, let's call him Jim, disappears. Talking to the nursing staff indicates that he was transferred to another hospital to get specialist attention; checking on the hospital reveals it's bogus. The cell scrambles, trying their best to track Jim down, even hacking the hospital security recordings and getting an ambulance with a fake license plate. Just as the cell is fearing the worst, Jim returns to the first hospital, seeming no worse for wear. He remembers nothing of his time away, but seems to have been well taken care of. However, a few weeks later, in the middle of a desperate fight with the wolf-people who put Jim in the hospital the first time, Jims arm suddenly grows to double its length, his fingers tipped with vicious claws that tear hunks of flesh out of the wolves hides. It reverts after the wolves retreat, but the cell is left asking... what the hell?!

                                                    Something like that.
