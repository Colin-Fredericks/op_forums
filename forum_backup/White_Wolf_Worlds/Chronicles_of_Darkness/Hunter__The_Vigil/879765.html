      It's a game about normal people dealing with the unnatural and the terrible cost of that. It's about not having any of the answers and trying to figure out what the hell is going on. While the monsters of the Chronicles of Darkness world are mostly free to rampage and screw with mortals all they like, you're the brave, stupid few who try and fight back. Whether you're alone and holding a cheap rifle from Wal-Mart or an initiated member of an occult conspiracy that stretches back thousands of years, you don't really know what the big picture looks like and the odds are severely stacked against you.

          I don't understand why Firefox keeps crashing when I'm halfway into writing a post on this forum. It seems to happen more frequently than any other site I visit.

          Vigil has two strengths. One: it strikes an excellent balance between versatility and customizability, and having definition to inspire ideas.

          Do you want to be four guys operating out of Steve's garage, planning out your opening to jump and sackbeat the sleazy guy one of you saw drinking a woman's blood one foggy night? That's in the corebook.
          Do you want to be the same four guys, but your buddies at work and your friends at the bowling alley lend you a sympathetic ear because they've seen some weird shit themselves and never had the boldness to speak up, so now they can keep an eye on the creepy blood guy for you when you're not looking, and slip you a pass through the employees only door when you need the evidence to prove your suspicions? That's in the corebook.
          Do you want to be the chosen few, burdened with a terrible destiny and fighting a perilous but successful battle to keep yourself on the side of the angels while you turn the literal fires of Hell upon the demons whose blood you share? That's in the corebook, including the literal Hellfire.
          Ghost hunters using Kirlian cameras and EVP analysis to uncover the science behind real hauntings? Corebook.
          Paramilitary special operations team armed with classified weapons technology to bust werewolves on the President's dime? Corebook.
          Grassroots congregations of the faithful who gather after prayer meetings to seek out monsters and save their souls for the Lord, or failing that, to cast them back to the Pit from whence they came? Corebook.
          Culture jammers hunting video footage of Bigfoot to grow their Youtube channel subscription base? Corebook.
          It scales up, it scales down.

          Two: Hunter books are a strong distillation of the Chronicles of Darkness at their best, in presenting a world filled with dangerous Fortean anomalies that can't all be easily categorized. They're not just about dudes banding together to exterminate the other big monster gamelines, although if you want to do that, it is absolutely something Hunter does without complaint. But you've also got alien architecture in the rifts between cities, totemic prophecies lurking behind the birth of the United States, living bronze ants from the time of the pharaohs who consume the human brain from within and operate the body like a puppet, and lurching men who survive impossible injuries not out of any sorcery or spiritual heritage, but simply out of their sheer, overwhelming urge to kill.


          Hunters live in a strange world, and they've often only got each other and a handful of lead to make sense of it. That's all right. Sometimes, that's enough.

              In a completely opposite direction from the previous posts, Hunter also makes a great tool for other games. They make for fantastic unexpected opponents in nearly any story, and scale very well with whatever scope your game is. Are your Vampires freshly Embraced and prone to making mistakes? You get idiots with videocameras trying to post them on Youtube and the angry friends of their last meal trying to put them down. Are your Vampires pushing a couple centuries of unlife? You get SWAT teams with vampire-detecting goggles and vengeful blood sorcerers coming for them the second they step out of line.

                  As mentioned previously, Hunter's core appeal is in its versatility. It isn't really one game so much as its a dozen games with enough shared elements that they can comfortably cohabitate in a single book. Miraculously.

                  Do you want to use it as a patch or an expansion to build off an existing mortals game? Easily accomplished. You can focus on investigation and discovery with Hunter as easily as killing monsters. Even in the killing monsters department it has a huge range. Do you want a claustrophobic, paranoid game about fighting an unseen adversary alone with only a baseball bat and a kitchen knife? Done. Do you want to lead a team of highly trained combat warlocks in a guns blazing raid to exterminate a nest of unholy abominations? Also done.

                          I enjoy hunter because by wielding the power 'necessary' to defeat the monsters of the world you dance the line of monster and mortal yourself. Then you have the risk of learning a truth that could undermine your entire drive and new found purpose. What if not all monsters are evil?

                          Hunter can be just a monster of the week, bust down the door and kill everything that isn't a mundane human but it can also be so much more.

                          For instance I have a character who is a lucifuge demonologist that uses 'evil' and the powers such can grant to have the strength to stand toe to toe with monsters. The ends justify the means type of character who willingly forsakes himself to save others. So not only does he have to hide his methods from other hunters and from the lucifuge themselves because of being a radical even among them but there is the possibility that one good monster could make him doubt very self imposed purpose.

                          So hunter unlike other lines allows you to willingly make yourself into a monster without noticing how far each little choice will take you until you are like the monsters you hunt. The big difference is they were made into what they are almost entirely against their will yet you chose to be their equal. These monsters in the dark must live in shadow in order to survive but you had a chance to live in a world of light. Yes you hunt because you saw into the darkness but why did you stand tall when others cower? Are you so different from the monsters you label as evil? Especially now as you slay beings just trying to survive are you so different? They were the stories told to scare you as a child and you have become the horror that monsters warn their children of. You are a hunter the lone thing that monsters fear.

                                              A major draw of Hunter, to me, is that I love an underdog story. The Chronicles of Darkness generally put mortal humans in the role of a background - they're victims and bystanders, maybe pawns. It's not as bad as it was in the old WoD, but still there. Hunter flips it over, and shows humanity biting back. Some of those weak, bog-standard mortals decide that they're not going to sit down and take it. And some magicians, blood-suckers or other aloof supernaturals are in for a nasty surprise.
