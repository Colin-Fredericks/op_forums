Even if "realistically" there's nothing special about it, it is certainly special from a narrative perspective.                     If we accept the base premise (which is noted in the HtV 1e core book as cited previously by nofather) that being a hunter goes beyond the pale of normal people being obsessed with something, even if it isn't a supernatural compulsion, then that's what wyrmhamster and I are concerned about the game not presenting well holistically. If you reject that premise... you're kind of rejecting the premise of the game-line.

                    If you really want to represent some sort of overpowering dedication to a cause you could just use that, couldn't you?
                    Not for the specific design goals in question. The whole point is to show that hunters go above and beyond. Just hitting them all with ready access to the same normal Condition doesn't really accomplish that. It's also highly restrictive. One of the points of the comparison to the Siskur-Dah Condition is that how you get it matters to what it does. A Blood Talon's Sacred Hunt isn't the same as a Storm Lord's Sacred Hunt. If we kept to my spitball idea of having a specific set of hunt-starting Tactics, those Tactics would each generate different effects for hunters Keeping the Vigil (it would also, incidentally, help with cross-organization play depending on implementation*). A wider array of possible Conditions leads to a greater variety of play experiences, and more depth to individualizing an organization based on which hunt-starting Tactics it uses.

                    * - This could be done "exclusively" where hunters need to have the same hunt-starting Tactic as the primary actor to benefit, which helps OOC pick organizations that have a certain amount of cross compatibility, or "inclusively" where any hunter involved in the opening can benefit, which gives greater cohesion to a more mixed organization PC group, and greater access to different Conditions to put up based on the nature of the enemy.
