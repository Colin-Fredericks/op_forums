They've already fallen off the"evil" side of things enough that they stopped doing anything for playing with morally questionable Hunters.

                            Originally posted by nofather View Post
                            I really don't like the idea of Ashwood Abbey as antagonists. Every compact and every conspiracy can take the place of antagonists in games, in fact their stereotypes usually play up this angle.

                            A question for the fans.

                            A lot of people seem to be coming at Hunter as if they're supposed to be the good guys, or it's the 'good guy' game, without any room for anything else. Is that how you see it Hunter the Vigil? How you think second edition should be?
                            I actually run Hunter as the villains. Chronicles of Darkness is a very grey vs. grey setting, where there are no clear good guys, everyone is just different shades of bad. But at my table, Hunters are the exception. They're the "good guys" in a setting where everyone else is the "bad guys" which makes them the de facto antagonists.

                            I like Hunters to look genuinely altruistic from a human-centric perspective; the lights in the darkness, driving back the forces of evil for the sake of all mankind. But in the Chronicles of Darkness, that unquestioned sense of righteousness is exactly what makes them the worst of the worst. At least the monsters are self-aware enough to know when they're doing wrong (wether or not they care varies from one individual to the next). But Hunters don't even consider that maybe they're not so morally unimpeachable as they think. It's like when a victim of bullying becomes a bully - you'll never convince them they're anything but a victim fighting back.

                            What I hope the Slasher Chronicle does is emphasizes that Hunters are just one little push away from being Slashers. Ashwood Abbey and the Hunt Club are a great example of just how similar Hunters and Slashers can be, and that's why I think it would be a shame to consolidate them. It takes a great moral quandary of "what really separates the Abbey from the Hunt Club?" and answers it with a predictable and boring "nothing at all."

                                                Personally, what I'd do to the Ashwood Abbey to preserve their "theme" and moral ambiguity but make them a lot more palatable, would be to go in one of two directions:

                                                A) The Ashwood Abbey tortures, yes, but they do it for a reason. No other Hunter group has more intimate information on the nature of the things that lurk in the dark than the Ashwood Abbey. They torture secrets out of monsters and keep a library of bloody confessions. They're rich, of course, even if they members weren't, they'd make several fortunes selling off their occult secrets. However, this version of the Ashwood Abbey does not do its grisly work for fun. It does it for a purpose, and that purpose is extracting as much knowledge as they can get from a monster before the body or the mind give way.

                                                B) The Ashwood Abbey hunts and kills monsters for fun, sure, but they don't torture. They are above such bloody, plebeian practices. Instead, this version of the Ashwood Abbey focuses more on the game of status, politics and accomplishments, rather than on the decadence. They're bored aristocrats on a world-spanning safari.

                                                Note that in both these cases, the sexual element of the Ashwood Abbey has been completely abandoned. This is absolutely crucial. I don't think I or any of my players could stomach a group that enacts sexual violence on anyone (even monsters) and isn't an antagonist.

                                                            Originally posted by nofather View Post
                                                            A lot of people seem to be coming at Hunter as if they're supposed to be the good guys, or it's the 'good guy' game, without any room for anything else. Is that how you see it Hunter the Vigil? How you think second edition should be?
                                                            The reason why a lot of people seem to come at Hunter as if they're supposed to be the good guys is pretty simple: when you get down it, Hunter is the game of "there are things that go bump in the night; it's your calling to bump back". The whole idea of the game is that you're a human who has the audacity to try and fight back against the creatures that lurk in the darkness - there's a very big "Mortal Empowerment" theme to Hunter.

                                                            Now, the game doesn't directly state this, but it's a theme that's readily seized upon by the "Humanity, Fuck Yeah!" fandom. Fighting monsters automatically makes you the Good Guy, it's just how people tend to think.

                                                            So, yeah, the majority opinion generally would be that Hunters are the Good Guy faction. Because they go out and oppose monsters. There's a reason that the least popular groups are generally either the cancerous ones (Les Mysteres and Knights of St. George as prime examples) or the villainous ones (Ashwood Abbey being the best example); these stand counter to the general assumption players have going in that "we're humans, they're monsters, we're supposed to be the Good Guys - we may risk losing ourselves in the fights, but we're still the Right Side going in".


                                                            On topic, I'm personally fine with whatever happens to Ashwood Abbey. It's always been Token Evil Teammate of the corebook's compacts, so as far as I'm concerned, it can't get much worse. The whole idea of it is that it's a Hellfire Club that's clued into the fact that there really are monsters out there. There's not much you can really do with that without leaving them heavily towards the Black side of the Black & Gray Morality scale. At best they're hedonistic thrill-killers who regard monsters as the ultimate thrill, or a cancer compact that happily interacts with monsters who can provide thrills (the two options not being mutually exclusive).

                                                            Really, the only way I've ever been able to keep the Abbey and the Club seperate in my head is by remembering that the Abbey at least has the guts to go after monsters that can really fight back. The Club just picks on ordinary humans, and usually ends up coming off the worst when it does bump into supernatural creatures.
