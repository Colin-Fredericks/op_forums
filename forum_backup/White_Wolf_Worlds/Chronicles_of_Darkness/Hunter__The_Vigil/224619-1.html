                        Character creation: this should be mechanically identical to the character creation rules in World of Darkness, as modified by the GMC rules update. There should be no “step five: apply the hunter template”, because there should be nothing in there to give the impression that hunters have a template. The only difference between mortal character creation and hunter character creation should be what sorts of questions are asked at step one (background) and what sorts of options are available at step seven (Merits).

                        Tiers: I like The King’s Raven’s notion that the difference between Compacts and Conspiracies is how organized you are, and I agree with the notion of divorcing Endowments from Conspiracies. I also like the notion that some groups that have been labeled as Conspiracies should be reclassified as Compacts.

                        OTOH, I also don’t think that “tier” should be used as a synonym for “cells, compacts, or conspiracies”. Rather, Tiers should be described in terms of intended play-style:

                        • Tier One is for those Storytellers who like the notion that hunters are no different from regular mortals, save only for the fact that they take a proactive stance when it comes to monsters. By definition, games played on this Tier don’t feature any special rules that regular mortals don’t have access to; nor do the hunters have any exemptions from any rules that work against ordinary mortals (e.g., Lunacy or the Lie). Practical Experience and Tactics are pretty much the only rules options that are suggested for Tier One play; and even then, they’re only suggested: it’s perfectly reasonable to run a Tier One game where the only special rules involved are the monster rules.

                        • Tier Two assumes that the protagonists are something special, though not supernatural. Practical Experience, Tactics, and Risking Willpower are recommended at this Tier of play. Also at this Tier of play, I’d suggest a new (to Hunter) option of Boosts, inspired by Strange Dead Love’s Heroic Mortals, which amount to more ways to spend Practical Experience: Attribute Boosts, Advantage Boosts (e.g., Defense, Health, Initiative, and Speed), and what I call Edges (options that let you do more with a trait such as Willpower, Integrity, or Skills than the normal rules allow). Edges would include such things as the Extended Willpower rules from Mirrors (i.e., more ways to spend Willpower), the Skill Tricks from Mirrors (more ways to use Skills), and immunity to the likes of Lunacy, the Lie, or Disquiet. None of the Boosts would be inherently supernatural, though; and each would describe not just its mechanical effects, but what sort of impact allowing it in the game would have.

                        • Tier Three assumes that the lines between mortal and monster have been blurred, and that hunters have reason to question which side of that line they stand on. Supernatural Merits (such as Endowments) are recommended at this tier of play, and Conviction from Mirrors becomes an option.

                        Note the terminology I’ve been using: “recommendations” are things that are assumed to be available unless the Storyteller specifically says otherwise; “options” are only available if the Storyteller specifically allows them. That is, recommendations are opt-out; options are opt-in.

                        Cells, Compacts, and Conspiracies: due to how Hunter was originally conceived, Cells and Compacts have been set up for what I’m calling Tier Two play (sans options), while Conspiracies have been set up for Tier Three play. Some of this lock-step nature can be addressed by downgrading some Conspiracies to Compacts (resulting in there being some Compacts designed with Tier Three play in mind); but I’d like the Chronicle book to go further and suggest ways that (what are currently Conspiracies) could be used in Tier Two or even Tier One games, or how Compacts or independent Cells could be adapted for use in Tier Three games. Not everyone in the Malleus Maleficatorum has studied Benedictions; and you could get some solid Tier One or Two play out of a cell of hunters from that Conspiracy who can’t call down miracles.

                        In short, I’d like Hunter to feature a highly customizable experience.

                                            Originally posted by ocel View Post
                                            I don't think Hunters should be immune to Lunacy, Blood Bonding, Disquite and so on, or have Conviction, 'cause we have the code for that, but I do agree with everything else you've said, Dataweaver.
                                            There’s a reason why I described those items strictly as “opt-in” things that are only to be used if the Storyteller wants to use them; and why I also suggested that they be accompanied by clear statements of the consequences of allowing them, so that nobody can say that they weren’t warned if it goes south on them. But if you check out The Lie and the Vigil, you’ll find someone arguing for why hunters who are a cut above the average mortal (which is what my proposed Tier Two is all about) shouldn’t be subject to the Lie; and you’ll find someone else arguing that being a Sleepwalker (i.e., not subject to the Lie) is a more natural state for humans and that becoming a Sleepwalker isn’t a supernatural change. That’s the sort of gaming group that the Edges involving Lunacy, the Lie, and Disquiet are intended to cater to.

                                            (Note also that I didn’t mention Blood Bonding; that’s partially because I’m not terribly familiar with Vampire, and partially because what I do know of Blood Bonding doesn’t strike me as being the same sort of thing as the others: as I understand it, nobody gets immunity to Blood Bonding, not even vampires.)

                                            Likewise, you’ll note that I listed Conviction as an “opt-in” thing for Tier Three, where the line between mortal and monster blurs: I don’t consider it to be a supernatural thing; but I’m well aware that there are others who do. I think it fits perfectly in the gray area that Tier Three games represent; but it should only be used if the Storyteller wants to use it. In particular, it’s not something that should show up on published Storyteller characters, any more than Tier Two’s Boost options should.

                                                I'm very much in favor of the Tier ideas expressed above and would, in fact, be in favor of going just a bit further. Instead of Tiers merely representing the scope of select organizations, I think it'd be neat if the book contained rules for modifying the existing compacts and conspiracies to fit that level of scope.

                                                For example: VASCU. Tier 1 "The Basement" (VASCU consists of a Cell of FBI Hunters working X-files like cases) >> Tier 2 VASCU as written >> Tier 3 "The Vanguard" (VASCU as an international Interpol-like organization given their mandate by the UN, maybe spun off even more from the Society of the Twelve Keys).

                                                Also, regarding a Hunter Chronicle Book, I think it'd be neat if it tackled the transformation of human into monster (ideologically and/or supernaturally), particularly if said transformation was a byproduct of a corruption of the Vigil. I think I'd call it the Contagion Chronicles and showcase the Vigil as something of a "force," although not characterized like the Principle or the Abyss. This Vigil gives Mortals who strike back against the supernatural a slight push unavailable to other Mortals in the form of Risking Willpower and maybe some slight bonuses when resisting Lunacy and the Lie (I'd probably have Hunters, in general, lump all the "forgetting" effects together into one concept called The Bystander Effect, or The Effect for short). However, the Vigil has a dark side to it in that it is easily contaminated by the supernatural, causing Hunters who too carelessly "use the devils tools against him" to slowly, painfully, and maddeningly cross the line from human into... something else. Fight fire with fire too often and soon the whole world will burn.
