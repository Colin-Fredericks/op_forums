    Originally posted by SunlessNick View Post
    Well, even in your example, I'd be sympathetic to the idea that an anti-vaxxer really needs a terrifying reminder of what the diseases these vaccines are against are really like (and a reminder where no one actually gets sick and dies is preferable).
    I have to disagree.

    From an ethical perspective if you can justify scaring an anti-vaxxer half to death; you can justify just giving their kids an injection behind their back. The moral principle behind anti-vaxx is a parents right to decide what's best for their children and the general right to refuse medical treatment. Once you've decided their views about vaccinees justify taking away that right, you have to think about the least harmful way to do that. Scaring them half to death until they consent is more harmful than just giving the shots.

    From a practical perspective anti-vax is the symptom not the disease. The disease is that humans like to feel in control; be it natural disasters or genetics, if it's uncontrollable we'll find some way to pretend it's not (usually religion). You can't teach people to accept their powerlessness through scaring them because; you're trying to teach them not to be afraid by using fear, it's not going to work.

    You scare them into using vaccines and they'll find some other ritual to ward off autism; feeling in control is a human need. Now maybe the next ritual will be less harmful; but governments are already coming up with more ethical ways to make people use vaccines than scaring them half to death.

                            In the interests of full disclosure - I didn't intend one way or the other. I came up with the concept of a Gandalf group and just wrote what came into my head. But when I read your view it seemed like a perfectly sensible reading.

                            The only things I really intended were:
                            • Their primary MO is manipulating heroes and well, being generally manipulative and scheming.
                            • They on occasion look and act like Gandalf.
                            • They had a rebuttal to the idea of teaching through fear.
                            • They're competent. (I always have this one. I'm not interested in playing an incompetent Hunter)

                            So providing you give them a reason to manipulate heroes that's correct and sensible enough that it doesn't make them incompetent it's compatible with my authorial intent. I get the impression that modifying Hero Gifts so that they're significantly better at fighting Beasts than guns or Lightweaving and making that the reason they use Hero pawns would not address your moral revulsion.
