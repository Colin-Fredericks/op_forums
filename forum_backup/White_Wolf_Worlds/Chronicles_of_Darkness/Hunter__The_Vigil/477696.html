    Your least liked Compacts/Conspiracies, and why?

    04-12-2015, 05:36 AM
    The Vigil contains a wide variety of Compacts and Conspiracies. Whilst this allows for a truly remarkable array of ideas to be covered, let's face it: they can't all be gems. So, I was morbidly curious: what are the Compacts and/or Conspiracies that, for you personally, rang as "duds", and why?

    For myself...

    The Keepers of the Source immediately springs to mind. They are just too unbelievable, in my mind. Homicidal hippies? Motivated by the fact that they're united by a particularly sucky variant of Unseen Senses that manifests as "feels pain when near loci and Essence holders"? A Cell with that sort of background, but it just strains my sense of disbelief that they A: have had the numbers of implied sensitives amongst their membership to develop as a faction, and B: not wound up dead as a result of picking fights with werewolves and mages.

    Ashwood Abbey is another obvious one. I get the whole black and gray morality thing, but from the start, these guys were just handled too poorly. They're the obvious bad guys - like saying Belial's Brood is one of the core player factions in Requiem. They had some interesting ideas, but they were just too hamfistedly the "token evil teammate" of the original C&Cs. And considering this same set includes the Malleus Maleficarium, whom I have personal reasons for *hating*, that the Abbey sticks out as my "#1 least liked corebook faction" is saying something.

    Incidentally, although I have very personal reasons for detesting the Malleus Maleficarium, the Long Night and Les Mysteres, I don't think I would include them here. This is because I do not think they are *bad* factions - in fact, I recognize all too well that they are natural, obvious factions to include in a game like Hunter. I just personally find them highly unappealing.

    ...Those are the only two factions that immediately come to mind when I think about "what Hunter factions do I not like". So, what factions do other people find unappealing?

          The Cainite Heresy. I'm a big fan of Ashwood Abbey.

          It seems like with the Cainite Heresy they went to great lengths to mesh it into the history of the setting in a way few other conspiracies were, having ties to the Aves of Minerva as well as the Requiem for Rome books. Their write-up has theories for how other conspiracies can find out about them because they've had contact throughout the years. They even played a part in one of the stories in the Strix fiction anthology. It may even be a shout out to Vampire the Masquerade. They have a wide variety of powers that are incredibly useful against their opponents, a mystery behind them to investigate. They even have good reasons for being so focused. While I love the Knights of St George, they're depicted as incredibly misguided and some people think they're even corrupted by evil aliens because if you just look at a Mage book, you can see they're wrong. And Les Mysteres are a bunch of do-gooders trying to kill the Forsaken, many of whom are trying to protect the world in turn. Vampires are easy to justify hunting, though. For every 'good one,' there's at least one sire who killed them in the first place and likely another two who would drain someone dry without a second thought if they thought it would give them an edge.

          But in addition to not playing well with others, they're, 'a crazy suicide-cult' of extremists who offer no compromise. It's just limiting from an options standpoint. There's things you can do with them, but they're unappealing to potential players due to the fanaticism.

          And also while I like the Promethean Brotherhood and the Faithful of Shulpae, and I'm not a 'balance guy,' I find it annoying that they have such a similar power while one is demonstrably better. This is fixed with a house rule, though.

                  None of my orgs so far, that's good anyway I've got quite a few opinions (where else does the fanwork come from).


                  Firstly I really dislike the idea of Hunters orgs that are pawns of the supernatural. Does it happen, sure, all the time. But it's antagonist material and it really bugs me to see it in the protagonist section. So we have:

                  Les Mysteries - Aside from being manipulated by spirits into fighting werewolves they're not really that bad. I'm not too fond of the genericness they have though, voodoo hunters, native American shaman hunters, Christian ecstatic hunters, alien abductees: All great ideas, but wrapping them into one Conspiracy robs them of their chance to shine individually. Most of all though, I feel there was a missed opportunity. Their Endowment is about a harmonious union of flesh and spirit, that's the exact opposite of the Werewolves enforcement of the gauntlet. It makes a far more interesting conflict than Spirits tricking them into fighting.

                  Division Six - I honestly feel that this is just a poor taste insult at technocracy fans. The picture of the sample hunter is what really gives me this impression (fun fact, I wrote the reality hackers specifically so I could portray someone who looks like that sample hunter as totally awesome). Fundamentally the Technocracy doesn't work as a Compact. Their whole shtick is that they're a world controlling Conspiracy and their concepts of "reality deviant" and similar need an Endowment, at the minimum, to let players say them with conviction.

                  The Knights of St George - It's been a while since I read them, but I have a distinct impression that they're actually under the control of the Faceless Angels, or the control of something. More than that, I just don't feel engaged by them. They fight witches, they have an Endowment, they're secretive, they're a cult. And? They don't really engage with any larger themes, certainly not Witch Finder's stated themes of moral ambiguity.

                  I'm not putting VALKYRIE here because it's easy to cut out the vampire nonsense (I prefer to say that vampires are metaphorical Afghanistan warlords to TF:V's USA) or the Malleus because I consider Ghouls and anything with an unmodified Morality/Integrity trait to be human. (If you're curious about how I justify the Athenic Church - simple. They're for Hunter: Cults, the usual rules don't apply).


                  Organisations who're founded on an inaccuracy, for lack of a better word. A group like the Promethian Brotherhood are evil, but their founding purpose is to steal magic and they're competent at stealing magic. In contrast The Keepers of the Source Are founded to protect Mother Earth from witches draining source. However draining source does not harm Earth. Nothing in the Mage or the Hunter rules says it does. I don't mind the homicidal hippies angle, it's twisted in a great way, but they need a better reason to be homicidal. I make them competent at manipulating lay lines to promote peace and love, and willing to kill any Witch who tries to steal their lay lines or nexuses.


                  I'm not a fan of Heritage House simply because I believe uniting all hunters misses the point. And worse, it's boring, the differences between goals and motivations is what makes the setting easier. The Scarlet Watch is a much better take on the idea of Vigil as a family business.

                  Speaking of Doubting Souls: It feels like Les Voyagers started with the idea of a Mountain Main / Fur Trapper as a Hunter (a good idea). But since there wasn't any room in the book for an individual Tier One hunter/Cell, they turned it into a Compact without doing anything to actually turn him into Tier 2. No origin, organisation, or reason for actually being a Compact. The economics of Les Voyagers don't make much sense either. They're in it to sell monster parts, but they're basically broke. If monsters aren't worth top dollar, why do they risk their lives instead of safer prey?

                  BTW. I actually like the Canites because they're great Vampire foils. They're the abused to vampire's abusers. They're (often nomadic) outsiders while Vampire often are The Man in the penthouse. Vampires are treated as pawns by their superiors, the Canites are given help by their "superiors" no strings attached (well maybe, who knows). Vampires power their magic by stealing blood, Cainites power their magic by donating blood. Vampire culture is dysfuctional and violent against fellow vampires, Canite culture is... dysfunctional but Canites genuinely have each others back.

                  This kind of relaitionship with the hunter and prey being different takes on the same core idea: abuse -> abuser / abused. It's something I really like and tried to capture a lot of when I did a Hunter splatbook. Look at the Lord Stewards compared to the Lost: The Lost are seeking a home - a community, the Lord Stewards maintain community and heritage on a national scale. Changeling powers come from contracts, which they twist and look for loopholes. Steward powers come from contracts, which they honour in spirit as well as word (same parallel with Pledges). Changelings were victimised by a god-powered and fundamentally alien being. The Lord Stewards have a working and mutually friendly business relationship with an aspect of the Wyrd, so a god-powered and fundamentally alien being. The Lost are very good at hiding, the Stewards are semi-public.

                          If I had to hate on any compact or conspiracy, I'd probably pick the Malleus Maleficarum, which is funny, because I kind of like them the most too.

                          I've ranted about it before, but the Malleus's focus is on vampires. A holy order whose sacred text says "Thou shalt not suffer a witch to live" and whose name is quite literally "the Hammer of Witches"... Focuses on vampires. An order founded in a religious tradition that still practices exorcism and says that witchcraft can unleash demons upon people focuses on vampires. An order whose religious heads have set forth decrees stating that signs of demonic possession include supernatural knowledge and abilities hunts vampires. Okay, maybe that last one a bit.

                          I could go on. The Malleus's focus feels off. They should be omnivores of the supernatural, sure, but their primary focus should be witches. A part of this is practical - if you believe that witches are the primary mechanism by which evil enters the world, killing a vampire is just putting a compress on a bleeding wound - but a part of it is theological too: Vampires, werewolves, and the like are victims of demonic possession and, therefore, either deserve destruction or exorcism; their choices are not their own. Witches, however, have free will. The receiving of the Great Commission, the duty of all Christians to go out and witness to the world, requires the Malleus to simultaneously try and save the souls of those who can be redeemed from witchcraft and to destroy those who are truly devil-bought.

                          Another good reason this should be the focus is the obvious fact that it allows for really clear schisms within the Malleus. I do want to say that I love vampires as threats for religious Hunters because the siren's call of the pleasures of the flesh is an awesome counterpoint to unworldly characters, but, at an organizational level, there's not going to be any interesting internal conflicts around this. They might argue whether vampires need to be destroyed, exorcised, or can somehow be redeemed/controlled - sort of a theological equivalent to Fuck/Marry/Kill (egads, why is that even a thing?) - but that's a schism every Hunter faces; not really a unique one to the Malleus.

                          No, if the Malleus's chief opponent is witches, it raises foundational questions about the Malleus itself. How would you tell the difference between a sorcerer and a true miracle worker sent by God? Plenty of the Obrimos Path amongst the Awakened certainly feel like they saw God (or something like it). How can you tell the difference? Is there such a thing as "holy" magic? What difference is there between the workings of a sorcerer and the Malleus's benedictions?

                          Phew. Think that's all of it.
