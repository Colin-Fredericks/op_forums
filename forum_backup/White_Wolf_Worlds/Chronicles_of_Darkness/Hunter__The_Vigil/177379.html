    The DiFillipo Family, "The Outfit"

    06-22-2014, 06:19 PM
    Just my attempt to try and make an organized criminal organization compared to the Night Watch and Ascending Ones.

    Organized crime. The two words immediately brings up images of a group of wise guys gathered around a big Italian dinner, talking about "whacking" someone and making offers that can't be refused. Sadly for organized crime, this image affected them as much as the rest of the world, and in the 70s and 80s images of Italian organized crime families were nearly destroyed by the efforts of the Federal government to dismantle their rackets and organizations. The younger mobsters didn't realize that the stories of the sharply dressed, wise-cracking wise guys were just that; stories. By 1993, La Cosa Nostra (Everyone who does their research knows it's only ever called the Mafia by outsiders) was a shadow, dealing with the invading Mafiya from Eastern Europe, the surge in gang violence by blacks, bikers, and Latinos, and the ever vigilant feds sniffing around.

    The Rafaello Family was such a family, trying to keep itself stable in Albany, NY. The influx of Russians, gangs, and street drugs forced the family into a desperate situation as their brothels and nightclubs were consistently snapped up by other groups. What saved them was the fact that they still held some politicians in one and, and a core team of loyal soldatos and "made men" in the other. Desperate, the boss of the family, Armand "Beltway" Sofia, decided to try and reach out to one of the other criminal organizations in the area for help. Sadly for him, he got it. After a few weeks, rumors were spreading that the boss was acting weird, ordering hits where they had no enemies before and getting their best associates killed or arrested for no reason that would help the family. Some of the men said he was getting pale, others said that he started to act like a junkie in a bad need of a fix. More importantly, one of the made men of the family saw the boss bringing what could only be his mistress into his house, with his wife and children still at home.

    Despite the behavior, the boss' turnaround somehow kept the cops and feds at bay, and the family managed to regain some ground from their competitors by '97. Rumors wouldn't die about the boss though, and eventually Armand had to beat one of the made men down who had been saying the boss didn't have his head on straight anymore. Before anyone could ask questions, they got a job to break up a worker's strike at a nearby construction project. The workers didn't budge when confronted, and the underboss of the family, Tommy "Guts" DiFillipo, ordered as much dirt as possible be dug up. It was a goldmine, the strike leaders dipping their hands into a lot of dirty deals; murders, assaults, arson, everything that would give any anti-labor supporter a hard-on. But the paper trail showed something else. The people they targeted sometimes had no background, no history. Others just seemed to pop out of nowhere. Several disappearances really caught DiFillipo's eye, since the names were associates the boss had said, "Weren't needed anymore". Digging even deeper (and against the warnings of his fellow made men), DiFillipo found that the money the family was laundering had been sent to the businesses attacked by the strike leaders, and had been attacked long before the family had even started laundering with them. Worried about an attack on the family, DiFillipo tried to call the boss' attention, but was brushed off like he was a child.

    Incensed, DiFillipo gathered his crew and marched them into the boss' home one night, desperate for answers. The found the door unlocked, and moved through the palatial mansion their boss now called a home. There were small spatters of blood over the carpets and hardwood, and the body of a local working girl on the dining room table half-dazed and with a small puddle of blood next to her neck, but no cuts or signs of harm. Guns drawn now, the crew worked their way upstairs. The bedrooms were a mess, and the boss' kids were found knocked out with heavy-duty sleeping pills. The master bedroom was the scene where they found the boss, his wife, and the working girl. Long story short, the working girl turned to ash, the boss and his wife were dead, and a new family formed that night.

    The death of a boss made for big waves, and DiFillipo was forced to answer to the Commission itself, brought to New York and the most secret gathering of the criminal world's leading men, three bosses and two consigleries of New York's Five Families, as well as the underboss of the Chicago Outfit. DiFilllipo tried to avoid the truth knowing that it would probably earn him a bullet to the head and a new iron chain swimsuit, but by the end of the meeting DiFillipo had to face facts: He told the men before him that he killed what could only be a vampire, and that his boss had been it's slave.

    His shock could only be imagined when the men before him admitted they had either known or suspected for years that there were things out there, but that the mob's code of silence had indirectly affected their ability to speak with each other on what they knew. Put simply, no one wanted to openly stick their noses into something that could get themselves killed and their families eliminated. With DiFillipo's actions, the creatures had a new target, but the Commission also saw opportunity. Cosa Nostra had never before tried to play against the monsters for fear of reprisals. Knowing that one of their own had managed to do so, they asked DiFillipo is he thought he could turn the knowledge of monsters into a profit for his family, but for now his family alone. Not seeing any other way out of the meeting, DiFillipo accepted.

    Feelers were slowly spread out to try and figure out just who was what in the new world the family found itself in. For a start, vampires were now trying to eat the family alive by worming in through associates and business partners. There were so many holes in the fence that DiFillipo had to purge hard. Associates were beaten and ordered to leave town, or otherwise disposed of. The cops that could play ball in the local and state police were bribed hard and watched carefully for weeks to ensure they weren't already slaves. Anyone who was had the threat of their dirty secret hung over their heads until they realized their error. The war in Philly covered the family, and soon DiFillipo found himself meeting with the leading vampire of Albany. It was a long, hard negotiation. Just DiFillipo and the vampire. No one knows what happened inside, but the vampire walked out looking half-defeated and ruffled. DiFillipo looked worse, but still alive and his own man. A deal had been breached; the vampires would pay their dues to the family, and in return the family didn't alert the workers to their location.

    DiFillipo realized that the creatures lurking around at night had possibly even more to fear than your average blackmail victim. Their families were sometimes kept completely in the dark, or even didn't know their loved ones were still alive. Their rituals involved strange materials only available through illegal means, or access to locations that only criminals would think of accessing. There was money and favors in that, something DiFillipo knew was more valuable than going around knocking every other monster's head together. Businesses owned by monsters directly or indirectly were strong-armed, the owners reminded that it would be bad for everyone's health if they tried to make a scene. Justifiably afraid of their secrets being found, the rackets started to pick up again. Bootlegged cigarettes, weapons, women, it started to move again, and through the family's hands.

    What didn't pick up was the drug trade. Watching as the Philly mob and others were laid low thanks to the War on Drugs, DiFillipo made the controversial decision to only take the trade from as close to his turf as possible. Local growers, meth cookers, smuggled prescription drugs, less well known and infinitely less dangerous to the family's new situation than the Fed's favorite targets of cocaine and heroin from overseas. Dealers and associates of the family questioned the decision, but DiFillipo stood firm; the weed would be their bulk product, and the rest the moneymakers. It worked for a while, but then someone started going after the dealers. Messages were left, start selling cocaine and heroin again, or else. Knowing he couldn't do anything for now without angering someone apparently powerful enough to evade his police and made men, DiFillipo started selling again, but at much lower rates than the other families on the East Coast.

    More importantly, DiFillipo had other hunters buying his guns, including the workers who had been on strike. Meeting covertly, they explained there were hundreds, maybe thousands of hunters in the world also knowing about the creatures. As a sign of good faith, DiFillipo did give them the guns, but made sure they knew that he was on their side when it came to monsters. Privately, he made sure the men's faces and license plates were photographed. Going to his new protection clients, he flashed their faces, and in a witch's book shop they were pointed out as the ones who nearly beat her to death three months ago for going into their neighborhood. Showing that his family kept their word, the men were arrested and tried on the word of the witch, and the monsters took notice. So did other hunters. Certain groups reached out to the Outfit cautiously, looking for couriers or contracted help for their operations. The monsters started coming for the same; any human that can face down vampires and not blink has something going for them alright.

    Currently DiFillipo is publicly a restauranteur and investor, as well as a local philanthropist to keep himself good looking for the editors and cops on the payroll. Like the old school gangsters, he builds himself up as a man of the people, which to him is somewhat true. He and his men protect them from the creatures in the night. If that means the Outfit takes a bigger piece of the action, all the better. Behind the closed doors, DiFillipo has his men concentrating on three main areas of activity. First is the street level stuff, selling drugs and weapons from the backs of the cars, hooking the monsters and people in the area hooked on what he gives them. Second, telling the monsters who's in charge now. A local pack of werewolves chafed hard at the thought of human criminals telling them who to pay every month, until they saw the DiFillipo had pictures of their homes and loved ones waiting for them. Written on the back of each picture, "Accidents happen". Some monsters have even begun working as associates for the Outfit, sensing a change in the wind. DiFillipo is happy to go along for now, since a threat against a witch can be easily backed up with a werewolf enforcer. He won't let monsters rise in the organization however. Along with the traditional rule of Italians only, humans are only allowed to become soldiers. There's even a supernatural protection racket, watching over a sacred space or lay line until someone makes a better offer.

    Finally, DiFillipo is trying to make more money from the big player hunters. So far it's led to a few but high-paying jobs; running strange objects from point A to B, acting as contracted muscle for fools in stupid looking jackets or even the Church, and helping the Feds with their more secretive activities. The main goal is to figure out who might be behind the fact that he's been forced to continue dealing drugs he knows will kill his organization.

    The Competition

    No one is an enemy, enemies cause wars. No, there's the "competition", the ones that you compete against but know better than to fight unless it's necessary. Instead, you're competing with them. You're competing for the best smuggling routes, the best corners, the best clubs and houses. In the shadowy world beyond Cosa Nostra, everyone is a potential competitor.

    The worst are the vampires, who have gotten proud over their abilities to manipulate and control, though witches are a close second. Thinking their powers give them an edge, they move against the Outfit nightly in trying to steal their regained turf. The Outfit fights back by using the daylight to their advantage, telling the police who to stop and where to take them. That's not saying the two can't be negotiated with, indeed the Outfit has so far met with vampires several times to hammer our who controls what and where in the city. Witches have also tried to come at the family as vigilantes, but these flashier witches and wizards don't quite make the leap from "magical superhero" to "automatically bulletproof" sometimes. The ones that do are left to someone that can handle them as the family falls back.

    Werewolves are actually considered good muscle for associates, and this has created a massive schism between the Outfit and any hunters who know. The hunters argue that werewolves are more harm than good, and that trafficking with them will lead to massive damage. The Outfit differs, since 500 pounds of solid muscle is just as desperate for money as the street dealer. Again, not made men at all, the wolves who work with the family do benefit from a better access to sacred spaces without fear of competition, and a steady income when most of their kind have to worry about their rage controlling them in the office.

    Creatures stranger than these are handled on a case-by-case basis. Sometimes a dead man who isn't dead comes walking through, and the family asks him why he's still alive. Good answer, pay a bribe and be on your way. Bad answer, there are some doctors who love getting things like you. A victim's ghost is whispering who did it? The Outfit will call their best exorcist and get that ghost gone, for a steep fee. This serves the dual purpose of finding people who can be convinced that it would be in their very best interests to continue convincing the family not to reveal what happened.

    Other hunters are truly competition, especially groups like the Night Watch out of Pittsburgh. They hate criminals, and the Outfit hates being tread on. Currently the Night Watch won't go out of it's ghettos, and the Outfit makes sure to channel vampires their way to keep them occupied. A more pressing concern is who has forced the family to continue selling crack. What DiFillipo has pieced together is that they operate over a wide area, maybe even into New York, and that they are powerful enough to be involved in opium and cocaine at the same time. DiFillipo is still putting his information together, but knowing someone is manipulating the mob to bring in more money for themselves, it could convince the Commission to act against them and drop the drugs until the Feds cool off and people controlling them stepped down.

    At the same time, competition can give you favors. Hunter groups including the church's Inquisition and government's black ops monster squad have requested aid from the Outfit before, and know enough to repay when the favor is called in after five or ten years. Smaller groups of hunters take the weapons being sold from the Outfit, but are warned point blank that running to the cops will get them arrested and jailed if they're lucky. Others, like fairies, traffic drugs into the city that the Outfit can only imagine using (basic first rule; don't sample the merchandise on yourself). In kind, the Outfit tells them whatever they ask. Some fairies do try and take over Albany's underworld, but they're quickly beaten back down when the family digs in and finds the remaining family of the fairies they're facing off against.

    Hunters

    You're an associate, technically not a real part of the family. But you're valuable, in that you're the best numbers runner and bookie in town. You keep track of who's betting on what, and if someone's too lucky to be normal, you know who to point out.

    As a cleaner, you see sick shit every other week. But you've grown numb to it, so when one of the capo's asked you to deal with a new corpse, you didn't think much of it. When you finally managed to dissolve that patchwork thing, you wanted to know just what was going on. Too bad for you, you got the answer.

    The boss wants things done, you do it. Collections, laundering, protecting the girls in the clubs, you do it. Beating witnesses or competitors, you do it. Taking a stake from the weapon's case and finding a priest you can trust, you do it.

    As an old man, you remember the actual mob bosses, you even got to meet "Big Joe" Castellano before that scum Gotti ordered the hit. What you do now is act as mediator, the man the other families can go to when they need a guy for some "special problems" their own can't handle. You have lots of favors for the family now, and when the boss asks, you call them in.

    The days of Murder, Inc. are long gone, but bosses still need hired killers. You provide a service, and that's all it is. Family men, crazed junkies, rapists the bosses don't like, as long as there's money you're in. Usually you don't worry about who your targets were, but somehow, killing monsters seems...right.

    Stereotypes

    Ascending Ones:     We don't want to do hard drugs because it calls down too much heat. We get suppliers trying to figure out why we're just grabbing from the local meth dealers and pot growers, and sometimes those suppliers get too pushy. A lot of'em have these weird drinks they carry with'em, but no one's dumb enough to drink something that smells like that.

    Network 0: Fucking bloggers think they're hot shit, going around filming everything. We had this one guy, following a crew as they took down this leech's guys, said he wanted to just show the leech existed. Broke his camera, but he was happy to accept our money for a replacement once he realized what we do. Bastard pissed himself too, fucking hilarious.

    Malleus Maleficarum: The priests, the Church, they all know. Makes me wonder about John Paul I. Anyway, they usually come from the church three blocks over, ask for Fr. Riviera. They know what we do, but we know what they do. As long as we're sinning for God, I don't think they care what we do.

    Union: Fuckers are still trying to run us down, acting like their shit don't stink. All they keep going on about is "Workers this, workers that". Bullshit, it's not about the workers for them. They're using the things as a fucking excuse, they can't hack it without stealing the money the things have. How do you think they buy all their guns and shit? They're skimming from the creatures, we're just honest about it.

    Crews and Status

    The Outfit is organized into three distinct Crews, given no name but what their role in the family is. Each crew is also organized by status in the organization, meaning that only full-human Italians can rise above status 2. Disobey the family or turn against them, and you won't live long enough to know what happens when you lose status.

    SIDEBAR: Monster Mobster

    As mentioned earlier, the Outfit does, in fact, take in creatures as the lowest members of their compact. So are these monsters also hunters?

    Thematically, you can play it either as they are and just ostracized from the compact, or that they aren't and are still ostracized. More importantly, mechanically a creature who is also at status 1 can still use the majority of their abilities. Whether this is a PC or not should be decided beforehand, to iron out which books might be needed for the character(s). They do, however, gain the ability to risk willpower and use tactics like hunters do if your group chooses.

    For the lesser splats (ghouls, wolf-blooded, etc), they function as hunters do for the purposes of willpower, tactics, and hunter merits.

    Associates (Status 1) are the compact's informants. Numbers runners, street dealers, all kinds of lowlife scum and malcontents. They don't quite have any power in the group, but are smart enough to realize that the Outfit offers better protection than nearly any other criminals from things that aren't easy to handle, and in return the associates tell the family what's on the streets. Specialty: Criminal Mind: Stealth or Larceny. Someone above you says jump, you jump as high as you can. As a bonus, they might just teach you something worth learning. Treat this as a one-dot mentor in the Outfit.

    Soldiers/Soldato (Status 3) are the "made men" of the compact, Italians who have proven they have what it takes to rise in the family. Governed by a code of silence not seen since Cosa Nostra formed, the Soldiers of the family are the muscle, organizing the hits and killings that the family needs to keep the wolves at bay from the sheep. Specialty: Dirty Fighting: Brawl or Weaponry. You've made your bones, and now can safely say that you have a little pull in the businesses you shakedown for the money. Treat this as Intimidation or Larceny: Racketeering.

    Capos (Status 5) command the soldiers and associates under them, and so act as the leadership of the group. Unlike other compacts, there is a clear message about who's in charge, and disobedience to a capo is often enough to earn severe punishment. Despite this, the capos are also the family's political side, negotiating with the cops and politicians to keep certain noses and ears from where there would be trouble for all involved. Specialty: Offer You Can't Refuse: Intimidation or Persuasion. The Outfit isn't going to risk someone like you breaking kneecaps, so you're in charge of managing the family's finances and interfacing with the local criminal element as well. This usually equates to Allies: Local Criminals, for two dots.

      I think what this needs is more profit in monster hunting, and more focus on profit instead of killing monsters. I can understand why selling out to the vampires makes piles of cash, but not why hunting them does so - they might snatch a brothel or a few blocks of turf but it doesn't seem like there's much reason to go after the vast majority who don't have any stake in the Underworld.

      There's plenty of ways to make cash. Running relics for the Ageis Kai Doru or subcontracting for Cherion. "You see this here line running from our warehouse to your tranquil grove, wouldn't it be a shame if we put a boxing ring there nice and legal."


      Some other thoughts.

      The Commission's reaction didn't feel plausible to me. A group that powerful, either they knew already or they knew already but were in denial. Perhaps they knew but had a policy of not poking any metaphorical sleeping dragons. DiFillipo asked if he could experiment with trying to make a profit from the occult, and the Commission agreed on the condition that it was at arms length from themselves; hence why he has so much freedom.

      The no-drugs policy felt like it was there because the author didn't like drugs. A stronger reason is needed - why would they care if monsters skim provided they're still making a profit? Perhaps once they started moving into the occult business the Jagged Crescent started to boss them around, they said no, so the Crescent cut them off from the supply and sold straight to the dealers. DiFillipo knows better than to pick a fight with the Cartels, and the Commission put him at arms length to prevent exactly this kind of unpleasantness from reaching them. So he wisely backed off.

      Also, I'd generally avoid ever saying a Compact has gotten itself into a Conspiracy's bad books, because that really lowers a Compact's life expectancy.
