    Mummies, and Prometheans, and Geists! Oh my!

    12-09-2015, 11:00 AM
    I've noticed that there is a tendency to make monster-specific groups of hunters that are geared towards hunting one particular type of monster. Knights of St. George, Caninite Heresy, Les Mysteres, and VASCU. Yet there don't seem to be specific groups geared towards Mummies, Prometheans, and Geists. I've left Demons from Demon: the Descent out because it seems as though groups like the Lucifuge and Malleus Maleficarum are sufficiently anti-fallen angel enough, and would rabidly jump at the opportunity to hunt them; all they need are one or two endowments specifically geared towards those types of demons, if that. I've already started another topic thread about Changelings, so there is little need to bring them up here. Beast: the Primordial already seems to have a group of hunters built into it. These other three groups though, they deserve at least one Compact and one Conspiracy each. Surely they've pissed enough mortals off somewhere that a bunch of people have gotten together and said "You know THOSE monsters? Not all monsters in general, just *those* monsters in particular. They need to go. We're going to kill them." Especially Prometheans, since they have disquiet and are really good at pissing people off.

    Ideas? Suggestions? Specific mechanics to use in a home brew? Spill it in the comments below.

          I'm pretty sure you can make a "The dead should stay dead" hunter group for Geists and Prometheans. IMO they're not numerous enough to deserve a group each (especially Prometheans), and they both have that highly specific thing in common.

          It's interesting to note that while this group will happily hunt vampires, they don't really have the same zealous fervour, since vampires aren't a strict "Defiance of Fate" like Geists and Prometheans are. It's extremely rare that vampires Embrace someone who died or was going to die by other causes, in order to deny them their rightful death. Instead, Geists and Prometheans represent a violation of the fundamental laws of the world. All things must die, and stay dead.

          I'd call this group the Compact of Ankous. "The Ankou is the henchman of Death and he is also known as the graveyard watcher, they said that he protects the graveyard and the souls around it for some unknown reason and he collects the lost souls on his land." Going by this, these hunters could see themselves as the servants of Death or Fate, ending those that have defied the ultimate Law.

                  Personally, I do not couple Endowments with Hunter group size.

                  So a potential Endowment for the Ankous Compact could be called Endings. Each dot in the Endowment gives you a single Ending. You may have up to 5. Calling upon an Ending renders the Hunter vulnerable to the living. Some examples:

                  Ending of Lies

                  Action: Instant.
                  Cost: 1 Willpower.
                  Roll: Intelligence + Subterfuge + 1 for every other Ending the Hunter has learnt.
                  Consequence: The Hunter takes a -1 penalty to rolls meant to detect the lies of the living. This lasts for 24 hours and not only stacks with itself, but each further use refreshes the duration of the Consequence.
                  Dramatic Failure: The lies of the dead are reinforced. Attempts to perceive the dead as they truly are suffer a -3 penalty for the rest of the scene.
                  Failure: The lies of the dead are too strong.
                  Success: The Hunter calls upon a prayer or quote that pertains to the ways the dead delude the living. When he finishes his utterance, he lifts the veil of lies that the dead use to shroud themselves. Any corporeal undead within 10 yards of the Hunter must roll Resolve + their Supernatural Advantage and exceed the Hunter's successes, or their true appearance is laid bare for the Hunter to see. Any manifesting ghosts in the area must roll their Resistance instead. For the rest of the scene, the Hunter can see the affected undead for what they really are, Disfigurements and all. Ghosts cannot conceal their nature from him behind an illusion, he ignores a Vampire's Blush of Life and he can see the marks of what killed a Sin-Eater as ghostly imprints on their skin.
                  Exceptional Success: Rolls to resist this Ending are made at a -2 penalty.
                  Suggested Modifiers: The Hunter is a pure mortal with no supernatural powers or merits other than the Endings Endowment (+1). The Hunter has not uttered a falsehood in the last 24 hours (+2). The Hunter has uttered a falsehood in the last hour (-1). The Hunter is wearing a disguise or is concealing his identity in some way (-2).

                  Ending of Tyranny

                  Action: Instant.
                  Cost: 1 Willpower.
                  Roll: Presence + Intimidation + 1 for every other Ending the Hunter has learnt - an undead creature's Composure.
                  Consequence: The Hunter takes a -1 penalty to resist the emotional or mental sway of the living. This lasts for 24 hours and not only stacks with itself, but each further use refreshes the duration of the Consequence.
                  Dramatic Failure: The Hunter accidentally reinforces the tyranny of the dead over the living. The affected undead creature gains a +2 for the rest of the scene on any rolls to invoke mental or emotional control over others.
                  Failure: The Hunter fails to break the dead's control over the living.
                  Success: The Hunter targets a creature that they know is undead, and upon reciting a prayer or quote that scolds the dead for attempting to hold sway over the living, they attempt to end any of their supernatural powers that is currently affecting a mortal's mind or emotions. If they are successful, they end a single instance of that power. For example, they can end a Galateid's Mesmerising Appearance, a Vampire's Awe discipline, a Sin-Eater's Passion Marionette, or reverse the Blood Bond by one step.
                  Exceptional Success: The Hunter may end two current effects instead of one.
                  Suggested Modifiers: The affected undead creature has Striking Looks (+1). The affected undead creature has flaunted its control over mortals, and the Hunter has witnessed it (+2). The Hunter is currently being affected by the target's mental or emotional power (-2). The Hunter has been affected by the target's mental or emotional power in the past (-1).

                  Ending of Passage

                  Action: Instant.
                  Cost: 1 Willpower.
                  Roll: Strength + Athletics + 1 for every other Ending the Hunter has learnt - an undead creature's Stamina.
                  Consequence: The Hunter takes a -1 penalty to resist grappling and other restraining effects when done unto him by the living. This lasts for 24 hours and not only stacks with itself, but each further use refreshes the duration of the Consequence.
                  Dramatic Failure: The Hunter's weakness and lack of training cause him to stumble and fall. He not only fails to stop the undead creature, but he also falls prone and takes 1 Bashing damage.
                  Failure: The dead are sometimes unstoppable.
                  Success: The Hunter latches onto the undead creature and prevents it from fleeing. Until the end of his next turn, the undead creature cannot leave the spot it stands on, and cannot use any supernatural powers that allow him to escape, such as discorporating into mist or insects, or using more esoteric forms of transportation. In order for this Ending to be possible, the Hunter must be within melee range of his target, and must be able to physically grasp him.
                  Exceptional Success: The Hunter's grip and finality is such that the undead creature is rendered completely immobile. It is considered paralysed and helpless for the duration of this effect.
                  Suggested Modifiers: The Hunter knows that the affected undead creature has a supernatural power that allows it to escape situations easily, such as Cold-Wind Caul 5 for a Sin-Eater (+2). The affected undead creature has a Speed greater than 15 (+1). The Hunter's Speed is greater than the undead creature's (-1). The affected undead creature is missing its legs or is otherwise impaired from moving (-2).

                  Just to get some ideas going.

                      Nice, though I think Ending of Lies should limit the ability to see the dead for what they are to the Hunter who used the effect. Think the sunglasses from the movie "They Live".

                      Using an ability that exposes the undead to the public would only put a target on your back. If the general public sees the undead too often it would trigger multiple groups to investigate why it happened and who caused it. This could end with TFV throwing an Ankous hunter into a black unmarked van while they walked down the sidewalk, or the vampire Mafia sending a hit man to kill an Ankous hunter in his sleep once they find out who he is. Discreetly revealing an undead creature to themselves, on the other hand would allow them the opportunity to stalk the creature, get it alone, and give it a more discreet final death.

                              well, I've wrote a number of groups, some from Geists, some for Prometheans and some for Mummies-

                              The Ebony Fangs- on ancient conspiracy on the verge of dying, which force the Old Laws of the Underworld upon both the living and the dead and keep them separated in honor of their goddess and an ancient tragedy.

                              The Ashen Collective- alchemists who deal with the darker side of the Divine Fire, as they attempt to discover the truth behind souls and how to convert them into the mythical Philosopher's Stone while creating all kinds of horrors in their labs.

                              Custodes Ignis- warrior priests from the time of Rome who manage the effects of Disquiet, Wastelands and Firestorms in order to maintain the harmony of the world and stop it from changing.

                              The Temple of Silence- a tradition coming from the first and only rebellion of Irem who worship a Named Goddess who was punished for her part in the rebellion and seeks to give the Nameless Empire its proper burial

                              Maw of the River- assassins who devour the relics of the past in order to return them to the River of Time and seeks to destroy all which was left of Irem's legacy while chasing down the Devourer and her spawn.

                              Maagal HaBaal VeHaAshera- biblical idol makers turned into modern artists who shape Sekhem in order to give life to their gods while fighting against the attempts of the Deathless at destroying their creations and stealing their secrets.

                                              I was bouncing around the idea of an Orthodox Jewish compact that deals primarily with prometheans (golems) and ghosts/spirits. It would be largely disorganized and carried through word of mouth through insular communities, but I'm also picturing a small network of "fixers" who are contracted out for situations that get out of control. I'm just not sure I have the credibility to do the idea justice. I'm figuring a group that, for a long time was essentially a loose information trade among spiritual leaders in Hasidic communities but has since been overtaken more by seedier elements (influenced by the real life story of Rabbi Mendel Epstein's black hat goon squad and the orthodox mafia elements in The Yiddish Policemen's Union), a small group of rough men who deal with problems of supernatural threat in communities that are reticent to go to secular authorities.
