                Originally posted by LostLight View Post

                My problem with the Enigmatics is very simple- their Vigil is explicitly non-violent. Now, don't get me wrong, I am totally fine with such groups, and they occupy a important niche of inspired artists which is really cool. However, their Endowments are really dangerous, especially for regular humans. You can call it "playing with fire", but again- those hunters do not focus on killing monsters, only by being inspired by them, and a lot of the harms are very much directed towards regular people (for example, turning people into cannibals, or having statues that demand human sacrifice, This.. ain't good. A non violent group which makes some very dangerous Endowments they don't fully control that are not meant to be applicable in the Vigil and has some very, very bad consequences for mortals is a very bad combinations for everyone around. I am not saying "those guys are slashers", but saying "are you sure they are not?" because either intentionally or not, it is not clear to me how their Endowments actually help their own Vigil of getting inspired by monsters or the Vigil as a whole. Even if they are not outright slashers- well, they are foot and a half in the way, imo. This is basically turns to, again, "how is this a conspiracy when all of their powers are environmental hazard" . Good concept, sure, but God it needs much more development to become playable imo.

                EDIT- note that if they were a violent group it would have made at least some sense for how they are using their Endowments for the Vigil. Again, I personally like the idea of artists being non violent in terms of their Vigil, but in that case the Endowments can't be so violent- because someone is meant to get hurt, and ion most instances the texts explicitly says that bystanders are going to be the ones getting screwed. It can't be a matter of wordcount alone considering that other orgs do not generally suffer from this dissonance (some have their own problems, as I mentioned before)
                Their Vigil doesn’t need to be violent, because they’re focused on the inspiration from the encounter. There’s no oath of pacifism, though. How violent a given Enigmatic might be is their own call. The artist hiding in the bushes with a sketchbook while other hunters deal with the threat is certainly valid, but so is the one that donates a cursed painting to gather a monster nest in one place before lighting the whole place on fire. They’re still hunters, and they can still have a moral compass that drives them to do something about particularly problematic monsters. They also (probably) can’t create their art if they’re dead, so unless the only monsters they encounter are things like Batman With Fangs, they’re likely going to need to defend themselves sooner or later.
                Their masterworks are all cursed, yes, and many do have negative effects on innocent bystanders. This requires them to take a creative approach to using them if they wish to minimize collateral damage. An Enigmatic who’s too cavalier about activating their masterworks is walking a slippery slope, and can easily draw the ire of other hunters or even become a particularly twisted slasher. A song that makes Dread Powers harder to use can be handy, but it’s better to use in an isolated area than on a crowded subway car if it also gives mortal bystanders a taste for long pork.

                                    Anyways, it occurs to me I have no laid out opinions, so on that note:

                                    Compacts, Least to Most Favorite

                                    8) The Recalibrators: When I first heard about this compact idea, my initial response was "Wait, isn't it that just low-budget Valkyrie?", and on this side of the release, I kind of find myself feel myself confirmed. While I definitely appreciate giving credit towards janitorial services in that way that Hunter can and does do and is part of it's point, and I'm always for that "this would be a cult if they weren't hunters" vibe, I find myself not clicking with the compact as a whole-mostly, it feels like the buck stops at it's concept.

                                    7) The Crossroads Convoy: It's not the sort of thing that comes up a lot here, but I fucking love road culture. I love road trips, I love driving all variety of cars from high end sports cars to rinky dink cans you're afraid of falling apart at 85 MPH, I have this weird affinity for gas stations that basically makes them the stores I will visit the most in any given location I'm in, so much so that I have mourned the loss of one in my college days, I enjoy hanging out with those in the land transport business when I get the chance to regardless of their political dispositions, and I feel like if trucking were as profitable and supported as it was before the late 70s, I'd be very down for it. Midnight Roads is one of my unsung favorite supplements over here in Chronicles land, and it's very much part of the reason why I have love for the Knights of Saint Adrian, as road culture is frequently a hand-in-hand companion with the life of bounty hunters, and going into Dark Eras, I've got a soft spot for the Gallow's Post. So imagine my surprise when I found myself cold on the compact handling all of that, even arguably going so far as to be a Hunter variant on the Gallow's Post. I'm not even really sure why that's the case, but it feels, again, a bit like the execution doesn't go a whole lot longer than the concept. Still, I'd rather have it than not, so.

                                    6) The Keepers of the Source: If you asked me what the second choice for old compacts to revive for this books should've been, I would've argued the Promethean Brotherhood, but that's a distinctly me problem because I hate the bands you like and there's a lot to like about the Keepers. The conflict of pacifism as an ideal against the needs that violence address, all the questions that go around the subject of defending land contrasted with commiting violence against communities doubly complicated by the metaphors of monstrosity, the softcore occultism that gets into questions of the place for concepts validity in terms of spiritual expressions and satisfactions, as well as questions about the cannibalization of legit folk practice into the commercial forms offered with New Age movements-the Keepers have a lot of things going on that make them likable, actionable, and compelling. I could never really personally grok it, though, and I was vaguely hoping there'd be a sharper presentation to actually get me hooked, but, and I say this as praise, the write-up's as good as it was in Witch Hunters. That means you have a really solid compact that still unfortunately doesn't do anything for me, but hey, it can't always be about me.

                                    5) The Eden Covenant: I disagree with Lostlight a fair number of times, and this was one of those that made it clear I needed to make my voice heard, because not only do I not think this compact is not all over the place, the only reason it's in the lower half of the compact list is because I have to admit that I can't see myself playing as one, a position that is only shared with the Recalibrators as this list goes. That said, the focus on dopplegangers is a very versatile focus I'm glad to see be played with, and it's juxtaposition with the power and resource brought in by the co-option with a hateful Christian Televangelist not only unifies the themes of othering that the compact is contesting with, but by neccesity forces it to dig into the myriad moral and ethical questions that should be explored with the theme. It's also a very on point of how Hunter explores ideas around the conflict of idealism against power, as the hateful evangelism forms a very real devil's bargain that's understandable and appreciable even as it manages to be reprehensible. Also, there's something brilliant and darkly hilarious that it's ultimate conflict is in conservative, capitalist Christianity's disgust at an entity whose ultimate journey is iconographized through a very significant Christian experience and practice. I know writers who use subtext, and they're all cowards indeed.

                                    4) Ashwood Abbey: Welcome back to the party, baby. Yep, it's the compact everyone loves to hate, and speaking as someone who has had a long road that started with loathing them to loving to loathe them, I'm glad to see Hunter stick to it's thematic guns on them. That said, they are the Abbey, and of all of the organizations brought back that really needed to incorporate as much related text from the supplements, it was the Abbey, which makes it disappointing that it's mostly just a cleaned up version of the First Edition Core's take on them, though appreciably being more clear that they do have lines they don't cross about it. On the other hand, the Abbey's power is one of those things that just comes out of the gate, and love them or hate them, would play them or would swear off anyone who would play them, the Abbey continues to do what they've always done been-be in-your-face transgressive, make you confront yourself, have more fun hunting monsters than you are, and look damn good doing it.

                                    3) Crimson Halo: Sex work is work, we love to see a prostitute gack a motherfucker. One could effectively make the argument that the Crimson Halo is to the Maiden's Blood Sisterhood what Night Watch is to The Union, and one would be right, and the more you grok everything in that, the more you understand how fucking badass this compact is. Really, I don't have a whole lot to say other than we stan, Yas Queen, Slay.

                                    2) Night Watch: Oh hey, look at that, who would've guessed. Night Watch being a choice supplemental compact to be restored is not surprising to me at all, given how strong and good a concept it was in the first books, and how very neccesary it was for engaging with class issues that the corebook was not always good at addressing. The revamp here is also the only one that does a lot to improve on it's overall concept, namely because it's factions are much more practical and utilitarian than the olds ones which were effectively just another form Status, and thus more useful for charatcer creation and single-roganization party composition. I do agree with the grouse about the artwork-while I can see an argument being made that it doesn't want to lean on the association of minorities with crime, given Night Watch's emphasis on marginalized communities needing to come together for mutual defense and getting resources however they can, I think that argument falls flat and the representation would be a great substantive approach-but otherwise, yeah, love to see the street angels back.

                                    1) Las Guadañas: This is probably some kind of very particular stereotype I should probably have more of a problem with, but a) I'm not sure what that stereotype is beyond Robert Rodriguez movies, and b) this shit is my jam, okay? There's just something to the death-cult-criminal badass acting as violent monster hunter and psychopomp that is just really fucking cool, and for as simple of the write up is, it drips cool for me. Having been raised on the US/Mexican border and having very strong opinions about how my peers get treated in today's society, I've also got a bias for Hispanic and Latinx representation, even if the criminal element and focus on Santa Muerta is kind of really boring (arguably bad) association. If I were to leverage a complaint against them, it might that it steps a little too much on the toes of the Jagged Crescent in the Ascending Ones, particularly since psychopompery is also a giant part of their makeup, but that's honestly a nitpick and I'd rather have a blast playing one in any situation that would reasonably call for it. Hasta la muerte, compadres. *
