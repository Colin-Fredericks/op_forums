            So I haven't laid out a similar listicle of my thoughts and feelings on the TtF Conspiracies, but it was long enough since the last time that I decided that, hey, we'll wait until the book was now publicly available.

            So on that note, Tending the Flame is now available on DriveThruRPG in both PDF and Print options! So to help create motives for buying it, let's talk Conspiracies.

            8) The White Hare Society: Whenever I think I'm doing the White Hare Society dirty by placing them at the bottom, I remind myself I started the section on them with "otherworldly conquistadors" and ended on "we explore otherworlds"-not "otherworldly explorers", just "we explore otherworlds. To borrow from a complaint about some of the compacts, the conspiracies whole starts and ends with it's concept, which is weird for something that has an admixture of "otherworldly conquistadors", "Alice in Wonderland/Mirrorland as organizational basis", and "going native" raised in it's write up. It's endowment is also another case that starts promising and ends up muddled, starting with adjusting themselves to their otherworlds in a way that also wrecks their minds, and ends on kind of coming down as an Elixir knock-off by way of Bioshock (and I suspect Dark Souls, but I can't pin it). I'm loathe to readily name something as bad, and I wouldn't go so far as to call the White Hare Society as such, but for something that was on the edge of Columbus founds Rapture in Wonderland, it's kind of bland, and that has to be noted.

            7) Watchers: This should be my jam-I'm down for recurring cycles and reincarnation as major motifs, and I'm always in the corner of Mummy's metaphysics and cosmology, and I am in the camp that wish Fable 3's second half had been better designed, and the Watchers are that in spades. But again, the idea never moves past the concept of accessing past lives, and while arguably it's Endowment is more coherent than the White Hare Society, it also doesn't have a lot of spice to it. Again not bad, and it doesn't promise so much it bugs me like the Hares, but it's kind of just there.

            6) Asclepeion: LostLight highlighted the dream medics as their take on the most complete idea of a conspiracy, and while I disagree, I completely get where they're coming from. The dream medics are a fascinating concept that's pretty well executed, and really feels like a sustained network. That said, I feel like the idea falls a little short in explanation-the Asclepeion play more like "sleepwalking healer-monks smiting the undead" on the whole, and while the dedication to their role as healers, it is their powerset that where I take the most issue with them-well, that and the design of the conspiracy betrays what I regard as a failing of Hunter 2nd Edition on the whole, but we're not here about that. For the most part, Enkiomesis is a weird combo of being kind of over-powered and narrow at the same time. You might be thinking about the way that it lets the medics hit the undead like a truck, and yeah, these guys wreck shop in graveyards, but honestly, I mean more that the healing itself is really powerful-having one of these guys on the scene can make threats need to step up a bit to keep being threats. But there's also just that everything these guys can do is based around healing? I think some people will hear "dream-medic" and wonder if we're going to see them be a peer to the Merrick Institute, or possibly pull some Beast shenanigans by dragging dreams spaces out into the waking world, but nope, just healing. Still, we're getting really solid here, so good.

            5) Operation Nebula: The only reason they're on this side of the divide of 8 is because I know I will always basically just slap them on as part of another conspiracy because of an arbitrary decision I made years ago, which means it's a me problem keeping them so low-but this is my list, so. Quite simply, this conspiracy slaps. The decision to settle on "aliens" as a particular enemy is an inspired idea, on the grounds that it's really versatile in what it means-the author interpreted it as all monsters, but I had a ready read of them being demons and changelings, and the fact that you can probably argue up and down that scale, or even to different ends, means that you have a lot of versatility in how to handle them both in the game and out of them, and leads to a lot internal and external dynamics that makes for good storytelling. The choices of factions is absolutely solidly playable as character archetypes, and the entire thing being wrapped up in the paranoia and mechanics of conspiracy theories just tells you everything you need to know about how this groups operates, up to and including a real sense that there's skeletons in the closet regarding the central node of coordination for the Conspiracy. Xenotechnology is also brilliant, not only because this is exactly how you want to get into the power and frustrations of working with alien technology, but because it feeds right back into the feel of the whole conspiracy. The entire thing works so well I would say anyone asking what conspiracy I could possibly throw them in with to be worth it has a valid question, and my answer is only going to piss them off.

            Because it's Task Force Valkyrie. Because I decided they had an alien thing because of Roswell really opening doors for them on the subject.

            Look, I never claimed this was a good idea, just one that I was always going to do.

            4) The Engimatics: Man, what a bunch of weaselly, up their own ass bunch of artistic hack sell-outs, trying to pretend they don't really bother anyone or anything, just chasing their muse and trying to keep a rein on their masterworks, like they aren't hunters violating the privacy of monster's lives or the sanctity of other people's free will.

            I kind of love them. I'm not entirely sure if it reaches beyond they are a bunch of weaselly, up their own ass bunch of artistic hack sell-outs. Given that I readily believe them to be a Deceived project probably goes a long way in that direction, given the Deceived are probably one of my top favorite antagonists in Chronicles. Inspiration also just slaps, with the base mechanic being extremely flavorful and all the Masterworks being equal parts delightfully messed up and darkly utilitarian. The conspiracy on the whole is great for opening up Hunter's conversation on what harm is and what extent and manner can it be justified with before it goes too far. It just, it speaks to me, as a cursed piece of literature should do.

            3) Campion Wildlife Services: The more it's argued that this should be a compact, the more I love this Conspiracy-which is weird, because I get and even agree with the complaints. In a lot of ways, this feels really small scale-even the endowment feels mostly like an exaggeration of what the average joe would think most animal control does have to deal with animals, and it's hard to imagine this being of a particular scale. But I dunno, I just really love this conspiracy. Maybe it's because, even if it feels like a compact, it feels like a 1st Edition compact-something in the flavor hits in a way a lot of 2nd Edition doesn't. Maybe it's because they're the closest organization to being the most morally upright, or at least morally uncomplicated, in the entire Hunter canon, and the fact I have to add qualifiers like "most" or "least" does a lot to explain how such a simple idea can become complicated real quick and how the morality that Hunter explores actually works. Maybe it's because I do recognize that, while it's not Advanced Armory levels of crazy, there's absolutely a step up here compared to it's real world counterparts. Mostly, I kind of get a feeling that it's like "the little conspiracy that could", where the more it seems like it should be a compact, the more you want to root for it as a conspiracy-and I think that blurring of how those words are used it mostly a net positive. Keep it up Campion, and don't let anyone knock you down.

            2) Aegis Kai Doru:If you're thinking they got this spot because it's riding on the high of all of it's other material, you'd be right. That should lead to your next question, being "why is it not number 1, then?" which I'm happy to explain. Out of the four organizations that got updated to 2nd Edition, AKD is not the one that needed the most updates, by a long shot, but there's a lot of quality of life adjustments that I'm disappointed in not seeing. On the mechanical front, things like the Zeus Stone acting as a magical RFID network for Relics is one of those things C&C came up with that seems like it should just be baked into the conspiracy from here on (even if you don't want to waste your space on the Zeus Stone as an actual Relic), and my supposition that they wanted to hold off on introducing the AKD before giving some serious thought on how to square their conflict with witches has only yielded the one change (which I'm positive about, but we'll get back to that). On the "fluff", I find it disappointing they weren't able to better incorporate the conspiracy's skeletons into the text, particularly that the Circle and highest heads of AKD want to become wizards again. And also, the selection of Relics was lackluster, not even going over some of the most interesting of older Relic selection and only adding Wonder Woman's lasso as a new item. That said, there is one change that warrant the high ranking in my opinion, and that's by expanding the oath from mages and werewolves to those who work magic and shifters- a much broader focus that is useful on a lot of front and introduces a lot of dynamics, not to mention that it handily accomodates arguments for both the Arisen and Beasts to be regarded as enemies of note to AKD. That, and all the bias for previous material, is what lands it here.

            1) Survivor's Club: I want to argue that someone enjoyed Scream 6 as much as I did, but that's robbing Peter to market Paul, to murder the saying. That asides, this is just my jam. Smart enough to not just stop at the final girl trope, but to connect towards a general survivorship, I think you could argue that this was the conspiracy people expected with Slasher, but man alive am I glad it let this notion stew a little bit, and has the contrast to VASCU. Maybe it particularly hits because I'm used to people who suffered from horrible action then turning around for whatever reason and saying that that horrible action may be justified looking at other people, but the particulars of survivor's running the risk of buying into Slasher mentality, and even being empowered by briefly connecting to that same darkness is just powerful for me. You honestly can't make a more summary case of what Hunter's about than with the Survivor's Club, trying to overcome trauma while still being shaped by it, and the wrestling match of not letting that darkness define you too much without denying it's reality, and what that should mean about how you deal with more of the same going out there-like, the Survivor's Club is just pitch perfect encapsulation. It's also fucking cool as well as a mechanical tool for characters even before you get to the Horror Within. It's really just my jam, and hopefully good reason for you to grab Tending the Flame.

                I like Las Guadañas in that I like having a criminal compact that aren't about taking out competition from monsters, but actually see their hunting as sacred. I know there are the Ascending Ones too, but the criminal element is only part of them.

                I like having a group that focusses on humanlike monsters, especially since the focus of the second edition has shifted away from that a bit. The Eden Covenant are cock-ends, though.

                Crimson Halo are great.

                I also like the Survivor's Club, though I would have liked the Maiden's Blood Sisterhood to be back too. I mean have both and keep the relationship between them, because having two hunter groups related in that way would be interesting.

                The Enigmatics seem more like a weird cult than a hunter group, but I guess we don't have a Cultist: the Mania line for them to go in.

                Cambion Animal Control sound like they're fighting Deviant conspiracies and humanely putting Manticores down. Which sounds great until you realise they might be putting Deviants down too.

                I like that some the Endowments feel like the same sort of weirdness as Advanced Armoury, Elixirs, or Thaumotech, just different in the details because those researching them had different goals.
