    I get the feeling from the 1e Core writeup that the only Hunters in Cheiron are the Field Projects Division operatives, not the upper management, not even the scientists or other pieces of standard business stuff. And the 5 sample hunters are not really happy about being a field agent for Chieron, they just don't have a choice anymore (one of them isn't against hurting people, just against the control that is held over him).

    I think field agents for Chieron should have issues with The Code, they are barely on the same side as other Hunters when they are forced to do what their bosses say (who might secretly be part of an ancient cult, etc.).
    I thought about that, but frankly that doesn't change much. Even if they aren't the upper management, they will still be encouraged to go against the Code because they are supposed to obey orders. And while I do agree it would make sense for some of them to have Breaking Points because of all the horrible stuff they are ordered to do, it's entirely possible some of the field agents the Cheiron Group recruits are sociopaths to begin with, or that some of them end up loosening their standards by modifying the Code as a way to cope with what they do. So they should still be allowed to modify more than one Tenet. And they are by far the group for who the social penalty makes the most sense, because they are second only to the Ashwood when it comes to being a sick hunter group.

                                                    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post

                                                    Let's roll with it - I start with my most loved/hated group - Malleus Maleficarum - here is analysis:
                                                    The MM always struck me as having a very broad definition of "witch". The Code says Endowed Hunters never count as Monsters, the MM says that all power comes from either God or the Devil, and they alone get their power from God. So any Endowed Hunter who's powers look magical (and doesn't have the Ghoul in Chief covering for them) would likely get on their bad side.

                                                    So I'd say definitely a yes to causing harm to a person, killing a person. I'd also say that while a code that lets you kill mortals is definitely a fast track to Slasherdom, treating Endowed Hunters as monsters should be safer. It's just not Hunter if you can't have a Conspiracy vs Conspiracy war now and again.

                                                    Anyway: VALKARIE

                                                    * Causing significant harm to a person - Yes: They've gone after other hunters before.
                                                    * Allowing harm to come to a person in pursuit of your Vigil - I get the feeling they're a bit trigger happy with the big guns. But I'd say that should be a Breaking Point for them.
                                                    * Refusing aid to a fellow hunter in need - Yes: more inter-hunter conflict.
                                                    * Killing a person - Yes: more inter-hunter conflict.
                                                    * Learning that a monster harmed a person when you could have done something to stop it but didn’t - There's that whole thing about the beaurocracy surrounding Vampires, but perhaps that should be a breaking point regardless.
                                                    * Putting the well-being or autonomy of a monster over that of a person - No
                                                    * Gaining or bestowing power from an obviously monstrous source - IMO: Advanced Armory is not an obviously monstrous source.
                                                    * Betraying a fellow hunter - I'd say no. VALKARIE wont betray your trust, they'll shoot you in the front.

                                                    So I'd say three at a minimum, all of which stem from the source VALKARIE has canonically fought rival Hunters. Which makes perfect sense. An armed group performing extra-judicial killings on American Soil or Endowed Russian Spies should get the government's attention, and if they're using supernatural tricks to do it then VALKARIE has jurisdiction. It doesn't matter that Elixirs are an Endowment, VALKARIE knows how to fight people with strange powers, most other government departments do not.


                                                    The Ascending Ones

                                                    * Causing significant harm to a person - Yes: They sell drugs.
                                                    * Allowing harm to come to a person in pursuit of your Vigil - Yes. If you're going to negociate with vampires or beasts you have to compromise when it comes to feeding. Also the drugs.
                                                    * Refusing aid to a fellow hunter in need - Yes: If they've negotiated a great peace that keeps their protectees safe and another hunter is about to destroy it by being a kill-em-all fanatic they Ascending Ones won't be helping them.
                                                    * Killing a person - Probably. They must get into the occasional turf war with rival drug dealers. But it's likely a Breaking Point when someone dies.
                                                    * Learning that a monster harmed a person when you could have done something to stop it but didn’t - No. Protecting people is what they're all about.
                                                    * Putting the well-being or autonomy of a monster over that of a person - I could see it happen. They're diplomats so they might put a friend among the monsters first.
                                                    * Gaining or bestowing power from an obviously monstrous source - Yes. I think Elixirs count.
                                                    * Betraying a fellow hunter - Maybe, realpolitik is a dirty game.

                                                    So there's only one definite no there. It's all maybes or yes.

                                                    Originally posted by ElvesofZion View Post
                                                    Isn't that just a bluebook game then though? Maybe with Risking Willpower and Tactics added in? Why does the Hunter book need to do that for you? you already can play that game.
                                                    Well, it is a game about playing a monster hunter Hunter's greatest strength has always been it's diversity, the fact that it always supported so many different kinds of monster hunters. That diversity is what I want to see preserved in 2ED and Hunter's who're pretty much regular guys but proactively train for the day a monster rolls into town are part of that diversity.

                                                        I'll try the Ascending Ones:

                                                        Causing significant harm to a person: They run drugs.

                                                        Allowing harm to come to a person in pursuit of your Vigil: They run drugs.

                                                        Causing a person to suffer a breaking point from exposure to the supernatural: I think they're ok there.

                                                        Refusing aid to a fellow hunter in need: Maybe? I can see KR's argument.

                                                        Killing a person: They run drugs.

                                                        Learning that a monster harmed a person when you could have done something to stop it but didn’t: They engage in diplomacy when they can, which entails leaving certain monsters alone if they think a deal has been struck - that leaves them vulnerable to this happening.

                                                        Putting the well-being or autonomy of a monster over that of a person: I don't think their diplomacy extends to this.

                                                        Gaining or bestowing power from an obviously monstrous source: I'd say they're good here.

                                                        Betraying a fellow hunter: According to their 1st Edition writeup, they think the Lucifuge should join their fellow monsters in Hell once they're no longer useful. But that has the dodge that once they're betraying, they don't consider the Lucifuge fellow hunters any more.


                                                        So they're vulnerable to certain things happening against the Code, and directly violate it three times over - but for the same reason each time.


                                                        And the Loyalists of Thule:

                                                        Causing significant harm to a person: Many of them have done this in the past and the Vigil is about making up for it; so I'd say they're ok.

                                                        Allowing harm to come to a person in pursuit of your Vigil: This is a risk - they're secretive to a fault, and careless secrets can cost lives as easily as careless talk.

                                                        Causing a person to suffer a breaking point from exposure to the supernatural: Everything about them makes this unlikely.

                                                        Refusing aid to a fellow hunter in need: Again, secretive enough that they'll sometimes do this.

                                                        Killing a person: Do Nazis count? (Serious question, really - they do take time out of the Vigil to fight Nazis).

                                                        Learning that a monster harmed a person when you could have done something to stop it but didn’t: Probably ok.

                                                        Putting the well-being or autonomy of a monster over that of a person: Probably ok.

                                                        Gaining or bestowing power from an obviously monstrous source: Probably ok.

                                                        Betraying a fellow hunter: Does obsessively keeping secrets from them count?

                                                        ------------------------

                                                        I see the point of a universalised Code - or a point to it, I don't know if it's the actual reason. The Integritype stat represents the psychological question no member of that species can ever get away from. How the Vigil affects you is that for Hunters. But Integrity can manage that largely on its own.

                                                        And I like the way in the first edition that the Code functioned in a way as a hunter's z-splat. All things considered, I'd rather keep that.

                                                        Or, take a leaf from Deviant and have two Integritypes: Integrity to represent holding onto the core of yourself, and Code, representing the question of "are monsters people." And for the latter, perhaps structure it like Harmony, with "yes" and "no" marking the ends of the scale, and breaking points leading in both directions.
