    Civil Wars

    05-16-2015, 05:54 AM
    So, I am re-reading Compacts & Conspiracies, and I've noticed that there are many potential group breakdowns and Civil Wars. The Secret Keepers from Network-0, the Rationalist vs the Open Minded in Null Mysteriis, the Sword vs the Scroll in the AKD, the Ascending Ones as whole, the Lucfuge (who may have broken once from the L'Enfant Diabolique) with the Truth vs and the Denial (who may reveal that not of them share the same bloodline), Task Force VALKYRIE who may turn upon itself and of course- the MM, whose Civil War may effect the other orgs more then they expect.

    So- how would you deal with those Civil Wars? Which would come true? Who will survive? Are the groups going to turn into a number of smaller ones, one would devour the other or that the organization itself would fall apart as monsters would use the opportunity to get rid of their old enemies? And most important then all- how would does Civil Wars may effect the other members of the Vigil- for as older groups may fall, new ones can take their place.

    your thoughts on the matter?

      The Rationalist vs Open Minds was a bit silly IMO. It would probably end up with them splitting into two groups with a lot of hurt tempers and no violence. It's not like they actually have much money or resources to fight for.

      My AKD drops the fanaticism angle in favour of an Da Vinci Code like focus on the long game, secrets behind historical events, and a proactive mission to find and reclaim the Relics that will let them reclaim their lost magic. So no civil war there.

      The MM on the other hand I have a personal future cannon for.

      Gallaher is the Irish Archbishop Emeritus of New York City; so I'd say his powerbase is strongest in the USA, Canada and Ireland. When the civil war strikes the Brotherhood of St. Athanasius scores major victories in those areas. The Order of St. Longinus is crushed, it's members either killed or driven from the region. The Order of St. Ambrose is also destroyed, but with far less zeal, most of it's members survive with some being conscripted into the Brotherhood of St. Athanasius but most of them successfuly flee the country while the Athanasians are occupied with the Order of St. Longinus. However without the backing of the church or a restraining hand on the fanacticism the Brotherhood draws unwanted attention from VALKARIE and monsters. It is destroyed within a decade.

      However there is a Dark Horse victory. A lot of Ambrosians don't want to flee their country or join the Brotherhood of St. Athanasius. Which means a sizeable amount of Irish Ambrosians end up in the Sons of CuChulainn. Since these are Athanasiusans they naturally join the Song of Erin. Still stinging from the Athanasius betrayal of the church, and of Catholic values, the ex-Ambrosians fall in love with the idea of using oaths to keep a conspiracy true to it's purpose and develop a hybrid Endowment (think oaths as a conduit for divine power). As the Brotherhood of St. Athanasius looses ground they're poised to fill much of the void.

                                                      Originally posted by Aiden View Post
                                                      Basically, if it exists and it is repeatable in some fashion then you can apply the scientific method to it, full stop.
                                                      It doesn't even have to be repeatable! It just has to affect something we can observe. See: the Big Bang, imaginary time, and prediction of the Higgs effect.

                                                      Heck, imaginary time is a great example - we used it because it makes singularity math work, and now it's looking like it's actually how singularities really work. If scientists have to add superfluous dimensions and universes to make their math work, they will, and when it turns out those superfluous dimensions and universes exist, they will be right there, shining a light down at the monsters and explaining to them exactly how they work, because they predicted it all.

                                                      In fact, that's a great angle.

                                                      The Eye (● to ●●●●●)
                                                      The true benefit of science is its ability to make predictions based upon what has come before. As you've hunter, you've gathered a wide body of knowledge. There are stories you don't believe, but you know enough about strange things not to laugh at them. Those in many practical science fields such as biology, geology, and paleontology have a tendency to refer to this as “the eye,” which emerges very quickly over the course of a student's career. To the untrained, the ability to pick out a pterosaur bone fragment out of a pile of postage-stamp sized stones or the ability to find a particular type of quartz in a pile of dirty rocks is outright mystical, and the ability to spot something odd and make extremely focused educated guesses is a particularly potent tool in a hunter's arsenal. At the beginning of a scene, you may roll Wits + Science + The Eye to quickly process and collate information you've pieced together from fragmentary reports and precious research. Successes on this roll grants you a pool of dice you can use in Tactics or Perception rolls to notice supernatural details as your ability to predict changes in the behavior of elements in that scene. The hunter can use these dice herself or gift them to nearby hunters, and may either only spend a single die on any given roll, or empty the entire pool.

                                                      Science isn't a cudgel or a wrench that stops working the instant you find a flathead screw - it's that little shapeshifting critter from Treasure Planet that literally turns into whatever tool you can imagine.
