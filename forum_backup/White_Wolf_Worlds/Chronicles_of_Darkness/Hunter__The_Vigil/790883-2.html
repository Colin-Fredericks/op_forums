Sure, some of them may fall too far, but for they hunt them too. They are the humanity at its worst- and they still manage to light a candle to light their way in the night.

                            So if you ask about how the Abbey contribute to Hunter's moral ambiguity as whole, it is by showing you just how far you can fall before you can no longer be considered as an hunter. Under it all the Abbey can be used to house hunters who are in it for their own reason and not for any ideaology or responsibility.

                                Some further thoughts.

                                Slashers don't actually fit the Abbey that well. A Slasher is defined by compulsion, and being a creature of very strict habits. Look at the Legend Undertaking for the most obvious example, or VASCU Case study : “None”. The Abbey strike me as the exact opposite, always on a quest for novelty.

                                A Slasher isn't the logical conclusion of the Abbey. The bottom of the slippery slope: First Werewolves, then Witches, then Humans. A Slasher is as far from the archetypal Abbey Hunter as it is from the archetypal Union Hunter. (Which is to say both Compacts produce Slashers).

                                Obviously you could create some Hedonistic Undertaking with a Frailty like can't use the same MO twice; but honestly that kind of feels like a Round Peg Square Hole sort of deal. Hunters are probably the most diverse splat, the ways they fall would be pretty diverse too. IMO an Abby Hunter is more likely to end up Claimed by an Infernal Demon attracted to their over indulgence of Vice than a Slasher.


                                I think this is probably my chief reason for not being too keen on Abby->Slashers. I'm very much a theme first kind of guy, and the Abby's theme is immoral hedonistic novelty seekers. Slashers are creatures of habit. These are clearly very different themes.

                                Even if the logical in-universe conclusion of the Abby's immorality is Slasherdom, I'd rather find some way to preserve that theme. Redefining immoral as "I won't let morals stop me having fun" instead of "Lets do the most depraved stuff we can!" is my way of preserving the Abby's theme. Plus, I do think it would genuinely be an improvement.

                                        As things stand now, I consider the Abbey a bit of a missed opportunity. While the other Hunter organizations have aspects that can cast them in both good and bad light, Ashwood Abbey's presentation doubled down on the negative. I think "Compacts and Conspiracies" made a step in the right direction, by showing their thrill seeking as not necessarily repugnant.

                                        There aren't many hunters who would be willing to actually socialize with the monsters or to spend the night in a haunted locale just for kicks. Trying to make friends with monsters opens up many possibilities plot-wise, which trying to kill them - doesn't. With a greater network to fund their activities, advise them on the consequences and stage an intervention should things go too far, these Hunters could accomplish successes and cause disasters unthinkable in other organisations. Each story shared with the whole of the Compact would warn the members what not to do, or more likely, how to do damage control.

                                        So far, the Abbey only gets close to tackling these themes. Their descriptions are overly focused on murder and decadence. Curiously, the Compact has potential to be one of the most diplomatic in its dealings with monsters, since once they start interacting socially with the supernatural, they appear on it's radar and can be held accountable. On the other hand, with their connections, experience and the proverbial "big stick", they can try to hold their own against manipulation. That is not to say everything is pleasant. Diplomacy can be really cutthroat, especially with monsters on one side and thrill-seekers on the other.

                                        This approach to the Vigil deserves more attention. Both expanding on the Abbey and creating a new Compact based around it are acceptable means to do so.
