    Wasted Potential

    07-14-2015, 02:31 PM
    This is something that struck me when I read The King's Raven's topic on the Counterculture USA Micro Dark Era project. Specifically, their notes about their planned Spirit Scapers compact being their personal replacement for the Keepers of the Source.

    Now, I'll be honest: I've always hated the Keepers. They just do NOT make any sense to me. Hippy hunters is one thing, but a hunter compact based on a number of people having a very specific form of Unseen Senses? I could buy a Cell with their background, but not a Compact.

    On the other hand, the Spirit Scapers DO make sense to me. They still have some fundamental flaws with their ideology - "positive" spirits being no less monstrous than negative ones means that, really, they're not going to undercut the fundamental problems of human/spirit interactions - but, at the same time, their ideology makes sense and is believable (which is more than can be said of the Keepers). It genuinely is possible to affect each world by manipulating the other, and so negative spirits like hate, rape, murder, pollution, lies, etc can be driven off and positive ones of love, peace, joy, family, etc can be attracted, and even if positive spirits are still inclined to Urge, Ride and Claim, well, they do generally make the world better through their presence, so it's not an inherently stupid idea to want to manipulate the spiritual environment to encourage their presence and dissuade the negative spirits. Their clash with werewolves and mages genuinely becomes gray, as the Spirit Scapers really are doing much the same things as these monsters are, in stark contrast to the Keepers.

    Now, anyway, the point behind this topic is this: what compacts, conspiracies, plot hooks or other general ideas do you think Hunter has that it really could have done better? The King's Raven and I have both observed the wasted potential of the "Hippy Hunters" as evidenced through the Keeprs of the Source, and TKR is fixing that in their homebrew with the Spirit Scapers, but what have you other Hunter STs fixed on as "could have been done better"?

          Thanks for the kind words VoidDrifter.

          Division Six: They don't have much VALKARIE don't, except that they're pawns of a Seer (which is something that belongs in Mage books not Hunter) and the technocracy shout outs. Looking at the picture it's clear that the book is sneering at them; which is always a bad idea. I can't see any way of redeeming them.

          Knights of Saint George: The problem I see is that these guys don't really fit into or say anything about the themes of Mage. They're just a group who hunt and kill mages. I'd much rather focus directly on the idea of Truth; with a Hunter Conspiracy that is making a coherent argument that Lies can be better than Truth.

          Aegis Kai Doru: I feel like there's a lot of focus on the least interesting parts of this group - collecting Relics, and their hatred of Werewolves and Mages. I'd rather focus on the fact that they see the long term view (they're both ancient and archeologists). And their internal philosophy which really needs to be more than just hating Werewolves and Mages. My solution would be to give them a belief that they could rebuild whatever ancient utopia they believe they came from with the right relics; and drop the grudge with monsters only being important if the monsters get between the AKD and their Relics.

          Les Mysteres: The unification of just about shamanism concept ever is basically nonsensical. I dislike the whole manipulated by spirits angle too; hunters being manipulated by monsters (in protagonist sections of Hunter books) is a deal breaker for me. I'd fix that by making them purely Voodoo; I'd keep the spirit manipulation but make it more of a risk to individuals rather than a something that's happened to the group. I'd make their conflict about them breaching the gauntlet.                                   Well... honestly, I'm of two minds when it comes to Les Mysteres.

                                  On the one hand, I agree they make no sense as a Compact. The basic idea, that religious individuals who practice in the right ways open themselves up to the very real spirits of the WoD, is certainly sound, but... we can't get two individuals from two faiths to agree in the real world. In the WoD, where A: people are bigger assholes in general, and B: these religious nuts have "proof" that their faith is the "one true faith", I certainly would consider it more likely that they'd tear each other apart than work together.

                                  On the other hand, the basic existence of Les Mysteres make a lot of sense to me. I don't mind the being manipulated by spirits thing because, honestly, that's what's going to happen. It IS possible to bind spirits, IF you have the right occultic knowledge to do so and manage to get very lucky. But when you're coming at things from the angle of "the spirits are good and holy and must be obeyed and respected", if you believe because of your faith that the spirits are inherently better than you, then you are giving the spirits an edge in your dealings with them. Even if your rituals do manage to bind them, if you let your beliefs bind you to the lies they spill and to the reality of their ecosystem, well, the ball's in their court. For the same reason, if you believe because of your faith that the spirits are good and/or a necessary part of the world's health, then of course you're going to mix up the Pure and the Forsaken - one side is revering the spirits like you do, the other is setting themselves up as superior to the spirits. You view the world through the lens of faith rather than fact, you're going to make decisions that are factually wrong.

                                  It's annoying to play the dupe, but let's be honest; the compacts & conspiracies are written up as much for NPC potential as for player potential. Les Mysteres are a PERFECT example of a hostile Hunter organization to use in a Werewolf game, or a cancer cell in a Hunter game. I'd never want to play a member of Les Mysteres, but then, I'd never want to play a member of the Malleus Maleficarium or the Long Night either, as religious-based hunters in general tend to make my guts roil.

                                                          I actually really like the Knights of St. George. Witnessing magic is damaging to the souls of the uninitiated, and it's easy to imagine the Knights as a group of individuals so deeply scarred by their interactions with the Supernal that they've turned away from it entirely, toward the Abyss or some kind of twisted Lower Depth. In a way, they fit the themes of Mage by acting as the antithesis of the Awakened; Mages boldly strive for higher truths, while the Knights are driven by fear and ignorance to destroy truth-seekers in the hopes of appeasing faceless gods that they can't begin to comprehend. They can also represent an object lesson in the consequences of hubris, both by embodying the horrifying consequences of exposing Sleepers to magic and by actively punishing Mages who practice their art too openly.

                                                          At the same time, I find the Knights sympathetic, in a sad sort of way. They're underdogs struggling (at least in their own view) to save the world from forces far beyond what they can comprehend or directly oppose. They're trying to be heroes, but they're too damaged to realize that they they became villains a long time ago.
