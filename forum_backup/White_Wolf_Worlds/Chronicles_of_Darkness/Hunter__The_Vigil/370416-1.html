                                        Interesting you'd say that, because I've always thought that Sin-Eaters are one of the easier ones to predict trends, if not specific numbers. See, people who die of old age never become Bound, because that's the one death that the geist cannot reverse. So, if we assume that includes death from things like heart/organ failure, Alzheimers and other things that are basically "body wearing down", that means you'd expect to see more opportunity (and thus, all being equal, more Bound) in places where deaths from violence, starvation etc are more common, relatively speaking. Oddly enough, those are also likely the places you'd expect to see more ghosts and suchforth.

                                        So, for example, in the city where I live in Australia, which has pretty low crime rate and very low rates of starvation and suicide and the like, you wouldn't expect to see as many as, say, a war-torn area in Africa, one of those places where the Four Horsemen like to vacation.

                                                When I run games my supernaturals tend to know of each other, but not necessarily know about each other. A vampire who owns a night club is going to rub shoulders with a lot of people, not all of whom are here for the dancing. A Werewolf who runs a pawn shop will likely meet a few ghouls, mages and hunters. You can only spend so much time in a city before you start to know the people who live in it. After 50 years a moderately social Vampire will have seen and been involved in a lot of things. The same could be said for a Werewolf, whose lifespan is practically doubled.

                                                What does this mean for hunters and supernatural populations? Just as many mages are likely to be in a shoddy garage band as a sleeper. A werewolf is just as likely to be in your yoga class as anyone else. I think that even with a small population (my current game has less than 50 supernaturals in a city of over 250,000) chances are they are somewhat connected to one another, even if it is via five degrees of separation.
