            I haven't seen it in this thread yet, but in the Summoners book there's a goetia (oops, mixed up my ephemerals) called Uther's Bliss, a personification of requited love. Here's the excerpt about it:
            " Facing down rogue Banishers and rampaging spirits was never the hard part for Uther, Thyrsus and Banner Warden of the Adamantine Arrow. No, that was the years-long struggle to work up enough courage to finally come out to Slingshot and confess that his feelings for the Enchanter went deeper than just friendship. The moments leading up to that revelation were the longest and most difficult of the Shaman’s life. When Slingshot replied with a passionate kiss, though, Uther knew that this uphill battle against his own fear was, at long last, over. "

            If you want more characters who are Gender and Sexual Minorities, just add it to their character. Not every gay character had a tough coming out and is a flag-wearing, pride-parade organizer. Sometimes you're just a normal person who is gay, came out, and that was that. Sometimes you don't even have to mention it in the backstory. It's just a fact. Yeah, it's a potential avenue for backstory, but sometimes you just want a gay character and being gay is a minor facet of his personality. Another good way is to make LGBT+ characters just exist if you're the ST for the session. The Prince's Sheriff has a human lover that you're going to blackmail him with to get him to leave your group alone. The neighboring pack alpha is blessed by Luna and switches physical gender as they please. If you aren't the ST, ask them about including these kinds of changes to your setting. These are fantasy games, and making your setting more open to LGBT+ characters (playable and non) is just realization that characters can be anything you want, and personality traits that flesh your characters out should not be punished with discrimination.

                                                I also feel like “it never came up because it was never relevant to the story” is a poor excuse, because the author is in full control of what is and isn’t relevant to the story. Treating queer characters as people is absolutely the key, but treating them as people doesn’t have to mean hiding the fact that they’re queer.
