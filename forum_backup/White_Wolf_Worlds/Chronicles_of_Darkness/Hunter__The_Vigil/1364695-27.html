                    I believe the public face of VASCU is a "bioengineering research group" called the Vanguard Initiative while the actual core to VASCU is known as the Vanguard Institute. The Initiative is mentioned three times in the write-up and is referred to as a "front" while the Institute is mentioned several times and seems to refer to the hunter organization that is VASCU.                     So with the final organizations previewed (barring the Ashwood Abbey and Aegis Kai Doru create-your-own-organization examples), I can confirm some of my own suspicions and predictions about the Compacts and Conspiracies. Close to all of the 1E organizations are dealing with some sort of upheaval or crisis of their own that is threatening the stability of their organization (with the only groups that have little to no internal uproar being the Union and, surprisingly to me, the Long Night. In contrast, SWORN, Nine Stars, and the Council of Bones seem rather stable in comparison (which I'd attribute to word count). The Endowments are what really caught my attention as there is no longer a power hierarchy or division in certain Endowments like Elixirs, Advanced Armory, Teleinformatics, etc. The number of powers are also rather scant, sitting in the range of 7-9 powers per Endowment whereas 1E averaged somewhere of 9-12 for each organization barring additions from other sourcebooks. I do hope that the final adds a few more powers to help offer more diversity for these organizations.

VASCU agents that pursue the explicitly supernatural non-humans do it without official VASCU sanction and cannot be use their association with VASCU for it. Implicitly, rogue VASCU agents hunting down the supernatural tend to do so violently.

                            Other cases concern killers who appear to be creatures from folklore. Some agents think it’s their job to bring such beasts to trial, but when a killer grows into a nine-foot tall monster (...), arraignment might be the last thing on most agents’ minds. If a monster can’t pass for human, (...) it’s up to the chain of command to decide whether to destroy the creature or lock it in Lansing.

                            An agent who wants to turn “monster hunter” has to do it in her own time, and be very careful that her extracurricular activities don’t dump the unit in a whole bunch of trouble. (...) Vanguard has common psychological profiles for the most populous supernatural creatures, but that’s it.                             It reads as if the "rules don't apply" line is more of a Hunter mindset rather than an organizational mindset, especially as the line comes after bemoaning the fact that VASCU needs to follow legal channels for their cases with non-monsters. An agent can go ahead and chase down a vampire bleeding victims dry in a backalley but it would be close to impossible to guarantee that the vampire would be able to be brought to legal justice via the system. VASCU won't reprimand a specific agent should that agent take justice into their own hands against something that isn't human but they are not going to officially support their actions. There are some instances of VASCU managing to contain a non-human supernatural (specifically the one vampire in Lansang which is, in and of itself, noted as an exception) but VASCU aims more towards instances of spree or serial murders, which is why their attention is more on Slashers and mundane serial killers.

                                                            Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post
                                                            ArcaneArts, I'm just wondering, you liking the game so far?
                                                            -Ish?

                                                            The thing of the matter is that it hits a lot of the major notes that I wanted it to hit-Hunter has not changed it's fundamental nature-but there's a lot of details that are not quite there.

                                                            The two big details I can name that hit my (personal) dissonance with it are the much more definitive nature of the Lucifuge's origins and VASCU's move over into private investigator against them as FBI, and these are equal and opposite directions-the former moving away from the murkier, mixed, complicated, and ultimately unanswerable metaphysics that is one of the things I love most about Chronicles (And Also Beast, because that game starts off Definite and Ends Strange, but that's a side tangent), and the latter for moving away from the real world deep criticism that was cleverly and subversively baked into the presentation of material. Hunter Second, on both counts, does move away from the elements of monstrosity that I feel make it fascinating, and I do personally feel like this version is actually legitimately weaker against it's predecessor for both it's weirdness and potential in terms of the supernatural, and it's critical examination of human nature in groups in a way that is better understood than simply saying "herd mentality".

                                                            I list those two as my Major Points, but it honestly is just emblematic of concerns that I'm seeing baked across the text, a distancing from that willingness to be murky and messy, and at the same time a distancing from a willingness to be really sharply critical. It rounds it's edges, so to speak.

                                                            Now this all amounts to basically a personal bitching. Hunter Second, so far, is still really good, delivering a lot of what is to like about Hunter on the whole. It is solid and cohesive, it is willing to promote itself while still solidly alluding to the flaws of the splat in the same way any of the Second Editions have been, and in particular while it has not gotten as critical as, say, C&C could get at times, or even the original core, it still is very good at looking at human nature on various levels and balancing human awfulness with human heroism and the fact that that's a complicated balance.

                                                            There's also a part of me that is both grateful and bummed out that it's basically not #iHunt, because on the one hand I want it to be more like #iHunt, but on the other hand, if it was, then I wouldn't have #iHunt.

                                                            So, I mean, so far? Yeah, it's solid, and I'm very 'kay with it.

                                                            But I'm not going to deny it's also not quite the Hunter I fell in love with.

                                                            Best thing about me though, and this Hunter, is that is a very workable problem. We can disagree, but baby we can work things out.
