    Supers hunting Slashers

    09-21-2015, 01:55 AM
    First of all, I apologise if this should actually be on another board, this just seemed the best place to put it.

    Alright, we all know from other threads here that slashers can threaten other supernaturals, and that they can scare them. What I'm curious about is this: if other supernaturals took it into their heads to go hunting slashers, how well would they fare?

    Let's be honest: the general assumption of the World of Darkness is that open war doesn't erupt because every faction understands the principles of live and let live towards each other. As a whole, they try to avoid getting in each other's way, because the nights are hard and dangerous enough, yeah? But slashers don't get that. Slashers have never been very good at refraining from pissing in the soup, as the saying goes.

    They make waves. They're disruptive. They kill people - possibly people belonging to or cherished by the children of the night. They spawn angry ghosts, fuel spirits of murder/pain/bloodshed/fear, and generally pollute the spiritual landscape around them. They stir up the police and the media. They make life even harder for "honest" supernaturals.

    Furthermore, there's a very poignant line in the Draugr entry in Wicked Dead, about how it's not often that vampires get to feel righteous, but hunting bestial Lust-driven draugr is one of those times. Well, there's something very similar in line when it comes to hunting down expies of Michael Myers, Freddy Krueger, Candyman, Leatherface and Jason Vorhees.

    So. The situation could be a Slasher has made a very poor choice in its next victims. Or it could be an analogue to that German film "M", where the children of the night are banding together to destroy a Slasher who is making too many waves. Or it could be a NWoD Monster Mash take on that comic "Hack Slash", about a group dedicated to hunting and killing Slashers. What skills can the various creatures of the night offer up to making hunting down and ending Slashers more plausible?

      Changelings can enter the dreams of a slasher. Sure, it is probably a risky maneuver, especially when dealing with masks, but it is a good way to gain valuable information on the slasher in question (its plans, motives, etc.). Some of the contracts are a good boon in tracking them down as well. For example, there are contracts that allow a Changeling to talk with objects and unless the slasher is aware of that fact, getting a good image of the slasher just by visiting a dump site is almost guaranteed. Whether or not their ability to travel the Hedge is a boon, depends a bit on the situation. The Hedge is a great tool to get out of Dodge, and it could help entering a hard to reach place (assuming the characters find a good path - not always easy). Come to think of it, Freddy Krueger sounds like a great opponent for a group of Changelings since it is all in a dream, although the Changelings are bound to be a bit sturdier than the poor victims in the movies since they are just as good in dream manipulation as Freddy

          Yep. Super vs. Slasher is a very different thing from mortal vs. Slasher... although, admittedly, anything over tier 1 (and even then, depends on the Cell) is very different to "innocents (as in, ignorant of the darker world) vs Slashers" theme of the actual films that inspired them.

          Anyway, nice commentary on Changelings. Some input from me...

          To me, it seems like the two Supers most suited for fighting Slashers, and most focused against Slashers, are Werewolves and Sin-Eaters.

          Both races are not only clued into methods of tracking Slashers that far surpass human police agents, but they are actively receptive to the "wakes" Slashers make. It's very hard to ignore that a psycho killer has set herself up in your neighborhood when you are either constantly fighting the murder spirits she spawns/attracts or are being screamed at constantly by the vengeful ghosts of her victims. It's also pretty easy to find the evidence when you just have to A: ask the ghost of her victims, or B: look for the swarm of murder spirits.

          Sin-eaters can attack Slashers from Twilight, either directly or by talking to the ghosts that the Slasher made. As Mortal Remains touches on, it's very, very hard for a loony killer to meaningfully assault a Sin-Eater, especially if he's leading the charge with angry ghosts.

          Werewolves are, to put it bluntly, murder machines. A pack of five or six raging Gauru will make mincemeat out of virtually anything they catch ahold of. Supernatural "bruiser" type Undertakings like the Mask can handle it a little better, but "face" types and most Scourges are pretty much kibble.

              Depends on the supernatural in question. A vampire could just mind control the guy. Dominate 1 means combat ends before it starts. Werewolves are used to hunting more dangerous prey than a Slasher. Mages would slaughter - few things can match a prepared mage, and if they're going hunting, they'll be prepared. Prometheans and sin-eaters laugh off damage. Changelings hunt through dreams and illusions. Mummies... hahahahahahhahaha. Demon? Yeah, starting character demonic embeds are more powerful than a highly experienced Slasher - and that doesn't include Demon Forms. Beasts would... make friends with the Slashers (hello automatic Good Impression), then possibly backstabbery.

              And all that isn't taking into account elders, alphas, masters, court rulers, founders, or cypher masters. Slashers are still humans - the upper bounds of their strength is still far below what a supernatural can achieve at high levels.

              Slashers are generally a bit stronger than hunters, and hunters can threaten supernaturals. However, hunters tend to be easy prey when the supernaturals start hunting them back. You need to be clever, resourceful, and in control to beat out a supernatural without any powers of your own. If the supernaturals are the ones being resourceful and in control, however? The shoe is on the other foot.

              Let's be honest: the general assumption of the World of Darkness is that open war doesn't erupt because every faction understands the principles of live and let live towards each other. As a whole, they try to avoid getting in each other's way, because the nights are hard and dangerous enough, yeah? But slashers don't get that. Slashers have never been very good at refraining from pissing in the soup, as the saying goes.
              Most supernaturals don't go to war because they dont' know about each other (the Masquerade hides vamps from more than just humanity), and even if they did, they generally don't share any interests. Wars are fought over idealology and resources. Vampires, sin-eaters, and changelings don't deal with the Hisil, so why should a werewolf care about them? Demons are hyper focused on their war with God; why do they care about mummies collecting artifacts? Its not mutually assured destruction stopping a war, its apathy. With the exception of mages, hunters, and beasts, everyone is generally internally focused. And, even in the case of mages and beasts, they're more focused on studying/meeting the other supernaturals than any policing action.

              Nothing against why supers would go after a slasher, but the war reasons struck me as funny.

                      Most of the time, the Mask's defensive ability isn't that big of a deal. Its no defense against a vampire's mind control or a grapple/feed/choke. Werewolves are good with pack coordination to take and pin someone down, not just go on a claw-killing-spree. Mages have a whole host of better options than direct damage. Same with changelings. In 1e, rocket tag was a thing. Whomever got to strike first tended to dominate the fight. Mask ability just let them shrug off that first attack to make their own. Against a supernatural, however, there are too many ways to take someone down other than pure damage, even for dedicated combat characters.

                      The hardest part of catching a Slasher is tracking them down. And Charmers / Geniuses are better at hiding than the other three, especially in plain sight. In a match of pure power, the supernaturals tend to come out on top, even against Scourges. That's true for hunters, and it remains true for slashers.

                              Generally speaking most slashers don't stack up all that well against supernaturals. They can be a threat especially to weaker starting characters but as a supernatural grows in power and ability they can easily end up pulling a favorite stunt of the slashers themselves: making the hunter the hunted. A charmer kills a young man not knowing he was wolf blooded and gets torn to pieces by the boy's angry siblings. A mask finds himself thrown into one of the insanity driven realms of the fae touched after stalking some oddly acting teenagers at a summer camp. A legend stalks and murders a young women not realizing that he's killed the last human descendant a powerful vampire cared about and now the vamp has pulled a few strings and is bringing a few friends who know a thing or two about dealing with local legends. A vicious seductress lures a young man to a secluded area only to find out that everything is going as the mage planned and she's to be a very special sacrifice for the ritual that will keep him looking young.

                              Now here's the fun part: There's nothing preventing a supernatural from acting or masquerading as a slasher. Imagine a group hunting down the killer in their area only to find out that instead of it being some weirdly empowered human with a jonesing for killing that it's one of their own kind and maybe they have powerful friends. Similarly you can twist things around and a group of players looking for what seems like a vampire is in reality a slasher with a similar MO. Heck maybe the slasher and the target they were after are working together.

                                  The real threat of most slashers against supernaturals is hardly physical. In fact, most slashers are going to die against a group of mortal Hunters in a direct confrontation. The problem is that slashers never fight fair. Sure, a vampire with Dominate can mind control a slasher, but first that vampire must realize what is going on and than track down the slasher. Granted, the slasher is at a similar disadvantage (unless he used to be a hunter), but I would place vampires among the easier targets of slashers. Their banes are too easy to abuse by a smart opponent, assuming the slasher even cares about actually killing the vampire in the first place. Most slashers apparently prefer to play with their favored police officer, or at least that is what the entertainment industry seems to think

                                  Of course, the real issue with slashers is in how to keep it fair to the players. If their car suddenly explodes because a genius wired it, they are rightfully going to complain. It might look a bit more sophisticated than rocks falling from the sky, but not by much Still, I am currently working on a slasher type story for my Changeling group. I am not aiming for the slasher to be a physically threatening opponent. Tracking him down is even going to be relatively easy. I am counting on a dream invasion, if not just that contract I talked about. The real pain is going to actually get into melee range. I just need to take care for it not to become too frustrating. I have never been a big fan of those reoccurring serial killers in all those detective series.
