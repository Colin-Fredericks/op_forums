    How do I allow a Mage template change?

    03-24-2015, 02:59 PM
    I'm planning on running a H:tV game soon and I want to give the players the opportunity to explore a very intriguing theme: What happens when the Hunter becomes the thing they hunt?

    So, I'm basically allowing template changes (although the characters will still be Hunters first and Supernaturals second). I'm allowing them a choice between vampires, werewolves, changelings, and sin-eaters. Although, ironic as it is that Mage is my favorite splat and that one of the NPCs that will be a superior to the PCs is a mage themselves, I'm having real trouble allowing them to become mages.

    The main problem being that mages can learn a heaping ton of information, and allowing only one or at the most very few (it's part of the whole alienation part for the template change theme to have only a few of the PCs become supernaturals) to have that in a chronicle that drip feeds the players a little bit of information at a time is severely unfair to the other players.

    If I limit the information the mage player(s) can get, I'm being unfair to them because all they're trying to do is play a mage properly. If I make the chronicle accommodate the mage player's capacity for gaining information, I'll vastly change the game, disorientating my players. If I make the chronicle accommodate them right from the very start, then what's the point of it all if no character becomes a mage?

    I really wanna give my players the option, especially considering that I'm allowing several other templates, but I'm afraid I'm gonna have to nix it. Can anyone offer any suggestions, please? Thanks in advance.

      Mages learn from what they find, so if they don't find the info they are looking for they don't get the info. Don't change your story or anything about it just let them fail or succeed at getting the info if they even think to do it. Plus if they are off guard or ill prepared they are behind the eight ball. So I say let them have it and give them only the info they can get from what they are looking for but don't allow them to know everything. A starting level template should not be able to know everything and deal with it. spreading their abilities so broad make for a know it all but useless when things really go down.

          But that's the thing: Why wouldn't they look for the info? If you're playing a mage properly, you're going to try to get as much info as you can. Even a combat-focused mage can dig up a startling amount of information. Case in point: Phoenix, one of my mage characters, who has Forces 2, Mind 2, Prime 1, and Life 1, can:
          1. Listen in on whispered conversations from a distance
          2. See anything on the electromagnetic spectrum
          3. Hear sounds that are outside the human hearing range
          4. Receive any data transmissions through the electromagnetic spectrum, such as radio transmissions
          5. Discern the emotional state of any given person
          6. Sense any and all minds within an area, and roughly what kind of minds they are (including things like ghosts and spirits)
          7. Detect any particularly powerful thoughts, such as psychic powers or any other kind of mental-based supernatural power
          8. Find out what an Artifact does and how it works
          9. See past Prime-based illusions
          10. Scrutinize spells very easily
          11. Find out the species, age, and gender of any living being, even supernaturals (provided he's familiar with said species, of course)
          12. Discern the state of health of any living being and any illnesses they have
          13. Sense any and all living beings within an area, and roughly what kind of life they are (he can also do this to discern whether something is not alive, such as the undead)

          And that's just looking at the spells from the core book. I can think of at least a dozen other things he could find out with CT. That's the problem, mage players can get way too much info if you're not ready for it or if your chronicle doesn't accommodate it. Herein lies the dilemma.

          Don't get me wrong, I'm not shooting you down to be mean or anything, so I apologize if it seemed like I was. I appreciate you trying to help, so thank you.

              Personally, I'd recommend you try to change your outlook on this.

              A mage having information the others don't have is not a problem.

              It's a theme.

              Dave Brookshaw mentioned "shining a deeply biased light of a specific color onto the world of darkness" in regards to Mage.

              Take that idea and run with it. The player has access to more information, in a way and form his colleagues and friends will not understand. His interaction with the others will be shaped by that knowledge, as theirs will be shaped by their knowledge of NOT having his knowledge.

              This isn't something you need to avoid, but something you can use use and should encourage. It's the core of mage. Knowledge and what he/she will do to get it.

              The way it alienates your player from the rest of the group. Taking that away cheapens the growth those transitions offer.

              Of course, at the end of the day, this means you have to provide and think of more information than you would in a game without a player Mage, but its also a chance to be deliciously creepy in your symoblism.

              “I have long since forsaken mortal blood. It offers me nothing but the stale taste of mayflies. I have partaken of Luna's ever-changing children and dined on the scholars of Magic. I have bled monsters, born and created. No more. Tonight I drink the blood of gods and slake my thirst for another thousand years of night.”

                  Well, while it really only slows things down, I'd start any brand spanking new mage with less innate benefits, the rules in mage for starting characters aren't intended for mages who awakened during their lunch break that day.

                  Looking at the rule for child mages for a clue, it mentions mages who start off with as little as 3 points in arcana- 2 in their superior, and another at their discretion. 2 or three arcana at level one, plus that generic unseen senses, aren't anything to sneeze at, but at least if the player buys up all the other arcana to turn themselves into the magical equivalent of sherlock holmes they had to work at it.

                  And really, all the templates you've mentioned have some good ways to know things:

                  vampires- auspex, dominate, animalism, obfuscate

                  Werewolves, even if they had no innate ability for investigation, can have spirits of knowledge or secrets on speed dial.

                  Changelings can use pledges to give themselves merits- Common sense, danger sense, eidetic memory, encyclopedic knowledge, language, library, trained observer, and contacts, just going by the list in GMC.

                  And sin-eaters have a great source of information- all these invisible intangible ghosts they can question.

                              As with all supernaturals (and most everyone else), its just what you can do, but who you know. A human hunter may have to rely on rumors, the local library, and the wilds of the internet, to discover the nature, motivations, strengths and weaknesses of what they hunt, Discerning fact from fiction is often quite difficult. A mage, vampire or werewolf, is usually thrust quickly into ancient societies, well-informed that respond with little more than an yawn or "meh" when questioned about matters and beings that are the stuff of nightmares.

                              For instance, hunters might need weeks or longer to even ascertain that the strange killings in their neighborhood are the work of vampires, no less know much more about them than what they learned watching Dracula. Conversely, a new mage need simply inquire of a new mentor or other mage friends, and their easily conveyed body of common knowledge would likely far outstrip anything that could by gathered by the hunters in a fraction of the time, all this without casting a single spell or using inborn abilities. If the mage contacted any well informed, more senior mage, he could also discover more detailed facts such as the vampires' political structure, numbers, political and related influences, rarer powers, and what Arcana are particularly useful when dealing with an "undead problem" (probably along with a prudent warning that blood sucking leeches and trigger happy mortal friends are best avoided).

                              It should also be noted that a mage has sufficient status, contacts, abilities and potential use to many other supernaturals that he could probably barter or negotiate any number of favors or information in return for the monster moving to areas beyond the purview of his hunter friends.

                              As Grunt sagely observes, the conventional knowledge, societal and cultural contacts, and inborn abilities of a mage (or other major supernatural) will generally completely alter the nature of a Hunter chronicle. Once a "monster" joins a group of hunters, many of the themes of Hunter inexorably change, including fear of the unknown and dealing with powers beyond their comprehension,
