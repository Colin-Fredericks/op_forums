And there's plenty of Slashers who use surprise. Not every Slasher is some brute that's just going to walk up to your characters and start attacking them.                             The Maniac's undertaking can sort of lead to something like that. I've been working on a microtemplate to expand on an idea I read about in a book, the First Victim's Club. In the book, very rarely, a serial killer's first victim will be sort of reborn and act as if under a state of Vinculum towards their murderer.

                            But if Slashers need help they can always reach out to the Hunt Club or their own Allies or the police.
                            Another good example of this is Johann from the anime/manga Monster. Just take his charisma and the orphans under his control and turn them toward more useful endeavors other than offing themselves for the lulz.

                            Now as I said slashers can be dangerous to supers much in the way that hunters can be a danger to supers but similarly unless they seriously stack the deck against your average medium tier super (who will almost certainly have some fair build up of combat abilities just to survive the other things in the WoD) most slashers will be the underdog in that fight. So let's look at some of the aspects of their various prey (Let's also assume we're considering slashers that are actually looking to pick a fight with a super and are actually prepared):

                            Vampires: They have some of the most obvious banes (Hell all it takes is a breath spray and a Bic to ruin a Kindred's night) but they have two traits that can make them major pains in the ass. The first is that vampires are the most likely of the supers to strike through proxies and this means that a slasher may have to contend with the likes of blood dolls, ghouls, and even possibly the vamp sending the police and/or VASCU after them. The good news here is that if a slasher is smart and can get the jump on them most of these proxies won't be too hard to handle unless they come heavily armed and armored and have large numbers to back themselves up (Hint: They rarely do). The nastier trait is the powers a vampire can bring to bare and the funny thing it's not the more obvious combat monster type powers a slasher has to worry about. Things like being able to command animals to attack, shapeshifting, mental domination, being able to seemingly blend into the shadows, and some of the other weirder powers can definitely present a problem unless the slasher can lure their prey to a special location that helps to deal with these abilities.

                            Werewolves: Again they have a very obvious bane though a little more expensive to get than the makeshift flamethrower mentioned above. While they do have many rites, a few funky artifacts, and the ability to turn into giant snarling death machines, a werewolf's biggest threat comes from the fact that they are pack animals. Mess with one alone or not and the victim's packmates will be looking for answers and eventually revenge. This is where having a group of slashers can be very helpful (And if we're talking a freak or mutant cannibal clan with a taste for furry it means more meat on the table). Really for werewolves the best strategy is either take them out quickly from a distance (The ole' silver bullet) or trick them into a tight area with plenty of hiding places so their strength, speed, and numbers don't count for as much.

                            Mages: They're pretty much human so surprise is what works best but that surprise better take them out. The more time a mage is given, the more likely they throw out something the slasher can't handle. The big issue with a mage is that it can be hard to figure out what they're capable of and plan accordingly. The social predator types of slashers can potentially have a leg up in this area by using their charms to disadvantage the victim prior to the attack.

                            Changelings: The big problem here is that many of their powers are such that a slasher may have a very difficult time countering them. Again, surprise and efficient killing go a long way.

                            Sin-Eaters: These guys can be a slasher's worst nightmare. Being able to communicate with prior victims' spirits can give them knowledge which can be used to predict and limit the slasher's options. There's also the thing about potentially helping said spirits be able to directly target the slasher. They're mostly human though so again surprise and kill quickly.

                            Prometheans: The bic flamethrower works here too. The real problem isn't their abilities more often than not but rather the fact that they aren't obvious to casual inspection. A slasher can easily get into trouble by attacking what he thinks is a human but it turns out to be one of these things.

                                I think the only problem with the Batman vs Joker analogy is the way you're coming at it. And, really, the way we're all mentioning how slashers work.

                                Joker doesn't hunt down Batman trying to kill him. He wants him to give up or give in. And as a Storyteller you're, generally, not trying to kill the PCs in your games, you're trying to make a story for them.

                                A slasher, likewise, doesn't have to be targeting a PC. They don't have to throw down in the middle of a club. Slashers can be insidious threats. A PCs ghoul is a slasher, and ghouls of the PC's rivals start disappearing. The Saint Satan killer keeps displaying his victims over powerful loci, tainting them with the attention. A mage's proximus 'apprentice' runs off to meet the mother who abandoned them, only to fall into a trap and be beaten to death with a crowbar. The opportunity for Slashers in 'regular' supernatural games extends beyond a Street Fighter type fight scene. Killing or getting rid of the Slasher is one thing, dealing with the fallout of what they've done and how its affected the PCs is what can make good stories.

                                                    There are limits to what plasm can do however. A sin-eater whose damage track is filled with negated damage can't spend anymore plasm for negation. They also won't be holding all of their plasm in reserve for healing blows (unless possibly if they're psyche 1) and plasm spending limits still apply. Given how quickly a slasher can rack up the damage with a series of frenzied attacks most of your average sin-eaters (psyche 1-5) are still going to struggle with keeping themselves going while still mounting any kind of successful offense. Either they have a hard time negating all of the damage of a given attack or they quickly run out of health boxes and have to take the full damage thereafter. Sin-eaters are tougher than normal humans but surprisingly they hit the limits of their resilience much faster than many other supers (This is balanced by them being able to easily negate incoming aggravated damage up to the normal limits of course).

                                                    Finally there are limits to the resurrection capabilities of a geist. If the body is severely dismembered (or even simply decapitated), cremated, or eaten the geist won't bother to try bringing the sin-eater back. Since I was talking about a slasher that at least understands the prey they're targeting the slasher isn't going to leave the body in a condition to be brought back.
