Maybe Mastermind would be better, but Puppeteers and Hypnos are better at that than they are, much as they would like to believe otherwise.

                                I'm on a roll. Here's a fan update to Strangers! Scourge coming when I have time. I renamed them, due to the fact I'm refocusing on them being bloody-handed scholars and questers to differentiate them from Undersirables, with their real danger being their ability to be patient.

                                Seeker

                                No. You're going to help me.

                                The Undesirable's equal and opposite is the Seeker. Both are driven ultimately by self-disgust, but while the Undesirable is driven by resignation he will never be anything better and should punish the world for it, his cousin is driven by the hope that she will be something better, if she pokes (and stabs, and scorches, and mutilates, and abrades...) the world in the right way. Faced with the inherent limitations of their selves, Seekers come to despise their own humanity, and become obsessed with removing with what they see as an inherent flaw or infection within themselves. This can be literal; more than one Seeker became that way over being faced with prejudice over a mental illness or physical deformity, in some way trying to become accepted by reforging themselves in its image, and so forcing the world to break in order to fit them. Some were faced with personal tragedies they couldn't move on from and came to blame themselves for, or simply found their own image of perfection was something they could never attain. Whatever the case, a Seeker is almost incidentally a slasher; their true focus is becoming something other than human, and to the Seeker, that means killing whatever is holding them back. Inevitably, that "what" becomes a "who", and the Seeker comes to the understanding that hurting those most emblematic of what they hate about themselves will yield the hidden lore they need to evoke their own ascension.

                                Seekers (and their scourge version, Avatars) tend to be the exception that proves the rule in a lot of ways when it comes to slashers. For one thing, they aren't (consciously) looking for new victims at all times, and their goal when speaking with humans is not always murder. In fact, Seekers tend to be quite empathetic with people they regard as being bound by the same flaws they are or show sympathy for their own, and often develop relationships not dissimilar to the Touchstones of hunters. Of course, by the time someone's become a Seeker, these people are mentally regarded as signs of what the slasher regards as their personal weakness and reasons to keep killing. Quite by accident, they end up becoming little more than camouflage and occasional cats-paws for the Seeker when it's time to sharpen their knives. More worrisome, Seekers are almost inevitably at least mildly skilled occult scholars; if one is planning to transcend humanity, after all, inevitably the Seeker starts studying ways it was previously accomplished. As a result, Seekers often aren't noticed until they've already developed quite the kill count, as they spend a long period between killings, their obsessions dedicated instead to researching their own evolution, and able to pretend to be fully human for extended periods of time. Seekers of any success tend to be expert planners, extremely stealthy, or frequently both. Worse, they often end up joining or forming cults and cabals with other slashers or monsters, trading resources and research for their bloody quest.

                                Hunters matching wits with a Seeker often think they're after a rogue hunter at first. To be frank, it's hardly the worst mistake in the world; Seekers are as diverse as their ideas of perfection, and they commonly show up in any culture with a strong mythology or focus on personal spirituality - which aren't small categories. More than a few focus on killing monsters at first to fully quantify their divine selves, which gives a Seeker plenty of opportunity to infiltrate a compact or conspiracy for their lore and techniques when they need to extract data from people. Worse, sometimes they end up valuing the friendships they develop when lurking in a cell's orbit, and try to help their new friends see the world the same way they do in order to preserve them - and Seekers are not so removed from humanity that they don't realize that explaining that maybe a slasher has the right idea is a good way to get exposed. No, the Seeker looking for assistants and peers is a subtle, gentle advocate of the idea that maybe the Code doesn't need to apply in some situations - and when the cell falls, the slasher is there, offering a gloved hand up, and perhaps some ideas on how they might avoid this kind of failure in the future...

                                Tells: A Seeker's Tell is less of a personal ritual they do to their victims and more of the remains of whatever experimental procedure they had in mind for a particular subject of their lethal curiosity. If the slasher is trying to make himself a vampire or other life-stealing immortal, his victims will be exsanguinated or be hooked up to electrical wires in an attempt to drain their bioenergy into batteries. If she is trying to cure her epilepsy through inducing psychic powers, the bodies she leaves in her wake will have the signs of amateur neurosurgery or the signs of dying in extreme fear, an attempt to provoke the sixth sense through trauma and life-threatening danger. Given how Seekers inevitably change their MOs as this technique or that ritual proves fruitless, this makes discovering their profile hard - were it not for the fact that, due to their peculiar patience, a Seeker often leaves a Tell behind when they use the version of their Talent that enables them to put off killing for a few days; the would-be vampire leaves behind holy symbols, garlic, and other folkloric banes behind, crushed in frustration when he realized they didn't have any noticeable effect, the psychic researcher her own jury-rigged isolation chambers and a string of parapsychologists a little frightened by how intense a recent email contact was when suggesting more intensive versions of Zener cards.

                                Examples: The Night Goblin steals babies from their cribs and leads their parents on wild, frequently dangerous and usually law-breaking quests and riddle contests as he follows the rule set out in the childhood fables he read when hiding from his abusive father. One day, he will draw the attention of Unseelie and other child-taking fae for them to train him in their magics and become unable to be harmed by those impure of heart. Helen watches as the monstrous cells she injected in her rival professor multiply and attempt to take him over, coldly taking notes as she notes the behavioral changes as they attempt to find and heal their progenitor - which, unlike her, has functioning legs and doesn't need coke-bottle glasses to see, and her cranial transfer tank is already showing promise. Yuriko sings a wailing prayer as her followers bisect the sacrifice, praying to the goddess they don't know is her future self, unable to be outshined by her "normal" twin sister any more. Mac doubles back to stalk the boxer who beat him in their latest match, looking for some sign of Fomorian ancestry. If he finds it, there will be a sudden, unannounced rematch, and this time, Mac will be eager to show his ancestors he's strong enough to beat a troll to death with his bare hands.

                                Talent: Steps to Transcendence --- Seekers may risk Willpower in the same way as a hunter can when pursuing any action dedicated to their personal mission of transcendence, with all the restrictions and benefits, although they cannot join or initiate Tactics. In addition, rather than having Addicted (Murder) as most slashers, Seekers have Addicted (Personal Quest); so long as they leave a Tell similar to their kills behind when pursuing an activity they feel will help them ascend, Seekers can satisfy their cravings. Naturally, murder is the most satisfactory and cathartic, but Seekers are more capable of being patient and getting nearly as much of a hit from less violent (and more easily hidden) means as death when laying low or planning.

                                Frailty: Not At Peace --- Seekers become highly uncomfortable and self-doubting in long-term social situations, unable to ignore their own sense of being less than those around them. They have to open two extra Doors in any Social Maneuvering attempt due to coming off insecure and insincere in regards to others (because they are always trying to hide their perceived deficiencies). When regaining Willpower through Anchors, the Seeker must roll Resolve + Composure if it has been more than a week since their last murder, with a penalty of -(number of weeks beyond the first); failure indicates the Seeker has become so unsettled by stymied progress that they regain no Willpower for the act, and their next satisfaction of their Addiction must be murder. As a side note, a Seeker cannot be cured of their Addiction, but for one who has regained their Integrity, they merely need to fulfill it, nonviolently or otherwise, in order to reset this Frailty.
