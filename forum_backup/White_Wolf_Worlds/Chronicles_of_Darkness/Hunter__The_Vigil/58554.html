      Keep in mind the Danites are a touchy subject as they feature prominently in anti-Mormon propaganda historically - Joseph Smith himself ended up disavowing them entirely in the harshest terms theologically possible.

      Of course, one could say the same thing of the actual book Malleus Maleficarum, so just be aware of it while you do it. MM works because they're NOT supposed to be an accurate representation of the Catholic Church, its teachings, or answerable to proper Church authority - they're a heretical conspiracy. If you're going to draw on the darker aspects of the Danites, you should probably emphasize the history of hostility between them and the actual Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints.

                                      Originally posted by herkles View Post

                                      There are a few things. I am setting my game within the Dallas-Fort Worth metroplex, and wiki says that there is a sizable LDS church community. So the fact that it is there is one reason I want to use them. I also like learning about other beliefs, and I will openly admit that I am not that familiar at all with Mormonism. I know it was born within America, and that they moved out west in the 1800s but really that is about it.
                                      Well, some basics (these are gross simplifications, and I'm speaking as an outsider who has studied the subject from a secular pov, I in no way mean to elevate secular-academic pov on a religion over insider-theological pov, both have value), focusing on areas of difference from other forms of Christianity (so only a sliver of the totality of the faith, but I'm not bothering to explain things like Original Sin, salvation through Christ's sacrifice, etc etc)

                                      - Mormonism is a distinctly American faith in many regards (not that there isn't a quickly growing non-American Mormon population, there is! I just mean it's nature reflects its core American elements). The history presented in the Book of Mormon is that of Pre-Columbian Americas as a land settled by Lost Tribes of Israel - many of whom (primarily the Nephites) accepted Christ when he appeared to them after the resurrection and delivered a further testament. These are who wrote and buried the Golden Plates that were revealed to Joseph Smith in the 19th Century. These Christians of the Americas died off before European settlers arrived, and only the descendants of those who spiritually lost their way remained.

                                      - This theology ties into 19th Century Manifest Destiny in many ways, as it presents the Americas as a historically Christian and white (the Israelites were originally white according to the Book of Mormon, with darker skin amongst native peoples being mark of having being cut off from God - please note the modern Church has worked hard to shed this racial baggage, and modern Mormons do not interpret these references to skin-tone literally), and the west becomes the new Promised Land (starting from New York, initially "the West" was Missouri - where the original Garden of Eden was to have existend; but then they had to push further Westward to survive due to persecution).

                                      - Interesting theological note: there is no distinction between a human soul and an Angelic being in Mormonism. All spirits have a pre-mortal and post-mortal existence. So while dead people becoming angels is a common misconception for most Abrahamic faiths, it's accurate in Mormonism. Adam was the Archangel Michael. Jesus was Jehovah (not to be confused with his Father, Elohim: more commonly simply 'Heavenly Father')...

                                      - There are three distinct beings within the Godhead, unlike the united Trinity shared by Catholics, Orthodox Christians, and most (not all!) Protestants. Jesus walked this world just as his Father did before him in some other iteration of the universe. The Heavenly Father is a being of flesh and bone.

                                      - Like Catholicism, and unlike predominant strands of Protestant theology (Luther's Justification by Faith Alone, and Calvin's external salvation through Grace alone), free will and ritual actions play central roles in salvation in Mormon theology. First off - free will is the root of the original fall - Lucifer didn't want it to exist, Jehovah (who is pre-mortal Jesus) did, as free will means the choice to be righteous and good would give glory to God.

                                      At the end of days, the only people cast into the "Outer Darkness" will be the ones who choose it. Post-mortal existence means the opportunity to accept salvation don't stop in life. So the "Sons of Perdition" who follow Lucifer do so knowingly by choice, because that must be the case for free will to have meaning.

                                      However, while salvation can be accepted after death, there are levels of Exaltation - and the HIGHEST level of Exaltation is reserved for those who were baptized into the Church (note that this is why the Church does posthumous baptisms for the ancestors of Mormon converts), and who are sealed in "celestial marriage". Family is central and deeply theological in Mormonism.

                                      This highest level of Exaltation entails becoming as unto God, continuing the cycle of universes. Yes, with the previous prerequisite, this does imply there is a Heavenly Mother (no, she's not the Holy Spirit [repository of the divine feminine in medieval theology], or the Virgin Mary). The Mormons I've discussed this with say she's respected but not talked about a lot.



                                      - As it's one of the things uninformed people are likely to have heard of, I'll briefly address plural marriage. It's a non-issue in the modern LDS Church itself, which abandoned the practice over a century ago (as with shedding the racial baggage, these reforms are possible while remaining theologically coherent because the President of the Church and the Quorum are 'prophets, seers, and revealators' subject to direct divine guidance). It is of historic note however, because both of the major schisms in the Church were rooted in it. Firstly, after Joseph Smith was martyred in Missouri, those who followed Brigham Young to what would become Utah adhered to plural marriage, on the basis of returning to the traditions of the Ancient Israelites. Those who remained in Missouri following Joseph Smith III rejected the practice (many biographers believe that Joseph Smith Jr[the original prophet]'s original wife, Emma Hale Smith, Joseph Smith III's mother, was less than pleased when her husband adopted the practice late in his ministry, and her son sympathized with her). While the former became the well known Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints, the latter became the Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints (today known as the Community of Christ) - often seen as the "mainstream Protestantism friendly" offshoot of the Latter Day Saints movement.

                                      On the other extreme, you have the Fundamentalist Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints - which split off in the early 20th Century when the LDS Church moved to excommunicate those Mormons who fought to continue plural marriage. It exists in isolated enclaves in the Southwest and continues the practice today. It also adheres to the old racial philosophy.





                                      Ok, that's all that's springing to mind as "basic differences between Mormonism and other forms of Christianity" - and is obviously grossly simplified and basic. ArcaneArts can give you a much more human outlook on the lived experience of the Church itself (as well as a lot of insightful folklore and biblical history details of real improtance to the faithful) than my academic wall-splatter.

                                      Just to clear up anyone's doubts: YES Mormonism is a Christian tradition; it centers on salvation via Christ's death and resurrection.

                                      The reason people often want to paint it as not Christian (beyond the normal bigotries that result in [for example] some hardline Catholics and Protestants claiming the other isn't Christian) is because of the erroneous reductionism that makes people want to force all Christian traditions into either the "Catholic" or "Protestant" box (I've seriously seen people argue that Orthodox Christians are Protestants on this basis, it's ridiculous). Mormonism is NOT a Protestant tradition, and beyond the principles shared by all Christians, has nothing in common with those movements. It is a completely distinct form of Christianity like Catholicism, Orthodox Christianity, and Protestantism (or "lost" traditions like Gnosticism or Arianism).
