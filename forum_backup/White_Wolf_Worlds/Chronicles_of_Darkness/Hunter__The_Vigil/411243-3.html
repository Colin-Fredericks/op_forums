    I think that if the book is called "hunter", playing an internal affairs crisis is off topic. The book promised monster hunting, not beaurocratic politics.

    Basically, if you want to preserve Tier 3 horror, you can do it with bigger monsters. The Laundry series of books by Charles Stross are an excelent example of this, showing how Tier 3 horror is different yet workable from Tier 1 horror.

    Sure, but they don't need to be compromised by monsters to do that. Humans have no end of morally murky deeds to their name. The Ascending Ones control drug cartels. You don't need a Mummy to make that morally wrong.

What you have in 1e is "There's a werewolf! Let's shoot it!" where a lot of hunters take a rather gung-ho take on the Vigil. The main threat being that of the monsters not really being monsters. (Whether that means mundane human or actually a "monster" that was a law-abiding citizen that actually hunted down only rampaging spirits and genuinely was a net benefit to society.)

                                            I'd like to see more work/text shown towards the interactions with "monsters" that aren't shooting the various other denizens of darkness, while also playing up the clandestine rivalries between the conspiracies and compacts, themselves. (Especially since many compacts and conspiracies are filled with "monsters" of their own... Even beyond Cheiron, there's a group of cannibals that eat witches so that they can become witches. How is that NOT monsterous? On the Vigil's radar, doesn't a "human" that got a witch's power through eating the witch sound exactly like a witch, anyway?)

                                            Besides that, different conspiracies and compacts are clearly geared more towards specific supernaturals than others, and because of their differing motivations for their hunts, are more-or-less willing to live and let live or even covertly ally themselves with different creatures that could put them into open conflict with the conspiracies that hunt their allies, even if only by mistaken association. (VALKYRIE agents passing off forged documents to changelings to keep a source planted close to a court gets jumped by Long Night hunters who just assumed anyone willingly helping the demons must also be a demon or at least worthy of death.)

                                            Basically, Hunters frequently try to look at their world in black-and-white terms when it's really gray. I'm saying to play up the gray. The moral doubts pop up occasionally in the little sidebars or small story hook blurbs, but they're not present in the main text, itself. It's "witches are EEEEEEVIL! Burn the witch!" throughout the main thrust of most of what's in the book.

                                            Beyond that, while perhaps moving a little in the other direction, I'd like it if some of the compacts weren't so clearly Too Dumb To Live. Null Mysteriis is especially flagrant, as it represents people trying to approach the supernatural with a rational mindset, and instead they come off as strawman take-that's at how scientists are stupid. Les Mysteres are almost an insult to a wide host of religions, since they are basically taking a whole range of faiths, and the general implication is that they're all just going to get spirit-ridden until spirit-claimed, as well as that the spirits they believe in are basically just parasitic, and creating those religions to throw their followers as cannon fodder at werewolves. Other groups that seem more like a cartoonish parody of a group of people than actually developed characters include Network Zero, Ashwood Abbey, and Keepers of the Source. I could also make an argument about Cheiron, Hunt Club, or Long Night, but you could kind of make the argument there are certainly institutions like them. It still opens the question of how many people play a game of Cheiron for the "horror", and how many play it as Paranoia set in nWoD?

                                            I mean, to go back to Null Mysteriis, the concept of a scientist trying to make sense of these things that clearly are out there is compelling, but it's presented to players with vignettes that basically go, "HI THERE, MR VAMPIRE! I'M ACTIVELY BLOWING YOUR MASQUERADE, AND I'M GOING TO GO OVER INTO THAT DARK CORNER OVER THERE AND INVITE YOU TO HAVE A BIG DISCUSSION WITH ME, AND OH GOSH, IF YOU'D BE SO NICE, TRY NOT TO MURDER ME TOO HARD!" Even when they tried to break that mold, they only wound up making them Cheiron with a fresh coat of paint - psychopaths with scalpels that ripped people apart for "SCIENCE!" Surely, there's something besides Lois Lane or Dr. Mangele they can be modeled after...

                                                            What I want to see from Hunter 2ed is the following...
                                                            1. Hunter Needs a Focused Theme - Vampire must feed, Werewolves must hunt, so what defines Hunter as a theme?

                                                              My pick would be that Hunters must endure. (Your pick could be different but still, please, pick one thing and make it awesome)


                                                            2. Building on that Theme - What does enduring mean in regards to the Vigil and the greater game focus?

                                                              It means that Hunters, differing from regular humans, don't turn away from the horror or their fates. It means, Hunters as characters are going to die. Often in heroic and tragic ways and often in terms of your counting the days till shit slips up.


                                                            3. Story Progression and Mechanics Integration - What needs to be in 2ed to support this theme?

                                                              Alternate XP system. If you have a character possibly dying, how can the next character step in and fill their shoes? Possibly something like the Sanctity of Merits but for Cells or Compacts or Conspiracies. Touchstones become the next line of Hunters. Storytelling and filling in downtime should also support this, because it takes time to fill in lost shoes.

                                                              Very simple (and fun) character creation. If Hunter goes this route and shows off just how fragile and deadly humanity is against the horrors, it should be a fun system and a quick one with lots of examples of how to transition and carry over what you loved about one character into another one.

                                                              Progression for the party, not the character. This ties into the alternate XP system. What you buy in terms of Skills, Attributes etc, should get worked into the next character or the Cell as a whole. Special Hunter Merits should persist and provide growing bonuses, stories should build towards resolutions not for just one or a few characters but the Cell. Emphasis on the story of the Cell as one shared character that literally has separate ''cells" of characters to support.


                                                            4. Killing Muthaf*cking Monsers - Hunter needs to be able to cross-over, and they need to be able to win.

                                                              Hunters are the bane of every other game line. They are the big ball of humanity that you threaten people with in those games. Make that ball here and build it with some really nasty toys. Show just how effective regular human beings can be at controlling their environment or settling scores or just being badasses.

                                                              Badass normal should be a trope in Hunter, it should something that is costly or grows from the forces of many. Humanity has numbers and cooperation. Cells have each other. Human spirit needs to be able to win out in the end or wither and die tragically. Equipment, Merits, Powers, Tactics etc. They need to support this. Things should suck if they fail, but be amazing if they succeed.


                                                            5. Dying like Weaksauce Mortals - Don't contradict the other game lines.

                                                              Humans are the fodder and chaff of every other game line. They are a resource or food or fodder or many other disposable things to the supernatural forces of the world. Every game line has ways to control them to their ends. Make it another cornerstone point of Hunter. You are facing something greater than yourself, and possibly risking those you love.

                                                              It needs to be implicit that death (or worse) is a very real possibility and that this is okay in Hunter. It's encouraged. It adds to the game and isn't punished because in the established World of Darkness narrative, its how things eventually go down for human beings. Maybe you change, you become the 'other' and that can be explored slightly in Hunter, but if you really want that go play one of the other game lines. Hunters die, go crazy or worse.

                                                              Maybe your the one Cell who is crazy careful, and is losing touch with their own humanity. That should also get focus.


                                                            6. License to Print Monsters - What Storytellers need to explore other things that Hunters hunt.

                                                              We need simple to use mechanics that interact with Conditions and Tilts that can build really cool and scary things. Broad powers with many possible outcomes that have a firm Storyteller only stamp on them. Something that is not unlike Influence for Spirits but a bit more detailed. Shadow creatures have shadow powers that cause fear of darkness. Fear creatures just cause fear outright or phobias. Same power, different themes etc.


                                                            7. Maybe Custom Powers - It's not what I expect out of Hunter...

                                                              ...but you do a good job of it with Mage, maybe something toned for Hunter could do? I don't really mind but it would make a lot of players happy.



                                                            That's what I want to see out of 2ed Hunter.

                                                            Firm supported theme, focus on group progression rather than character progression, highlighting humanity as both awesome and frail, cool toys and custom monsters.
