            Anyway further elaborating on that compact idea of mine.

            I followed suggestions of other NWoDers, including atamajakki, to make it a conspiracy.

            I also switched from anti-vampire focus to more anti-supernatural in general.

            Reiterating, it's an extended family of Hunters, dating back to times of Roman Republic, who employ necromantic sorcery in their Vigil. Their signature method is letting their ancestor spirits possess their bodies temporarily, granting the experience and abilities of a veteran Hunter to even relatively untested one. The catch to this method is that if botched the spirit may possess the host permamently, gnawing slowly at the sanity of the host and ultimately even displacing the personality of the host for good.

            Who knows if the highest echelons of this conspiracy are actually people from ancient times, just in bodies of their distant descendants?

            Apart from this possession technique, the conspiracy has access to other necromantic techniques, and are not above binding troublesome ghosts they encounter to their service, which according to one Geist player would mean a state of cold-war between this organisation and Sin-Eaters. I don't know much about this line, so I would be glad if Geist people could offer some advice.

            And somebody I know suggested me to make some connection between them and Birds of Minerva, though nothing is set in the stone at this stage, obviously.

                                            Hey! While most of my Hunter knowledge is second-hand, I've had some ideas I though it would be neat to share. Not sure if some of these guys would be better as Compacts or Conspiracies. The names in parenthesizes are names I acknowledge are probably stupid and need to change. Sorry if this is "thread necromancy"(after all, I know how y'all feel about witches! )

                                            •"Arbitrators": Not all groups of Hunters have a particularly martial bent, and the Arbitrators number amongst them. Instead they prefer to broker more equitable relationships for all. Why waste time and effort hunting, they ask the vampire, when the group can get them regular donations and willing groupies, so long as the vampire treats them right....and makes sure to sign on the bottom line, of course. Because, for all the group's smiles and kindness, they learned the group's Endowment, their understanding of oaths and bindings, from beings born of brimstone and brambles; if you value your life, your sanity, your very soul.... make sure to keep you side of the deal.

                                            •"The Cult of the Dog": The group was originally founded by a group of Wolf-blooded who managed to escape from a Pure breeding camp, who ended up allying with each other in order to survive. Over the years, their focus and recruiting have expanded, lashing out against groups that would tear down civilization, from Predator Kings and nostalgic Arisen to a group of "mere" ecoterrorists who want to return humanity to the stone age. Despite their seemingly "domesticated" mission, the group tends towards the aggressive, not helped by an Endowment, a process known to them as "Feralization", which grants them physical potency at exchange for making them more animistic.

                                            •Association for the Preservation of Abnormality: This one is decidedly a Compact. More specifically, it's a group created to support and aid fellow "abnormals", that is, people who possess Supernatural merits or "Independent" minor templates(Offspring and Wolf-blooded could be members, but Ghouls couldn't, if that makes sense?). The group is involved with the Vigil in order to better reach out to fellow abnormals and because they're so often forced to defend themselves from "bigger" supernaturals that now they want to go on the offensive.*

                                            •The Crossroads Society: An organization founded through a merger of the more dedicated members of the Silent Imperative and a few Ghouls and Kindred who foretold the fall of the Gallows Post. The group focuses on "under the table" transportation, ferrying people and objects place-to-place discreetly and without question(for a small fee, of course). They also specialize in the "Otherworlds", such as the Hedge and the Great Below, using them as specialized shortcuts, and are somewhat infamous amongst the Vigil for their willingness to work with monsters.

                                            •Arm of the Angels: This group dates itself back to the dark ages, where the G-M sent one of its Psychopomps to pretend to be an angel of God in order to convince a small, isolated village to produce some needed Infrastructure. But one of the villagers ended up becoming Stigmatic, and in the resulting fever dreams received what he believed to be a vision of the Archangels, who told him that the "angel" that had enslaved his village was actually a demon in service to the great Machine, Lucifer himself, and charged him to oppose the adversary. Emboldened and furious, he brought the others to his point of view(helped by the fact that the vision had granted him his first dot in the yet- unborn group's Endowment) and led them in killing the cultist who had brought the demon into the universe, and the Infrastructure keeping it alive as well. In that moment, the villagers founded the Conspiracy, swearing an oath to destroy the dark rites Satan needed to work his filth in our world. Today, they exist as a loosely organized collection of Christian "freedom fighters"(and we all know what they say about Freedom Fighters) arranged against the G-M and its works. While I'm not sure how they might work mechanically, their Endowment(let's call it the "Angelic Approbations") work in a way disturbingly similar to manifesting a Demonic Form Power, each themed after a different angel of traditional mysticism.

                                            •"The Chaos Theorem": The group known as the Chaos Theorem, based around a small book of the same name, believes that disorder is necessary. They might not like chaos, or they even might gear it, but all believe that if the universe ever goes beyond a certain threshold of order, the universe will stagnate and die. Therefore, the group has dedicated itself to ensuring the world is kept safe from this fate, overturning long-held structures and disrupting older systems. This puts them at odds with supernaturals such as the Invictus, the Seers, and, most of all, the Machine, who they believe is the personification of Order, who wants to calcify the world out of spite against organic life, which is far too messy for its tastes. Their Endowment would be "Irrational Numerology", a number of complex equation-koans that allow for limited direction of the localized chaos.

                                            •The Sybil Selection: More of a "Convention", as some on this forum would call it, than a Conspiracy, this group is united by the fact that all it's members are prophets...sort-of. Inspired when I read the Wikipedia page for a tv show called Early Edition(about a man who receives tomorrow's newspaper, today!), members receive hints and prophecies from an unknown source in a number of odd ways, from newspapers that haven't been printed yet to websites that don't exist, and are forced into the Vigil lest they be overwhelmed by insistent messages. Their Endowment would obviously be this prophetic talent, and the information it allows them to access, from the name of a murderer burnt on their toast to a phone call from a unknown number that calmly explains that there's an invisible vampire in the room.

                                            •The Game: This group, I feel I need to note, was inspired by LostLight's work on converting The Union into a Conspiracy. As far as most people are concerned, The Game is a mundane, if worldwide, Augmented Reality scavenger hunt. But some people look closer. Maybe they want to win more than anything, maybe they need something and they'll do anything to get it, or maybe they just stumble into it. Bur whatever the reason, these Players get invited to the "True Game". By completing various Contests, they get Points, which can be redeemed for a number of(sometimes eerily personalized) Prizes, some mundane, others not. Want enough money to buy and sell your boss? There's a prize for that! Want the impossible to find medicine that can stop the supposedly incurable disease that's killing your daughter?*There's a prize for that! Want the vampire blood you need to ensure your immortality? There's a prize for that! And so, Players are slowly drawn into the Vigil, as Contests transition from "Solve this riddle" and "translate this passage from Sumerian to French" to "Kill this Vampire" and "Stop this human sacrifice".*

                                                    The Dead Musicians are a Conspiracy consisting of musicians, grave-diggers, and mediums; founded in Weimar-era Germany and ousted as the Third Reich rose to power, their unifying feature is that they can hear the Totentanz, the Dance of Death. They see themselves as help for the natural sequence of things; "unscheduled" deaths ruin the Dance nearby, as do the walking dead and the unnaturally alive. Their Endowment, also referred to as Totentanz, sharpens their sensitivity to the music; while any of them can hear murders and death by Supernatural causes, it takes real training to hear corpses in their hiding places, deafen the undead with a whistle, or put down an angry ghost with a violin.

                                                    I keep having a mental image of Hunters going out to hunt zombies with a string quartet pop up; these guys came out of it. On a more serious level, there's a long association of death with music, so I think these guys are thematically solid. It'll also be nice to see a German Conspiracy/Compact that didn't have its start in thanks to the Nazis.
