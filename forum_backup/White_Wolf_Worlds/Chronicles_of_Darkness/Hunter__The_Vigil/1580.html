    Compact: SG-14; The Bund

    11-03-2013, 02:36 AM
    Schutzgruppe 14
    “The Bund”

    Germany doesn’t like to talk about World War II. It was a nation’s darkest moment, the time when German culture was subverted by madmen and human monsters. After the war the Allied nations that saw the end result of Hitler’s “Final Solution” forced the Germans to see the horrors their inaction had unleashed, forcing entire communities to walk through the death camps and ghettos to see exactly what happened when a person gave up their morals for safety and prosperity.

    Put simply, it worked. West Germany learned to internalize that kind of hatred of what it had done, while the East merely tried to please its Soviet masters. West Germany embraced its roots before Hitler, rebuilding itself as an industrial nation on par with what it had been before World War I. A new constitution was written to remind both Germany, and indirectly the world, that human rights were not violable. That people of all kinds were to be accepted and protected. The German military powerhouse before 1945 was made subservient to the government again and the German states made self-governing while the Federal government of Germany oversaw the affairs of the nation on the world’s scale. When Germany became one again, a hole had been filled.

    So when the Allies also found the fullest extent of what Hitler’s SS and Gestapo had been doing during the war, from trafficking in relics to consorting with witches, they at first tried to utterly destroy Germany’s occult secrets. Some organizations stripped Germany of its occult assets, others did their best to expatriate those “lesser” Nazis whose knowledge could be used for a positive purpose. Small cells of soldiers and occupying forces seeing the horrors that lurked behind mankind on the battlefields and in the ruined cities went about clearing the monsters from the German states, but soon realized that perhaps they had been too hasty in their attempt to destroy what could possibly be used to protect Germany once they had left. So, in the utmost positions of power, the generals and politicians of America, Britain and France had to enact a way to protect Germany from its occult crimes.

    Their solution was the Bundesamt für Verfassungsschutz, the Federal Office for the Protection of the Constitution. A rough equivalent to the United States FBI formed initially on crushing neo-Nazi and communistic elements in West Germany, the Allied-backed compact was inserted in a small line item in the BfV's second budget. With a shortage of “qualified” hunters, the Allies were forced to accept former Gestapo agents into the ranks of Schutzgruppe, men who had not only hunted the seditious elements under the orders of the swastika but also combated vampires and rogue witches in running street battles even against their “allies” in the SS and Wermacht that had decided to throw in their lot with the monsters.

    This is the story of Schutzgruppe 14.

    Their first missions were primarily in support to other organizations from the Allies; assisting TFV in scouring German rocket bases for advanced technology guarded by paranormal threats, or giving MI18 vital information on potential Nazi sympathizers high in government that had been part of the Reich’s .

    To the comfort of many other monster hunting groups, the Gestapo contacts in the BfV were soon revealed after the first president of the BfV defected to East Germany, and reforms were instituted through Schutzgruppe. It was decided that the compact needed to be divided, to prevent the forces inside from gaining “seditious ideas” against the government. SG-14 has since then focused on the elimination of Germany’s paranormal threats.

    Typically, SG-14 recruits its agents not through the military or German police departments, but through universities and colleges. The chief of SG-14 wants agents who are intelligent and able to improvise to new situations, not ones who are willing to break out the firepower at the slightest haunting. Agents are evaluated not only on their ability to handle the paranormal, but also on their willingness to question their superiors’ orders if the situation seems suspicious. They are given training with firearms, but they also have training to a specialty they are assigned, or based on possible prior experiences in the Vigil.

    [Image: A young woman, blond hair in a tight bun, holding an H&K MP7 and a cross. She is wearing a uniform of the Bundespolizei and has an iron cross on her left breast. She is firing at something to her right but her face is merely scowling.]

    For the Fatherland?
    No.
    For humanity.

    The Divide

    SG-14 was split due to the fear that Germany’s leadership would once again decide they would attempt to form a new German empire. Initially split into four primary sections, named “Jaghunds”, each section is expected to be expertly trained and knowledgeable in their chosen specialty, whether that be witches for “Blau Jaghund” or the incorporeal for “Schwarz Jaghund”. In theory this plan was meant to both streamline operations on certain monsters and help prevent collusion to usurp the government should its initial ex-Gestapo agents decide they wanted to return to Hitler’s Reich.

    In practice, there are growing issues with the system. In an age where governmental organizations are becoming more and more interconnected, using redundant systems to prevent dramatic failures in intelligence gathering and action, SG-14 steadfastly refuses outright to enact such reforms. Partly out of tradition, but partly out of (some would say outdated) fear of what might happen should they merge.

    There is also a small but present gap between the conventional government and SG-14’s operations. While it appears that other groups utilized by other governments combat monsters under an almost glaring level of anonymity, SG-14 must work its way through the BfV in order to secure its funding and continue its operations. While the president of BfV knows that SG-14 exists and continually gets reports on its operations, details never go beyond that which can be explained through mundane words. Secrecy is kept only because the truth would never make it past the first secretary.

    Interestingly, the Bund keeps excellent track of its hunters not only operationally, but psychologically as well. At least three trained psychiatrists are on hand at BfV headquarters for members of SG-14, and members can elect for voluntary leave should they need it. Though it can at times result in manpower shortages, SG-14 considers a stable agent more valuable than a strung out scarecrow.

    The Enemy

    There are seven acknowledged Jaghunds in SG-14, but only four existed at their founding. Three more were added over time, and rumors continue to fly that there is an eighth Jaghund, which the leadership promptly shoots down. Each Jaghund focuses on a specific “threat entity”, each with a single database that flows into one single database accessible only by command itself. Generally, SG-14 tries it’s best to observe and determine if a monster, if not friendly, can at least be counted as non-hostile like a residual haunting or a sentient flock of birds who only want a place to roost. If the monster is proven as a threat to the civilian population, the specific Jaghund will commence offensive operations.

    So do other hunter groups count as an enemy? Without a doubt. SG-14 has formed over the years a large database of disparate groups through Germany, from radical cells of workers in Berlin to Catholic operations in Bavaria. Mostly, these operations keep tabs on these other groups and making sure that, while they cannot fully control their actions, SG-14 can at least keep their actions under some umbrella of organizational control.

    Yet there is one group that the Bund will attack without pause. A group of occultists calling themselves the Loyalists of Thule, remnants of the Thule-Gesellschaft, the occult force that allowed Hitler’s Aryan ideals to lead to the death of millions. Any trace of this society is found, interrogated, and shipped off to blacksite prisons where they serve out the rest of their lives atoning for their atonement.

    There are other groups too. The Vril Society still lives, lurking in the understrata of Germany’s cities, while larger conspiracies attempt to subvert Germany into their fold. The near collapse of the Eurozone is weighing on SG-14’s budget, and the BfV’s accountants are starting to ask questions.

    Yet of anything else, the biggest target that SG-14 goes after are neo-Nazis and other white supremacists. They will sabotage any rally they can, murdering certain demagogues in the night. They consider it being the sin-eaters of Germany, swallowing the filth of their nation so that the innocent can sleep peacefully at night again.

    In times of desperation, the members of SG-14 can be seconded to the Bundespolizei and Landespolizei, in the cases of large-scale urban unrest and national emergency. Yet even then, SG-14 agents are ordered to observe for any paranormal activity, to ensure that even if the crowd gets rowdy, people can walk away alive.

    Stereotypes

    Loyalists of Thule: Tinkering with such evils is what drove Germany into the darkest depths mankind could dive to. If they want to atone, they’ll do it by our rules.

    Null Mysteriis: We attempt to contract them, exchange our money for their knowledge, but they consistently turn us down on “ideological grounds”. What do they fear by exchanging their knowledge with us?

    Task Force: VALKYRIE: The Americans still think they are the world’s policeman, and it’s not entirely inaccurate to say they have started to lie down on the job. But they helped to save the world when we attempted to destroy it, and now they are allies. Treat them as such.

    Aegis Kai Doru: The bunker had to be redesigned again, the bastards nearly breached the door this time. It’s time they realized that we will not allow these evils to see the world as long as we guard them.

      Hunters
      Your graduate degree in literature was on the topic of Germanic folklore in the modern age, specifically the belief in fairies as written by certain neo-pagan groups. Then one of those groups approached you, drugged you, and nearly had you sacrificed to a real fairy. That was when the undercover SG-14 member started shooting and broke up the ceremony, offering you a job. You’re researching the same things now, only it’s telling fact from fiction.

      You were a member of the Heer, stationed in Afghanistan, working alongside Americans and British as you tried to bring order to a land that seemed to actively fight it. That’s when you started seeing the ghosts of your dead allies as you were on patrol. Trying to shake it off, you were discharged and took the first plane home, and the ghosts followed you. That was when and SG-14 agent who could also see ghosts came to you and offered to help you get your friends’ spirits to move on. Two weeks later, your wounds have healed, and your friend in SG-14 has your seat all ready for you. First assignment? Save a family from their relative’s ghost.

      The Black Forest nearly killed you when the giant wolf came after you. Your fiancé died fighting it, but he bought you enough time to get to the car and onto the road, only to break down barely a mile from the thing. You wandered for days, looking for help while avoiding the monster, when a hiking party of college students walked up on you. You tried to run to them for help when the students brought out guns and shot the monster dead. Impressed at your survival skills, you’ll be nominated for SG-14 when you’re out of counseling.

      The river you lived on had fish-men in it, you knew for sure it did. They came out every month to exact a price for the peace in your village. You wanted to fight them, but the village was almost under a spell. So you had to take action. On the night they went to the center of the town, you covered the banks of the river in pitch and waited. When they started marching back, you ploughed through them in your car, until none were left alive, and then burned the bodies. The Stadspoliezi wanted to string you up for nearly burning your village down, but then two men from the BfV came in to your defense. You listen to the doctors, so why do you still want to find more fish-men to kill?

      Your shame is that your grandfather was a member of the SS. It’s been drilled into you every day since secondary school that your family would never, should never forget that he served at the Death’s Head. So one night you went to Dachau alone, intent on burning the place down so that your family might finally live in peace. That was when you ran into a man shouting about Thule, and getting ready to use the camp as his offering to get there. You didn’t hesitate to pour the gasoline on him and fire the flare gun. As he burned, a helicopter got you in the spotlight. For killing a known enemy of Germany, and for not burning his supply of occult information, you were offered a post in SG-14. You finally found a way to break the chain in your family’s history.

      SIDEBAR: WEISS JAGHUND

      The conventional line of thought goes that if enough people say something is real, there’s a good chance it is. Well more than enough agents agree there’s an unofficial “White Hound” that is tasked with the absolute worse missions, and filled with the worst hunters to carry out the missions assigned. There are differences to each story, however, and four have become the norm.

      1) It’s the Loyalists or the Vril Society, no one’s quite sure which. The important thing is they’re trying to weasel in through SG-14 to the files Kennel has, files that no one else should ever be meant to see.

      2) They’re a deeply embedded black op trying to find Germany a means to put them on an even footing not only with the monsters, but the more powerful hunter organizations. They’re known to abduct monsters and bring them back for “testing”.

      3) It’s a body harvesting op by one of the bigger players in the Vigil. Cheiron is a likely culprit, though TFV or Section 9 wouldn’t mind a few subjects either. Whatever conspiracy it is, they plan on using the information to eventually burn the Bund from the inside.

      4) There is no “White Hound”. The whole thing is just that, a rumor, and anyone claiming to be from it is a liar and a threat. After all, hunters are like sailors and actors, and suspicion and fear are a part of the experience. So what do humans do when they’re afraid? They make stories to rationalize their fear.

          HUNDEHÜTTE /THE KENNEL

          The command cell for the Bund, Hundehütte observes the actions of SG-14 through Germany, ensuring that all operational cells act in line with their specific specialty. It also coordinates requests (Or more accurately, lack of requests) by the leaders of Jaghunds for specialists from other Jaghunds to advise or assist on other cases, or to act on information in cases from opposing Jaghunds.

          The Kennel is located within the HQ for the BfV, in a far back office section that houses a small library of files on previous cases and information. Despite being only made up of a single cell, each member of the Kennel is an expert at administration and public administration, ensuring that cells in the field never have to fear for their supplies. Conversely, this means that few agents in Kennel have any field experience. Despite this, agents of the Kennel don’t long for glory. They come in, do their jobs, and live to continue to do so. Each file is accounted for, and each weapon is tracked, with the famous German efficiency and diligence.

          Despite their efforts, the Kennel is still a governmental office. Files are still misplaced or misnamed, the agents in the field might not give enough details to the agent on the other end of the phone, and sometimes a simple sick day can mean that a phone call asking for orders goes unanswered.

          The enemies of the Kennel aren’t monsters, they’re the bureaucrats trying to make a name for themselves by trying to cut wasteful spending. Often, these are easily deflected by the greater BfV, but with the Eurozone struggling to keep afloat, more of these bureaucrats are showing up calling for an audit on SG-14. The fear is that the enemies of Germany who hide in the shadows can use these means to subvert Germany’s protection, so each member of the Kennel is briefed on how to wrangle the budget and keep things quiet. This means each member of the Kennel learns quickly how to navigate the national bureaucracy.

          Status: To gain status in the Kennel, an agent just does their job, nothing else matters, measured by how successful they are at being a go-between for agents in the field. However, if an agent is blatantly fouling up the tasks given to them then status is easily dropped.

          Status 1: You’re given the brunt of the office work; filing junk reports, cross-referencing previous incidents, and occasionally squashing a small flying spider with a thick binder. Mercifully, you also get the specialty: Government Files for either Research or Investigation, as well as the Bureaucratic Navigator merit automatically.

          Status 3: After a couple of years, you’ve started to gain a knack for making sure the agents that contact you get their equipment quickly and with exactly what they’d need by getting the proper forms filled. Treat this as Politics: Paperwork.

          Status 5: You know the insides of the file room inside and out, and you’ve been out in the field at least once on observation, and seen what the field agents go through. You can apply this as a version of the Encyclopedic Knowledge merit, but only in regards to the files and information stored by Schutzgruppen 14.

          SILBER JAGHUND/SILVER HOUND
          Therianthropes

          Silver Hound was the first of the original four jaghunds formed to combat the paranormal in post-WWII Germany. Werewolves roamed nearly unchecked after the Nazis fell from power, stealing and murdering through Germany, the situation only exasperated by the now-proven nonexistant “Werwolf” partisan movement that the Allied forces had to investigate. From 1950 onward, Silber Jaghund hunted through Germany’s cities for werewolf packs that had decided it was easier to kidnap, rape and kill than live as productive members of society, with its remit eventually expanding to include all shapeshifters in Germany’s borders.

          Typically, Silver Hound tries to understand werewolves, reminding themselves that persecution of an entire group for the crimes of the few can lead to darker places. So Silver Hound attempts to communicate with wolf tribes and packs, letting them know that they are not to be killed simply for existing. Understandably, most werewolves simply try to run and hide, or fight the agents sent to find them. It was only after an incident involving GSG-9, a farm in Lower Saxony, and a blood-soaked barn, that SG-14 found a wolf-blooded human willing to talk.

          As far as can be determined, there are two factions of werewolves; one that attempts to patrol the world destroying spirits that would threaten humanity, and a faction that wants to force humans to the positions of breeding stock and worse. Other shapeshifters usually try to stay away from the larger conflicts of the world, and Silver looks over them on a case-by-case basis. If the shifter is merely a deer-woman trying to find a place where she and her flock can roam and graze in peace, the government will advise them on places safe to stay. If the shifter is a cat-man stalking the ghettos killing criminals that escape the law, that’s when Silver breaks out the weaponry.

          Despite their name, Silver has discovered that the silver bullet might be worthless against a shapeshifter. If the creature isn't a werewolf, a trial an error process has to be undertaken to find a specific chemical weakness of some kind in case of future conflicts against a similar breed. It's resulted in many casualties over the years, and the fact is that these files are few and far between. Most agents have to put in some time to actually find anything useful.

          Frequently, Blue unit is attached to Silver’s operations, since many witches have the ability to morph into animals with their own powers. Red sometimes is attached as well, since some vampires have the powers to become wolves or swarms of bats. Usually, Silver agents don’t give a damn. They just file the paperwork and shunt the agents back to their own hounds as soon as the mission is completed.

          More often, an agent of Black unit causes the most problems for Silver. Since some spirits can cause bodily “adaptations” in a human being, but lack any vulnerability to metals, many a Black Hound liaison has been killed or wounded trying to expose the target to a spirit’s ban instead of a therianthrope’s weakness. While Silver understands what the Black liaisons are there for, they still would prefer it if Black learned to slow down their spirit search until the target is restrained securely to make sure if it’s a therianthrope or actual spirit-possession.

          Status: To gain status in Silver Hound, members need to ensure either the safe negotiation on behalf of Germany to shapeshifters, thus avoiding conflict, or conduct research on shapeshifters that reveals unknown data on the creatures. Murdering a shapeshifter, while valuable, is not seen as highly as the previous two.

          Status 1: The predatory aura that surrounds many shapeshifters stops affecting you as heavily. When affected by Lunacy, treat your Willpower as 1 higher than it is.

          Status 3: You’ve learned a lot about various breeds of shapeshifters. Apply this as an Occult specialty to a specific type of shapeshifter.

          Status 5: You’re at the point where you’re able to practically smell the shapeshifters, no matter what they look like. Treat this as the Unseen Sense merit applied specifically to a single species of shapeshifter.

              BLAU JAGHUND/ BLUE HOUND
              Witches, Psychics, Relics

              Witches were heavily embedded into the Nazi regime during the war, and when the Reich fell, these witches scattered. Some attempted to cling to the power they had gained during the war, others tried to quietly extend their lives for a time when the glory of the Aryan race could live again. Other witches worked tirelessly to destroy Nazism, seeking a way to end the horrors their beliefs were put through, whether they were gypsies who practiced folk healing or psychics who tried to battle in secret with their abilities.

              Blau unit was formed alongside Rot unit, to track down witches that collaborated with the Nazis to find new occult weapons and systems to ensure an Aryan victory. Himmler himself was a major proponent of the occult, and when he committed suicide, many of his secrets went to the grave with him. So Blue was forced to search through camp after camp, lab after lab, holding the threat of trial over many members of the research staff who had gone into hiding for information.

              One group escaped this purge; a group of perfect hermaphrodites, with a single eye on back of their skull behind their hair, known as the Eisler Group. This group, guilty of assisting in experimentation on political and religious prisoners, is reportedly still in existence, with its surviving members still alive despite their age. Despite scattering to the winds, Blau has evidence that indicates some of these individuals are still in Germany, practicing their dark arts.

              In the case of other witches, Blau tries to approach them in their civilian lives, trying to work with them in understanding what they do with their powers, how these powers function, and which witches are which. A hausfrau casting little spells of hearth and home harms no one, while a cult built around arcane power and human sacrifice usually gets multiple cells to destroy it. Psychics are approached similarly; an old man using his abilities to find killers for the police is different from a horny teen using his “gifts” to force women into his bed.

              Relics are a different matter. Records indicated that the Nazi government found many in both the deserts of Africa and the snowy wastes of Scandinavia. Whether these really won the war in the early years for Germany, or were so dangerous that they were sealed away, Blau Jaghund is tasked with finding the hiding places for these relics. So far, nothing concrete, and it doesn’t help that some groups would kill to find these powerful artifacts. Other relics are cataloged, filed, and kept in secure facilities to ensure that they cannot be used by others.

              More often than not, Blau must grudgingly work alongside Silber in cases of recorded shapeshifting, often because the shapeshifter might be nothing more than an unfortunate victim that, hopefully, can be turned back. They also need to go in alongside many Rot kill-teams, since what might be a vampire is possibly a witch using blood-based sorcery.

              Where Blau runs into the biggest problems, however, is in trying to work alongside Agency Cooperation. Blau has information on groups such as the “Cult of the Phoenix”, the “Guardians of the Labyrinth”, and other occult groups inside of Germany’s borders that are most definitely using magic or magic-empowered objects. The Kennel and AZ usually let a single attaché from Blue accompany an AZ team on missions, but for now, command is content to let these hunters do the dirty work for the Bund until they start causing trouble, something that worries Blue Hound.

              Status: Witches are often still human beings, and need to be treated as such. Successfully finding and negotiating with a witch to ensure their abilities are only utilized in times of either extreme need or at the government’s behest earns a healthy amount of respect from an agent’s superiors. Killing a witch without evidence they were even dangerous, however, can mean a massive drop in status.

              Status 1: You don’t know everything, but you know enough of the basics about witches. You gain the specialty Occult: Witchcraft

              Status 3: As time goes on, you’ve become skilled in certain specific subsets of occult practices and their followers. You get the following two specialties for free under Occult: Alchemy, Cultural Beliefs, Paganism, Satanism or Superstitions.

              Status 5: You’re an expert at waiting out the wizards and witches now, knowing exactly when they’re using their abilities that will prove what they are. All Perception rolls are considered Rote Actions, the equivalent of the Trained Observer merit in Dogs of War.

              ROT JAGHUND/RED HOUND
              Vampires, Ghouls

              Red has a relatively straightforward mission compared to many of the other jaghunds; find vampires and their blood slaves. The trick then becomes finding where the vampires are hiding, and how to get to them. Vampires have never been a major problem in Germany, but it has been known by Rot Jaghund that vampires moving from Eastern Europe attempt to settle in Germany, in hopes of avoiding old ties. Native vampires sometimes adopt the trappings of Nazism, in order to give themselves an air of mystery and diabolical natures.

              To effectively fight these beasts, Rot has to enter into their world. Agents are sent in undercover, given fake IDs and backgrounds as bloodslaves or ghouls. Certain agents become the willing servants of the vampires, others feign resistance as they are slowly brought into the vampire’s web of servants and slaves. They are monitored as best as they can be by fellow agents, and try to keep their minds clear as vampire blood and powers suck their minds into black pits that allow for only a microscopic chance of escape. Countered with the threat of non-governmental hunter groups looking to kill off as many slaves as they can, or worse, save them without proper understanding of what they’re getting themselves into, and Rot unit has no stroll in the park.

              Rot unit works slow. It drains back accounts, it dismantles ghoul societies. If a lower ghoul becomes suspicious, the Rot operative is authorized to neutralize their threat. If a vampire from an opposing faction is attempting to overtake another, Rot operatives will try to coordinate efforts to destabilize both. Yet Rot Jaghund can’t be everywhere, and at times a single vampire without a large power base can simply be staked and sent into the sun with little issue. And the ghouls left behind after the vampire is ash? The budget is strained enough; rehabilitation is the ghoul’s own problem.

              Often, Rot is made up of those who have already had negative encounters with vampires already, becoming driven in their hatred of the creatures that ruined their lives once before, primarily undercover agents. For the rest of the Jaghund, the members are trained in staking and killing any vampire they come across, and getting undercover Rot agents out of danger. While many other hunters see this as more mercy than most vampires deserve, the rest of SG-14 does not think much of Rot Jaghund. Rot has filed multiple reports that vampires already have a society that polices itself, and information from both "friendly" (bribed) vampires and other hunters seems to indicate that if Rot somehow managed to destroy all of vampire society in Germany, this would leave any survivors alone, and desperate to do whatever they needed to in order to survive. Not only that, but Hundhutte feels that Rot is often far to quick to judge.that all vampires are worth a stake. In an organization that prides itself on being willing to look into the realities of the hidden world, the fact that many Rot operatives have suffered from negative actions at the hands of vampires makes Rot a possible point of contention within the organization.

              Since vampires have such a varied number of supernatural abilities, and their ghouls can take on such abilities as well, Red and Blue Hounds have often started fighting each other over keeping a valuable ghoul informant alive, and often times Blue unit reports fail to mention the blood-magic being used by the “witch” that died from rapid aging.

              Silver also never gives Red much rest. From vampires turning into wolves, bats, and even a sentient horde of insects and bugs, Red often has to remind a Silver attaché that the target isn’t going to fall, no matter how much silver they pump into it. Silver bullets have already been tried against vampires; the squad that tried is still unaccounted for.

              An attaché is also usually put into Gelb Jaghund, in order to ensure that the undead being hunted is truly undead and not a vampire, something the Rot attaché will almost always go out of their way to prove.

              Status: In Rot unit, status is easily gained by gathering as much information as possible on vampiric societies and targets. Elimination of an undead enemy garners some recognition in the Bund, but not much.

              Status 1: You don’t know much about vampires in the field, but you’ve read plenty of Rot Jaghund’s files to make up for it. Gain the Occult: Vampire specialty for free.

              Status 3: You’ve seen plenty of the tricks up a vampire’s sleeve, and even though you aren’t immune from them, you’re as ready as you’ll ever be. Take either two dots in the Indomitable merit, or the Unbondable merit, the only time this merit can be taken outside of character creation. All other prerequisites apply as normal.

              Status 5: Having killed enough bloodsuckers and their slaves, you’ve learned to read the signs of when they’re around. Treat this as Unseen Sense: Vampires, or if your ST chooses, Unseen Sense: Vitae.

                  SCHWARZ JAGHUND/ BLACK HOUND
                  Ghosts, Spirits, Demons, Incorporeals, Possession

                  Schwarz cut its teeth on some of the world’s worst hauntings, hauntings so strong and spiritual scars so deep, many in the unit know that their jobs will never be finished. They have gone to Dachau on seven separate occasions to keep the spirits inside from spilling out. Bergen-Belsen has only just recently been deemed fully safe for tourist visits. Buchenwald always has one possession a week, and a member of the Jewish faith trained in exorcisms is kept on retainer at all times. They’re just about given up on clearing Auschwitz, and Treblinka hasn’t even gotten a dent in it, so they’ve set up checkpoints to ensure those leaving are clean.

                  The ghosts Schwarz fights are not only the ghosts and spirits of the dead. They are fighting the ghosts of a dead ideology that wanted to overwhelm the world with its hate. With that hate, thousands of ghosts and spirits were formed, looking to return Germany to its Third Reich, combined with the hundreds of ghosts and spirits that appear on German soil every day, from Teutonic knights claiming medieval castles to spirits of the Black Forest looking for hosts to spread the wild lands to where it used to be.

                  Of all the jaghunds, Schwarz agents are the most likely to be or become more spiritual in their actions, thought spiritual or religious convictions are not necessary for the position. Yet the constant interaction between the dead and demonic beings that exist can force Schwarz agents to accept that sometimes a spiritual explanation is sometimes right, and reconcile their own beliefs on such matters.

                  They’re also the most likely to travel and liaise with other groups outside of Germany. Due to the nature of possession and spirit hosts, an agent may have to hunt down a tourist who went back home with more than a few souvenirs, or find a traveling group of German nationals who are carrying an anchor object, if not being anchors themselves.

                  Often, Black Hound sends an attaché to “work” with Silver. They’ve noticed there is an upswing in spiritual activity near wolf dens, and do their best to try and accompany any Silver cell with a specialist ready to exorcise the spirits possessing the humans nearby. Silver just tries to ignore the attaché until the subject can be subdued.

                  Blue and Black Hounds also don’t get along easily when it comes to spirit-focused investigations. Some witches are able to channel the abilities of spirits to their will, primarily for more criminal activities. Blue always feels the witch is the bigger problem (that which possibly little more than a living anchor), but Black wants to go the safer route and remove the witch’s serving entities. Which is why Blue always tries to make a run for the goal and eliminate the possible witch, and why Black has to prove that only the spirits need neutralization.

                  Rot and Schwarz rarely interact except where rampant vampiric activity is the cause of a recent upswing in an area’s spiritual activity. Often the two simply deal with their separate problems and try to not step on each other’s toes.

                  Status: Status is gained in Black Hound by either resolving the unfinished business of a ghost or spirit, as well as exorcising demons. Gaining information on various types of incorporeal creatures or just destroying a ghost’s anchors are also valuable. To lose status is incredibly difficult, but should an agent simply focus on the target and not the human cost around them, other agents may find their reports suddenly cited as a red light by the Kennel.

                  Status 1: Schwarz might not have all the knowledge they need, but they have enough to give their agents an upper hand. Treat this as Occult: Ghosts.

                  Status 3: Schwarz has a supply of secret, supposedly blessed items (WOD Rulebook pg 214) that have detrimental effects on ghosts, spirits and demons. Blau knows nothing about them since Schwarz agents take care to exclude them by calling them “destroyed” in the records. The Kennel knows the truth, but for now lets the actions continue. The Spezialgebiet Ausrüstung collection continues to grow.

                  In mechanical terms, this means all three-dot and higher members of Schwarz Hunde gain a two-dot blessed object or, Storyteller permitting, a two-dot relic related to ghosts/spiritual/demonic activity. This can be created using either the endowment equation for the Aegis Kai Doru or rules from Reliquary.

                  Status 5: Despite all logic on how it might work, your name is known around the invisible world, and it’s not exactly well received. Every spirit you come into contact with instinctively flees in your presence. This can either be the Null merit (Spirit Slayers pg 140), or the Saintly merit (Book of Spirits pg 110).



                  GRÜN JAGUNHUND/GREEN HOUND
                  Interdimensional Entities, Alternate Dimensions

                  Green Hound formed in the wake of the neopagan revival of Germanic beliefs in the 1980s. Fearful this was an attempt to utilize paranormal forces to recreate a German empire, the Kennel formed a mixed group of agents to discern exactly what was happening. Made up primarily of Schwarz and Blau agents, with two agents from Rot and Silber, Grün tracked some of the neopagans to a ritual where animals were being handed over to something that had horns growing from its forehead and a beard of thick holly bushes. Breaking up the gathering and taking prisoners, the resulting interrogations led to the creation of Grün Jaghund in 1987.

                  Green has had to dive deep into German and Scandinavian folklore for more detailed information. Members typically keep a hand-forged iron combat knife on them at all times, even off-duty. Some trawl through abduction support groups for stories that don’t appear to be the result of drugs or drinking, while others scan the missing persons through the nation for cases that lack a clear explanation, if any at all for the disappearance.

                  Green has actually made some small headway into reaching out to the native changeling community in Germany, offering them refuge in exchange for information. Sometimes these changelings are more trouble than they’re worth, and Grün is constantly trying to prevent its own agents from being stolen back into the strange dimension the fae call home. Other instances have Green running to and fro as mediators, trying to prevent the creatures from killing each other, and innocent people in the process. These are rare incidents, however, as most changelings are often willing to throw an agent to the dogs to protect themselves from being taken again.

                  Unsurprisingly, this causes no end of moral problems for Green. The changeling creatures were, at one point, human, and are similar to the thousands of refugees and foreign nationals that reside in Germany for asylum. Treating changelings as a group as criminals and unwanted individuals strikes too close to the past for comfort, yet trying to root through their social structure, freeholds, and whatever they classify themselves into takes time that could have been spent trying to prevent these issues in the first place.

                  A more pressing weakness is manpower. Green is a relatively new part of the Bund, and so is understaffed compared to the other Hounds. As more and more fairies are discovered Green has more and more reason to recruit other hunters into their unit. Right now, barely anyone has answered. Most human beings that have had interactions with fae are either hopelessly enslaved, maddeningly obsessed, or drugged to the gills. To recruit from these pools makes them, and by extension the rest of the Bund dangerously compromised. Yet as more dreams are turned to nightmares and citizens turned into something utterly alien, Green is going to have to make a choice soon.

                  Green has also been tasked with the exploration of other dimensionalities, where it runs into the most friction with others Hounds. Black and Green have been locking horns for years now, Black trying to prevent Green from prying into their investigations. Green is convinced that ghosts are part of another class of breaches in the dimensional fabric, and need to be compared to fairies.

                  Status: Green is more interested in finding fae gatherings than individuals. Locating a changeling market or just a house full of the creatures will make it easier the next time Green agents must go fairy hunting. Loss of status can occur if a fae creature is eliminated without proper reasons as to why. Too many dead changelings, and AZ might come calling.

                  Status 1: All new agents are given a cold iron combat weapon, the same material that is said to have a detrimental effect on fae. The agent gains not only a specialty in said weapon, but that weapon deals either 2 lethal or 3 bashing damage.

                  Status 3: Learning in the field has taught you more than using the Grün handbook. Treat this as a 9-again on Occult rolls related to fae or the Hedge, as well as other dimensions.

                  Status 5: After dozens of experiences with dimensional breaches and fae slave rings, you’ve nearly gained a sixth-sense to finding them. You gain Unseen Sense: Fairies, though seeing through the mask that hides them is another matter entirely.

                      GELB JAGHUND/YELLOW HOUND
                      Reanimates, Undead, Misc

                      Everyone knows that Victor Frankenstein created his horror in Germany, not far from Ingolstadt in Bavaria. Few know that the Nazis made copies of this research, in the hopes of creating a race of expendable super-soldiers to use as shields for more valuable living troops. From the bodies of Soviet prisoners, the minds of American and British prisoners, and the “miscellaneous” parts of various others, the Nazi SS though they would be able to create the perfect soldier in little under a year.

                      Come the 21st century, and Gelb unit is still trying to rectify what had happened. After only a few weeks of the project, the German military burned the base it was on and evacuated the area, leaving whatever prisoners were left to their fate and the monsters they created to hopefully rot. Gelb wasn’t so lucky. Some of the creatures being tested on escaped into Germany, and into the wider world during the post-war confusion. Some followed the course the Monster took in the book, fleeing towards the Arctic Circle. Others followed fleeing Nazis to South America, hiding out in the slums and rainforests. Since the rediscovery of such monsters during the Lüneburg Heath Fire in 1975, Yellow has had to follow each lead through the decades, tracking each monster with only half-burned SS records to guide them.

                      Yellow has a reputation for being one of the most emotionless of the various Hounds. Where other agents will at least try to relax and take their minds away from the horrors they have seen, Yellow agents appear introverted and separated from the rest of mankind. The unit has one of the highest washout rates in SG-14, and the average expectancy for green members is roughly three months, taking into account the relative rarity of intelligent reanimates and the strange emotional magic that surrounds them.

                      More commonly, the emotional toll comes from whenever Yellow agents are called to neutralize an infestation of zombies and other unintelligent undead. The detachment is a necessary evil, so the agents don’t dwell on the people that they have to eliminate in the course of their actions. Cases of Yellow agents putting bullets through their heads after neutralizing a “family” of zombies are unnervingly common, and the Kennel has one dedicated psychiatrist in Yellow’s offices at all times, and another constantly travelling to check on the various field agents.

                      A smaller unit than the others, Yellow is out of its depths when it comes to proper cataloging and identification of its targets. Intelligent reanimates are rare creatures, and the unintelligent undead have so many varying causes that marking them all down takes time. It also gets the brunt of the Bund’s stranger cases, from cryptid sightings in the Black Forest to UFO reports on the Baltic. In reality, it’s the dumping ground for cases that the other jaghunds have no real interest in pursuing without the necessary push from the Kennel. This also forces the few hunters in Gelb to become experts at dealing with the unexpected, able to deal with what at first appears to be a spaceship, only for a long tentacle to form out of its landing legs.

                      Status: Since no one really cares about the undead the way they worry about vampires, Yellow operatives can easily gain status by killing any reanimated or undead they come across. Any even better indicator of status is successfully cataloging a new entity or incident for the Bund’s files.

                      Status 1: Since they deal with them the most, green Yellow agents learn as much as possible about the strange or the undead. This translates as either an Occult specialty in either Forteana or Undead.

                      Status 3: With all the weird shit you’ve seen, Yellow agents often find comfort in the thing they use most; their weapons. They gain the Favored Weapon merit.

                      Status 5: By this point, most Yellow agents have either washed out or are so hardened they barely feel anymore. They also know exactly what’s out there now. This acts as either Unseen Sense: Divine Fire (the life source of some reanimated monsters), or Unseen Sense: Forteana.

                      AGENTUR ZUSAMMENARBEIT/ AGENCY COOPERATION
                      Slashers, Hunters, “Internal Matters”

                      Despite the rather benign name, the various Hounds quake when they hear it. The Kennel only keeps them in reserve, for times when the crisis is truly great. The reason for this fear? AZ watches the watchmen.

                      “Publicly” implemented after a disastrous incident involving a rogue member of SG-14 (rumored to still be on the run today in the Ardennes), AZ’s duty is to police not only SG-14, but all of Germany’s hunters to prevent civilian casualties and knowledge of the paranormal. This remit affords them not only great respect, but a large amount of fear from their fellows. With one recommendation, an AZ agent can not only have a fellow hunter imprisoned, but darker rumors suggest that AZ agents are allowed to “terminate on sight”, a rare order even for the emotionless members of Yellow Hound.

                      At first, AZ was a rumored “fifth” hound in the earliest days that the Bund was formed, comprised of Allied personnel ordered to make sure that the earliest Gestapo elements in SG-14 were kept in line. As time passed and the Nazi elements were removed, AZ was expanded to investigate cases of the “Mörder”, or “Slayer” phenomena. Eventually, it was also tasked with investigating the newly reunited Loyalists of Thule after the farmhouse bombing outside of Paris in ’68, which gradually involved investigating other hunter organizations within Germany’s borders.

                      AZ has had to deal with everything, from amateur filmmakers trying to expose monsters to the world, to Catholic terrorists attempting the firebombing of government offices to “expose the corruption”. It also has the daunting task of combing through the BfV files to cross-reference any serial murder cases with possible slayer sightings.

                      This also means that, in the course of its duties, AZ has had to imprison and even kill its fellow Bund hunters. The Kennel only hands down such orders when the evidence is clear and present, but the fact that they cannot let the members of the target’s Hound know what is ordered means that AZ hunters are seen as little more than cold blooded killers, blindly following orders without questioning the morality, earning them the enmity and fear of all other SG-14 agents.

                      Status: Despite what the other Hounds think, agents in AZ gain status for proving at their fellows aren’t going insane or are plants for other groups. It can also be gained by either convincing smaller hunter groups to put down their torches and give up the Vigil, or recruiting more experienced hunters into SG-14.

                      Status 1: In order to prove their fellows are still loyal, AZ hunters need to be skilled in finding out everything about another hunter. This becomes a specialty in only one of the following: Investigation: Body Language, Larceny: Electronic Security, or Streetwise: Body Language.

                      Status 3: You’ve formed a web of informants across a wide spectrum of hunters and specialists in the paranormal. This becomes two dots in the Contacts merit, as long as it applies to hunters or hunter groups.

                      Status 5: The field is not a place for you anymore, but you can still pour over the data you get from the safety of your computer. You also have a younger AZ agent as a free two-dot Retainer.
