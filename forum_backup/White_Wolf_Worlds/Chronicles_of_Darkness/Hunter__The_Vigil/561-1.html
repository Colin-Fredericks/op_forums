                    Just to start with, Eldagusto's example is a good one: A member of an ancient mystic order that while recognisably Jewish has gone of in their own direction for a very long time.


                    But more generally, I wrote Hakollel to be a Promethean-focused Conspiracy as well as a Jewish Conspiracy. That means that Promethian's theme of "Pilgrimage" or just change, growth, development is an important aspect. The parallels make for some really interesting characters; a Promethian who grows through learning partnered with a Rabbi who grows through teaching. A Promethian as a blank slate compared trying to fill that emptiness compared to a Rabbi as a product of millennia of tradition trying to adapt to the modern world.

                    The theme of Change/Pilgrimage is most prominent in The House of Israel ben Eliezer, which basically is me saying "pick these guys if you want your character go on his own Pilgrimage equivalent" but that's hardly the only time it shows up: Many of the earlier groups such as the Sons of Loew were changed significantly by Hakollel's birth. Hakollel's formation is directly tied to the formation of Israel, a major change for Jewdism itself.

                    For that reason I think Hakollel should include the ways Jewdism has been changing, accept the new movements* adherents as members. You can't really explore the question of how a millennia old tradition adapts without including half the actual real life examples of it doing so.


                    * Within reason, believing that God hasn't intervened during history dosn't make much sense in a group where His Names have magic powers and angel watching is a highly respected profession.

                        Just to start with, Eldagusto's example is a good one: A member of an ancient mystic order that while recognisably Jewish has gone of in their own direction for a very long time.
                        Such as who? A hypothetical group that doesn't exist isn't really a good example of a concept that doesn't work.

                        In contemporary Jewish history this really hasn't been a huge problem. Haredi Ashkenaz groups found they have way more in common with haredi Sephardi and Mizrahi groups than they do with more liberal Ashkenaz Jews and, while they don't agree on lots of stuff (which is normal for them because they have their own internal arguments over stuff anyway), accept each other as haredim. The closest thing to this example happening in reality ended up with the small group rejecting wanting anything to do with the larger Jewish orthodox community, not the other way around.

                        A group of haredi mystics from, say, a remote part of India that have finally made contact with the rest of the Jewish world are going to have a very different tradition than Ashkenaz haredim, but if they still follow haredi principles there's no reason why they would be shunned from an all orthodox Hunter group; one that's already got lots of different schools of orthodoxy in it as it is.

                        Heresy in Judaism is a pretty tricky thing to label because outside of some very core concepts (which are pretty hard to go against and still be Jewish in a meaningful way) there isn't an actual authority to be heretical against.

                        That means that Promethian's theme of "Pilgrimage" or just change, growth, development is an important aspect.
                        I get that, and I very much appreciate that part of this concept.

                        But the point I'm trying to make is that you don't need to look outside the orthodox Jewish community to find this. Orthodox Judaism is in a state of flux right now as some groups are starting to push harder for modernization and reconsideration of long held rules. We're seeing orthodox Jews push for things like more equality for women, a better stance on homosexuality in the community, removing a lot of the isolationist walls the ultra-orthodox have built up, etc. That's sort of the whole impetus behind the modern orthodox movements.

                        I also think, to an extent, the theme of "pilgrimage" is harder to pull off if you make the group too inclusive. Because instead of the people in the group undergoing a journey of discovery and re-evaluation leading to change, the change becomes an external element brought in by different people with different ideas.

                                I added a new sidebar about binding Golems. This side-bar was a delicate balance, picking on Promethians is especially bad in my books because they're basically as innocent and vulnerable as possible (at least in the beginning). But I didn't want to ignore the fact that ultimately the most famous rabbi-with-magic story was, in the WoD, about a Rabbi magically controlling a sentient being who didn't like being controlled. At the same time I wanted a real justification for why The Sons of Loew wouldn't have learned better since then. Preferably one that wouldn't end up destroying all sympathy.


                                I ended up turning to the original story to design the drawbacks instead of Disquiet and the Wasteland. This is for hunter so I can overrule bits of Promethian if I want . In one version of the original story it went on a rampage because Rabbi Loew forgot to turn it off for the Sabath, in another it fell in love but was rejected. This led to my two rules: Unkosehr behavior may trigger a Golem escape, and not treating it correctly correctly can trigger its escape and rampage. (It's a bit of a damned if you do, damned if you don't isn't it. What are you supposed to do if you don't love it back? Though the impossibility of that situation could be seen as, basically, The Point)


                                It also leads to a neat bit of metaphysics IMO. The reason this works is because it usees a theologian who, whether you agree with the answers in Jewdism or not, has at least studied the big questions about humanity - the same ones every Promethian is on a Pilgramage to learn - a lot more than the average person. In essence, instead of overcoming Disquiet by learning morality, the Promethean overcomes disquiet by becoming an extension of someone who has mastered morality well enough to think for two.

                                And if you screw up at being moral such as by forgetting to take the scroll out for the Sabbath, the whole magic collapses. (Incidentally this points to an answer to the impossible conundrum above. Find a good match for the Golem before he falls in love with a bad match).

                                        Just to get this out the way; Hakollel have knowledge about pronunciation that real life Jewish scholars (and non-Hakollel WoD Jewish scholars) do not. That's the core of Shemot.


                                        Now for your core question. That's... not quite "the point", but it's supposed to be there. In my understanding part of the point for rules about banning graven images and taking the Lord's name in vain is to forbid trying to control god through rituals. The religions nearby would use idols in rituals to ward off disease, bring rain, etc, and the ancient Jews said you mustn't try to force God's hand like that.

                                        From this perspective having magic powers that you can call upon at will by speaking God's name is insanely blasphemous.

                                        And that's intentional on my part. I'm not actually saying or intending that Hakollel are blasphemous or bad Jews; rather, I'm saying that Hakollel's Judaism is not the same as Judaism in the real world. Just for one thing, they can and do challenge God Himself to rabbinic debates. For another thing, if God exists in the real world he's not the same as the Abrahamic God found in the WoD. The Divine Fire from Promethian might literally be the Abrahamic God, and that thing has a few important differences from the God portrayed in the Bibile.


                                        I admit I never asked any rabbis when writing Hakollel, but I think in the real world do not take the Lord's name in vain would mean that Shemot are forbidden. Hakollel interpret it "in vein" as "frivolusly": do not use Shemot frivolously. In the world of Darkness, where history, theology, and God Himself are different, they may be entirely right. Or they may be entirely wrong.


                                        Basically: Hakollel breaking rules like not speaking the name of Yhwh is intentional. It's supposed to ask questions about whether Hakollel have been tempted away from holyness by the temptation of magic power; questions about the nature of God in the WoD; and also just simply reflect the fact that the WoD is not the same as our world and thus there are subtle differences between real life Jewdism and WoD Jewdism. There are, intentionally, no answers provided by me. Though I do have my own private preferences.


                                        I can understand that some people might be uncomfortable with players trying to pronounce "Yhwh" but there is a reason I chose that word. My suggestion would be to say Adonai or Adonai-Rapha with the understanding that when you say Adonai your character is saying "Yhwh".

                                                There's probably not that much to worry about.

                                                As organisations go this one (Endowment aside) is on the borderline between Compact & Conspiracy anyway. Structurally most of HsH is individual Shemot wielders (mostly Rabbis) each with their own loyal cells; and no central authority to help coordinate the work of different cells. That's practically a Compact already, so I imagine that your shrunk down version would probably look much like HsH does now, just with fewer members.

                                                One thing I would watch out for is that Israel's political system gives a lot of kingmaker power to tiny parties - so it's very easy for a group like this to have quite a bit of influence. If you want to shrink them down to just the UK or just Newcastle I'd suggest reworking the backstory to say that HsH was formed from an alliance of Newcastle scholars and hunters - when monsters discovered the scholars had rare scrolls they traded knowladge of Shemot to the hunters in exchange for protection.

                                                But in general HsH is already very Compact (Endowment excepted) like so you should be fine. Let me know if you have any other questions.
