    What Endowments Belong To Extinct Conspiracies? [Whimsy!Thoughtbash]

    12-01-2015, 02:42 AM
    You know, it would be kind of interesting to come up with some "extinct" Endowments, whose parent Conspiracy was destroyed/co-opted. I can see having scraps of them available mechanically, in terms of "these are the notes you could probably scrape up on how to use the Endowment in game" - you can get an edge if you're just a lone Cell, or can try to expand on those shreds of knowledge in an attempt to give your Compact some "big guns".

    Here's one that I've been thinking of:

    The "Vierineinz" was an short-lived Prussio-German Conspiracy whose heyday was a few decades after Jung first published his theories of psychodynamics. While few of their own records remain, a patchwork history can be worked out by looking at the records of other European conspiracies who were active in that period. As far as can be gathered, they were avid psychonauts who figured out a method of creating stable "thought-constructs" through (supposedly) mundane means, which they referred to as Daemons. While similar in theory to techniques like memory palaces, a Daemon was far more sophisticated - on the low end, the Conspiracy's members were capable of maintaining multiple trains of thought simultaneously. On the high end...

    Records report that the Vierineinz were capable of near super-human feats of memory, analytic thought, and self-control - while other Conspiracies were better equipped to fight monsters, the Vierineinz were an almost ideal counter to entrenched Supernatural threats. At their height, they possessed a rudimentary empathic network that spanned Europe, could pick out the ripples caused by a conspiracy by glancing through a few different newspapers, and were capable of precisely determining and counteracting nearly any amount of supernatural tampering with their minds, up to and including the mental changes caused by retroactive changes to history.

    Up until roughly a month prior to their dissolution, the Vierineinz were on good terms with the other major players in the European Conspiracy "scene" - it was relatively common to attach a Vierineinz Cell to a given major operation, simply due to the massive increase in brainpower and intelligence analysis.

    It's a shame that they had to be put down like rabid dogs.

    The theory at the time was that a member became a Slasher while connected to the empathic network, thus infecting every other member - though there was some muttering that they all descended into Slasherdom on purpose. In either case, they suddenly declared open war on every other Conspiracy they were aware of - which led to their relatively rapid annihilation, though not before they unleashed several incredibly nasty memetic attacks. Their targets ended up having to deal with unwitting moles for decades after the fact - one of the Vierineinz' more clandestine projects were weaponizing the techniques that allowed them to create their own Daemons.

    In modern nights, anyone who wanted to pull together their old methods would find that anything more advanced than the basic mental optimization methods and "reality checks" were either lost or burnt with extreme prejudice years ago. However, they could at least get the following out of the few remaining training manuals (which were determined to be safe to read):

    • An excuse to buy dots in practically any Mental merit or a bunch of Specialities in Mental skills - the methods included are almost universally useful in terms of improving memory or mental organization.
    • The ability to buy one-dot Daemons - treat each one as a one-dot Mentor, except A) they are alternate personalities rather than other people, B) can only choose Mental skills, and C) cannot choose Resources in place of a skill. In exchange for being stuck in the budding Vierineinz' head, a Daemon allows them to pass a purely Mental Extended Action related to one of the Daemon's skills to it - if they do so, the Daemon takes over rolling its host's appropriate dice pool. On the downside, the Daemon can't do anything else without interrupting the Extended Action... but on the plus side, the host now no longer needs to pay any attention to the Extended Action, merely receiving the results once it is completed.
    • The ability to buy a specialized version of Unseen Senses; rather than being triggered by a specific type of Supernatural being, it is instead triggered whenever their memories, thoughts, or emotions have been tampered with. It may be focused (gaining the Spooked condition) to immediately indicate where the holes in memory are or reveal which trains of thought aren't theirs.

    Lost techniques include the ability to shunt unwanted mental modifications onto a Daemon, Daemons with much wider uses (such as allowing the choice of Integrity as a "skill"), access to the Advanced versions of Danger Sense, Eidetic Memory, and Library (without needing a massive Safe Place to stick their Library), weaponized Daemons, and their own versions of Egregore and Masque. Of course, any records of those techniques substantial enough to be used to teach them could be... booby-trapped.

                                      Imagine for a moment that there was an ancient "Conspiracy" way back when the Aryan people first tried to occupy the Scandinavian peninsula. They came and found that it was already occupied...by trolls. We're not talking about Fae trolls, but the trolls of Norse mythology, whom today Null Mysteries call Neanderthals. Well, trolls and humans didn't get along at all. They tried to wipe each other out. The Aryan people were no physical match for the trolls, so they prayed to Wotan to give them an edge. Odin gifted them with the Berserkergang, which allowed them to push their strength and stamina past normal human limits as long as they allowed themselves to be consumed by the bloodwrath. With the bloodwrath of the Berserkers on their side the Aryan people suddenly found themselves on equal footing with the trolls on the battlefield. Eventually, the humans spread throughout the Scandinavian peninsula, and they drove the trolls to extinction. They were successful. Too successful. With no more monsters to hunt they had no more reason to band together, no more reason to be allied and coordinated. The Norsemen soon formed into tribes and turned their violent attentions towards each other with Berserkers as the front line soldiers. Eventually, when their thoughts turned towards other lands they would go a-viking to collect goods from far off lands and bring their Berserkers with them....

                                      Were they truly Hunters with endowments, or were they early slashers of the Brute variety? Could go either way, or both ways, but if it is the former, then the Berserkergang should be found to pass from parent to child down their blood line. A descendant of the Berserkers need only awaken his bloodwrath. If treated as an Endowment, Berserkergang should ignore all damage penalties and give the Berserker one dot in both Strength and Stamina for every dot he has in the Berserkergang endowment even if it pushes those physicals past 5 dots. The berserker also gains as many points of temporary health as points of Stamina. The effect lasts for the duration of a battle, no matter how long the battle lasts. At the end of a battle the Berserker becomes exhausted, looses the bonus to physicals and suffers from all damage penalties. If the Berserker loss in temporary health points brings him down past zero or if he's been fighting for a whole 12-hours or more in game, then he dies when the ability wears off.

                                      Modern hunters who learn about Berserkers through scholarly study and manage to track down a modern day descendant of the Berserkers (all appropriate research rolls apply) should meet with the consequences as well as the benefits of trying to exploit a Berserker for the Vigil. When enraged Berserkers can't tell friend from foe, and are likely to relentlessly attack their allies if they get to close. To lethal effect. Also a Berserker who gives in to the bloodwrath should suffer moral degeneration to the point where they quickly become a Slasher after a few battles. An experience like this would make an interesting chronicle for a research-oriented Compact, and serve as an object lesson in tampering with certain sources of power just to gain an edge over monsters.
