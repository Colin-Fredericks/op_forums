    Hunter 2E Open Dev Follow-Up: Hunters and Slashers

    01-13-2016, 10:00 AM
    As a follow-up to my Open Dev post, I wanted to mention that I can confirm that I am seeking to clarify definitions for both Hunters and Slashers, and that may stretch to clarifying what monsters are as well. This is similar to clarifying tiered play, too, and I want to emphasis that that is not the same as "fixing" issues, because Hunter 1E is a great game as is. These clarifications will be presented in the same toolkit format that has been used in the past, both semantically and mechanically, but I feel as developer these ideas are something to explore in the brainstorming phase. I tend to be very thorough but dense in my development/writing style, and this often translates into the addition of a sidebar or a small section in some cases. In other instances, due to the rules changes from 1E to 2E, more effort may be required from a systems perspective. I'm also the type of dev that doesn't differentiate between "fluff" and "crunch"; I think of the rules as the engine of a game, and the setting as the vehicle. Thus, I feel that the rules should absolutely support the basic concepts of the game, regardless, and I want to get the most out of 2E to enhance gameplay.

    One of the things I've noticed from the beginning is that your (e.g. the fans) iconic definition of a Hunter is so varied, it's also bleeding over into how people think of Slashers in general. Are Slashers a type of Hunter? My feeling on this is that they are not, but this is why I need to take a closer look at these basic definitions. To me, this means that there's confusion occurring at some point, and those questions may translate to a frustration with how the 2E systems are presented or a lack of understanding as to what the Vigil is, what a slasher is, and perhaps what a monster is as well.

    If you'd like to help shape these definitions that will be supported by the 2E rules, I think the forums is a good place to discuss what the iconic, human "Hunter" who has no access to the supernatural is. Then, take that same Hunter, and tell me what the heroic and villainous versions are. I'm also interested in how the definitions shift between a Hunter who has access to the supernatural, and one who does not.

    I'd like to reinforce that nothing is set in stone at this point, and I haven't made any clear decisions yet. These discussions are hugely important--especially prior to the outline phase. Looking forward to seeing your thoughts on this.

                          Mine definitions of core terms to clarify what it is:
                          ​
                          Hunter                           - human (mortal) that are obsessed with confronting a monsters, as so called Vigil ( and more plainly "hunting" ). They do not need weapons or combat to this - Null Mysteriis and Network Zero are still hunters, even if they typically do not kill any monsters. Still, most hunters use weapons to survive the confrontations.

                          Monster - entity that prey on humanity, literally or metaphorically. Most monsters are supernatural, but not all supernatural are monsters. There are some examples of "human monsters" - people so dangerous for population they need to be put to rest. If monster looks and act like human, hunting on him is much more difficult (many times risking Integrity).

                          Serial Killer - human who kills other people in large numbers. Many can become Slashers. Other could still be hunters, if they dedicate themselves to kill "human monsters". Man killing more than two terrorist is, technically, a serial killer.

                          Slasher - human who become obsessed with killing so much, it's open him to some dark forces of Chronicle of Darkness universe. He has urge to kill people and he cannot stop it. He can rationalize himself it, but deep under his consciousness there is awoken instinct to kill others. He can have smaller or larger Dread Powers as Undertakings. Most dedicated serial killers become Slashers. Hunters can become Slashers often - as hunters obsession with confronting other beings can lend to obsession in to killing other brings - and from them to killing humans. Slashers can sate their hunger for killing preying on monsters ( like Dexter in titular show is doing ) but in the long term they need human victims.

                          Heroic and Villainous versions of Hunters:

                          Heroic Hunter - Someone that in his obsession to confront monsters is doing this in the name of saving others lives. They do what they need to save people. Like Sam and Dean in last Supernatural season - "Saving people does not mean killing monsters".

                          Villainous Hunter - Someone who is so obsessed with confronting monsters, he will use other people to get to kill monster. Those do not save people - they sacrifice people to let them kill the monster they are all from the start.

                          Slashers can come from Villainous Hunters as they make shift from "sacrifice people to kill monster" in to "kill people" themselves.

                          Having or not Endowments does not have anything to being Heroic, Villainous or Slasher Hunter.

                                  DaveB has mentioned in the past that humans in CofD have a tendency to shy away from the supernatural. That is why one of the five Breaking Point questions in the CofD Rulebook is “what [supernatural encounter] have you forgotten?” — the premise behind that question is that virtually everyone in the setting has had an encounter with the supernatural at one point or another, and nearly all of them have put that encounter out of their mind, willfully forgetting what they saw. The protagonists of the CofD core book are among these “ordinary people”, but have recently had (or are about to have) an encounter with the darkness that they cannot ignore. That's the framework of the “default” Chronicle of Darkness: ordinary people dealing as best they can with the horrors and nightmares that lurk in the shadows, despite their natural inclination to hide from it and pretend it doesn't exist.

                                  By contrast, a hunter is a human who willfully confronts the monsters on a regular basis. Whatever it is that causes ordinary people to ignore the monsters has no hold on the hunter; instead, he or she does the exact opposite and seeks out the supernatural. That's what the Vigil is: a reversal of humanity's normal instincts concerning the CofD's mysteries.

                                  That's the conceptual distinction between a “blue book mortal” and a hunter, as I see it. The mechanical distinction is almost entirely embodied in Practical Experience and its applications (in 1e, Risking Willpower is the only “template ability” that hunters have that isn't purchased with Practical Experience): as I like to think of it, Practical Experience is what a hunter learns from an encounter that a non-hunter would overlook or forget.

                                                  • serial killer: someone who has no problem killing.
                                                  • ripper: a serial killer who is driven to kill.
                                                  • scourge: a ripper who has literally become inhuman.

                                                  In 1e, “slasher” meant “ripper or scourge”, and furthermore involved an Undertaking that put the slasher a cut above an ordinary mortal (if a ripper) or turned him into an inhuman killer (if a scourge). In some ways, they felt a bit like Hunter: the Reckoning's Imbued, in that even a ripper was empowered. In 2e, I'm hoping that the definition of “slasher” gets expanded to include serial killers, leading to something like a three-tier setup, with killers being the “Entry-level Slashers” who don't have any extraordinary or supernatural abilities. This makes it easier to slide over from Hunter to Slasher, since the distinction between hunter and killer is so easy to blur.

                                                          Here's my definitions:

                                                          Hunter: A mortal human or minor template (Thamaturge, Ghoul, Wolfblood - they may not have a Supernatural Advantage) who has overcome the urge to pretend monsters do not exist that is common to humans in the World of Darkness.

                                                          Heroic Hunter examples
                                                          • Take up arms to defend mortals from supernatural predation.
                                                          • Acquire supernatural power for virtuous reasons. Such as healing magic.
                                                          • Study the supernatural knowledge for virtuous reasons. Such as a priest communing with the gods so that they can be a better spiritual leader.
                                                          • Live normal lives but advise the people around them. E.G. Comforting a friend who was bitten by a vampire. (rarely PC material)

                                                          Neutral Hunter examples
                                                          • Acquire supernatural power for personal gain. E.G. using healing magic for the money.
                                                          • Acquire supernatural knowledge for personal gain. E.G. A Null Mysteriis who's just in it for curiosity.
                                                          • Adopt an occult lifestyle simply because it works for you. E.G. joining the Silver Ladder because you think working magic and the doctrine combined make a compelling argument.
                                                          • Live a perfectly normal life, don't deny the supernatural exists, but don't pay attention to it. (rarely PC material)
                                                          • Grab some popcorn, watch the latest output from Network zero. (rarely PC material)

                                                          Villainous Hunter examples
                                                          • Acquire supernatural power for personal gain and without ethical restraint. E.G. Selling your firstborn son to a demon
                                                          • Take up arms to defend mortals from supernatural predation and no ethical restraints. E.G. Blowing up an orphanage with one monster inside.


                                                          Monster: Any supernatural creature that has at least one dot in a Power Trait (Wyrd, Primal Urge, Gnosis, Rank, etc). Supernatural creatures that are notably inhuman may qualify even without a power trait.

                                                          Slasher: A specific kind of antagonist, designed to mechanically portray the villains of the Slasher Movie genera. Note that I would consider Rippers to be probably not monsters by the above definition but Scourges to be monsters; that said I do give Scourges a Power Trait.

                                                          As a guideline for what I mean by "mechanically portray the villains of the Slasher Movie genera" I consider the following four traits key to Slashers.

                                                          1. They're a serial killer who primarally targets regular mortals.

                                                          2. They fall on a power level ranging from non-supernatural but inhumanly skilled (think Jigsaw) to low powered supernatural: Freddy Kruger is undeniably using supernatural abilities but he's only got a few tricks and I would say that a Mage or Changeling could easily outdo him at dream manipulation.

                                                          3. They have an exceptionally strong sense of theme that they're psychologically or supernaturally compelled to follow. Iconic outfits, a persistent modus operandi, favoured weapons. Etc

                                                          4. A Slasher's psychological issues form the core of their charachter and are the source of their borderline-supernatural or outright-supernatural abilities.
