                Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post

                Hunters hunt monsters. The problems of the splat all stem from a failure to resolve that problem.
                Strange alliances and stranger bedfellows is like...absolutely boilerplate for the genre, though. Hunter lives in the liminal space between man and monster, and like half the game is about how the line between them is often subjective and a matter of perspective. Exposure to the Supernatural is a breaking point because unnatural phenomena are freaky. If the relationship represented by the Touchstone is itself a mundane one, I don't really see the issue, except that it's a golden story hook to have the touchstone burn out or endangered due to the "Oh my god honey, you're a vampire?!" effect.

                But until then a lot of the supernaturals are basically just human beings 99% of the time. If a hunter's touchstone is their therapist, and the therapist is a ghostwalker, and the hunter hasn't found out yet, it seems like just a plot hook in waiting. And following the feature episode around that plot hook, the character decides to "forgive" the ghostwalking, make some accommodation, I really don't see it as fatal to the theme. Sometimes exploring a core theme of a game involves compromising it, carving out exceptions, etc. And complex relationships are such good game fodder it seems weird not to indulge it here. Maybe some other hunters are less compromised and decide the ghostwalking therapist really needs to go. Any number of directions you can take a story hook like that.

                At the very least, "Discovering your touchstone is a monster" sounds like a fun breaking point.

                                                            Can I say I love the new names?

                                                            Frankly, it's a solution I didn't see to the whole "Psychos are like less dangerous chatty Masks and have no Talent for it" problem - now, they're Hypnos, and are actually pretty on the ball and self-controlled when focusing on their ideology, thanks. Which makes them infinitely more compelling, dangerous, and has a clear link to Charmers; a Hypno is what happens when a Charmer starts wondering if they can fix what repels them about humanity, no matter how many people need to be stabbed. It also makes them rather more sympathetic, and thus compelling - on some level, the Hypno wants to connect again, and this empathy powers their obsession and their ability to organize other slashers. And try to corrupt a hunter into something like themselves.
