    Originally posted by milo v3 View Post
    The issue I see with this, is that while that is the intention the rules don't seem to reflect that. There is nothing stopping the mortal rules from representing tier 1 and tier 2 hunters.

    In Chronicles of Darkness, you can play a guy with Eye for the Supernatural, Esoteric Armoury, Tolerance for Biology, and Occult skill dots.... So a guy who knows about the occult, can identify when something is supernatural, has a stash of gear specifically to fight against the vulnerabilities of various monsters, and has had enough experience that he wont go into shock or get sick from seeing rotting zombies and monsters who have blood spikes coming out of them. In no way does that sorta thing equal &quot;Head in the sand&quot;. Mortals do stuff about the darkness.
    Thankfully there's a new edition coming out that has eight years worth of learning how to remedy that.

    For now, the difference between the two is in tone. It's up to 2e to back it up with mechanics.

        I live in a nation that has a hearty population of folks who own a gun "just in case", but don't actually do anything about crime or terrorism or anything like that.

        Mortals sometimes do something about the darkness. For the most part, they live with it, and they do something as it's needed. A lot of the time, it's "just in case". It's "So long as they don't bust down my door..." Occasionally, it's pruning.

        There's no sometime with Hunters. There's no "I've got this box of silver bullets just in case" with a hunter. There's "Something has to be done about this" that never leaves the hunter's mind for long. You could never call the extent of what they do pruning.

                                                    I argue that you can have a long term mortal game, so long as the mortals are being actively harried by the supernatural and don't actually seek it out.

                                                    A group of mortals that is either cursed or simply a natural magnet for supernatural things makes for a fine long-term mortal game. You can also string plots that hinge on the previous one, where the mortals find themselves not being able to put behind the horrible supernatural shit that keeps coming back (for example, the group moves into a haunted house, they resolve the haunting, which leads to a Geist trying to take over the house for its delicious deathly energies, which leads to the Geist's lover, a Vampire, seeking vengeance against the group for killing his lover, which leads to a Horror trying to devour them because the Vampire was keeping it at bay, which leads to a local werewolf feeling threatened by the characters' proficiency at killing the supernatural, and so on).
