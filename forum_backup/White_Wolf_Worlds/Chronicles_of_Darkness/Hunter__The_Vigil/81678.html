    The Planetary Mysteries - New Fan-Made Gutter Magic

    02-28-2014, 02:12 PM
    Note: In order to understand the content presented here, you will need Hunter: the Vigil - Witch Finders. This extra material has been made using the Gutter Magic system provided in that sourcebook.


    THE PLANETARY MYSTERIES
    Spells Born of the Heavenly Bodies














    They are strange, feared and ancient. They have brought awe and misery to hunter and witch alike. They are the born from the workings of the stars, of the celestial bodies and the vast welkin, harnessed only by those who are carefully in-tune with the highest and most mysterious of nature's forces. They have often been lost, only to be found again and harnessed by less-restrained witches; for thousands of years, hunters have tried to snuff them out, bury and burn all knowledge of these spells and their sources. As long as the Earth is in orbit, however, and the sun rises in the east and sets in the west, these strange powers will remain.

    They are the Planetary Mysteries, magic born of the heavens and worlds alien and beyond.


    A Brief History

    The Planetary Mysteries have existed since the earliest shamans of mankind. The first sun-worshiping and moon-worshiping cults tapped into them; many witches speak of an ancient sky-god, his name in a tongue now lost to mankind, sending his beloved daughter down to teach the first humans these sacred powers. She was the Dawn Goddess, riding down on a brilliant white horse that glowed like a second sun, and it was her that banished away the first monsters to ever hunt mankind. Blood-sipping demons disintegrated from her touch; flesh-eating sons of wolves and men were blinded by her heavenly glow. Eldritch sprites of thorny brambles shriveled away; charlatan willworkers were burnt alive as day broke. The Dawn Goddess taught the shamans how she manipulated the sun to work for her, and to give libations and prayers to her father, who revealed himself as the Dawn's teacher. To him, said the Dawn, they should be grateful.

    Use of the Planetary Mysteries has appeared all over the glove, whether in stone-circled ritual or dances around sacred fires. References to bringing about eclipses, causing an eternal night, the destruction of extra suns and new stars appearing in the sky are all possibly caused by powerful Planetary interference. Accounts of night-wielding tyrants being overthrown by day-bringing heroes and gods are possibly folk-memories of hunters lost to time, deified as cultures grew, expanded and flourished. Unfortunately, it can be difficult to pinpoint when and where these Mysteries were in use, as their terrifying power has resulted in others frantically trying to scrub away their existence. What is merely a recurring motif, and what is actually an account of the sun being multiplied to scorch the earth? What is actually a hero being transformed into stars, and what is actually a witch putting more stars in the sky so that she might influence how properties are made?

    Perhaps thankfully, the Mysteries presented her are not as broad as they used to be. The greater powers attributed to more alien stars and planets have been lost to history, perhaps for the better. After all, if one harnessed the terrible power of Jupiter's storms, unleashing them upon the world in a fit of vengeful rage, the worst hurricanes of Earth's history would be compared to brief showers!

    As a rule of thumb, due to the power and narrow focus they bring, all Planetary Mysteries are Tier Three Mysteries.


    SUN
    Presence
    Source: A witch with the Sun Mystery will gain Source if they meditate, for at least an hour a day, under the midday sun with no protection. This will not work on days where the sun is fully or partially obscured — the witch must be under its direct light.

    The oldest of the Planetary Mysteries, the Sun Mystery grants power over the sun's light, heat and associated properties. Revered as the Dawn's first gift to mankind (and, by extension, mortal witches), many witches with this mystery are feared by denizens of the dark. Hunters may find themselves more willing to work with those who know these powers; they illuminate dark spaces, they almost always inflict horrific damage to monsters, and there is a aura of "goodness" that surrounds this Mystery. It's also perfect for a witch that wants to appear as a shadow-banishing saviour.

    Even though this Mystery is solar-powered, it does not affect sunlight-hating creatures (like vampires) as the sun might. Sunburn will affect them like fire does, and of course, exposure to the sun during Halt the Solar Chariot will burn them as a normal sun might.


    Light the Way (*)
    Cost: None
    Action: Instant
    Duration: Scene
    Range: Self; 1 WP to affect someone else
    Dice Pool: None

    The witch is able to create a ball of sunlight that illuminates light like a strong flashlight might. By drawing on the power of the rotating sun, and how it might bring forth day after a long stretch of darkness, so too does the witch bring the gift of light to blackness-drenched places. With a point of WP spent, she is able to invoke the power of fear day might bring; against a single other, she imposes a -2 on all sight-based actions. So long as she wields that ball of light, it appears bright, dazzling and disorienting to those she temporarily curses with the power of the sun; vampires roll for Frenzy, requiring two successes. Strix are also repulsed by the light, and are at a -3 disadvantage when in sight of the witch. This is the only spell in the Sun Mystery that doesn't have a penalty associated with casting it at night, and if cast just before the sun goes down, it will continue to glow in the dark.


    First Morning's Vigour (**)
    Cost: 1 WP
    Action: Instant
    Duration: Scene / Special
    Range: Single target; may roll to affect other targets, but with a -1 penalty that stacks per target
    Dice Pool: Presence+Medicine

    The ancient dawn goddess herself is a young and healthy being — she is still firmly in the grips of Maidenhood, according to certain triple-goddess interpretations. The sun is a provider of health and wealth, and so too can its witches provide such things to those they take care of. With this power, a witch subtracts up to -2 dots worth from their Physical skills, and either give +2 to their target's Physical skills for a scene, or add +1 to a target's Physical skills until the next dawn. An exceptional success means that WP is regained at the end of the spell's duration; a dramatic failure means that a witch has -2 to her Physical Attributes until the next sunrise, her very being damaged by trying to gift her power to another. If the witch's help is acknowledged by her target, she gains a point of Source.


    Shimmering Smiles (***)
    Cost: 1 Source and 1 WP
    Action: Instant
    Duration: Scene
    Range: All targets within a radius of feet = 2 x current permanent WP level
    Dice Pool: Presence+Empathy

    As a goddess, the Daughter of Dawn could strike fear into the hearts of mere mortals. She was clearly alien to those who first witnessed her — her hair blazed with fire, her skin was golden with the first rays of sunshine, and her eyes were a pupil-lacking amber. To ease their terror and coax them into listening to her, she wove herself a glamour out of the beauty of the morning sky, and became a human woman both enchanting and spry. The disciples of Sun know that they, as willworkers, are terrifying to many; this power allows others to feel at ease around them, the warmth of the sun reflected in every smile and gesture. Success gains the witch the equivalent of the Striking Looks 4 Merit (Striking Looks 2 for GMC) for the spell's duration; all face-to-face social rolls are made at a +2. Any attempts to badmouth, discredit or blame the witch for a misdeed are at a -1, and the witch also glows slightly, standing out in darker areas. The radius of the willworker's glow is equivalent to her Presence x 2 in feet.

    On a dramatic failure, the witch receives a -2 penalty to any Social rolls for the spell length, as her glamour is improperly woven; she gives off a horrific vibe to anyone nearby. The -1 advantage described above becomes a +1 for those trying to discredit her. Exceptional successes mean that the witch regains her used WP when her glamour wears off.


    Sunburn (****)
    Cost: 2 Source and 1 WP
    Action: Instant and Resisted
    Duration: 1 Turn Per Success
    Range: All targets within a radius of feet = 4 x current permanent WP level
    Dice Pool: Presence+Intimidation

    When the Dawn Goddess marched on the denizens of shadow, she would give them warning beforehand to flee before her might. As the day broke, their skin would begin to prickle and burn, the sunlight lightly scalding them as a deterrent from the approaching goddess. When warnings were not enough, the Dawn Goddess unleashed the wrath of the sun upon the unjust. When a witch uses this power, her own aura becomes like sunlight, intense and flaring as it lunges like a snake. The witch becomes an aetheric fireball, immediately doing 1 point of lethal damage per turn that this once-per-Scene power burns brightly. If she so wishes, she may fill one of her own health boxes with lethal damage per turn to inflict aggravated damage, with a maximum number of Agg levels inflicted equivalent to her Gnosis. A dramatic failure means that all of her health boxes are filled with lethal; exceptional successes are their own reward.


    Halt the Solar Chariot (*****)
    Cost: 1 level of Aggravated damage, 4 Source and 1 WP
    Action: Extended; requires 10 successes to work, and then another roll to determine how long it works
    Duration: 1 Success Equals 2 Turns of Effect
    Range: 1 Mile sq. Per Dot of permanent WP
    Dice Pool: Presence+Stamina

    Witches at this level of power have a small measure of authority over the sun itself. Though it is incredibly painful and draining to do so — the feeling of casting is equivalent to someone twisting and slowly yanking one's entrails out — a witch can temporarily halt the passage of the sun itself across a given area. Reality bends slightly, anyone in the given range of this spell witnessing the sun halt in its place, though time will still flow as if normal; nocturnal beasts will rouse as if the sun has already set. When the spell ends, there will be a noticeable "speeding up" of the sun, which will then move and act normally once it catches up with where it should be at that time.

    A dramatic failure will fill every one of the witch's health boxes with Aggravated except for the last. Exceptional successes are their own reward. This spell will not work at night, and only may be used once per Story. (At the ST's discretion, however, they may revise it to once per Chapter; this best fits if the witch is supposed to be exceptionally powerful.)

    ***

    MODIFIERS:

    - Spell is cast during the early afternoon: +1
    - Spell is cast during midday: +2
    - Spell is cast during the late afternoon: +1
    - Spell is used defensively: +1
    - Spell is cast at daybreak: +2
    - The witch knows a spell (of any level) from the Space or Nature mysteries: +1
    - Spell is used against an innocent or for selfish reasons: -1
    - Spell is cast at dusk: -1
    - Spell is cast during the night: -3; autofail on Shimmering Smiles and Halt the Solar Chairot
    - Spell causes a Breaking Point (GMC rules) or a Morality drop (pre-GMC rules): -4

              The powers in Witch Finders are supposed to be a "quick and dirty" version of the powers found in Mage, as it was the Hunter book for the Mage gameline. The best way I can describe them as that they are like the spell lists found in D&D, in which they are tiered powers you can obtain at certain levels of power stat. They can be very easily turned into a Dread Power, and it's a book you can mine for ideas outside of using it for Mage, as some of the powers are also equivalent to those found in Second Sight.

              The final power I am debating on making a "once per story" type deal. Halt the Solar Chariot is one of those things that could trick a slumbering, elder vampire into getting roasted like a peanut. It's a brutal, highly sacrificial power that could come at the end of the story, when the heroes are in need of the sun itself to defeat an enemy plaguing them the entire story. It's one of those powers you worry might break the game if it can be used more than once; slowing down the movement of the sun kind of loses its "holy ****" factor if you do can it more than once.

              Also: a (very) stripped-down reference list for Gutter Magic, to give you the idea of what I'm working with: http://wodindex.wikispaces.com/Gutter+Magic
