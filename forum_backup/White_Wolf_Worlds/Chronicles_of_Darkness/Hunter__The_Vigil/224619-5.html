    I'd like to see Cells, Compacts, and Conspiracies retain their individual flavors, but not feel like you're being "nerfed" by wanting to play only in a specific Tier. I like the idea of the Ashwood Abbey, but the Endowments of Task Force: Valkyrie are just too cool for words. Now, my hypothetical Ashwood Abby Hunter shouldn't be walking around with a raygun, but something I as an Ashwood Abbey member have that wouldn't make me look at a TF:V member with unrequited jealousy. You know, like that commercial that airs all the time when you watch The Walking Dead On Demand: the grizzled old veteran talking about how he's survived the zombie apocalypse so long, and one of the folk he's talking to replying "And you have. . . that." And we see his awesome pimped-out zombie-killing ride. "Yeah, but you have that." "It's a stick." "But it's a really sharp stick."

    Some of the ideas behind the Compacts are WAY cooler than those behind the Conspiracies, but playing a member of a Compact as opposed to a Conspiracy kinda feels like bringing a sharp stick to a pimped-out zombie-killing ride fight. And if you're not even in a Compact? Damn, I wish I had a sharp stick!

    Not necessarily saying "Endowments for everybody!" because some of the Compact's great flavors come from the fact that they aren't these global organizations with the resources to create/obtain all this outlandish stuff. The Union is awesome precisely because they're just the average Joes doin' what they can with what they got. They can't match the biotech know-how of Cheiron Group. But should they have their own ways of doing things that get just as good results? Absolutely!

    For instance, The Union's whole thing is that they're blue-collar workmen who've stumbled across the supernatural and are keeping their friends and family's safe. So utilize that background, give them cool things they can do relating to that background. Sure, they can't build the kinds of stuff that comes out of TF:V or Cheiron's R&D departments, but if you put an electrical engineer, carpenter, and construction worker in the same room and tell them to come up with something to kill a vampire, they'll come up with something to kill that vampire DEAD. Similarly, Ashwood Abbey may not have access to artifacts or alchemical potions, but a bunch of bored socialites with nothing to do but think about their next big thrill are going to come up with some pretty intense ways as regards how to get it. I don't just mean Tactics. . . those are too integral to all Hunters, I feel, to use them as a "separate but equal" way of balancing things out.

                                    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                    As for Heroic Mortals, I think that rule belongs in Mirrors. Hunter is at it's core about ordinary mortals and what they can achieve united (and yes, supernatural powers is what ordinary mortals can achieve united). Heroic Mortals belong in mirrors, but giving explicit advice for the core Hunter orgs in Mirrors 2e is probably a good idea.
                                    IMHO, a large part of Hunter is Mirrors-style content: moreso than any other WoD gameline, Hunter isn’t based on a single, coherent metaphysics (in-game) or a single playstyle (on a meta level). In fact, the three tiers were originally conceived of as a vehicle for incorporating three very different takes on how to play hunters into one game. As I see it, it’s better to make the support for different playstyles explicit.

                                    How does this relate to Heroic Mortals*? Simple: there are a lot of folks who approach Hunter with the idea that the protagonists are a cut above your average mortal. Risking Willpower and Practical Experience are game systems that already exist in Hunter that are geared toward such “exceptional mortal” play. Conversely, there are those who dislike even those rules being available because their approach to hunters is to treat them as much like ordinary people as can be managed given the assumption that they’re willing to do something about the monsters in the shadows. Including a 2e version of heroic mortals in the game is a way of catering to the “I want hunters to be a cut above” crowd; tying them to a particular style of play so that they’re only available if the Storyteller opts in to including them is a way of catering to the “I want hunters to be just like everyone else” crowd.

                                    And Supernatural Merits (including, but not limited to, Endowments) are for those who want to blur the line between hunter and prey. That, too, should be presented as an option that the Storyteller shouldn’t feel compelled to allow.

                                    You’ve laid out a compelling vision for how Hunter might be played; and far be it from me to tell you not to play the game that way. But by the same token, I wouldn’t want the second edition to cater exclusively to just that one style of play. As I said at the start of this section, Hunter is the most “toolboxy” (to coin a word) of the WoD gamelines; I’d like to see 2e become even moreso.

                                    * Who were introduced in Strange, Dead Love, BTW; not Mirrors.

                                                            Some thoughts.

                                                            RE: Endowment Availability

                                                            What may be a good option is to create a merit that allows a Hunter to gain an endowment from an organization they aren't officially part of (similar to some of the merits being proposed for VtR's Secrets of the Covenants open development). This way, it enables concepts that include some cross-pollination. (such as a Hunter that got abducted by parties unknown and released with few memories and some..thing..inside their body).

                                                            RE: Cells, Compacts, And Conspiracies

                                                            What I would like to see is the clarification between the in-game scope of the organization and the scope of the playstyle. Like other here, I would like to see a bit more flexibility in how an particular organization is presented, so it becomes possible to have Cell-Level play with Conspiracy level Organizations.

                                                            RE: Organization Flavor and Background.

                                                            So far in second editions, most of the main splats have had their flavor tweaked and revised. I would expect the Hunter Orgs to get a similar treatment.
