    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    Psychological consequences are great, but in the real world soldiers don't have a direct correlation between psychological consequences and number of foreign soldiers they kill.
    That's one of the limitations of an abstract rules system. It will never model reality perfectly. In real life, there is a correlation between killing people and having psychological issues. Probably not causation, but definitely correlation. RPG rules are not great at modeling correlation without causation. But they do their best - after all, not every Breaking Point leads to Integrity loss. In fact, not every Breaking Point even leads to a Condition.

    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    Just like Buffy in fact. She did have plenty of psycholgoical consiqunces from her Vigil, she just didn't have a Breaking Point for every last vampire. That is what I would expect a Hunter with a Code to look like, casual about killing monsters. Seriously torn up if a friend gets hurt.
    Alternatively, you could read her as having a tremendous success rate on all those Breaking Point rolls she made. Likely due to all of those Touchstones she had. And when her Touchstones got hurt or killed, then she failed rolls.

    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    Those sorts of situations are precisely the reason I like the idea of Hunters saying &quot;monster's don't count&quot;.
    I agree entirely. They're also the reason I like the idea that no matter how much Hunters tell themselves "monsters don't count", monsters totally still count.

    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    Besides, vampires, werewolves, etc don't have clear definitions either. Is a Wolf-Blooded with a Tell that lets them Shapeshift into a wolf a Werwolf? What about a Mage who frequently uses Life to turn into a Wolf and uses the moon as a Yantra?
    More reasons I prefer specific types of creatures as exemptions from the Code, rather than monsters in general. The moral ambiguity is what makes it interesting. Who is it or isn't it ok to kill, and what are the moral implications of the lines a character chooses to draw?

    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    Fortunately I think it's not only possible but easy to have both

    1) Personal horror
    2) Not mechanically impossible to play Buffy

    At the same time.

    After all, 1E did it just fine.
    1e was fine. I would like 2e to be great. If the writers and developers ever have to make a choice between making the personal horror more compelling, or making other play styles easier to accomodate, I dearly hope they chose the former. This is one case where I think the core themes of the game would be weakened by choosing the latter.

    Going by Willow now, or Wil for short. She/Her/Hers.

        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
        1e was fine. I would like 2e to be great. If the writers and developers ever have to make a choice between making the personal horror more compelling, or making other play styles easier to accomodate, I dearly hope they chose the former. This is one case where I think the core themes of the game would be weakened by choosing the latter.
        I actually agree with the principal. However I don't think it would weaken the focus on horror to allow Codes which permit all monsters. In fact, I think it would strengthen the personal horror. Hear me out on this one.

        I think that in most games of hunter (not Network Zero, but definately VALKYRIE) you're supposed to kill monsters regularly. I think that in practice a Breaking Point every time you kill a monster will numb the players and the plot to them. If you drop from Integrity 7 to 3 in the first two sessions; then there's no time to roleplay your decline.

        More importantly. Hunter has multiple sources of psychological horror: There's the isolation from friends and family. There's the horror of choosing between protecting your kids from monsters or putting food on their plate. There's the horror of the unknown. And of course, there's the horror of being a killer.

        As a toolbox game, it should provide tools to let me create a story, or even a charachter, that focuses on one particular source of horror above the others. If I want to create a charachter / story that draws psychological horror from the growing rifts between my charachter and her family I should be able to downplay the psychological importance of killing monsters and play up the importance of friends and family.

        Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
        More reasons I prefer specific types of creatures as exemptions from the Code, rather than monsters in general. The moral ambiguity is what makes it interesting. Who is it or isn't it ok to kill, and what are the moral implications of the lines a character chooses to draw?
        Sure. I think that there are better ways than Breaking Points to show this though. It's the arguments with other charachters and

        Ooooh! I just had an idea! I think you're going to love this.

        What if The Code can't say "X isn't a breaking point". It can only invert things so that "Instead of X, not X is a breaking point".

        So for example. You can't have a Code that says "killing a vampire is ok" but you can have a Code that says "killing a vampire is ok; sparing a vampire's life is a Breaking Point".

        This has three advantages.

        1) It has an automatic balance. A code "Suffer not a witch to live" has both advantages and disadvantages over "suffer not a supernatual to live"
        2) It has a very very natural path to being a Slasher. The moment your code says you must kill regular mortals (no matter how rare the circumstances) you're in the danger zone.
        3) Possibly the biggest one: You automatically create mechanical space for drama centred around that kind of ambiguity. The MM hunter with "Suffer not a witch to live" has a breaking point when he wants to show mercy to a witch. That's probably the most interesting and dramatic time for one that's possible.


        What do you think?
