                                            I had a complex house rule about the Sleeping Curse/Lunacy etc that attempted to model the "deeply held taboo" idea in horror fiction, that talking about the supernatural is somehow embarrassing or shameful and people are highly reluctant to do it. My model was the novel "Declare" by Tim Powers, where people would talk themselves out of believing what they'd seen and wouldn't want to talk about it, but wouldn't necessarily just get auto-amnesia'd a la the existing rules for the Sleeping Curse (which close off too many story hooks for my taste).

                                            Then I just threw up my hands making a complicated rule to handle non-player characters, and just merged "Sleepwalker", "Fitful Sleeper", and the 1e merit "A Little Knowledge" into a one-dot version of Unseen Sense and gave it to all hunters/slashers for free while they had taken up the Vigil or an Undertaking.

                                            Unseen Sense 1 offers a +2 to parsing out supernatural involvement from forensic evidence, newspapers, local gossip, and other forms of indirect evidence. It signified freedom from the invisible shadows of the mind that prevented most regular people from remembering what they've seen, or repressing/denying supernatural encounters. It's the "Sam Winchester uses Google to find this week's episode" merit.

                                                            Originally posted by Professor Phobos View Post

                                                            It just feels grossly overpriced to me. It's a full one dot for...permission to participate in the game?
                                                            Different definitions of Participation.

                                                            Without adjusting anything, Hunters interested in the Awakened are kind of fucked with a Catch-22. They can be a)Sleepers, and therefore fucked over by the Sleeping Curse in terms of Integrity damage and also non-meaningful interaction with their prey in all but the most narrow of circumstances, but also benefit from Quiescience and the ability for spells to eventually slough off, or b) Sleepwalkers, which means they can TRY and interact meaningfully, but any and everything that an Awakened can do to them sticks. Both options suck ass and really don't empower Hunter.

                                                            But that's kind of the point of Awakening.

                                                            Mages are monsters, and one of those ways is as acting as the metaphysical 1%. Hunters, by contrast, aren't anywhere near their game, and at some point the sheer world reinforcing that divide helps with the metaphors at work for Mage, that upper-class elitism creating an untouchable chasm that divides mages from their former humanity, and keeps the "lower classes" in despair of affecting them, to the point their only real hope affecting change on that level is getting advocates on the other side.

                                                            BUT BUT BUT

                                                            THE BIG THING MISSED OFTEN IN THIS CONVERSATION

                                                            is that it's kind of the point of Vigil too.

                                                            I have argued before that part of the reason Masquerades of various sorts are in place is of course the Vigil-but a really big part of the themes of Vigil is the despair over where humanity just can't do anything. The ignorance, the capacity to grasp the world as it is without the neccesary tools, the impossibility of wrangling nature, human or otherwise-all of that's there. Vigil never promises that Humanity Wins, only that it tries, and often ineffectually, because human fallibility is as important, if not more so, as human empowerment to Hunter the Vigil. Vigil, as SEIZE THE NIGHT as it is, is actually really bleak and pessimistic. BEAST is more optimistic about humanity, on the whole.

                                                            And in this way, Hunters being basically powerless to their most human and most personally inhuman opponents is powerful commentary...that admittedly sucks if you want to play the Knights of Saint Adrian punching out a Seer of the Throne. But it plays perfectly into the themes of both games for that divide between hunter and mages to not only be present, but honestly kind of impassable. And honestly?

                                                            I mean, this is me, but I'll take that kind of thematic honesty over "And then I punch the Praetorian though his Force Field" any day of the week.

                                                            I have no commentary on Lunacy, I feel like everything Hunter fans could want came in with the changes to Lunacy in Second Editon Forsaken. But Awakening versus Vigil? Kids, you don't like it, but it's perfect the way it is.
