                        Thank-you, you just articulated what I was having trouble placing my finger on. One thing that I love about the CofD's post-human splats (IE. Vampire, Mage, Werewolf, etc.) is how well they emphasize this slow drifting away from humanity, where you can look back on a character's story arc and go "how the hell did we get here?"

                        Integrity, for me as a player and a Storyteller, fulfills a similar but different narrative role where it best represents the character's drive and connection to others. If you fight to maintain Integrity, the character can push on, they can endure, but the more Integrity fades away, the more dangerous subsequent encounters with the supernatural become, until it just becomes prudent (and safer) for the character to retire... unless they are willing to risk losing themselves. Obviously, this doesn't work for Hunters, so the Code needs to give them more "staying power", it needs to push them to react with violence instead of poking their noses into dark corners or keeping their heads down.

                        Something I was thinking about last night was, potentially, developing a Code by replacing Integrity Breaking Points with your own Code, lines you won't cross, at the cost of limiting your maximum Integrity. So a Hunter could desensitize themselves to their lifestyle, developing their own lines in the sand, but at the cost of distancing themselves permanently from making a full recovery. Or maybe it would be like a Vampire's Banes instead, penalizing all subsequent Breaking Point rolls.

                                I just want to note that I do like the concept of Cells/Compacts/Conspiracies having each their own Code, which may contrast with the hunter's- that the exact interpretation of the Code and how it differs from hunter to hunter is something interesting to explore. But it also needs to be something which adapts and changes with time- not only the interpretation, but the spirit of the Code itself. Something like that when you become a part of a Cell, you and your cell-mates make together a list which represent you own "Code", and each Compact/Conspiracy has a Code of its own. Each hunter needs to take a certain amount of Breaking Points from their Cell's list, and a certain number from their Organization's list (if they have one). As their Integrity is exposed to Breaking Points, they may replace existing/add new Breaking Points to mend their psych and allow them to withhold similar trauma in the future- in exchange for becoming more obsessive with the Vigil. Something like that.

                                Now, this is only a preview, and we still don't fully know what exactly Monica has in mind- perhaps it is more or less what I want, perhaps it doesn't. We'll have to wait and see. But I do like how falling in Integrity makes the hunter more obsessive and dedicated to the Vigil, and how the Breaking Points mechanics are handled (with the different Conditions which accompany the role). I also like how recovering Integrity is handled, as it totally gives a reason for a hunter to look in the eye of a monster before it shoots it between the eyes and say "before I kill, you, I want you to know why". I just want it to feel like a personal vow signed with your organization, friends and yourself instead of an objective truth which tells you you are hunting the "right way".

                                            This is how I'm thinking I would house-rule The Code if it was published as we see it on this draft.

                                            The standard Breaking Points for mortals are all "Innate." Then there could be a few universal Code Breaking Point examples, a few that are particular to certain Compacts and Conspiracies, and players can develop personal Code Breaking Points at ST discretion. The bonuses, penalties, and roll results listed in the preview apply to Code Breaking Points, while Innate Breaking Points follow the normal Integrity rules.

                                            When you fail an Innate Breaking Point, you can choose to take a Code Breaking Point, linked to your highest available dot of Integrity. If you do so, the failed Innate Breaking Point no longer applies to you. You only roll for Code Breaking Points at or below your current Integrity, but if you have four or more Code Breaking Points associated with dots above your current Integrity you take a -1 penalty to Code Breaking Point rolls and gain the Persistent Vigilant Condition. If you have 7 or more Code Breaking Points associated with dots above your current Integrity, the penalty increases to -2 and you gain the Persistent Merciless Condition. If you have 10 Code Breaking Points above your current Integrity, you become a Slasher.

                                            I think that's the best way to keep the mechanics as true to the preview as possible while making it a modification to standard Integrity instead of a replacement for it.

                                                    One reason to keep the name would be because hunters are just regular people who've taken up an extraordinary task. The rules have changed to reflect how the vigil changes the hunter, but the intent and usage hasn't. In fact, with this precedent it's quite possible that other Breaking Point inducing occupations such as deployed military, police officers in certain countries, gang members, and cults that have successfully made supernatural contact. The only reason we don't have specific Integrity rules for them would be because we don't have gamelines about them (although I'd really like to see a 2E blue book about cultists that does explore how their Integrity is changed by their specific experiences).

                                                    Another reason to change the rules would be to give hunters more of an identity separate from regular people. Especially since there's a myriad of Supernatural Merits available for regular people in 2E that balances the Endowments available to some hunters. Having an updated Integrity system helps justify hunters having a gameline rather than being a collection of themed blue books.
