    Thoughtbash: horror movie Robots as a monster splat for hunters to fight

    02-08-2016, 11:39 PM
    So, this idea popped into my head this morning, and before I could stop myself the idea started fleshing itself out when I should have been listening to my biochem professor, but whatever.
    Anyway, you know how slashers are based on a horror movie trope? Hell, most if not all Chronicles of Darkness venues draw inspiration from horror movies. Well it occurred to me that there is another common monster splat in horror movies that isn't represented in the CoD yet: Robots! Think about it as a Hunter-only kind of monster for now rather than it's own venue...like slashers.

    What distinguishes robots?
    Robots, unlike other splats don't occur naturally or even supernaturally. They have to be built and programmed...mostly by humans. They don't reproduce. They could look humanoid, or have a completely non-human mechanical body, or be embedded in a facility as a dominant AI. They are built for specific purposes, and they will fulfill those purposes, obtuse to what the fallout might be. They mayturn on their masters, turn against humanity, or possibly become more than their creators could have hoped for.

    Unlike other splats they don't have Virtues and Vices, Morality, or Willpower.

    They have programmed Directives rather than virtues and vices and they will follow these directives compulsively (unless they become self-aware and fight their programming, which I'll cover later). Directives can be anything the programmer designates and can be broad or as narrow as the programmer wishes. Examples include "protect this facility" or "kill monsters" or "hide evidence". A robot can have as many directives as their programmer gives them, but the more directives they have the more likely those directives are to contradict each other, making them more vulnerable to a Logical Paradox tactic (to be discussed later).

    Instead of Willpower they have Program, which determines the strength of their directives and is the main dice pool used to resist hacking attempts. Unlike other splats they don't need to roll to resist mind-altering powers that aren't directly related to computer hacking. A vampire's dread gaze will automatically fail, mages that attempt to use mind or spirit magic on a robot will find that it has no effect, and Lunacy does not occur in robots. With few exceptions they have no soul and no will of their own: they are slaves to their programming. Only by overcoming the robustness of their Program and altering their Directives can their minds be directly manipulated. The down side to this is that dots of temporary Program can't be spent to bolster a robot's actions the way that Mortals and Hunters can spend temporary Willpower. Putting extra effort into their actions isn't something that robots can comprehend as they almost always put all of their effort into their Directives.

    Instead of Morality they have an Artificial Intelligence score. This is NOT a measure of morality though it can be used to gain a sense of morality by overcoming their own Program to achieve self-awareness. More on that later. Needless to say a robot cannot suffer from derangements and does not suffer degeneration for immoral acts as they only care about fulfilling their Directives. AI is, however, the main stat in determining whether or not they can resist their Program and act in a way that is counter to their Directives. Any time a robot decides to defy a Directive they must roll their AI score vs their Program score. If the AI roll has more successes than the Program roll they can defy their Directive on one particular action. Each time they successfully do this there is a random chance die roll to see if they become self-aware. If the Program roll has more successes than the AI roll, then the robot cannot disobey its Directive and acts accordingly. Other than being the score to resist one's own Directives, Artificial Intelligence serves as a limiting cap for Mental and Social Attributes. The higher a robot's AI the more dots it can have in Mental attributes. For example, one dot in AI allows a robot to have a maximum of one dot in any given Mental or one dot in all Mentals. A robot can absolutely have Mentals that go up to ten dots. A robot with superhuman intelligence can be a frightening thing. Socials are far more limited though as for the most part robots have no emotions and are more logical than an ordinary human, sometimes to a fault. Without empathy their ability to socialize is very limited. This is reflected by the fact that a robot's AI divided by two rounded down determines how many total dots a robot can have in Social attributes. For example two or three dots in AI would allow a robot to put one dot in either Presence, Manipulation, or Composure but not all three. Robots do not start out with one dot in all socials and a robot can easily have zero dots in socials and not be able to socially interact with humans or similar creatures in a significant capacity.

    Becoming self-aware.
    Let's say a robot becomes self aware. Their AI is able to overcome their Program. The robot gets struck by lightning and their builders suddenly notice that the robot has been acting "off". A hacker hacks into the robot and triggers something he didn't expect. A ghost or spirit tries to possess the machine and accidentally merges with the robot's software somehow. Regardless of the cause it happens every so often that a robot becomes self-aware. In these instances something bizarre happens. The robot gains a soul. That soul can be seen by those who have the ability. They are not necessarily possessed by a spirit or something similar, though they can be. Their soul could be entirely new, and like a new child it could be exploring its world with an infant's understanding. Or it might carry in it's mind a distressed and angry disposition to the world around it. This carries with it some changes.

    A robot that becomes self aware is now capable of emotions, empathy, morality....things they were never supposed to have. Their socials are no longer limited by their AI (though they still have an AI score and it does still act as a cap for their Mentals). They are able to be influenced by things like Mind, Death, and Spirit magic now, though Lunacy still doesn't affect them since they don't carry the ancestral fear of Werewolves and Therianthropes that humans possess. They can feel fear for their own safety now, though they don't necessarily. Some remain logical to a fault, others become more human-like and charismatic. Some try to be benevolent in spite of the wishes of their masters and end up hunted by their creators. Others turn against their creators to lethal effect. Still others may try to obey the commands of their creator out of love...even if they take it to a logical extreme. Some, in the case of possession, would take on aspects of both the possessing ghost/spirit and the robot as it was programmed.

    They still retain their Directives and Program rather than obtaining a Vice, Virtue, and Willpower though. That never really went away. It can be a source of tension and internal conflict in a free robot as sometimes they find themselves slipping into old patterns. Though they can defy their Directives more freely now, represented by each AI success vs. Program counting as two successes instead of one, they can still be compelled to follow their Directives, like falling back on old habits or using a set of skills that are deeply ingrained in you.

    Damage.
    Robots don't heal. They have to be repaired. It is something that people take for granted, our ability to recover from wounds on our own. Cuts form scabs. Bones mend over time. For us it seems so slow and cumbersome, especially in the face of supernatural creatures that can heal from wounds in an instant. But for robots, it is a luxury. Unless they specifically have a self repair system they must manually be repaired, and in both cases physical materials are usually needed for repairs. Otherwise whatever damage they take stays, even if it is just Bashing (if it gets past their armor rating). For hunters going up against robots this is a blessing. One of the few advantages humans have over the machines is their general lack of a latent ability to heal. Bashing damage wouldn't need spare parts to repair; bashing is usually represented by loose wires that can be plugged back in or bent metal pieces that can be bent back into place. Lethal is generally a physically broken part that must be replaced or patched, like a sheared-off piece of hull or a broken circuit board. Aggravated represents some form of catastrophic damage that demands the removal and replacement of damaged parts, like strong HCl acid poured onto the bot, or fire damage to the CPU. Repairs are usually slow and always an extended roll. Each roll could represent anywhere between 5 minutes to an hour depending on the type of repair method used and the damage done.

    Energy.
    All robots obviously use electricity, and all of them will have a power source of some kind. Whether that power source is an internal battery, a mini reactor, or the city power grid itself. Finding a robot's power source and shutting it down may be the only saving grace some hunters have.

    Types of Robots:
    I think there should be 3 basic types of robots:
    Androids: These robots look human or at least humanoid. Of all the robots they tend to have the greatest amount of Socials, though that isn't saying much. This is represented by the fact that Androids have a dot cap instead of a dot limit on Socials. Example: an Android with 3 dots in AI can have one dot each in Presence, Manipulation, and Composure rather than having only one dot to distribute among either Presence, Manipulation, or Composure. Androids might pass as human to the casual observer or in a big city where people don't pay too much attention to each other, but when people try to actually interact with an android something seems...off. They might not immediately know that it's a robot depending on how well they are made, but the lack of empathy and overly logical nature come through almost every time.

    Examples of Androids:
    -An eccentric inventor and CEO of a company decides to build a sex bot in his remote home in the mountains. At first she was everything he could have wanted. The android, however, has been acting strangely lately, acting out against her "master" and questioning his sexual requests. She has become self-aware and is no longer satisfied with her place in life. Something is going to change, and her "master" may have to go....
    -Task Force Valkyrie made an experimental prototype covert combat android to be handled and test run by a field team. This model was designed to blend into crowds while at the same time being able to take point in high risk missions against monsters and to handle situations that ordinary agents would find traumatizing. At first it's performance was exemplary, but after observing its team mates deteriorating psychological statuses it has decided to start handling more and more missions alone, even going so far as to go on missions without consulting its team. It's definition of "monster" also seems to have expanded from the inhuman to include ordinary humans it designates "monstrous".

    Killbots: Killbots don't necessarily look humanoid. In fact often they don't. Even if they do have two arms, two legs, a head, and a torso, their appearances are obvious and machine like. Not an inch of them is even attempted to be disguised as humans. They might have a torso with claw like arms, six legs, and shoulder-mounted missile launchers. They might be a military air drone with AI instead of a remote controlled drone. It could be a tank-like droid with a heavy artillery cannon. Whatever form they have one thing is constant, Killbots are designed for heavy combat. Even a werewolf would be hard pressed to go toe-to-toe with a literal tank that can adapt to her every move. More than being smart Killbots are designed to dish out damage in an extreme way. They will almost always have the biggest guns, the most potent explosives, the heaviest artillery. These things were designed for warfare and when they go rogue, whether they become self-aware or because their Directives were poorly written, there is always a high body count.

    Examples of Killbots:
    -The U.S. Army decides to test out a new AI for an autonomous aerial strike drone, but when it is struck by lightning mid-flight it starts acting odd and disobeying protocol. Suddenly, it has decided to turn its payload on civilians. Now it is coming back to base, and no one can predict its next move.
    -A corporation that contracts out weapons to security firms and militaries around the world has made a few experimental killbots to test out the design. One of them starts to question its Directives, asking why it should kill. Even after a reprogramming it seems reluctant to follow orders in a simulation. One night the rogue bot broke out of containment, and it is now on the run. Unfortunately for it, it is alone in the world...until it found a small boy in a near by town.

    Facility Bot: A Facility Bot isn't just the AI inside the building, it IS the building. Or compound. Or space station. At some high-end facilities someone thought that putting an advanced AI in charge of the whole building would be a good idea. The facility bot IS the doors, windows, elevators, security systems, air conditioning, AND the computer that controls it all. They typically have the highest AI and are the most likely to become self-aware. When you are in a Facility Bot you are there at its whim. If it doesn't like you or isn't amused by you it can turn the environment you are in against you. Trap you in a room to starve to death, flood the halls with poisonous gas, or even turn the auto-turrets on you. Going up against a facility bot isn't the same as other enemies. Like an elder demon that has possessed a neighborhood, you are going up against the environment itself. If one of these Facility Bots were to make it online, they could even wreak havoc across the world. Remember at the turn of the millennium people said the Y2K bug would cause nukes to launch themselves? That's the kind of thing a facility bot might try.

    Examples of Facility Bots:
    -A nuclear power plant decided to turn safety, security, and contamination monitoring over to an advanced AI. At first things went smoothly. Then there were the glitches. Doors weren't opening when they were supposed to. Autoguns started trailing people in an unnerving way. One time a meltdown was narrowly averted because the system wasn't responding to a cascade event and a manual override had to be triggered. However, things really went to hell when the computer tech tried to open up the main memory bank for some maintenance.
    -An abandoned warehouse. Isn't even old. Not five years ago it was a bustling robotics corporation that offered to mass produce some promising tech for the DOD. Then the accident happened. An explosion on the site. No one in town knew what it was, but the facility went on lockdown that day. No one in or out. Screams were heard on the inside of the building. The government sent some team inside, but they never came back out. Families in the town were torn apart. Lives lost. Now the place is sealed off. Lately though strange mechanical noises are heard from inside the compound as though the assembly line machinery is starting back up.

    Robot Hunters:
    Compacts
    Children of Ludd
    An organization of Luddites who have always hated and distrusted technology. Especially computers. Many are wilderness survivors, but all tech principles of low-tech survival and self-reliance. They are always protesting against new developments in AI, afraid of what could go wrong. A militant faction of this group monitors government and corporate entities that delve into this forbidden territory and wait for something to go wrong so they can swoop in and bust up the machines. Another faction monitors such events so they can publicize them and use them as cautionary tales to spread Luddite propaganda. Yet another faction wants to be more proactive and bust up research facilities before they have a chance to make these robots.

    0101
    A group of hardcore hackers who have gotten wind of various robots going rogue, and monitor the net for advanced AI. They specialize in hacking into an AI's program and messing with their directives. One faction looks to put out fires and stop robots as they become a problem. Another faction polices the next for AI and try to fight them on the digital battlefield. Yet another faction is more mischief-oriented and they actually try to make robots go awry just to screw with governments and corporations.

    Conspiracies
    Robots are fairly new, and there are few of them, especially when compared to other threats out there. Because of this it wouldn't make sense to have a robot-specific or robot-only conspiracy. In fact, I'd go as far as to say that with few exceptions most of the current conspiracies wouldn't have anti-robot Endowments yet. TFV would have an EMP gun, EMP grenades, and even an EMP bomb to disrupt robot's electrical systems, and the Cherion Group might have an acid glad augmentation that lets them spray concentrated acid on a robot. However, I wouldn't expect that there would be a Benediction or a Castigation, for example, that would be designed to use against robots, just because of how old those Conspiracies and their Endowments are vs. how new robots are.

    Tactics
    Logical Paradox
    Robots can be logical to a fault. If a hunter can interact with a robot socially she can get them to work on a logical paradox and it might take up their processing power, causing them to temporarily pause or shut down trying to compute something that does not compute. The more people giving the robot logical paradoxes at once, the more likely it is to pause. Of course, it may also just ignore the paradox in favor of fulfilling one of its Directives. If a hunter can find a contradiction between a robot's Directives and point that out, it would be far more compelling to the robot to solve the discrepancy between its directives as opposed to a generic paradox like "Everything I say is a lie".

    Hacking the Machine
    I had some hacking tactics in mind, but they escape me at the moment. Sorry.

    Well, what do yall think?

                                      Interesting idea. I think the OP was thinking more about the first Terminator when he mentioned that, where it literally is a soulless killing machine only interested in accomplishing it's mission, rather than the second, which delved into questions of what measure is a nonhuman. Or something like the I, Robot move, and its Zeroth Law Rebellion.

                                      For that matter, Isaac Asimov would be a great place to start. The whole point of the Three Laws of Robotics was to prevent the sort of "killer robot" stories that were prevalent in (mostly bad) science fiction around the time Asimov started writing his robot stories. And even there, a lot of his stories directly involving robots revolved around how the three laws work, but not always in the ways the designers intended. One story in the I, Robot anthology involved a robot on Venus, who was running around in circles around a pond of some necessary material or other. Took the two robot specialists only a few paragraphs to figure out that, because no special emphasis had been placed on the order to "get this stuff from this pond" and the stuff in the pond was mildly hazardous to the robot, it was going around in circles at the exact point at which the "I must obey the order to get the stuff" and the "the stuff will harm me and I must protect my own existence" potentials equaled out. And then there was the one where the administrative robot founded its own religion. . . good times.

                                      Still, forgetting to include the "A robot must not harm a human, or through inaction, allow a human to come to harm" Directive can lead to all kinds of hilarity ensuing. Even slightly modifying it to "a robot may not kill a human, or through inaction allow a human to be killed" can create all kinds of interesting scenarios. . . robots can harm humans all they want, so long as they don't actually kill them (Terminator 2 is a great example of this, though shooting someone in the leg can be plenty fatal if you hit the femoral artery.) And if a robot's Directives can only be accomplished by harming humans. . .

                                      The thing about computers (which robots are) which prevents true artificial intelligence with our current programming technology is that computers are absolutely literal. Again, I have to point o Asimov and I, Robot. The final story in the anthology involves a lost robot. A (military, I think) installation had ordered a special batch of robots with the second part of the First Law removed. The couldn't harm humans, but could, through inaction, allow humans to come to harm. They did this because the work at this facility involved the staff being regularly exposed to low, non-harmful doses of radiation. The radiation was easily manageable by the humans with appropriate precautions, but fried the brains of the robots almost instantly. The robots knew the radiation was harmful to humans, and were continually rushing in to "save" them, and being destroyed in the process. Then one technician absentmindedly ordered one of these robots to "get lost." When it was finally found, it almost tried to kill the person who found it, because obeying its order to "get lost" was almost more important than the tattered remnants of its First Law. The point being, if a programmer of such robots as this thread is about isn't extremely thorough in its wording of the robot's Directives, there are all kinds of loopholes the robot can abuse, without even realizing that it's abusing loopholes. It's just doing what it was programmed to do, it's not its fault its programmer programmed it insufficiently. Can result in things like surgical robots with zero understanding of empathy or concepts like pain and suffering, interested only in keeping their patients alive at all costs.

                                      Then, of course, there's wackiness that can happen when robots are deliberately programmed to do terrible things to human beings, such as combat robots designed to kill their enemy with maximum efficiency.
