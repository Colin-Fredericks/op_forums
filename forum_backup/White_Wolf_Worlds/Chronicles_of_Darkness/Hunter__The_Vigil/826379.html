    How Do You Use Hunters When You're Playing The Other Games?

    02-08-2016, 07:32 PM
    How pervasive is the fear of the sheep becoming wolves? What methods do they use to hunt you down? How do you fight back? How stable are the occasional alliances?

    I, for one, feel like a game with supernatural PCs and not a single confrontation with a Hunter is very strange. If they never strike or cause any trouble, why all the skulking and sneaking around?

      I find a lot of supernatural games I hear about from others tend to have a lot of focus on those supernaturals. If you aren't going out of your way to do things in public and attract attention, and the worst acts are between fellow monsters, a hunter's suspicion is not likely to be raised.

      In my own games, generally humanity is pretty much like it is in real life. Not everyone is a hero waiting for the monster to appear, and when the monster appears they won't act like they did in all their fantasies and will probably excuse themselves over it later. Better to be safe than risk the horrors of that other world. But some people do end up becoming hunters and they tend to be either horribly misguided or focused. Even at that point it's rare to find one who's actually good at it.

      To be completely fair to the players of other game lines, the hunters usually have to be at least a little bad at it. It's not exactly fair if halfway through your first session you're told that your character was staked and beheaded by a vampire hunter they never knew existed and never had a chance to know.

      So whether they're misguided or focused, they tend to be sloppy or flamboyant. Both cases offer a chance for the players to notice and react. Either the hunters bumble through or they announce themselves broadly, enough to put the PCs on guard. Occasionally I've tied hunters into the pasts of characters, allowing them a reprieve from their depredations based on 'old times,' but one that shouldn't be tested.

      In our last game the players encountered a hunter in his lair, not knowing he was a hunter, and while he was unprepared still managed to drive them off with his martial skill. The whole thing was hilarious, and made the characters very angry, because he was arrogant and boastful about it as he was pummeling them and they didn't manage to lay a finger on him before running away.

          Humorously, I feel the opposite: if you're in a supernatural game and you have a Cell on your trail, you've been careless and you screwed up. And even then, realistically you would have to mess up a lot: every incident doesn't call down a Hunter cell.

          As an ST, Hunters are occasionally a sort of speed bump if they do come up, but mostly are just a "hey you guys have messed up"...but usually that's the role of other supernaturals. Vampires police Vampires, Werewolves content with spirits, other werewolves, and any sort of spirit wizard, so on and so forth.


          And, as always, the sad truth is that if a group of supernatural characters can't deal with hunters, they assuredly can't deal with their actual antagonists. There's a reason that the Hunter bokks gives you weakened versions of the other splats to fight.

                  Hunters make great deniable assets.

                  I don't think the average supernatural society has much to fear from the average human (save for maybe Prometheans), if only because mortals know so little about them. That changes when you have the local cell/compact/conspiracy receive some interesting intelligence form a shadowy patron that just so happens to know what weapons work best on werewolves, or where the local vampires sleep, or where the mummy has his artifacts stashed. When the cold war between factions goes hot, most if the action is done by mortal pawns who have no fucking clue what they're mixed up in; damn near every line has some sort of faction representing leadership of a Cult.

                  Vampires rat each other out to hunters often, allowing one to damage their enemy without the loss of face associated with a more explicit attack; like we see in Berlin, hunters can even become a bona fide part of the Kindred establishment. Werewolf packs can easily get into what look for all the world like mundane gang turf wars. The Guardians, Ladder, and Seers all have tons of dedicated mortals in their pockets, and every Mastigos out there has the ability to implant a Manchruian Candidate assassination mission into anyone on the street. Fights between Arisen can span continents and centuries; I'm sure portions of the Crusades and Inquisition had Iremite hands moving the pieces.

                  Of course, the fun in all this is when things spiral out of control. Having your own private hit squad is great right up until they start picking their own targets and your child or packmate or mentor winds up on the wrong side of a suicide bombing. I'm sure more than a few Seers have been caught in Division Six's sights, just as surely as VALKYRIE has iced a few wrong vampires or Arisen cults have self-destructed (like the Faithful of Shulpae may have).

                  Remi. she/her. game designer.

                          I like to run it one of two ways. One way has a group of hunters as antagonists in a story line, or side antagonists and a sub-plot. Maybe there is a plot-driven reason to have a group of Hunters in the game. It shouldn't feel forced though, it should flow with the plot.

                          The other way I like to run it is to have the "dice of doom". This is for when a character screws up and does something that can't not be noticed, public denial of the supernatural be damned. I roll a d3 to determine the tier of hunters coming after him, a d10 to determine the number of hunters on his tail, and in the case of tiers 2 and 3 I roll a d10 to determine the group coming after him, with each number corresponding to a different Compact or Conspiracy depending on the tier. That's more forced and artificial, but it's also reminiscent of the "random encounter" dice of certain other table top games I could mention...

                                  I've used Hunters twice now. The first was in Vampire, the hunters took out some of the locals and the Sheriff gave the players the task of doing clean up. It gave them the chance to face humans who actually knew how to fight back, not the cake walk they expected.

                                                  Oh, now that's is a can of worms I can answer!

                                                  Read "How to Hide From the Internet" by Mike Bazzell and "How to be Invisible, 3rd edition" By J. J. Luna. They go into detail about how to be anonymous in the modern age. People give out alarming amounts of personal data without realizing it or even thinking twice about it. Bruce Schiener goes into detail in "Data and Goliath." That's hefty reading list to be sure, but if you're interested in this topic, it's a place to start.

                                                  Any group that relies on information for survival, be it for legitimate purposes like a newspaper or for less than legit purposes like organized crime or a community of monsters, (who operate almost exactly like organized crime) becomes very, very good at having feelers out: sympathetic ears in the local police department, contacts in relevant industries, established sources, etc.

                                                  For supernatural groups, the ability to recruit sources and gather information is magnified a hundred fold. Vampire have networks of ghouls: ultra loyal agents kept in line with blood addiction, some of which have been in place for centuries, to supplement Auspex. Werewolves have a terrifying array of senses, spirits to watch their back and vast support networks. Demons can give a person whatever they want in exchange for information or obedience.

                                                  Now, imagine you've just confronted the supernatural for the first time, what's the first thing you do? Buy a gun? Start researching the occult? Do your players know how to do these things without tipping off the authorities or using the internet?

                                                  As for what kind of rolls players need to avoid detection, I recommend a narrative-based approach to drive home that they are no longer safe in their ignorance, and need to be careful every single day.

                                                  Make your players buy merits like Anonymity and Safe Place, and emphasize that these represent major life-style changes to avoid detection: cash purchases at the grocery store, online purchases are made using temporary credit cards under an assumed name, houses and cars are owned through a New Mexico holding corporation (seriously), internet use requires sandboxing, a VPN, and Tor. As for rolls, I recommend Wits + Investigation+Contacts to get advance warning of someone looking for them, and Manipulation + Subterfuge to control the information they give. Have them make resource spends to get the necessary computer equipment and invest XP in specialties like "cover online tracks."

                                                  Hiding from the opposition shouldn't be a roll or fail thing, it should be an atmosphere thing: this is what the World of Darkness is like, and this is how separated from average humanity a Hunter has to be to operate safely.
