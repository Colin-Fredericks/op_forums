    I think restricting Slashers to the narrow slasher trope is not only a waste of material but a dilution of theme (as slasher movies are often campy and unserious, and can dilute the theme of a more serious chronicle). I've actually never run a Slasher as the "slasher movie villain." Not even once. All my Slashers have been people who have become defined by the act of murder, one way or another, and their current existence revolves around killing.

    I would agree with the psychological angle, but then again, what intelligent being doesn't have psychological issues? The reason psychological issues are so revealing/damning in the case of Slashers is because their single-minded focus on a single activity opens them up to have clear strengths and weaknesses. The same could be said of any person that devotes themselves to a single activity. Eventually your psychological issues will come to the fore, and you will become easier to read.

        A relevant bit of text from God-Machine Chronicle's rules update appendix that I can't find in Chronicles of Darkness:
        “My Character Kills People All the Time”

        Is it possible for a character to reach a point where killing another human being is not a breaking point? Players might make the argument for soldiers, policemen, gang members or good-hearted serial killers to be exempt from suffering breaking points from taking a life, up to a point. For mortal, non-supernatural characters, our recommendation is that if a character kills a person, it's always a breaking point, even if the player gets a positive modifier to the roll.

        Note that we said “mortal, non-supernatural” character. Vampires, werewolves and other shadow-folk aren't fully human and don't play by the same rules. Their rules, in fact, will be addressed in the appropriate books.

        This blurb was my inspiration for saying that removing the Breaking Point for murder is a key part of becoming a Slasher.

                    Originally posted by Dataweaver View Post
                    To me, the question of how a hunter becomes a slasher is linked to the question of what 2e should do with the Code. Here's how I'd do it:

                    2e should have a version of the Code that inoculates the hunter from Breaking Points at the cost of picking up a Tell (modeled by a Persistent Condition), with guidelines as to how this differs from a more natural evolution of the hunter's Breaking Points. To become a Slasher (more specifically, a Ripper), two things must happen: the hunter must choose to use the Code to negate killing as a Breaking Point; and the Hunter must adopt a murder-based Aspiration. Once these two conditions are met, the hunter becomes a Ripper and is eligible to pick up an Undertaking. You don't need an Undertaking to be a Ripper, and a Ripper who doesn't have an Undertaking differs from a hunter only psychologically.

                    You do need an Undertaking to become a Scourge, though; to become a scourge, the slasher must transition from a Ripper Undertaking to the associated Scourge Undertaking. I'm not sure yet what the requirements for that would be; possibly just an appropriate expenditure of Practical Experience. Once you become a scourge, you enter Monster territory: you gain a dot of Potency and can develop Dread Powers.
                    This sounds like an absolutely great way to handle the transition, because each step makes sense as something a Hunter might do, and they present a logical path for the well-intentioned extremist to become a character defined by murder. Removing killing as a Breaking Point seems like a logical choice for any Hunter, because they kill a *lot* of sentient monsters. And the second requirement makes it easy to justify because you can always tell yourself "it's fine as long as I don't take an Aspiration to kill anyone". But when that one target eludes you time after time, and you start getting more and more obsessed with taking it down...

                    What ever system they end up using, I hope it creates as smooth and natural a path to Slasherdom as this suggestion.

                        I like the concept but I would specify that the Code must nullify murdering mortals as a Breaking Point and then you need an Aspiration to kill a mortal. The classic, "Monsters Don't Count" is not enough.

                        Otherwise monster-of-the-week themed games where a vampire dies every session would be either unplayable (or at least unthematic) due to plummeting Integrity or everyone turning into a Slasher the moment they put stakeing a vampire down as an Aspiration. Dean and Sam's Integrity plummeted, but it wasn't guilt from slaying vampire's that did it.

                        If you specify mortals you still have an incentive to take the Code - it's hard to fight a vampire when you have to keep his human minions alive. And once that police detective (Dominated? Just suspicious that a crate of explosives went missing?) causes a bit too much trouble for your Cell... I think specifying mortals is a better balance that still presents a silky smooth Hunter->Slasher transition, without making common playstyles unworkable.


                        Taking the Code and avoiding taking the Aspiration to kill any monsters sounds a bit too meta-gaming for my taste. I would say that if you spend more than a few in-charachter hours planning to kill a monster it probably should be an Aspiration.
