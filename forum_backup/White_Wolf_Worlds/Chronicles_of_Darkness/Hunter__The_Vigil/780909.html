    Alien Hunters and (venue idea) Alien: the Conspiracy

    12-06-2015, 12:43 AM
    I wasn't sure where to post this, but the hunter forum seemed like a good idea because I can suggest compacts and conspiracies for this creature type. Before I get started I'd like to acknowledge that the subject of aliens in the nWoD, particularly Aliens with their own venue, is a prickly subject. It has been brought up several times over the years and each time it has been discouraged. At best aliens are handled in game as misinterpreted creatures of other supernatural origins. Extra dimensional spirits, demons, fae, primordial beasts and the like. At worst it has been said that aliens from other physical worlds don't fit into the World of Darkness because it is a world of spirits, mages, vampires, werewolves, and other such blatantly magical creatures. In a setting where magic is reality and Earth seems to be at the center of the universe, figuratively if not literally, what place is there for scientifically advanced beings that utterly disrupt that notion of mystic anthrocentrism?
    My first response to this is to point out that all venues in the World of Darkness are based on, or inspired by, folklore and mythology, and aliens are a part of folklore. Vampires, mages, werewolves, and others creatures have a near bottomless well of folklore and religion throughout history to pull from, and all of it has in one way or another been used to develop the rules, theme, and mood of each game venue. Aliens are very much a product of modernfolklore. Ever since Orson Wells did a radio broadcast of H.G. Wells “War of the Worlds” that incited mass hysteria, they have been a part of the public psyche. And after the famous incident at Area 51 near Roswell, New Mexico (which ought to be the signature city of an Alien venue), the idea of aliens being real became cemented in our minds and the stories began. A modern folklore has been crafted around the idea of aliens hiding among us, and at the center of this folklore is always a theme of conspiracy. Whether it is an alien conspiracy to secretly invade Earth, or a government conspiracy at the highest levels to hide the truth from the public, the modern folklore of aliens is inseparable from the rumor of conspiracy.
    Maybe the aliens are studying us to determine weaknesses before a full scale invasion? Or maybe they are monitoring us passively, waiting until we a ready to meet them in the open? Maybe the governments of the world are trying to hide the existence of aliens to avoid mass hysteria? Or maybe they have made contact with the aliens and are working together with them to devise a scheme to have absolute control over the human race? Whatever the case, the elaborate stories and theories that have sprung up around the idea that aliens are real and among us have made for a very rich modern folklore that can easily fit within a world that seems normal on the surface, but underneath is a world of monsters...a World of Darkness.
    My second response to the complaint that aliens don't seem to fit in a world of wizards and fairies, is that they fit precisely because aliens disrupt the notion that Earth is the mystical and figurative center of the universe. To quote page 8 of the World of Darkness Reliquary:
    “Within the World of Darkness, there seem to be exceptions to every rule. Every “always” likely has an “other than”, every “must” has at least one corresponding “unless”. These supernatural creatures are a certain way, except when they are not. These legendary places are just like this, other than when they are different. These people behave this way, except when they do not.”
    The fact that in a world of magic where ancient and mythical monsters walk the world disguised as normal people the idea of aliens from another planet seems out of place could, in and of itself, be a source of fear even to other supernaturals. They are those things that “shouldn't exist”. Our awareness of them is only very recent. Their very existence disrupts the anthrocentric assumptions upon which both human societies and supernatural metasocieties are based. If they actually exist, then their very existence would be disruptive to everything we have built our beliefs and societies on because they don't fit within those beliefs and most people can't handle something that really exists but doesn't fit in their belief structure. That disruptiveness is profoundly scary. In other words, they should exist in the World of Darkness precisely because they “shouldn't”.
    With that said I have an outline in my head as to how an Alien venue should look. From this point on I will launch into this outline, so continue reading if I have captured your interest. If at this point you still think that aliens are given enough justice in “Mirrors The Infinite Macabre” book, then read no further, and simply type a response you feel appropriate. Either way I want to see how much support there would be for an idea like this.
    This starts with the concept title of what an alien role play venue should be and why, the theme and mood of an alien chronicle, the types of aliens that should be in an alien venue based on modern alien folklore and who the main protagonists are, the main overarching conflict that drives an alien venue, the morality of aliens that operate on Earth, the unique power structure that aliens would have separate from the powers of other supernaturals, and finally the kinds of alien hunters that would be in an alien venue.
    Concept name: Alien: the Conspiracy
    Alternatively “Alien: the Invasion”. I'd go for “conspiracy” because, as I said earlier the modern folklore surrounding aliens is mired in conspiracies. From reptilians posing as human politicians to grays signing unknown agreements with world leaders to “men in black” covering up the truth everything about alien folklore is intertwined with some plot by some group of powerful people for some nefarious purpose that we are not privy to. This also makes for an extremely rich bed for plot devices on which an Alien chronicle or alien Hunter chronicle could be built.
    Theme and mood:
    The theme should, duh, be “conspiracy theory”, and the mood should be “espionage”, real cloak and dagger. With conspiracies comes sociopolitical intrigue, and with political intrigue comes spy work. The real excitement and danger is not in knowing the full depth and truth of a conspiracy, but in knowing that there is a conspiracy, but not knowing what the conspiracy is exactly. Maybe you are a human who is trying to uncover “the truth” about aliens, and you put yourself in harms way trying to uncover that truth without getting caught or killed. Or maybe you are part of an alien conspiracy, and you are just a cog in the larger machine and don't see the whole picture, but you suspect more is going on than what you are being told. Or there is more than one conspiracy by more than one group of aliens who work at cross purposes to each other, and you must engage in spy/counter spy tactics to hinder the best laid plans of your otherworldly adversary while doing everything in your power to make sure your schemes go off without a hitch.
    Whatever the case you don't dare risk working in the open in an alien game. If you are a human trying to expose the alien threat to the public and you approach them in the open like a white knight, they could work from the proverbial shadows to quickly and easily make you and all your work disappear. If you're an alien you stay hidden lest aliens of other factions find out about you and set events in motion that lead to your downfall. Standing in the light and proclaiming the truth only serves to put a target on your back that others WILL shoot at. In an Alien game the only sensible way to operate is from the shadows.
    Alien types:
    Grays. The bulbous headed aliens with black eyes that are said to ride around in flying saucers abducting people and cows to probe and study them. In game they are a race that once colonized Mars back when it was fertile and warm, but went to war with Mars when the colony seceded from the Gray's government and tried to be independent. Also the race involved in the Area 51 debacle.
    Reptilians. The alleged human-eating carnivores that hide among us and plot against us. In game they are dimension-traveling aliens who, while technically at peace with the Grays still spy on them as well as the Gray's enemies. They are on Earth for their own self-interests, and do not care for interference.
    Mothmen. The urban legends that hide in the woods only to chase off unsuspecting locals who stray too close to their territory. In game individuals of this race act as guns for hire. Their race as a whole has no dog in the fight, but specialists are willing to be hired to perform specific tasks and tend to care more about getting the job done than keeping a low profile, though they do understand the values of keeping Secrecy.
    Norns. The human-looking aliens who are said to show up in people's bedrooms. In game they would be the descendants of humans who were abducted from Earth long ago, and now have their own world to live on elsewhere in the galaxy. They show concern and empathy for the humans of Earth, and wish to help them survive the cold war between the Martians and the other aliens. While not as powerful, well equipped, or organized as the other races, the use of espionage helps to level the playing field for them a little. Mostly they are a nuisance to the other races and not a real threat.
    Little green men from Mars (Martians). Aliens that supposedly live in subterranean caves throughout Mars and regard Earth with envious eyes. In game they are the main protagonists. Driven underground in a war for independence against the Grays who obliterated the surface of their planet and turned it into the barren desert it is today, they seek to get back at the Grays for this ages old offense and use Earth's natural resources to help facilitate this revenge. They have lived in Mars for so many generations that they have evolved into a new species separate from the Grays. They even genetically engineered their own people to have chloroplast pigments in their skin to help them survive on bioluminescent light sources all throughout their subterranean cities. They only need to eat for the purpose of getting vitamins in their diet.
    Main conflict:
    A cold war between alien races with Earth as the staging ground. The primitive locals provide excellent cover, and can sometimes be a useful tool against the other aliens. The races engage in “games” of cat and mouse against each other while trying to obtain information and sabotage the others' efforts.
    The goal of the Martians was originally to use Earth's more plentiful resources to bolster their own species' prosperity. Different factions on Mars have different philosophies as to how that should be done. Some call for peaceful coexistence while hiding within the human race and building them up to the point where they could accept the Martians' existence peacefully, while other factions call for some variation of exploiting Earth at the humans' expense up to an including one faction that advocates open invasion and conquering. The discovery of Gray biologists surveying the life on the planet changed those conversations, and while the goal of using Earth's resources is still on the table, a new goal of assessing their old enemy, what their current strengths and weakness are, and how to cripple them became a priority.
    The Martians orchestrated the crashing of a Gray ship in the desert outside of Roswell, New Mexico and the following cover up through the use of agents planted in key positions of the human military. Using Earth as a staging ground and the human race as their cover, the Martians intend to stage a covert war of genocide against those who tried to wipe them out.
    The Grays and their political allies, the Reptilians, became aware of the Martians' activities through intelligence gathering operations that followed, and have themselves placed agents in key positions within human societies and governments. While they do not know that there are Martians still on Mars, they do know that the presence of hostile Martians on Earth could be a bad thing. They are determined to find out more and to possibly end the Martian threat, even if it means wiping them all out.
    Morality: Secrecy
    Secrecy. While aliens that live on their home planet among their own kind in their own civilizations may have a morality that is more based on social interactions, laced with culturally specific norms and taboos, those aliens that are allowed to travel the vast depths of space to other worlds undergo thorough psychological conditioning and mental restructuring designed to make them adopt a moral outlook that centers around maintaining the secrecy of their kind and technology. Individuals who cannot be successfully conditioned (Secrecy 6 or higher) are not allowed anywhere near a spaceship. This is a mode of operating that has become the standard norm across multiple planets, so all alien races in this venue effectively have Secrecy as their morality.
    An alien with Secrecy 10 blends in so perfectly with the native Earth populace that even supernatural characters with supernatural means of detecting non-humans can't tell that said alien is not human. They would even allow themselves to be killed if they thought it would help them maintain their Secrecy. On the other side of that scale, and alien with Secrecy 1 or 0 would basically say “Screw it” and go on a rampage in the streets with a flying saucer or a tripod, blasting everything in sight with a death-ray. Needless to say they wouldn't last long. Under this system of morality killing a human or a member of another alien race would not constitute a degeneration roll below Secrecy 8 so long as the alien doing the killing made sure that it didn't expose them or another alien. Examples include hiding or disposing of the body, and making a human's or other supernatural's death look like an accident, suicide, or mundane murder.
    Power: Psionics
    For aliens the mind is the soul, the soul is the mind, and the energy of the two is blended into one. The mental/spiritual discipline of harnessing Psi energy is not just an esoteric discipline among aliens, but a cornerstone of their advanced technology. Incidentally all aliens should have at least one dot in Psionic Technology. Other Psionic powers may vary by discipline or profession. While I haven't thought about all possible types of Psionic powers that aliens might employ, the following examples should be a good primer for what abilities aliens could have under this system.
    Psionic Technology: (1 to 5 dots). Alien technology cannot be used by just anyone. Humans who get a chance to examine it, or get a good enough look, see smooth non-Euclidean metallic shapes with no apparent means of manipulation. While human technology can be mechanically operated with buttons, switches, levers, triggers, and other mechanical interfaces, Psionic technology requires a direct psionic link with the user in order to be operated. An alien must physically touch a piece of psi tech and integrate some of their psi energy into the tool before it will become functional. Establishing a psi link with a piece of alien tech is instantaneous. Once they have established the psionic link the alien tech responds to their mental commands.
    Pieces of psionic tech can range in size and complexity from a one dot ray gun to a five dot super computer on a spaceship. The use of any piece of psi tech requires that the alien using the tech must have a dot rating in the ability Psionic Technology that is equal to or greater than the piece of tech they are using. For example: Grok lays a human down on an examination table on his ship to analyze his anatomy. The examination table is a 4 dot piece of psi tech. In order to use it Grok must have 4 or more dots in Psionic Technology.
    Aliens are also just as if not more creative than humans. So new psi tech is always being invented with a range of functions only limited by the imagination. Certain psi tech might require an investment of Psi energy. For example a pulse pistol might require the user spend 1 Psi per shot.
    Telepathy: Each dot rating of Telepathy grants an alien a more advanced body of telepathic abilities. Unlike human psychic mind reading abilities, those of aliens are far more potent and less taxing on their minds. They are able to do things with their mind that human psychics only wish they could do so well and with such ease. All telepathy rolls are Intelligence + Telepathy.
    1 dot: Mind Reading. Able to look inside another's mind and read any thoughts, conscious or unconscious, unless that mind is shielded. In which case target rolls Intelligence + Telepathy, or other appropriate trait against caster's Intelligence + Telepathy roll.
    2 dots: Telepathic Defense. An alien is able to defend her own mind against attempts to either read her thoughts, attempt to alter her thoughts, attack her mentally, or use other forms of mind-based powers against her. Since for aliens the mind and soul are the same thing, Telepathic Defense also applies toward any supernatural attack designed to directly affect the player character's spirit or soul. The role play elements of Telepathic Defense can be described as anything from a suit of mental armor to a psychic maze that one has to penetrate to affect the defending alien's mind/soul. Defending alien rolls Intelligence + Telepathy against whatever effects are directed at it's mind/soul.
    3 dots: Telepathic Hypnosis. The alien can now use telepathy to plant suggestions into another character's mind that they will be compelled to carry out. The alien using this can trap a subject in a mental labyrinth of their own mind while they physically stand there doing nothing, trick them into thinking they pulled the trigger on their gun when they didn't, bury a memory, or even make them bok like a chicken. Basically just about anything you could do with regular hypnosis only because the suggestions are planted directly in the mind via telepathy they are more compelling and harder to resist. If a subject is told to do something that would normally be against their will, or is able to know that their mind is being messed with they can make a contested Willpower roll, or roll any applicable defense, against the alien's initial Intelligence + Telepathy roll to leave the suggestion. Cost is 1 Psi per suggestion.
    4 dots: Mind Bullets! The alien at this level is able to directly attack another character's mind. The player can specify before initiating the attack whether the attack does bashing, lethal, or aggravated damage. The investment of Psi per point of damage depends on the type of damage done. Bashing costs 1 Psi per point, lethal costs 2 Psi per point of damage, and aggravated costs 3 Psi per point. Player rolls Intelligence + Telepathy vs. any applicable defense, if the target has any telepathic defenses, and if the player rolls more successes than the target they do that many points of damage IF they have enough Psi to cover the damage. For example if you attack a target, specifying aggravated damage, and you get five successes, but only have 3 Psi, then you only do 1 aggravated damage. Effects can range from a headache to forced nosebleeds to the target's head exploding depending on the amount and type of damage done.
    5 dots: Mass Telepathy. At this level and alien can perform any of the lower rated telepathic effects on a crowd within a radius around him. The amount of Psi invested in the area effect determines the radius. The cost is 1 Psi per ten feet. For example, if an alien wants to plant a suggestion in the minds of the people around him that they can't see him before running into a crowded street with no disguise on, he can spend 4 Psi for a 40-foot effect radius and 1 Psi for the suggestion. He is then able to walk into the crowded street without anyone seeing him, though he better stay away from video cameras. When used in conjunction with Mind Bullets, the Psi energy applied to the amount and type of damage done does not increase per person. The alien has, for example, 4 Psi in its Psi pool, and spends, say 3 Psi on a 30 foot radius, and 1 Psi for bashing damage. Everyone rolls any applicable defenses if they have them against the alien's Intelligence + Telepathy and as long as the alien gets at least one successes on everyone in that radius, they all receive 1 bashing.
    Telenautis: Aliens with this ability are able to control raw Psi energy with their mind. They can use it to throw bolts of Psi energy that do aggravated damage from their hands, coalesce it into a force field, or disrupt the powers of supernaturals. I haven't fully thought out what all this power would do at each dot level. Maybe its is worth giving more thought. Maybe it isn't. We'll see how much my ideas are accepted or rejected.
    How Aliens are Viewed by Other Supernaturals and Vice Versa:
    Ironically enough the ones that may have the hardest time dealing with the existence of visitors from another physical planet would be the magical and supernatural denizens of Earth themselves. Mages and Werewolves who have regular encounters with all kinds of spirits and bizarre creatures, many of whom seem alien enough in their own right, would be apt to rationalize the existence of space aliens as being creatures from the Shadow Realm. Changelings might spot an alien and think that it is a True Fae. Even some hunters who have more experience with other supernaturals might be apt to write off an alien they encounter as a cryptid or a demon or something they find more believable. For the most part true aliens are dismissed by the supernaturals of Earth simply because they don't fit within most people's paradigms or belief systems.
    On the other hand cells of alien spies might become aware of the existence of supernaturals on Earth, and might think them a curiosity to be studied or a threat to be avoided. An isolated vampire might get abducted by a flying saucer one night and never heard from again. A Promethian might find itself on a Martian examination table being vivisected. However, the aliens will never attack a group of supernaturals if they can help it. Doing so would give them the advantage of numbers, and besides could constitute a violation of their code of Secrecy. Only lone victims that they can easily overpower are at risk of being abducted, and even then only after they have been studied from a distance in order to learn what powers they might have and how those powers might be used. If a spy cell thinks a supernatural they are looking to abduct will be missed by its fellow supernaturals, then they may very well just leave that Sin Eater alone, and avoid her as long as she doesn't disrupt their operations.
    Alien Hunters:
    Those who hunt aliens can range from the Tier 1 to Tier 3 as usual in Hunter: the Vigil. Tier 1 hunters might be the neighborhood kids who find out that their 5th grade teacher is an alien or a group of paranormal investigators who notice something is going on in their local state park. Tier 2 could be a Compact like Network Zero who are trying to get the truth out there for all to see. It doesn't have to be Network Zero though. It could be a dedicated group of UFO hunters who are trying to expose a government cover up of the existence of aliens. That brings me to Tier 3. TFV is described as the “men in black” in the Hunter book, and that they do go after aliens. However, it would not be out of the question, given that the Grays and Reptilians have infiltrated certain high level positions in world governments, to make a dedicated alien-hunting Conspiracy designed to ferret out and kill the Martians. Such a Conspiracy would have not only privileged information regarding aliens that gives rise to anti-alien tactics, info that unbeknownst to them actually comes from aliens, but they would also have advanced technological Endowments designed specifically to disrupt Psionic powers and Psionic Technology. Endowments that were, once again unbeknownst to them, designed by aliens to be used by non-psionic humans against psionic aliens.

    Well, that's what I got. I hope someone read all that. Let me know what you think.

                              Originally posted by Quanutmshard View Post
                              Aww, no xenomorphs?
                              Sorry, no. They're more pop culture, less folklore. I've been playing for about 10 years, and I've heard a lot of people complain that aliens are not mystical, magical, or religious monsters and so they don't fit in the WoD. There have been peacemeal attempts at having "aliens" in the WoD as a nod to those who want aliens in the WoD while still appeasing those who think aliens break the gothic horror mood. I could go into a long list of examples of this from both the old WoD and the new WoD. The point is, if we want to convince people that aliens belong in the WoD (as aliens and not as Umbrood or Fae or something like that), and *maybe* get a cannon venue out for Alien, then we've got to emphasize the modern folklore aspect of aliens, compare that apples to apples to the folklore aspects of the other venues, and be able to play up supernatural aspects of them like mental powers and such. Sadly, that includes sticking to the alien types that are talked about in alien folklore and not delving into the space opera territory of movies and TV shows.

                              Additionally there has got be a way of saying, "Wizards, and Werewolves, and Fairies, and Demons, and Aliens are REAL [in the WoD]," without making it sound as stupid as that sentence just sounded. That's a mood killer.

                              To illustrate what I mean I am reminded of one of the last games I played of Mage: the Ascension in the Camarilla. This was when all the end of the world stuff was going down. I played a Son of Ether called Dr. Yuri Rostovski (guess where he was from) who was a physics professor at a Technical university in LA, and was a paranormal investigator who looked for aliens in his spare time. Well, to summarize we were all fighting Hit Mark 7, and it had merged with a Mother Weaver spirit and was wreaking havok Godzilla-style, and it was being fought by an army of Mages, an army of Werewolves, an army of Ratkin, THE Army [courtesy of the Technocracy], and some Technocrats. We had access to miracle chips that game, and let me tell you if not for those chips we'd have been SCREWED. I don't remember all the names of all the characters present, but there was one Order of Hermes person who summoned a bolt of pure prime to strike the HM7, one Euthanatos who transformed into an avatar of Kali the Hindu death goddess to attack HM7, and I spent my miracle chip to summon help [it came in the form of "aliens" (umbrood) Yuri had contacted earlier]. So with all this other crap going on, everyone sees a freaking flying saucer float down from the clouds and launch smaller ships at the HM7 to attack it. DJ Noise, the local big wig of the Cult of Ecstasy, sees this and yells, "NOW THERE'S ALIENS!?!?! THAT'S IT! I QUIT! I'M GOING HOME!" We all broke character and had to laugh our asses off for a minute before the game could resume.

                              That, fellas, that....kind of humor, is an example of why people say aliens break the mood of the WoD. Many people find them so out of place it can be comical. In order to fit aliens into the WoD that sort of humor has to be downplayed and an air of mystery, folklore, and a fear of that which is strange and doesn't belong has to be interjected.

                                  As to how Hunter Conspiracies would react to them and what an alien-backed conspiracy would look like, let me go down the list and give little blurbs.
                                  Aegis Kai Doru: They have collected and stored a few pieces of Psionic Technology in the past 50 years, but have been unable to get any of it to work. Sometimes these alien "relics" are obtained when they pursue a reported skin changer that turns out not to be a werewolf. Sometimes the Guardians of the Labyrinth find their operations bureaucratically hindered when they shouldn't be, but after investigating the people behind the interference find more layers of bureaucracy with little if any hint of otherworldly involvement. While Aegis Kai Doru may suspect that extraterrestrials exist, and have some ideas as to what their relationship to certain ancient relics might be, their actual knowledge of aliens is little greater than that of the general public.
                                  Ascending Ones, Malleus Maleficarum, Lucifuge, and Knights of Saint George: As is said in the Hunter: the Vigil core book the term demon is used as a catchall for otherworldly creatures, and members of these Conspiracies would tend to use that term to describe aliens even IF they believed that God created intelligent life on other worlds. Most of them don't. Most of them posses a religious world view that doesn't leave room for life on other worlds. Sure, the stars and other planets are there. That doesn't necessarily mean anything though. For the most part the true extent of a religiously minded person's "extended universe" is the spiritual realms and what lies there in. Needless to say being confronted with aliens would leave most of the members of these groups with an existential crisis. Fortunately for them aliens are usually so good at subterfuge that members of these conspiracies only have to deal with them on the periphery and are free to form their own conclusions as to what they are playing cat and mouse with, even if those conclusions are just small-minded affirmations.
                                  Les Mysteres: This group is so eclectic that It maybe be hard to pin down a stereotype. Some might be open to the idea of aliens as visitors from another world as well as alien spirits. Some such people may even believe they commune with these aliens on a spiritual level. Indeed it may be easy for a telepathic alien to influence a group of Mysteres by leaving suggestions in their mind or visiting them in their dreams, but never in person as that might be a violation of Secrecy. Other Mysteres may be so religiously paradigm locked in what Voudou is and is not that they would refuse to recognize an extraterrestrial in the unlikely event that they come face to face with one. In any event the relationship that any one group of this conspiracy has with any one group or cell of aliens can be very fluid.
                                  Task Force Valkyrie: They know what happened at in that farm outside of Roswell, New Mexico and they were involved in passing off the premature Martian invasion of Grover's Mill, New Jersey as a hoax. They have access to Area 51. They've seen the bodies. They've examined their technology. They are used to covering up the truth. They told the public it was a weather balloon over New Mexico. They told the CBS executives in the middle of the "War of the Worlds" radio broadcast to tell Orson Wells to add a disclaimer that it was all pretend and in good fun. Their position on aliens is similar to their position on other supernaturals: ferret them out and kill them while covering up their existence. However, the proficiency with which aliens are able to insert themselves into government agencies has some wondering if TFV isn't compromised on some level....
                                  Cherion Group: A few high ranking executives have managed to rub elbows with and grease the right palms of leadership within the TFV, gaining them limited access to the bodies at Area 51. The Cherion Group shows a keen interest in alien biology and adapting anything useful of it to human beings. This has, of course fed conspiracy theories of aliens attempting to create human-alien hybrids among groups like Network 0.
                                  Vanguard Serial Crimes Unit: Unlike the TFV or the Cherion Group, VASCU is not neck deep in privileged information and cover up conspiracies. Most agenst, however, have seen enough weird crap in their line of work to know that it pays to keep an open mind. Most VASCU who do get on the tail of an alien find their investigation quickly taken over by TFV, and their involvement abruptly cut short. VASCU is at once the one conspiracy most capable of investigating alien activity, and the one least able to do anything about it if they manage to ferret one out. Bear in mind that VASCU is interested in multiple homicide, not paranormal events per se. They don't care if the perp making people disappear is another human, or something more otherworldly. They wouldn't go investigate crop circles. Not their jurisdiction. If, on the other hand, a person mysteriously disappeared, and then his friend that went looking for him disappeared, and the family that went looking for the friend disappeared, and crop circles just happen to be involved...well then VASCU will take a look.

                                  Alien Hunter conspiracy Majestic 12: Aliens from other planets don't limit themselves to one country or even one continent on Earth, and those that wish to hunt them must similarly act without borders if they are to be effective. Unlike TFV, MJ12 is not beholden to just one government. Every government in the United Nations secretly hosts a chapter of MJ12, and at least one member of each of those governments is a MJ12 liaison if they are not the leader of their nation's chapter of MJ12. They are aware of the existence of aliens and have access to PseudoPsionic Tech that is specifically designed to detect hidden aliens in ways that other methods can't compete with and to disrupt aliens' psionic technologies and abilities. MJ12 is very highly politically connected and has deep pockets. They are able to shuffle money, resources, and men across the world, silence investigators who get too close to uncovering them, and look to frustrating the political machinations of anyone their directors point them towards. Although its an open secret that the organization has 12 directors, very few know who those twelve are. Only that the tactics, information, and technologies disseminated by the 12 have shown to be effective. The number of people who know that those 12 are themselves alien.....if even one close to them knows, they aren't letting on to it.

                                          Alien religion. Instead of having aliens worship gods that look like them, act like them, think like them, and share their culturally specific norms and taboos (as humans are apt to do), I thought that it might be interesting to have the various races follow their own version of a Mystic Pantheism in which they view the Universe itself as the higher power, or "god", with the universal subconscious as a form of consciousness that is interwoven throughout reality. Since they travel space and have a more expanded public consciousness of the Universe they might be less apt to view themselves as being the most important thing in it. Instead of becoming a ghost in the Twilight or a spirit their mind/soul would fade into and merge with the universal subconscious upon death. This would make sense given their view of the mind and soul being the same thing and would explain how there are no alien ghosts in the Twilight of CoD (even though aliens would die on Earth).

                                          However, since the release of the "God Machine" chronicle I was wondering if I should incorporate a set of alien interpretations of the God Machine as religious views instead?
