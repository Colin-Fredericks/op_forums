    Is it time to divorce Tier from Cell/Compact/Conspiracy?

    12-30-2015, 09:57 PM
    With Open Development in full swing, I figure it's time to bring back a debate almost as old as Hunter (and long-championed by The King's Raven): can and should Hunter organizations be viewed separately from Tiers?

    In every other Chronicles game, Tier is a reflection of scale and scope for your chronicle, roughly analagous to a Local/National/Global divide. Hunter is shackled to an older form of it, and one that doesn't make too much sense when viewed rationally; Tier 1 is lone individuals, Tier 2 is a membership in a group, Tier 3 is membership in a special group. This system already falls apart when looked at too hard - I'm dead certain that Network Zero has more members than the Lucifuge - but it's also the only game with this weird structure.

    Is an independent cell of dogged investigators travelling the globe in order to stop a vampire conspiracy not a Tier 3 game? Is a lone VALKYRIE cell, cut off from their superiors and sent to investigate why a tiny town has such a high missing persons rate not a Tier 1 game?

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

      I already do this in my games, along with decoupling Endowments from the groups they're from. I completely ignore Tiers, I allow players to pick up any Endowments they like if they can justify it with fluff (for example, a devoutly religious Hunter being "blessed" with Benedictions despite never having even heard of the Malleus, or a mad doctor learning to vivisect monsters and create the equivalent of Cheiron's grafts for himself and his fellow Hunters), and I ignore how big or small compacts and conspiracies are supposed to be. I tend to upscale Compacts a bit and globalise them, and then downscale the reach and power of Conspiracies.

      And I'll most likely keep doing this in Hunter 2.0.

              I'm all for the division of tiers from compacts and conspiracies, but I'm also for a redefinition of compacts and conspiracies. The Tiers have long since been tied to 'scale', and I think it's both baked into enough of the Chronicles of Darkness and useful enough to keep the idea of local, regional, and global organizations.

              Conspiracies and compacts, in the mean time, need to be the difference between those that employ extra-normal/supernatural means and those that don't. Network Zero is a Tier 3 compact, Promethean Brotherhood is probably a Tier 2 Conspiracy, as examples.

              EDIT: Both sides need to be manipulated by the supernatural as much as they are not, too. Let's have more of this careful dance between humanity and monstrosity.

                  I think the Tiers definitely need to be divorced from Compacts and Conspiracies. In the 1700s travel may have been difficult and truly global organizations limited, but it's almost 2016! Anyone with some money (which every Compact likely has) can hop on an airplane for globe trotting adventures; anyone with a few bucks a month can set up a website dedicated to their cause and reach out globally.

                  I think the difference between Compacts and Conspiracies should be the existence of centralized authority with clear chains of command: a Conspiracy has a single, powerful authority directing it (such as the US government, the Vatican, or Cheiron corporate HQ) whereas a Compact should be more diffuse and informal with many local leaders and more variance in local policies.

                      It still kind of bugs me that the word Tier was introduced into Hunter and ment the size of the group, and was then used for something completely later; but you can't change the past.

                      My thought's is that there's multiple important factors that all tended to get compressed into one word "Tier" in Hunter 1E and in 2E they should be split apart:
                      • The overall size/resources/power of the organisation
                      • Do they have an Endowment. Later expanded with the lower power Compact Endowments.
                      • How are they structured? The Union is bottom up. Task Force: VALKARIE are top down.
                      • The scope of the game.


                      In 2E what I'd like to see is:
                      • The word Tier means the scope of the game - that's fixed now.
                      • Compact refers to groups with a shared philosophy. Conspiracy refers to groups with an actual chain of command. This means that, for example, The Lucifuge would be a Compact (most Lucifuge are pretty independent as I understand it) and The Loyalists of Thule would be a small and underpowered Conspiracy.
                      • Endowment is completely divorced from other concerns. The Lucifuge would keep Castigation, the Loyalists of Thule wouldn't get a new Endowment.


                      Basically. When I play a Compact I want to know that me and the other PCs will be a mostly independent Cell; but we'd be expected to have certain values and ideals and there are people who have similar values and ideals we can mostly trust. If we're Null Mysteriis we'd be expected to be perusing scientific understanding, as so long as our goal remains science rather than the extermination of vampires there are people who'll have our back. If we're Task Force: VALKARIE we'd expect someone to give us orders, and perhaps the opportunity to give our subordinates orders.

                      Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                      Conspiracies and compacts, in the mean time, need to be the difference between those that employ extra-normal/supernatural means and those that don't. Network Zero is a Tier 3 compact, Promethean Brotherhood is probably a Tier 2 Conspiracy, as examples.
                      Sure. It's a big thematic difference, but IMO it's a smaller thematic difference than how the organisation is structured. United We Stand and all that. I'm not sure it's nessacary to have a special keyword, but if it is I'd be partial to sticking "Empowered" on there. So the Promethian Brotherhood are an Empowered Compact, VASCU are an Empowered Conspiracy.

                      I think though, I would prefer not to use a special keyword for this. Just because of the edge cases. Are Task Force: VALKARIE an Empowered group because of Advanced Armory, or are they not because AA is mostly non-supernatural? I think it might be better to just let players and Storytellers read the Endowment section (and status benefits) and apply their own judgement on what that Endowment means for the themes.


                      I'd also like to draw a distinction between groups who're strictly anti-supernatural. Groups who've got a few non-reproducable tricks, and groups with the ability to grant all their membership some mojo - with only the last of these being an Endowment.

                      E.G. The Long Night might have one or two members who can call down miracles from God, but they can't teach anyone or only 1 in 100 students learns enough to pull of a working miracle. The Malleus Maleficarum can teach just about anyone to use Benedictions; so that's an Endowment.

                                  I'm also all in for separating Tier of play from Compacts and Conspiracies. To use most Hunter appropriate media inspirations - The X Files are chronicle about VASCU cell on Tier 1, without all backing up or Teleinformatics powers. In meanwhile Supernatural is non-organisation play from Tier 1 to Tier 3 and back again, in that Winchesters save single people, then rescue a one state and then save whole world - all without rally joining any Compact or Conspiracy. ( Well, there was Roadhouse in season 2, but it was still more "we are only meeting some hunters from it - we are not part of that crew" kinda thing. )

                                  I say that we should separate Tier of play from structure of organisation. All Compact and Conspiracies can be split in to those groups ( from the bottom ) that have geographical jurisdictions:
                                  1. Cell - Town, city's district.
                                  2. Chapter House ( Division?) - Region, small country, state in Americas.
                                  3. Headquarters (HQ) - Whole globe.

                                  And with this partition we easily can have Compacts that are based on Chapter Houses from whole the world. each slightly different and with local variation, maybe even it's own name - but still answering as one Compacts name. We can have one "Union", that is divide in to lesser Chapter Houses ( various "unions"), each with it's own agenda in the bigger creed of Compact, leaders and even names. Our Chapter Houses may even fight between themselves. Or that we can various "congregation" of Long Night, each being different church in reality. Compacts still can have one HQ, but it's center of the group only in name - each Chapter House is in reality independent. Each "Ashwood Abbey" Chapter House is, in reality, different Hellfire Club, with it's own name, policies, leaders. First Chapter is more the model to repeat or embody than direct commander.

                                  Conspiracies, then, are groups with direct chain of command. HQ is sending orders, you must to obey them. Chapter Houses can still fight, but central make sure it's not all out conflict and in the end - you are agent to the same force, nor similar.

                                  It's nicely sum up mine idea - Chapter Houses in Compact are loosely similar ( and could be name differently ), Chapters in Conspiracy are the same, in the end.

                                  And yes, in this model, Loyalists of Thule becomes Conspiracy, because of the power Three Man of Munich on the rest of members. Probably Ascending Ones, as so loose, various cults, become then Compact.

                                              I think that at the least, there needs to be a more mechanical distinction between Conspiracies and Compacts, and that there should be a distinction. Global scale and access to travel is independent of this distinction, the real question I feel is that of power and obligation. I don't mind compacts getting Endowments, but I do think when you're in a Conspiracy, there should be some mechanical presence representing the privileges you get from being in a well connected and hopefully well funded organization that at the least, has some sort of infrastructure for you to call upon for information or narrative control. That's the power part, that the Conspiracy (and by extension, you) is deep in this shit. They don't just investigate secrets, they also create them. Those secrets give them power, but are also a big weakness. Compromising those secrets is not in the conspiracy's interests.

                                              Now, compacts have secrets as well, but I think the difference is that compacts remain loose enough to where they can survive where conspiracies can't, that any secrets they have effect individuals more than they do the organization as a whole. Mechanically you could represent this as Hunters in a conspiracy having standing or assigned orders they must obey or get shafted, something like that. As long as there's some meat to the idea that being in a conspiracy means being obligated to uphold their secrets and expectations.

                                              I will agree that geographic scope should be independent of this distinction. The lone VALKYRIE cell in the OPs example I think is still a tier 3 game because the PCs in that scenario should have more resources and tools (they are still government agents with Advanced Armory gadgets and training from peers who have dealt with the occult) to call upon to figure out why there's so many missing people, and more importantly have more ability to deal with a cause that's far weirder and more dangerous than what a less connected cell that's either working independently or in a compact can be expected to handle. It doesn't matter what the cause actually is, they just have more options.

                                              So...no, I don't think tiers need to be divorced from compacts and conspiracies, but we do need to reaffirm what they mean, and emphasize those differences mechanically to give players and storytellers some neat new tools to play with to represent the pros and cons from joining such organizations.
