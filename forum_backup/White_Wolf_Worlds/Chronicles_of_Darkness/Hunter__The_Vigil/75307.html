    Tiers revisited

    02-19-2014, 07:20 PM
    So this is something that I've written for Hunter: Cults my slow burn project to be a counterpart to Hunter Slasher; and perhaps even more. Normally my hunter work just dodges the tangled definition of the Tiers. Not this time, this is where I look at every possible way you can define Tier, define the four Tiers by that metric, and talk about what group would be where in this scale.

    It's been done for a while so some of you may have seen it before, but since Cults won't be ready for quite a while and I think this turned out well I thought I'd post it here. If you haven't seen it yet: Enjoy!

    Tiers Revisited

    Among everything else, people want to belong to something. Whether it's a religious faith, a community group, or a social clique, human beings as social animals want to insulate themselves from the barbs of living by forming bonds with others in similar situations. Some people function better in large groups where they can find anonymity, while others seek focused groups to prove themselves in as some of the best around in whatever their skills are.

    Hunter is unique among the gamelines in the World of Darkness in that it was designed from the ground up to be played at different tiers. Different organisations populate the world of Hunter: Hunters can start in a small local cell, become part of a regional compact, or take a paycheck for doing a conspiracy's bidding. A hunter's way of holding the vigil can even influence the Tier they rise to. It is a sad fact, for hunters as well as humans, that people who serve the organisation rise faster than those who serve the organisation's mission while an idealistic hunter may well shy away from joining a Conspiracy with skeletons it's closet, which is all of them.

    However the choice to tie the Tiers to organisations has led to some inconsistencies: Is Tier three about global scope and tier two about regional scope? Baring the occasional exception VASCU's legal jurisdiction dosn't spread beyond the USA. The Loyalists of Thule are international.
    Is Tier three about the size of the organisation? The Union is larger than the Lucifuge's 666 members.

    Perhaps the tiers refer to the command and control structures of the organisation? A member of Division Six is under tighter control than a member of Les Mysteries' informal web of connections.

    Even the presence of Endowments is not a hard and fast rule. A Cell where every member has the Gifted Merit, completly permitted under the rules of tier one play, has far more magic than Task Force: Valkarie's advanced technology.

    So if nothing can conclusively claim to be the underlying division between Tiers let us take a closer look at the possibilities:

    Size of the organisation

    The size of the organisation is perhaps the most natural way to divide the organisations between the Tiers. Every other aspect of Compact or Covenant is influence by it's size. The more active hunters or cultists, the more operations can be run at once.
    Does the group have an Endowment? Perhaps they make magic rings, then the more people they have the more that can work on earning money to buy gold and gemstones and the more poeple sitting at workbenches crafting rings. In short more people, if used properly, leads to a better Endowment.

    Is the group organised? Five people don't really need more than the occasional hour to catch up and agree what each one will be doing. Five hundred thousand people would split into multiple independent organisations in a week, in practice if not in name, unless they had some way of organising themselves.

    And yet size is almost irrelevant to a roleplaying game. Unless you are playing as the organisation itself with a nice box to write size on your charachter sheet and rules to use it, size is going to be a distant abstract thing. Five hundred thousand people, there's no way the players can talk to all of them and build an emotional connection. Size is only relevant when it affects what kind of stories the players can get involved in. A war between hunters and cultists can be an interesting story, the players could be either the generals and high priests at the top or the little guys at the bottom. A larger organisation could also prevent certain "undesirable" stories. Vampires may be political masters but their rivalries and territorial nature limits their domain, sending agents abroad can let them hunt Vampires while minimising the risk that friends or family will be targeted.

    In short the size of the organisation is a good rough measure for the capabilities of the organisation. It is a rough measure: A cell of four hunters and one billionaire backer could hold an international globe trotting vigil. They would certainly have more capabilities than a cell without a billionare but maybe not as much as a true compact. Just hopping on a plane with a suitcase full of guns is a bad idea. Once they arrive they'll need to put effort into finding information and weapons, something a Conspiracy with people on the ground don't need to worry about. While raw manpower can be replaced with money, skills or magic, size makes a good general rule for the capabilities of an organisation.

    Tier one

    When defined by Size a Tier one organisation would probably have less than ten members. An absolute maximum would probably be around 150 members: The commonly accepted value for Dunbar's Number, the number of social relationships an average person has the brainspace for.

    Tier two

    Tier two could begin with as few members as fifty and perhaps grow as large as 5000. A tighter definition might put the limit at 150 to 1500. As the only organisation with an explicit membership, the Lucifuge's 666 members clearly place them as a Tier two group when measured by Size.

    Tier three

    However big Tier two is, Tier three is anything bigger than that.

    The Organisation's Control Over it's Own Members

    Of all the possible ways to define tier, the structure of the player's organisation is probably the factor with the greatest direct impact the players. A loosely controlled organisation may be seen as an enabler: It provides money, information and backup. Whatever the players decide they want to do; be it track down the vampire who stole their kids, peruse the holy MacGuffin or kick every demon out of their home town. Well the friendly organisation makes it that much easier.

    In contrast a tightly run organisation might be a disabler. The boss refuses to let you go peruse a personal vendetta while a cult is busy building political influence in the heart of the USA. This does not have to be a bad thing, part of the fun of playing in a secret conspiracy or military unit is the fact it actually is a secret conspiracy or military unit rather than just your choice of Endowment. Being given mysterious tasks, participating in a large operation and climbing up the ladder can all make for interesting stories. None of which can exist without a controlling hierarchy.

    If you define tiers by the organisational structure then it does not hold true that that a tier three organisation is more dangerous than a tier two organisation. After all, it's hard to beat the level of top down control you might see in a ten man cult.

    The rest of this section will assume the players have the same rank.

    Tier One

    The first tier of control is that of perfect freedom. No one tells the hunters what to do. Since humans are, well human, this almost certainly means that that there is no one else to try and boss the hunters around.

    In theory the players might find and join an organisation that gives them piles of free stuff without even asking what they will do with it but one has to ask where on Earth anyone would find such a thing and if they really want to give hunters free equipment, made a bureaucratic oversight or have some sinister plot.

    Tier Two

    The second tier is social convention. There's a way we do things around here. In The Union we look out for our own. In Network Zero we record and share clips of monsters. Most Tier Two organisations can't formally order their members and the rest just generally don't; so they favour the carrot over the stick. People who follow the organisation's party line get noticed, respected and get more of whatever the organisation offers it's members. People who don't languish at the bottom of the Status ladder.

    There is also a practical advantage of sharing a party line. A lot of Hunters tend to have extreme personalities, they're dedicated and stubborn. If they weren't they wouldn't have taken up the vigil. Sometimes that stubbornness can cause problems, when you're trying to deal with a curse wielding witch you don't want to end up fighting other hunters over whether witches count as human and must be taken alive to face justice or as monsters and can be taken out with a sniper rifle. If you know where to find people who'll agree with you on most of the big issues, well that cuts right through those problems.

    It must be said that these loose organisations can attract people to take without giving back but then, most probably don't mind so much. Network Zero calls theirs "the audience" and try to increase their number. There are hunter cell's who's membership of The Union amounts to nothing more than reading the forum.

    Tire Three

    Finally at the third tier you have top down control. It could be strict micromanagement but it probably just means you have a boss that gives you orders and she has a boss that gives her orders.

    From the perspective of the guy being ordered around the disadvantages of this are obvious but there is also an advantage, beyond the fact that most Endowment offering conspiracies are tier three. If you're in an organisation that can order you around, the other members can be ordered around too. This makes it much easier to plan things, if your cell needs support in clearing out a hive of bee spirits that's started infesting people it will be a lot simpler and easier organising that support when you only need to talk to the one person with the authority to order a few cells rather than talk to six cells individually and try and find a time that works for everyone.

    The same advantage applies on a larger scale. From the perspective of an individual cell it's nice to know your rescue is well organised. From the perspective of humanity it's nice to know there is someone who can organise an effective defence. If a cult has a hundred armed men that go out and kidnap young virgin men to sacrifice it's not enough for a the hunters to have two hundred armed men. They need to get them all into once place with their guns and ammo, they need food, water, and safehouses to sleep in. When they start fighting the cult they need to work together, not constantly trip over each other while the cultists pick them off. Organisation isn't enough, you also need the manpower, resources and perhaps Endowments to organise, but it is vital.

    Cult Tier

    A hypothetical forth tier of control would not stop outside the member's skull. They wouldn't be satisfied with control over what their subordinates do, they'd want to control what the cultists think.

    Such a system offers few benefits for the brainwashed (obviously they'd disagree, they're brainwashed) but many advantages for the leaders. On the level of the organisation itself it provides the same benefits as the third tier, organisation, with an additional helping of loyalty. The disadvantages are potent though: Brainwashed cultists often lack initiative, stoke the leader's ego to dangerous levels and the use of brainwashing alone is enough to make enemies.

    Mixing it Up

    The simple fact is that organisations are not monoliths. A cell in the union might be run democratically, by rule of charisma or have a captain. Large and tightly controlled conspiracies will have specialists that are trained in going undercover or behind enemy lines for extended periods without contacting anyone above them in the chain of command. It is not quite the same freedom as might be enjoyed by the lower tiers, they were given orders before departing and will have to give a debriefing on their return, but it's a rather different story than one where the colonel says "jump" and you say "how high".

    In short. When Tier represents the organisation's level of control it represents the general picture. It gives an idea of how easily the organisation can focus it's resources, what it is like to be a member and what the members will be like if you meet one as an NPC.

    Scope of the Story

    Tier One

    Tier one cells are geographically constrained. They stick to their familiar places, patrolling their neighborhoods and towns with little thought of going to other locations. Usually this is because they can't afford the expenses of keeping a wider territory. A small focused vigil has it's advantages: They know each store owner, most of the local cops, and maybe even the repeat offenders and career criminals, when new arrivals come to town the cell is quick to notice. Geographical constraints also have noticeable disadvantages, leaving your turf can put you at a significant disadvantage and a larger enemy with a well solid base outside your territory from which to launch repeated attacks can be a significant threat.

    For the size of a Tier One setting a good rule of thumb is that no major character is ever more than three degrees of separation from a PC, unless they are supposed to be an outsider.

    Tier Two

    In a Tier two game the group never leaves a single well defined region, usually a city or a stretch of countryside with a few towns and villages. There is room to move, places away from the beaten path where you can lay low, and people who haven't heard of your name yet who you might get help from after a few bridged burn. Of course, in a Tier 2 game the monsters and cultists get those advantages too.

    For the size of a Tier Two setting you should assume that any large event will be noticed and effect the entire length and breadth of the setting. If you're playing in Philadelphia and "take care" of the mayor after what you saw her and her cult doing to those kids, well that's going to affect all of Philadelphia.

    Tire Three

    In a Tier three game the group can travel to a region where nothing they did before will have had any impact at all. If the players spent the first story hunting Vampires in Tokyo then they're reassigned to fight a scorpion cult in Cairo, the only thing that comes from Tokyo is the experiences they lived from and maybe the status they've accumulated with their Conspiracy. But the third Tier isn't just the ability to travel beyond the effects of your action, it is also the possibility to affect the world with your actions. If Task Force: VALKYRIE's top agents rescue the President's son and prevent the United States being blackmailed into a war with Iran, that's a solid Tier three story. Even if the agents never leave Washington DC, with foreign ambassadors, allied and rival intelligence services, allied and rival Conspiracies and international organisations monsters, all with a stake in the crisis. They don't have to travel the world, the world just came to them.

    For the size of a Tier Three game, the whole Earth.

    Tire Four

    A Tier four game goes beyond the Earth itself. The Hunters travel through spirit reams, underworlds, gods' domains and strange alien realms. Simply including the spirit realm isn't enough to reach Tier four, for the right group a Tier one or two game could easily include the spirit realm. The trick is to keep it local and bind the ephemeral tightly to the material. The forth Tier consists not only of other dimensions but also ambitions to match.

    Players travel between multiple realms and bargain with the gods to affect the whole world at the least, and perhaps more. Exterminating vampires entirely is modest for Tier four, being on the borderline with Tier three. Turning your ideals of morality into a fundamental law of the universe, creating new forms of supernatural power, or helping your beloved deity defeat a rival are the sort of things a Tier four game might include. Naturally, few hunters ever play at this level.

    Power of Endowments

    In this section the Tiers are divided by the raw power of the Endowments available at that level. If anything could be an accurate measurement of how tiers have been defined so far this would probably be it. Tire 2 organisations gave you free traits with Status and the ability to call upon hunters from your organisation. Tier 3 organisations added Endowments to the benefits. However later books broke the pattern, and thus this definition is no longer completely accurate.

    Despite that, the strength of Endowments does have a noticeable effect upon the themes of a Hunter game. Humans fighting to protect humanity from the supernatural looks very different if you're casting magical spells to do it. Hunter has also included a small theme that Conspiracies made moral compromises in order to acquire their Endowments. The Ascending Ones had to turn to the drug trade to fund their expensive Elixirs, The Lucifuge's powers came from Hell itself.

    This rule was never strict. Ashwood Abbey were always less moral than the Lucifuge (did people ever need magic to be evil?) and in Witchfinders a group called The Promethean Brotherhood made perhaps the biggest sacrifice of morality in order to gain supernatural power in the hunter setting; yet they are a Compact and were a Cell when they first used their Endowment. Yet the rule does not need to be strict in order to be an interesting theme that you could choose to include in your game.

    Tier One

    Tier one is simple: There are no Endowments.

    Tier Two

    Most Endowments at the second Tier can be described as "about the supernatural" rather than "supernatural". They arise because an organisation has enough knowledge about the monsters in the shadows to teach useful skills and techniques. Any scientist can think rationally when confronted with the supernatural but Null Mysteriis have over a century of experience in knowing which scientific methods work best.
    Some of the Endowments at Tier Two, perhaps a Tier 2.5 if you will, are supernatural. These Endowments consist of one single trick. Despite being supernatural these Endowments fit better at Tier Two because at heart their users have a similar relationship with their Endowment as the practitioners of mundane Endowments: A Tier two Supernatural Endowment works with and enhances a Hunter's existing skills but it cannot replace them.

    Sidebar: What makes an Endowments

    Ever since Witch Finders introduced the Gifted Merit it has been possible for even independent hunters to acquire magic tricks. The Goblin Markets in Dream Catchers, psychic phenomina and Thaumaturgy from Second Sight, and Supernatural Merits. At this point it becomes worthwhile to answer the question: What makes an Endowment special; why are they different from merits like Gifted?
    The answer is the ease in which it can be shared. Most magic either relies on some innate gift that cannot be shared or it's like highly advanced mathematics, anyone can study it but only the best will ever understand it. In contrast an Endowment can be easily shared: Anyone who studies hard will pass the course on Benedictions, Task Force: VALKYRIE can give you Advanced Armoury as easily as putting it into your hands, learning to develop new Elixirs is harder than cutting edge chemistry research but once the Southern Temple cracks a new formula any amateur garage meth cook can follow the instructions.

    This makes Endowments exceptionally valuable. They may not be as powerful or flexible as the magic wielded by fully qualified Witches but numbers are the biggest advantage hunters have over the monsters; when the effectiveness of an Endowment is multiplied by the membership of a large Conspiracy the vigil turns from a candle into a raging bonfire.

    Tire Three

    Conspiracy Endowments: Either a broad list to choose from like Benediction or a single trick that can create a broad array of powers like the Rites of Hectae. Most of these Endowments are supernatural in nature and all of them can define how you approach the vigil through their flexibility and potency.

    Tier Four

    Hunters are usually the underdogs when it comes to raw magical power - even if a Conspiracies size, money and influence makes it the top dog overall - and a forth tier Endowment would most probably continue that trend. A Tier four monster is most probably a God. A being so powerful that it could rewrite the world, if not the universe, in it's image and compared to that a Tier four Endowment is a parlor trick, even if it could turn ancient vampires into dust left right and centre.

    If a God is like a king then a Tier Four hunter organisation would be like The Gunpowder Treason. Their numbers are tiny compared to the king's army, their resources are pitiful compared to the royal treasury, but they have secrecy and a trick that lets them play in the big leagues. To turn the metaphor into a practical example: Imagine a group of fanatical witch-finders who (somehow) have gotten hold of one of Zeus' thunderbolts and a mask that once belonged to Loki. They plan to sneak into heaven and assassinate Heka (an Ancient Egyptian God who is magic itself). If they succeed magic would cease to exist, or at least, part of it would.

    In short a Tier Four Endowment doesn't put divine power in the hands of hunters, it "merely" allows hunters to interact with the Gods and just perhaps subvert their power or the laws of the universe to their own ends.

    There is a catch though, there is always a catch. Just as many, too many, Conspiracies had to make moral compromises to acquire their Endowments; humans who gain Endowments powerful enough to play on a cosmic scale are usually Cultists; or at least they are servants of a divinity.

    Gods use mortal pawns all the time. A Cult at any tier could be sponsored by a God but humans creating a Tier one Cell without divine sponsorship is trivial. Even a Tier Three Endowment is far from impossible to regular mortals, hunters or cultists, to acquire or create, but it is so difficult for mortals to obtain a forth tier Endowment that the odds of Hunters doing so is actually lower than the odds of a Goddess bestowing the Endowment upon Her followers as part of a sinister plan.

    Attitudes of Endowments

    Every Compact and Conspiracy has an Endowment, ranging from magic to training to the by-products of their Philosophy. This section attempts to classify the Tiers by how much influence an Endowment will have on the world-view of the hunters who use it.
    This classification scheme produces very different results to the breakdown presented in previous Hunter Books. It also doesn't tell you much about existing organisations. If all you knew was the Tier of an organisation you couldn't tell much about their attitude or their morality. The simple fact is that what magical powers they have genuinely can't tell you too much about a group or an individual hunter.
    This is a mark against the suitability of measuring Tiers by the Attitudes of Endowments; yet the concept isn't completely worthwhile. When designing a new group it can certainly be useful to think about how their Endowment would influence their culture. When the Storyteller is designing a game it can be worthwhile to think about the effects of Endowments on the Hunter's attitudes and maybe select Endowments to support a theme. A game where only the Tier one and three Endowments exist looks a lot different, and it could be a very interesting setting to explore.

    Tier One

    At the first Tier hunters either have no Endowments at all or Endowment's that come purely from the mundane half of their lives. If these Endowments don't reinforce a Hunter's ties to who they were before the Vigil they at least don't do much to change a Hunter's world-view or attitudes. The Union's Your Friends and Neighbours is a solid example of a Tier one Endowment.

    Tier Two

    A lot of hunters will quickly form strong opinions about the supernatural and then stubbornly hold onto them. These opinions range from the stereotype of the hunter who hates everything supernatural to more esoteric occult beliefs. Do Witches hail from an ancient utopia that they destroyed? Who knows, but try telling the Ageis Kai Doru that they didn't. Other Hunters avoid the trap of dogma and take an open minded approach to the supernatural.

    When Tiers represent the Attitude formed by Endowments the second Tier is where Endowments help form these beliefs. These Endowments ask questions which can be answered by dogma or depend on attitudes that a Hunter will have to incorporate into their self image; but the Endowments do not themselves come with answers or specific dogma.

    The Ageis Kai Doru's knowledge of Relics ask questions: Where did these relics come from? If Witches can create new Relics how come we can't? Should I steal that person's Relics? Their story of the Witch War answers these questions in an extremely dogmatic way. Yet one could easily imagine a different Conspiracy approaching Relics with a more flexible outlook.

    The Hunters of Null Mysteriis are Doing Science. This Endowment encourages an analytical and intellectual approach to the vigil. The beliefs of an O.R.A.S hunter are then formed and refined through experimentation and testing. While an open minded and rational approach will affect how the Hunter comes to see the supernatural; it won't do so as much as results of her experiments.

    The Sons of CúChulainn are renowned for their loyalty and trust in their brothers in arms. This attitude pre-dates their Warrior's Code but it was reinforced by their Endowment. When someone swears a magically binding oath, it's usually safe to assume they mean it. Having a trusted support network while you explore a strange new world will influence how you come to see the Supernatural, but it's not enough to define your eventual views.

    Under this classification scheme Tier two contains most of the Endowments in the Hunter line. This is not particularly surprising, the Compacts and Conspiracies were written as individual groups with their own goals and ideologies rather than as a bit of fluff around an Endowment. As they should be. Examining the Endowments reveals what was already known to be a theme in Hunter: Acquiring supernatural powers asks questions of self identity and morality but the answers vary from the outright fanaticism of the Malleus Maleficarum to the conscious decision to not judge lest ye be judged promoted by the Lucifuge.

    Tire Three

    At the third tier the Endowment enforces one specific dogma. It cannot get inside the Hunter's head and change their personality, at least not in any significant sense, but it still manages the mould their views.

    The Athenic Church's Devotion Endowment would be a good example of the third Tier. Devotion only works, as it's name implies, if you are genuinely Devoted to the Goddess Minerva. Minerva's priestesses serve as the Gatekeepers of the Endowment, all the powers are temporary and only the priestesses can renew a Hunter's abilities. With an Endowment like that the vast majority of hunters in the Athenic Church will quickly become devout followers of Minervra's testament or quickly leave the Church. In short, with their Endowment there was no possible way the Athenic Church could have turned out any way other than devout followers of the word of Minerva. This is what makes their Endowment fit into the third tier; though it is worth pointing out that a more flexible God might result in a Tier Two Devotion.

    The Rite of Hecate would also illustrate the third Tier well. While Hecate's theology covers far less ground than Minerva's this Endowment does have an undeniable flaw: It is powered by murder. The Rites do not ask their users "is murder ok", they come with an answer. Either that you agree that it's ok to kill witches and steal their power or you don't use the Rite. The Promethean Brotherhood might have become a bit more discerning in their practices, they could have enforced a few rules about only targeting witches who "deserved" it, but once they decided to adopt The Rite of Hectae they were always going to become a murder cult. It couldn't have gone any other way.

    The third tier Endowments are especially interesting for a game focused on Cults. Hunters with these Endowments, just like cultists with Endowments, have traded their world view for supernatural power. You can talk about the differences, hunters are choosing study powers and coming to logical conclusions while cultists are brainwashing each other but the uncomfortable truth is you're getting to razor thin distinctions. If you focus on the conflict between cultists and tier three hunters, or outright remove tier two Endowments from the game you're creating a world where all power must be paid for in belief. When the distinction between the Hunters and the Cultists narrows down to who got the better deal for their magic you get to explore how fair it is to call the Hunter's "the good guys".

    The same principle works just as well for a game where you climb the tiers. The idea that climbing the tiers introduces moral questions in exchange for power has always been present in hunter but it has typically been expressed through the Conspiracies unethical behaviour. While the Conspiracies dark secrets makes a nice parallel to Cults' own dark secrets, a setting where all Endowments must be paid for by adherence to a strict dogma parallels a differnt aspect of Cults and could be interesting to explore.

    Cult Tier

    If the third tier already requires you to adopt a specific world view before it will function, the forth tier will cut out the middle man and simply force you to adopt it's world-view: Reading The Tome of Forbidden Lore grants you magical powers but it also turns you into a schizophrenic utterly obsessed with summoning strange world-eating alien gods from distant stars.

    While in theory a cult tier Endowment might make you a better person, the themes suggest that these are dark and horrific powers. The innocent fool who reads the book without knowing it's true nature and the kidnapped student forcibly baptised with unholy oil are tragic victims, anyone who knowingly seeks these Endowments out is beneath contempt.

    Distance from Regular Society

    Tier One

    At this tier the hunter is unrecognisable from regular people.

    There is a huge variance between regular people, sometimes this variance is larger between two different groups of mundane mortals than between a mortal and a witch, so simply comparing the hunter to a hypothetical average person isn't a fair or accurate way of measuring. Instead a good measure of thumb is to place the hunter among twelve regular folks of a similar persuasion, a jury of his peers if you will, and ask if the hypothetical average person could spot the odd one out. Could the hypothetical average person tell which of the twelve factory workers fights for The Union? Spot the Werewolf hunter in a group of big game hunters?

    You could even ask if the hypothetical average person could tell the witch from a group of twelve wiccans. That is, twelve serious wiccans who cast spells and perform rituals even though they have no actual magical powers. For genuine magic isn't incompatible with the first tier, it is merely difficult to remain at this level with anything more than the barest hint of genuine occult ability.

    Depending on her training a witch might look like a wiccan, an academic, a priestess or any of a hundred things but to a trained eye genuine occult lore will make her stand out. Magic might require certain behaviours, and genuine knowledge includes knowledge of dangers which in turn influence behaviours. The followers of active interventionist deities might include "Christmas and Easter cultists" but proof of a God encourages you to either turn up every Saturday or run for the hills. Things like Werewolves, Vampires or the Fae. Well they might want to be normal humans but with their alien psychology it's vanishingly rare for them to fake it to perfection.

    So most of the people are this Tier mostly are the regular people they look like. The Hunter's aren't even part time, they are regular people who just keep an eye out and take up the Vigil if a monster walks into their part of the world. Afterwords they put the candle back in the cupboard, until next time. The Witches, slightly unstable scientists, Psychics and other people who have access to occult power don't have enough of it to stand out and choose to keep it quiet.

    Tier Two

    At this tier the hunter is the odd man out but still fits comfortably into regular society. Consider a Hunter with a Tell, certainly it won't go unnoticed that Joe is scared of getting any fluids on his skin wound but people are unlikely to conclude that it's because Joe hunts Vampires as part of the Union and has picked up a phobia about mystical blood contamination.

    You could apply a variant on the jury of his pears idea to a Tier two Hunter. Take eleven of Joe's co-workers from the same factory but choose people who have a mild mental condition completely unrelated to the supernatural. Would the other workers conclude that Joe's Tell is somehow different, somehow other, when compared to his peers? To qualify as Tier Two the answer should be no.
    If the Tier 2 Hunter or Witch has an Endowment or some other occult power they have enough of it their personality will be affected in noticeable ways but not enough that their personality will be noticeably different to their peers. The Witch who can only make themselves lucky can have their good fortune noticed by their social circle and will probably develop a relaxed confidence in life, but people most will assume the luck is just luck and the casual confidence is easily explained by that luck.

    A person who finds and spies on people with a magic mirror might start acting quite oddly around some people and their change of behaviour will be noticed, but why would anyone assume that she's found a way to spy with magic? Someone with no magic but a genuine book of occult lore might start avoiding certain unlucky behaviours, most people have their superstitions this guy just takes it a little further.
    A Tier two Hunter, Cultist or Witch has acquired enough changes to their personality from their exposure to the supernatural that others can notice the difference, but these changes are not strange or drastic enough to make the hunter the outcast; merely the oddball.

    Tire Three

    At the third Tier the Hunter or Cultist is recognisably apart from regular society, but not so far apart that they cannot live among regular folk. Imagine a soldier fresh back on home soil and trying to readjust to civilian life, for a third Tier Hunter, life is always like that. At least until they quit the vigil, then they might begin to reintegrate properly.

    Maybe she happily tells people that she worships the Dread God Kroll and keeps an alter made of bones in her basement. The police haven't found any sacrifices other than store brought meet, but they look around every couple of weeks.

    Maybe the Hunter's home has horse shoes, dream catchers, totem spirits, household gods, lines of salt, and every other protective charm he could find in the local library. He tries to keep his nighttime activites secret, but everyone knows him as the occult weirdo.

    Another hunter works full time for the Conspiracy, 9am to 6pm, Monday to Friday, talking about supernatural with fellow Hunters. When he comes home at night he finds he doesn't have much in common with his old school friends, they notice that he turns evasive any time someone tries to ask about his job, and occasionally a reference to vampires or magic slips out.

    Cult Tier

    At this point the Hunter or Cultist has gone so far that they can no longer function in normal society. If they're not isolated among their own kind, things usually go badly.

    A cultist might turn every single conversation into one about his God, and if that wasn't bad enough when he does so people's ears start to bleed! Either the cult's going to find themselves in unmarked graves very soon, or everyone else is in big trouble.

    A Hunter is reasonable enough, but only after she's checked your reflection in a mirror, examined your teeth and had you touch a blessed cross. If you refuse she pulls a gun and asks for a second and final time. Her friends and family are scared to go near her, and either the police or the doctors are going to take her into custody soon.

    A Witch constantly speaks to people in rhyming riddles. She's not even trying to be obtuse, she's just thinks that the process of figuring out a riddle is the fastest way to understanding her point and she's forgotten that most people don't have a lifetime of experience at solving puzzles. Because of this she has few friends that stick up for her when something bizarre goes wrong around her - the price of her wild and chaotic magic.
