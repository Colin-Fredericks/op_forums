        @Leliel So thinking a bit more about the evil psychic buddhists, this is what I came up with. It should definitely be checked for authenticity.


        Their philosophy is an extreme form of Buddhist asceticism. You know how Buddha found his middle path, well Jesus said "judge not" (looking at you Westbro Baptist Church). They take asceticism to such an extreme that they let their bodies die - and they think of themselves as teachers with the goal of makeing everyone do the same for their own good.

        They use monk-like training to reach altered states of consciousness where the god-minds can communicate with them; the god-minds teach them psychic powers which lets them detach from their bodies and become beings of pure psychic energy. While in this state they must feed on other beings psychic energy - basically they need 1 Willpower point a day, and since they have no more Virtue or Vice (Nilhisim!) they eat Willpower points. At first they just feed of their own uninhabited body, but it still ages and eventually dies. At which point they're thematically similar to Preta.

        So you essentially have disembodied psychic Buddhist monks controlling their old bodies like a Puppet while feeding of it. And just to top it off their bodies basically shut down without a mind or soul inside - only rudimentry brain activity is left, but that's enough to feed off - so they have to preserve them; but they still end up puppeting bodies that look like Sokushinbutsu for most of their life.


        As for the god-minds themselves; who knows or cares what they actually are. The cult thinks of them as Bodhavistas - they could have attained enlightenment but they chose to remain and teach others. All we know is that they can train you to be psychic; and they're promoting this nhilistic idea that everyone should be freed from worldly desires by abandoning their bodies.

                                Ok, about my cult;

                                I was thinking they have a great deal of sympathy for Pretas, hungry ghosts. More than that, I think the idea is that the World of Darkness actually isn't the human world, but the world of the pretas, might be a good plan. Everything's so miserable, everything's so defined by wanting the intangible, that certain people who admit the supernatural exists might go on to say that since the world is obviously not the mundane and relatively stable human world, it must be something else. The cult are people who have begun to remember they are actually pretas and not human, as evinced by "remembering" their supernatural natures and abilities.

                                Of course, this also leads into their nihilism and Gnostic aspects; since reincarnation as a preta means you weren't a very good person in your past life or finished a term in Naraka. Either way, any suffering you undergo is simply your abysmal Karma enforcing itself, including what the cult brings.

                                In place of the slarecians, they serve (what they think of as) Vajrapani, the bodhisattva symbolizing raw power, and his two incarnations, the Nio. As pretas, they do not deserve the mercy of Buddha, but they will follow the "tough love" approach of the Nio, as they both purify themselves through maddening and painful meditation, and the world through their schemes to create chaos and enforce Karma, possibly shaking the rest of "humanity" out of the delusion they are actually humans.

                                Good?

                                        I was actually playing Digital Devil Saga, and had a fit of inspiration.

                                        Basically, the first game, as gradually shown, is actually a training simulator for Combat AI programs...who are actually reincarnations (in the Hindu sense) of people who were killed during the real world's apocalypse (how the apocalypse happened, according to the resistance there, is something I take umbridge with, but it's implied they're wrong in universe). Thing is, you see from the perspective of the AIs as they (re)gain the ability to feel emotions and gradually realize how strange their world is, and that they remember things they didn't do (as AIs). Up until the sequel, they don't quite grok they aren't normal humans, even as they gradually realize how incomplete their world is (for example, they only realize that "children" are a thing that exists about halfway through).

                                        That same sense of there being something very very wrong with the universe as how bizarre the whole thing is impresses itself on everyone was what tickled my brain. Yes, the cult is probably wrong, but the idea that the supernatural being a sign of something very important being very non-present stuck with me. Not to mention that what happened to begin the reclamation of memory and awakening to the artificial nature of the world seems pertinent to the nWoD in general; everyone in the simulation was infected by a virus that turned them all into shapeshifters.

                                        And of course, this also serves as a convenient explanation for all the human-derived supernaturals ("Ah, well they were touched by something that reminds them of a specific way they are not actually human, and so have remembered a way to use their power to affect this illusion we call the real world, and themselves...but are simultaneously bound tighter by it, because they don't realize that their delusions just have more elements of truth").

                                                            Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                                            Here's what I have so far. Now to find someone who can say if this feels Buddhist.
                                                            From my own dime-store understanding, I think these guys could use a little more ideology, invoking a bit of Hinduism into the thing.

                                                            Basically, one of the things often forgotten about the Kali Yuga (the Last Age of the World before Vishnu goes to bed, the one we're in right now) is that it's a great time to seek enlightenment; the world's suffering has become so great, so inescapable, that letting go of it becomes relatively easy. It's not as painful to let go of things that hurt you, after all, and doing good is something that in these dark times where it is a truly thankless task means it is done purely for the sake of having done good, uncorrupted by expectation of reward.

                                                            I expect the reason they manipulate things into becoming increasingly shitty all around is a version of that; if suffering brings enlightenment and growth, than ensuring the world suffers is actually a sublime act of charity. That, and a lot of them don't feel like doing the world any favors, and it helps them gain power and influence. A desire fulfilled is a desire that no longer troubles them, after all, and they can also help mold the world into an environment favorable to their faith.

                                                            (Naturally that last part is going to start raising alarm bells with mainstream Buddhists).

                                                            There's been some scares about fundamentalist Buddhists, recently, too; might be worth looking into the 969 Movement, the Ikko-shu and similar things for inspiration. Naturally the Empty Monastery feels much less bound to the idea of nationalism, but I can imagine them helping other religions destroy themselves and running secular power into the ground. To them, a state is simply another attachment in need of being severed or taught its own mortality. Especially if they feel rather bitter about something the state/corporation in recent years did; I can see them arising in the wake of the Rape of Nanking or the conquest of Tibet. And ironically, they don't let go of these things (if the former, I can easily see a plot hook where it's hinted they provoked the Lost Generation of Japan as long-overdue, pointless retribution).
