    Conspiracy Play Advice

    06-11-2016, 12:53 AM
    So I'm working on a game where the players are agents of Aegis Kai Doru, and dealing with that (orders from above and suchforth) will make up maybe a third of the game.

    Does anyone have any recommendations for how conspiracy Hunter games tend to be best handled? I've only played in cell-level games before. Well, except one, but that was really dumb.

      A lot should depend on their status. But to separate things from the cell structure, you will want to up the cloak and dagger stuff. As a cell-level hunter you have the police to worry about, maybe some enemies you've made. A conspiracy like the Aegis Kai Doru has been breaking laws, making enemies and allies for centuries if not thousands of years, depending on how much of the setting you decide to take as fact in your game. They would have plans and scripts in place for getting people to certain places, safe-houses and contacts already in place. Higher status members would be able to take advantage of it more, while lower status members would be more 'along for the ride,' basically following superiors. Still, even with all these contacts it's not as if others have the Hunter the Vigil book, a certain black market dealer may know of a group willing to pay big money for certain ancient artifacts, an old vampire might recognize the symbol as belonging to tomb raiders and thieves, the secret police know that with particularly mysterious cases they can reach out to the old man at the museum who seems to wrap things up. You could take a lot from spy movies or novels for things like that.

      You can even play with some politicking there. If you send the players to a vault and they get help from a certain NPC they like, maybe he can try and get them to bring him along and get him out of the security guard business. I always like rival NPCs, more jerks than enemies, who could throw tiny wrenches in the works and try to claim credit for anything that goes well and denounce the PCs for anything that goes wrong. But you could go all the way if you wanted, maybe they're ghouls or about to go rogue, rob a vault and try and disappear, leaving the blame pointed at the player characters. That's a more specific kind of game, though. You could have rumors of other artifacts and legends that might be big finds but aren't really what the hunters were sent there for. Let them balance the risks and potential rewards.

      With a conspiracy you can make the stakes larger, or at least have more reason to. Especially with the Aegis Kai Doru. You can go the more down-to-earth route like a cell-based game, but it's a great time to whip out the big legends. I think Armory Reloaded has a few interesting relics, like the rumal that might end the world or turn its user into some sort of mad prophet of death.

      Just a personal thing but I like the idea of conspiracies versus other conspiracies. An antagonist wouldn't 'just' be a vampire, but a dynasty of vampires with a vendetta against the players' conspiracy, maybe formed by an ex-member trying to retrieve a specific artifact, or who captures and ghouls members of the conspiracy in order to infiltrate it. Individual antagonists can be good for tearing down the masquerade. If they survive they could dog the hunters, peeling away layers of the conspiracy's secret cover.

          What I was thinking was that while being a member of the conspiracy is beneficial as they receive a (meagre) paycheck and some support, which is absolutely to their benefit, they're also subject to orders from above with little consideration of the difficulty or explanation, e.g. "Capture a shapechanger alive and deliver them to X address by tomorrow night". So I was going to try to balance the positives and negatives as closely as possible, erring on the side of the positives. This'd probably be mostly embodied by their handler-type figure, who'd be responsible for co-ordinating cells over the wide region.

          There'd also be the involvement of the PCs day jobs - say one's a GP, he'll be used as the go-to medical attention guy for anyone remotely close. Or if they work in a bank, they'd be expected to watch certain accounts or for patterns. Or whatever. Apart from fleshing out and involving the characters backstories and situations, I was thinking this would help build up the influence of the conspiracy - they have contacts everywhere. Maybe not all hunters, in fact most of them not, but it'd help establish that more clearly.

          AKD would end up playing the role of grudgingly-put-up-with ally - too valuable to discard (and really problematic if they tried), but too much hassle to really like.

          One thing I've always struggled with in H:tV games - how do you motivate the players to be really motivated to destroy the supernatural the way they're described in the book? I mean, I have no problem with breaks from the stereotype, but I've always found the best games are when the ST makes the player feel the same way as the character, albeit usually through different means. Things that annoy a PC can be RP-ed as such, but things that annoy a player as well as the PC (in a good way) makes for huge amounts of fun. Suggestions?
