    Originally posted by nofather View Post
    It really seems like the books have managed to cover most of the bases. Everything I think of is some variant of 'like that existing group, but with this instead of that.' But maybe I'm too close, being a fan of the game line.

    So, in the vein of Ashwood Abbey, but with money instead of thrills, how about 'The House.' Essentially an underground gambling syndicate that has operatives that act to poke the bears, so to speak, provoking monsters into act, and betting on how they'll react. Obviously among the many problems with this is the big one that implies that they already know about supernaturals and how they act, which already makes them more informed than most hunter compacts. But, say, a bunch of wealthy folks (and some not-so-wealthy but connected to the underground necessary to make the operation worked) started having some death sports. Bum fights to the death. And one day things got weird when Something Happened. Maybe a demon went loud or a werewolf death raged or a promethean let loose. Either way, a group of insiders formed, realizing they could get wealthy folks into it. Sure, some folks could be fulfilled by a contract with the Bear Lodge or the Ashwood Abbey, but who wants to get their hands dirty when they could watch others do so from the comfort of their home and an internet connection?

    While there'd be the folks who do the behind the scenes work (getting investors involved, scouting out staging grounds, maintaining the servers) the hard but well-rewarded work would be done by the folks who scout out locations and 'political' situations so they could send in the grunts, who do the real hard work, doing things like luring pure into forsaken territories, angels to demons, pointing hunters towards vampire havens and, rarely, just out and out kidnapping to drop things into a makeshift arena.
    The major problem with this one is the amount of knowledge required on the part of the organization itself is... pretty high. They'd need to know where to find Werewolves, which I doubt is a simple affair, and they also have to set up cameras or something so that the following fight can be watched. Except, depending on where it is, unless they somehow drag the monsters back to a pit of some kind, they'll find the angle will probably suck. And that would matter for the patrons I'd imagine.
    Vampires, by the way, would spoil things because they just show up as fuzzy images, which would probably make the recording a lot less, "fun," to say the least.

    Sorry. They just come as the Ashwood Abbey, if they were a lot lazier yet paradoxically more knowledgable. Hope this helps you come up something else though.

                    I have two rough-sketches of ideas:

                    The first is the Friends of the Deceased, a Compact whose members believe in the sanctity of the afterlife. They know ghosts exist, and, unlike many denizens of the World of Darkness, believe them to be actual continuations of the people they were in life. They take desecration of the dead very seriously, and depending on where you are, any given cell might range from a particularly quirky local historical society, to a spectral ALF. Ghosts, whatever the truth may be, like them, since the Friends keep them in supply of Essence and lucidity. As far as Hunter activities go, they're primarily a social threat, taking advantage of the emotional ties people have to the dead. Wealthy members might arrange funerals for those whose families can't afford them, while others might show up graveside with a hip flask and a shoulder to cry on. Their primary targets are anyone who abuses the dead--necromancers, ectophages, bokor, etc.

                    The second is a Conspiracy I was thinking of today, though they're a bit muddled at the moment. It started with the idea of an Endowment in the form of a "pet" Pandoran--a horrific twist on the "bonded companion" trope. The Conspiracy has some sort of connection to the Divine Fire; maybe alchemists of some sort, maybe religious scientists, maybe wannabe Demiurges (one might consider all three of these to be the same thing, of course). They provide members with a small object filled with Vitriol, which is alchemically-attuned to a specific Pandoran. The hunter can use the device to awaken the monster, though naturally it's difficult to control and will viciously attack any source of Pyros it comes across.

                                    Off the top of my head (and because I've been reading some Mythos related material), I've got a few ideas for Compacts:

                                    1) The Innsmouth Collective: An off shoot of the Marsh Family of Old Innsmouth who learned of curse they bear and are now trying to find ways to stop it. They hunt in order to interrogate and dissect monsters to better understand their own condition. Unlike their country kin, they make it a point to be sophisticated and live as far as they can from the sea and it's Call. Most are very well learned as well.

                                    2) The Dunwich Militia: After their town was ravaged by a Horror, the men of Dunwich have taken it upon themselves to never be unprepared for creatures like the Whateley Brothers again! The Militia began it's training under Dr. Armitage and, while the Good Doctor had to return to his school, the men kept on with their studies. Using books Armitage left behind as well as correspondence with the Miskatonic Teaching Staff, the Militia has kept both their town and the surrounding area safe from unknowable creatures from beyond time and space. They also spit a mean hog.

                                    3) The Miskatonic Teaching Staff: Arkham's famed center for higher learning has always attracted a specfic element to it's hallowed halls. It is only through the continued vigilance of it's world renown Teaching Staff that the world has not fallen into utter ruin! Seriously, all it takes is the wrong freshman making off with a book from the restricted section for the Dead to Rise and the Sun to be blotted out from the Sky. The leader of the Staff, the Dean (not the head of the school however), is very good at sending the right professor for the right job. I thought about making them a Conspiracy that had access to Dark Magics that cost them their Sanity. The "Retired Staff" are those teachers that now reside in Arkham Sanitarium. They may not have "Tenure" anymore, but they still have knowledge to share!

                                    Anyway, just some thoughts. Not really fleshed out yet.

                                                I wanted to design something more oriental for Hunter: The Vigil, but all I come up with is stuff about ninjas. Actually, I got really inspired from reading about a video game conspiracy (Secret World) simply called "Dragon" that focuses around the "Art of Chaos." Tell me what you think.

                                                The Dragon Lotus
                                                Masters of the Nameless Path

                                                "Change requires chaos, and chaos is ours to orchestrate."

                                                A controlled society is a sick society, especially one secretly ruled by the supernatural. The Order of the Dragon Lotus wishes to keep all things in harmony by allowing the natural flow of Chaos to balance the stagnation of corrupted Order. The teachings of the Dragon Lotus were first taught by a man simply called the Green Monk, who uncovered a rare flower atop the peaks of a mountain. Where this mountain was changes with each telling, but has always been believed to be within Asia. It is said that, for days and nights, the Green Monk contemplated the flower, and how its petals moved with the currents of the wind. From this did he scribe the sacred teachings of the Dragon Lotus, which taught of strange magics that would bring about the greatest change onto a world stagnant and dying. However, creatures from the shadows detested the Green Monk's work, seeing their bases of power crumbling into oblivion as the mortal gained followers both noble and commoner. Quickly, they attempted to take his life and end his meddling, but the stone had been cast, and ripples would grow.

                                                For centuries, the conspiracy has existed, rising and falling as their needs are met. Their strategies are incomprehensible to any and all outsiders looking for a pattern. To the dragons, however, there is no pattern, only a chain of events set off by a single action. Every cell acts separate from the other, yet all are united in their cause to save humanity from within and out. Whether by sponsoring a secret dojo, blowing up a vampire's haven, or simply warning a witch of another hunter, all is ethical so long as it brings chaos to heal a paralyzed world.

                                                Factions

                                                The Dragon Disciples are made up of those most intent on studying the teachings of the Green Monk. Cautiously, they observe chaos from a distance and seek to learn from what later transpires from it. This makes them appear less involved with the world, but their knowledge has granted them a rather uncanny foresight in the actions of both human and supernatural creature. Enough, at least, to stay one step ahead of them.

                                                The Emerald Flame is the "teeth of the dragon". Though not comprised of just anarchists and vigilantes, this faction encourages active attempts to harass the supernatural, especially ones with great control over the status-quo. Like fire, they burn away the disease that plagues humanity, and yet, like the emerald, they do so to heal and bring natural harmony to all.

                                                The Unified believe that the order should keep better connection with its various cells. Too many times have fellow dragons crossed each other by accident and brought about their deaths due to lack of coordination. However, critics of the faction see this a violation of the Green Monk's teachings. They argue that even those who master chaos must still accept that they are not immune to it, lest they abuse their power and become like the monsters they fight.

                                                New Endowment: Dragon Sutra
                                                Through the lessons taught by the Green Monk, his disciples learned to guide the ebb and flow of chaos to aid them in their cause. These teachings, called dragon sutras, are taught to agents of the order to utilize in the eternal shadow war.

                                                Changing Face
                                                Similar to Hensōjutsu, the art of disguise and impersonation, the sutra of Changing Face teaches that even the truth is subjective to chaos when the mind is unprepared. Channeling the force of chaos into her face, a dragon agent can alter its texture and shape for a number of hours equal to successes rolled.

                                                Seed of Discord
                                                An advanced sutra based on subtlety and deception, the agent plants a suggestion in the mind of his target through a simple phrase or gesture. This suggestion is one of rebellion that, as time goes by, becomes harder and harder to ignore. Once acted upon, however, the target is released of the urge completely (unless it was already there to begin with).
                                                Cost: 1 Willpower Point
                                                Action: Instant; contested.
                                                Dice Pool: Manipulation + Dragon Sutra vs Composure.
                                                Roll Results
                                                Dramatic Failure: As per failure, save the target cannot be affected by the sutra for another twenty-four hours.
                                                Failure: Nothing happens.
                                                Success: The target becomes compelled to act against what they perceive as oppressive. The target is allowed to resist the compulsion with a Resolve + Composure check each hour, but gain a -1 to that check per hour they resist until either knocked unconscious or the effect is dispelled supernaturally. The caster has no control over the sutra once it has been cast, nor can he dispel the effects whenever he pleases.
                                                Exceptional Success: As per success, save the agent can dictate what the target rebels against.

                                                Yanwei Fengbao
                                                The Yanwei, or "swallowtail," is a butterfly native to the provinces of China. Based on the concept of the Butterfly Effect (or perhaps the other way around), the dragon agent can channel the forces of chaos into a single action, which will cause a domino effect to take place. Depending on the number of successes rolled, this will either bring about good or ill fortune in the near future.

                                                            I LOVE the Wu-Keng idea! Federal Faiths Mutual is also amusing to me, but would likely work better as NPCs than as PCs (unless maybe the PCs were insurance fraud investigators, and had to prove that the damages were insurable).

                                                            I have two ideas that have been drifting through my head lately:

                                                            The first don't have a name- not even a secret name or a working name. Maybe TF:V call them Unsponsored Agents (Type 4). Maybe the Lucifuge call them The Evangelicized. Let's go with that. Whatever they are called, they are individuals who developed an ability similar to the Imbued from H:tR, only the messages are even more vague and show up inside their heads instead of in the world around them. When they're near certain species of monsters (which vary from a single WoD gameline species to all species including other Evangelicized), or the creations of those species (areas of Paradox, Wyrd locations and items), they get a very, very strong feeling, almost like a conscience whispering to them, that this is a wrongness and they should get to the root of it. The monsters themselves cause the conscience to SCREAM OUT "It's wrong! It's evil! Self-defend it to death!" The vast majority of these Hunters get put away in psychiatric prisons for a VERY long time. A lucky (or perhaps unlucky) few manage to survive multiple encounters on their own or join a Tier 1 Conspiracy. The rarest pinnacle are brought into (or captured by) a Tier 2 or 3 Conspiracy. The Evangelicized don't get outward Imbuements. Instead, each Imbuement gives them Hunches related to the hunt. For instance, a player could spend points on I Know Your Weak Point: One point gives the hunter a slight knack for telling which traditional weaknesses a monster has through their research (Fae are weak against Cold iron, kidnapped children probably aren't), three points gives that for more specific monster lines that they are able to research (Nosferatu vs. Daeva vs. Gangrel) and a vague idea of what individual monsters' weaknesses are from looking at them, and five points basically outright tells the Hunter one or two major weaknesses that it has and one traditional weakness that it DOESN'T have. The player could also take You Can Run..., which allows the character to, say, spend a point of Willpower a certain number of times per scene to get a hint of where to go next (basically a merit that gives the character out-of-character knowledge). It's up to the DM whether these are psychic abilities, or a quashmallim, or anything else they can think of. DM is also encouraged to forbid certain merits like You Can Run... that give the character impossible knowledge, be it for game balance or to reinforce the possibility that the characters are just criminally insane and happen to have good gut instincts when it comes to hunting down monsters (a la that breed of Hannibal Lecter-esque Slasher).

                                                            The other are the Sect of Returning Light (I'm sure I can come up with a better name, but I kind of like the SRL-Serial puns that can be made). They're Lucifuge whose ancestors split off from the group, or who weren't born into the Lucifuge, and were picked up by a cult who believe that they're the key to allowing demons to return to grace. Different from the Reconciliation in that they are working to return ALL their ancestral Fallen to grace, and not just Lucifer, and returning them to God's arms is a goal in and of itself (not a step in the process of destroying Hell and preventing anyone from ever being damned). The Fallen-blooded of their many splinter factions far outnumber the official 666 Lucifuge, and possibly the entire Lucifuge organization, but they rarely work together and not even the most populous branches combined have anywhere near 600 Fallen-blooded members. As could be expected, they don't have the genealogical information (or genealogical propaganda) that the Lucifuge use. They can summon and contain creatures of the Inferno, but unless they have the sect-specific Infernal Virus ritual that turns the creatures into mindless voodoo zombies that do nothing BUT work-to-rule, the SRLs can't control them. The truth behind the matter is that their ancestors, as Unchained, don't WANT to be allowed back into the God-Machine's grace, and the climax of a SRL character's story involves either A: unwittingly sending their ancestor back into slavery or B: discovering that they must give up either the spirit of their lifelong goal ("grant my ancestor peace") or the letter of their lifelong goal ("return my ancestor to their place with God").

                                                            Edit: P.S.: I always liked the idea of Hunters who believe the monsters are different type of some other thing.
                                                            Fairy-Hunters: Changelings? Changelings! Demons? Fae with fire and speech powers! Prometheans? Fetches and reanimated bodies! Werewolves? The True Fae equivalent of Beast changelings!
                                                            Fox Mulders: Changelings? Abductees! The true fae? Aliens from another dimension! Demons? Aliens from a fiery planet on whose technology all Terran computers are based! Mummies? Goa'uld technology!
