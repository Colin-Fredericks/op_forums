    Open Dev Three: Slash! Slash! Slashers!

    01-12-2016, 06:54 PM
    It's up!

    http://theonyxpath.com/hunter-2e-ope...lash-slashers/


    My thoughts:

    Perhaps it will be an unpopular opinion, but I'm somewhat wary of creating a prominent connection between Slashers and fallen Hunters. A story of a Hunter gone bad can be very powerful and I think putting Slashers at the forefront can distract from all other ways it could happen.

    The Vigil is a very stressful undertaking and I can easily see Hunters become extremists, use their resources for selfish purposes, create criminal enterprises, lash out at their close ones, grow resentful of people who do not protect themselves, be broken by the hard decisions they've taken, sell out to Monsters or Conspiracies. I'm afraid that overly focusing on the Slashers can give an impression that this kind of failure is all there is. It can be easily avoided by careful writing.

    Also, it would bear explaining why a Hunter suddenly shifts their quarry towards ordinary Humans. There is a difference between growing callous and outright taking up serial murder. Of course, a Slasher could choose targets valid for the Vigil, but it doesn't carry the same visceral feelings as a human monster stalking the innocent. Additionally, I am curious of the difference between Slashers and ordinary serial killers.

                      Reposted from the comments on the Blog:

                      1.) A Slasher is a monster, often (but not always) a serial killer, who used to be human. Michael Meyers, Jason Voorhees, Freddie Kruger, Pinhead, The Tall Man, Chucky... They were all human at one point, but at the time the films that star them take place, they're all distinctly supernatural in one way or another. On the other hand, Norman Bates, Leatherface, Hannibal Lecter, the various killers behind the Ghostface, Jigsaw? They're "just" human serial killers, monstrous but not actual monsters. By the same token, the Leprechaun from Leprechaun, the Djinn from Wishmaster, the monster from Jeepers Creepers, they're all "just" monsters with a pattern to the way they kill people. To me, a slasher needs both to have been human, and then to have crossed the line that separates human from monsters.

                      2.) Absolutely. To me, Hunter the Vigil is all about characters who straddle the line between human and monster - both figuratively in the "monstrous behavior" sense, and literally, in the sense that some Hunters use actual supernatural powers. To me, Slashers are the natural outcome of Hunters who go too far, and the personal horror of Hunter comes in questioning just where that line actually is.

                      3.) Eh, Cabals of Slashers don't really interest me that much.

                      4.) I agree with some of the other comments saying that the distinction between Hunter and Slasher should be subtle enough that the character is constantly asking themselves if they might be a Slasher. On the other hand, you should be able to easily recognize a Slasher when you see one. There's this incredibly powerful moment in Bloodborne when you first encounter Father Gascoigne. He's brutally hacking away at an obviously dead Beast, but as you approach he turns his attention to you and mumbles "Beasts all over the shop... You'll be one of them... Sooner or later..." In that one 30 second or so cutscene, I instantly understood that every Hunter was just a hair's breadth away from being one of the Beasts they hunt, and that Father Gascoigne had crossed that line. He was killing indiscriminately, and using "they'll all be Beasts eventually" as an excuse, and I had to kill him before he did the same to me. I also wondered, "how long until I end up like that?" and a little part of me wondered if I already was like that. After all, I was trying to kill him with the same justification he was using to try to kill me. If the 2e rules for Hunters becoming Slashers can allow for moments like that, I will consider it a tremendous success.

                          To me, the question of how a hunter becomes a slasher is linked to the question of what 2e should do with the Code. Here's how I'd do it:

                          2e should have a version of the Code that inoculates the hunter from Breaking Points at the cost of picking up a Tell (modeled by a Persistent Condition), with guidelines as to how this differs from a more natural evolution of the hunter's Breaking Points. To become a Slasher (more specifically, a Ripper), two things must happen: the hunter must choose to use the Code to negate killing as a Breaking Point; and the Hunter must adopt a murder-based Aspiration. Once these two conditions are met, the hunter becomes a Ripper and is eligible to pick up an Undertaking. You don't need an Undertaking to be a Ripper, and a Ripper who doesn't have an Undertaking differs from a hunter only psychologically.

                          You do need an Undertaking to become a Scourge, though; to become a scourge, the slasher must transition from a Ripper Undertaking to the associated Scourge Undertaking. I'm not sure yet what the requirements for that would be; possibly just an appropriate expenditure of Practical Experience. Once you become a scourge, you enter Monster territory: you gain a dot of Potency and can develop Dread Powers.

                                              But slashers *focus* on humans. It doesn’t make a lot of sense for a hunter who goes off the rails to switch focus to humans over monsters – and having slashers see their victims as adjuncts to monsters narrows their range too much.

                                              So if slashers are to be a dark mirror to hunters, I think it should be the parallel of their actions – that they stalk and kill, and are hunters better just because they focus on a more socially acceptable target (hell, many slashers focus on targets that society tacitly accepts even if it pretends otherwise) – that’s at the root of it.

                                                      1) Slashers are inhuman humans, monsters for what they do rather than what they are. They can't relate to humanity at large anymore, don't understand right from wrong, are compelled to kill, divorced from reality, or lacking all empathy. Eventually they aren't human anymore - they're dead souled monsters drawing power from the evil that they do.

                                                      2) Hunters becoming Slashers underlines the theme of human frailty and the question of what it means to be human. From a play point of view I think it gives people a chance to ape stories of people who have been pushed past the point of burnout or becoming what they fought against. It provides a terrible psychological consequence to character's choosing expedience or violence to solve moral dilemmas.

                                                      3) Cabals of Slashers would be a nice way to model a 'mirror' foe without Hunters having to kill other Hunters. For a Storyteller it would give a means to make Slashers more than just a singular monster of the week antagonist. I would probably use them as either a direct analog to the Hunter's Cell - there but for the grace of God sort of thing - or a well connected network that lets its members get away with all manner of horror, like say the Court of Owls from Batman or 'the family' from True Detective. Although, now I'm picturing a Hills Have Eyes cannibal fest and that seems pretty cool too.

                                                      4) Clear rules to support transformation from a Hunter to Slasher would give players and Storytellers the guidelines to not have to hand wave when a character makes the leap from one to the other. I think maybe tying it to the Integrity system in a way that lets you alter your character's answers to the Integrity questions might be a way to make it work. This is the approach I've taken in my God Machine Chronicle rules Hunter the Vigil game.

                                                          Question 1: Definition of Slasher

                                                          First of all I'd define a Slasher as an antagonist designed to mechanically replicate the tropes associated with the vilians of the Slasher Movie genera. Not any serial killer qualifies, not even every slightly supernatural serial killer qualifies. An Apostle of the Dark One (Second Sight Thamaturge) who regularly commits human sacrifice is unlikely to be a Slasher.

                                                          To give a more solid definition, I'd say the following four traits are key

                                                          1. They're a serial killer who primarally targets regular mortals.

                                                          2. They fall on a power level ranging from non-supernatural but inhumanly skilled (think Jigsaw) to low powered supernatural: Freddy Kruger is undeniably using supernatural abilities but he's only got a few tricks and I would say that a Mage or Changeling could easily outdo him at dream manipulation.

                                                          3. They have an exceptionally strong sense of theme that they're psychologically or supernaturally compelled to follow. Iconic outfits, a persistent modus operandi, favoured weapons. Etc

                                                          4. A Slasher's psychological issues form the core of their charachter and are the source of their borderline-supernatural or outright-supernatural abilities.

                                                          Question 2: Am I interested in the possibility of my hunter becoming a Slasher

                                                          Not in particular. My Hunter charachters have never focused that strongly on killing monsters, and while I am interested in the potential for them to fall it never felt right that they'd become Slashers if they hit the bottom. For example I once played a Task Force: VALKARIE signal intelligence officer. I could see him becoming possessed by some strange entity that lives in the wires, or becoming a Mage after he gets an Obsession with some strange signal he detects. But a Slasher just doesn’t fit.


                                                          To me Slashers were always a Dark Mirror to Hunters. They have similar obessive personalities. Like Hunters their primary goals involve killing (Excluding Null Mysteriis, Network Zero, etc).

                                                          What made them interesting was not the risk of becoming a Slasher, but how Hunters justified why they were different from Slashers. The Hunter says "I kill vampires", the Slasher says "I kill sinners". Is the Hunter morally better? The answer is yes, but explaining why is a rich and interesting source of character.


                                                          Question 3: Could you see yourself using a slasher cabal in your game.

                                                          Not at all. To me every single interesting thing about Slashers traces back to the same root: The Slasher's psychology. The moment you stop thinking of the Slasher and start thinking of the Cabal you distance the story from the Slasher's psychology.

                                                          To illustrate this, The Hunt Club doesn't have a single mention of Undertakings in it's write up, and their focus on pleasing judges who's tastes change makes it nearly impossible to have the hyper focused Modeus Operandi that Slashers are known for.

                                                          I like the Hunt Club, but I wouldn't actually call them Slashers. A question to the audience, which 1E Undertakings do you feel would work for members of the Hunt Club, and why?
