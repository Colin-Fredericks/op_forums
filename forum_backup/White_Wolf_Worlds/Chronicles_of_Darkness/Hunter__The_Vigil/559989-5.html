                I lean against that because I find the idea that VALKYRIE considers many other Hunters (to say nothing of Heroes) to be dangerous vigilantes and terrorists that do fall under their purview to try and keep in-line, quiet, etc - to open more plot doors than it closes.

                Of course my take on VALKYRIE is that they knowingly accept the existence of many powerful supernatural conspiracies and if anything use their work to prop those dominant groups up in the name of stability, like the U.S. Government and certain Middle Eastern (and historically Latin American) dictatorships. And thus are prone to come down hard on Hunters who do things like go after certain entrenched supernatural powers that enforce the Masquerade and the like.

                That's what I swapped out their leadership working FOR vampires for. It's not that they're puppets. It's that they make hard compromises like that all the time, and accept, and even protect, a certain amount of supernatural nefariousness to stop all out wars in America's streets.

Alternatively cutting some under the table deals to spread fear of diseases - ranging from "no one is buying our expensive medicine because bed rest is better value for money" to "people are dying because of anti-vax nonsense, make them scared of mumps instead". With a few implants Cheiron could qualify as Kin after all.

                                        Or for opposing Beasts. "My contract say if I meet quota I get to put company resources on a personal issue. People are so scared of their children being autistic (Anakim rather than Namtaru I think) that they're avoiding life saving vaccines. So I'm taking some heavily armed company resources to kill the fear of your child being autistic".


                                        This might also be an opportunity to add another possible origin* for Cheiron. Centaurs were monsters, but mythological Cheiron was basically the one good Centaur. Perhaps Cheiron was a Beast who turned his back on monstrousness by never spending Satity and thus never needing to feed. Or perhaps he turned doctors into kin using Thaumatechnology, taught them medicine so that they could "hunt" sickness and provide 100% ethical Family Dinners.

                                        This could mean that Cheiron knows the mythic side of Beasts, and have Thaumatechnology to get into Lairs, after all a Horror will have more interesting body parts than a human. In fact, the whole thing about Hercules using the Neman Lion's pelt as armour or Perseus using Medusa's head as a weapon could be the core foundation of Thaumatechnology. A bit of chopped Horror to impose mythic rather than scientific/occult laws. Hunted rather than farmed monsters so the powers are earned, and suddenly impossible transplants might not even have side effects.

                                        * I like the idea that Cheiron has fallen apart several times in history and each time it has been rebuilt by someone discovering the remains of the previous Cheiron. But one of those groups has to be the origonal.

                                                In my own mist shrouded mythology of Hunter the first Cheiron Group was the cell of disciples that gravitated towards an experienced hunter in the ancient near east. He was one of the first to be a true terror to the monsters beyond the fire, being a precocious horseman and an expert in rites of extispicy. He trained many new hunters over his long years, and one of these disciples would spell the doom of their cell. The disciple was a troubled man plagued by manias but he was a tremendous warrior in their battles against the unnatural, and their leader thought he could be focused with training. The problem was that the disciple wasn't just a hunter, he was a Hero. He would never be able to rest until he finished his bloody labors, and anyone who stood in his way even for a moment would become a footnote in his great destiny. He betrayed and murdered their master, breaking the cell and scattering it for centuries until it passed into legend.
