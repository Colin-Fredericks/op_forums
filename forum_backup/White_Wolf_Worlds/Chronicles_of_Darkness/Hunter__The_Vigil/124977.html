    Conspiracy: The Fog Men, 'Ghostwalkers'

    05-04-2014, 12:10 AM
    Mortal Remains paints the potential for cooperation between Sin-Eaters and Hunters as being very possible and perhaps even more likely than antagonism. A curious idea struck me while leafing over it; why not instead write them up as a Conspiracy? I’m aware not everything within Geist: the Sin-Eaters is ported over here, but this isn’t intended as a replacement. Consider it a fun thought experiment.

    (Note: I haven't quite figured out what I want to do for their Status levels or Endowment. I'll get it done eventually. Maybe.)

    The Fog Men (“Ghostwalkers”)
    Death is not the end.
    Many know this to be true, that even as the heart ceases to beat and the last breath is exhaled, there remains something that science has yet to quantify. Many religions speak of an afterlife – either lands of due reward for the virtuous and wicked, or simply a place the dead inhabit – and belief in ghosts is common across cultures of the world. Those who uphold the Vigil know a slightly different take; the dead don’t always rest easy, and can even be a threat. Helping them pass on takes esoteric rituals or repeated swings of a blessed weapon, and they can do immense harm before the hunters reach them.

    Those who call themselves the Fog Men know what waits on the other side of the veil better than most. Each and every one of them died at some point, but was offered a deal by something foul in their final moments. Opinions differ on what these morbid shades are – and they should, for individual entities seem to have little in common – but their promise is always the same; return to life, so that you make keep the balance of the dead.

    The return is miraculous and life-changing, offering new perspective on what truly matters. It offers strange gifts – the ability to see the restless dead and interact with them – and carries a heavy burden. Each of the ‘ghostwalkers’ must suffer the constant whisperings of the dread thing that dragged them back to the world of the quick, and is bound to their duty to make things right with the dead. They cannot rest until their duty is done.

    Those Who Guard The Gates
    Few can agree on just what exactly the Fog Men make their pacts with. The beings are diverse in appearance and character, with no little tying them together other than their purpose; one may be a burnt corpse in gang colors with smoking bullet holes for eyes and a grin made of gold, while another may appear as a Christ with his crown of thorns and pierced body, and a third might be a desiccated horror in the tomb regalia of a pharaoh. Some make demands, other preach, and some still offer it as a kind gift. Regardless of circumstances, all Fog Men grasp the deal for what it is: a second-chance.

    Arcane scholarship is not the strong suit of the Fog Men, who exist largely as individuals or isolated cells with little chance to discuss the finer details of their condition. One of the more popular beliefs is that they serve the loa of Voodoo tradition, while those united by their faith might claim to have seen certain martyrs and saints. Ghostwalker beliefs run the gamut from piecemeal pop spirituality to dead faiths and strange perversions of what is familiar. Their lot in life is a strange one, and there are few concrete answers for those who look. Many don’t think it matters much; the fact is, they have a new lease on life, a job to do, and the abilities to do it.

    Remi. she/her. game designer.

      The Enemy:
      The Fog Men primarily deal with ghosts. Their primary task is sending the dead to their final reward however they can. Ideally, this would be done by helping the ghost move on, but this can prove difficult if little record remains of who a specific dead man might’ve been in life or if the conditions for such are simply not feasible. If this is the case, then the offending entity is ‘coerced’ into passing on with strong ceremonies or their ability to physically tear the restless dead asunder.

      Their Vigil extends beyond ghosts, however. Any mortal or monster that causes death and suffering is a potential target for ghostwalkers enacting vengeance on behalf of those who cannot seize it themselves. A woman might not be able to pass on until the man who murdered her is dead; some of the more jaded Fog Men would have no qualms about putting him in the ground to help her. A bloodsucker leaves dozens of teens exsanguinated in a ditch and must meet the morning sun for them to pass on. Some ghost stories require less violence; a cruel bully might be forced to make peace with someone they drove to suicide, while another ghost simply needs his father to admit that he was always loved.

      Special hatred is reserved for two groups: witches and the undead. Necromancers pierce the barrier between the two worlds and force the dead into servitude. This perversion of the proper order is unforgivable and punished without mercy. Equally offensive are those who cling to a life they do not have (reanimated corpses and unbreathing leeches alike), for they prevent their own natural passing. Some Fog Men make claims about reincarnation of the karmic wheel, but the majority simply know in their hearts that the dead should just stay dead.

      Hunters:
      • You ran a red light and got t-boned by an 18-wheeler. The paramedics said it was a miracle that you survived at all, and the hospital staff were equally amazed at how quickly your broken legs healed. You know exactly why it happened, and how you must repay your debt.
      • Your gang was your life, and you were happy to die for them. You were riddled with bullets covering your brother’s escape and made your peace with the end as you bled out, but something dragged you back. Something more powerful than family drives you now.
      • You had a heart attack at the office and nobody lifted a finger to help you. Coming back to life taught you two important lessons; there’s something more to life than a paycheck, and people are fucking assholes.
      • You were murdered in cold blood by a fanatic who rambled some insane nonsense under his breath the whole time. The life of a ghostwalker has been yours for two years since, and it just hit you last week; he was a Fog Man, too.
      Stereotypes:
      • The Long Night: I mean, sure, there’s something pretty cool about an exorcism straight out of The Exorcist, but it rarely does any good. Don’t count on their help to be worth much… or for them to not think of you as a witch.
      • The Loyalists of Thule: We tend not to be ‘learn-by-doing’ types, and that can hurt us. These bookworms have a lot of old books that cover the holes in what we know, but they don’t like to share. One weird thing to keep in mind; something about them makes them magnets for hauntings, and really gnarly ones at that.
      • The Lucifuge: These guys are weird as Hell, pun intended. Then again, we are too. Stay out of their way so they can do their jobs, they’ll do the same for us, and together we leave the world a safer place at the end of the day.
      • Task Force: VALKYRIE: You can count on the government to look at a ghost problem and think “the clear solution here is to shoot as many bullets as we can at it.” Fucking idiots. Stay far away and you won’t end up in some blacksite hellhole somewhere
      Archetypes
      Nothing governs the Fog Men other than their shared task and how they were burdened with it. However, three prevailing methodologies make up the bulk of the ghostwalker response to the duty handed down to them.
      • Advocates are the standard portrait of a Fog Man, hoping to peacefully resolve what keeps ghosts trapped in the realm of the living. They’re willing to get aggressive if they must, but operate largely out of a desire to help ease the suffering of the dead.
      • Reapers are the opposite of Advocates; the surest way to keep a dead man down is to pound him into his grave and put a boot square on his chest. They also ruthlessly pursue necromancers and the undead with the intent to kill.
      • Pilgrims seek to understand just as much as they wish to aid and punish. They interrogate the living and the dead about the nature of the soul and the afterlife, and relentlessly comb over occult texts in an attempt to grasp just what the hell they are.
