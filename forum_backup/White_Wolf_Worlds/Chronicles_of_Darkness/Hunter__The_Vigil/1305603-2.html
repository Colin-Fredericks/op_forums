                            Originally posted by Ruger View Post

                            It always felt backwards to me. More like "OK, I have this cool idea for a group! They use magical relics despite being normal humans otherwise!" "I see, I see. So they're hunters, right? They'll probably end up fighting the type of monsters that have artifacts, so they can get more." "Right, makes sense! So the big book monsters that have artifacts are...Mages and werewolves. Here, I'll write up a backstory giving them a reason to hunt them beyond killing them and taking their stuff!"

                            If the book where written now, it would certainly have "and mummies" tacked on if not shifting focus on them primarily.
                            Well

                            a) It's not written now. It was written over a decade ago with several books of built up mythology, structure, modes of operation, and more, and with that, the focus has been clarified and developed, and to arbitrarily go "Well NOW we know about this game, so, you know, shove it in there" feels pretty damn disingenuous compared to the clear theme it has.

                            b) You make it sound like that's not part of the design process. In this case, they started with the idiom of relics, which at the time covered werewolves, mages, and changelings. So they worked backwards to develop a story that was cool and evocative out of there, arrived at the destruction of a paradisical world, built into those themes(Because it is a major part of the text in Hunter 1E's Core, even if it's not the first part), cut out changelings because it didn't fit, and built their story from there. That's just how game design works. If you applied the same principle today, anyone looking for Cool, Evocative, and Weird is probably going to latch onto the same common root, and maybe create a hierarchy of enemies that allowed for emphasis on the Arisen, but would probably still give precedence to mages and werewolves. You don't always arrive where you start, but when you try to get to the heart of a thing and deal with strong ideas, you end up where you need to.

                            c) Just because something sounds obvious doesn't mean it always work. You have to take a long hard look at the tangle of things and see how it spins out to find the really strong thread, and then ruthlessly shave things out in the holy name of word count, and keep your focus on that core. Paradise Lost is a stronger thread than Oh Hey Relics, and can in fact better support Relics than Relics could support, say, Paradise Lost. With that said, some things get lost as a result of that.

                            d) In order to be horrifying, weird, tantalizing, and otherwise intriguing, what horror and comedy share is the twist of expectations. The sheer fact people complain about it without thinking about it but how it works with a little examination basically means the way AKD is now is a damn good fit for the dark mystery and horror angles of the game.


                            "But They Both Care About Relics" is a surface level argument, and a dumb one at that. It does mean there's accounting to be done, and certainly a rivalry to explore, but to pin them to the center of the mass of mythos would be...well, dumb and superficial.

                                    On the subject of the "AKD and relics", I just want to note that in Compacts and Conspiracies the Sword are presented as a faction which actually had enough with relics. C&C (which is one of the best Hunter books, IMO, but I also like Witchfinders so take my word with a grain of salt) goes deep into the Paradise Lost theme and shows that for the AKD, relics are a reminder of what they claim was lost for them. It is their inheritance, sure- but it is also a reminder for their, and the world's, fallen state, and who is at fault for this.

                                    I'm not saying that mummies can't get into this (relics as the inheritance of humanity may overlap with the way the Arisen treat the same relics as inheritance of Irem), but just like the MM hunts vampires first and the Lucifuge have a special hate toward demons, the AKD have their own enemies- and mummies never broke the world. Perhaps their creators changed the stars and forced kingship into the world together with messing up with time and memory, but they have never caused the world to fall from grace. Irem was far from a perfect utopia (more of Atlantis's negative, saying the truth), and the legacy it left behind is cursed for all eternity.

                                    I mean, Mortal Remains sums up pretty well the interaction between the AKD and mummies. Competition, game of cat and mouse, intelligence gathering and espionage. Making the AKD into specifically mummy hunters beyond what is already established instead of their shapeshifter focus earns little, IMO and cost a lot by diluting their themes into "just relic hunters".

I'm not saying it is not possible- but you need to be careful doing such things. Like, careful.
