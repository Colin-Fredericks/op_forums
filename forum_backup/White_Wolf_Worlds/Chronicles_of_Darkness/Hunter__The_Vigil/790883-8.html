        I think Option 3 would serve to broaden Hunter as a whole and rejuvenate Ashwood Abbey.
        1. In my play experience as a Storyteller the compact has been a source of conflict. One PC was an affluent S&M enthusiast – he read the core and that’s what sprung to mind for him. During one encounter with a ‘black widow’ (who happened to be an immortal Blood Bather) he was the only PC who bought into the monster’s argument that her victims wanted to die. A later session involving the Little Black Book (WoD Reliquaries – a cursed item used to compel women into being raped) he manipulated another Hunter into letting him keep it, ostensibly to improve his prestige in the Abbey. In another game a player was Tommy Lansetta (from The Keepers SAS) an Ashwood mob brat. When the police became involved with a series of murders the cell was investigating the character had to ‘go underground’ as his mafia connections and appearance at crime scenes made him appear to be the culprit. Eventually the character lost his mind.

          The first example was genuine antagonism, the player leaned into the “Something has to be fucking something else.” (to quote from Mortal Remains) of Ashwood Abbey and made a character that eventually betrayed his cell.
        The second hit the “humanity is fragile” theme dead on. His reason for hunting (killing monsters for kicks and doing good incidentally) just wasn’t enough for him to survive intact.
        1. Ashwood Abbey and the Hunt Club are thematically drawn from the same well Hellfire Clubs and “the Most Dangerous Game”. They are both firmly built on wealth, status, and hedonism. Ashwood even name checks “The Most Dangerous Game” and the Hunt Club refers to its killing as the “Great Game”. It’s the same idea, they are hunting monsters for some kind of a sport, it’s a game to them. The difference is Ashwood claims the prey are the villains, the Hunt Club knows that the hunters are.
        1. It will make room for a new compact in Hunter, which as much as I love the game it lacks the ‘global’ reordering that Vampire 2nd and Werewolf 2nd have achieved.

        Consider this:

        They began as Ashwood Abbey, now hunters have a new name for them “the hunt club.” A fallen hunter compact born of all the misogyny, racisms, and classism of a 19th century aristocratic order who chose to hunt monsters simply for the thrill. Some new recruits and allies have no idea they’re surrounded by killers and slashers until they find themselves standing over the corpse of a human being. There are rumors of a compact within the compact trying to reform Ashwood from within.


        I think something along those lines would give Storytellers the latitude to not invalidate Ashwood Abbey characters that already exists, give hunters a dark and interesting set of antagonists, and create stories about the ones trying to save them or being drawn in.

            Of the three options and how I interpreted them, I vote for 1 but with some modifications. I think the Abbey shouldn't be in the character creation chapter as an option as it stands, instead I think it should be mentioned in a create your own section for the Hunter axis as a failed or fallen compact/conspiracy. The section could explain the Abbey as we knew it in 1st Ed and then show the what happens when a group falls as it transitions from option 1 to option 2 and then collapses in on itself in a catastrophic self destruction before reaching option 3 because I'm certain they'd go out with a bang rather than become the Hunt Club outright. This would allow those of us who are fond of the Abbey keep them as we know them now in our hearts and mind, which is to say ****ing on werewolf loci while being **** off by a faithful squire in the face of certain death which certainly involved unloading more than an elephant gun.

            My reasons for this are:
            1) I love the abbey just the way they are but they are a group of Nihilist which requires just the right group/game to run smoothly making them more of resource book material.
            2) I think Hunter could use a bit of history to liven it up some and why not a failed or fallen compact, in fact, add a few more so that the players understand the sacrifices that have to be made by any organization that has a firm foundation. Maybe even mention how hard it is to rally forces in the World of Darkness to show us why people have to do it behind closed doors in tier 3 games.
            3) Because it'd make for a great story seed. The fallen compact/conspiracy will rise again. From the ancient Lodge in Scotland, the players could breathe life back in to the old girl until she was ready to make things bleed again, reforming it to something that the players would be proud to be a part of while it maintained a bit of the "ultra-violence" that we associate with it now.

                                                            Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                                            I still think something like this will introduce a contradiction to the Abby's core charachter. The Abbey are about violating rules, giving them any rules other than "don't spoil other member's fun" just doesn't make sense.
                                                            Enh, I dunno if I can buy this characterization. Let me sort of step through it.

                                                            The Abbey kills monsters for being boorish company, they're hardly nonjudgmental. High society excess has tons of (sometimes unspoken) rules that People of Class are expected to Just Know, whether it be about the etiquette of bringing your mistress to a social function or which cut of jacket to wear on a quasi-legal foxhunt. Even inside the Abbey there will be an intuitive layer of codes of conduct, an ability to read the atmosphere, that lets an Abbey hunter know that using a golf club to hit a wounded witch in the ribs a fourth time is absolutely hilarious while a fifth hit is obviously crass ("Taking it all too a bit too seriously, good man.") and will see him uninvited from all of the season's important social functions.

                                                            The Abbey's doormen would turn away someone attempting to attend the hunt in a t-shirt and jeans, because, really, One Simply Doesn't, do they? Being transgressive in certain avenues doesn't mean that they don't hold to a whole constellation of implicit and explicit rules rigorously. Let's put it this way: Who has more (official and unofficial) rules about dress, membership, and conduct, a street gang or a country club? Which one is a hunter from the Abbey more likely to be a member of?

                                                            The Hunt Club's scoring system, traditions, and rotation of judges aren't going to be at all alien to members of the Abbey - they're familiar trappings that mirror the ways in which the Abbey's hunts are social events (often with their own traditions and rules, like drawing lots or jockeying for points.)
