    What Happens if Hunters Learn about the Exarchs?

    04-13-2014, 03:51 PM
    Since you guys have the Hunter campaigns and characters imagine this. A mage has taken the time to catch your character or group and explain, with solid proof, about the Exarchs. He proves to your character or characters that the Exarchs broke reality, they have used their catspaws (the Seers of the Throne) to warp and pollute all human institutions, Vampires et al can only prey on humanity because of the crimes of the Exarchs, and that only the defete of the Exarchs will end the corruption ofthe world.


    Do they...


    Kill the Mage and start finding out how to hunt the Seers of the Throne?


    Do they hire on with the Seers?


    Do they seek to Join the Atlantian Mages in an alliance?


    Do they just go out and have dinner and talk about how the scary crazy just wanted to preach at them?


    What do they do?

              Frankly, the hunters would be rank amateurs if they didn't at least start asking questions about this guy's motives in telling them. Now if said mage could actually back some of his intel up and show them that the Seers and their masters are the bad guys, maybe they'd at least consider him an ally. Groups like Network Zero or the Loyalists would be incredibly interested in finding out more about the Exarchs, while the Abbey would probably lead the informant mage along for a while for kicks. Smaller cells would probably buy the story whole but be utterly ineffective (presuming they don't break out the ol' witch burning kit), though they also wouldn't last very long against forces like the Seers on any level higher than local.

              Conspiracies, that's where the meat of the question is. The Malleus, TFV, and Cheiron would all have an interest in a group that has apparently infiltrated the highest echelons of religion, business, and government in a big way. The question isn't whether these guys sign on with anyone, it's how well the Seers can survive the sudden interest these conspiracies have for hunting them down.

                  It really depends on the players and how the actual events go.

                  But when you get right down it, a large number of player characters wouldn't be able to do much with that information. It's a chronicle changing sort of thing to believe it and try and do something about it personally.

                  On top of that, it's really not surprising the higher up you go. There are all sorts of people who seem to have their fingers stuck in the pies the Exarchs poke at, and just as often that particular pie pokes back out into the people poking it, and you have parasitical(Burial at Sea and Andrew Ryan seem to be infecting my language) relationships all over the place. In a game where you report that sort of thing to higher ups, assuming you even get there, and let them handle it, their response might honestly be "So what?". For the upper echelons, it's business as usual.

                                          As with all Crossover questions it comes down to: 1) the individuals on both sides of the equation, 2) the situation they meet, and 3) how much knowledge they have of each other. Seers tend to do more horrible things in the name of their "gods" than the Pentacle mages, so I tend to think that an average Hunter who followed trails to an evil witch would end up finding Seers (or Scelesti) more often than someone from the Orders.

                                          Though, in Hunter's case, there's an example or two of Seer controlled hunter groups in the Witch Slayer book. That's a thing, where the books give reasons why Hunters would side with black hats of other settings against other PC splats. That said, the antagonist splats do more horrible things, so that's probably a storyhook where they see the bad things and have a heel-face-turn situation against their evil puppet masters.

                                              Also, probably worth mentioning, that for some Hunters, the Black Iron Prison is pretty close to what they want the world to look like.                                                   If the hunters have a good reason to believe what they have been told, there are 4 major ways to act, IMO-

                                                  1) support the Pentacle- I think only those hunter which are driven by the hope to make the world a better place would choose that option, and only if they believe that the world the Pentacle would make will truly be a better place for humanity. As long as it is a Tier 1 game, the choice won't have much consequences beyond being dragged to a world were reality is false and you search for signs everywhere, but at higher Tiers the hunters would have to convince their organization that they truly do the right thing.

                                                  2) support the Seers- I mean: come one. Sure, the Exrachs may have broken the old world, but it was ages ago, if it ever was. What they do now is to support the status que, and if the world isn't that bad, why should they endanger that frail peace for the small chance of "awakening humanity"? Also- are you really sure that a world in which everyone knows about magic and enough people can use it is truly a good place? No one promise you that a Slasher won't Awake, and that things like soul stealing and flesh shaping would stop when it would happen- on the contrary, actually. The Seers make sure that most of humanity won't know about magic, and thus they stop the greatest horrors from happening. They may not be the best option, but the others are worse.

                                                  3) keeping Neutral- so, you are telling me that everything fucked up in this world is because some big bad magicians became gods? ok.. what do you want me to do with that? I think that most hunters will have that reaction. I mean, someone comes to you and tells you why the world is fucked up, and that maybe if you'll help him something may change- but its not likely. You are not a witch, and you don't care about the politics of witches. Let them kill each other about "philosophical disagreements". You have a better way to make the world into a better place- and that is by your gun, knife, books and your demonic blood/ blessings of God/ whatever

                                                  4) Kill them all!- you are an hunter, someone who devotes his life to protect humanity, and a mage come and tells you that mages have destroyed the world? Great! He has just gave you a reason to kill him, his family, friends and his magical enhanced puppy! One of the main reason that hunters who are willing to kill a mage do so, is because magic is dangerous, and the story about the Exrachs only demonstrate how dangerous is it. It also tells you that every witch and warlock out there is a potential madman who could, one day, try to become a god by himself out of his selfishness and break the world AGAIN. Those things would only justify agendas like the one of the Knights of St George, that even if a mage tries to do the god thing, he still has the potential to ruin everything- and in order to protect the world, he must die.
