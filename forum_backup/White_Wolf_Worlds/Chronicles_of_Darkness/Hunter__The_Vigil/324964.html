    Compact: Parole Gang

    11-28-2014, 10:42 PM
    A mask wearing freak roams a suburban neighborhood, armed with an axe, but he isn't looking to chop up humans. A much fiercer prey is what he looks for, and is why he's covered in deep gashes. But why does he have the look of last year's most wanted, and why does he wear a fake ring?

    A woman roams an alley, carrying a machete, her face covered in cuts, yet still wears a wicked grin. In her other hand, she carries the head of a local preacher whom never seemed to have daylight sermons. Why does she have a cheap bracelet, and why does the police investigation grind to a halt despite witnesses claiming they saw who did it?

    A businessman puts on the smile that has gotten him so much, this time to stick a razor thin slice of silver into someone's neck. Why is he out and about however, if, for those who check the news, he was arrested for the brutal murder of seven people last month alone? And, what's with the plastic necklace he wears everywhere?

    A cute girl promises much with a wink, before leading someone into an alleyway. Gunfire erupts, but when the area is checked, all that's found is candy wrappers and a bunch of bullet holes. A cheap watch in the dumpster goes unnoticed, despite having chunks of flesh on the underside.


    All of them are former convicts, and were tried for multiple murders. Many could be described as, "Slashers," but many others are just bog standard murderers, killing for rather mundane reasons, such as money. So, why are they given another chance, despite being charged with little doubt as to their ultimate guilt? Because they were given an offer by an organization that is both very old, but has also gone through many changes.

    Once, they were in 19th century Britain, giving people a chance to get their debts forgiven for killing supernatural threats to the people, although distinctly not the Crown, of the United Kingdom. Their focus expanded to the United States with time, and the relative success of their strategy, however they were shut down when their activities got quite a few elites in power killed. But, it formed again, under different leadership, this time busting people out of incarceration to purge American society of hostile supernatural elements. Once again, it was eventually found and busted, after killing quite a few people in power, however not before having some noteworthy successes, and taking all the way until the 1970s.

    Its most recent formation, in the 1990s, marks another run of the organization. Each form has been ever more decentralized, with the latest having no centralized leadership, unlike the first, or even strict requirements for whom can get busted out, like the second. It has become more like a strange marketing brand anyone can use if they fulfill certain requirements rather than any sort of unified organization. This extremely loose structure causes to have it appearances ranging from North America to Europe, and even recent locations in Far East Asia. Its latest name is the Parole Gang, however that's a nickname, and may change in the future.

    There are only two rules to become part of the organization. The first rule is the acceptance of the central saying, "Use Evil to fight Evil." The saying both relates to using criminals, not necessarily violent, who are both assuredly guilty, and only fighting those who pose a harm to innocent life. The latter is intentionally vague, meaning human threats to innocents can frequently be included alongside the killing of supernatural threats of innocent persons.

    The second rule is contained in the only other saying that has survived every iteration of the organization. "Anyone whom is innocent is open to having protection." This means innocent supernatural entities, how this is defined seems to be a very case by case basis matter, are not a target, and, more importantly, that innocent supernatural entities can call upon the services of the organization. This service is used surprisingly often, most often as protection against overzealous hunters, putting the organization in a unique position in relation to the rest of the Hunter community.

    If these two rules are followed, and a certain ceremony is done, tools are provided to the local branch of the organization with which to carry out their objectives. These allow anyone to be broken out of prison, but also their restraint in the form of an object. The objects themselves look like everyday, and frequently dirt cheap cheap, items but carry the ability to prevent the wearer from deviating from their assigned task until they've earned their freedom. Even then, as a sort of final punishment, a chunk of flesh is peeled off when the item is taken off.

    Contacting them is done in a plethora of ways, with only anonymity as a shared requirement, from the risks the latest iteration of the organization posses. Sometimes, it means writing your name and problem on a piece of paper, then handing it to a bartender in a certain of part of town. For another, perhaps it means spray painting the symbol you need in the area having problems, and, in a few days, the area will be cleaned out by people once on death row. One can even, in a certain city, call them, although you won't get any verbal reply, and it has to be on a very specific pay phone. To know how to contact them usually just means knowing some local urban legends, as a sort of hiding in plain sight form of communication.

    (Note, I'm going for a sort of reverse of my prior organization, the Favors Association, because, essentially, this one is protecting innocents by using horrible people, while the Favors Association was using innocent people to carry out horrible things. That, and hey, what if there were Hunters that protected say, Changelings every once in awhile.)

      The Enemy
      Anyone, and anything, can be, and probably at some point, has been a target. Everything ranging from Vampires and Werewolves, all the way to the truly bizarre and other Hunters have been targets. The last, killing other Hunters, has gained the organization a bit of notoriety, especially with the Cheiron Group from frequently preventing their acquisition of supernatural creatures, and Task Force: Valkyrie, after stopping a critical operation through murdering an entire cell's worth of operatives. VASCU also has an extremely hostile relationship with the group from breaking free Slashers to use as murderers against other gangs. The notoriety does little to stop Parole Gangs, because of their decentralization, however it makes them wildly unpredictable to other Hunter conspiracies.

      That Parole Gangs are made up of convicts, and frequently, outright Slashers, doesn't help matters. The organization, whatever its current name, is considered a terrorist organization by the United States, Canada, Mexico, the United Kingdom, Ireland, Australia, and after a recent incident, Japan. This has been put Parole Gangs into frequent conflict with police forces, and in some cases, even the militaries of these countries. Only the constant supply of fresh bodies, seeing as violent criminals in jail seeking parole are hardly rare, and the organization being so decentralized as impossible to realistically do much against, has kept it around. The occasional bit of supernatural help, in this case from perhaps those they have protected in the past, has certainly helped cover up their crimes.

      The sheer variety of those hunted however is matched by those who have used Parole Gangs to protect themselves. Frequently, someone whose innocent only requires that they avoid doing evil actions they have any choice in, once allowing a group of Vampires to use a Parole Gang against the Long Night. More strict interpretations perhaps require the person has never committed murder, ruling out most but not all supernatural entities, or require that they have only killed lawfully, which rules out Vampires but not Werewolves. The most strict, and rare from being difficult to implement and at times leaving too many people open to targeting, interpretation is the innocent being someone whose never done significant harm to another sapient being. The interpretation matters in not only who can employ Parole Gangs, but who they are used against. After all, innocents cannot be targeted, which itself has caused complications in many a hunting operation.

      Why the supernatural would turn to Parole Gangs is itself complicated, however is usually to fight other Hunters, which they cannot themselves because of the weapons possessed by said Hunters, or because killing said Hunters is a... complicated affair at best. The other reason can be to fight other supernatural entities, where they are cannon fodder that is very disposable from the viewpoint of the entity in question(a tactic by Werewolves has been to use Parole Gangs against other Werewolves before securing their territory.) However, the most frequent employer, for good or ill, of Parole Gangs are other human beings.

          Hunters

          You didn't kill him, but forensics claim otherwise. You can't prove you were framed, so you thought it was over for you. However, you were given an offer. Kill an evil being, and you can go. They didn't mention that you had to hunt a freaking Vampire, but that's another matter.

          You know they deserved to die, but that doesn't hold up in the court of law. Neither does a witness surviving who can point out who did it. But, someone's offered you a way out, and you just have to kill more people who deserve to die. That they happen to carry assault rifles, or on the flip side be rather hard to kill with bullets doesn't change a whole lot.

          You're not someone who people would take for being a brutal murderer. On the surface, you look like a choir girl that has never had a sinful thought in her life. But one day, and your still not sure why, you just snapped. One minute, things were calm, and the next, your family was all over the floor, with you holding a bloody axe. Someone has found a better use for you, and if you can kill people who deserve it perhaps you can earn forgiveness.

          You're not sure what happened, but you are very much sure you're in the right. Sure, you may have killed that boy, but he had the look of one of those boys that liked to beat you when you were their age, or throw you in the mud. He'd probably do worse to someone else if you didn't intervene, and set things right, assuming you even did. And now, someone's going to give you the chance to truly set things right. You'll get your favorite gun back, and you can kill people who truly deserve it. What's there to lose?

          Yeah, you're no Angel, but these guys are weird. Why do they want someone who stole some shit to kill a freaking Werewolf? You can barely handle the average gun, much less use it well enough to take out something that disobeys everything you know about biology. Well, they did say this would be the last time, so maybe... you can go after this.

          Factions
          The Parole Gang has quite a few separate philosophies, considering how widespread the group is, and that its really more of a bizarre marketing brand at this point than anything else. However, three very broad tendencies have emerged, two of which are based off those who take one of the prior two iterations as a model to follow, while the third is a modern tendency reflecting the current realities the organization has to deal with.


          Debtors: Like the first iteration, they're more interested in getting those who have committed crimes to pay their debt to society than what they hunt, or who they defend. They frequently take the symbolism of Angels associated with forgiveness, such as the Archangel Zadkiel, as they frequently view themselves as saints who are giving the damned the only chance they have left, along with preventing the innocent from having to commit sins themselves. They also have the most strict definition, usually, of the term innocent so as to keep the list of targets wide, and as part of their religious fervor. Disturbingly, many of those they recruit fully buy into their philosophy, such as Slashers motivated by religion before, and the many violent implications of such. They're most common in the Bible Belt of the United States, and enjoy some support within certain parts of the United Kingdom.

          Purgers: Taking inspiration from the second iteration, they're most interested in killing those who they view as evil, and use former prisoners because it allows them to avoid dirtying their hands, not so that the criminals can gain forgiveness. Like the second iteration, they believe it is possible to purge society of certain types of evil through concentrated violence against it, which gives another reason to use former murderers and the like. Who better to conduct a purge than those who are already so skilled with violence? Embodying this, they frequently use hammers in their symbolism, to present themselves as massive concentrated force against disgusting elements in society. Additionally, they frequently have the broadest interpretation of innocence, so as to allow a narrow target for their hunters to pursue.
          They're most often found in the Western United States, but recently, they have appeared in Japan.

          Defenders: The newest, and by far most common, group, its focus is on protecting the people they view as innocent. Criminals are used because they're disposable in the service of this purpose, and because they have high ability to commit violence, especially if they are Slashers. Their symbolism is that of a shield, promising protection to anyone viewed as innocent, and that, while not as wide as with Purgers, is a very large group. Their modernity also ties into that they view themselves as a market brand in a bizarre way, profiting in terms of credibility and people helping defend them than monetary gain. This has given them the furthest reach, as they try to open up new branches of the organization in the manner companies like McDonalds or Starbucks try to have global reach. They currently are based in Canada, the American Northeast and Mexico, however groups using the ideology have been found as far as Australia and Scotland, with rumors going around that a branch may soon open up somewhere in South America.


          Notably, all the tendencies are growing, although the Defenders have the fastest growth. Even the Debtors, with the slowest growth over all, are enjoying steady growth in the religious parts of the United States, and may expand operations further in the United Kingdom, with areas like Ireland already having a major presence. As some may say, interesting times are ahead for the Parole Gangs.

                  Status

                  Status doesn't really work as it does in a traditional organization, because of how decentralized the Parole Gangs have become, and because the recruits doing the dirty work aren't really the type that can get promoted. The rest are handlers that get willing persons out of jail, facilitate their continued cooperation with the special items, and otherwise just act as behind the scenes support. Instead, status is based on how much a recruit has shown they are eager to do the work, so is instead gaining them things to help them.

                  Status 1: You've taken the deal, and have gotten the item insuring you aren't going to skip out on it. They helped you get out of jail, and in the process, taught you some tricks to insure you aren't getting caught again. Add 1 dice to either Subterfuge or Stealth when either tricking or avoiding authorities who would attempt to get you back.

                  Status 3: By demonstrating loyalty, and staying alive this long, you're given help in insuring that you will complete this. You get a 3 dot safe house, all the dots assigned to secrecy, to insure that you will continue to operate as a successful agent, and one day, complete your forgiveness.

                  Status 5: You're in the home run, and they're insuring you get there. Your safe house now becomes 5 dots, in terms of secrecy, and has one dot worth in a cache of equipment that you choose, whether it be weapons or need surveillance equipment.

                  (Like them? Think they're too powerful? Compacts can, unfortunately, be hard to judge with this kind of thing.)

                      The Warriors of Redemption

                      The first iteration of the Parole Gangs, and what would set the groundwork for much to come, began somewhere in the United Kingdom. The only other concrete detail know about its beginning is that it began within religious institutions, although whether it was the Church of England, the Catholic Church, or some other church isn’t known. Later on, the organization would end up involving quite a few different churches, only united in the purpose of using the sinful.

                      There is a myth that it began with a bishop, whose name has long been lost to history. Story goes that this bishop was frustrated with what he saw as a cycle of sin. People committing sin so deep, and having no way out, so going further. That he began to see monsters, and in his eyes worse, people committing evils from position of powers where they were unable to be touched. This frustration would come to a head, however, when he visited a prison, and met a man destined to be hanged. Seeing the man feel so guilty gave the bishop an idea.

                      The Bishop stood up and blessed a cross with such force as to rip a hole in the prison itself. The cross was then given to the man, who was told to kill those who deserved it to redeem himself for his sins, and that the cross would keep him on track. Story ends that the man went on to carry out the work, slaughtering evildoers beyond redemption until his grave.

                      Again, this tale is probably a myth. For one, the initial Warriors of Redemption used those stuck in debtor’s prison, and got them to kill in return for bailing them out with cash. Binding objects didn’t come into play until much later in the organization’s life. However, the founding myth is more meant to demonstrate the organization’s values, and does contain the grain of truth that the religious were integral to the organization.

                      However, while their founding myth is probably untrue, their exploits are most certainly not. For the most part, the Warriors of Redemption actually focused on eliminating those that spread evil throughout society, who more often than not, were human beings. Their killing of the supernatural came as an extension of this concept, as would, eventually, the most unique part of the organization. Defending those who were of supernatural origin.

                      Near the organization’s height, it was realized that Debtor Prisons wouldn’t work anymore for they were going out of fashion. Around this time, the Warriors of Redemption had spread the United States, recruiting the zeal of Christians throughout, and murderers were the primary source of new recruits now. It was in this time, starting in the 1850s, that the Warriors of Redemption introduced two precedents that would allow future iterations to arise, but also what would ultimately destroy them.

                      The first precedent was that supernatural entities could be innocent. This was partially because of the usage of binding objects, as witches were necessary to the magic that allowed the binding objects to keep recruits on track. The other component to this has been theorized to be how so many prisoners were gotten out, again depending on the magic of witches. Regardless of why however, this expansion of who could be considered innocent would set a precedent with many complications later.

                      The second precedent was that of the Warriors of Redemption becoming a rather bizarre marketing brand. Many communities wanted to have their own branch, and they were easy to setup, like some kind of Hunter equivalent to Wal-Mart. While they didn’t profit in terms of cash, they did gain resources they wouldn’t have otherwise, and access to all the types of help their organization required through this. All of this was facilitated through easy setup, as the rules aren’t hard to learn, and once done, magic items can be created.

                      Both of these would, in the long run, allow future iterations of the organization to arise. However, it would also doom the first iteration of the organization, because it brought it so much attention. By the 1880s, a coalition of other Hunter groups, government organizations, supernatural entities that had been targeted quite a bit, and most of all, those related to human victims of the organization, banded together to destroy it. Its spread also meant it having enemies all over the place to combat it, and its relations with supernatural entities made it easy to tar, isolating it from needed resources.

                      That was how the Warriors of Redemption would come to be destroyed. However, in the process, it left the tools for future iterations of the organization to form. More than that, it created a want for such an organization, from both human and supernatural sources. This need would eventually build strong enough for a new organization to form in the darkest days of the Great Depression. More than that, it would show lessons for future iterations to follow, such as the need to become more survivable.

                          Order of the Rotten Hammer

                          If the Warriors of Redemption came out of the religious community, and took much of their imagery from such, the Order of the Rotten Hammer took similar influence from vigilante groups. In the depths of the Great Depression, they promised to protect the poor, and to give a second chance to those driven to insanity by the conditions they were submerged in. But, who started it?
                          Once again, their name has been lost, but this time to the secrecy the organization emphasized. All that’s known is that they had gained access to information about the Warriors of Redemption, and decided to use it in a way not anticipated. They recruited those destined to be executed, and used them to kill those they saw as evil within society. With an extremely broad definition of innocence, this acted as the only restraining bolt against an organization obsessed with violently purging parts of society didn’t like.

                          These targets would end up being either supernatural beings that preyed on the innocent, usually Vampires and certain Mages, or on persons who committed great evil, a mix of serial killers and, controversially for the time, the KKK, along with similar organizations like the Silver Legion. And, while the organization would stop in the 1940s from many members going off to fight in World War 2, it would come back with a fury in the 1950s.

                          It was during that era that Order of the Rotten Hammer would hit its stride. Its symbolism was based on evil being used to destroy evil, and during this period, they had a bit of both to combat. They killed members of the Mafia, Anti-Communists who enriched themselves off witch hunts, monsters that preyed on those unable to fight back because of social circumstance, such as African Americans, and with time, would even begin operations against Hunter organizations that helped hold up what they viewed as a rotten status quoe. The paranoia of the 1950s they exploited for their own ends, turning it against forces in the dark like Vampires, Werewolves, witches, or even other Hunter organizations.

                          However, the 60s and 70s would bring new challenges, the latter decade being what would do them in. The Vietnam War especially would tear the organization apart, not over whether it was just, but how to deal with the repercussions of members getting pulled out by it. In addition, the social anxieties of the time made the organization have a harder time surviving, along with once again organizations teaming up to combat it.

                          This time, as well, their enemies were far more organized, and much more able to strike at them. However, the downfall of the Order of the Rotten Hammer came from internal stresses. They had failed to crush elements of society through violence, despite by then over 40 years of effort devoted to the task. This caused the organization to lose morale, and frequently, support. No one wanted to support a violent crusade that seemed to have no end in sight, or even coherent goal frequently.

                          However, while it existed, it set more precedents that the third iteration would use to stick around for a while. Both of these precedents arose out of the weaknesses the organization had, and attempts to correct them that were too late. The first precedent was that any future organization needed to become even more decentralized, to the point where there was nothing tangible to be hit by an enemy to destroy it. Such was expressed through constant writings of those in the organization such as frustrations about not being able to pick who was considered innocent, a want many branches of the organization expressed.

                          The second precedent was the need for diversity in goals, so as to suit any location, tying back into the Warriors of Redemption becoming a bizarre marketing brand. This idea continued, but the brand, as it were, was a little too restrictive, from having such a narrow list of targets to be considered, broad definition of innocence after all, and left a lot of potential recruits cold. Hence, unlike the Warriors of the Redemption, the Order of the Rotten Hammer, at its height, was only just able to cover the United States, with most of its base being in California and the American Midwest of all places.

                          These precedents would feed the incredibly loose structure, and lack of official title, for the third iteration that began in the 1990s. And, while the Order of the Rotten Hammer collapsed in a rather unceremonious way in the 1970s, it did accomplish, in some ways, more than its predecessor did. It was able to solidify trust to the supernatural community, along with giving reasons to use the service, namely avoiding direct repercussions. After all, serial killers and the like aren’t people anyone will miss, or assume are connected to larger organizations.

                          The last went to the one successful precedent set, the ability to employ Slashers. With an already immense murder rate before being sent to prison, it was unlikely they would ever fulfill their needed forgiveness, and even if they ever did, by such a point, they would’ve had an impressive career. They were considered excellent weapons, being disposable, and much harder to stop than the average human being.

                          This precedent though also lead to another factor that killed the Order of the Rotten Hammer, because of the association with Slashers causing credibility problems for the organization. That the Order of Rotten Hammers didn’t focus as much on redeeming the agents it used made it come off as cold whenever they used say, someone who turned out to be framed later. These all related to the need for more decentralization and diversity in goals, so as the brand wouldn’t be hurt by individual questionable actions taken.
                          The Present

                          It was through all these precedents, all these lessons, that the third iteration, the Parole Gangs, would appear. It would take until the 1990s to re-appear however, needing a decade to build up the need for the organization again. And, being even more amorphous, there’s no known concrete beginning for the organization. Its just known that they started somewhere in the Northeast of the United States, and gave birth to a new image for the organization.

                          For its start would be protecting the impoverished and college students, when they avoided overlap with another group, showing it to have a perversely… hip image. Soon, they secured funds from the middle class, and from occasional wealthy donors who needed an organization that had disposable soldiers. And from there, it spread, its flexibility breathing it to life in a way that most have not found many ways to challenge.

                                              But okay, in the spirit of some other posters, some closing thoughts on this, as I think this is pretty much finished. I hope this Compact can help you improve a Chronicle, or however you want to use them in a future game.

                                              My main idea with this compact was to give an opposite to my prior organization, the Favors Association(which was a Conspiracy.) The Favors Association is a clearly evil organization using innocent people as weapons, and exploiting other Hunter organizations, along with various supernatural entities, to help do it. By comparison, Parole Gangs are using evil people(well, most of the time), in the form of violent criminals and slashers to protect innocents from evils of the World of Darkness, including other Hunter organizations if the need arises. They are used by the supernatural, perhaps exploited in some instances, and are of the only groups that would say, be willing to protect Werewolves from other Hunters.

                                              Both organizations play with the theme of reluctance, because neither are using consenting agents. The Favors Association tricks people into being its servants using the tricks of a con artist, while the Parole Gangs are using people that are frequently on death row, or otherwise doomed to a life in prison otherwise. Additionally, the threat of death, or taking away of free will, through either the threat of being kidnapped by an Eldritch Abomination of some sort or, in the case of the Parole Gang, having an object that forces one to, "play by the rules," as it were. I think it'd be interesting for PCs to play someone who... well, who has to hunt monsters or die pretty much, and what kind of characters that opens up for use.

                                              The other theme that unites them is that they have mixed relations with the Hunter community. The Favors Association I see being used as a... necessary evil, for lack of better words, considering how often groups like the Cheiron Group may use them to get monsters they want. By contrast, Parole Gangs would just be hated, as they use Slashers, and have frequently murdered other Hunters, as they are protecting the Supernatural. In either case, more conflicts between Hunters should be interesting to play with.

                                              Anyway, hope you guys like this Compact. I would like to know if you guys think the Status mechanics work very well, and later on, I may try adding some mechanics as to how the objects force Hunters to stay on track, but otherwise, I'll call this the end of the DVD commentary for this, if you will.
