                        I based these on the 1E rules and not my own GMC update.

                        Splinter (Ripper)

                        Talent: Two-Faced: Splits have two distinct personalities and appearances, which are completely irreconcilable. They do not think the same way or have any of the same habits. Their abilities, virtues, and vices are the same, but they manifest in different ways. When investigating a Splinter, a regular Success on a roll to uncover a Clue that incorporates a Social Skill produces a Clue with the Tainted Tag.

                        For supernatural abilities the Storyteller must use their own judgement. Teleinfomatics' Deep Background will be thrown off my inconsistencies between the Splinter's background and their behaviour during the murder and Polygraph will only reveal that the Splinter genuinely believes "it wasn't me", but Postcognition could catch the Splinter changing between personalities.

                        Split (Scourge)

                        Talent: Physical Psyche: As the living embodiment of a repressed personality trait a Split's dark side is not bound by the limits of the flesh. A Split who's good side repressed his anger may bestow his dark side with the ability to enter a berserker rage, while one who developed then repressed pyrophillia after a childhood trauma may control fire itself. Most Splits have simple dread powers that enhance their physical capabilities, the rest have psychic phenomina. If a Split wants more occult or magical abilities they have to crack open a tome like anyone else.

                        Stranger

                        (Note, I think the Stranger write up is too focused on becoming supernatural. It should have more support for Buffalo Bill style attempts to become a different kind of human)

                        Talent – Occult Modus Operandi: The Stranger's murders are part of a deeper occult pattern, it's not enough to heterochromes and discet their eyes for the occult secrets within. They must kill them under the light of a full moon while a north wind blows. And occult rituals, they have a momentum all of their own. Once per scene, when the Stranger intends to commit murder, they may roll Intelligence + Occult vs Resolve + Potency. Successes automatically inflicts a Condition on the victim (usually Spooked), and until the Condition is Resolved the Stranger gets 8-again in all rolls directly targeting their victim.

                        Frailty – No Heart But Another’s: At his very core, the Stranger seeks to become something else because he hates what he his. He is not comfortable in his own skin, and everyone can tell. When the Stranger makes an Expression, Persuasion, or Socialize roll, he makes it at a -3 penalty at all times. If he is pursuing a quarry, he can spend Willpower to offset the penalty and even risk Willpower to ensure great success, but in all other cases, the Stranger’s true emptiness shines through.

                        Transcendent

                        Talent – Transcendent Terror: The Transcendent was right, for all that they appear mortal their rituals, alchemy and murder worked. Transcendants gain a free dot of Dread Powers every time they kill. These Dread Powers must relate to the Transcendent's desired form, one who wishes to be a vampire will never weild power over fire or sunlight.

                        Frailty – Only Human: The Transcendent may believe he has grown beyond the bonds of humanity, but in many ways, he is still fettered to the corpse of his old self. His weakness lies in being reminded that he is still mortal, and his “true self” is mere decoration added to human clay. Each Transcendent has a trigger, it might be hearing their old mortal name, or their mother's perfume, something that reminds them of everything they hated about themselves and want to escape. If the Trasncendent is exposed to this trigger they loose all their Dread Powers for the scene and immediately suffer a Breaking Point.

                        The Predator (Ripper)

                        Talent- I Have Your Scent
                        A Predator fixates on his target. He stalks them, learns their habits, and savours every moment of the hunt. Once per scene, and only after making his target aware that they are being pursued, the Predator may roll Presence + Intimidation vs the targets Composure. If the Predator wins he earns a Stalking Point, up to a maximum of Intimidation. Stalking Points can only be spent in the scene where the Predator attempts to kill his pray, and only once per turn. Each point can:
                        • Remove the target's Defence for a turn.
                        • Double the successes on an attack roll, but only for the purposes of seeing if the attack inflicts a Tilt.
                        • Provide Wits automatic successes on a Stealth roll.


                        The Beast (Scourge)

                        Talent- Top of the Food Chain

                        Those who encounter a Beast feel the full effect of their animalistic nature. The Beast may use Stalking Points in the following additional ways. All of these require a successful Presence + Intimidation vs Composure + Potency roll.
                        • Reduce all the target's Mental Skills by Intimidation to a minimum of zero for a scene.
                        • Reduce the target's Firearms Skill by Intimidation to a minimum of zero for a scene.
                        • Trigger the fight response; the target will attempt to engage the Predator in melee combat until they are Beaten Down or dead.
                        • Trigger the flight response; the target will attempt to flee the Predator on foot.
                        • Assert dominance over another predator, either a mundane animal, a Predator, or another Beast; the target will lie down and show throat, or it's species equivalent.

                            The copy of the Splinters and Splits I had actually came with an alternate set of Talents and Frailties. I have no idea whose alterations these were.

                            Splinters
                            Alternate Splinter Talent                             – A Strange Case: The Splinter is an entirely separate personality, which means the Jekyll is very good at convincing people they aren't a murderer, because in a completely literal sense they are not. They add their Wits as automatic successes to all Persuasion rolls to convince others of their innocence. By hiding behind it's other half a Splinter remains ahead of their pursuit.
                            There are some logical limits to this advantage. If the Jekyll personality actually has committed a crime they cannot benefit from A Strange Case against that particular crime, nor do they get any benefits if directly questioned about split personalities or mental illness. The other logical limit is that the Jekyll has to cover for their darker side, but remember that Splinters are created because part of the Jekyll wants to murder. They create their other personality to commit the crimes they are unable too, most cover for their Hyde.
                            Splinters share their Attributes and Physical Merits between both selves. Skills, other Merits, Virtues, Vices, Codes and Integrity may differ. Both have a separate Willpower pool. The Hyde reverses the mechanics for Virtue and Vice.
                            Alternate Splinter Frailty – A Warning Light Glimmering In Red: The murderer within can only come out at certain times. Each Splinter has a trigger and when it's pressed the dark side takes over for one scene at which point it must do everything in its power to commit murder before their time is up. Splinters don't have the luxury of planning; they kill on the whims of their psychosis. After the deed is done the Hyde has the remainder of the scene to cover up the deed from the law or their other half, but some Splinters relinquish control early and let their better half take care of the cover up.

                            Splits
                            Alternate Split Talent                             – Who is this Creature that I See: Splits are two different people, literally. When either personality takes over they physically transform. The Jekyll and Hyde can look almost identical or as different as a mother and son, but they're always recognizable as different people and always have a strong family resemblance.
                            What this means in practical terms is that Splits are highly resistant to forensic profiling. Though genetic analysis reveals a family connection it also clearly points away from the innocent personality, fingerprints say likewise. What's more the Jekyll is the original, so the Hyde is created fresh with no presence on any database. This of course can look odd but in the World of Darkness most law enforcement will accept flimsy explanations in favor of a supernatural transformation.
                            This transformation effect extends beyond the body by a few meters. If there's a few hairs on the jacket the Split is wearing when he transforms that get left behind at the scene of the crime, those will have transformed too.
                            If a Split develops further Talents, they only apply while their darker side is in control.
                            Alternate Split Frailty – Free: A Splinter was created as an excuse to commit the crimes the Jekyll couldn't. A Split is free from its creator, its own murderous person. The two halves hate each other. Each side has three triggers (one of which comes from A Warning Light Glimmering In Red). When one of the dormant personalities’ triggers is pressed, roll Resolve vs. Resolve. If the dormant personality wins control is reversed.
                            While Splits start with identical Resolve one side may raise Resolve above its counterpart. This imbalance is psychological unhealthy for the weaker party and a Persistent Condition should be inflicted as a result. If the Hyde gains a Talent that boosts his Resolve a more severe Persistent Condition should be chosen, one likely to cause trouble for both personalities.

                            I know that I added them in but I don't remember where they came from or who they were by. Will still add these as alternate Talents and Frailties for Strangers/Transcendent and updated Talents for Predators/Beasts.

                                                Sent over. Predator/Beast has been revised and Stranger/Transcendent have their Alternates. Also wrote up a different Scourge Undertaking for Minders. Here are the Collectors.



                                                Collector (Scourge)
                                                ‘You are just so…beautiful.”

                                                While a Minder entraps their victims and subjects them to severe mental and physical torment, they do acknowledge that their victims must, in time, leave them to make it out in the world. Minders cling to their victims because they care or try to care or want to care; they love their ward and their wellbeing is ultimately their goal (and if their ward passes, they will move on to the next). For all that they do, Minders still view their victims as human beings that deserve to be treated as such.

                                                For a Collector, they view their victims as objects: dolls, works of art, treasures. A Collector seeks out their victims without the twisted selflessness of a Minder, only focused on their own wants and desires. They abduct their victims because they needed a blonde to act as a sister in their decaying family dinner or because they have the perfect body structure to pose as The Thinker. The void in their life aches to be filled and they are more than willing to search out for that missing piece.

                                                While Minders use control as a means to gain authority, Collectors use control in order to enforce their idea of perfection. Everything about their victims is altered in order to bring their view out into reality. One Collector bashes in the kneecaps of an older woman in order to make her the spitting image of their grandmother, forced into a wheelchair and all. Another abducts conjoined twins in order create a “museum of the strange”, immobilizing them within a twisted display case.

                                                Eventually, a Collector’s collection will start to break apart. Victims run, bodies decay, or maybe the Collector just saw fit to break their collection itself because there was just one thing that was off about it. However it happens, the Collector will be spurred to search for that next piece. And this time, they will make sure that their collection is perfect.

                                                Background: The main driving forces for a Collector are their obsessive thoughts and their want for completion. Whether they are haunted by memories of a broken house and smiling dolls or their desire to express their morbid taste in aesthetics, Collectors spend their days observing those around them like a mantis ready to snatch up its prey.

                                                Collectors often operate in periods; a successful abduction and efficient means of preserving their victims means that they may go from months to years without actively hunting. When they do go off on their hunt, they have a type that they are on a look out for. Thick arms with a shoulder tattoo, sunny red hair and skin kissed with freckles, a Collector’s select target seldom differs from previous targets.

                                                Minders that prefer to immobilize their victims have a tendency to become Collectors; the ease of maintaining a still victim allows these Minders a greater degree of control that Collectors enjoy. The lack of response when interacting with the victims may even fuel the delusions that haunt Collectors. The key point in which a Minder begins to turn is when they realize that having only one is not as satisfying as having another, the indulgence is what causes their descent.

                                                Notably, Collectors often anthropomorphize their victims even after their death, playing out skits or having intense arguments. They reenact memories of a close, loving family or act out fantasies of protecting their loved ones. They dance with nobles and parade around sculptures, each victim playing the part of a background character to the Collector’s grand delusion. To those uninitiated to the works of the Collector, these scenes may even seem as real to them as they are to the Collector.

                                                Inspirations: Sander Cohen (Bioshock), Doctor X (The Cat Lady), Professor Henry Jarrod (House of Wax)

                                                Attributes: Manipulation or Composure

                                                Undertaking Skills: Crafts, Investigation, Medicine, Brawl, Weaponry, Empathy, Expression, Persuasion, Socialization, Subterfuge

                                                Talent – Real to Me: Collectors have immersed themselves deeply into their delusion, seeing a world that exists only in their minds. However, that world empowers them, feeding their fantasy and fueling their actions. When a Collector is within the same location (roughly an average building or a small open field) with at least one of their victims, they can bring part of their delusion into the real world. The Collector may position their victims as if their victims were alive and capable of movement. This only occurs if the bodies are unnoticed but may occur even if the Collector cannot physically manipulate the victim or is otherwise indisposed.
                                                Furthermore, if the Collector can visibly see at least one of his victims, they become emboldened, working with the strength and ability of many. The Collector’s physical rolls are treated as if they had benefitted from teamwork (roll Manipulation + Composure once for the teamwork bonus).

                                                Frailty – My Strange Obsession: A Collector’s mind is firmly rooted in the strength of their collection; there is nothing more important than maintaining their collection. Collectors are affected with the Fugue Persistent Condition with the trigger being a new addition to their collection. When the Collector sees a target that matches up with a motif from their collection, they trigger Fugue and automatically fail the Condition. They become locked in their daze until either the end of the scene, until they lose sight of their target, or they are attacked.
                                                This obsession extends to the safety of their collection; though their reaction is on the opposite side of the spectrum. When a Collector notices that their Collection has been tampered with, they fly into a frenzy, gaining the Fixated Condition with the command being “Inflict hell on the person that damaged my collection”. If the tampering occurs outside of the Collector’s notice or is due to a natural or otherwise nonhuman occurrence, the Collector directs their fury towards the next living person they meet.

                                                VASCU Case Study: Prince Charming
                                                Transcribed from a letter found on the body of one Melissa Poe, 23. Poe was dressed in a custom-fit evening gown and tiara when police found her body at a downtown park. Contents of the letter appear to be the same as the Charming case 7 years ago. Sent to evidence for processing.

                                                "My Dearest Angel,

                                                When you failed to wake on that soft Tuesday morning I had feared that the spectre of death had come to take you away from me. I pressed my ear against your chest to once again hear your beating heart but it was nothing more than the silence that keeps me up at night. My darling Angel, I watched each sunbeam fall onto your cold cheek as your body hung limp in my arms. Why, why did the spectre choose you to take?

                                                "But with a kiss, you stirred in my arms and I felt the weight of your endless joy in your body, my sweet! Our Love! Our Love is what brought you back to the waking world! I knew you were weak from your sleep but I drew your bath as you sang those beautiful words that you spoke to me those many years. Though, such words could only come second to your radiance.

                                                "My Angel, I write to you to remind you of the memories we have made. To remind you of the stars that graced us with their light and allowed me to bask in your alabaster glow. To remind you of the days we would stroll through the garden and tease the thought of one day eloping. To remind you of the time we shared dancing through the halls of my soon-to-be maison. To remind you of all that when you run from me once again. But, as always, I shall remain here, faithful, until the day you run back into my arms.

                                                "Til that day comes, I wish you safety.

                                                "Farewell,

                                                "Your Prince Charming"
