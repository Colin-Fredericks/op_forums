        It is in the nature of a conspiracy to hide themselves from view. Part of the reason they don't use what power they have to blow the lid off the supernatural and get the whole planet in on the Vigil is because doing so would also expose them to the monsters that rule the universe.

        I'd argue that part of the reason they survive is because they have been subtly compromised to one degree or another. Just enough that one supernatural patron or another finds them useful to have around and helps shield them from other outside meddling. This is emphatically 'not' the same thing as control though. These are organizations that melt into the paperwork and live in forgotten institutional folkways. Their bureaucratic autonomy is not easily suborned and regulatory capture remains an insidious factor.

                    A couple of answers:

                    1. Sometimes they don't. Sometimes the enemy wins and dismantles the organization. However...

                    2. This is harder than it might seem just by looking at pure supernatural advantage. Human groups have some organizational advantages, and most importantly, aren't as endangered by blowing the masquerade; overt counterattacks by, say, vampires, at a bunch of government employees engaged in classified work would attract a lot of attention. It does the Invictus no good to shut down Task Force Valkyrie if World War Vampire is the result. The supernatural communities are mostly balkanized, they have huge incentives to stay under anyone's radar, and they've already got investments in keeping the local masquerade afloat. As powerful as a given city's prince is, he might not have any ability to counterattack against Valkyrie in Washington DC. So he can maybe take out the cell bothering his city, but another might well show up later. Coordinating the destruction of a major hunter conspiracy would take a major supernatural conspiracy, and those groups can't always get that kind of thing organized.

                    3. Look at it from the supernatural perspective. Say City X has a really good cell of hunters apparently backed by the government (for example). They've taken heavy casualties and call for backup. Does a neighboring group (coterie, cabal, etc) risk themselves and risk conflict with an evidently dangerous group of mortals? Some of the supernaturals will be risking their immortality, or their obsessed investigations into a local mystery, to stick their neck out like that. Even the groups dedicated to defensive actions (the Invictus, etc) have limited resources and information. So the full on counter-attack is just as likely as, say, a political accommodation or tacit coordination or avoidance or alliance. And all the supernaturals have problems of their own. "I've got three good killers I can use to strike back at these mortals," says the Invictus shot-caller in Baltimore, "But if I lose any of them the Carthians will strike while we're weak...I can use the Sheriff as well, but I've got him tracking down reports of Strix..."

                    4. It's not all that easy to take down a hunter group using cell structure, compartmentalization, security precautions. They'll know their enemy's main weaknesses and capabilities, some of them have supernatural tricks up their sleeves as well, conspiracies are well distributed enough to rebuild themselves even after heavy losses, etc.

                    5. But, basically, there's no guarantee a given conspiracy survives. They're not immune. Some die out, others emerge, the Vigil continues. They win some battles, they lose others. Supernatural groups are at least as vulnerable; there's nothing guaranteeing the Invictus is still around in a hundred years, or the Pentacle survives the Great Seer Attack of 2035, or any werewolves live long enough to find a cure for the weaponized viral idigam engineered by Cheiron and put into popular energy drinks.

                                    Honestly I think it comes down to a number of different factors such as:

                                    1. Conspiracies tend to have a fair amount of resources with which they can hide some of their activities. Yeah that werewolf may have been hunted down and killed but does anyone really know if they can't find the body/murder scene?

                                    2. Many supernaturals have this idea that they are at the top or near top of the food chain and don't consider the idea of a group of humans waging a coordinated war against them very plausible.

                                    3. Sure some supernaturals get killed but what proof do their peers have that this was done by a large scale group of humans with wide ranging power across the globe? More than likely they think the killers are a local group of pissed off or extremely deviant humans (the latter being the most likely conclusion when the Abbey rolls into town for a night of "fun") thus creating a weird kind of smokescreen for the larger, usually more cautious groups.

                                    4. Many conspiracies seem to have this here today, gone tomorrow MO. They're basically Mulder and Scully coming in, dealing with a particularly obvious problem, and then leaving right afterwards so while they do rack up a body count it's across large areas and given that most supernatural groups don't necessarily communicate across long distances very often it's not likely they'd put two and two together.

                                    5. Some conspiracies are likely to operate using a public front. Very few werewolves, vampires, etc would suspect that the Cheiron Group that just set up the factory downtown actually has a secret level in the building for processing their most interesting "acquisitions" especially if they disappear without a trace.

                                    6. Some conspiracies might be suspected to exist but no evidence can be found or they're obfuscated by myths and misdirection. TFV pretty much can hide behind the men in black nonsense to some extent despite the fact that some supernaturals suspect that the government probably has some kind of monster buster unit because it cleaves a little too close to what the nutcases keep saying. Many might hear whispers that the Inquisition is alive and well but most would dismiss it as an unproven conspiracy theory. Many would outright dismiss the origin and motives of the Lucifuge even if someone respected brought up the idea just because it sounds so crazy.

                                                        To clarify my point which was I admit rushed and a bit lacking in detail many supernaturals cultural beliefs and accepted truths can end up working in the conspiracy's favor. Vampires and mages have been known to get it into their heads that they are far superior to humans. Werewolves will deal with humans when necessary but often try to keep to the periphery. Changelings tend to fear their old masters more than they do humans and put their focus elsewhere. Promotheans are still trying to figure out what it means to be human and may not have discovered the idea of a herd mentality yet. Many of these groups also live with the general idea that they live in the shadows, going under the radar, and that the most massive breaches are generally dealt with because if they don't humanity will rise like a tide to smother them all. Add to that the more "mundane" and plausible explanations the supernatural can come to and the idea they hit upon there being a giant human conspiracy gunning for all supernaturals is far less likely to occur.

                                                        To look at your example let's say a vampire is attack by a group of well armed and knowledgeable humans. They could be ghouls, blood dolls, smucks on the street hired by a rival, a small group of people victimized by vampires and out for revenge, etc. All of these are more plausible than them being part of a crack global strike force out for the vampire and all of its ilk. Similarly for werewolves they could just be people who are spirit possessed and for changelings they could be the fair folk in disguise. Point being your immediate focus is on what you are familiar with rather than diverging into the more outlandish. Keep in mind that conspiracies are generally not amateurs and don't make the usual mistakes as Anachronistic pointed out and often set up ways of misdirecting prey to one of the more "obvious" conclusions rather than to there doorstep (or set things up so if the supernatural does make it to their doorstep it has no hope of escape).

                                                        Finally again as Anachronistic pointed out there are probably some supernaturals that have found out about a given conspiracy and haven't outed them yet. This usually takes two forms and the supernatural either A: Tries to subtly manipulate the organization so as to avoid exposure and to benefit itself in some way such as sending them after rivals or B: Realizes that if news of a large scale, well equipped, coordinated, but not omniscient human effort at the extermination of them and their ilk is known it will cause panic that would only serve to make things worse including possibly ripping the veil from all the sheeples' eyes. In the latter case they may try to subtly subvert the organization trying to weaken it without getting caught while doing their best to spread disinformation about the group's existence to avoid the panic.
