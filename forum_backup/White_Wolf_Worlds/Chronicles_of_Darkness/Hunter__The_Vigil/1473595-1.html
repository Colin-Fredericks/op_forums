                        Les Mysteres

                        Well, here comes a all time fan favorite- the Spirit Emissaries!

                        (I was sarcastic. Please don't shoot me)

                        Now, I personally have no problem with the Les Mysteres- but from what it looks like, the fandom in general does not seem to be very fond of those guys, saying the least. Between them being "duped" by the spirits, how they are literally on the wrong side of the field in terms of the Pure-Forsaken conflict, they very fractured society and the amount of nonsense that their members believe in, I can understand why people may have some dislike towards them. As the only conspiracy that originally got the axe during 2e Kickstarter by being briefly mentioned to be dead and gone (something which was removed in the final draft- and no, Merrick doesn't count because that line made NO SENSE, was never clarified during the Kickstarter and was thankfully removed considering that, again, it made NO SENSE), it is perhaps an indicator that maybe even Hunter's 2e team wasn't too fond of them (again, we should remember that the Abbey was on the verge of getting the axe or turn full blown Cabal as well- and I have some sense of pride in taking a active stance against it during the discussion which may have kept them as a playable faction).

                        Wait- where was I?

                        Ah, yeah, people does not seem to like the Mysteres.

                        And that seems to be the situation also in setting.

                        I mean, hunters generally fear compromise more than anything else- and here we have a conspiracy which does not only admit being compromised, they practically celebrate it. Getting ridden by the spirits and serving them is viewed as a good thing. They only attack monsters which try and abuse the spirits- and in their eyes, it means anyone who stops the two worlds from melding together. That's... not a good thing. Like, really- the averaged spirit in the CofD is a psychopath who just seeks to increase their preview and gain Essence out of it at any cost. I still remember Chris Allens' example of a hospital spirit that wants more sick and injured people so they would need medical treatment. Spirits, as a whole, make no sense whatsoever for the common human mind. They are hungry, alien, terrifying creatures- and the Mysteres serve them? What the quack? Why would anyone in their right mind willingly surrender their body, mind and soul to such selfish and twisted entities which we know to abuse people at the first chance they get if they are left unchecked, and attack those who actually protect humans from their predatory tendencies?

                        Because maybe, just maybe, it is the right thing to do.

                        First, we need to remember that Spirit Slayers was written under the framework of Werewolf 1e- which means that a lot of the way the Uratha are treated there is irrelevant. We no longer have spirit cops, but the Wolf Must Hunt. Spirits are actually more terrifying in 2e, IMO, but the Uratha are less "we must keep the Shadow and the Flesh apart" and more about, well, not being crazy m*********rs which outright support it or do other crazy stuff. It is more about spiritual harmony and fulfilling the role of the primordial God of the Hunt- something which the Pure actively forsake (haha) as they seek after a primordial "state of grace" where the Spirit and the Flesh are one. Basically, the spirit world is less "I Hate the Forsaken", even though the Pure do have more spirit allies, I think. I mean, the Pure are far more tied to Hisil as when you reap your Auspice out, something else comes to fill the void- but I did once wondered how those actions, in a way, makes the Pure and their Firstborn more magath like- they sacrifice their definitions to become more open in what they can hunt, which again is 100% spiritual insanity. In short, the Forsaken have became closer to Hisil, while the Pure became even closer, and yet all of them became much more crazy and occult (especially with how lodges are basically cults).

                        Now- does any of that change anything for the Mysteres?

                        Not really, honestly.

                        I mean- sure, the Uratha are less human and more integrated to Hisil in 2e than in 1e- but it is still not Pangaea. It is still not that perfect melding of flesh and spirit- and thankfully, it can never be. Not as long as the God of the Hunt is dead. But the fact is that with spirits being less "we all hate the Forsaken", there is a fundamental difference here when we consider the Mysteres- there is no spiritual consensus of "evil werewolves who keep the world apart". There is the Wolf Must Hunt and the Pure who hates them and have far more members and reach (and again, we can't ignore the fact that the Fire Touched are in better relationship with the Shadow than, well, probably anyone. Those are crazy bastards after all). This is important because we need to understand that even in 1e, the whole "the spirits keep the Mysteres alive" was kinda a shaky logic. I mean, sure, we had tons of spirits who really hated the Forsaken, but considering how predatory Hisil is it felt... weird that those entities can coordinate something like the Mysteres. No, a faction of spirits makes more sense, but even if we say "the Pure spirit allies are those who keep the conspiracy together", there is still something to account for- that spirits are not nice. They can and will abuse people if and when they can. They want control. They want power. They want EVERYTHING- but the Mysteres don't give them everything. The Rites du Cheval, for whatever reason it may be, allow the spirits to ride their flesh and posses them while still leaving the hunter in control. That is not the norm- that's the exception, and that is important. Because if that's true, than perhaps the Mysteres are not as dupes as we might think they are.

                        Let's start from the beginning. Like, as the very, very beginning.

                        The Sundered World.

                        It is easy to miss it, actually. It is merely a small section in the whole Vinca society as it was imagined in that Dark Era. There, among the many different roles which people may take upon themselves during those dark, dark times, we have a short mention of a role- but not a true role. It is generally a temporary act- serving as a priest for the old gods. In there, we can see what could easily be missed but definitely is the first instance of a Spirit Emissary- a person of any walks of life wearing the sacred mask and reaching out to the spirit world, bargaining with them on the benefit of their people. Some got Claimed, some got killed, but at its essence, what we have is everything that the conspiracy is all about- people who are willing to endanger their lives in order help their communities, being a bridge between the Flesh and Spirit. That role is so fundamental that it just makes sense to be there- yet for the Mysteres it is also something important to keep in mind. That those people have existed before the Forsaken and the Pure fell into a blood feud. Heck, before the world was even broken. When the spirits still roamed the world freely, they were there- and they were hunters. They had their own Vigil- putting their lives on the line in order to protect their communities against the horrors of the night, yet instead of killing those entities, they viewed them as gods which had to be appeased. Instead of using violence, they turned to bargain.

                        And that, in its core, what this conspiracy is all about. Bargain with the Old Gods of the Shadow, in order to keep their people safe. And that, in my opinion, is very Vigilante.

                        We have traces of this concept even in the modern organization- as it says, everyone can be a Mystere. No one is keeping the magic hidden- and yet, when the common person gets Ridden by the spirits, they don't become hunters. Heck, generally it does not end well for them at all. More than that, spirits are generally very, very jealous for their Ridden, as they are Essence generators. Considering how fractured the Mysteres are, there must be some unifying factor which keeps them together- again, those are the spirits, but in that end we should indeed ask ourselves how and why in Hell those spirits have decided to share their Ridden, and only possess them when there is an agreement between the sides. All of that makes me think that the Mysteres are not as dupes as it may be- because the very fact they have control, the fact that they are emissaries and not slaves and that their existence has been around for, well, eternity, yet only recently they can actually be defined as a conspiracy, all of that makes me think about what could have been the reason that the Mysteres are what they are, and not one of the many sad tragedies which spirit cults generally turn into.

                        Also, did you noticed that once again we have monsters who through the Vigil maintain their humanity? Makes you see a recurring theme here, don't you think?

                        Back to the topic- while the Mysteres are all about syncretism and many different versions for the truth and such, it is impossible to ignore the role of Voodoo in the formation of the group as a conspiracy. I mean, again, we always HAD spirit emissaries- but the Mysteres are something a bit different, due to the control they have over the possession. We can see it in the system of the Crossroads which they formalized in order to avoid spiritual manipulation. We can see it in the names of their Endowments. Heck, we can see it in their own name. But most importantly, we can see it in their history- for while the Mysteres have always existed, the origin of the group as a organization came from West Africa, where there was a community of local shamans which have established what looks like the first form of the conspiracy- one which got shattered during the trans Atlantic slave trade. And that is where things changed- because those shamans had to preserve their rites somehow in order to let them work. Centuries of traditions getting cut and broken and something needs to fill the missing parts, especially as those who took them from their lands have also tried to break their original belief system. And in order to keep it alive, in order to keep it going, in order to maintain who they were- they filled the pieces with whatever they found, with things which resembled their original faith, not the same yet pointing in the same direction. Voodoo was formed when native religions got folded into Christianity- and those shamans have used the same methods in order to complete their rites. Syncretism was the way to keep the magic going- and perhaps, it was also the way to free the spirit emissaries from the gods they were forced to serve.

                        The core aspect of the Mysteres is that truth can only be understood through syncretism. The spirit world is so wide and strange that people can't just use one belief system in order to describe it- but every one get their own experience, get some things right, and by taking those parts which make sense and tying them together, something more true can form... freedom through the lack of definition. While the act of bargaining with the spirits was always an aspect of the spirit emissaries, the Mysteres changed things by embracing syncretism, having spirits which connect them all across the world by being willing to accept different faith because in the end, everything leads to the same truth. Voodoo was the basis for the Mysteres, and the fact that it is the most well known name for the conspiracy it definitely still is- it is very likely that Haiti and Brazil serve as the most unified part of the conspiracy, even though they membership includes other chapters all around the world. The fact here is that the Mysteres choose to be left undefined, and that their rites are all a part of a vast system which accepts everything and rejects nothing as long as it leads to truth, as long as it serves the Vigil, no matter the contradictions.

                        And that is very much the antithesis for the spirit world.

                        Spirits like to have things defined. They like things which have meaning. Resonance describes what makes sense for them to eat and what doesn't- and yet here you have a group that has some very close ties to the spirit world from one hand, yet embraces the multitude of definition. And I think that this is the key to understand the conspiracy- that by pure chance, they discovered the method where the shaman does not only bargain with the spirits for power, but keeps their own will during the possession. And that is because that by syncretism, they dilute the Resonance which ties them and the spirit together. In a way, just like the shamans of old, they are putting a mask in order to shield themselves from the spirits- yet this time, the mask traps the spirit. They define it according to their own system, and force to to be defined according to the will of the hunter, and that methodology makes sure that while the spirit will possess the hunter, it would not take control. Not because the spirit is nice, not because it wants to, because it can't. Because it has nothing to grasp upon. The Rites du Cheval are not as mixed to the point of a magath- but it is the same direction. The same direction as the Pure.

                        Ha, you see what I did here? IT WAS ALL CONNECTED ALL ALONG

                        While the spirits which serve the Pure are not magaths in general, the fact that both the Pure and their Firstborn are less defined and as such become more free may very well explain why the spirit emissaries are on their side instead of the Forsaken- not much about "free the spirits", but something more metaphysical. In a way, the Mysteres are as "Pure" as humans may be, establishing a connection with Hisil based around not being well defined, and allowing the Shadow to fill the voids which miss in the formula. That passive control means that the Mysteres are not some lost souls in the grasp of the spirit world- but that they do know what they are doing. And even if the organization as a whole is unaware of why their rituals work, why anyone which hears the call of the spirits can cross the boundary between the worlds and remain unscathed, there is probably some understanding among those groups which act as the core of the organization (again, probably the Afro American chapter) for how syncretism serves as a protection mechanism, together with the system of bargains and masks. Anyone can be a Mysteres- if they can accept that the truth is wider than they can imagine, that all belief systems are valid and that by accepting all stories it is possible to patch out the true form of the world. And yeah, I am sure that the common person can truly accept that they are both right and wrong in the same time, and can totally accept when other people act in a way which they view as heresy. Because here is the thing- in order to be a Mysteres, you need to truly believe that you are right, and accept that others are as right as you are. You need to accept that Something Is There, and that you don't have all the answers. That there is a balance between truth and faith, and that faith leads to truth no matter the details. That antithesis for the spirit world is the way that the Mysteres can extract their will upon its denizens- in a way which could be more insidious than we can imagine.

                        I mean, if we accept that through syncretism the Mysteres can maintain their control over the spirit world, and that spirits draw upon Resonance- what if the syncretic form of the Rites works as some form of a spiritual virus? An imprint of the hunter's will upon the spirit, formed as part of being Ridden and the bargain they make. And that syncretic imprint could modify the spirit a bit in order to make it fit into the system where all truths must be accepted and concepts must be diluted. And that spirit, now imprinted by the hunter, now leaves the bond at the end of the possession- but the imprint remains. And when a different hunter on the other side of the continent communes with the spirits in order to gain their help, they leave upon them the same imprint as the first spirit. And only spirit which carries the same imprint becomes a vessel through the syncretic network, allowing the imprints to commune with one another, transfer information and in a way, "possess" the spirit in order to make it into the form which the Mysteres call upon. That would unite the conspiracy's spirit allies as unaware carriers of a strange, almost magathic Essence which would allow two different entities across the world to momentarily become the same entity, transferring information across the globe for disparate communities all united through syncretism. A Purification of some sort. A magath of some sort. Not one spirit composed out of many different Resonances, but singles archetypes, the Loa, which are scattered among countless different pieces, countless different spirits, each of them being its own thing yet still part of a greater whole. Unity through syncretism.

                        And that could serve as a good foundation for the conspiracy's endgame- if they knew it even existed.

                        Here is the thing- we know what the Mysteres want: to unite all the worlds together. They want Flesh and Spirits to becomes one, to bring Pangaea back to life, yet they still want to protect their communities, which is why they don't surrender themselves to the spirits. That formulation of syncretic Essence across the globe may be the key for this- creating the greater Truth which would possess the Shadow, create a bridge between mortals and their gods by keeping humanity in control. A world where everyone is right, everyone can safely bargain with the spirits and everyone can coexist along one another. A world where the Flesh is bound to Spirit, yet the Shadow is much less defined, much more magath like, much more.. Pure. By binding Hisil through the syncretic network, by connecting into it the greater of gods and ties them to human will through bargains, it may be done- but the Mysteres are not aware of it, and as such they can't initiate it by themselves. Sure, some of the older and more unified chapters (again, Caribbean and South America) may have some concept of that potential, but that would require some decent coordination which may very well mean breaking some Mysteres cells from the network. However, it could be that the Mysteres don't actually need to do anything- the imprints and the Loa which rise from them probably start to unknowingly work towards that goal by themselves. They seek to spread, to manifest themselves through every spirit and any part of of the Shadow- and could very well be the ones to motivate the hunters in turn to keep going, pushing them to make more bargains, to propagate the Loa, ton unify more spirits through diversity. The Forsaken, of course, will be very much against such magath demigods- which could explain why the spirit allies so much motivate them to attack the Uratha. It is not about keeping the worlds apart. It is about stopping those spirits which seek to become a channel to the mysteries from taking over the Shadow. And the worse thing? That it can't actually be stopped. The Mysteres are everywhere, the Loa are already here. You'll have to kill every Mystere and every spirit which carries a Loa imprint when the hunters and the spirits are not even aware that they are a Mystere and a imprint. And even if you'll manage to find and purge them all, people would just return to speak with the spirits again later, and people would then start sharing their ideas and connect the dots and leave syncretic imprints over the Shadow- because that's the natural course of things.

                        The Mysteres are inevitable. They just don't know it.

                        Well, that's it for the Big Three! Next time we are moving to Mortal Remains. Hope you liked it!

And that is where things changed- because those shamans had to preserve their rites somehow in order to let them work. Centuries of traditions getting cut and broken and something needs to fill the missing parts, especially as those who took them from their lands have also tried to break their original belief system. And in order to keep it alive, in order to keep it going, in order to maintain who they were- they filled the pieces with whatever they found, with things which resembled their original faith, not the same yet pointing in the same direction. Voodoo was formed when native religions got folded into Christianity- and those shamans have used the same methods in order to complete their rites. Syncretism was the way to keep the magic going- and perhaps, it was also the way to free the spirit emissaries from the gods they were forced to serve.

The spirit world is so wide and strange that people can't just use one belief system in order to describe it- but every one get their own experience, get some things right, and by taking those parts which make sense and tying them together, something more true can form... freedom through the lack of definition. While the act of bargaining with the spirits was always an aspect of the spirit emissaries, the Mysteres changed things by embracing syncretism, having spirits which connect them all across the world by being willing to accept different faith because in the end, everything leads to the same truth. Voodoo was the basis for the Mysteres, and the fact that it is the most well known name for the conspiracy it definitely still is- it is very likely that Haiti and Brazil serve as the most unified part of the conspiracy, even though they membership includes other chapters all around the world. The fact here is that the Mysteres choose to be left undefined, and that their rites are all a part of a vast system which accepts everything and rejects nothing as long as it leads to truth, as long as it serves the Vigil, no matter the contradictions.

Resonance describes what makes sense for them to eat and what doesn't- and yet here you have a group that has some very close ties to the spirit world from one hand, yet embraces the multitude of definition. And I think that this is the key to understand the conspiracy- that by pure chance, they discovered the method where the shaman does not only bargain with the spirits for power, but keeps their own will during the possession. And that is because that by syncretism, they dilute the Resonance which ties them and the spirit together. In a way, just like the shamans of old, they are putting a mask in order to shield themselves from the spirits- yet this time, the mask traps the spirit. They define it according to their own system, and force to to be defined according to the will of the hunter, and that methodology makes sure that while the spirit will possess the hunter, it would not take control. Not because the spirit is nice, not because it wants to, because it can't. Because it has nothing to grasp upon. The Rites du Cheval are not as mixed to the point of a magath- but it is the same direction. The same direction as the Pure.

In a way, the Mysteres are as &quot;Pure&quot; as humans may be, establishing a connection with Hisil based around not being well defined, and allowing the Shadow to fill the voids which miss in the formula. That passive control means that the Mysteres are not some lost souls in the grasp of the spirit world- but that they do know what they are doing. Anyone can be a Mysteres- if they can accept that the truth is wider than they can imagine, that all belief systems are valid and that by accepting all stories it is possible to patch out the true form of the world. And yeah, I am sure that the common person can truly accept that they are both right and wrong in the same time, and can totally accept when other people act in a way which they view as heresy. Because here is the thing- in order to be a Mysteres, you need to truly believe that you are right, and accept that others are as right as you are. You need to accept that Something Is There, and that you don't have all the answers. That there is a balance between truth and faith, and that faith leads to truth no matter the details. That antithesis for the spirit world is the way that the Mysteres can extract their will upon its denizens- in a way which could be more insidious than we can imagine.

An imprint of the hunter's will upon the spirit, formed as part of being Ridden and the bargain they make. And that syncretic imprint could modify the spirit a bit in order to make it fit into the system where all truths must be accepted and concepts must be diluted. And that spirit, now imprinted by the hunter, now leaves the bond at the end of the possession- but the imprint remains. And when a different hunter on the other side of the continent communes with the spirits in order to gain their help, they leave upon them the same imprint as the first spirit. And only spirit which carries the same imprint becomes a vessel through the syncretic network, allowing the imprints to commune with one another, transfer information and in a way, &quot;possess&quot; the spirit in order to make it into the form which the Mysteres call upon. That would unite the conspiracy's spirit allies as unaware carriers of a strange, almost magathic Essence which would allow two different entities across the world to momentarily become the same entity, transferring information across the globe for disparate communities all united through syncretism. A Purification of some sort. A magath of some sort. Not one spirit composed out of many different Resonances, but singles archetypes, the Loa, which are scattered among countless different pieces, countless different spirits, each of them being its own thing yet still part of a greater whole. Unity through syncretism.

That formulation of syncretic Essence across the globe may be the key for this- creating the greater Truth which would possess the Shadow, create a bridge between mortals and their gods by keeping humanity in control. A world where everyone is right, everyone can safely bargain with the spirits and everyone can coexist along one another. A world where the Flesh is bound to Spirit, yet the Shadow is much less defined, much more magath like, much more.. Pure. By binding Hisil through the syncretic network, by connecting into it the greater of gods and ties them to human will through bargains, it may be done- but the Mysteres are not aware of it, and as such they can't initiate it by themselves. You'll have to kill every Mystere and every spirit which carries a Loa imprint when the hunters and the spirits are not even aware that they are a Mystere and a imprint. And even if you'll manage to find and purge them all, people would just return to speak with the spirits again later, and people would then start sharing their ideas and connect the dots and leave syncretic imprints over the Shadow- because that's the natural course of things.

                                                    Faithful of Shulpae

                                                    Ha, yes, those guys.

                                                    One of the things that I like to say about hunter that no organization is for everyone. We should remember that when the CofD was still the nWoD, Hunter took a somewhat "out of the box" method by not only giving you different Tiers of organizations to which your character could belong do, it also actively encouraged you to make new ones- something which was less supported in other gamelines where the number of Y splats was much more rigid both mechanically and conceptually. As such, Hunter gave a much wider navigation reach to explore new compacts and conspiracies, which is why we ended up having 40+ orgs in Hunter- which is way beyond any other Y splat from any other gameline (you could argue that it is because Hunter lacks a Z splat, but again, that's still out of the box compared to the rest of the gamelines during that time). As such, when you have so much variety of organizations, it is almost guaranteed that you would like some groups and dislike others, for variety of reasons- and for me, the Faithful of Shulpae are found in the "dislike" pile.

                                                    Now, I am not saying that they are not appropriate to the setting or that they are not hunters- but I have some... issues with their Endowment. I mean, yeah, I am a huge fan of the Abbey doing horrible things to monsters because "monsters don't count", but actually eating other people, even if they are monstrous, just doesn't sit well in me. However, I do think that they make sense within the setting, and use themes which are appropriate to their main antagonist- which is more than the original reception that they had among some of the fans, especially Mummy fans.

                                                    Now, this is a thing that Hunter gets ALL THE TIME, especially during the early days of the game, and which it still gets from some fans of other gamelines- very much like the "anti magic lobotomy" thing that Mage hunters hate (nevermind that the concept actually came from Mage but that's another topic), every time that Hunter goes out of the box and allows Hunters to do things which are not "allowed" by other gamelines, some fans go into rage over it- and that was true even about the Faithful of Shulpae. Why, you ask? Because the Faithful eats mummies, in for some reason, Mummy 1e have took the wordcount to mention that the flesh of a mummy is inedible for regular beings. While for me it looked like a weird argument considering that the Faithful use magic as part of their Endowments and for me magic solves everything, but some people had a issue with it. Heck, not just "some people"- iirc, one of the writers (or maybe even the devs) has pronounced dislike for the idea, claiming that Mummy's team wasn't consulted with and said "the Mummies they eat are not our Mummies". Which, IMO, is really an overreaction.

                                                    And we should actually be thankful for that overreaction.

                                                    Now, when Hunter does something which another gameline does not accept, there are two methods- either to deny it and go against it with all of your available wordcount (like how Bleach's mangake did his best to drive the anime team into the corner in terms of canon) or try to find a way to integrate it into the wider canon (like almost any other sane mangake does). For reasons we are not going to discuss, Mage took mostly the first path- while Mummy took the second one, for while the original reaction was denial, sometime afterwards we got Sothis Ascends- and in there, we had the Cannibal Hymn, dedicated for King Unas, who was blessed to eat the gods themselves.

                                                    Really, it can be more clear than that, right?

                                                    The history of the Faithful may be somewhat shallow and full of holes, but their origin seems to be very clear- that during the reign of King Unas, his Shuankhsen advisor have manipulated his desire for immortal lives and experimented on him, giving him to eat the flesh of the mummies while using all kinds of strange occult methods, making him less of a human and more like a Lifeless. That should not come as a surprise to anyone- Endowments which allow someone to eat a mummy are, like 95% to come from the Devourer in some way, shape or form, with the last 5% being dedicated for the Prince of 1000 Leaves and the Eater of Names. Now, the interesting thing is that this experiment have worked- King Unas left his own tomb, which says that through the use of the forbidden rituals he have became somewhat of a pseudo-Shuankhsen- which, again, fits the recurring theme of monsters becoming hunters through the use of Endowments.

                                                    It should be stressed, however, that Unas was not, by modern standards, a FoS hunter- for the simple reason he was still not a hunter. He was very much a monster, driven by hunger and unstable powers. Probably he had more in common with a Deviant than with a hunter. However, we do know that by the time of Hawara the followers of King Unas have establish a certain form of a organization- the Maw of Unas, yet instead of using Sekhem Sorcery they have turned to utilizing alchemy instead, targeting Prometheans in order to fulfill their hunger. That is an important part of their history, because it 1) gives them a form of shared rituals and organization and b) opens the ability of devouring monsters into more than just mummies. Sure, at this point at time we are yet to talk about hunters, but we do have a precedence of alchemists becoming hunters in the form of the AO. So taking it all into account, we do have an instance where Sorcery and Alchemy get melded together, united by raw and primordial hunger- something which probably facilitated the formation of the modern Faithful.

What we do know that sometimes after that point, the alchemists and sorcerers of the Maw would have become more and more specialized in self transformation- the use of Sekhem Sorcery in order to draw upon the powers of the Devourer and the use of alchemy in order to transform their own bodies into being capable to not just digest monstrous power, but also to absorb and manifest it within themselves. The fact that they currently name themselves after Shulpae leads us to believe that for whatever reason it may be, the cult have moved its center of operation from Egypt to Mesopotamia, as Shulpae is a lesser known god of the Feast. He also seems to have aspects of warrior god and ruler of the animal kingdom- as well as some connection to Jupiter. Now, this last part is important, because in Babylonian astrology, Jupiter was also connected to Marduk- which, in turn, is connected to the Nibiru and the Vigil as a whole. Does it mean that the FoS are the Nibiru? I doubt it- but it may be a sign that this point in time was when the Faithful actually became hunters- their foul rituals have reached perfection, creating their twisted religion where the gods are to be eaten as worship. It is just that their method of "hunt" is corrupted by the rite of Anthropophagy- which correlates to another concept which I have noticed in hunter. The theme of sins.

A liminal place between hunters and monsters, so to speak.                                                             Faithful of Shulpae

                                                            Ha, yes, those guys.

                                                            One of the things that I like to say about hunter that no organization is for everyone. We should remember that when the CofD was still the nWoD, Hunter took a somewhat &quot;out of the box&quot; method by not only giving you different Tiers of organizations to which your character could belong do, it also actively encouraged you to make new ones- something which was less supported in other gamelines where the number of Y splats was much more rigid both mechanically and conceptually. As such, Hunter gave a much wider navigation reach to explore new compacts and conspiracies, which is why we ended up having 40+ orgs in Hunter- which is way beyond any other Y splat from any other gameline (you could argue that it is because Hunter lacks a Z splat, but again, that's still out of the box compared to the rest of the gamelines during that time). As such, when you have so much variety of organizations, it is almost guaranteed that you would like some groups and dislike others, for variety of reasons- and for me, the Faithful of Shulpae are found in the &quot;dislike&quot; pile.

                                                            Now, I am not saying that they are not appropriate to the setting or that they are not hunters- but I have some... issues with their Endowment. I mean, yeah, I am a huge fan of the Abbey doing horrible things to monsters because &quot;monsters don't count&quot;, but actually eating other people, even if they are monstrous, just doesn't sit well in me. However, I do think that they make sense within the setting, and use themes which are appropriate to their main antagonist- which is more than the original reception that they had among some of the fans, especially Mummy fans.

                                                            Now, this is a thing that Hunter gets ALL THE TIME, especially during the early days of the game, and which it still gets from some fans of other gamelines- very much like the &quot;anti magic lobotomy&quot; thing that Mage hunters hate (nevermind that the concept actually came from Mage but that's another topic), every time that Hunter goes out of the box and allows Hunters to do things which are not &quot;allowed&quot; by other gamelines, some fans go into rage over it- and that was true even about the Faithful of Shulpae. Why, you ask? Because the Faithful eats mummies, in for some reason, Mummy 1e have took the wordcount to mention that the flesh of a mummy is inedible for regular beings. While for me it looked like a weird argument considering that the Faithful use magic as part of their Endowments and for me magic solves everything, but some people had a issue with it. Heck, not just &quot;some people&quot;- iirc, one of the writers (or maybe even the devs) has pronounced dislike for the idea, claiming that Mummy's team wasn't consulted with and said &quot;the Mummies they eat are not our Mummies&quot;. Which, IMO, is really an overreaction.

                                                            And we should actually be thankful for that overreaction.

                                                            Now, when Hunter does something which another gameline does not accept, there are two methods- either to deny it and go against it with all of your available wordcount (like how Bleach's mangake did his best to drive the anime team into the corner in terms of canon) or try to find a way to integrate it into the wider canon (like almost any other sane mangake does). For reasons we are not going to discuss, Mage took mostly the first path- while Mummy took the second one, for while the original reaction was denial, sometime afterwards we got Sothis Ascends- and in there, we had the Cannibal Hymn, dedicated for King Unas, who was blessed to eat the gods themselves.

                                                            Really, it can be more clear than that, right?

                                                            The history of the Faithful may be somewhat shallow and full of holes, but their origin seems to be very clear- that during the reign of King Unas, his Shuankhsen advisor have manipulated his desire for immortal lives and experimented on him, giving him to eat the flesh of the mummies while using all kinds of strange occult methods, making him less of a human and more like a Lifeless. That should not come as a surprise to anyone- Endowments which allow someone to eat a mummy are, like 95% to come from the Devourer in some way, shape or form, with the last 5% being dedicated for the Prince of 1000 Leaves and the Eater of Names. Now, the interesting thing is that this experiment have worked- King Unas left his own tomb, which says that through the use of the forbidden rituals he have became somewhat of a pseudo-Shuankhsen- which, again, fits the recurring theme of monsters becoming hunters through the use of Endowments.

                                                            It should be stressed, however, that Unas was not, by modern standards, a FoS hunter- for the simple reason he was still not a hunter. He was very much a monster, driven by hunger and unstable powers. Probably he had more in common with a Deviant than with a hunter. That is an important part of their history, because it 1) gives them a form of shared rituals and organization and b) opens the ability of devouring monsters into more than just mummies. Sure, at this point at time we are yet to talk about hunters, but we do have a precedence of alchemists becoming hunters in the form of the AO.
