    Getting into Hunter: The Vigil (Hopefully)

    05-07-2014, 12:37 PM

    Hello, everyone.

    I’m a long time GM and player, and my group has more-or-less consisted of the same people for about 25 years. Over the years we’ve mainly stuck to our core game, WFRP1e, with Shadowrun, Call of Cthulhu, Vampire: The Masquerade (we played in the 90s), and some other stuff thrown in in between campaigns.

    Currently, the group would like to do a Call of Cthulhu campaign set in the 1970s. The reasons for this are mainly because of the lack of ease of communication compared to current day (with cellphones, internet, etc.), but also because it’s much more familiar-feeling than, say, the 1920s. It allows the players to feel comfortable with the setting while not streaming what they’re seeing from Google Glass, or whatever.

    At any rate, the group prefers more of the “hunting” aspect, even in CoC. They do enjoy the investigation but like to have the investigation culminate in some sort of climactic battle with them as participants, rather than just being witness to things, and ultimately, their characters going insane.

    Also, they like subtle story development. Many of the published CoC adventures, for example, have Elder Gods showing up, even though the adventures themselves are one-offs. This makes no sense to me, and the group would rather follow a trail to a ghoul cult — unearthing mysteries and tangential plots (part of the fun is finding out whether or not what you’re investigating pertains to what you’re actually after) and have a battle with those forces than, say, seeing something from another dimension; the latter is far less relatable in a sense (yes, I know it’s not supposed to be relatable for the characters, but lack of familiarity isn’t fun for this player group), and summarily, far less impactful.

    The players like playing their characters for a long time, and they like campaigns where they invest lots of time. Most sessions are 10am to 10pm, and we meet about twice a month. It’s less than when were were younger (if we MISSED a weekend about 10 years ago, it was considered a bad month).

    At any rate, I’d really like to run Hunter for this group, as I have had the core book for quite some time. Additionally, I have purchased the updated Armory book, The nWoD core book, and Slashers. Is there anything else that would come in handy?

    In addition to these questions, is there a place I can direct my players to on-line that give a breakdown of the different compacts, etc., and other information about the game? That is, when they ask, “What’s Hunter? How’s it different? What kind of characters are available to play within the Hunter: The Vigil World?” I’d like to be able to direct them somewhere, even if the description is simple; some mystery can be quite helpful.

    Thank you for your time.

          My suggestion is to start by asking if they want to play alone against the world, with a large network of like minded contacts, or as part of a large organisation.

          Then play Tier 1, Loyalists of Thule, or VASCU - since those are the groups that best fit what you've said your group likes; in my humble opinion. Hunter orgs tend to be good ad investigation or combat, but there's nothing explicitly described as good at both, but I think those two will come in closer than others.


          There isn't really much online that I can think of that summarises Hunter. However the keyword I would say is diversity, you can play hunter as anything from manly men with shotguns fighting monsters to a group of teenagers wearing black and trying to become real witches. Every Compact and Conspiracy in the core book is practically a different game all by itself.

                                  If you want your game to become more Cthulhoid than buying the full GMC is not a bad idea. It deals with a giant machine which is trying to regulate everyone's life for reasons I can't tell you, because I would have to kill you. . No really, it's motivations are unknown. Slasher is imo the best book of the nWoD, so good buy on that one. The other books give you more info on either vampires, werewolves and mages/witches. Mortal Remains deals with the rest, changelings, mummies, prometheans (Frankenstein's monsters), demons and other thing I might forget. The Horror recognition guide is a really cool prop, but it's all fluff just like the vampire clanbooks.

                                  If you are mystery focused and you like the toolkit design of the nWoD, you might want to get your hands on Mysterious Places, Urban Legends or any other book out of the blue line. They all need work though. You must make everything your own, because they give mostly story seeds, which need to be fleshed out. I like to use the Book of Spirits, because it adds the entire Spirit World to your games. That books provides you with a setting, plus you can use spirits as an excuse for all the nasty things happening in a setting.

                                  Personally I would recommend getting Compacts and Conspiracies and the setting pdf Block by Bloody Block. Both add useful info and are cheap.

                                          Originally posted by Katsura View Post
                                          I personally appreciated more Damnation City for Requiem than Block By Blood Block, but that's my personal opinion.
                                          I only use the chapters containing the districts, the sites and subjects. It's an unique book, but a lot of words for such a simple concept. In the end you only need a few residential area's (rich, poor, suburbs) an industrial area (or maybe a harbor) and a few commercial area's (both offices and nightlife/food service). You can also use a city model (Hoyt, Burgess, Ullmann) or Sim City 4 (residential, industrial, commercial office, commercial service) for building your city. I found the more robust BbbB method more to my liking.

                                          Just a tip: a good buy is the Haven City of Violence RPG. You can buy the pdf for $2 and it gives you a blueprint of a city covering all bases. It works with 6 district and each district has several neighbourhoods. I found the setup to be pretty much the same as any city there is. It covers all bases. The distinction between district and neighbourhood works really well. You can have a very poor neighbourhood in a very rich district for example. Just like Harlem in Manhattan.
