                    Originally posted by Deionscribe View Post

                    And regarding the Kivrei Tzadikim, are they supposed to be the R.A.M term for Lifeweb oases? And how about the Amkhata? Do they also have a designation of their own, and is there a possibility of lesser examples being made due to Rukh technology?
                    no no- Kivrei Tzadikim is a real thing. It is a religious phenomena in Israel- people earch for the graves of old rabbis and kabbalists in order to lie upon them and ask for wishes. Mostly women who search for an husband. The graves are usually governed by a small groups which works to protect the grave and draw people to it. In the CofD, some of those graves are actually covers for Arisen cults.

                    Amkhata are usually seen as some sort of guardians created by the Pharaonim to protect their relics and tombs using some sort of magic. The hebrew word for guardian (Shomer) is usually used for them. Considering that the R.A.M work to protect people from the negative influence of relics and mummies, and that those beings are objectively an abomination upon reality, they are another dark aspect of the curses formed by the relics of old. So no, the Authority would not make Amkhata even if they know how to.

                                    oh, the Deathless would hate it. The relic isn't destroyed during the process of making a Tselem- it is contained. In a way, it is put into a state similar to a death sleep of the Arisen. As a consequence, the scientists of R.A.M can use the technology to make "downgraded" versions of the contained relic, which are powered as long as the relic is "asleep". Destroying the relic would stop the Tselamim made from it from working. The process is somewhat similar for sacrificing a relic to the Judges, only that the gods of Duat gain nothing from the process. As for the Tselamim themselves, trying to cannibalize them for Sekhem or give them to the Judges would fail- the item would just disintegrate between their hands. A Tselem is not, after all, the original- just a hollow image of the true relic- and you can be sure it would anger the mummy in question. In their eyes, relics are their own heritage, meant for the scions of the Nameless Empire alone. Having them not only stolen by a such a young organization, but also mass produced into versions which any mortal could safely use, is probably their greatest nightmare.

                                            Originally posted by Vent0 View Post
                                            Would Tselem then be extremely useful for Kepher senses in tracking down the source Relic?
                                            ammm I haven't gave much thought about the subject, so let's see-

                                            as written in the Enemy section, the R.A.M are using Rukh technology in order to interfere with the resonance between relics and the Arisen. That means that as long as a relic is kept "asleep", it is practically invisible for passive Kepher senses. Attempting to actively sense a stolen relic would lead the mummy to the nearest Tselem- although they will notice that something is weird, as if the relic is found in a number of places in the same time or that the relic is found both near and far away, or maybe it is like hearing an "echo in a cave". Let the ST decide upon the exact description. Anyway, if the relic does not have an active Tselem, it would be, of course, invisible for Arisen eyes.

                                            Once getting their hands upon the Tselem, the mummy in question would undoubtedly be furious- but there is still hope! Tselamim may not be "real" relics, but are the best "proxies" one can have- after all, they are created from the relic just as the relics were created from the relic maker! After finding a Tselem, the mummy can roll Kepher once more- with only a -2 modifier. However, the disturbances created by Rukh technology are still in effect, reducing the Mummy's dice pool by [6 - Vessel's Rating]. The dice pool can't be reduced lower than 1, of course, so there is still a chance they may discover the relic's location. After the roll, whether it succeed or doesn't, the Tselem is destroyed. Using another Tselem of the same relic would automatically fail- as all of the Tselamim are, in a way, the same item (at least, spiritually speaking). And of course, as the roll is always an active role, the mummy in question needs to knowingly try and use the Tselem as a proxy in order to make the roll, which would require them to actually understand (or at least guess) the nature of the item.
