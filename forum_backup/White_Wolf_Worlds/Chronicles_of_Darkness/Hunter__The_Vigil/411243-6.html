        Originally posted by saibot View Post

        I think the specific reason why they went with the God-Machine instead of Outsiders is that Outsiders in general do not lend themselves well to ongoing stories and are more “monster of the week”.
        Kinda, yeah; but what’s wrong with that? As I see it, that’s one of the main differences between blue-book chronicles and Hunter chronicles: blue-book protagonists haven’t taken up the Vigil; they’re not out watching for the supernatural. Rather, they stumble over something in the dark that will likely kill em if they ignore it. So they deal with whatever monster has forced its way into their lives; and if they survive, they then go back to their regular lives. They aren’t suited for ongoing stories, save only for those cases when the thing they initially stumble over ends up taking an ongoing story to overcome.

                Well, like in Blood and Smoke where you have a mask and dirge, maybe Hunter could reflect the meaning of a double life. A sort of "person" and "calling" division. The "person" highlights who a hunter is a a human being--their friends and family, their jobs and livelihoods, their hobbies and burdens (Perhaps you work as a tax accountant during the day, maybe even work at soup kitchens sometimes). Then you have the "calling", which reflects the your personal vigil--which monsters you seek and why, as well as the organization which you associate with and why, and what your feelings are as a hunter (heading from the soup kitchen you torture a ghoul for the whereabouts of the vampire that controls him)...

                Also, each organization (compact, conspiracy, or other) could represent a certain theme in terms of the vigil. You carry a light in the vast expanse of darkness, you carry the vigil, and for what? Maybe you are in the Ashwood Abbey--which represents the idea of the THRILL of it--you have chosen to fight the darkness because you think it's great fun; because fighting overwhelming odds and facing horrors beyond you wildest imaginings is just your idea of a good time. Maybe you're in Task Force: Valkyrie--which stands for the idea of DUTY--you love your country, and you are duty bound to fight to protect it. Or, maybe you're in the Lucifuge--which represents the idea of CHOICE--you fight the darkness because you choose to. An act of ultimate defiance; destiny does not control you. The blood of the Devil might flow through your veins, but he doesn't own you--it's your choice. Or, maybe your in the Malleus Maleficarum--which represents FANATICISM--you slay monster because they must be slain. All the powers of Hell will fall like wheat before your Scythe, and you won't stop until they all burn in Hell!!!

                I also agree with others--endowments could work on all levels of organization. But, I might also like to see endowments divided into gifts and equipment (much like coils and scales were given to the Ordo Dracul in Blood and Smoke) so as to make things a bit more versatile. Like say for the Ascending Ones, you might have the gift of Elixirs (alchemically poisoned potions) which enhance the body for the hunt, but for equipment you might have alchemically treated swords or poisoned arrows.

                        Instead of replacing Virtue/Vice, what they could do is in addition to the Profession merit you could take something that represented your "style" as a Hunter. They could even - dare I say it - call it a "Creed."

                        Another idea that sort of manages the Lunacy/Quiescence issue (though Lunacy is fixed now) is just slap them with Conviction instead of Integrity. And like Harmony or Memory play with the system, I could see loss of Conviction risking dropping the character "back into" Integrity. So it more acts as a kind of buffer to weirdness and horror, letting the Hunter keep on the Vigil, rather than a full alternate morality (which would be weird since, y'know, people). But playing around with the morality systems seems like something they're willing to do these days. And an alternate morality scale doesn't really "dehumanize" them that much; Mages have Wisdom and are still people, Hunters could have Conviction and still be people. Integrity represents the theme of nWoD characters not necessarily experienced much with the supernatural trapped in a horror movie type one-off situation; Conviction represents the inner strength of will needed to then intentionally start diving into horror movie situations on purpose.

                            In Vampire and Werewolf, Touchstones exist to pull you toward a certain “karmic trait” extreme: High Humanity or Flesh/Spirit Harmony. In Hunter 1e, Professions were described as “grounding” you and preventing you from becoming too much of a monster. IMHO, though, that never really worked. OTOH, I could see Hunter 2e protagonists getting Touchstones that work a lot like the ones in Vampire and Werewolf: some relationship with a person who is not involved in the Vigil that helps keep the hunter sane.

                            As for Conviction, I still think that something like Mirrors’ Conviction-as-a-power-stat would work best — on the condition that it be a strictly optional trait for those who like the idea of a scale measuring how devoted you are to the Vigil (just like 1e’s Code was a strictly optional system for those who liked having a mechanism for immunizing hunters from certain repetitive Morality losses for a price).

                            And speaking of the Code: that is Hunter’s tweak to the “karmic trait”; and it works quite well as written. The main thing I’d change concerning it in 2e would be to adapt some of the psychotherapy rules from Asylum as a means of undoing the Code and bringing the hunter back to a more normal mindset. (Bear in mind that the Code itself is an adaptation of a system that first appeared in Dogs of War, as a way of describing how someone who shoots other people for a living could keep from dropping to low Morality.)

                            Finally, I’d want 2e’s Aspirations to be supplemented by one or two Vigil-specific Aspirations that grant Practical Experience. That would be where your goals as a hunter play in, not as an Anchor — that is, it would be something that speeds character development, not something that generates Willpower or resists Integrity degeneration.
