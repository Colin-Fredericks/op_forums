        Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
        This rule have totally sense for normal mortals in Chronicle of Darkness universe - they are victims in the horror story and what they see scares them to death - but Hunters are, by definition, mortals that should confront supernatural - so they should not go a bit mental unstable from every confrontation. This discussion is not about "will werewolf Lunacy or mages spells lower mine Integrity". It is about "should every supernatural occurrences lead me to emotional instability".
        I think anyone who has run Hunter with the CofD rule set has had to come up with some kind of a homebrew solution to this problem. My personal solution was to swap out Integrity for something I called Conviction.
        Conviction
        The Morality system described in the God Machine Chronicle replicates the degradation most mortals experience at witnessing the terrible truths of the Chronicles of Darkness. Hunters however have chosen to abandon their notions of the world in light of what they have experienced first hand and found a means of coming to term with it. Whether a single mother who takes up the Vigil after witnessing the theft of her son or a priest letting his own blood for the first time having accepted the hidden teachings of the Knights of Saint George both hunters have seen the world illuminated for what it is.
        Conviction is how hunters cope with the Chronicles of Darkness, it represents the strength of commitment to the Vigil as well as their own ability to withstand the horror of what they have chosen to stand against. As such it is as much a product of the hunters own belief as well as their overall mental well being.
        While Conviction is slightly more robust, at least in terms of how the supernatural effects a character's psychological well being (again hunters have made a conscious decision to hold a light to the darkness) the murder of a human being is always a breaking point at least until a hunter redefines his definition of human.
        The following questions help to define the character's breaking points and their relationship to the Vigil.

        What is the worst thing your character can imagine himself doing in pursuit of the Vigil?
        In choosing to confront the inhuman how inhumane is a character prepared to act? What is the line that your character would not cross? What is he prepared to do to save himself or another? Would he torture a monster's servant or use an innocent as bait?

        What is the worst thing your character can imagine someone else doing?
        Monsters (human or otherwise) are capable of atrocities that can destroy minds and rend souls. What is the worst thing your character can imagine befalling someone (or himself)? Is it the thought of a monster destroying his family? The possession of an innocent? Cannibalism?

        What can't your character forget?
        A character who carries the Vigil their first exposure to the supernatural often leaves them with memories they cannot let go of. Decide what memories the character carries with them. Did she lose her father to living shadows? Watch helplessly as her mentor was burned alive by a spirit? Kill someone under a vampire's command? Describe this event in as much detail as you can. This is a breaking point in its own right and sets a benchmark for what your character would have to be exposed to experience one now.

        What does your character believe?
        This is a belief that the character holds most deeply, the center by which a hunter holds himself in the face of experiences that threaten to overwhelm him. Note that this is something the character clings to without rational proof. Is it faith in a higher power or some conspiracy theory? Intrinsic loyalty of family? Scientific rationalism? Fate or some greater destiny?

        Who or what is the enemy?
        What is a monster? Where is the line between human and inhuman? Is it biological? The existence of a soul? The definition of a behavior? Or is anything with abnormal "power" a potential target? This establishes not just a single breaking point but helps define what the character defines as murder.


        I also had each Tell be a persistent Condition you gained by choosing to change the answer to one of the questions above, rather than roll for a breaking point.

        I think the point here is that there needs to be some alternation to how Hunters confront with the supernatural - not the blanket kind of immunity that say becoming one of HtR's Imbued (cannot become a monster, spend a point to become immune to mind control, etc.) offered - but some system that makes them capable of engaging in a protracted conflict with the supernatural as well as rules to tweak that to your liking. So if you want to play 'Blade' then you can, but if you're looking for 'Frailty' you've got other options.

            I think my preferred answer to this is something akin to what SpahishInquisition describes. Hunters shouldn't be able to completely ignore the instinctual gut reaction CofD mortals have to the supernatural. But they should be able to interact with it differently, so they can engage in prolonged conflict with it - whether it's an old-school stakes-and-shotguns hunt, research, rescue attempt or negotiations.

            The more actively mind-screwy effects, such as the Quiessence Curse or Lunacy, are something different. But hunters do need some way to overcome them. It shouldn't be easy, and should probably mess with their heads in a different way, but it should be possible.

            As I've said before, this is a bigger thing than just crossing Hunter over with other gamelines. Hunter is unlike any other gameline, since hunters are still mortals. Their capabilities have a much more pervasive impact on the general dynamics of the World of Darkness, should one choose to include them.

                        I think it's worth looking in the Chronicles of Darkness book again and looking at the section on Integrity. Don't have access right now, but I do feel the language on the "what have you forgotten?" Question might be worth looking at again vis a vis supernatural breaking points.

                        I also don't think it's appropriate for Hunters to avoid the breaking point for seeing the supernatural. I definitely think they should be more effective in resisting if, possibly through dice bonuses and a different set of Conditions for success, but the risk that you'll lose your nerve, that it'll wear you down, that it can BREAK you should always be present. It's an important part of the drama of Hunter.

                        To draw one comparison, in XCOM it's not that your higher level troopers CANT become panicked, it's that they're less likely to. The risk is always there and oh nelly, it's bad when it happens.

                        EDIT: Okay, so looking at the text:

                        Originally posted by Chronicles of Darkness Corebook, pg. 30

                        What has your character forgotten? In the Chronicles of
                        Darkness, it’s next to impossible to grow up without
                        any exposure to the supernatural. Decide what your
                        character saw and forgot. Did she see a vampire take
                        the form of smoke and vanish? A man turn into a
                        wolf? Maybe she caught a glimpse of an impossible
                        nightmarescape through a door that should never
                        have been propped open? Describe this scene in as
                        much detail as you can. This is a breaking point that
                        already occurred, but it helps set a benchmark for
                        what your character would have to see in order to
                        experience one now.
                        Emphasis mine. This implies something something interesting for the conversation, doesn't it? That this thing seen before helps determine on a case by case basis what might constitute a breaking point versus what doesn't when it's supernatural and you see it? I don't know if that solves the problem but it strikes me as interesting.

                                        The great thing is, they don't have to be mutually exclusive. With what counts as a Breaking Point being determined by past experience, each player can decide where their character lies on the spectrum from "I roll Breaking Points for seeing my own shadow" to "I laugh in Chthuhlu's face" based on their character background and their answers to the five questions. If you want to play a game where Hunters start out immune to Breaking Points from witnessing the supernatural, just generate characters who have seen some shit (even if they have little practical experience, they might still have witnessed horrific things) and don't roll Breaking Points for witnessing the supernatural. If you want to play a game where any supernatural event is a potential Breaking Point, have all of the characters generate normal mortal characters and add the Hunter template during play. If you want a mix of the two, have each player decide individually how much experience they've had with the supernatural and work out what kinds of things might count as Breaking Points for them to witness.
