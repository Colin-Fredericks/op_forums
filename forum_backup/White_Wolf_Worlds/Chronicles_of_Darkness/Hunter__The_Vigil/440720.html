    Benedictions

    03-13-2015, 09:21 PM
    I decided to start a separate thread rather than further derail the Second Edition thread... I think a lot of issues with Benedictions would be resolved if the game treated them as a plot hook with narrative weight rather than handwaved magic niche that need no discussion.

    That being the case, lets talk about their history and how they're wielded.

    - Do Benedictions predate Malleus Maleficarum (born in the late 15th/early 16th Century)?

    - If yes, how did Malleus get a monopoly on them? If no, how did Malleus discover them?

    - What does it mean to "know" a Benediction? Does each have a specific ritual incantation? Because if it's just praying and then a miracle happens, what is it you need to "learn" to gain a new one?

                          I imagine its a question of faith. I could recite as many Ave Maria's as I please, but they wont mean anything to me. They will just be words. Although to a devote Christian may find a sense of relief and comfort in those words, and it'll mean something entirely different to them. Additionally, a different prayer may mean very little or nothing to a Christian, even if its a Christian prayer, if that person has never heard about the prayer before.
                          Likewise, each Benediction is another such prayer. When you sit down and recite a Benediction, it wont work untill you're 100% unwaveringly convinced that it will. And nobody is dumb enough to just accept it, when somebody comes up and goes; "Hey mate! Recite this 10000 word prayer over a corpse, and it'll come back to life! For realsies!". That kind of mental conditioning and indoctrination takes time and effort.
                          Running with this, I could easily see something like forcing the owner of a Benediction to recite some small prayer each morning, to make sure that the mental conditioning stays intact. When he has a lot of Beneictions, thats alot of praying each morning.
                          This could tie in with something I think was wirtten in Mage. I think there were mentioned something about every single human essentially being a Mage to a certain extend, and having the power to reshape the world. The Awakened then, was merely the people most proficient at doing so. This could mean that the benedictions are merely a collective act of mage. If 1 person can shape the world very insignificantly with his thoughts, then ten million of them who all think alike, can do a hell of a lot of 'insignificant'.

                          In my own game though, I've just flavored them as the literal power of the Christian God, channeled through his mortal Paladins. Such power isn't given without an immense show of faith, however. The reason they dont go around healing people and resurrecting folks in the streets, is because very few people have enough of Gods fervor to literally bring people back from the dead, and they're afraid that the few people who possess such power will be murdered by the Churchs enemies if they go public. They're in a shadow-war with several very powerful supernatural foes, after all.
                          I imagine this "We dont do it, because then we'll be fucking murdered" thing works in general.

                              Originally posted by Krobeles View Post
                              I imagine its a question of faith.
                              The book specifically emphasizes that that is not how it works thought. Being an apostate, losing your faith, etc doesn't strip away your Benedictions. That was intentional by the authors to emphasize the Catholic theological themes - where the spiritual authority of priests is based not on their own piety, but on the institutional mandate granted to St. Peter by Christ, hence the authority of the Church to forgive sin even when its members sin themselves, and the rituals of the Church carrying real divine power in and of themselves (transubstantiation for example), not just symbolic or faith-based.

                              That distinction is pretty important to the thematic contrast between the Long Night (who are all about "Faith") and Malleus (who are all about "Works").

                                                  It's not about their faith. It's about the faith of something else. Maybe the organization. Maybe an individual within it.
