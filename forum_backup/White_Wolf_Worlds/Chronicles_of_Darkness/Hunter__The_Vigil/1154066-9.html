                Cutting out smell, taste, touch and proprioception from a genuine supernatural experience goes a long long way toward cutting out its ability to rattle your understanding of the world — seeing a video of a haunting is not nearly as traumatic as being present at the screening of a haunted video, because most of the unconscious cues the human mind/body/soul picks up on are not purely visual or auditory. My point is that that's simply not how Integrity stats work in these games and the purpose of the trade-off is a combination of Werewolf's two free dots of Rites to allow every starting character to get Sacred Hunt if they want it and the exceptions Wisdom and Humanity lets you build in to emphasize that organized hunters can be strange and unsettling in their priorities.

                A vampire who takes on a bane and then strives constantly for high Humanity has his work cut out for him but can arrange a suitable environment for it if he twists and tilts his surroundings with every tool at his disposal, including proxies and other support networks; a mage who inures her most versatile spells and holds to high Wisdom does her best by living the life of a hermit, funneling Mana into her spells, containing Paradoxes, and culling Strong sympathetic connections she doesn't have immediate need of while leveraging front organizations toward her goals at a remove; hunters trying to maintain high Integrity with Code modifications explicitly benefit from connecting deeply and often with people they trust, and best practices for keeping that rating include contending with the known supernatural peacefully or indirectly wherever possible, not privileging the monsters over the mortals in the course of negotiating a workable outcome, and taking the time to harden themselves against as broad a variety of supernatural interaction as possible before they make this retreat/retirement in the event that they have to encounter or kill or torture a monster — a High-Integrity hunter who maintains that rating is an old hand and a living resource in a group that will allow him to operate in a manageable locale with competent associates.

                The mage and the vampire both have to lose their respective Integrity-alikes to get their bonuses; the hunter gets their trade offer the moment the opportunity for the roll comes up. Those aren't descents that reverse themselves quickly or easily, and being able to stabilize at 6 is tricky enough for the average hunter without a creed to fall back on, which is why I don't view the smash-myself-against-the-glass-breaker splat as particularly in need of a better exception clause than what they're presently given.

                        Fair enough, and I dare say I think that was intentionally reinforced in 2E, since 1) They wanted the Integrity/Equivalent to play a bigger role than be a stat you can maintain high just by avoiding doing a few things you'd only do when you're an asshole anyway and 2) You get a Beat whenever you get a Breaking Point, so it has become a major way to gain Experience rather than just a negative point. I suspect this also was a factor in adding in Touchstones (to give a better chance for the Breaking point to succeed).

                        But I think that in this case, the problem is more that the Code tells us something about your character's way of thinking, which you should have more freedom to decide.

                        Things like Integrity, Humanity or Clarity are more like a state of mind not directly linked to your thought or belief (at least in 2E). They measure things like your sanity, how much you still are able to connect with humans despite being literally a walking corpse, or your ability to discern reality from fantasy. Traits like Hunger or Cover are even more alien, describing how hungry the nightmare you have as a soul is or how efficient your human disguise is.

                        The Code, meanwhile, describe a code of conduct and philosophy of life your character alledgedly chose to follow of his own will and genuinely believes in. Even if he joined a group who believe something entirely different or if his player thinks this entire Code is bullshit. In other words, it limits the creative control you have over your character and what he/she thinks. And I think that's a problem.

                        It makes sense for the Ordo Dracul and the Lancea Sanctum to lose humanity when doing their things because regardless of what they personally want or believe, it does distance them from humans. It makes sense for the Courts to lose Clarity when dealing with Fae because regardless of their goals, Fae will still mess with how they distinguish reality from fantasy. And it makes sense for Demons to risk their Covers when acting their Agenda because whatever they want doesn't factor on their ability to maintain a disguise.

                        But it doesn't make sense for Compacts and Conspiracies to feel torn apart about not following rules their factions are explicilty stated not to care about. If the Lucifuge judge monsters on weither they are good or evil rather than on weither they are monsters or humans, they should not feel bad about defending some monsters against humans. If the Union just fight everyone messing up with their neightborhood supernatural or not with no discrimination, they shouldn't feel bothered about hurting humans when they are assholes. Granted, these aren't absolute (for exemple, the Cheiron Group might recruit people who believe in the Code forcefully), but the possibility of some of them not caring about this Code should still be real.

                        Anyway, I'd like to get back on the exercise if nobody mind. Who's up for doing the next groups?
