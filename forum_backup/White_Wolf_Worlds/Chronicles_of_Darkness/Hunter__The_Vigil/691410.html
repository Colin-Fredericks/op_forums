    Compact: Ride to Damascus - The Beastcrafters

    10-06-2015, 01:45 PM
    Ride to Damascus
    The Beastcrafters






    In 1961 a bus of Freedom Riders found themselves in an impossible situation. The crowd of racists that surrounded the bus grew thicker and thicker until the human wall became the pitch black walls of a tunnel. Onwards the bus drove towards a hellish red light and every soul on board heard either the sounds of a slave auction or cannibal drums from an Africa that never was.

    But one man aboard was no man. Reverend Clarence Jackson was a Changeling and through his supernatural nature he knew that they had entered a story, a story with a moral: Fear that which is not like you for they are dangerous. As a Freedom Rider it need not be said what Clarence thought of this and his feelings about another story were even stronger.

    Clarence remembered the ogre’s beatings, and he remembered how the fear was so much worse than the pain. Most of all he remembered the ogre patiently explaining how this was for his own good.

    But Clarence was no longer a man, and his magic held a stronger dominion over stories. There were many good Christian tales with better morals that began when the Lord’s believers found themselves in a land of peril. He rose to his feat and preached like he had never preached before. He preached of the Valley of the Shadow of Death, and of the Pilgrim's Progress. With his faerie magic he gave the story a twist, and the story became a Christian morality tale.

    Jackson allowed the bus to arrive and he bid the riders to disembark so he could show them the truth. This hell held only one tormented soul. A figure so wrapped in shadows and chains that none could tell if it were a man or woman, white or black. “Behold” preached the reverend, “This poor soul was once victim of violence, and that violence inflicted a disease called hatred. Now he lies here in hell, his own hatred is the demons that torment him. But what power has hate compared to our Lord who is love and who is forgiveness.” he turned to the horror “So I say onto you, forgive those who have transgressed against you and free yourself from this eternal torment!”

    With those words Clarence unleashed his faerie magic. The chains shattered, the shadows became light and gold, and then the hell just faded away leaving the Freedom Riders standing outside their bus on a deserted interstate.

    From there they went their separate ways. Reverend Clarence used his magic to turn invisible and escape the inevitable questioning. Deciding that this event was far too close to his own slavery for comfort he returned North to live a life free from conflict.

    Convinced that she had experienced a genuine religious revelation a woman named Caitlin Lawson published her experiences accurately, save for the honest mistake of casting Clarence as an angel in mortal guise, and published it first as a successful work of short fiction under the name “Ride to Damascus”, first in civil rights newsletters and then in an anthology of fiction. She remained an activist until 1965 whereupon she became a nun.

    While no Hunters were formed that day, many passengers took up the vigil in the weeks and months to follow after some time to think. They used Julia’s story, and unpublished details, as a code to identify each other and form their own Cell.

    The Civil Rights movement was a turbulent time for the Vigil and occult knowledge flowed freely. After many contradictory and confusing explanations from witches, shaman, hunters and more the Cell’s search took them to a Goblin Market. There they found a tutor for hire who agreed to teach them who Clarence Jackson was and how he performed his miracle. The Cell left the market with renewed purpose and a book containing the secrets of Talecrafting.

    The Price was high. Not one member of the cell lived to see the Civil Rights Act passed into law, but by the time the last founder died they had already trained twice as many students.

    [Picture: An elderly African American woman in traditional dress sits on an armchair surrounded by books. She is reading a thick leather tome with a picture of two faeries dressed and posed like the Brothers Grimm on the front.]

    Fear leads to hatred.
    Hatred leads to the dark side.

    The Enemy

    Like Clarence Jackson, half of Ride to Damascus’s founding members, the half descended from Africans, saw fear as the tool of their oppressors; but even they don’t consider fear the enemy.

    Beasts inflict fear yes, but terrible as it is, a Beast’s hunger is less damaging than a vampire’s thirst or the more omnipresent fears inflicted by one’s fellow man. It’s not fear that’s the problem, it’s the Beast’s lessons. Lessons from mankinds savage and brutal past, lessons that have no place in the modern world.
    And so, Ride to Damascus use the power of Talecrafting to drag primordial Horrors into the modern world. They identify the monster’s legend and enter it’s Lairs, but they do so while invoking modern tropes and recraft the legend into a modern story with a modern lesson.

    To the Beasts this is an act of violence, the wilful destruction of their identity. To Ride to Damascus it’s an act of violence as justified as any stake through a murderous Vampire’s heart. It’s the defence of the Beast’s victims, and her victim’s victims, and her victim’s victim’s victims.

    Stereotypes

    Ageis Kai Doru: When we brought our Book the merchant’s final words were “don’t let The Greek see it”. I have no doubt he told some form of truth and I’ve read enough stories to know you do not ignore a warning like that, but that doesn't mean I have to like it.

    The Long Night: Reverend Clarence himself was both a faerie and a devout Christian. To their credit the Long Night do not see any contradiction, but they can’t understand that faerie magic can be used for good.

    Ashwood Abbey: Our prey come from the depths of the human soul, is it any surprise that we might see people like them in both nature and denemour? But they are humans and can be dealt with by human law. Focus on our true mission.

    Llannerch Athrawon: Our little magic trick can’t cure so called Heroes so we mostly avoid them. But I did see this one, I was tracking a particularly vicious dragon, when I saw this fat slob in a cape led around by his dick by someone dressed, I kid you not, like it was the 1930s. He ended up killing the dragon without causing any trouble, I wish I spoke to her when I had a chance.

    Tickets

    Ride to Damascus has a narrow and focused mission, consequently they have a stronger ideological unity than many Compacts however they still have a division of labour.

    Tickets North have retired from active hunting due to injury, fear, or just “having no more fight in them”. Those that haven’t retried entirely serve the Compact as recruiters, trainers, and as storytellers who promote tropes useful for talecrafting. Ride to Damascus doesn’t have any storytellers influential enough to make a difference, but it lets wounded veterans retain a sense of dignity.
    Free Specialty: Expression (Storytelling)

    Tickets South are the Compact’s muscle and scouts. They track down beasts, serve as bodyguards to the talecrafters and if necessary they’ll cut a path to safety after a hunt gone bad.

    Free Specialty: Firearms (Shotgun) or Streetwise (Beasts)

    Tickets to Damascus are the heart and soul of the Compact. They’re the talecrafters, and by convention they’re usually the leaders of their cell.
    Free Merit Dot: Status (Ride to Damascus) or Talecrafting

    Status

    Ride to Damascus gain Status by having other members vouch for them. Your own cell provide little, they are supposed to vouch for you after all, so helping out other cells with advice or support is the fastest way to rise. This system leads to a correlation between Status and age, as older successful hunters are credited by the cells they inspired or recruited back in the day; and as those cells recruit others and rise in status their word carries more weight. The unofficial leaders or elders of the Compact are all in their twilight years and tell stories of their days in the civil rights campaign.

    O: To use stories as a tool you must first know stories. Take the Morality Tales specialty under Expression or Occult.
    OO: Talecrafting requires keen observation to identify the start of tropes that can be then pushed to completion. Take a dot in Trained Observer
    OOO: Beasts might be the enemy, but they’re the enemy within all mankind. To forget this and see the Beast as the hated other is to step off the righteous path. Take the Sympathetic Merit and do not forget to use it on friend and foe alike. At this level you’re also trusted enough to learn that the Compact’s power comes from an (eminently desirable and stealable) Relic and you may learn Talecrafting.
    OOOO: After so long manipulating the power of stories some of that magic rubs off on them. Take two dots of Striking Looks, like the heroes of the stories their inherent goodness is plain for all to see, whether they’re truly good or not.
    OOOOO: Use your Composure as though it was Potency to resist a Beast’s Nightmares and add Composure to any roll to resist the effects of a Beast’s Lair Tilts, for example add it to your Perception roll (making the roll Wits + Composure + Composure) to see in a dimly lit Lair.

    Endowment: Talecrafting (O - OOOOO)

    It quite literally cost the entire founding Cell their lives to make it happen; a Goblin Merchant sold the faerie’s secret magic of Talecrafting to a young Cell which thus became a Compact.

    Who can say what Fate or plot was behind such a momentous event? Ride to Damascus certainly cannot, but they use their powers with skill and enthusiasm.
    Ride to Damascus use the rules for talecrafting in Swords at Dawn with the following modifications:
    • They spend Willpower in place of Glamour.
    • They cannot force the Pattern by spending Willpower. They rig events or wait for them to happen naturally.
    • They cannot pull of a Reversal of Fate.
    • They do not suffer Talecrafting addiction, mortals are just more resistant to madness than faeries.
    • Their dicepool is Talecrafting alone, however Ride to Damascus can Talecraft as a teamwork action. Secondary Actors roll Expression and may apply specialties relating to a genre of stories. Secondary Actors do not need dots in Talecrafting and take no penalty if they have no dots.

    Beastcrafting

    Beastcrafting, also known as Legendcrafting or Laircrafting is using Talecrafting to rewrite a Beast’s Legend, Hunger and personality.

    Beast crafting begins with reforging the Lair. In turn, this begins by learning the locations of the Chambers and the stories behind how those places became the Beast's. If a Beast emerged from the shadows behind a drunken reveller in a dark alleyway; then Ride to Damascus must enact the beginning of a story that involves a drunk reveller, a figure emerging from the Shadows and of course it be enacted in the same dark alley that is now a Chamber. This is the Pattern.

    Once they have enacted their Pattern they roll for the hook. Roll Talecrafting vs Lair. As always Ride to Damascus can roll as a Teamwork action with secondary actors rolling Expression.
    Dramatic Failure or the Beast wins with an Exceptional Success: The environment changes around the Cell until they're inside the Chamber. The Beast is instantly aware of the invasion and a Cruel Twist of Fate is poised to strike. The Cell are not aware that the attempt failed.

    Failure or the Beast wins. The environment changes around the Cell until they're inside the Chamber. The Beast is not aware of this invasion but may discover it through the normal ways. A Cruel Twist of Fate is poised to strike. The Cell are aware that the attempt failed.

    Success and the Beast's roll is overcome. The environment changes around the Cell until they're inside the Chamber. The Beast is not aware of this invasion but may discover it through the normal ways. If the cell complete the story begun by their Pattern the Chamber is successfully Laircrafted. No Cruel Twist of Fate occurs.

    Exceptional Success and the Beast's roll is overcome. The environment changes around the Cell until they're inside the Chamber. The Beast is not aware of this invasion, and if the Beast or the Horror uncovers it then until the Cell complete their story it can only enter that Chamber by adopting a role that fits the story. This most likely prevents it from using it's supernatural abilities or violence. No Cruel Twist of Fate occurs.
    If the Beast or it's Horror uncovers an attempt at Laircrafting in progress it is likely to object. The simplest way is to simply attack the Cell and attempt to prevent them from completing the story. If the Beast cannot do so, usually because the Cell rolled an Exceptional Success for the Hook, it can try to subvert the story in progress. Roll the Beast's Manipulation and add the Successes to the earlier Lair roll made to Contest the Cell. If the new combined total exceeds the Cell's then when the end of the story is reached the Chamber will not change and the Beast is once more free to act as it pleases. Storytellers are strongly encouraged to give modifiers to the roll based on roleplaying.

    Effects of Beastcrafting

    Chambers changed by Beastcrafting become pleasant safer places even for intruders and the Horror must spend either a point Satiety or one Willpower per crafted Chamber to attack anyone within. However the real effects happen when the Beast itself is affected.

    Every two weeks measured from the last time a Chamber was recrafted one Chamber is freed from the effects of Laircrafting, however if over half of the Lair has been reworked then instead one more Lair will be reworked. The Heart is always last, and after the Heart the Horror itself is next. The Beast's Hunger, Legend, and Nightmares are all replaced with new wholesome traits that promote the positive morals Ride to Damascus approve of.

    There is also a faster but more dangerous way to Beastcraft: Targeting the horror directly. Within the Heart or anywhere after the Heart has been reforged the Horror itself can be targeted by Beastcrafting. By using a Pattern where a what appears to be a monster eventually turns out to be harmless and friendly Ride to Damascus can reforge a Beast all at once. The obvious drawback is that this requires contact with either the Beast or the Horror; and as there's a role in the story for a monster the Horror will have full access to its powers. Reverend Clarence used his faerie magic to chain the Beast until the Story's conclusion. Ride to Damascus usually use actual chains on the rare occasions they try things the fast way.

    Reforged Chambers never provide advantages to a Beast who's being his usual Beastly self. For example the Beast could not use a new Lair Trait like "brightly lit" to flood a brightly lit room unless he has a completely wholesome motive in fitting with the new story Ride to Damascus are creating.

      Ride to Damascus came from two sources.

      1) While I'm very satisfied with Llannerch Athrawon I felt they failed at one of my goals when writing them - to write a rebuttal to teaching through fear. I try to keep my politics out of my homebrew and let individual storytellers interpret things but in this case it went too far. Llannerch Athrawon had a rebuttal but how would it ever come up in play if they rarely interact directly with the Beast or the Beast's victims? Ride to Damasucs have to express their rebuttal directly through their choice of story and occasional improvised storytelling duels with a Beast.

      2) Discussions with PenDragon about how Princess and Beasts are thematic opposites. Much of Ride to Damascus is just me translating my Princess ideas into Hunter form. That's why Ride to Dmascus are one of the most moral compacts I wrote - just compare them to Llannerch Athrawon's manipulative behaviour against the mentally ill. And yes, Princess can Talecraft. The fae focused Z-Splat lets them do it at Inner Light 7.


      Llannerch Athrawon probably knew how to do Beastcrafting at one point. They might have owned the very same book that Ride to Damascus own. They'd totally steal it given a chance.

      I'm very strict about keeping Hunter as "mortals defending mortals", having Reverend Clarence be a Changeling skirts that line. I felt that since Beast naturally portrays a positive crossover friendly view of Changeling-Beast relationships it was important to show the other side somewhere. It fit in nicely here.


      Hopfully I won't have to change too much once I see the 2E talecrafting

                  Okay, maybe it's just because I never really liked the redirection of Beast, but I actually think that this "empowered compact" is pretty good.

                  For one thing, it makes a lot of sense as a thematic contrast to Beasts, and is quite intimately intertwined with them. Llannerch Athrawon is similarly strong, but held at a distance by virtue of their very set up - playing Llannerch Athrawon is more like playing The Old Men of Marduk using the Marduk Society as disposable WMDs than anything.

                  For another, it's a nice example of Light Gray without being "too White Hat" for the World of Darkness. Why do I say this?

                  For starters, the ideology of the Ride to Damascus is based on "talecrafting for a happy ending". That opens up possibility to a lot of dissent and inner conflict as to what defines a "proper" use of talecrafting. For example, I can easily see zealous Christian beastcrafters who try to use talecrafting as a sort of extreme Hell House - defanging the horrors, but at the same time using them as tools in guiding people towards "good Christian behavior".

                  Secondly, alongside this implicit grayness, there's the fact that the compact is given a realistic presentation; their numbers are small, and focused, and talecrafting is powerful, but they don't always win. In all honesty, I think the Ride to Damascus probably has more of a positive effect on the Vigil in that they are more likely to subtly leak warnings about local horrors and to generally inspire people to take up the Vigil, if not necessarily as part of the Ride.

                  Thirdly, the sheer moral ambiguity of their powers and plans keeps them firmly down in the mud. Seriously, Beastcrafting is literally raping somebody's soul. You are forcing your way into somebody's very essence and mutilating it, torturing and resculpting their heart, their mind and soul into a shape that the Damascans consider more acceptable. This is violation of a nature we don't even have words for, the sort of stuff you'd expect to see hard-core villains using. The only thing that keeps Damascans from being villains is that A: they have genuinely good intentions - which would still leave them, at best, as Well Intentioned Extremists, were it not for B: the creatures they are using this power on can legitimately be claimed as being worse. No matter how you shape it, Beasts are abominations that literally feed on the pain, suffering, sorrow, misery, despair and terror of humanity as a whole. It's violating to force such a change on them... yet, still, the results can be legitimately for the greater good.

                  I love this. It really feels appropriate for the World of Darkness. On the one hand, you have people arguing that this makes the Damascans little better than those "straight camps" that Christians send their gay and trans kids to in order to try and force them to be normal. On the other hand, you have people arguing that this is truly the better option, both for the people around them and for people who'd otherwise have to die for what they are. And both sides are right.

                  That moral grayness, that slippery slope, that razor edge between good and evil, the looming possibility that those fighting against oppression may have become oppressors in turn... that';s the sort of thing that makes the World of Darkness feel right.

                  If I have one complaint, it's that I don't agree with the assessment that Damascans should be immune to Talecrafting Addiction. Power always has a price in the WoD, and furthermore, the Damascans are mucking around with something they shouldn't. Talecrafting lets a person mess around with the very fabric of reality, twisting events to confer to your plan for them. That's not conducive to a person's sanity.

                  Honestly, I think Damascans should be more likely to get addicted to Talecrafting than Changelings should. Changelings have the buffer of having a hole where their soul should be filled with the Wyrd already - humans who tap into the Wyrd are being exposed to all its raw power, naked and unshielded. It's the difference between giving street-grade crack to a straight-edged goody-goody who's never taken any drugs before and giving it to a hardcore junkie - who do you think is going to handle it better?
