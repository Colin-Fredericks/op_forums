      They're actually somewhat present in basic mechanical form in the book: they're the examples used in the Storytelling chapter for the guidelines for creating compacts and conspiracies, including 2e Status benefits for both organizations and the rules for two sample Relics.

      There was some conversation during development on whether or not to deemphasize Ashwood Abbey as an up-front core example of player character behavior. I'm not as familiar with the decision to sideline the Aegis Kai Doru, aside from the general need to free up word count.

              Perhaps there could've been more emphasis made on the 'hunting the most dangerous game' and 'occult socialite' aspects of the Abbey and less on the ''''parties''''.
              The thing of the matter is that the general approach the Abbey, hunting monsters because it's fun, was it's thematic point, and while hunting the most dangerous game or being an occult socialite can play into that, neither can stack up to that tentpole-particularly given how in being so, the Abbey reflects the very thing people are doing when they play Hunter: the Vigil. That said, the concensus was against Abbey, and they wanted room for a new compact, so it got relegated to being an example.

              While we know the goal was to show off a new conspiracy with the new edition and that space issues meant that another conspiracy would take the bullet, it's never been precisely explained why it was Aegis Kai Doru that took it (I mean, the other most likely candidate were the Ascending Ones, and I'm a bigger AO fan than an AKD fan, so I'm not complaining), but if I had to speculate a reason, I would wager that they wanted to have a longer time to workshop making the relic answer to the sacred prey of mages and werewolves work more fully and completely, which had a handy side benefit of downplaying arguments about Hunter's relationship with Mage Second Edition around release.

                                              Originally posted by Aspel View Post

                                              This feels wildly reductive.

                                              The keyword to Conspiracies is "compromise", which on the one hand means, yes, there's a lot of monsters at the top, and a lot of their action is definitely suspect, but on the other hand also means reaching a equitable middle ground on a lot of different fronts, which often means finding ways to work out where the alternatives are just out and out conflict.

                                              Like, I am the first person to point out how Hutners are not good guys, but to just put things into perspective, let's go through our list:

                                              Task Force: Valkyrie's dedication to the support and enforcement of the various Masquerades is useful when you remember that magic in the world of Chronicles is inherently damaging to the psychology of humanity, and that by maintaining the divide, it maintains a lot of the mental and physical health of both human and monstrous communities, and is a big support in helping more reasonable monstrous communities curtail the more extreme elements of monsters.

                                              The Malleus Maleficarum's dedication against vampires, demons, and mages, however warped the motivation can be, does mean that there is a targeted pressure against some of the most exploitative monsters out there, particularly since those are the ones that can damage a person's sense of self and psyche the most, and thus ensure that debilitating habits, addictions, and patterns of behavior are kept at least to the limtis of human management, and correspondingly encourage monsters to rely on dehumanizing tactics less than they might otherwise, and thus have less of their own problems eroding their own humanity.

                                              The CHeiron Group is just plain evil, but their practice proves that there is a whole world of medical and scientific advances to be discovered that could do a lot to improve the health and wellbeing of various kinds of communities, and while ultimately you'd still want to have more ethical and humane actors than extra-dimensional capitalists, they have already proven a host of wonder treatments and procedures that could not only change the standard of life for humans, but for monsters as well.

                                              The Aegis Kai Doru's crusade helps to combat against two of the most interested parties of the supernatural community in shaping regions to their own interests and counter to those of the people living there, and meanwhile their efforts to collect potentially harmful tools, understand them, and finding careful and safe application of the safer and more usable of them can end up doing a lot of good in the communities they operate. For the monsters, in creates a pressure to work more with the communities they're trying to shape and meet half way to better hide among them, and again that encourages more connectivity to their own humanity.

                                              The Ascending Ones, as a very old and proven institution, is a reliable institution for parties to reach out to for arranging peaceful and equitable compromises, between monstrous comunities, human communities, and between those two, and in providing backup to any coalition of actors who would wildly hurt a community. Their practice in elixirs also means that they have a wide a thriving pharmaceutrical background that can support the communities they're in, and those drug dealers (Who aren't inherently evil, let's note) allows for those to move more freely than they might in a regulated market, and for cheaper prices than they might otherwise be-and those drug dealers are also a very useful immediate response emergencies, which goes doubly useful in communities that are neglected by official organizations, and as final point, the over all economic strata of the three branches also means there's a lot more economic flow generated because of them.

                                              The Lucifuge are institutionally one of the most empathetic organizations out there, with any of their agents predisposed to help people dealing with struggling with their metaphorical demons, and are also the forefront actors against the most inhumane and uncaring monster sets out there, reining in the worst ruiners of human lives.

                                              The Knights of Saint George may actively serve the Abyss, but they do it in the interests of keeping the Abyss out of the world, and in so doing apply pressure to a monster that is all too keen to abandon careful and considerate application of their powers.

                                              The Les Mysteres do a lot to help maintain a healthy spiritual balance in the communities they participate in, and very vitally can provide a means for spirits to sustain themselves without neccesarily allowing them to go into excess, and thus are a powerful buffer their manipulations as well, and can provide pressures on werewolves to be mindful of their impact on spiritual communities as well.

                                              The Cainite Heresy are a bunch of fucks*, but their presence is a very solid reason for vampires to not give into excesses, control the damage they inflict on their victims, and with that possibly even have more reason to reflect on that humanity.

                                              The Vanguard Serial Crimes Unit is very good at rooting out what are often the most damaging and toxic elements of their communities, and their need to act in accordance to the law (in theory) allows for moments of possible mercy that might not otherwise be afforded.

                                              The Feast of Shulpae are a very effective deterrent towards carelessly and thoughtlessly trying to build cult activity, and can be an effective binder against the most egrigious of a class of monster whose carelessness and thoughtlessness on the whole can devastate regions, and all while still giving some due diligence to the nature of those beings, for whatever that's worth.

                                              The Knights of Saint Adrian are a bunch of fucks*, but their presence in the cultures of transience, which are much more vulnerable, provides a mesaure of defense, and their targets are often reality terrorists who are often willing to accept more devastating damage to reality.

                                              And the Merrick Insittue are a bunch of kids taking down a corrupt agency that is otherwise being constantly shielded by any number of interested parties, while also being among some of the most effective actors against one of the most adaptable monsters out there, and both by being-as-warning-of-consequence and action-as-pressure encourage more humanity in them.

                                              And by contrast-I actually don't know if I'm supposed to take the first compact being (apocalyptic) Christian Fundamentalists as a positve or a negative, so to immediately counter-play that, they are also (apocalyptic) Christian Fundamentalists.****

                                              Now, does any of that make any of those good guys? Fuck no, one of them is still the FBI**. With the exception of the Merrick Institute (because it's hard to really take a bunch of kids to task), every single one of them have wildly fucky attitudes and actions-but to say they're flat out evil ignores that they do, in fact, do good-maybe not what everyone can agree on as good, but again, that's kind of the point. THey're complicated, in a game where one of the points is that what can often seem like a completely black and white affair is instead more complicated than that. They engage with monstrosity, but then again, so does everyone else in Chronicles, and there's real humanity that is often engaged through that as well, even as it just as often leads to greater monstrosity.

                                              "Hunters aren't the good guys" isn't a refrain of mine because Hunters are, consequentially, "the bad guys". Hunters are, like everything else in Chronicles, people, and people are wild, weird, varied, and not easily boxed. That's not to say there aren't good people, and that there aren't bad people, but at the end of the day, peoples is peoples.

                                                      Okay, that clarifies on those points, and let's be clear-I am not on the side of the police, Christian fundamentalists, exploitative hyper capatilist pharmaceutical and medical organizations, etc., etc., and Hunter is definitely a lens to examine all of that.

                                                      But, and this is the important part, in the context of a game franchise about monsters in a gameline that is about the monstrosity of humanity in direct pursuit of acting against monstrosity, and recognizing the humanity that exist in that monstrosity, it centralizes on the duality of the problem-one, that opposition towards building better worlds are still human, and two, that humanity doesn't excuse their strife.

                                                      The people who make up these systemic evils are people, like anyone else, and are motivated a lot of the banal things that everyone participates in. If we can play Vampire with an expectation that we're to find humanity in a creature who sucks blood, addicts people to their personages, and warp communities, then Hunter does the same with FBI agents. To deny that humanity is to commit to the same sin of Hunters in general, taking against monsters of the night without accounting for their humanity, and that very rapidly escalates to slasherdom as people who do that basically begin to self-justify excess power being exercised.

                                                      But, in the same way that it draws a line between their humanity, Hunter draws a line between the strife committed by the vampire and the FBI agent-and have no doubts about it, the games still does not deny the damage a vampire commits, and pretty well makes the argument that even the best intentioned of them is still a long term cost on any given community. That analogy cuts both ways-just because the FBI is made up of people does not stop the FBI from being a harmful organization that, at the end of the day, needs something done about it. Not doing something about either encourages a systemic rot that will only get worse the less is done.

                                                      What Hunter asks is to balance the two factors, and it's conclusion is a very good lesson for progressives trying to build better worlds, particularly as the violence to push for that better world only continues to escalate-the need to violence corruption (however you go about it) cannot deny the humanity of that corruption if it wants to avoid becoming more of the same. Hunters are victims of the creatures of the night, and their crusade is often an immediate response to that harm inflicted, an emotional reaction that is useful if used correctly.

                                                      The hall mark of a lot of anti-progressive movements and institutionalization is, quite frankly, hatred against the perceived non-normal, and that hatred is not going to handled with clean white gloves-the system needs to be violenced. But if we're going to avoid Nietzschean cliches in dealing with that, then that violence cannot also dehumanize the opposition. It has to deal with the injustice, it has to deal with the corruption, but it also has to recognize the humanity of the people that is interwoven with that. There needs to be anger, fury, determination, a coldness to act and get through, but it also demands empathy, kindness, understanding. And balancing those two is REALLY HARD, and it's not an even distribution at all times. It's going to be case by case, handling the building brick-by-brick, moment-by-moment, and that's going to have missteps and errors and costs. That's why the whole damn thing is so damn hard, even disregarding the complete imbalance of it all*.

                                                      So, yeah, I still think it's a bit reductive. It's not untrue, mind you, but it's definitely not the whole picture.

                                                      And I say this as someone who recognized the dangers in the political situation in their country because I recognized that "Hey, the Union's kind of fucked up actually." That thought is a very significant kickstarter to my being the leftist I am today.

                                                      *like hell, there's another useful metaphor-Hunters, because they lack power, aren't very often in a position to offer meaningful mercy, and in fact have to rely on excessive violence to make a difference, but by that very fact, the excessive violence is an act that risks dehumanizing the actors, and there's no real way around that-but by acknowledging it advance, it can be planned and worked around.
