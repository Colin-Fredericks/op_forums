    I know this might not be popular, but for my own table, and what I'd do if I was designing the setting, I'd drop the Cainite Heresy as a distinct Conspiracy, and harvest its coolest elements for Malleus Maleficarum - specifically have Malleus have originated in a vampire ruled ghoul-cult (via vitea-tainted communion wine) that rebelled in the early 16th Century, hence the Ghoul Cardinal still at its head, and harvest the Rites of Denial for "Benediction" (keep the Catholic names and prayers, but have them work through sanctified blood - that being a controlled resource and the dark nature there of actually makes it make in-setting sense that Malleus keeps their powers so secret, rather than the Church resurrecting the dead left-and-right).

        I don't think that's going to be popular.

        It takes out the Cainites who're just awesome. It makes it impossible to play a genuine god powered Catholic hunter; which is a very powerful archetype.

        For why the Church hides Benedictions, here's what I have. If they did the reaction would be divided into three groups:

        1) Genuine open minded skeptics (think Null Mysteriis) who'd say "yes that's very impressive, but how do I know it's God and not just witchcraft? I'd like to learn how to do it though"
        2) The average WoD mortal who's petrified of the supernatural and would flee from the Church.
        3) People who would become genuine believers in response. This group is smaller than #2 and most of them are in Church anyway.

                                    Personally my idea of the WoD god is a well meaning Deity who's gotten promoted beyond his competence. The Peter Principle in effect.

                                    He's basically the god of desert dwelling nomads. He understands about protecting his chosen people from bandits, from diseases, and making sure their crops and flocks are healthy enough to feed the tribes.

                                    Basically he's like an uneducated peasant who's son went of to university. He's very proud, but when his son comes back to ask for advice about problems he's having in the city or for help with his philosophy dissertation god has zero clue what to do. I imagine him watching a bunch of Rabbi's or Cardinals debating theology and alternating between thinking "how wonderful to have such smart children" and "I wish someone's sheep would get sick, then I'd be useful".

                                    Power wise he's roughly equivalent to things like Gaia, or the Hindu Pathenon.
