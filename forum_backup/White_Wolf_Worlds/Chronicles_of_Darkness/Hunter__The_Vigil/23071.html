              I had a Child Slasher, with a mix-matched undertaking. Basically I gave her the Mask's damage cap and gave her a Psycho's other characteristics. She would charm her way into homes and kill the families with a small folding pocket knife... And had apparently been doing it for years. Her parents kept moving her away from places until the little killer ran afoul my vampire Coterie. The littler monster didn't show up on Aura Reading, at all and always smiled while wearing a plastic tiara. She didn't act odd normally. She didn't kill animals. She even acted like she was having fun while killing people.

              The players decided that the kid was born soulless. I never came up with an explanation as to why she took so little damage, but it was quite the creepy effect.

                  My favorite antagonist is actually one that I created whilst playing as a PC in a game. For most of the game he worked alongside the other PCs and was even amicable and friendly... Right up until the point he got his hands on a power that put him head-and-shoulders above the other PCs. Then he engineered events to split them up and used his power to kill each and every one of them whilst they had no backup and support, ranting and just about frothing at the mouth from spewing bilious accusations of their self-righteousness and prejudice over so called 'corrupt' beings.

                  For some context, this was during a D&D3.5 gestalt campaign. I was playing a Fighter/Cleric devoted to Nerull alongside a Ranger/Artificer, a Psion/Monk and a Paladin/Druid devoted to Obad-Hai. He'd been fostering resentment and contempt since about the fourth session, the first time they came across a necromancer and the group unanimously expressed feelings of disgust and hatred. This shocked him as, until then, they'd all been fairly mellowed. They'd even treated amicably with an Orc tribe that had been displaced by a natural disaster and helped to secure new tribal lands for them. The other players were very scared by the sudden and seemingly inexorable slaughtering of their companions by the end of it, mainly because we sorted out each individual confrontation separate from those who didn't yet know what was going on. Calm, calculated cultists be crazy cool.


                  As for antagonists that I've come across as a player created by GMs... I've never really had one that scared me, or that even left a lingering impression. Sat here for ten minutes trying to think of one and can't come up with any details where the recollection is defined by my memory of the villain rather than my, or another player's, character.

                          Hmm, I've had a couple truly, despicable antagonists that my players fondly hated. The first was an androgynous and corpulent flesh-eating abomination that I was inspired by whilst reading through Immortals. While it looked vaguely humanoid, it had rolls of fat all over its body and face, and emitted a foul odor that poisoned the air around it. Its teeth was sharpened to the point where it could tear through raw flesh easily, and it prolonged its existence by cannibalizing other humans. The first time my players encountered it was when they disguised themselves to attend a masquerade ball hosted by demon-worshiping cultists. While they expected it, they weren't ready when the party turned into an orgy of sins. Eventually after stumbling around a little, they encountered this monster (they named it Pudge) eating screaming children chained to walls and cutting their flesh up with a giant cleaver. They almost blew their cover, but managed to control their emotions.

                          My favorite antagonist however was this slimy, pale lawyer named Melvin that was actually a ghoul working for one of the most powerful vampires in the city. He had greasy, slicked back hair and was extremely thin and tall. All of my players instantly hated him for his persistent wheedling and manipulation, pretending to be very close to the PCs and talking to them as if they were his best buds. All the while, he managed to create internal conflict within the cell to the point where none of the PCs trusted each other anymore, playing each of them against the other. After dissolving their trust, he uses his contacts with the police to arrest one of the PCs on charges of (somewhat accurate) possession of drugs and intent of distribution (the PC actually did deal in drugs, but the evidence the police found was planted by Melvin).

                          He then uses his master's resources and legal expertise to tie the character up in prison before secretly transporting the same PC (using his police contacts) to an abandoned warehouse for interrogation and torture. Meanwhile, he starts to blackmail another PC devoted to the Church into doing what he wants. Eventually he was killed after the PCs realized what had happened and banded together to rescue the PC being tortured. However, by that point the PC that had been imprisoned and interrogated had lost one of his eyes, gained a derangement that made him prone to emotional outbursts, and started developing a blood addiction. While the PCs had triumphed over Melvin, the psychological trauma and suffering the one PC had endured at Melvin's hands eventually led to their downfall later in the chronicle when he kills the other PCs after becoming insane and subsequently, possessed.

                              Not necessarily frightening but I did have a duo that made a nice impression on my PCs. It was during a Mage game, but probably would have worked as well if not better for Hunter. There was an NPC who had been fired from his job at a major toy factory in the area. He teamed up with a Centimani Promethean (more on him later) but he developed a way to "paint" his enemies as if they gave off Pyros. He'd then send them pandorans in the mail. They'd sense the false pyros and activate, tearing the poor schmuck to shreds. Once the PCs figured out what was really happening he was a pushover, the real shocker was the Centimani that lived in the guys basement and helped him gather the pandorans he needed. He had altered himself almost human centipede style and from the waist down he had a long torso/tail made of other upper torso, literally giving him many hands (though they functioned as legs) Really freaked the PCs out and I think they actually ended up running from him instead of trying to kill him.

                                  Actually, just remembered a pair of antagonists that I sent up against my players in a Dark Harvest: Legacy of Frankenstein game. The characters were working with the Resistance and had managed to steal the identity of a member of the nobility, so they decided to cash in and get themselves some top notch augmentations. As they were close to finishing their recovery from the surgeries, a pair of highly competent and tough-as-nails hitmen by the names Mnemor and Mori showed up and informed them that the jig was up. The players looked like they were going to cooperate but the moment they got ambulatory they pulled on the hitmen and scored two solid double taps, center mass. They were very surprised when their enemies completely ignored the bullet wounds and proceeded to pull out a pair of heavy pistols and start shooting the place up.

                                  The surgeon died during the scuffle and four civilians ended up catching lead outside as the players got desperate and started using human shields. They barely managed to get away and were badly wounded when they crossed into the next county. Ended up skipping ahead a few months for them to convalesce. They never encountered the Partners again, but they were paranoid about them for the rest of the game.

                                  Sometimes simple competence, along with highly effective and low-profile body armor, is all you need to scare the crap out of your players.

                                      My favourite antagonist that I've had in a game was most likely Alex Blanc. Ran a five session story arc (Each session with our group is about 10 hours long and equates to about a chapter... we make a day of it) where they were a Tier 1 Cell coming up against something that Tier 3 hunters might have some trouble with. Managed to rattle some of my players real hard in the first few sessions because it was nothing like what they were expecting.

                                      Back Story of Alex Blanc is that he was a story I wrote a few years back, I decided to write up some basic rules for what interacting with him would be like... considering that he doesn't exist this was hard. Not like "Oh my god he is a character in a game" doesn't exist but more like, "He fell out of reality and legitimately is now a white expanse that walks around creating ripples in what has and hasn't happened. Game started off with me informing the group that they were a Cell that had been opperating for a few years, had a couple of hunts under their belt and had just finished their last hunt. Gave each of them a dream sequence at the beginning to (quote unquote) teach them the style of the game I'd be running. Each one was very disconnected from the other: fighting against a series of animated toy soldiers, Running through a series of hallways where something was chasing them and trying to deal with a creature that was legitimately pulling out the wires of the walls in the basement of a building to heal up whilst the rest of the party was down for the count.

                                      Everyone then woke up at 3am, exactly... From there I just made them do their everyday things, go to their jobs and all that jazz. It was just a slice of life game, nothing strange was happening out of the ordinary apart from newspapers having missing articles and a bunch of school children going missing (Had died in a bus crash but Alex Blanc removed them from existence, making the bus crash articles disappear as well as people's memories of what had happened vanish along with them). Then I pulled one of my favourite whammys on my players.

                                      New Characters, First game, strange dreams... One of them checks their phone and goes through their messages from the night prior and they find a photo on their phone... I made them roll Resolve plus Intelligence to remember it... no memory. They call up the other members of the cell. Everyone else makes the same roll... nobody remembers what they were doing last night. The game had been going for over two hours last night and then they suddenly realized that nobody ever thinks of what their character was doing the day before the game starts... maybe the month before, maybe even the entire year or life of the character (hopefully life is plotted out a fair bit) but nobody actually actually plots out what their character had been doing the night before unless you, the storyteller, ask.

                                      They figure out that they were actually finishing the last hunt, they go to the location they'd tracked the creature to and then reality starts to warp even more. Actions that they take revert when Alex Blanc comes near, they kick down a door they hear sounds coming from behind of to find a staircase leading down... toy soldiers strewn everywhere in teh building they were in... the dreams were what they remembered from the hunt last night... the rest was wiped from existence. But they stand their at the top of the stairs discussing what their course of action is. One of them shoots down the stairs to see if they can spook what ever is down there out... only to find that the bullet never hits and just disappears. They continue talking... finally they agree that they'll attach someone to a rope, send them down the stiars and pull them back if anything bad happens, they pull out the rope and say "I head down the stairs" I tell them "You place your hand on the door to open it and a sudden feeling of dread fills you..." Everyone looked at me then it sunk in and someone's jaw dropped when they realized that the door had been kicked off it's hinges and something had put it back whilst they were trying to figure out what to do.

                                      The rest of the session legitimately was a clusterfuck of timelines overlapping, the past night repeating over the current night and hints of the man appearing and disappearing in and out of reality. Sound, light and all things just happening at the wrong time. Two players walked away legitimately shaken by the game. The next few sessions was them learning the mechanics I wrote down for how things happen in "The Folds" which was the area around Alex Blanc where things that were ripped from reality ended up and eventaully tracing him back to the original point where he was removed from Reality.

                                      Best Antagonist I've ever put my players up against. Either that or the Pre-God Machine Angel that I put them up against a couple years back. That was great... They kept on trying to deny it being an actual Angel that was killing people until they themselves had killed it and I told them "Yeah no, that was an Actual Angel." Killed it with a camera as well... ah... good times.
