    The Staff of Janus, The Gatekeepers

    07-20-2014, 11:08 AM
    Magic is found at the thresholds. It arises at dawn and dusk, it is found at the border between civilisation and barbarism, the one who is neither male nor female but something in-between wields it.

    Magic has all the danger of the threshold. It is familiar enough to tempt, but foreign enough to betray us. It is the time our world changes, and it is the door through which strangers enter for both good and for ill.

    But doors have gates, and gates have gatekeepers.

    The first Staff was a openly acknowledged Roman mystery cult. It’s adherents were merchants who incorporated rites and beliefs from the barbarians beyond the Empire. They prayed to Janis for protection on their voyages and they spoke of the unique wisdom that could only be seen by those with ties to both the Empire and the barbarians. The first Staff was denounced by Justin Martyr and broken by Emperor Constantine.

    The second Staff was formed from the ashes of the first, from everyone too devout to convert, and it hid itself in the furthest reaches of the Empire. It was the second Staff that discovered the Sabine Sacraments when their augur sent half his soul to Terra Incognita and conveyed the wisdom he learned from the god he met there. In the end the second Staff broke itself to form the third Staff.

    The third Staff is still with us today. It hides itself as a fraternal organisation of academics, financiers, gardeners, lawmen and librarians; A workers union of gardeners that no longer regulates a trade but invests it's old money and sponsors education. The true Gatekeepers sit at the heart of this organisation where they still practice the rites and faith taught by that long dead augur. They wield the sacraments to control the gates between Terra and Terra Incognita and from the gates flow power and wealth which they exchange with the outer circle for favours, money and the chance to select new recruits.

    The Gatekeepers have stood on the edges of civilisation and protected humanity from what lies beyond for almost a thousand years, and there is nowhere they’d rather be.

    The Enemy

    The Gatekeepers make one thing clear to every recruit. They are not heroes, they do not hunt monsters or rescue the innocent. They are gatekeepers, they guard the doors and peruse any illicit visitors. That's all they do.

    Central to the Gatekeeper's ideology is the idea of liminality. The Gatekeeper's are human, but they are humans who have adopted ideas, rituals and practices from beings who dwell beyond the world. They stand on the border between humanity and the other. This unique perspective gives the Staff, and the Staff alone, the wisdom to govern the gates and with it, the right to own and profit from the gates.

    Even if the Staff of Janus isn't as wise as they like to believe, they are certainly not fools. They know that the creatures who use the gates are liminial beings in themselves. Changelings were once human before they were twisted, and still partly are. Werewolves can become a man or a wolf as they will. The Bound have passed through the gates and returned with something from the other side.

    So how do the Gatekeepers justify their unique rights and privileges when others are clearly more liminal? The answer is the kind of self centred thinking common to most human organisations. We are humans, and this is humanities' world. No matter how liminal a supernatural being is, it belongs in it's own world. Providing the monsters understand that then the Gatekeepers will have no problem with them, and will even be willing to form alliances. There is nothing untoward about a border having a guard on both ends. The wealth and influence the Staff can call upon to offer sweeteners has led to some stable and successful alliances.

    Of all the monsters it is the fae with whom the Gatekeepers have the longest history. Other monsters may travel between different realms, but none create new doors with the casual ease that faeries do. The Staff of Janus fears that if left unchecked the Fae could tear down the walls and let their world flood into ours, sowing madness and mayhem in it's wake. They wield both diplomacy and guns in their attempt to stem the fae's travel, but their belief that even Changelings should not be permitted to live upon Earth has made them few friends.

    After the fae The Staff of Janus concerns itself with Werewolves and the Bound. Werewolves have a lot with in common with the staff, both believe that the borders between this world and others must be maintained and both believe they uniquely have the duty and right to protect and own the doors, so naturally they hate each other. The Bound are a diverse lot, and many could agree with the Staff's mission but very few will agree that they are supposed to live in the Underworld. Nobody wants to live in the Underworld. Still, The Bound rarely create new doors so the Staff takes a less hard-line view, and The Bound lack the instinctive dogma of the Werewolves. So there is some room for compromise on both sides and the Bound are so very bribeable.

    Witches and other Hunters are another kind of being that may try to travel through the gates. If they don’t then the Gatekeepers have no issue. If they wish to, well both Hunters and Witches tend to be liminal people anyway so the Staff would be happy to let them cross. At the right time and place, for a payment. If they play by the Gatekeepers rules both Witches and Hunters can find an ally, but if they insist on crossing under their own terms they will be enemies. Both are considered a source of recruits as well, but it is rare to find a Witch without her own incompatible understanding of the world and the Staff has no interest in recruits who do not have "the wisdom to manage the gates".

    Hunters
    You didn’t think much, at first, about the bricked-over archway in the basement of your new house. Once you moved in though, weird things started to happen. The milk kept disappearing, sometimes before the bottle had been opened. The cat somehow hung itself from the chandelier. No matter how many times you pulled the ivy off your bedroom window, it was back the next day. When you caught your landscapers trying to break in, you demanded to know what was going on – and they told you. Now you help them guard all the doorways, including the one you live over
    .
    You never planned to get involved in the occult. You're a financier, you deal in cold hard cash, business opportunities. Though you won't say no to formal dinners and tax deductible donations. Then one day it happened, something disguised as a human made you a trade that was too good to be true, the beginning of a plan to get your soul. You didn't know what it was, but your gardener did. He told his superior, his superior told the augur, and she told you. You're still in trouble, that thing was good at what it does, but you've got someone on your side. That's more than most people in your situation ever had.

    About a month after you finished probation at the library your boss showed you an honest to god secret passage filled with books, mostly in Latin. She taught you to the passcodes members will use, gave you a promotion, told you what books you should start with if you wanted to learn real magic, and promised that you would never work again if you told anyone. Being a librarian for a secret conspiracy is just like being a regular librarian, it still keeps you awake at night.

    You were looking for an edge at the office. There was rumour one of your peers was promoted because she was in the same club as your boss, so you thought there would be no harm in joining yourself. At worst your fingers would get a little dirty. Then you found out the club’s “communal garden” is much bigger than it should be, and some of the exits don’t exactly lead to Kansas. Even though you’ve managed to trade your silence and help for that promotion you wanted, you’re not happy. No one seems to know what happened to the woman who got promoted before you.

    You always thought the Worshipful Company of Gardeners haven't had anything to do with gardening since the medieval ages, but when they explained they employed a few landscapers just for tradition's sake it seemed reasonable enough. The pay was above average, the job was secure, and if you worked at it you could get a great deal from a bank or law firm at the delicious dinners. Over time you overheard a little hear and there, until without quite knowing when it happened you realised you were part of a conspiracy with a basic understanding of what you were doing. So long as they don't ask you to do anything weirder than plant extra mistletoe you don't mind.

    Your college Latin club never had more than three members. So when one of your late-night meetings was interrupted by ominous chanting in your favourite language, you couldn’t resist investigating. Unfortunately, the cultists you discovered decided a three-person sacrifice would be just the offering to give their master after summoning it to this dimension. If the Staff hadn’t intervened to prevent the gate from being opened, you would all be worse than dead. After that, you’ve been eager to offer the organization all the help you can – although you’ve started to wonder, was saving your souls just an afterthought?

    You were part of an ancient Oxford drinking club. You were also secretly transsexual. Now you always thought those two facts were completely unrelated until the professor who chaired your club tapped you on the shoulder. One highly surreal journey later you returned to Earth in the body you'd always dreamed off, and all the paperwork had been taken care of. Your professor is getting rather old and needs somebody to replace him. On Monday you'll start learning how to secretly prepare people for a journey like your own, and learning who is worthy to travel through the gates, and how they might repay The Gatekeeper's for the privilege.

    Stereotypes

    Les Mysteries: They live on the borders and see the worlds as you only can when you live outside them; yet they would throw open the doors, tear down the walls and see us all become one. I suppose even clear sighted people can disagree, I have faith that in time our wisdom will be proven correct.

    Malleus Maleficarum: First throw away the keys, then forget the gates exist, let the walls crumble down and put a thin line of armed guards in their place. How did those fools ever seize the crown?

    Maiden's Blood Sisterhood: After centuries of famine the old knowledge is returning in the most unlikely of places. Wait for understanding to bloom them welcome them into our garden.

    Network Zero: You can see much when you're outside of society, but without gates of wisdom your mind is open to weeds of misinformation.

    Structures
    The majority of Gatekeepers form The Grain. With a gun they guard the doors and peruse intruders into the world. With a watering can and sheers they shape the landscape, drawing on the protective power of plants to lock and seal the gates into the world.
    Free Speciality: Crafts (Gardening) or Occult (Plants)

    When the time is right stewardship of a door passes from The Grain to The Key. For a limited time the gate is opened and, for the right sort, passage is permitted. The Key are diplomats who welcome guests and merchants who profit from trade, but they are also guards who keep out the wrong sort.
    Free Speciality: Empathy (Liminal People)

    Supporting both The Grain and The Key is The Arch. Formed of investigators and scholars The Arch seeks out undiscovered gates and studies known doors. They assess the nature and danger of each, determining how much effort should be dedicated to keeping the door shut, and under what circumstances travel should be permitted.
    Free Speciality: Investigation (Gates) or Occult (Gates)

    Status

    In the Gatekeepers Status is strictly defined between the inner and outer circles. At one or two dots you are a member of the fraternal order that the Staff of Janus uses as a cover story. You do favours for other members, and the real Gatekeepers, they do favours for you. You might be a armed hunter or you might know nothing about the supernatural and just donate money or cover for your employees when they need extra time to work on a certain garden.

    Even with just one dot of Status a Gatekeeper may be trained in Sabine Sacraments, but either they don’t know why they’re being asked to grow these special plants or they told exactly what it’s for but led to believe it’s a harmless kooky tradition.

    At Status three a Hunter knows the basics: They know that the Gates are real, the Staff of Janus has the sacred duty to guard them, that only liminal people have a chance of mastering the wisdom needed to govern the gates, and that the Sacraments are the secret tools they use. Further mysteries, occult truths, and details of the Gatekeeper’s theology are revealed at Status four and five.

    Climbing the ladder requires service to the Conspiracy’s mission, performing favours for your fellow Gatekeepers, honouring Janus with rites, and demonstrating you have the wisdom necessary to guard the gates by mastering the theology revealed at your current status.

    However if you are not a liminal person you will never rise beyond the second dot of Status. It doesn’t matter how well you debate the theology, a central tenant states that only liminal people can acquire the wisdom. Any evidence to the contrary is summarily ignored.

    Liminal People
    So if you’re thinking about playing a Gatekeeper you’re probably wondering if you should play a liminal person; so how do the Staff of Janus define this central pillar of their philosophy?

    Janus is the god who governs the fluid spaces on the boundaries, it is no surprise that his worshippers understand that liminality itself is fluid. The Staff looks for anyone who is symbolically between two states, but they also look for someone who’s been alienated for their perspective; they believe wisdom requires an outsiders perspective.

    So if you want to play a liminal person by the Staff of Janus’ perspective what you have to do is simple: Pick two aspects of human existence and explain why your character exists between them, then explain why this gives your character an outsider’s perspective on society.

    O: Whether it's at the university, wall street or just a blue collar landscaping company the Gatekeeper's network of connections and back room deals has given you a leg up. Take a dot of Status applied to a completely mundane organisation in the financial, academic, gardening, law enforcement, literacy, legal, or museum world.

    OOO: You've advanced beyond the front organisations and are dedicated to the Gatekeeper's sacred, secret, mission. You've been trained in the lore of gates and briefly travelled to at least two different realms. As a result of your focused education you can take the Unseen Sense merit applied to gates. Your sense is fine enough to tell when a gate opens or closes.

    OOOOO: You're recognised for your wisdom and successes, traits the Staff are keen to see in future generations. You are introduced to a three dot Retainer who will serve you loyally in exchange for the opportunity to observe and learn.

    Endowment: Sabine Sacraments

    The Gatekeepers practice secrets rites of hawthorn and mistletoe. The outer circle believe that gardening is just a metaphor and a brand image, the inner circle know the truth. The Staff of Janus grow their magic from the ground. All the money men and power players are only there to aid the gardeners in their duties.

    Sabine Sacraments are tied to a location, and a place has only so much room to grow plants. Use the Safehouse Size Merit from Hunter: the Vigil as a guide. If the Gatekeepers are limited to potted plants and hardwood furniture then they may have one dot of Sabine Sacraments per dot of Safehouse Size. If there is a garden they may have two dots of Sabine Sacraments per dot of Safehouse Size. Finally if there is enough outdoor space that they can engage in landscaping they may have three dots of Sabine Sacraments per dot of Safehouse Size.

    Picture: A gardener steps through an archway made of twisted tree branches - leaves and red berries grow from the arch. He is wearing boots, outdoor clothes and a flatcap. In his left hand he holds a hoe over one shoulder - it has mistletoe wrapped around the shaft. In his right hand a submachine gun hangs by his side. He looks like he is whistling.

    Cloneing Corridor (OOOOO): One of the most powerful Sacraments, a cloning corridor is a short corridor shaped like the letter P. Anyone who approaches from the bottom and traverses the corridor will arrive at the mid section a short while before they depart, assuming they maintain a steady pace. In defiance of all causality it is perfectly safe to travel into the past and then stop your past self from travelling into the past - effectively cloning yourself.

    After about an hour or as soon as he steps out of the area affected by Sabine Sacraments the future clone will vanish as reality overcomes the supernatural effect, but until then you have a completely expendable pawn of yourself; well some clones find the experience terrifying but others calmly throw themselves in-front of bullets to protect their "real" self. After all, they remember exactly what they were doing and why they did it when they cloned themself.

    Attacking the wrong safehouse can be an effort in futility as the Gatekeepers retaliate with an infinite number of expendable clones. Find a trick that number's won't counter, or go elsewhere.

    Drawback: Seeing yourself die, or seeing yourself slaughtered again and again is naturally a Breaking point for all but the most hardened hunter.

    Faerie Treasure (O - OOOOO): The Gatekeepers can create pocket spaces to hide their equipment from prying eyes. Each dot of Faerie treasure provides one free dot of Safehouse Cache and increases the penalty to find all caches in the building by one.

    Example: A safehouse has Cache 3 and Faerie Treasure 2. Thus the Hunters have a total of Cache 5, and any attempt by intruders to find a cache takes a -7 penalty. That nice hardwood chest of draws isn’t hard to find, but unless you know the secret way of opening there’s no way you’ll discover that it contains more guns than could possibly fit inside.

    Drawback: Bending space requires precision. If the sacrament is damaged everything inside is expelled through a vortex of twisting spacetime. Unless it’s supernaturally durable it is most certainly destroyed.

    Fast Library (OO): The Staff of Janus like to build their libraries in bubbles of accelerated time. A safehouse with this Sacrament has a place where time flows ten times faster. It works just as well if just want a place to bring borrowed books.

    Drawback: The internet is really slow inside the time bubble.

    Janus’ Door (OOO): The Gatekeeprs grow special doors. When a cutting is taken from one of Janus’ Doors, carved into a key, and used to unlock another, for a moment the two open directly to each other.
    The Staff of Jannus don’t see much need to smuggle more than their own personal and equipment, but they’re one of the most mobile Conspiracies in the world. The Gatekeeper’s network of transport hubs move men and equipment across continents in the blink of an eye.

    Looking Post (OO): If you have a gate to protect it’s a good idea to see what’s trying to get in. A Looking Post is an empty wooden frame, or several frames, that let you see into the other side of a gate to Terra Incognita.
    Drawback: While it’s harder for a monster to physically break through a Looking Post than a tightly sealed gate, some monsters operate on different principles and might be able to exploit a Looking Post . Consequently Looking Posts are designed to be easily destroyed from several points around the safehouse.

    Onion House (O - OOOO): By growing plants just right the Staff of Janus can make buildings bigger on the inside. Each dot of Onion House adds +10 size points. Handy if you have a large number of Hunters to hide.
    Drawback: Onion Houses were intended as hiding places, not battle ready fortresses. If the building (or more likely, the garden outside) is severely damaged everything inside is forced back into regular geometry through a vortex of twisting spacetime. Anything without supernatural durability is destroyed and the results of trying to force a large building into a small one is usually explosive.

    Seal the Gate (O - OOOOO): The most sacred of all Sacraments affects one single gate. Any monster who wishes to enter our world through that gate takes a dice penalty equal to Seal the Gate. A monster who can open gates without a roll will instead roll Potency - Seal the Gate. The Gatekeepers, of course, can can create keys to their own locks.
    In addition the Staff may take cuttings from the plants used to seal a gate. If a monster successfully opens the gate from either side these cutting immediately wither and die alerting the Gatekeepers. A monster with a power to prevent this must roll more successes than dots in Seal the Gate.

    Seal the Path (O - OOO): The counterpart to Seal the Gate. Seal the Path protects a safehouse from approach on Earth, and without a supernatural gate to hold shut it is consequently more powerful. A monster cannot enter or project supernatural power into protected area unless their Potency is greater than Seal the Path * 2.
    The protection from this Sacrament rarely forms a perfect square or circle, though any competent Gatekeeper can ensure the protection extends beyond the plants used as a focus - so even without a garden you can normally protect the entire safehouse.

    Sadly nothing stops monsters from standing outside the protected area and shooting mundane weapons at trees until something gives.

    Wards (O - OOOOO): Each dot of Wards provides two free dots of the Safe Place or Safehouse Secrecy Merit (or one each). In Essence the Staff of Janus get half price on safehouses.

    Watchtower (O): A simple trick lets the Staff of Janus create windows (well empty window frames) that look out from a nearby vantage point. Though this sacrament has been mostly made obsolete by CCTV, freeing up garden space for other magic, it is sometimes still used. It is particularly common to have the vantage point be somewhere above the safehouse, especially if thick foilage hides the safehouse from sight but blocks visibility of potential threats.

    Thaumaturge Tradition: Augur
    The Sabine Sacraments are the most precious of The Gatekeepers secret for anyone, from the wisest librarian to the most worldly gardener can learn and use their power. However every generation process a few geniuses who master Janus’ most complex teachings and unlock a greater magic.

    Their rituals of hawthorn and mistletoe are performed in minutes, not months, using a combination of occult herbology, ingesting natural plant hallucinogens and poisons, and animal sacrifice. All Augurs wield the powers of Distortion - the power to warp space and time. Boundaries - the power of connections and causality. And of course Augury - the power to see into the past or the future.

    Any spell cast by an Augur can be made permanent by investing dots of Sabine Sacraments equal to the spells dots. If an Augur for reasons of her own created a doorway to the spirit realm then she is well within her rights to direct the Staff’s gardeners until a three dot Sabine Sacrament makes it permanent - just so long as gardeners continue to tend it (and in this particular case, further sacraments could be used to secure the new gate).

    Augur’s use the rules, but not the fluff, of PenDragon’s Sibyllines. They have no link to the Mysterium, and their version of Distortion is explicitly very good at working with passageways to Terra Incognita.


    New Rotes:

    Fortify Temporal Boundaries (Boundaries OOO)

    The Augur fortifies the boundaries between divisions of time, any time a dicepool penalty or a cost is incurred by crossing boundaries of time it doubles.

    Example: Julia has invited a Witch to their safehouse, the Witch is willing to negociate a very good price for passage into the Underworld. Julia knows that the Witch is nosy and will attempt to discover the Gatekeepers secrets but is willing to take the risk. She also knows that the Witch may open her third eye for Potency turns and must spend a point of Willpower every turn to keep it open afterwords. So she performs a ritual to invoke Fortify Temporal Boundaries. While within the affected area the Witch can open her third eye for Potency Turns and must spend two willpower a turn to keep it open afterwords.

    Redraw the Maps (Boundaries OOOO)

    The Augur defines one place as part of another. Supernatural effects which affect a place such as "my house" instead of an area will follow the Augur's design. Some Augur's protect themselves form the Wyrd Sorcery of their faerie enemies by puting part of their safehouse outside the building, but most use it to project the effects of Sabine Sacraments far beyond the gardens in which they are grown. (Pre existing Sacraments gradually grow into the new space, it takes one scene per dot).

    Modify Temporal Boundaries (Boundaries OOOOO)

    The Augur moves temporal boundaries. Each success moves a boundary by one fifth of the contained time (so 72 minuets for a day, a day and a half for a 30 day month). Any supernatural effect that ends or begins on a set day, year, etc will follow the Augur's design providing it is within the affected area. An Augur could make a deal with a faerie and then extend or deminish it's duration. Naturally this has no effect on mundane contracts, but some lawyers have reported feeling inexplicably uncomfortable when they argued so.

    There is considerable debate between Augurs about whether other Gods would defer to Janus as the final authority on bounderies, whether Janus' role in the Pantheon forbids him from doing so, and of course weather mere Augurs have any right to put their God in such a position. Suffice to say that The Staff of Janus don't consider curiosity a good enough reason to move another God's holy day around and see what happens.

    In short, I'm not saying what this power does to Benediction's +5 on feast days.

      The Gatekeepers belong to what I'm (inaccurately) calling my "phase two" of Hunter homebrew. Hunter's who's goals are not actually about killing monsters. The Staff of Janus has plenty of oppotunities for conflict, but they spend even more time growing plants, prophesising about stock options and generally acting like a highly profitable merchant company who's executives love to kick back at the suprisingly inclusive club for old boys.

      I would go so far as to say a Gatekeeper game could look a lot like Awakenening. Lots of readng books, politicking in a very old school leather armchair setting, and investigating mysteries. This goes triple if you actually play Augurs.

      What I'm most disapointed with is the names for the Sacraments, so suggestions there are welcome. As are additional Sacraments. Speaking of which, I have suprised myself by creating a group who's Endowment is even less combat foucsed than the Lord Stewards. Which is impressive since the Lord Steward's most combative endowment was the ability to make mundane social rolls on non-humans. (Maybe they'll fight for you if you ask nicely?). There's no particular reason for this, since the Gatekeepers are more militant than the Lord Stewards (who isn't?) - I just didn't think of any. Some nifty powers to use when defending your safehouse would be fitting though.

      Speaking of the Sacraments, whole aspect of them only working in your safehouse is another experiment. I think they have great potential, but only if their advantages are showcased by the story. Or rather, if you stop Hunter's usual trait of ignoring just how much trouble the law would be for large internatinoal armed terrorist groups; because basically what a group who ships armed people around worldwide to blow up stuff is going to look like, and arguably what they are. If the Malleus Maleficarum is constantly moving safehouse to avoid the scandal of priests being found killing people and the airports have stringent checks against people transporting guns - suddenly that network of teleportation doors looks invaluable. Instead of "we have one cell who can be there tomorrow, and a dozen witin a week" it becomes "how many people do you need in the next couple of hours?"


      Finally. Augurs, they're something I've wanted to see for a while. Blood Sorcery style imprvised magicians in most of the Conspiracies, who gain special bonuses when you combine their magic with Endowments. I never really got Mage, because Willworking never felt like magic. Blood Sorcery, with it's rituals and extended actions for everything, actually does feel like magic. All it needed is a few more styles for veriety, and hunter provided a ready made setting. I probably should write my own version of Augurs but they really would look exactly like Pendragon's. At most a few custom Janus themed rotes, and of course different fluff.


      Finally you all know who origonally had this awesome idea. Then abandoned it, left it to rot, and will suffer for his crimes. MWAHAHAHAHA cheers for the idea Glamourweaver. Now finish off KSI29
