    How common are "monsters"?

    01-14-2015, 08:43 PM
    This is a question I am sure is bandied about a lot, but permit me to ask it again: How common is the supernatural in the World of Darkness?

    This isn't a question I expect an authoritative answer to; I'm asking here for how folks have done it in their own games. How common is magic, big and small? How likely is someone to stumble across it? I ask this in part as a general nWoD question but also for Hunter in particular.

    Are you likely to walk into your local occult odds-and-ends store and find some odd talisman? How often in a city do you hear of strange deaths? Could a Hunter who is particularly alert find a new supernatural creature every day? Or do they typically go years without seeing something odd? How common are both "big" and "small" types of magic? Do you find people all over the place with small, odd abilities - perhaps not even aware of them - if you know how to look?

              I think the cop-out answer applies there as well, humorously. If your PCs are groaning and rolling their eyes because every session is a Monster of the Week episode then you're definitely undermining the game's mood yourself.

              On the other hand some games have different levels. To me, Vampire's night life is teeming with blood-sucking, soul-devouring, flesh-eating, memory-consuming predators that potentially lurk behind every dark shadow - Kindred or not - but that's a mood. That's not a presented outline of every creature and its abilities and motives. It's about the creeping terror that, as a mortal, something is in that sketchy alley and you're its prey.

              Especially in the 2.0 edition the books definitely imply that mortals know or mostly believe/guess in the supernatural but they don't talk about it

                          Also keep in mind that pretty much every playable supernatural has some kind of rule about keeping vanilla mortals in the dark as much as possible, be it the Masquerade, the Veil, or "The Herd Must Not Know."                               As long as you keep pressenting it in a justified light, you can do whatever you please. I have an example.
                              In a Hunter scenario I'm part of, the Storyteller made the mistake of allowing us to stumble upon Demon, after Mage, after Conspiracy after Ghost, after Werewolf, after Occultist, after Criptid, after Spirit, and it just seemed like things were so laughably common that it made the entire "This is a secret!" seem like a bad sell.
                              On the other hand, I run a Sin-Eater scenario myself, in which I've introduced a Cabal of Mages and a family of Vampires all within a relatively short time. The Vampires were contacted by the Sin-Eaters themselves ("Hey look! That dude is dead! Lets go talk to him!"), and the Mages contacted the Sin-Eaters after they had caused some loud metaphysical 'noise' with a Ghost. I dont think this undermined the secretive part of it, because the only reason these things happened, was because the Sin-Eaters allready have a foot firmly steeped in the realms of supernaturality.

                              So to bring it back to a Hunter relevant scenario; Make sure events tie together. If The Cell encounters some Mages, some Ghosts/Spirits and some Werewolfs, all within the same month and entirely disconnected from each other, thats gonna seem cheap. But if they encounter some Mages, who gets pissed at the Hunters for interfering in their work, who then sends some Ghosts/Spirits to kill the Hunters, and the Werewolfs are enemies of the Mages, wishing to ally themselves with the Hunters to bring down the Mages, then it doesn't seem so cheap, since one event acted as a trigger for everything else. Potentially, everything can be tied together like this. As long as the trigger event was the same, you can hinge almost as much as you please on that. Its the disconnected triggers, thats gonna cheapen the deal for people, I believe.

                                  I usually go "rare, but influential". In a large city, New York for example, 20 vampires can still have a pretty sizable impact on the experience for most humans: three or four reasonably powerful vampires manage to ghoul/Dominate/whatever a few middling-to-high officials, use the influence to affect building zones and suchforth. Roughly the same number of mages will cause plenty of friction, given mages tendencies to work together only reluctantly (even the Conventions or whatever have plenty of internal friction), and so on. Say 10 Bound, 10 Uratha (and 15 Pure), 15 Changelines and maybe 100 Misc gives a supernatural to human ratio of roughly 1:85,000.

                                  This ratio has the advantage of having the supernatural numbers low enough that finding them is difficult for Hunters, and makes the players pretty important: 3 to 6 out of a population of 20 is a significant proportion. And players should always be important in how games unfold, otherwise what's the point of the game.

                                          With the story I'm running, I have set it in my hometown of Winnipeg, which is the largest city in the province and the capital. It has a relatively well-known reputation for strange and unusual things, and so I've represented that accordingly by having people with minor supernatural powers be fairly common, and people are at least marginally aware of the supernatural. Ghosts are pretty well believed in- there's a room at a hotel downtown that is always booked for 8 months straight because it's the most haunted in the hotel. Most people know that the Legislative Building, the provincial capital, was built according to occult mathematics. In my game, it's an open secret among the Unchained that it's a God-Machine Facility, but no one wants to get close enough to find out what.

                                          Perhaps more importantly, because of how spread out everything in the province is, and how important having lots of people around is to concealing most supernatural beings, there's a pretty high concentration of them. I think the most numerous I have are minor supernaturals like stigmatics, then Changelings, then Mages. Though, to be fair, the Changelings and Mages sometimes even come from different provinces. It's a weird city, with a lot of different powerful supernatural crap going on- they can't help but come. More importantly for the game (and unfortunately for real life) there's a pretty high rate of violent crime and missing people. 5,000 people go missing yearly, and about 250 stay missing- more or less. My personal opinion is that real life should help write the story. Based on where you're setting the game, and what goes on in that town, and what it's like around it, how much supernatural weirdness can you get away with before any semblance of a Masquerade is gone?

                                          Even by my, probably, generous numbers, the overtly supernatural community forms only about 0.14% of the population of the city. So, it really depends on what kind of story you want to run.

                                              I agree very heavily with the last part of what Arcanist said. It really depends on what kind of story you're trying to tell. Part of the emphasis I want in my own Geist chroronicle, is that the Sin-Eater exist as gestalts, in a quasi-state between two worlds, which are unable or unwilling to reconcile each other on their own. I want this thematic to echo out throughout the different supernatural groups in London, all of whom have a tenuous, but intensely xenophobic and fearful peace with each other with alot of unresolved hate and animosity even amongst themselves. Sort of a laissez faire approach to diplomacy and peace. Only the Sin-Eaters really understands or appriciates that a middle and common ground is needed for everything to work smoothly.
                                              If I want to run with this thematic, I need the supernaturals to be somewhat more pleantyful than they might otherwise be. If theres only about 20 of them total in the city, the thematic falls immensely flat.

                                              However, in a typical (perhaps typical is a wierd word, given Hunters notoriously varied gameplay) Hunter story, this isn't a thematic you can really run with, and neither is it a thematic you'de want to run with, if you had the chance. Ultimately, I think it comes down to figuring out what sort of thematic and/or story you want, and populating your setting accordingly.

                                                  In my city, I have a few rough numbers. 70 (Used to be 120 but Strix happened) Kindred in the city, 65 Uratha (3/4ths Forsaken due to the Pure pinning everyone in the city), 45 Awakened (Including Seers and Left handed), 5 Prometheans, 80 Lost, 30 Sin eaters, and around 20 Demons. There are also 15 Arisen but only 3 are active at any given time.

                                                  Hunter Cells (as in plural) number around 80 (3-7 members each) and the vast majority of the time, they rarely deal with the known supernaturals types at all as they are either devoted to researching endowments, working their professions, or going after stuff that WoD throws out as a matter of course. I would say about only about half actually know of the known supernatural types while around 24 cells actually work on dealing with them (through conflict or trade) on a regular basis.

                                                  The modern mega city is a freakin' huge place. Weird shit happens all the time and there's more than enough space in the sandbox where Hunters can hunt weird shit without dealing with any of the splats at all.
