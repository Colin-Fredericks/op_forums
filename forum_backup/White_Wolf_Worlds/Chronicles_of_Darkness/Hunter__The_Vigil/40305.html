    So who hates who, and which hunters tolerate what supernaturals?

    12-31-2013, 10:36 PM
    Some hunter groups formed specifically to fight a particular type of creature, and were surprised to learn that other types of critters even existed.

    On the other hand, others are pretty single-minded, or have broad policies, like the Cheiron Group's 'bag and tag' or Network Zero 'gotta catch 'em all (on camera)'

    Now, there are bits of fluff here and there about how each hunter group deals with various supernaturals, but has anyone seen anything like a big relationship chart with "The Union ---Tolerates--->Mages" Or "The Long Night ---Hates---> Vampires." (Setting aside the views of individual hunters, and assuming that members of a certain splat haven't yet done anything to get a hunter gunning for them, of course.)

    If not, I was thinking of making such a chart, and would appreciate input on how the various big factions deal with each other on a very general basis.

      There's a bit of a problem with this, in that Hunter groups are hardly uniform. Unless a compact or conspiracy is founded around hunting a particular thing that goes bump in the night, the approach can and will vary from city to city. Also, a lot of them just don't really give a damn about the origins of the supernatural wreaking havoc on their watch. Still, if I were to make a rough rundown of the core groups...

      The Ashford Abbey members will tend to go after anything that promises an exciting, adrenaline-pumping hunt. The type of supernatural isn't terribly important to them, I think.

      The Long Night is a very down-to-earth group, and their members come from all sorts of environments. I don't think they have it out for a particular type of supernatural, nor do they overlook any type.

      The Loyalists of Thule are among the least violent groups, and they target supernaturals based on the knowledge they can gain, the knowledge they already have, and, of course, the trouble a given supernatural thing gets up to.

      Network Zero wants to expose supernaturals and it doesn't really matter to them which ones they capture on camera. Having said that, I suppose their mission might put them in conflict with vampires relatively often, given the bloodsuckers' Masquerade - no other supernatural has such a strict law governing their secrecy from the eyes of mortals.

      Null Mysteriis, unsurprisingly, also doesn't care. They're there to study the so-called "supernatural", and any opportunity to do so is welcome. Individual members may specialize in a given type of monster, but as a group, they don't have a focus.

      The Union's general approach is clear - if it threatens the cell's territory, it needs a good whack on the head. Whether it's an undead blood-drinker or an insane faery doesn't matter all that much.

      So, it would look like that in case of the core Compacts, the chart would be filled with "depends on the situation". It gets a bit more interesting as we get to the Conspiracies:

      Aegis Kai Doru, obviously, has it out for magic users and shapeshifters. They'll fight them without mercy. Everything else is more relative, although they're likely to target supernaturals that possess some interesting occult objects.

      Ascending Ones don't seem focused on any particular supernatural type. They'll fight, negotiate or otherwise deal with everything that threatens their people... or infringes on their drug-trafficking business, as the case may be.

      The Cheiron group, much like you said, has a "bag and tag" policy, and their hunters tend to hunt whatever the higher-ups tell them to.

      The Lucifuge aren't discriminating, although I imagine they have a particular interest in demons. It's not necessarily aggressive, though.

      As far as Malleus Maleficarum goes, all supernaturals are the spawn of Satan, whether they know it or not, and the only thing to do with them is to send them back to the Pit. Sure, they've had a lot of success hunting vampires in the past, and their name implies a witch-hunting bent, but I don't think it has much of an impact on their night-to-night hunting.

      The Task Force: Valkyrie goes after whatever their superiors decide threatens the interests of the US and its citizens... although obviously, their superiors are actually vampires.

      That's my assessment of the core groups. It gets less uniform as we look at splatbooks, of course.

          Keep in mind that many Ashwood Abbey chapterhouses aren't even into murder-hunts and the like. Some of them just seek out partying with vampires, or magic-induced rushes, getting high on Goblin Fruit, werewolf sex, etc etc. This touch was one of the subtler and better additions of the Compacts & Conspiracies book.

          Generally the most reliably moral groups (i.e. the one most likely to actively judge a supernatural's creature's actions and only kill them if they kill people [or do equally horrible things like steal sells, sell victims to the Gentry, etc]; and leave innocent people that just happen to be supernatural alone) at least at Tier 3 are the Lucifuge and the Ascending Ones. From an external (Doylist) perspective, they get to be this way because they get their requisite Tier 3 darkness from other places: the Lucifuge from their powers & blood-heritage, the Ascending Ones from their drugs & drug dealing.

          For the same reason, Malleus Maleficarum is one of the most prejudiced and hypocritical factions when it comes to the supernatural - because their powers would be a shining beacon of spiritual hope in and of themselves, so the group's nature needs to be in stark contrast.

          As I'm rewriting Task Force VALKYRIE (who do NOT work for Vampires, but can & do work with them at the top), they have a working-relationship with a couple of Supernatural factions that represent entrenched power and the status-quo. Groups like Invictus and the Seers of the Throne. They recognize that all out warfare with the supernatural power-players is a terrible idea, and not remotely worth the cost of the resulting social upheval - and thus they are willing to align with stability, fight supernatural threats to said stability, and accept the toll Invictus and the Seers take on the American people as "acceptable losses" given the alternatives. This truth is not known by 99% of VALKYRIE agents, but it does shape their orders from above.

                      Since this is Hunter, Mage's themes are mostly irrelevant (and the Ladder, arguably the whole pentacle on a bad day, are extra legal authoritarians too). Now that's over with there's two ways too look at this.

                      The first is that point to VALKYRIE is that they have chosen stability over protecting people, and that leads to them selling out.

                      The second is the point to VALKYRIE is that they have sold out, and that's the underlying reason they have chosen stability.

                      I much prefer the first one as I think it leads to a more consistent characterisation. To VALKYRIE stability is only one facet of their larger goal: Protecting America. The Invicticus don't actually want to threaten America, they don't want to subvert the president, they just want a big fat pile of cash and the masquerade - there's room for common ground with TF:V. The Seers of the Throne on the other hand have philosophical goals that require bending mortal governments to their will; that's a direct threat to America. TF:V might sell out some random guy on the streets but not their own govnment.

                      What's more, one of those philosophies is literally Divide Humanity. TF:V want stability. The Seers of the throne explicitly want chaos and divisions in humanity.


                      TF:V making bad deals with the supernatural in exchange for stability is fine. However the Seers just don't make sense as a partner.
