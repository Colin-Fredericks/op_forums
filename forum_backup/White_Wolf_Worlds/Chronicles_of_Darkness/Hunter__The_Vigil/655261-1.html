The ideas of compilations of various methods comes from years of going through the process of working on the original models of understanding until it's broken and requires a new model. In the process, hunters can and will have factually fault models that nevertheless work for them. It gets more complicated when those hunters embrace powers that defy the normal rules of functions, but so long as the model answers the questions, hunters don't stress too much about the details. Most of the conspiracies(Oh hey, significance of that word again) are at this point, where they have a model that answers for most everything they'll come across and makes unusual cases easy to not worry about, Once those cases gain mass and break the model, then they might revise it, but for them, it'll take centuries to do.

                                                    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
                                                    Hunters get monsters mixed up all the time. Classifying monsters is hard. But that doesn't stop them from trying. Their lives are on the line, they care deeply about correctly classifying monsters.
                                                    I don't think we are debating that is not the case. It is only human nature after all. What we are debating is its effectiveness in practice, especially when it comes to specific banes. After all, you and I know enough about the setting to realize there is a difference between a darkling changeling draining the energy of somebody or a vampire. The Hunter will not, at least not at first sight, and unless he comes across a lot of changelings, he is never going to realize that it was anything else besides a vampire with odd powers. I certainly feel that Hunters just do not come across enough monsters for any meaningful classification, except a vague notion of the various creatures. Tier 3 hunters might have a better go at it, but they run across another problem: too many monsters with too widely different powers that makes classification hard. As a result, basing your hunting tactics on any classification is just as dangerous as using none. It just helps you narrow the field a bit from the start.

                                                    Mind you, it has nothing to do with the fact that the castigations apparently work against True Fae and other extra-dimensional threats. Although that might be more of a ruling to make the powers more fun for the players, since it would otherwise be rather limited in a typical Monster-of-the-Day campaign.
