                Updating slashers at the same time is kind of low-hanging fruit; they were introduced as Hunter antagonists when Hunter rolled out, they also risk willpower, Undertakings could probably interact with the Condition/Beat system somehow, etc.

                I feel like cult and cult organization rules are broadly applicable, better suited to a World of Darkness book that brought an elaborated Mummy-style cult system to everybody.

                A lot of the Endowments and Tactics could use a reworking, just...generally. I think the Aegis Kai Doru need a revamping with the recognition that "Relic" is a merit anyone can basically take, and "has a magic item" is too broadly applicable a character concept to sustain a conspiracy's big trick.

                I'd envision giving them something else, and opening up Relic as an endowment any tier of hunter can take - "General Endowments", say. Could also be used to model low-level ritual magic with a Blood Sorcery -style system for the "Ritual" endowment.

                                    Originally posted by MachineIV View Post
                                    And yes, there's plenty of room for a Chronicle book. If, hypothetically, I were to pitch to do one, I probably wouldn't go with slashers or cults. I would definitely, hypothetically, go for something that screams "monster," not just messed up human.
                                    If you're interested I'd love to debate the merits of cults vs something-screams-monster with you? You've made a lot of great decisions and I'd like to see what I'm not seeing about the other options.

                                    The reason I think cults are appropriate is two fold: Diversity and Scalability.

                                    Firstly diversity, Hunter has always put the ability to play hunters vs any other WoD gameline as a core feature and I think making a specific type of monster into a strixx like core focus could detract from that. Cults on the other hand can, more or less, tie into every gameline. Vampires and Mages lead cults of mortals. Cults can worship Spirits or True Fae. Etc.


                                    Secondly scalability. With cults you can increase the supernatural ability of the cultists/cult leaders independently of the cult's mundane resources like size, influence and wealth. For most monsters the two are a bit more linked (Vampires use their mental powers to acquire resources, and even if they don't a fledgling vampire has significantly more supernatural power than Ron Hubbard)

                                    I think that this kind of flexibility will be great for a core opponent because Hunters tend to grow massively in social/organisational power but hardly at all in personal/supernatural power. As a core-focus Cults would provide opportunities to get really let loose and show what happens when the Catholic Church gets proof that the international Cult of Dagon is sacrafacing virgins and decides its time to end them, but you can also use cults as a lead-in to the classic hunter story where the cult reappears with new members everywhere that True Fae does, so a group of mortals have to go into a gunfight with a vastly superior supernatural foe.

                                    I think that the way you can scale cults to each tier - in the same ways that hunters scale at each tier - or use them as a lead in to monsters that scale in entirely different ways really makes cults shine as a hunter antagonist.

                                    The fact cults can be seen as a dark mirror of hunters is just the icing on the cake.


                                    So that's why I'm pro cults, if you're willing please enlighten me to what awesome advantages that thing-that-screams monster has that I'm not seeing.

                                        Sure. Mind you, I don't have a TON of time, so if I back away or post slowly, understand that I'm facing some intense deadlines right now. I'll read it regardless.

                                        The short end of it is:

                                        1) I think cults are mostly comprised of victims, and I don't really want to do a game about stopping and ostensibly killing victims.

                                        2) Cults tend to imply the existence of bigger things. We could do God-Machine cults, but I feel like those have a better place in Demon. They could be big Lovecraftian entities, but I don't really get much out of those.

                                        3) I think monsters serve as a really good anchoring point for "what is a monster, and what is human?" debates. When hunters default to hunting clear monsters, that gives them a strong foundation that justifies what they do. But then when they move into other territory as an exception, they have to reconcile that as an exception, handling each as a case-by-case situation.

                                        4) I like hunters being definitely the underdogs, needing tactics, compromise, and attrition to win their battles. Big inhuman scary things work well with that.

                                        5) I think that killing cultists removes a lot of the gray areas from Hunter. While not all Hunter characters would resort to killing, it'd be a standard for at least some of the Hunter groups. And I find that to be pretty irreconcilably wrong.

                                        6) When I think of Hunter, I think of hunting monsters. Cultists aren't monsters. So there's a cognitive disconnect.

                                        7) Cults tend to have an "othering" element to them. It's one of my big gripes with Lovecraft. While I don't think it'd be a huge problem with my writers, I think it'd be an easy thing to fall back on. I don't want to see another WoD: Gypsies.

                                            You make some very good points

                                            The morality/theme ones particularly, it didn't really occur to me before how making cultists the default enemy could skew the perceived morality/themes of hunters. I guess it's because I consider a lot of the monster splats to be victims and because I don't like species as a justification for murder and would hold a morality system that can be applied consistently to humans and monsters as the ideal Hunter code (it would have to include something like fair trials where possible of course) - but I acknowledge that you're completely correct. Even if the writing intentionally tries to soften the blow, making the primary enemy humans will be a major change in tone, and not for the the better.

                                            The one I strongly disagree with is number 4; to me the coolness behind the Tiers system is that you can have your hunters as the underdogs and your Hunters as the top dogs in the same game. I like the idea that a Tier 1 Cell might use compromise and attrition to deal with a single vampire. TF:V might just blow up an entire Elysium from a safe distance with long range hellfire missiles. (Obviously TF:V is still an underdog when compared to any Rank 6+ Spirit).

                                            I'm also not sold on number two. I mean I love mythology and since religion/myth is already a major element of Hunter so I'd be ok with including a lot more "bigger things", but I think that agnostic cults could easily coincide with those who worship active interventionist deities. The foie gras cult in Hunter core for example.


                                            I guess my follow up question to you, then, would be what monsters could you see that is both scalable enough to work at every Tier and diverse enough to keep hunter's tagline of letting you play humans vs any other WoD gameline? I think a lot of the reason I latched onto Cults as the name for a chronicles book is that I can't think of anything else that would satisfy those two points. Can you think of something or a reason why those aren't as important as I think?

                                            The only other thing I can think of is to not name the Hunter book after a particular kind of monster. Mage is named after The Fallen World after all. One of the other reasons I liked Cults as a focus is that focusing on an organisation would also be usable for Hunters - I do feel Hunter needs more support for playing as part of a well oiled conspiracy, rather than an independent cell with Endowments; that stuff could separated out from the stuff about antagonistic cults. What do you you think?
