    Conspiracy Theory: the Supplementary

    01-30-2022, 05:30 PM
    Alright, so we have been through the Core Conspiracies, we are now moving to what is commonly thought about as the Supplementary ones- conspiracies which have either a more focused Enemy, lesser influence, more regional in nature or all of the above. Such conspiracies are very narrow in scope, and a lot of them (especially those who were not presented in a dedicated supplement) have a lot less material for us to work with- even the Big Three (KoSG, LM and CH) which relatively get a lot of wordcount compared to the others, can't really compete with conspiracies which had many different books detailing different aspects of their activities, mythology, symbolism and agenda. There was no way to plant clues all across the different books, and unlike VASCU they also lack a updated version which may include some evolution in time (yeah, sure, Merrick and LM were initially listed as being defuncted, but it was removed in the final draft because, as it seems, fans don't like to have their splat options being killed off offscreen- something which VtM was infamous for). As such, we have a lot less to work with, and as it seems the writers knew it, giving either some VERY heavy hints about the true secrets of those groups or even outright stating the truth behind them. All of that just means that it makes sense to fold them all into a single thread (even though each would get its own dedicated post, don't worry). Anyway, in this case, we are going to explore the conspiracies by publication order instead of alphabetically, and as such we are going to start with...

    Knights of St George

    Now, if you were to ask me which of the currently Supplementary Conspiracies is the most deserving for a spot among the Core ones, I would give this one as the answer, even before VASCU (even though that VASCU does get bonus points due to the focus on slashers in 2e). There are a number of reasons for it- first, while they do have a narrow focus, the KoSG are stated to hunt other monsters besides witches. In fact, it is outright stated that the Third Revelation members use propaganda and misinformation, lying to lower ranked Knights in order to make them think that all monsters are magic related and needs to be slain, just to motivate them to hunt things besides witches with the same zeal. Which, honestly, it is a bit weird when you think about it- why do hunters need motivation in order to kill monsters besides witches when it would make more sense for the most common hunter to require motivation to kill only witches? The fact that this is a lie which the conspiracy works hard to maintain already starts smelling fishy, which would make you wonder what more we are taking for granted about the Knights.

    Besides the fact that the KoSG are not as narrowed as you may think (they deal with demons, vampires, werewolves, slashers and probably everything else), it should also be noted that the conspiracy is related to one of the strongest Christian denominations- the third largest, and the biggest one among the Protestants to the point that some scholars of religion think about is as its own thing. While Anglicanism has a lot of believers, according to British Hunter fans I had a chance to talk with, there is a joke that "Being a good Anglican does not require believing in God"- that it is more tradition and social focused than religion focused, which is a subtle joke by presenting the KoSG as "God Fearing Men" when their God is very much not God (or is it?...). By being related to a big organization (the Anglican Church) and a powerful nation (the UK), it immediately lends itself for why the KoSG are close to the Core Conspiracies in terms of power and influence, but there is another point- that like all of those groups (besides the CoB*), the KoSG play on real life conspiracy theories. Not just in terms of Anglican related conspiracies, but very much ion the whole Knight Templars angle- all the stories about the secret witchcrafts and heresy of the Templars which, according to conspiracy theories have escaped their demise by going to England, joining the Anglican Church and becoming the Illuminati. Which is funny when you consider that the Templars have actually survived- but not in England. In Portugal. Apparently the king during the time felt uncomfortable with raiding the lands of the Templars and taking their stuff like other European rulers did (because the Pope said it was "free real estate, baby"), so they changed the name of their order, did a face lift and stayed there. It is actually still around- it was on a break for around 7 years after the monarchy was abolished, but yeah, we still have Templars, and no they are not the Illuminati. Yes, unfortunate, I know.

    Anyway, the point is that like most Core Conspiracies, the KoSG actually have a relatively wide Vigil, a connection to powerful institute and nation and are based around real life conspiracy theories. All of that serves the point that among the Supplementary Conspiracies, they are probably the most dominant one, which makes them very much noteworthy. Now, we can also see that in the wider Hunter context- as the KoSG are very dominant in Doubting Souls, being responsible for the Shadow Court which is being held against the hunters during the Salem hysteria. Expectedly, the Knights take a much more antagonistic role in this Era, doing their witch hunts and killing other hunters for suspected relations to demons and the supernatural- which I think shows that it was mostly done by First Revelation hunters and probably scared the hell out of the Third Revelation hunters- unless that Knights needed that paranoia for some reason. Again, the Knights do make some strange choices. Probably the result of having 72 angels singing in their head.

    Now, while we do know that the Knights have been a dominant power during Salem's Witch Hunt, we can imagine that they were probably active all across the world during the British Empire- even though their influence have probably declined with it. Still, what we do know is that the Knights as a organization have already existed during the Third Crusade, as we have a document which mentions their members fighting a witch using a "Name That Must Not Be Written". That relation to the Crusades is also emphasized by their connection to Richard the Lionheart, as their leader must be part of his lineage- in fact, the conspiracy is obsessed with finding his descendants, with Richard being initiated into the conspiracy by himself after founding it. This Crusade atmosphere only emphasize the lingering connection to the Templars, even though the Knights are clearly their own thing, simply playing on the same themes of Crusades, hidden heresy and witchcraft. Now, while that puts the beginning of the Knights in 1189, the text also stated that the Knights were, in a way, always around- there were always people that could have shaped the dreams of the Faceless Angels, and in fact we know that the royal line of England is claimed to have been plagued by the dreams of those entities. While we can play around with it considering that the Knights lie all the time, the part about some people having a connection to the Angels does make some sense, and I did played around with it during Igniting the Fire.

    Now, on the other hand, the Knights could be lying about their connection to Richard the Lionheart- they definitely have been around during the Third Crusade, and they may indeed have been established by King Richard, yet the way the text is written makes it feel like Richard had bastard children almost everywhere, and that their lineage is all scattered around the world... Only, that it does work. The Knights are stated to keep records of Richard's illegitimate lineage, and we do know the recruit among those descendants when possible as it is a part of the Hunters writeup. That makes me think that the Knights are found in a situation somewhat similar to the Lucifuge and the AKD- they too have certain emphasize on bloodlines, and they too have some concept of people being naturally burn into their power, with the Gospel simply teaching them how to use it in a safe manner. Now, it doesn't seem like all of the Knights are born with that talent- but it does have some recurring theme. Among the different Hunter entries, most have some strange details about their personality- a priest who does not believe in God yet knew that there was something old about mages, a rising star with unstable and wild personality with claimed lineage to the Knights, a employee in a large firm who looked through his boss's books for a curse for insomnia (inability to sleep) and someone who is outright born to Richard the Lionheart's line. So none of those people had any supernatural abilities like the proto-Knights had, but they all had either a blood relation to the Knights, unstable personality, sense for the strange and of course, and I can't stress it enough, sleeping issues.

    And the Knights have a big thing about keeping stuff asleep.

    So here is what we do know- first, the Knights lie, mostly to their own members. Second, they hunt witches, but also hunt things who are not witches by lying to their own members. Third, there seems to be some affinity required in order to learn the Goetic Gospels- and that affinity is related to bloodline to some extant but could also spontaneously manifest itself. Fourth, the Knights have some issues with Richard the Lionheart- where while he may indeed had some illegitimate children, it could be that some of the genealogies are not, in fact, his own lineage, but are simply claimed as such by the Knights. Finally, the Knights have some connection to Ars Goetia, Faceless Angels and a Name That Must Not Be Written. And of course- St George, the dragonslayer.

    Ok- so before we dive into the strange symbolism and mythology of the Knights- let's come back to King Richard for a second.

    God, I am getting tired of using King Richard in here.. not that I have issues with him, of course, just that when I was a child there was that animated TV series about some knight who screamed KING RICHARD every episode which somehow imbued him with the power of Plot Armor to defeat all enemies which I really hated and my sister really liked.. COULD IT HAVE BEEN A KNIGHTS OF ST GEORGE PROPOGANDA?!

    Ahem

    Back to the subject- as we all know, the Knights lie all the time, and we do know that they are very much likely to have been founded by King Richard, due to the whole Crusade involvement. We also can't rule out that King Richard may have have had a bastard child or two- but here is the thing. Generally, when you "find" a "missing heir", you generally just kidnap a small child and convince them they are related to some royal lineage in order to have claims for the throne in the future. So between that and the tendency of the Knights to lie, it sounds like a legit that the Knights have never actually "stole" Richard's child... with only one problem. Unlike the common case, the Knights have done nothing with that claim, and they also don't plan to do anything with it. They have no reason to lie, and we do know that generally, when the Knights lie they do so for a good reason. As such, we can assume that while many of the lineages that they claim to be "of Richard" are in fact not part of his legacy, but could probably be traced to other, much lesser known people who had an affinity to the Angels and their dreams- except, again, that we have that strange line about how without the blessing of the Lionheart, the Spirit of St George won't watch over the a new Knight- which means that there is an importance for the Knight Commander, at the very least, to be an actual part of the lineage. And that somewhat relates to the part about how King Richard got initiated by the proto-Knights and established the order after being cursed by a witch for dreams of death- a curse which the Knights have lifted.

    And that makes me wonder- was King Richard actually cursed? Or maybe he had those dreams for other reason. A reason that the Knights wish to hide.

    Here is the thing- we KNOW that the Knights lie about SOMETHING in this story. Of course they lie- that's their shtick. We have been warned to not listen to their lies. However, the whole part about King Richard does make sense, and they have no reason to lie about it- except of the part he got cursed by a witch. Heck, maybe the dragonslayers have actually lied to Richard in order to get him to their own side. The reason I think that this is the lie which the conspiracy tries to hide in the story is the nature of the curse- dreams of death, and the Knights do have some very strong relation to dreams. Could it be that the "curse" was in fact one of the rogue dreams of the Angels, taking root in the Oneiros of King Richard and corrupting it- and by using the initiation, the Knights were able to "cure" the curse- by making Richard the Lionheart being able to use his powers, with a strong motivation to find witches and kill them after learning their "danger"? Now, that makes sense.

    Ok, so if we were to accept the history of the Knights as mostly true- there were proto-Knights with certain affinity to the Angels, of which King Richard was one of them, that Richard indeed had a illegitimate child and that having a Lionheart as a Knight Commander is important in order to allow other Knights to access Goetic Gospels, let's move to the more interesting part of the Knights of St George- the origin of their Endowments.

    They are Abyssal, of course.

    I mean, they don't have to be, but it does make the most sense- the Abyss is a source of antimagic which infects the world, the Goetic Gospels are a form of antimagic against those who "infect the world". That checks out. The Faceless Angels could be a Supernal Entity or some God Machine mechanism to regulate mages, but the Abyss makes the most sense considering how much it despises magic, how well its denizens resemble demons, and most important, the relation for dreams. It should be noted that when Witch Finders came out, the most common form of Abyssal entity was Acamoth- beings who corrupt the souls of mages and other people to grant them power, just hanging out in their Oneiroi- something which is especially suspicious considering how much the Oneiros was important for the summoning of Goetia. Among all possibilities, it seems like the most likely one is that the Faceless Angels are the Annunaki- the ancient ungods which sleep in the depths of the Abyss and dream the endless possibilities of none existence, and basically wants the world to end just because they want the light out. The resemblance between the Annunaki and the Angels is uncanny, and makes the most sense among all other possibilities. But- and it is a huge but- it does not explain how exactly the Knights are able to use their dreams. The fact that the Angels are Abyssal is one thing, but it does not explain anything about the Goetic system which the Knights choose to use or the wider mythology they have.

    The key part in here is "choose".

    According to the Knights, before their foundation the proto-Knights had many different methods for shaping the dreams of the Angels and myths about their origin- the ones used in the Treatise of St George is simply the most successful one because it became integrated into the Church of England. The potential for the Goetic Gospels may be harnessed by the Knights and requires the blessing of the Lionheart- but it is still there regardless. After all, if people once had the ability to become dragonslayers, it makes little sense that this power just stopped manifesting itself once the Knights were founded... unless it is a lie, of course, but we are currently under the assumption it is true because I used it in the past and it solves me trying to explain "who initiated Richard if they were founded by him". Now, the Goetic system is a choice, a method that the Knights developed through the corruption of Christian elements in order to control the demon dreams they use- and that makes me think that the situation of the KoSG is very similar to the state of a Lucifuge hunter. You can have the power to shape the dreams of the Angels without being a Knight- but that would make you a monster, not a hunter, for there is no control over your antimagic. That means you are better represented as having a constant headache and bad dreams at best and having outright Dread Powers at worse. And as for the source of that power- that, by all likelihood, is by taming Acamoth and putting them into the soul of the Knight. Disgusting, I know.

    So here is how I think it works- we know that the Abyss corrupts, well, everything, and more than that, we know that the Abyss can produce corrupted lineages. It could very much be that one of those lineages was Richard the Lionheart's, a some sort of a Proximi bloodline going wrong. As it seems, the original Knights were people who got infected by Acamoth yet somehow managed to tap into its power and control their own demon- and that for reasons that even the Knights probably don't understand, the Acamoth which possessed Richard, well, evolved. It became infused into his bloodline, making them into conduit for the dreams of the Abyss. The process of the Initiation practically summons a Acamoth and binds it into the candidate- and it may require some natural affinity to the Abyss, as it is mentioned that the Knight Commander has the final say about recruitment. The Knights then use the Goetic system in order to tame their own demons and summon them to fight witches, hence why they use Endowments and not Dread Powers like monsters. Some people are born to their Abyssal legacy, others harness it during their lives or may even are marked by it as part of their Initiation in order to let the Acamoth to come through. In fact, it could very well be that all Knight Commanders have the same Acamoth, one tied to the bloodline of the Lionheart and which passes from generation to generation, and that the Acamoths which the other Knights harness are not summoned from the Abyss- but are birthed from the Knight Commander's own mind and soul. The whole thing about the "Spirit of St George"? Yeah, there's nothing "saintly" about that horror. That plays on the same themes of "capturing a monster and turning it into a human before it is too late", which comes from the Lucifuge.

    Now, if that's the case- why the lies? Why Goetia? Why Christianity specifically?

    Because the Abyss needs to be defined antinomianly.

    The Abyss is everything which is not- and as such it requires what it is through negation. It needs to corrupt a certain system in order to work, or else it has no meaning. The Knights must be strictly Christian or else their whole Abyssal resonance which keeps their Endowments together will lose its power- they need to be Christian on the outside but heretics on the inside. The best Knight is one who is a good Christian who does not believe in God- a paradox, something for the Abyss to grasp upon. The Knight Commander needs to be a illegitimate Lionheart because King Richard had no children and plays on "false royalty". And the Goetic system? That is what gives shape to the shapeless and name to the nameless. Any system could have worked- but they needed one which fits their definition. A Christian system which is viewed as heresy, which uses demons and angels and uses as a conduit the power of a King. Richard the Lionheart serves as the inversion of Solomon- defining the King of Wisdom who master all demons through antinomian logic to become the King of Ignorance who is a slave to all angels. Heck, it works even better if the line is indeed fake- like, it is indeed a continuous bloodline which the Knights carefully guard, but it is not actually from Richard the Lionheart, which makes the Knight Commander a false king, which further strength the paradox which feeds the Abyss- and explains why they would lie about this part in their history. And through the Goetic symbols, the Endowments gain meaning- for the symbols are important, important through inversion.

    Let's elaborate on it for a bit.

    There are a number of key symbols in the KoSG's system- demons, angels and the Name Which Must Not Be Written (NWMNBW). Now, while the most common thing that people know about Ars Goetia besides that those are 72 demons controlled through sigils once bound by Solomon, there is also another aspect for it- HaShem HaMeforash. The Explicit Name. This, in Judaism, is a reference for the True Name of God, the one which you must never pronounce. In the context of Ars Goetia, however, this refers to 72 Angels where each governs one of the demons- which explains WHY the power behind the conspiracy takes the form of angels- the Faceless Angels are the ones which produce the demons used by the Knights, governing them, and their collective form, the one which governs the Angels themselves, is the NWMNBW- an inversion of the Explicit Name. An inversion of God. In order for the system to work, the Knights need to define God antinomianly, just as they define King Solomon and his demons in the same way. The Knights lie because the lies are essential for the system to work- which may relate to why they lie about other monsters being magical in nature. That lie is not meant for the hunters- it is meant for the Abyss. The Abyss needs to think that they only hunt witches- perhaps it helps to satisfy it. Perhaps those acts make sure that the system will not be broken- definitions are important to the Abyss. The Knights are Witch Finders, and they can't just violate it- so they must believe that all monsters are originated from magic so they could hunt them all equally and still be Witch Finders. Is this a gnostic mess? Yes, but we are talking about the Abyss were there no causality and truth is just a recommendation. Really, it makes sense by having so little sense behind it.

    Now, I could talk about the whole "dragonslayer" thing a bit- but that would just be a waste of wordcount. It is very clear, especially in Mage's context, that the Dragons are part of the Tremere terminology which was embraced by the Diamond which in turn found its way to the Knights. The Dragons are basically the Supernal, either as a whole or the Oracles specifically. Whether the Knights got that terminology from the Diamond or the Tremere is unknown, but considering the "thing" that the Tremere have with souls... yeah, it could be that the Treatise of Saint George is partially based on the Tremere's sacred book of nonsense, the Suspire (the style is indeed similar).

    Now that we more or less agreed on the true history and lies of the Knights and how their powers work- let's talk about the actual goals of the conspiracy. This, of course, is a simple one- the Knights want the Faceless Angels asleep. As such, they want that there will be no more magic in the world- not just awakened magic, any form of magic. Make sure that the Angels would remain asleep forever. For that reason, the Knights need to slay the dragons which grant magic to witches- which means the Watchtowers, if we follow the Tremere mythology, and that would make sense. Basically, they want to bring an end for all forms of Awakening, cut off the Supernal from the Fallen and keep the Abyss outside. That's... dangerous. Very, very, VERY dangerous. Because the Exarchs have already tried it- they removed the world from the Supernal as much as they can by putting the Abyss in the middle. If the world will be completely cut off from the Supernal- that may very well be the end of all existence. All of reality would sink into the Abyss, bringing the end of EVERYTHING, unless a new big bang would ignite the flames of wisdom to push the Abyss from the world. Now, while that indeed may be the goal of the Annunaki as a concept, the Acamoth itself does not outright tries to end reality- it tries to replace it with something else, and that is something that the Knights can work with. I mean, they have managed to bind the powers of the Great Dreamers through the antinomian system based around Ars Goetia- could there be a possibility to maintain all of reality through the same concept? Stabilize reality within the dreams of the Abyss by defining it through paradox, make the world into its own reality bubble within the vast void of nothing, through a Name Which Must Not Be Written and Angels Without Face? Could the Knights actually stabilize the world within the ability through their antinomian system alone, bringing an end for the threat of the dragons and the Abyss which they invite? A world of silence and peace where everyone could remain asleep?

    Maybe. Let's just hope that they won't mess up- because if they'll make even a slight mistake, it would truly be the end of the world.

    Alright- that's it for the Knights. Hope you enjoyed it and we'll see you next time in the same thread :P

                                      Cainite Heresy

                                      *Pulls sleeves* Well, time to get into this hellhole.

                                      Here is the problem with the Cainite (besides the fact that I can't spell their name correctly and constantly needs to make sure I don't forget that extra 'i')- this is a freaking hot mess of nonsense. Seriously, there is no other group in Hunter which is as chaotic and nonsensical as the Cainite. Never mind their decentralization- I can live with that. The thing is that when I generally goes to examine the "truth" about a conspiracy, I their history, symbolism and agenda to do so. I seek after the strange things which does not make and try to tie them together, attempting to explain their secret history, solve inconsistencies and tie it all up with secret agenda about the future of the world. The Bird of Minerva is the Owl, and we all know that there is a kind of Owl which hates vampires. That just seems so natural, especially as other hunter groups seem to recruit the natural enemies of their targets as part of their Vigil (the Abyss, the Devourer, Spirits, the God Machine, etc.). The Strix as the secret identity of the Source would make sense both in the terms of the naming and the themes. Oh sure, there was the problem that the Cainite were also hunting the Strix- but the Cainite are ignorant and the Strix are CE as heck so nothing problematic in here. And then Paterfamilias came and showed how the Aves also viewed the Strix as desecration of Minerva's symbol, and I was like &quot;Well, maybe there are some Strix who do VII for their kind?&quot;, which is a bit loose but could still work. And now- we have this. A magic which works by establishing a strongly bound community- and I have nothing to do with that. The Strix are the very antithesis for this concept- the Owls can't make a stable community even at the cost of their lives. They are all about individuality and freedom, the idea of serving as a vessel for a unified community just... doesn't work. Is it truly a Red Herring? Or can the Strix truly serve as the heart of the community for not just the modern Cainite, but also for the Aves? That's the key question here- and it also says that if it is not the Strix, those ancient spirits of darkness and doom with strong affinity for blood magic, than who is the source of that magic?

                                          Let's look at the Denials for a second.

                                          While they look very simple at first glance, the truth is that there is a hidden level of complexity in here. It is in their name- they are about denial, but what exactly is being denied? The things is that Night Stalkers gives us three options, which all make sense- they deny the dead their power, they deny the Cainite their knowledge, and the magic itself was denied by the dead. The play on the ignorance of the Cainite is clear, and the fact that they deny the power of vampires makes sense considering their application- but now, the last part? The magic being &quot;denied&quot;? That is interesting- because we do know that the Strix do claim to be &quot;denied&quot; by the kindred. That is also more interesting when you consider that vampires do tend to develop Banes, many of which fit the folkloric perception of vampiric weaknesses- things which the Denials abuse. In a way, the Denials use the nature that vampires deny against them- the magic that is being denied is their own Banes, their own monstrosity, something which the Cainite use. And that, my friends, come all the way back to Cain- who in here, is claimed to be the First Vampires. The Source of Vampirism. A Source. Like in Sources.

                                          And that's where things finally click together.

                                          Minerva, the Goddess of the Owls, serving as a pillar of community during the time of Rome, a time when the Strix were the Source of the Dead Julli. Cain, the Bearer of the Mark, serving as a pillar of community during modern times, claimed as a Source of vampirism. This is yet another pattern which alluded us- the idea that at the center, at the temple, at the heart of the community, stand a figure which serves as both the Source of magic, the Source of community and the Source of the Curse. And those figures, while being followed, are also being treated as enemies. Cain is the Source of Vampires, yet vampires are still being hunted. The Strix were the Source of the Julli, but they are are still being hunted. The Source is being denied by the community which worships it- and that's the final key for it to work. That explains why the Denials are the First Rites- they are the base, the origin. The hunters gaining the power which was denied by the dead, the Power of Curses, the Power of Banes. They form a community which denies and is being denied, a heresy, something which reaches out to the bloody heart of vampirism and squish it to make it scream. Through blood bonds and faith, they create a pseudo Clan- a Clan of human vampires. And through a vessel which was sanctified by the Sources of Vampirism, the many hungry mouths denied by the vampires, ones which may have originally being the Strix alone but now is the many, many different abominations which have created the undead. This is the heart of darkness, the origin of species. Will and Blood are, after all, the two key components for the existence of vampires, representing the Man and the Beast- and the pseudo Clan of Cain, the one which uses the Curses to consecrate their own blood and form sympathy have used those Curses in order to deny the dead their power. The vampires play at being human, deny that they are monsters, deny that they are dead. It is time to remind them. Who is Cain? He is you. He is every vampire. Every act of murder. Every bit of pain and suffering caused by your actions. The name doesn't matter- the Question does. The Mark does. Cain is just like Minerva- a image meant to unify the conspiracy, and which represents the Curses which were denied, and which want what was denied from them- and the Kindred are the ones to pay that price.

                                          Now, after we more or less patchworked the Cainite into a basis which makes some kind of internal sense (using the Source of Vampirism in order to become a pseudo Clan which uses the denied monstrosity of the dead against them and which could potentially serve as a base for a more elaborated magic system used by the Aves Minerva to hunt other kinds of monsters), let's ask ourselves the next question- what do the Cainite want to do? It should be important to mention, of course, that in this scenario the Sources themselves are still, well human- they just contact the ancient powers of hunger and blood which have created the Kindred, the Strix included. Basically, they are the same as the priestesses of Minerva were- something external to the conspiracy yet still the heart of it, humans who serve the origin of vampirism. As such, it is pretty much clear that there is a kind of dichotomy between what the Curses may want and what the Cainite may want- the Cainite simply want the Kindred dead, the Curses want them alive but monstrous. This means that the choice falls on the Sources- the linking part between the horrifying hunger and the humans who fight it, those who truly control the conspiracy, to make a choice- and I think that their will is pretty simple. The want the vampires denied. Not gone, not destroyed- denied. Denied from the world, unable to use their powers, being riddles with curses which makes them killable, which makes them easy to be destroyed. That's up there in the book- the Cainite work towards an apocalypse where all vampires are gone, and then there will be peace- but the Curses will demand replacement, and the Cainite will probably be that replacement. That's why they become a pseudo Clan- humanity will find freedom in damnation, while vampires will be denied from the world, crumbling to dust under the weight of their own Banes. The Cainite will take upon themselves the Mark of Cain, becoming the new Source of Vampirism which would feed the Curses without feeding on others- forever be bound to each other in order to state the thirst of the Curses. That would bring the end of the dead- at the price of their own humanity. But that's just the matter which would suffer- true freedom is found in spirit. Make the flesh suffer to purify the soul- and through faith, true ascension will be achieved.

                                          A denial for the dead, freedom for humanity, sacrifice for the Curses- and peace for all.
