    Knife and Scream: Slasher in the Chronicles of Darkness

    03-04-2018, 04:21 AM
    KNIFE AND SCREAM
    Slashers in the Chronicles of Darkness


    Introduction

    The following is a fan update of the rules found in Slasher for the Chronicles of Darkness. I wrote these back when the God-Machine Chronicles first came out, trying to build a fan update. With Chronicles out, I’ve tried to further update the rules to reflect the new subsystems (particularly Investigation and Chases). With the second edition of Hunter already in development, I dunno how long a shelf-life these have, but who knows? Someone might get some use out of it?

    Aspirations

    Slashers get three aspirations, which function as they do for mortals. However, Slashers are fundamentally shallow creatures, and their obsession with killing leaves little room for anything else. At all times, at least two of their aspirations must revolve around an eventual kill (though intermediate steps, such as “gain the trust of my intended victim” are permitted). As with most things that cause PCs to take a beat, achieved Aspirations grant an NPC Slasher a point of willpower instead.

    Virtue and Vice

    Slashers mostly hew to the same Virtues and Vices as regular humans, though they are much more likely to tend towards the ‘archetypal’ Virtues and Vices of the original World of Darkness. Slashers are simple beings at heart.

    Professions

    Slashers no longer lose their dots in the Professional Training merit upon becoming Slashers.

    Integrity

    A Slasher’s soul is a scarred and calloused thing, indelible wounds scratched across its surface by whatever caused him to fall to Slasherhood in the first place. Slashers begin play with an Integrity score of 4, and most eventually stabilize at an integrity rating between 1 and 3. A Slasher’s player may choose to start play with any persistent condition that can be gained by failing at breaking points, which functions as normal.

    The Slasher’s calloused soul leaves him without any inhibitions against violence. Witnessing or performing even extreme violence is not usually a breaking point for a Slasher. Being the victim of violence still is, however, as are all other ‘normal’ triggers. Additionally, Scourges are truly supernatural creatures, and as such gain a +2 bonus to the Resolve + Composure rolls whenever a breaking point roll involves the supernatural. However, they also gain a 2 die penalty to all other breaking points, as they are even further removed from humanity than regular Slashers.

    Slashers also have unique breaking points of their own. When creating a Slasher character, the five questions answered by mortals are replaced by the following five:
    “What events led the character to their first murder of a human being?”
    “What type of victims does the character normally hunt and why?”
    “What goal does the character believe he serves by killing?”
    “What is the worst thing your character can imagine someone else doing?”
    “What is the most traumatic thing that has ever happened to your character?”
    These five questions supercede the rule that Slashers do not normally experience breaking points from witnessing or performing violence. A character who came to Slasherhood by way of child abuse might still experience a breaking point from witnessing violence to children.

    Finally, if a Slasher attempts and fails to kill a victim, this is always a breaking point at -3.

    Combat

    It is recommended that any Slasher action that does not directly involve a Player Character is handled using the Down and Dirty Combat rules (if a roll is needed to begin with). If the Beaten Down & Surrender optional rules are used, Slashers are immune to both. They cannot have the fight beaten out of them, and they do not need to spend Willpower to kill anyone who surrenders. Additionally, if a Slasher kills someone when this was not their Intent, they do not lose a Willpower point.

    Slashers and Willpower

    Lacking natural power or fuel stats, Slasher NPCs are far more dependent on Willpower than most NPCs. As such, any Ripper Slasher always starts the scene with one more Willpower points than indicated on Page 87-88 of Chronicles of Darkness, while a Scourge has two more points. Both Slasher types may also gain a larger amount, up to their regular maximum.

    As an optional rule, these rules may apply to any NPC heavily dependent on Willpower, such as those who have Psychic merits.

    Example: A Brute Slasher with Composure 2 and Resolve 3 being pursued by VASCU agents for a string of murders will usually only have one point of Willpower (named, recurring hostile, but not the focus of an entire Chronicle and does not spend a large amount of time among PCs), and will not be able to gain more than one point. Under these rules, they would instead start the scene with two points, and could gain up to their regular amount of five.

    Slashers and Tactics

    Slasher cells may use Tactics in the same manner that Hunters do (including Dynamic Tactics, if those particular custom rules are used). However, as dark reflections of the Hunter ethos, singular slashers may also use Tactics (and may spend experience on them), though with the following caveats:
    • A singular Slasher does not get the benefit of any Secondary Actors.
    • If Dynamic Tactics are used, the Slasher may only use an improvised Tactic at the extra cost of a point of Willpower.


    Undertakings

    Like regular mortals, ripper Slashers no longer need to pay two dots to obtain the fifth dot in any attribute including their Undertaking Attribute.

    All Slashers have a talent and a frailty – and Scourges have both their own and the one given to their Ripper cousins. Whenever Slasher’s frailty causes them significant trouble, they take a beat.

    Avenger

    Talent – Force of Obsession: More than even other Slashers, Avengers have an obsessive focus on a particular type of victim. When chasing that particular victim, the Avenger’s obsession comes to the fore, allowing them a near-supernatural degree of skill for a brief time. When an Avenger spends Willpower for bonus dice in pursuit of killing a given target, it grants an additional benefit. For non-combat actions, the Avenger gains the benefits of an exceptional success provided they rolls even a single success. For combat actions, they may – provided that the Slasher causes any health levels of damage – instead cause any Tilt the Storyteller considers appropriate for the type of attack.

    Frailty – Nothing but the Mission: As per original. Whenever the Avenger fails her Resolve + Composure roll to overcome their obsession with a given target, her player takes a beat.

    Legend

    Talent – Strength from the Tales: The strength of a Legend’s narrative nourishes them rather than anything physical, and they do not need sustenance of any kind nor do they age.

    Additionally, each Legend has a mythos: a collection of stories and urban legends attached to their origins and previous murders. This mythos empowers the Slasher, providing him with several advantages.

    First, the Legend always has a summons: something that calls to them and helps them find and track their victim. This may be a literal spell or incantation to summon them (such as the Sator Square used to attract the Sower’s attention), or a type of behavior or events (such as Shotgun Daddy’s hunt for sexually-active young males). This power has no limit on its range, but functions only in places where the Legend’s mythos is known to at least a few people, or that includes people or items associated with the Legend’s mythos.

    The definition here is deliberately fluid; ‘places’ can mean anything between a single neighborhood block and a county-sized rural area, while ‘a few people’ can be a large subsection of the population, a cult, or even just a kindly old couple that still remembers the Piano String Killer’s murder spree back in ’51. The exact definition should be set such that anyone seeking to investigate the murders stands a good chance of eventually running into the mythos.

    Whenever the Legend’s summons is invoked while not already on the hunt, their player immediately knows the exact location of the summons, and may choose to spend a point of Willpower to appear somewhere within five miles of the summons’ location bearing appropriate Aspirations towards murder. The Legend will appear in whatever manner, time and place makes sense for their mythos (Bloody Mary appears immediately in the mirror, while Samara Morgan appears on a nearby video screen exactly seven days later). In many cases, the Slasher simply appears out the sight of cameras and onlookers; as far as any evidence will show, the Slasher simply appeared out of nowhere (which is, of course, exactly what happened). A Slasher’s sense of their summons weakens somewhat after appearing: they can still sense anything within five miles that would trigger their summons, but only to an accuracy of about a hundred yards.

    Frailty – Trapped in the Story: Legends have almost completely lost their existence to their mythos. This is not hyperbole; when not brought into existence by their Summons, the Legend literally does not exist. They have no corporeal location, cannot be affected or sensed in any manner, and cannot roll dice or take actions. This may be played, depending on the Legend’s mythos, as the Legend being in Hell unless freed, the Legend being a ghost whose rest is disturbed by certain actions, or the Legend simply being a mysterious figure who seems to exist for their kills alone. While nonexistent, the Legend heals as usual, and regains a point of Willpower every midnight. The Legend may remain in existence for as long as desired, but their player must spend a point of Willpower for each hour of existence after the first. At the player’s choice, or whenever the player does not pay a Willpower point, the Legend disappears in whatever way makes most sense to their mythos (including just vanishing whenever the Slasher is out of sight).

    Additionally, each Legend has one or more (at the player’s option) weaknesses. These weaknesses allow the prepared to ward off, recognize, or harm the Legend as though it were either a Ban or a Bane. If a Bane, this additionally allows the Legend to be Warded away as though it were a Rank 2 ephemeral entity.

    Additionally, these weaknesses cannot be hidden. The Legend’s strength and existence derives from their mythos, which always includes hints of his weakness. Depending on how active and widespread the Legend is, an appropriate Investigation can always discover the weakness with 3 to 5 Clues. An appropriate Encyclopedic Knowledge or Eye for the Strange Merit will likewise provide some information on the Legend’s weaknesses.

    Whenever someone uses the Legend’s weakness against them, their player takes a beat.

    Brute

    Talent – Unstoppable: As per original.

    Frailty – Blinded by Blood: As per original. Whenever a victim successfully hides from the Brute, the Brute’s player takes a beat. Additionally, the Brute does not take automatic damage from Style merits.

    Mask

    Talent – Unstoppable Killing Machine: Mostly as per the original. Additionally, no fighting Style merit works on the Mask at all.

    Frailty – No Mind but for Murder: As per original. Whenever the Mask’s inability to communicate or receive communications causes him significant danger, their player takes a beat.

    Charmer

    Talent – Disarming: The Charmer may, upon first meeting a new person, roll Presence + an appropriate social skill (depending on the context, this can be Expression, Persuasion or Socialize). This roll is contested by the victim’s Intelligence + Subterfuge, can only be transmitted in person (television and phone conversations do not work), and can affect no more than ten people per scene. If the Charmer wins, the victim gains the Besotted condition.
    Besotted (Persistent)
    Your character has come under a Charmer’s influence. They absolutely refuse to see anything wrong with the Charmer and will support and protect the Slasher. Non-supernatural merits such as Common Sense and Danger Sense that would ordinarily warn the character that something is amiss do not take any effect. Any Clue they personally find regarding the Charmer gains the Tainted tag. Finally the character is treated as though he has two fewer Doors for any favor the Charmer asks.

    Resolution: The character witnesses the Charmer harm someone without good cause, is present when the Charmer’s Frailty is invoked, or is away from the Psycho’s line of sight for at least an hour.

    Beat: The character acts against his own obvious self-interest due to this condition, or a merit or skill that would normally warn him fails due to this Condition.

    Frailty – Thin Veneer: Every Charmer has a trigger that causes them to lash out, as well as a general trigger caused whenever they are called out by someone who does not suffer from the Besotted condition. When this occurs, roll the Charmer’s Composure. A failure causes them to lash out verbally. Anyone witnessing the scene loses the Besotted condition and additionally lowers their impression level (p. 82 of the God-Machine Chronicle) to average. Should the Charmer’s player fail a Composure roll in the presence of one or more Besotted witnesses, they take a beat.

    Psycho

    Talent – Deadly Distraction The Psycho can distract anyone who suffers from the Besotted Condition, provided that they can get his potential victim alone. This requires no roll and is a form of Hard Leverage (p. 194 of The God Machine Chronicle) with the same long-term effects on people. When choosing to use this distraction to attack, the Psycho can automatically deal bashing damage equal to the victim’s health and instantly knock them out, or (if he has a weapon) deal lethal damage equal to their dice pool + weapon damage modifier.

    Frailty – The Broken Mask Whereas a Charmer is a glib killer willing to play the long game, the Psycho has more pressing needs. They want their victim dead, and want them dead now. The Besotted condition imposed by the Disarming Talent is fades whenever the victim spends an hour or more away from the Psycho’s presence. Unlike a Charmer, a Psycho may choose to re-establish the Condition whenever he next meets the victim (provided it was lost by the specific effects of this Frailty). Should the Psycho’s player ever roll a failure on imposing the Besotted Condition, the victim’s impression instantly becomes Hostile.

    Freak

    Talent – Revolting: Freaks are universally deformed, and the generally disgusting nature of these Slashers makes it instinctively revolting to grab or even touch them. All attempts to grapple with Freaks, perform unarmed or weapon attacks on them, or otherwise touch the Slasher require the expenditure a Willpower point to even be attempted. This includes any skills that would cause automatic damage, such as that granted by the Martial Arts Merit.

    Frailty – Deformity: All Freaks are hideously deformed, causing disgust in all others, and the rejection of their fellow humans is often a major cause of them becoming Slashers in the first place. For purposes of Social Maneuvering, assume that impressions to the Freak start at hostile. This means that without significant efforts to the contrary, Hard Leverage and the resulting fallout is the only social option open to Freaks.

    Whenever a Freak is ill-treated or incapable of getting something done due to his looks, their player gains a Beat.

    Mutant

    Talent – Sharp Senses: All Mutants are highly sensitive to stimuli. They gain the rote quality to any perception roll, and cannot lose Defense due to surprise.

    Additional mutations can be represented with the Body Modification or Mutated Flesh Merits. These can represent inborn traits, but further instances may be bought provided an appropriate in-character justification is found.

    Frailty – Sensitive: Mutants are even more damaged than Freaks: whenever they are rejected, they lose a point of Willpower. Additionally, their enhanced senses make them vulnerable. Each Mutant chooses a trigger stimulus; when they encounter it, they roll Resolve + Composure. If the roll fails, the Mutant must flee immediately. If the roll succeeds, they may stay but suffers a -3 penalty on all actions and must devote their energy to removing the stimulus. In either case, his player takes a Beat.

    Genius

    Talent – Profiling: A Genius can attempt to profile any character whom he has spent at least one minute observing by rolling Intelligence + Empathy. This is ordinarily contested with the victim’s Integrity, as the Genius has trouble understanding ‘whole’ and functional people. If someone is aware of this power, they may instead substitute their Composure + Subterfuge score as they attempt to send out false signals. This Talent can only be used on a single person at a time. If the Genius wins the roll, he can later predict any action the victim will take with regard to a given stimulus or event, which requires the expenditure of a Willpower point and a successful (unopposed) Intelligence + Empathy roll.

    Should the Genius use this power again on someone he has already profiled, he may instead opt to learn any of the following: Something that counts as a Breaking Point for the victim; the details of any single breaking point suffered in the past; or any conditions the victim currently suffers from.

    Frailty – Intolerance for Chaos: As per the book. This Frailty explicitly applies even to lost opposed rolls. Whenever the Genius loses Willpower due to this Frailty, her player takes a Beat.

    Maniac

    Talent – Compelling Madnes: The Maniac’s powers of profiling are enhanced. He now gains the 9-Again advantage against anyone he has previously profiled. When he prepares a trap for someone he has profiled, they gain a -3 penalty to detect or avoid the trap.

    By engaging his victim in conversation, the Maniac is able to manipulate them much more strongly than other people. The Maniac may use the Social Maneuvering system against a profiled victim to force them into a breaking point. Doing so successfully makes the victim even more easy to manipulate in the future, cumulatively reduce the amount of doors that need to be opened by two, rather than one.

    Frailty – Obvious Lunatic: The Maniac’s instability is obvious to those more whole than them. Any social rolls he makes against non-profiled people that are not based on Intimidation or Subterfuge take a penalty equal to the victim’s Integrity.

    Merits

    Slashers may take Supernatural Merits the same way that a regular mortal character might (that is, if the Storyteller feels it matches the theme of the story). This is especially appropriate for certain Scourges, such as Legends, Mutants, and Maniacs.

    Atavism (●)
    Remains unchanged, but can be picked after Character Creation. If character ceases to qualify for this Merit, he can choose to reallocate the Merit dots.

    Body Modification (●+)
    Prerequisite: Stamina equal to the amount of Merit Dots chosen, or the Mutant Undertaking.
    Effects: Your character’s body has been changed in order to become a better killer. Despite the name, not all of these mutations are caused by self-inflicted surgery. Some Slashers (particularly those of the Mutant Undertaking) are born with these Merits.
    This Merit is actually multiple related one-dot Merits, each of which is taken separately. Mutants may have as many total dots in these Merits as they can afford; others are limited to their Stamina. Each Merit can represent multiple things: The ‘Sharpened Teeth’ Merit can mean that the Slasher has an elaborate set of false teeth, or it might simply mean that they have been filed to a point and sharpened. Other Merits along these lines likely exist.
    The Mutated Flesh Merit has several powers that overlap with these. If the character gains these later on, the Merit dots are refunded as per the Sanctity of Merits. The Merit dots shown here are there to set the example, but players can invent more with Storyteller permission.
    This Merit replaces the ‘Body Modification’ rules in Slasher.
    Sharpened Teeth: The character gains a bite attack with a damage rating of 1, that can only be used while grappling.
    Claws: The character may deal lethal damage unarmed.
    Armor: The character gains one point of armor against bashing attacks.
    Concealed Pouch: The character can hide an object of no more than size 1 in a subdermal pouch.
    Drawback: Bodily modifications have a disturbing effect on other people: The Slasher gains a permanent -2 to all non-intimidation social rolls whenever any of his modifications are visible.

    Damnable Certainty (●●)
    Prerequisite: Slasher
    Effects: Once per session, when your character kills someone without suffering a Breaking Point in the process, they gain 1 willpower.

    Frenzied Assault (● to ●●●●● , Style)
    Prerequisites: Strength ●●●, Stamina ●●●, Weaponry ●●
    Effects: This Fighting Style represents the Slasher at their basest form: the blood-soaked, relentless killer. It must be used with a weapon, which must have a damage rating of at least 1.
    Bestial Instincts (●): Your character is used violence, and springs readily into battle regardless of weapon. Any initiative penalties suffered for weapon choice are reduced by one for every dot your character has in this Merit.
    Terrorize (●●): Once per scene in which the Slasher has dealt at least one point of damage, he may reflexively roll Strength + Intimidation, contested by the Resolve + Composure score of any onlookers. All onlookers who lose the contested roll immediately gain the Terrorized Tilt, which gives the character -2 to Defense and any attacks against the Slasher, and turns into the Shaken Condition upon the end of combat.
    Hard to Kill (●●●): Unchanged.
    Savage Rending (●●●●): The Slasher reduces penalties for targeting specific body parts by two, inflicting Tilts as normal.
    Trance of Death (●●●●●): Unchanged

    Hands of a Killer (●)
    Delete this Merit, as its effects are superseded by those of the Improvised Weaponry Style.

    Lumbering Gait (●●)
    Prerequisite: Strength ●●●
    Effects: The Slasher often seems lumbering and slow, unable to catch up with those who run. Yet, appearances can be deceiving, and your character is a master at driving people into impossible corners. Those chased by your character do not gain bonuses from having a higher Speed rating. If they suffer from any fear-based Condition, they take an additional -3 to their rolls.

    Morbid Fascination (●)
    Merit is unchanged.

    Mutated Flesh (● +)
    Prerequisite: Mutant Undertaking
    Effects: Mutants are far removed from human, and the odd gifts they occasionally display only reinforce this fact. As with Body Modification, above, this Merit is actually multiple Merits from which the Mutant’s player can choose. Other options than those mentioned here are possible, and players can invent more with the Storyteller’s permission.
    Limber (●): The Mutant has unusually flexible bones, rendering them capable of moving through any space their head could move through.
    Weapons (● or ●●): The Mutant has a claw or bite attack with a damage rating of 1. For an additional dot, the damage increases to 2.
    Armor (●● or ●●●●) The Mutant gains an armor rating of 1/0. An additional Merit dot increases this to 2/0
    Water adaptation (●●●) The Mutant is capable of surviving in water for extended periods of time, gaining a swim speed equal to their land speed. Additionally, multiply the time they can spend without oxygen by 10.
    Wall Climber (●●) Your character can climb walls at his regular move speed without a roll. They are not at risk of falling unless forced by other circumstances, and ignore all environmental penalties that would make it easier to lose their grip.
    Drawback: These mutations render it essentially impossible for a character to ever pass as a normal human without something that covers their entire body. They also gain a -2 penalty to all non-intimidation social rolls.

    Murder Expert (●●● )
    Merit is unchanged.

    Telltale Murder (●● or ●●●)
    Prerequisite: Intelligence ●●●●, Investigation ●●
    Effects: When killing, your character can use the crime scene to send a message. With the two-dot version of this Merit, you can roll intelligence + investigation either just before or immediately after your character kills someone, changing the murder scene and the body. Every success allows him to send a message that can be understood by anyone who rolls even a single success on an Intelligence + investigation roll. In addition to the message, the realization makes this a Breaking Point with a penalty equal to your successes for anyone who directly witnesses this.
    If your character has the three-dot version of this Merit, they can additionally use his skills to foil profilers: use successes as a penalty on any die roll to investigate the crime scene.
    Drawback: If the character wishes to kill someone without sending a message, he must spend a point of Willpower.

    Signs of Slaughter (●●)
    Prerequisite: Slasher, Scourge Undertaking
    Effects: Your character’s intended victims (as defined by Aspirations) experience signs of their impending death, such as nightmares, blood welling up from his eyes, or other supernatural occurrences chosen when the Merit is first bought. This is usually a breaking point for the character. In addition, should the character fail at this breaking point at any time, they lose 2 points from their Defense when facing the Slasher.
    This Merit may be bought multiple times, causing more Breaking Points.
    Drawback: Your character cannot choose when these signs appear, and they are likely to warn the victim that something is amiss.

    Supernatural Slayer (●● or ●●●●)
    Prerequisite: Slasher, Scourge Undertaking, any other Supernatural Merit
    Effects: Your character can use their supernatural abilities more freely than most. Whenever your character uses a Supernatural ability to help them kill another living being, waive the first point of Willpower cost. At four dots, the Merit instead refunds the first two such points.

    Wages of Sin (●●)
    Prerequisite: Slasher
    Effects: The Slasher is a morality tale of sorts, punishing those that give in to their baser nature. Whenever a character regains Willpower from their Vice, for the next 24 hours, they automatically gain a -2 to resist or contest any roll by the Slasher, including Defense.
    Drawback: The reverse is also true. Anyone who has regained Willpower from their Virtue gains a +2 to all rolls to contest or resist the Slasher.

    Weaponry Monomaniac
    Drawback: Instead of a derangement, your character gains the (Persistent) Obsession Condition, fixated on the weapon’s return, until they regain their weapon.
