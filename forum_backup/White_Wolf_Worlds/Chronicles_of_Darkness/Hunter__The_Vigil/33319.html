    Using Fan-Games As Antagonists

    12-19-2013, 02:41 PM
    The World of Darkness has a lot of wonderful fans who create a lot of content. There's tons of fan-made games each about their own 'monsters,' and with that comes the open promise of them being shown in a less sympathetic light in Hunter: the Vigil. The big three - Vampire, Werewolf, and Mage - have all received their own books, and the limited gamelines are all being addressed in Mortal Remains. But for original content, there's little hope of a Hunter take on things beyond what we create themselves.

    I'm not terribly familiar with many fan games other than Outsider: the Calling, which I played no small part in (it's a shame that it's in the state it is now, but good ideas are there), but want to expand on the potential antagonists for Storytellers that want something a little bit less on the beaten path.

    So stuff any ideas for how to use new antagonists, where these other supernaturals fit into your World of Darkness, what different Compacts and Conspiracies think of them, and anything else beyond. (It would be super cool if we came up with some new Dread Powers, Compacts, Conspiracies, and other such fun stuff).

    I'll post something more meaty soon, I promise!

    (If anyone can help me with Leviathans as bad guys, that would be awesome. The closest I can get is ocean gribblies with reverse-Disquiet.)

                      For Leviathan it's simple. A typical Leviathan is basically the leader of a Lovecraftain cult, and a giant monster with poor impulse control. One thing that's worth noting is that the primary antagonist for Leviathan is The Marduk Society, a Hunter Conspiracy. The Leviathan's weakness to electricity is symbolic of their weakness to human reason; so you're thematically justified in playing up all sorts of science beats monsters theme. Their other weakness is to human virtue. Basically, if you can't spin something hunter appropriate out of weaknesses to reason and virute in a hunter game back to ST school with you

                      Basically, Hunters vs Leviathans is easy. Just focus on Hunters working together to contrast with a Leviathan's dysfunctional cult and sicnece as a symbol of the way mankind is makeing things better for ourselves to contrast with the way Leviathans have failed to make the world better for their species.

                      It's also a great excuse to pull out all the stops. I've written a piece of TF:V fiction that's basically a combined Air, Land and Sea attack on a Leviathan controlled town, right down to a couple of guided missile cruisers on standby encase there is a Leviathan present.

Tears constantly go around corrupting areas until they become spawning pits for gibbering hellbeasts. Any one of these could easily convince someone that Princesses are evil.
                      A fun fact is that there are a LOT of Hunters/Cultists (this is where the line gets really blurred) following the Queen of Storms. A monster kills your family, you vow revenge and next thing you know you've got powers and an insane Queen promising to lead you in eternal battle. Alternatively you're an upstanding MM demon hunter, but you hate demons. One day you see a Darkspawn, sure looks like a demon, so you hate it too. Next thing you see what looks a lot like a goddess offering you magic for servitude. Is your faith in the true god enough to keep you from the false path?

                      Actually: If you're playing Hunter and your storyteller wants to include the risk of inadvertently becoming Stormwracked some details on how the Process works is probably a good idea. The short verison is that the hate in your heart is the Queen of Storms. You don't become Stormwracked by flying out your body and meeting the Queen, all the power comes from within yourself, so if you refuse Storms at the last minuet you won't be hit by a lightning bolt. Once you reject the hate there's no one to throw a lightning bolt at you.


                      Note that in addition to all this, the Queen of Storms is a giant cloud formed of the souls of everyone who died from Tempesta backlash that's rampaging around The Dark World destroying everything in sight, and occasionally sends some of it's component souls to do battle upon Earth.

                      And in addtion to both of those, the Queen of Storms is also the cosmic principle that people hate evil monsters and tend to fight back at them. Though I'd say she's about 100% the hate in your heart, 100% the specific hate targeted at the Outer Dark, 100% a giant storm cloud, and only about 1% a cosmic principle. Still you can see why no Archmage has tried to destroy her, what would the Pax say about removing humanities ability to hate and damaging sic semper tyrannis? I think a lot of Pentacle Seers really like the fact humanity has a tendency to overthrow tyrants.

                      Basically, gods are really complex and I love playing with those ideas. (Why do you think I'm doing Cults . In Cults' mechanics Stormwracked would have the Avatar Merit)

                      Another thing to note about. There's not just blind anti-witch prejudice to make fanatical evil hunters go after Princesses. Princesses are social reformers, occasionally radical. Quite a few Hunters are going to just have different politics which is worth a game.

                      Here's one idea. Players are a hunter team assembled by a politician, pick his level of evilness to suit your tastes. Maybe he's a genuinely decent family values good old ways Christian vs a Princess of Diamonds. PCs can be political muck-rakers that target the Princses, and they could be part of the anti-darkspawn force the politician put together to prove that normal humans can defend themselves.


                      BTW: General tips for Hunters vs Princesses.
                      1) Sniper rifles can kill Inner Light 10 Princesses with no trouble if she's not Transformed when you take the shot.
                      2) A lot of Princesses will surrender if you threaten a hostage. Be very careful, the other Princesses will often do terrible things to you as a warning to others.
                      3) A Princess' powers is her emotions. If you're having trouble attacking her physically, try to attack her emotionally.
                      4) The followers of Tears are pragmatic and can be bribed to leave you alone however the followers of Tears might execute you on the spot if you try to bribe them to act against another Princess. They might do so of their own accord, but for a commoner to try to incite violence against a noble without going through proper channels is treason.
                      Finally Geniuses.

                      I'd focus on the Inspired as basically: They do the same things that you do, only better. Hunters have a numerical advantage, not against Beholden or mass produced robots. Hunters have communication and teamwork? Geniuses had an internet since the 18th century and managed to run a tightly organised war machine that spread across the solar system, time and parallel dimensions. Hunters have tech advantages... do I need to say it?

                      In short. The Genius advantages are the same as the Hunter advantages, and Geniuses do them better.

                      But Geniuses have one disadvantage that Hunter's don't have. They're batshit insane.


                      So basically I'd suggest focusing on the inferiority and the insanity. Dan from the union might think he's pretty badass for patrolling for zombies with a shotgun. Then he meats Professor Challenger, who reminds him of his insecurities about never going to collage, who always has a plan while Dan survived by luck alone a few too many times, and who doesn't have a shotgun, he has a raygun!

                      But at first Dan's jealous that Challenger can find monster hunting fun, while he's terrified and does it just because of duty. Then he realises that actually monster hunting for fun isn't enviable, it's actually insane. Maybe he doesn't turn against Challenger, but he could at least "win" by not doing something so stupid you'd have to be a genius to think it up. Or perhaps by saving the hostage when Challenger runs of to have more fun chasing the already defeated fleeing monster. If you need to fight a Genius, find the right psychological leaver and pull it hard.


                      And I point out that playing Beholden could look a lot like playing Hunters, just with cooler Endowments. Replace the sinister TF:V bureaucracy with a sinister mad scientist and you're set.

                                      Threw together a little bit of stuff on Outsiders as a half-hearted attempt at the writing call. I might try and refine it into something more decent, but in the meantime, enjoy. I'm a little proud of those Dread Powers.

                                      It’s always nice when the monsters fit into nice little categories. Is it pale, nocturnal, and thirsty for blood? Chances are it doesn’t like a wooden stake through the heart. Massive, covered in fur, and howling at the moon? Silver bullets are a fair bet. So on and so forth, through all the zombies, ghosts, fae infiltrators, mummies, and all the other critters that go bump in the night.

                                      Some creatures refuse to be so easily sorted.

                                      Beyond the edges of the universe lurk things that exist on a cosmic scale, beings of mind-boggling size with minds utterly alien to human thought. They are largely constrained beyond the veil, but sometimes minute cracks appear, allowing these entities to leak the most microscopic fraction of their touch into reality. This corruption warps space and time, creating horrors that don’t play nice with the laws of physics.

                                      When this influence from beyond the dimensions known to man comes into contact with this living, it creates creatures that lack any shared features other than their origin and profound wrongness. Reality rejects them. Anything within them that might have once been human is now obliterated by the touch of something wholly antithetical to sanity and reason. The universe splinters and fractures around them.

                                      They are outsiders.

                                      A number of occult texts describe the potential progenitors of these outsiders. Many of these dread tomes are compiled by misguided cults that seek to worship them; these beings (thankfully) exist so far beyond individual mortals actions that faith does little to draw their attention. The only common agenda these beings might share is invading the universe and tearing it asunder. Something holds them at bay, so they must trust their mad servants to carry out their incomprehensible will.

                                      Dread Powers:

                                      Flux Morass (**)
                                      With this power, an outsider can wreak havoc with the reaction time of those around it. Those normally quick-witted feel sluggish and disoriented, while the dim find themselves moving with unusual haste.

                                      Action: Instant
                                      Cost: 1 Willpower
                                      Dice Pool: Intelligence + Resolve

                                      Dramatic Failure: The outsider succeeds in confusing none but itself. For the rest of the scene, it acts last in the Initiative order.
                                      Failure: Nothing happens. The outsider spends the Willpower point without effect.
                                      Success: For the rest of the scene, the Initiative order is reversed; those with the lowest rating act first.
                                      Exceptional Success: As per a regular success, but the outsider gets to act wherever it wishes within the initiative order.

                                      Kinetic Inversion (***)
                                      Some creatures have little respect for the laws of reality as understood by man. With a little focused intent, they enforce their alien mindsets on space around them. Flesh reacts strangely to harm and weapons behave in strange ways.

                                      Action: Instant
                                      Cost: 1 Willpower
                                      Dice Pool: Intelligence + Resolve

                                      Dramatic Failure: The outsider fails to impose its will on reality and is hit with painful backlash as the laws of physics reassert themselves. It takes bashing damage equal to the dice pool.
                                      Failure: The outsider cannot distort the world around it. The Willpower point is spent to no effect.
                                      Success: Space distorts in a strange manner, causing weapons and flesh to act in strange ways. Until the end of the scene, all bashing damage taken by the outsider is converted to lethal and vice versa.
                                      Exceptional Success: As a regular success, but all attacks against the outsider suffer a -1 penalty.

                                      Split (* - *****)
                                      Theoretical physics have found particles that exist in multiple places simultaneously; with this power, a monster may do the same, creating multiple weaker duplicates of itself. The original does not return to existence until all but one of the duplicates are destroyed; this last one then retroactively becomes the original again.
                                      (This power may also represent some sort of horrible ooze creature dividing like a gigantic cell, a legion of ghosts that all occupy the same space, or any other number of strange creatures).

                                      Action: Instant
                                      Cost: 2 Willpower
                                      Dice Pool: Stamina + Resolve

                                      Dramatic Failure: The outsider’s body attempts to break apart physically before snapping back together, causing excruciating pain and possible internal damage. It suffers a -2 to all rolls until the end of the scene.
                                      Failure: The outsider cannot split apart. The Willpower point is spent to no effect.
                                      Success: With a sickening tearing sound, the outsider splits into a number of duplicates equal to the number of dots in this dread power. These duplicates have all the attributes of the original, but only one Health box and cannot spend Willpower. Each duplicate suffers a penalty to all actions equal to the number of duplicates still alive. The last one alive becomes the original, exactly as it was before the use of the power.
                                      Exceptional Success: As above, but the duplicates suffer no penalty to their actions.

                                      Remi. she/her. game designer.

                                          For me, my mind revolves around the idea that Hunters are most-likely hunting the wrong monsters, killing off the (comparatively) benign creatures and allying themselves with the (again, comparatively) malevolent creatures. Princesses are an incredibly hard sell, considering that they are paragons of goodly goodness. I've tried incorporating elements of the "evil" teams from the Super Sentai/Power Rangers/Sailor Moon series, but the motivations for the villains only go so far as "conquer/destroy the world" and "unleash chaos".

                                          That having been said, I do enjoy playing around with how mortals would actually see a Princess in action in the real world. With Mages, a close equivalent to Princesses, the cell could be going against a Lovecraftian world-shaper who has become a power-hungry megalomaniac, allowing the zombie plague to reign the planet. In the terms of a small-scale session, the Mage could simply be a shy necromancer working with the dead to commit revenge on the mortals that have done wrong.

                                          With a Princess, the focus is less on power and more on duty. Princesses could work as a stand-in for Angels, sent off on a mission and willing to do whatever it takes in order to succeed. A mortal who has not encountered a Princess is more-likely to dismiss the cosplayer screaming about the Light as being very strict on their cosplay or as being plain insane. Also, keep in mind that Princesses are enemies of the Darkspawn. Perhaps a Princess of Swords is taking her status as "the chosen one" too seriously and has launched a jihad against the impure mortals that have sunken to the level of the creatures that inhabit the World of Darkness. (In my mind, I believe that Colette from Tales of Symphonia [post-spoilery events] serves as a fairly threatening Princess, should an analogue of her ever enter the World of Darkness)
