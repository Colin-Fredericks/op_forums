                                    Two things.

                                    1) In line with the story of Changeling, a lot of emphasis has been placed on making the True Fae feel like the Big Bad again. A large part of this is shifting the active presence of them onto Huntsmen and Loyalists, while letting them be the story in the background. True Fae are now emphasized to be rare, and when you meet them, they're terrifying to come across. Your instinct should be to run, not fight. How is this done? What's keeping the True Fae from just sweeping in? Well, why you don't want to fight hasn't been brought up yet, so mum on that. But how do they just not sweep in?

                                    2)This comes to a thing that didn't come across in the original writeup of the Court System, as I found out with Dave while making Los Angeles's Courts, so I figure now's as good a time as any to bring it up.

                                    So, you know how there are Approaches Huntsmen can take to gain Yearnings, whatever that is? Well, they can do that, but they don't have to.

                                    The True Fae don't have that luxury. They must go by the Approaches when they enter into a Freehold's territory. They must fulfill every single one that is involved with going after someone.

                                    Los Angeles has four Approaches, one for the freehold, one for each court. Paris has ten Approaches, two for each court. Can you imagine how much tripping over existential laws can happen with that?

                                    Yeah, no one wants to deal with that, which is why they send in Huntsmen and Loyalists first to break down the Bulwark.

                                                Because the implications of your statement goes beyond just Castigation. Imagine if TFV's ethereal bullets only hit ghosts. On a hunt, the PCs find a haunted house. Except, its actually a death spirit. The bullets are worthless, and 9 times out of 10, hunters aren't equipped to be able to tell the difference between a ghost and a death-themed spirit. Or an angel. Having to be highly specific is detrimental to the game, because it requires you to be highly specific on identifying the monster you hunt, and hunters just aren't equipped for that.

                                                Castigation being reduced to just "infernal demons" hurts the Lucifuge because the game moves from being able to deal with a demonic ghost. Unless you get the Summon power or face another demon, the ability to bind or command demons becomes worthless. And not everyone that takes those powers wants to be a summoner. And, again, it requires you to accurately identify the difference between an evil ghost, a wicked spirit, a depraved angelic Mission, an Abyssal entity, a rogue goetic, and something actually infernally tainted.

                                                And so on. Being highly specific means that you can't use your abilities out of highly specific situations, which is a poor way to design powers. And that's just from a mechanical design stand point - ArcaneArts touched on thematic aspects of it as well. The game is about standing vigil against the monsters in the night and fighting back. The game does not revolve around studying and identifying classifications - that's Mage. Or arguably Beast. The entire game's focuses are different, and you want the mechanics to support the game's themes and not force it into another direction.
