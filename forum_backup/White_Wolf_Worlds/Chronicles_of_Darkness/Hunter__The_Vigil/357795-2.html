                You make it sound like Cainites get paid and Division Six don't, which is kind of backwards. I'd say most of the Conspiracies don't get paid for it. Course, most of the Compacts don't either.

                I find the Compacts to be the natural reaction of people to the darkness when allowed to go on en masse, but Conspiracies are where the dirty questions come up, as well as the truly hopeful ideals fight to grow. It's where the human/monster dichotomy (mostly) breaks down and some serious questions about what we're actually trying to do comes up, with the vague hope that maybe we can transcend the circumstances and make a world where we can all live together comfortably regardless of humanity or monstrosity sitting somewhere in the middle of all of that.

                Certain people don't like the Conspiracies because it quite often seems like monsters are pulling the strings, and that's true, but it also creates an interesting situation where human and monster work together. Some are in bad company, like the Knights of both Saint George and Saint Adrian, but the principle is still really hopeful-if you believe you can work through the vast thorny maze that precedes it.

                                I find the idea that the Conspiracies aren't in-genre a little bizarre though I understand your perspectives, I think they focus on only one aspect of the mentioned conspiracies themselves.

                                Spin, spin, spin.

                                The Malleus can't be anymore the epitome of classic hunter, after all, they -are- the Exorcist or Bugenhagen from Omen. They're the experts on the "devil" and his agents on Earth! They're not only doing it because it is the right thing to do but because it is the -righteous- thing to do. While you can look from the corrupt perspectives of those controlling these organizations, player character hunters are going to be the men on the ground and for them it will likely be the mission statement. You're going to probably be Father Merrin or Bugenhagen more than the Pope or the Cardinals.

                                Aegis also struck me as while carrying plenty of room as greedy and corrupt individuals, they're a bit like the Library, from those be movies from the mid-2000's. (Though a little darker in tone.) They hunt monsters and acquire their artefacts so that they can be used to help people rather than hurt in the hands of the monsters.

                                The Lucifuge and Cheiron are for those players who want to play the Hellboy trope a little. They want to blur the line of what it means to be a Hunter and the thing that you hunt. I feel like these two conspiracies would be more hidden than the others, especially their agenda, and especially from their boots on the ground. For the Lucifuge it's a 'family that took me in' after some Malleus guy tried to kill me claiming me the antichrist, while the Cheiron group might be more "You did good out there today, bill. Shame about the leg. Listen, there's some... experimental surgery the boffins down in R&D have been pushing. ...just... sign here, wouldja?"

                                I feel like all of these conspiracies are well within genre. Perhaps Lucifuge and Cheiron are more for those Hunters who falter on their path up the tiers but I think Malleus and Aegis are definately easy to see as 'good guys against the dark'.

                                    The conspiracies are essentially the epitome of the dark mystery described in the World of Darkness core book than most splats have gotten. They turn a standard game of hack and slash into a World of Darkness hunter game.

                                    Not liking vampires running Task Force Valkyrie or other conspiracies being influenced by dark forces is more of an out of character preference. 'I like Task Force Valkyrie. I don't like that they're controlled by vampires.'

                                    From a gaming standpoint, it's great, because it allows you to play a hunter while also uncovering the dark mysteries of the World of Darkness. And a quote from the core, 'The world is one of shadows within shadows. Those who participate in these conspiracies should uncover as much of them as they can, lest investigators become unwitting pawns in the games of greater forces. But drawing back the curtain on one mystery reveals even more curtains, each hiding new secrets. It's not possible to uncover them all. Yet, characters can certainly work to reveal more than would otherwise be known, and so free themselves from these dark influences.'

                                    Your character doesn't know that they're being manipulated by vampires, or being used as a pawn in a struggle for control of a conspiracy by two opposing forces. But they have the opportunity to find out, and create more story.

                                                    Eh... I would disagree; even if the original Hunter book came out before Mage or Second Sight (it came out three and two years after, respectively), it has buckets of Dread Powers that you could use to devise magicians with. More to the point, there's other groups in Hunter whose primary target is magicians (and others whose primary target is vampires) and it feels odd that the Malleus isn't one of them. The Malleus Maleficarum feels like it should be fighting demons and witches first and foremost on the name alone, but behind that is also the history of the Inquisition and the very nature of the holy book they use in their worship that states "maleficos non patieris vivere". Their chapter fiction is all about them fighting demons. It makes the whole "Their primary target is vampires" bit feel... Silly.

                                                    The Malleus, to me, is sort of an opportunity to have fun with a grand, slightly dogmatic conspiracy where questions of faith brush against a reality that does not give easy answers, and where the compassionate heroes of one week are very easily the bigoted villains of the next. Maybe that's treading too much on the Long Night's territory, I don't know, but the secret vampire-eating conspiracy bit feels out of place with an organization like that. I could understand if there was a heretical schism, say, where one group wanted to bind demons to use against the devil's purposes, or perhaps claims that the angels that sometimes provide aid/assistance to the Malleus seem... Strange, somehow; these would feel like natural internal troubles for such an organization to have. The vampire bit just feels strange.

                                                    Your mileage may vary, this is just my feelings on the matter. In any game I run, that's how I focus them.

                                                        I love conspiracies for one simple reason, the tension that comes working for them. Imagine that the players are happy and loyal hunters of one of the conspiracies and then little by little you start introducing things that make them doubt. Suddenly their friendly handler doesn't look so trustworthy anymore, they start to question why some objectives are chosen over others and they wonder where really do their endowments come from. My point is the potential for paranoia and tension is endless, never knowing who to trust or who you can rely on. Not only they have to worry about what are they hunting, they also have to watch their backs less their supposed benefactors betray them.
