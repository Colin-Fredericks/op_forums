    “Witnessing the Supernatural” for Hunter 2e

    01-13-2016, 11:33 PM
    Spun off from the third Open Development thread:

    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    Oh, on the subject of Hunters rapidly losing Integrity, that's going to happen whether or not killing non-humans is a Breaking Point, because “witnessing the supernatural” is also a Breaking Point, and that's kind of a Hunter's whole schtick.
    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    That's definately gong to have to change. Perhaps immunity to that will be part of the Hunter template.

    IMO if Breaking Points are that common, they loose all ability to create drama or story. It would be like making feeding a Humanity 1 sin for Vampires.
    Originally posted by Crazy_Ivan View Post
    Indeed. I've GMed over 40 sessions of Hunter with Integrity rules, and “witnessing the supernatural” had to go. It was tiring, undramatic and mechanically unsatisfying when one of the characters dropped from Integrity 7 to 3 in four sessions merely from seeing supernatural phenomena, and others had similar results. On the other hand, one character had good Resolve+Composure along with Meditative Mind 3 from the start, and lost maybe 3 points of Integrity over 11 months.

    And yes, the campaign was monster-of-the-week sandbox, and so I gave bonuses to rolls from killing as the monster hunting was the point of campaign. Greatest horror was gained from fucked up decisions characters did that had nothing to do with violence and spiral of despair some characters faced, not from endlessly wondering if it was wrong to dust murderous vampire.
    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
    I mean, they kind of need to be frequent to matter. It's really pretty hard to fail Breaking Point rolls, unless they have really heavy penalties.

    And again, there's always The Code to get rid of inconvenient Breaking Points... At a cost. There's a good reason ordinary people avoid and even willfully ignore the supernatural in the world of Chronicles of Darkness. If you're going to confront it anyway, you're going to have to deal with the consequences. For some Hunters, that will mean developing severe PTSD as they slowly succumb to Integrity loss and Condition pileup, while for others that will mean using the Code to desensitize themselves to the trauma of the supernatural, with all of the issues that come along with desensitization.
    Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post
    Better rare Breaking Points then boring Breaking Points IMO.
    Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
    Will only mention idea of one of users from other topic...
    SpanishInquisition wrote:

    I completely agree with this. In fact in my own GMC / Mortal Remains Hunter the Vigil game I've replaced the question, “What have you forgotten?” with “What can't you forget?” Which served to give players a definite motivation to hunt and me ideas about what sort of monsters they want to see. For example one PC survived a Slasher attack, when the Cell encountered a Slasher it put his character in the spotlight. Hunters are human - they're just incredibly committed they can't turn back.
    And yes, Breaking Points on witnessing supernatural should be limited - I proposed in our RPG club game that till the creation of Code character need to have BP from seeing all supernatural - after the Code, only most mind breaking. Then I would only point this in extreme situations - like, I don't know, someone turning on the left side from the inside or something like this ( think John's Carpeneter Thing here situations ).

    Interesting would be if Hunters should be immune on Lunacy or Sleeping Curse based on resistance to Breaking Points from witnessing supernatural. But this is probably an offtopic now...
    I'm pretty sure that DaveB will shoot down any suggestion of using the Code to get around the Sleeping Curse; he's said as much in the past. Likewise, I don't think Lunacy will be sidestepped in this way. And I suspect this will expand to also include similar effects such as Disquiet and Sybaris, if and when they're revised to invoke Breaking Points. It's something that ought to be pointed out in Hunter 2e, though. But I don't want to get back into this argument again, so I'll keep my mouth shut about it for now. If the Hunter dev is interested in my views on the subject, she can ask and I'll point out the threads where Dave and I had our knock-down drag-out fights so she can decide for herself.

    But the main point of the above quotes is the question of what to do about the “witness the supernatural” Breaking Points in Hunter 2e. I can see three options:

    • Hunters are always subject to this sort of Breaking Point, unless and until they develop immunity to specific kinds of supernatural phenomena (e.g., through the Code, or by picking up an appropriate Supernatural Merit).
    • Hunters are never subject to this sort of Breaking Point; it's a benefit of being a hunter on par with Risking Willpower.
    • Some sort of compromise between the two, such as wyrdhamster's suggestion above.

    If Hunter 2e divorces Hunter-style Tier from GMC-style Tier and from orgs, I could see this question being answered differently based on the Hunter-style Tier: low-tier gameplay would assume that these Breaking Points are in effect by default unless and until individually bypassed; high-tier gameplay would eliminate them entirely; and mid-Tier gameplay might offer some sort of compromise such as a modified low-their model where every “supernatural witness” Breaking Point that's neutralized grants a bonus to resisting the ones that aren't.

    That said, it would probably be cleaner to pick one and apply it across the board. The first has the advantage of minimally undercutting the horror of the setting; the third has the advantage (according to Crazy_Ivan's unofficial playtesting) of making the game playable.

      I pretty much approach suffering from this Breaking Point as a prerequisite to becoming a capital H Hunter and that changing that Breaking Point with the Code is when the Hunter template actually crystallizes - while there might be some Hunters who gain the template without that step, they don't really stabilize and focus their mind until they do so. This doesn't give them blanket immunity from actively aggressive supernatural effects like the Sleeping Curse, Lunacy, Disquiet or Sybaris but it does keep their mind from trying to repress it all away, etc.

      (On a related note, I believe Slashers do much the same thing with at least one Breaking Point related to murder, most often dealing with premeditated murder, but that's a whole different topic.)

          My houserule on the matter is that if you pick up either a supernatural merit or a minor template (or both, of course), you become immune to most Breaking Points against the supernatural. You can still experience them, if they're significant enough. However, I'd allow a way for Hunters to gain Integrity immunity to some of the most dire supernatural effects (such as Lunacy) by taking the following steps:

          1) Experience the supernatural effect and suffer a Breaking Point.
          2) Fail the Breaking Point and lose Integrity.
          3) Buy back that dot of Integrity using Practical Experience.

          Once the process is completed, the Hunter is now immune to, say, Lunacy.

                                          I personally house-rule a third Anchor to Hunters called Fixation.

                                          Mortals aren't ignorant of the supernatural so much as they aggressively deny it. That's why viewing the supernatural is a breaking point, the sight of it traumatizes them because their mind desperately wants to lock it away. In that world lies only madness and despair, but just shut your eyes and you can live a care-free life (or at least, relatively care-free that is). It's escapism done as a defensive mechanism, and when that defensive mechanism fails...

                                          Hunters are fundamentally different to mortals in that they simply can't look away. Due to a personal tragedy or some other harrowing experience, they can't deny the existence of the supernatural. They become obsessed with ridding the world of it, or at least the things that break people in body and/or mind. It's always on their mind, a Fixation. A Hunter's Fixation is what drives their hunt. It's how they personally define the Vigil for themselves. Whenever the sight of things they were not meant to see stretches their psyche to the breaking point, the Fixation is what keeps it from snapping. A Fixation could be a loved one they wish to protect, a moral principle they uphold, or a kind of monster they have vowed to rid the world of. Whatever the case, the Fixation must relate to the Vigil in some way.

                                          In game terms, a Fixation is a hybrid of a Virtue and a Trigger Point. It's like a Virtue in that if the Hunter sticks to it against difficulty (they manage to protect their loved one, uphold their principle, kill the type of monster) they regain all spent Willpower. They may do this once per chapter (thus giving them a third refill per chapter, instead of the usual two). It's like a Trigger Point in that it replaces a breaking point. If the Hunter fails to stick to their Fixation (the loved is seriously harmed or possibly killed, they go against their principle, fail to kill a monster and let it escape) they suffer a breaking point at an automatic chance die. Their psyche comes crashing down as the Vigil betrays them. Another benefit to the Fixation is that they can risk Willpower a second time in any given scene, so long as it relates to their Fixation.

                                          So basically, Fixation is the context of which the Hunter interacts with the story. It encourages both the player to defend their character's charge, as their world comes crashing down if they don't. It also gives them quite a boost to do so, as successfully defending it regains all spent Willpower, thus encouraging the player to go for broke since if they succeed they get all their Willpower back anyways. They can also risk an additional Willpower on top just for that charge, so it's quite likely they'll have two instances of risking Willpower to defend their charge, further encouraging the go-for-broke attitude. It also fits brilliantly with the all-or-nothing theme of Hunter.

                                          Mind you, Fixation does nothing to protect against things like Lunacy or Dissonance. Fixation is simply something that keeps the Hunter from ignoring the supernatural, it does nothing to protect them from active forces screwing with their head.
