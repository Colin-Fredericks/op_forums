    How prevalent are Conspiracies on their home turf?

    01-01-2016, 04:54 PM
    I don’t know if this particular topic is addressed somewhere in the line (I only have read the core and Compacts And Conspiracies).

    Some Conspiracies (and, I guess, Compacts) have a canon HQ where their head honcho resides and does whatever the heads of a conspiracies like to do. But just how much of a presence the members of the conspiracies have in that particular city and how is nightlife different because of that?

    I am asking this because the two biggest cities in my country (I’m Italian, so Rome and Milan) are both the headquarters of conspiracies, and those whose highest members are fairly important for the organization itself (take the Lucifuge out of the Lucifuge and you have… a great plot line, but a completely different set of hunters. Baudolino is slightly less important but still no one to laugh at). Also, they know each other and have tea parties. Ok, maybe not tea parties, but I like that image. I mean, if I go by crossover canon (and I like to go as much by canon as possible), this kinda limits what sort of stories I can tell.

    In my games the Malleus Maleficarum has a “pact” with the resident vampires (the Lancea takes blood tolls from the citizens and gives some of them directly to the Inquisition, Baudolino is happy, the MM looks the other way), and Vatican City is strictly off limits, but I think I am stretching canon as much as I personally can.
    And I don’t know what the presence of the Lucifuge could do to Milan. I mean, she’s an ancient badass, fairly protected and hell (eh eh) bent on destroying evil. Being supernatural in Milan must not be fun.

    Are there some info about this? I know I can bend canon and do what I want cause a pirate is free and I am a pirate, but having definite answers would help a lot. Thanks.

    Italian nuisance. English is my second language, so be patient!

      Canonically there's no answer but I like to play it as though the Conspiracies are among the biggest baddest players in the entire WoD. So to me, the MM rule Rome with an iron fist. You might get the occasional witch or demon hiding beneath notice, but no monsters have a serious power base. (I don't think Baudolino would strike a pact with Rome's Vampires, it's too risky, he'd get blood from further away).

And the reason the Pontifical Swiss Guard still have swords and halberds is so they can bless them with the Wrathful Sword of St. Michael.

      (I see Vatican City as the setting for a heist plot. The ultimate secure fortress that players might raid for rare manuscripts.)

          Up until Blood and Smoke came out, I described the Mediterranean as a killbox, a place monsters avoided at all costs. Even with Vampire's Athens poking a hole in that and creating wiggle room, I still largely run the Mediterranean as a tough place to exist as a monster.               I tend to give Conspiracies less political and geographical power, in exchange for more efficacy. Gangs and mafias that hold a city with an iron grip tend to have problems with secrecy, because an important part of intimidation is knowing who is intimidating you and what they're capable of. I tend to run Hunter groups as extremely efficient and deadly, to give them the weight they need to keep the supernatural at bay. My Hunter groups move in the shadows and instil fear in the supernatural precisely by being unknown. Vampires exist in Rome (especially the Lancea), but they live in fear that the random guy they drag into an alley to feed off ends up being a Hunter who knows powerful Benedictions. So they are far more cautious and sneaky than vampires in other places.

              My supernatural vs. Hunter headcanon is a cold war in the shadows. The sides of the conflict don't fight in the open, they try to outmanoeuvre each other with secrecy, assassination, pawns and so on.

                              Belial's Brood, specifically the faction known as the Mercy Seat, have their oldest and largest stronghold in Genoa, where they constantly chip away at the Catholic Church, avoiding direct confrontations. So there's something you could add to the vampire side of things.

                              I've never really run a game that took place in one of Hunters' canon strongholds or headquarters. There was one with a Task Force Valkyrie barracks, though. They operated in a military base of some sort, it's been so long I forget which. It was mostly a jumping point where Valkyrie could jump off into the Caribbean if needed, but there were a group of hunters there, including one military prisoner. The entire operation was overseen by a bully of an officer who was a ghoul. The players shifted to the Abbey before that plot was resolved but they both died off-screen. Before Hunter came out I had cribbed some stuff from Hunter the Reckoning that led to having a very influential Hermit having retired to the city.

                              What I'd factor in is this. Basically, providing they aren't completely inept, if a Hunter has five dots of Status, providing they live in a set place and aren't moving around, they've begun molding the world around them. Not necessarily in a magical sense, but these are people who have seen a lot of shit. They know not to trust certain kinds of people, they know some people might succumb too easily to promises of power. And through whatever means, they're going to try to make sure the right people get in the right places, as well as have people who will report back to them about oddities or just keep tabs on local news agencies. That's just regular hunters, though.

                              When you're talking about conspiracies and their bases of operations you're talking about a place that's had multiple Status 5 and probably a lot more Status 2-4 there for decades or even centuries. I wouldn't go out and make an army of hunters, but they would have access to enough people that trust them that they could have a makeshift army, if necessary. Police, local gangs or tribes, militias and political activists, basically any organized and large group of people would have been watched and infiltrated or allied with, with at least a few people knowing what's really up and being willing to help if the situation comes up. And of course, in decades of preparation you've no doubt gathered enough ordinance to arm them.

                              I wouldn't say these places are completely supernatural free, though. Sure it's easy to weed out vampires if you have a large and organized force, but there's always going to be one paranoid loner or some elder in torpor who could easily be overlooked. And most monsters don't organize in neat factions where you can track one back to its den and eliminate it. There's a lot more than the gamelines in the World of Darkness, and for a lot of the more dangerous things there's nothing anyone could do about it. As horrible a time a mortal would have facing a second edition werewolf down, a mortal facing down an idigam would probably gladly switch with them.
