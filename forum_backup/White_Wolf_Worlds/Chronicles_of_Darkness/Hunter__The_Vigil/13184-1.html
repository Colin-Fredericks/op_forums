    They still kill vampires, they still kill other supernatural threats. It leaves what the vampires behind it exactly want up to the ST. The Lucifuge aren't deprotagonized by getting their powers from hell and having their mentor be some weird immortal mystery woman.
    The enigma comes when what does the individual vampire or vampire cabal behind it wants. Maybe it's En Isiratuu returned to bring another 12,000 year rule over all of vampire kind. Maybe it's a bunch of America's founding fathers, turned to vampires, leaving themselves a foothold. Maybe it's the VII, maybe it's a democratic union of 50 vampires who vote on everything. Maybe it's the Endiku ensuring that they always have more vampires to spawn with. I can understand you being upset if it said 'TFV is run by John the Vampire who wants to rule all of humanity,' but there's nothing like that. There's a vampire, or vampires behind it, and they occasionally let vampires off the hook.

    As for Nightstalkers, I think you're misquoting, it actually says the Barrett Commission has a senator or two. It's the hunters who don't have the pull.

    All that said, I can certainly understand why people might not want to run it that way. It seems to hearken back to Vampire the Masquerade for me, where vampires were behind everything, but to be fair vampires do have a lot of abilities to control minds and crowds and bind people to them, so it does make sense that at least some vampires would have power beyond their domain and covenant. Being the toolbox that it is, however, you are free not to run it like that. It doesn't affect anything if you take it out. You can easily play Task Force Valkyrie as not having any vampires behind it, just like you can have every conspiracy run by vampires behind the scenes.

                Originally posted by nofather View Post
                The Lucifuge aren't deprotagonized by getting their powers from hell and having their mentor be some weird immortal mystery woman.
                Deprotagonised is a hard thing to pin down, but there is a few fundamental difference between the Lucifgue and TF:V

                1) Lady Lucifuge is a part of the Lucifuge, the Vampires are outside TF:V.

                2) The Lucifuge's major goals (protecting humanity and preventing it's members from becoming evil) are probably shared and supported by Lady Lucifuge. She probably created them. In contrast TF:V thinks their major is protecting humanity from monsters but in fact it is protecting vampires from humanity. Protecting the Masquerade, a campaign against other monster hunters.

                (Having powers from hell doesn't matter here nor there. It's what you do with said powers and what you had to do to get them that matters).


                Deprotagonising is a very hard concept to nail down in general. However I would say that the way this moves TF:V's ability to decide their own goals to the vampires is at least a little deprotagonising (for the organisation as a whole).

                                I think something we forgot about is the need for black funding. Vampire money doesn't even show up on a senate subcommittee for this stuff, the $850,000 talked about in C&C is just to keep it government in the first place. It makes perfect sense when you consider some vampires have money enough to do this, and that they definitely have enemies. Plus, TFV's mission is to conceal everything. Vampires want to conceal everything. It doesn't mean TFV is blindly subservient to the vampires and their orders either. Example, how do we know that STERNE isn't supposed to allow hunters knowledge of what's going on so that they can slowly get out of vampire control? Plus, it's been said in more than a few books that the bureaucratic hangups mean that a hard-charging TFV cell can easily eliminate a few vampires before command comes down on them for it.

                                Also, TFV might not want to be under complete government control, because face it, the US government doesn't know half the time what half of it's agencies are doing. We've seen the ATF give guns to criminals, the CIA running drug rings, the military testing germ warfare on US civilians, and the FBI and NSA observing everything. TFV and the vampires to me represent just another example of the government's agencies not following their own rules because to them it needs to be done.

                                    To me having vampires control TFV fits in with what I've always seen as an underdeveloped possibility, vampire monster hunters. After all the Masquerade depends on a lot being hidden and while vampires are generally willing and able to clean up after themselves, what about all those other random things? Vampires arguably have a far stronger interest in killing the supernatural than hunters, a hunter may want to save those he knows or the people hurt by the supernatural but a vampire is hunting things that if exposed could destroy his entire species. And given vampires tendancy to get things done by manipulating mortals controlling the governments response to the supernatural seems a natural fit.

                                    While its still corruption I see it from the perspective of the interests of Hunters and Vampires lining up pretty well. Vampires want the supernatural destroyed and hidden with the possible exception of other vampires(and even then they don't usually like each other), hunters want the supernatural destroyed and accept that its going to remain hidden. Its win win for the most part. And where its not win win it can become an interesting struggle between idealism and pragmatism. Will a cell of TFV disobey orders and kill a given vampire? Even if it might mean the end of TFV?
