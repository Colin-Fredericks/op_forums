    Compact: Llannerch Athrawon - The Wizard Behind the Hero

    06-28-2015, 08:24 AM
    Art by Reaper Verse












    “Recognise her?”

    “I think it’s that celebrity doctor... Dr Litwak, Magus?”

    “I told you, I’m not in character. And time for a test. What monster was she in the mythic age?”

    “Obviously vaccines hadn’t been invented back then, so she couldn’t have opposed anti-vaxxers. It would be something similar. Sick children… social status of medicine... some sort of demon who punished the wicked with plague. She gave people an excuse to banish the sick, you say disease only strikes sinners so you feel justified kicking them out to prevent contagion.”

    “Full marks. Question two, why don’t we want her going after anti-vaxxers?”

    “Come on Greg, you ask that every time. Fear is the disease not the symptom. Scared people are irrational, especially about their kids. Scare people into using vaccines and they’ll panic about something else. Will killing her really make people more rational?”

    “No. If you want to do that study medicine and minor in journalism, if you think kids should be protected from scared parents go into politics and make vaccines mandatory. Or don't, humanity will figure something out, it usually does. Our job is just to smooth over a few bumps on the road. Now” he handed over a folder “we want a full excision from the mythscape. The Dreamwalkers have identified three Heroes on her tail. Third test, who shall we use, and how?”


    When asked about their origins the Hunters of Llannerch Athrawon tell this story to their apprentices, it might even be true.

    In the hearts of all mankind dwell the archetypes, primordial ideas that humanity created to make sense of a confusing world. At the dawn of humanity the most powerful archetype was known by many names. The Shaman, The Priest, The Hermit, The Magician. The man who walked beyond the borders of the village to confront mankind’s fears, to learn from the quest, and then to return as a teacher.

    As history progressed The Shaman was supplanted by The Hero. Like his predecessor the Hero would go to face fears, but he would slay them and return not to teach, but to bask in glory.

    However an Archetype as powerful as The Shamen is hard to erase, instead it transformed. The Shaman’s existence began to revolve around The Hero, a stepping stone on The Hero’s path to glory. Yet something unexpected happened. Freed from the need to teach, the Shaman’s knowledge became ever more esoteric. Wisdom that anyone could learn became occult lore that ordinary men could not and did not want to comprehend.

    Llannerch Athrawon claim that their earliest founders were ancient wise men whose archetype caused them to realise that they were in fact vessels for a archetype from mankind’s subconscious. These ancient wise men uncovered the secrets of the mythscape, they learned that their role was a side character in the Hero’s story, and they decided to roll with it.

    [Picture: A long bearded man wearing a robe and wizard hat flourishes an enormous scroll.]

    Behold! I am Myrddin!
    Keeper of the ancient scrolls!
    Harken, Hero, to the prophecy and learn your destiny!

    Fast forward a few thousand years and you get to the modern Llannerch Athrawon. They may or may not be touched by fundamental human archetypes, but like all myths the literal truth is not the point. Llannerch Athrawon choose to act as though they are because the Heroes have been touched and playing to archetypes is the best way to direct the Hero’s power before it’s wasted on plans that would be sensible, would be morally acceptable, only if they were in a story.

    Hunters
    You were always a power broker, an attaché or nebulous assistant to politicians. You were the master of the back room deal, the urinal chat, the quid pro quo, but you weren't in it for power or money. You believed in the semi-technocratic, economic, and globalist ideals that governed the world. And when the markets crunched you saw educated people flocking to populist demagogues on the far left and far right. The question burned inside you. Why? Why was humanity so easily seduced by easy answers. The experts never predicted this, they couldn't explain it, so you called on every contact you had to find someone who could.

    When you were a child your brother was torn apart by a mob, accused of witchcraft. Your family fled to America and you spent your life becoming the anthesis of the ignorant superstitious mob that you escaped. In your studies you learned a lot about why people take up torches and pitchforks. The symbiosis between the Beasts who cultivate ignorance and superstition, and the Heroes who direct it. Once you wanted nothing more than to turn your back on the world you left behind but somebody has to be the hero, and if the Heroes can't do it that leaves you.

    As a teenager you dreamt of being swept off your feat by a blue eyed lantern jawed hero. A Beast decided to teach you that a woman should be strong and independent, and the lesson tore you in two. You're pushing thirty but half of you is still that wide eyed teen hoping for a strong hero to protect her from a vast and scary world. The other half is cynical, street smart, and psychologically incapable of forming an emotional connection to another human being. You can switch between your two halves at will, which is why Heroes will fight any foe for the chance to rescue you, and how you make sure the Heroes always win.

    In your youth you studied in the Towers of High Sorcery, received the blessings of Mystra, and dredged up forgotten lore from ancient ruins and vast caverns beneath the Earth. But you don't have time to play Dungeons & Dragons these days, nor do you quest through dungeons or fight dragons in real life. That's the Heroes' job. But your days planning a quest through a dungeon taught you what you needed to plan a Hero's quest into a Lair, and knowing how to stay in charachter is vital if you want a Hero to stick to your plan.

    Who could predict that a show about colourful ponies making friends, having adventures, and working in harmony would be popular outside the pre-teen girl demographic? You could. You saw the signs in the mythscape, people were ready for a change from the ironic and the cynical, and you made sure your show was first to market, providing uplifting idealism with a large topping of teamwork and respect for society.

    The Enemy

    Llannerch Athrawon consider their prime enemy to the be the mythscape, the part of humanity’s collective unconscious that contains primal ideas and archetypes. The order believes that something damaged the mythscape back in prehistory (though many suspect the “damage” is a natural product of evolution creating the human mind piecemeal). Beasts, Heroes, and their own abilities are products of this damage; of course only the first two are a problem.

    Beasts are teachers, but they teach only through fear and according to Llannerch Athrawon that’s the problem. Fear is the mindkiller, the enemy of rationality. Fear is all well and good for simple problems, fear of a tiger will keep you safe from the tiger. Fear of global warming will push you towards comforting rituals rather than the sweeping technological or sociological changes needed for a real solution. Fear of people outside your tribe might have valuable lessons but they’re just not worth the costs.

    As for Heroes. Their tendency towards collateral damage would be bad enough, but as a subconscious archetype they represent selfish and violent solutions to problems. That belongs back in the bronze age.

    However the Compact has also realised that on the whole, whatever damage afflicts the mythscape, it’s not that big of a deal. The damage happened back in the bronze age (or earlier) and it never stopped humanity advancing in the millennia since then. The true Llannerch Athrawon still try to fix the damage regardless. They are psychonauts exploring the collective unconscious for a solution and storytellers who try to manipulate humanity’s mythology. Llannerch Athrawon realised a long time ago that trying to be a behind the scenes puppet master for all of fiction was a fool's errand, but their ranks contain master storytellers who can change the course of fiction with a single masterpiece, and of course, the inevitable legion of unaffiliated copycats.

    The rest of the Compact, the not-true Llannerch Athrawon, comprise the vast majority of the members. They just deal with the symptoms not the disease. They keep Heroes from causing collateral damage, they use Heroes to prune individual Beasts from the mythscape. It won’t change the world, but a life saved is a life saved.

    Stereotypes

    Ageis Kai Doru: Those assholes stole our bag of dream sand!

    Lucifuge: Heaven and hell are real enough all right. I've seen the proof with my own eyes. But Christianity, that's just a story people tell to each other, and I can't help but feel it's making you unhappy friend. Try reading this.

    Ride to Damascus: If it were as easy as putting the juju on a few Beasts we'd have fixed the mythscape already, but we know better than anyone not to trifle with someone carrying that kind of power... still, I wouldn't mind having that for myself.

    The Wilde Society: If you want to commission a true master of the written word speak to the Wilde Society. If you want to commission a professional who cares about deadlines or common courtesy speak to anyone else.

    Archetypes

    Different members of Llannech Athrawon specialise in different techniques for manipulating Heroes.

    The most common role played by Llannerch Athrawon is The Wizard. They work behind the scenes manipulating Heroes to ensure their success, and when they interact directly it's through disguises or proxies.
    Free Specialty: Suberfuge (Long Cons)

    Known alternatively as The Lover or The Lickspittle, some hunters integrate themselves into a Hero's circle. As Heroes are archetypical characters themselves their relationship to people who resemble other archetypes has been codified long ago. The damsel who is constantly being rescued, the mysterious mentor who rarely answers questions, or da chief who chews out the Hero in an act of reverse psychology, Llannerch Athrawon have played them all.
    Free Specialty: Subterfuge (Staying in charachter)

    The Visionary remains true to Llannerch Atherwon's origonal mission, to understand and fix the mythscape. Visionaries quest through the Astral to try and discover what has damaged to humanity's psyche and how it might be fixed, or at least mitigated.
    Free Specialty: Occult (Astral)

    Status

    Status in Llannerch Athrawon is an odd thing. Partly it exists as an informal grapevine between members, but mostly Status consists of acting like a wizard, at lest while you're "on the clock". Guiding Heroes, enacting schemes, and even learning about the Occult all increase Status. The unusual training Llannerch Athrawon provides it's hunters gives them the ability to instinctively recognise each other, and how well they fulfil the Compact's mission.

    O Someone thought you had what it takes, and introduced you to Lightweaver in the astral realms. You passed the Archetype’s test and were inducted into Llannerch Athrawon. Your training grants the Omen Sensitivity Merit, but ignore the Drawback. In addition you may study Lightweaving.

    OOO You’ve done your job well and your name has started to spread among Llannerch Athrawon. Members of the Compact trust you enough to introduce you to their assets, and you’re starting to build a network of your own. Take the Fixer Merit.

    OOOOO: As you reach the apex of Llannerch Athrawon the supernatural senses granted by the Compact's unique training grow. Take the Unseen Senses Merit applied to all supernatural phenomena. In addition, you can discern different types of supernatural phenomena. You could not tell that a Vampire actually is a Vampire, but you could tell two Vampires are the same kind of monster and if you identify one as a vampire you can identify Vampires from that point on.

    Lightweaving (O - OOOOO)

    Llannerch Athrawon has historically had a problem. Drawing powers from occult archetypes has made them surprisingly good at finding Endowments. Being small and not well armed they have been vulnerable to larger Conspiracies stealing their Endowments; and then “enforcing their copyright”.

    They have good feelings about Lightweaving though. It can only be learned deep in the astral realms from an Archetype that nobody’s heard of in a good fifteen centuries. He’s referred to only as The Lightweaver to throw other Hunters off the scent; but as he dwells in the Archetypical realm shared by most Wizardly archetypes it’s a dangerous place to make trouble; and Llannerch Athrawon has a lot of friends there.

    Most Weaves have a role; the hunter must be dressed and acting the part of the weave simply won’t function. If someone sees through the disguise or the hunter breaks character they can no longer use Weaves that depend upon their role for the remainder of the scene - usually this means that they have almost no Weaves at all unless they can slip into a bathroom and quickly change disguises. Justifying the presence of a second wizard in the area can be a stretch, especially if people are already suspicious of a fake wizard, but a second policeman or some other common role is usually straight forward.

    Agonise

    Cost: 1 Willpower

    Role: Any role which might plausibly have the chosen mundane or magical weapon

    Roll: Presence + Firearms or Weaponry

    The hunter creates a purely illusionary weapon. It cannot do damage, but it still hurts. A successful roll creates a weapon that lasts for one scene. Swords, pistols, rocket launchers or even plasma cannons or magic wands are all valid options - though the weapon's Damage cannot exceed the Hunter's dots in Lightweaving.

    Track illusionary damage as normal, but remove it at the end of the scene. Supernatraul powers that defend against mental influence can provide Armour against illusionary damage and illusionary damage can be cured by both normal methods or countermagic. Unnecessary stitches or other consequences are of course fully real.


    All A Dream

    Cost: 1 Willpower

    Role: A therapist, a police man, or some other authority figure who's supposed to explain what happened.

    Roll: Manipulation + Empathy

    Beasts don't remain in their lairs but go forth and feast. Heroes grandstand and pay little attention to collateral damage. Both draw in bystanders, witnesses and authorities whose involvement might threaten a hunter's delicate plan. So the Llannerch Athrawon find ways to distract them, or when they're feeling generous to help put people back together after a Beast terrorised them for it's own amusement.

    All a Dream is a very intricate piece of illusionism, Lightweaving cannot affect your memories, but it can create an auditory illusion that sounds like your own thoughts or a visual illusion directly on the eyeball that looks just like a memory. Done properly, to a person in the right psychological state, Llannerch Athrawon can convince people that a Beast's Nightmare was just an ordinary nightmare or that the Hero was horrified by the car crash - not bragging that the Beast died in the collision. The ethics of this practice are debatable (especially since mindgames can go horribly wrong), the effectiveness is not.

    All a Dream can only be invoked on a character who recently Failed or Dramatically Failed a Breaking Point.

    Dramatic Failure: The hunter only convinces his target that whatever authority he represents is part of a conspiracy dedicated to hushing up the supernatural truth.

    Failure: The hunter just sounds implausible, as this is the world of Darkness the mortal may still decide to keep quiet but the damage remains.

    Success: The effects of a recent breaking point caused by exposure to or an attack by the supernatural vanish and the target believes the hunter’s story. This may even restore a lost dot of Integrity.

    Exceptional Success: As above, and the Hunter gains a positive condition. The mortal may open up and provide Informed or offer a favour at some point.


    Bleaching the Grey

    Cost: 1+ Willpower

    Role: None

    Dicepool: Manipulation + Lightweaving

    A simple Weave, Bleaching the Grey creates a dead body. While originally invented by a member of Llannerch Athrawon to fake his own death hunters have found that there's no ends of practical uses for a dead body.

    The body created can appear to have died of any cause the hunter wishes and reacts naturally to post-mortem trauma; which allows for tricks like creating a body that died of untraceable poisons then shooting it with your enemy's gun.

    Surface examination will never reveal the ruse, though a bizarre or implausible death may still attract suspicion. Medical examination, a perception roll by a creature with superhuman senses, or magical examination may uncover the truth if they roll higher than the Hunter's successes.

    The corpse created lasts for Lightweaving * Willpower spent days.

    Crystal Ball

    Cost 1 Willopwer

    Role: Wits + Science (potentially opposed by magical defences)

    One of the simplest but most useful weaves, Crystal Ball creates an auditory and visual illusion of another place and time. Be it what's happening right now on the other side of that wall or what happened in the Hero's home the night he became a Hero. The hunter must know the exact distance between herself and her target, taking into account the movement and curvature of the Earth. Over short distances or anywhere the hunter can see with their own eye (including with binoculars or telescopes) this is automatic, for longer distances LLannerch Athrawon simply hired a few programmers and can get the numbers by typing in the GPS and date they wish to see into their phones.

    In game terms, the player may ask the Storyteller one question per success rolled, which he must answer (within reason).

    Dreamwalking

    Cost: 1 Willpower

    Role: A shaman or a similar practitioner able to commune with other worlds

    Dicepool: Wits + Lightweaving

    Any mortal can in theory reach the Astral world, but it takes decades of training. Consequently whenever they have an Endowment one of the first things Llannerch Athrawon do is try and find an easier way in. Fortunately Lightweaving provides.

    Dreamwalking (also known as Holodecking) creates a full five sensory illusion of the Astral realms. For some reason this in turn allows Astral beings to perceive the hunter, Llannerch Athrawon don't know why but they aren't going to look a gift horse in the mouth.

    In short, Dreamwalking provides quick and easy access to the Astral Realms, without years of training or meditation.

    Originally Llannerch Athrawon would Dreamwalk in empty flat fields, walking in the Astral requires them to walk in the real world, but recently they've found a better way than fulfilling the old stereotypes of mad ecstatic shamans. It turns out they didn't need to walk, just move their legs in a walking like fashion. Now, so long as they have the necessary equipment, the hunters suspend themselves in mid air where they can wave their limbs without moving and in complete privacy.


    Mask of Many Faces

    Cost: 1 Willpower

    Role: None

    Dicepool: Manipulation + Lightweaving

    The most common weave, Mask of Many Faces allows the hunters of Llannerch Athrawon to disguise themselves as just about anything of the same Size and general shape. Usually this means as a person or as an inconspicuous inanimate object.

    The illusions are powerful and cover all five senses. With this weave a large (but not Giant) man could convincingly pass himself off as a demure woman (or vise versa). Indeed, Llannerch Athrawon has long known that most Heroes dream of rescuing a prince or princess depending on their tastes and quite a few won’t let their own sex bar them from such a useful method of persuasion.

    When disguised as a person the hunter only needs to make a subterfuge roll if they break character or someone has a good reason to be suspicious. While disguised as a police officer everyone will believe they are an officer of the law unless they have a good reason not too. Disguising yourself as a specific person usually requires Subterfuge rolls when interacting with people who know them. If a Subterfuge roll is made add Lightweaving to your dicepool.

    Disguising yourself as an inanimate object provides Lightweaving automatic successes for Stealth, so long as you’re acting like an inanimate object. Mask of Many Faces won’t help if people see a tree start walking. Recolouring yourself as a camouflage pattern and similar tricks simply adds Lightweaving to your Stealth dice pool for the scene.


    Perseus’ Shield

    Role: Someone who can create or is likely to possess magical artifacts.

    Roll: Manipulation + Subterfuge vs Composure + Empathy or Subterfuge

    Llannerch Athrawon say that the story of Perseus and Medusa is an half accurate retelling of a historical battle between a Hero and a Beast. When Llannerch Athrawon found Perseus he was halfway to inflicting an Anathema upon Medusa, one which would make her so ugly that any man or woman would flee from her; after this was done he intended to take an ordinary sword and fight a monster who did not in fact have any weaknesses.

    In response a hunter of Llannerch Athrawon disguised himself as the goddess Athena and gave Perseus a mirrored shield which would protect him from Medusa's Nightmares. The mirrored shield was in fact perfectly ordinary shield with a cheap trick (no matter what Endowment they're using at the time, Llannerch Athrawon find some way to use it to create Perseus' Shield) to make it reflective and look like a powerful artifact. It was Perseus' belief that turned the shield into an Anathema.

    In short, if the hunter beats the Hero in a contested role they can choose which Anathema the hero will create. Since the Hunters of Llannerch Athrawon are sane and actually understand how Anathema work their choices are usually superior.


    Wizard’s Weapon

    Role: None.

    Dicepool: N/A

    When taking this ability the hunter gains one weapon of a size equal or less than Lightweaving that is permanently disguised as an equally sized mundane and harmless object. If combined with Mask of Many Faces the disguise will adapt to every mask. For example a shotgun could be disguised as a large staff while acting as a wizard, an ordinary shotgun while disguised as a farmer, but shift into an umbrella when the hunter changes into a businessman.

    Wizard's Weapon does not provide a gun, the Hunter must provide their own weapon.

    Guns and similar items also disguise their ammunition, that shotgun disguised a staff could disguise both the sight and sound of firing as a lightning bolt.

    Apart from being able to travel in public with a weapon, when the weapon is disguised as a supernatural object attacking mortals with "magic" triggers a breaking point, as would any form of supernatural attack. The hunters or Llannerch Athrawon aren’t particularly great in combat, but throwing a lightning bolt can quickly sow panic in their foes. It’s a good way to clear out a monster’s cultists without the Hero having to kill them, or to scare off a couple of muggers without having to get into a real fight.

    Trying to use the weapon outside an appropriate role will break the illusion, though a shotgun disguised as an umbrella can still be extreemly useful even if you only get one shot before you break the illusion (along with your Mask of Many Faces).

    Wizard's Rune of Destruction

    Cost: 1 Willpower

    Role: Anyone who could have access to supernatural objects.

    Dicepool: Manipulation + Lightweaving

    This weave is a variant on Wizard's Weapon. It lacks the ability to create a permanent illusion but it may be used on any weapon or trap the Hunter wishes instead of a single signature item. The illusions created last one scene

    Because this weave doesn't provide any method for carrying a weapon in public, it is mostly used for improvised weapons and traps, or even accidents if they see it coming with enough time to weave an illusion.

      So the usual second post commentry.

      Llannerch Athrawon (Rough translation: Mentor's Glade, yes their name is a place) started off as an idea back when Beasts was just spoilers. If there was Heroes than obviously there would be room for Gandalf. The fact Heroes were both insane and antagonists just made it work even better - and I had the rough sketch waiting for the Kickstarter preview so I could see how Hero powers work and work that into the Endowment (see Perseus’ Shield). However reading the kickstarter document made me loose interest. Basically I agreed with the common criticisms; and at risk of this thread derailing I shan't say any more on this topic.

      In the end the revisions made me change my mind again. The idea of Beasts teaching through fear - and before I continue I want to say this next bit is strictly from an in charachter perspective - struck me as such a bad idea that I just had to write a rebuttal. Llannerch Athrawon is that rebuttal, the intro fiction makes it explicit; and that's the most of my politics that's ever ended up in my writing. Hopefully it remains the most political I get, I mean, I did manage to write Princess: the Hopeful without letting my own politics in


      One thing I am disapointed at is the Status Merit. In retrospect it ends up saying that their Archetype theory is correct; but I had to justify Omen Sensitivity somehow. It's key to the whole wizard concept. On the other hand I really like how the Dreamwalking power turned out with the suspension ropes. It's like seing a film set and seeing that there's no roof. It doesn't make it a lie, but it shows that it's... not fake, but fictional. Fictional is good word for Llannerch Athrawon.


      Fun fact. Both Stephenie Meyer and George R. R. Martin are members of the compact. Between Twilight and Song of Fire and Ice a generation of copycat storytellers are writing without a classical hero. Edward is a red herring, he's that way to sell books, the real plot is to push the urban fantasy genera from one of heroes and quests towards one of romance. I thought about making that cannon but decided it was a step too far.
