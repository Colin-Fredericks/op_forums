    Hunter question - starting equipment

    07-24-2014, 12:54 PM
    I'm starting my character generation session with my players tonight, and I was wondering if any other Hunter Storytellers had any established methods of determining how much equipment players are allowed to start with?

    One method I've seen recommends granting one or two equipment purchases rated at one dot higher than one's resource or professional training rating, along with unlimited purchases one under the aforementioned ratings.

      That's generally how I've run it when I've ST'd Hunter - I go off of dots in resources/professional training as most have recommended, but I'll also have allowances depending on backstory/justification. Hunter or not, having a guy running around packing five/six guns is silly!

      Generally, I tend to limit characters to one 'primary' weapon (large ones such as shotguns or rifles), a sidearm or two such as a pistol or a revolver, and a blade of some kind. However, this may vary - I know it's entirely possible to carry more than one large weapon, even if it isn't all that feasible. It's mostly case-by-case, but I try and be fair. The housewife isn't going to be lugging around two rifles, but the ex-hiker who works out probably could. Ammo and such... again, varies depending on what the Hunter in questions makes an effort to outfit themselves with. It's sorta complex and I suppose you could get into 'too realistic', but I like to try and cap that as well. Clips are easier to carry (in a sense) than individual shotgun shells, after all. And they're certainly easier to reload with in a tense situation.

                      Normally I let my troupe start with equipment related to their characters' jobs. A mechanic will have tools, possibly a modded car. A career thief might have lock-picks and similar tools. I will also usually allow a small arm or 1-2 resource dot long arm, regardless of their merits.

                      I realize that this can sometimes be unbalanced (a cop having access to way more equipment, flares, police tape, guns ect) than say someone who works in a business, but usually it balances out in gameplay via creative uses of whats available or the cop having to explain where all their equipment went.

                      That being said everyone starts with, or quickly obtains 1 lighter, 1 flashlight, rope. Without a doubt because we have never had a game where those things have not been useful. (also when we have a game and someone forgets them or is new and did not buy them they are almost always screwed because of it down the line)

                          For WoD I've never worried too much about Resources. After all, I'm effectively Resources 0 and yet I still have a laptop and a desktop (albeit crappy ones), and there are homeless people who pony up for Smart phones with data plans because of how important they are.

                          I don't make my players give me a list of Equipment. Anything they think they might have, they have. This isn't D&D or Warhammer Fantasy or something like that, and people generally have or can find enough basic things easily, or can get them cheap at a hardware store. That said, for Promethean or Hunter games where I'm really pushing the Supernatural or Hack/Slash style that I love so much, I might want a list of everything in the PC's car or unmarked van.

                          Usually, though, it's just a matter of what they think the characters might have. What they have on hand is a little more restricted, but if we're talking about what they could go home and get, it's pretty loose. I mean, going off of what Disper said, a normal person who works at an office still might have road flares or a gun.

                                          While I understand the role esoteric armory plays as a merit, and the general feel it gives for Sam and Dean's trunk. I felt it was needlessly vague and story breakingly useful.

                                          "How did I get this blood of a 7th son? Don't worry about it cause it says I have it on my character sheet. A piece of the true cross? Ditto. I don't know why I have a piece of the lunar lander, but its all cool"

                                          To me it takes out the bit of the story where they have to obtain the ban/weakness of the entity, and thereby sidesteps any kind of drama or moral questions that arise from it.                                               In my experience, as well as what I think I would do myself if I ever ran a Hunter quest myself, is to let my players start with relatively little equipment (letting it be based on their resourceses rating), and letting them aquire it in-game themselves. We started a Hunter quest last year, and we had the most beautiful scene, in which my character had to explain to an illigal arms dealer, that he needed something suited for hunting "lions".

                                              I think I've mentioned this before, but just giving the players the equipment from the get-go is doing yourself and your players a major disservice. You're effectively depriving them of a rich and fun oppetunity of finding those things in-game, and roleplaying how they aquire said equipment. You're removing game-play for the sake of convinience, and since we're here for game-play, this is not preferable. It should be noted however, that if you're playing a short-term story, or one that isn't very focused on your characters day-to-day life and experienceses, then this method of handeling your players equipment off-screen might work, so as not to deprive from the important parts of your chronicle, which is often the story in these scenarioes. I dont prefer those kinds of games myself, but they're entirely valid and in those, the "you got it off-screen" works to a degree.

                                              Ultimately, ask your players and see what approach they prefer and try to reconcile that with what you intend with your scenario. Me and my friends all prefered aquiring an increasingly threatening arsenal as the game went on, but some players wants to have their cake and eat it, straight off the bat. Which is fine, so long as the storyteller is prepared for it. It hardly makes sense to disallow characters to start with equipment, if this only means that the first session is going to an equipment hunt anyway, with no real trigger for said equipment hunt, the only trigger being that the character in question preferes to be armed.

                                                          To be fair, it's only at high ratings of Esoteric Armoury that you have everything you need for anything. Imagine a rank 5 spirit with a bane where depleted uranium won't cut it).
