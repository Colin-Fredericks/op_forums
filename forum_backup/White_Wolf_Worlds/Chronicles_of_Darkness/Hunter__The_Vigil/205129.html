    Hunter Families

    07-22-2014, 01:53 PM
    Hey guys!

    So I had something hit me today - do any of the Hunter books make mention of family lines of Hunters? I'm picturing something akin to The Winchesters of Supernatural fame - parents pass on what it is to be a hunter to their children and such. I know most wouldn't want to expose their children to such a life and would rather they live a normal, blind existence but I can equally see those who couldn't stand the idea of sending their children into the world blind in case something happens to them and they end up under the fangs of a vampire or something of the sort.

    Is this referenced or would this happen at all?

    Thanks,
    Mabs

              For some hunter groups, lineage is an important thing:
              - for the Aegis Kai Doru, the higher ranks are easier to reach if your family has been involved in the conspiracy, the longer the better. And the Council of the AKD is only made of people whose family has served the group with distinction for generation.
              - Task Force Valkyrie, if I remember, has a bit of nepotism, but no more than the average government agency.
              - In the same order of idea, the Malleus Maleficarum is an arm of the catholic Church, and in the old catholic families, it was (and in some case still is) customary for only or more members of a generation to become part of the Church or at least to serve it as a lay member. This must have conséquences on the MM.
              - the Lucifuge is essentially built around the idea of lineage, demonic ones. If you're in, it means one of your ancestor was involved with dark forces, one way or the other. And since there is this optional rule that the higher a hunter is ranked in the group, the greater his longevity gets (see the Chevalier Thélème and the Dame of Milan), you can figure that multiple générations of the same clan can simultaneously find themselves in the conspiracy.
              - the Knights of Saint George: Witchfinders make it clear that some members are recruited because their lineage. Moreover, the conspiracy leader is always a descendant of King Richard Lionheart (preferably a male, which puts today's group in a crisis).
              - and for the Ashwood Abbey, it is shown that some people get in because of family push/tradition. Same thing with the Barret Comission.
              That's all I can think of now. I hope it helps you.

                                          I've always kind of been fascinated with the idea of members of hunter families who went rouge and sided with the supernatural against their family, in part because I'm fascinated with people who are raised in cult, fundamentalist and hate group back grounds yet manage to break away. In the aborted Wod revealed campaign I had planned with my friends before they moved, there was going a, important NPC who was the son of an Ashwood abbey father and a Long Night mother (no I'm not going to explain how they wound up together, save that they were based off the parents of someone I know). The marriage ended in a nasty divorce and the NPC wound up losing faith in Christianity due to a friend's accident, while also finding his father's activities disgusting, with the result that he decided not to become a hunter. The plan I had was for him to gain an interest in shamanism as a religion and start hanging out with the local werewolves as result. The PCs, in turn were going to wind up protecting him and the pack he was friends with from both compacts while he prepared to testify against them as part of a major case the Center for Supernatural Civil Rights (a compact of anti-hunter lawyers in the planned campaign) were preparing against them. But like I said, the campaign never got off the ground.

                                          I know that there was a member of the Lucifuge who fell in love with a werewolf in one of the stories in spirit slayers, but have there been any other rouge members of hunter families in the Wod's history?
