    Conspiracy Theory: Compacts

    07-13-2023, 08:40 PM
    So I didn't plan on starting this thread so early, and I am not sure I am going to actually go deep into it immediately after this post, but it is a scratch that I have to itch and so this is what we are doing- the "Step 0" of this part of the project. The Culling.

    I mean, there are two main reasons why it is simply unrealistic to go through all compacts for this project- first, the sheer number of them. Compacts serve as the majority of Hunter orgs, and so while covering all conspiracies is realistic, covering all compacts to the same level would be a hell of a headache to me. However, I would probably have done so- if I were to think it is appropriate. Thankfully, it is not the case- most compacts are really not "conspiracy material". They don't cover the same reach and require the same lore as conspiracies. Sure, each could benefit from a C&C like treatment, and each also has its own secrets, yet having a weird masterplan to take over the world and/or the potential to use supernatural abilities? Yeah, that's a bit much, especially for fella such as the Union. Compacts are generally compacts for a reason, and most of the time, it should stay that way.

    But sometimes, there is a diamond in the rough- one which could be polished into something much more... interesting.

    So in here, we are going to go through all canon compacts and decide which one should be treated gain the "conspiracy treatment" and who shouldn't. Obviously, I am open for your feedback on the subject, but I am currently going to judge using the following criteria, ranked by the order of significance:

    A) Text- in a few rare instances, the text itself suggests "this org could be a conspiracy". In such instances, I obviously will include the compact in the project regardless of other factors.

    B) Endowment Material- generally, that refers to some supernatural ability a compact holds, which might be developed to a full blown Endowments, even though that scientific/mundane tools could also qualify if they are viewed as worthy enough. This includes, potentially, a "supernatural origin" of the hunters, even though it is not a common thing for compacts (those are already conspiracies, generally). It should be noted that even if that in those instances, even if the compact itself can't be developed into a conspiracy, there is the potential for its potential Endowments to serve as a base for another which may achieve it, and as such are worthy to be examined.

    C) Structure- not all conspiracies have a structure, but those with it are definitely in a better position to serve as such. 2e, for some reason, have focused a lot around fractured and small scale conspiracies, which, heh, I have some opinions on it, but regardless structure on its own may serve as a good base for a conspiracy, especially if it allows the compact to extract its power and grow beyond its limits.

    D) Patron- when a conspiracy is fractured, it generally means that something holds it together- and generally the conspiracy itself doesn't fully understand who controls it from the shadow. Compacts who are sponsored by some entity, be it supernatural or otherwise, could potentially develop to a global scale depending on the interests of the patron, which means it is something we should consider.

    E) Age- compacts are found in a metastable state. Most don't survive more than a few decades. A compact which, according to its canon, have survived for quite some time, is probably on the edge of becoming something greater than itself- and as such could potentially start working in their own secret plan for the future.

    F) Vibes- the most fluffy test which is why it is in the bottom of the list, yet also important on its own, "vibes" basically refer the to the feeling of the compact- does it resonate with some real life conspiracy theory bs? Does it feel like it is occult and significant? Does it have a weight in the setting which should be considered? Because it is so fluffy, it is subject to personal interpretation, so I am going to avoid judging according to vibes alone, and which is why the fact that the compact is being loved by someone is not enough for it to be a conspiracy.

    S) Uniqueness- this "special" test is here to make things more complicated and annoying for everyone. Before 2e, I would not have considered this test, yet 2e have added the very cool concept of chapters- basically, "regional variants" of conspiracies. Conspiracies, after all, are global entities, which means that localization is an essential part of it. Unfortunately, this concept remains as just that- a concept. We only have 2 actual examples of chapters, none of which is actually called as such- the Otodo and the Order of the Black Knight. Potentially, we could also include things like the Society of the 12 Keys or the Swords of Jibril to the mix, but that's more "iffy". Regardless, this means that if a certain compacts works well on all categories, yet lacks enough narrative weight to be distinguished as a conspiracy from another one, I would consider it to be a chapter instead its own conspiracy. I do plan to examining chapters as well, but that is a subject for a future thread. Obviously, this test is also somewhat subjective, so opinions may vary on this one.

    So with that in mind, let's see what the 2nd Tier has to offer, shall we?

    1e Core

    Ashwood Abbey: a easy yes. They answer criteria A (C&C suggests that they technically have the resources and means to be a conspiracy if they'll just stop just being a pyramid scheme), C (the whole license system), D (someone is standing at the head of the pyramid), E (dating all the way from 1855, so they are more than a 100 years old) and F ("rich people having a secret murderous society" falls within the tropes of conspiracy theories, and in 2e they are stated to have absorbed other compacts, so if a compact has chapters, isn't it basically a conspiracy?). One could even argue that the Bacchanal could serve as answering B, and they are definitely unique enough to answer S.

    Long Night: a bit more complicate case. The Prayer Endowment that they have in C&C feels proper enough to answer B, but the rest is a bit of problematic. Protestantism is a very varied branch in Christianity, and the Long Night basically rides this part all way home in C&C, which make C into an issue and the rest on extension.. but things are a bit different when Dark Eras is taken into account. While the Long Night as we know it trace its origin to around 1970, Dark Eras 2 actually extends it much further, to the time of the Scandinavian Witch Trails if not beforehand. Now, it is very likely that the Long Night of that period was very different from the modern version- especially as, considering the region and time period, we are talking about Lutheran version of the org, which is likely to still exist in Europe in modern times, and Lutheranism is much more structured compared to other Protestant Churches (even if not to the degree Catholicism is). As such, it is possible that the Long Night is either a local chapter of a wider conspiracy, or an incarnation of a fallen conspiracy, which would answer C and E criteria, while still maintaining enough degree of uniqueness- so it answers a majority of the criteria and as such gets a place on the list.

    Loyalists of Thule: another very easy yes. They have structure and hierarchy under the Three Old Men so they answer C, date to the Thule Society so they have passed the 100 years (which answers E), deal constantly with the occult and include material on ghost-bound pistoles and summoning rites of root-race ghosts (B), have a lot of conspiracy vibes (I mean, occult Nazis? Do you need more?), and potentially even have some God Machine influence (D) as per the Cold War Dark Era, not to mention that they definitely answer the S test of uniqueness. So yeah, they easily get a spot on this list.
    ​
    Network 0: so they get an immediate "yes" as they answer the A test- C&C outright present the possibility of them becoming a conspiracy, which also answers B by extension. As such, other tests are really just overkill and aren't really needed in that case.

    Null Mysteriis: a more complicated case, the Null Mysteriis seem to answer the B test as their C&C Endowment does feel more like a "true Endowment", or at least, the base for one. Additionally, they answer the Age test, being around 130 years old. They also seem to have enough organization to have competing factions within them, and the academy does lend itself into structured hierarchies, so C is also being answered to a sufficient degree. They are also definitely unique, so S is maintained. The main problem with them, however, is F- they just don't really have the vibes for a conspiracy. On the other hand, Campion are a conspiracy, so.. I don't know, I need to think about it more.

    Union: and easy no. The fact that they are 2nd Tier is an achievement on its own, and they basically fail every test- which is a good thing. They don't want to be a conspiracy, after all.

    Witch Finders

    Division Six: a bit of a oddball. While they have some structure, they are still a bunch of conspiracy theorists being manipulated by a Seer, so I assume that they are getting C (like, barely) and definitely get D. Their "extreme conditioning" could qualify as B, and they have around 47 years- which is a bit short for me to let them take E, but some may argue otherwise. The God Machine knows that TtF gave us younger conspiracies- but you probably know what I think of that. The main problem is whether they have the vibes, and more importantly, that A actively goes against it, with describing them as being absorbed by TFV. So.. yeah, really on the fence, and if B and D weren't on the table I would have just dropped them from the list on A alone.

    Keepers of the Source: those guys have a really strong B, I must say- the Dowsing Sense really fits the criteria, especially is they are indeed Lucid. They also have some support from F in terms of their description in BotD and the fact they got republished in 2e. They are definitely unique, and barely pass the Age criteria (53 years). Sure, they also have a lot against them, but I would argue that B alone would make it worthwhile to put them on the list.

    Promethean Brotherhood: easy yes, text already gives us everything we need, not to mention B from the Rite of Hecate, already described C, being apparently a incarnation of an older organization (E) and so forth.

    Spirit Slayers

    Bear Lodge: found in the same problem as Division Six- A is outright against them, mentioning that they have been absorbed to the Ashwood Abbey. If we are to ignore it, they do seem to have some material for Endowment from their Unseen Senses and reduced Lunacy (B) and some structure (C) and are over 100 years old (E). Honestly, I think that they are unique enough from the Abbey to stand on their own, so if A wasn't an issue I would have given them easy S, but A puts some very heavy burden here so... yeah, need to think about it.

    Illuminated Brotherhood: definitely have B, between the special connection they develop to the spirit world through drug use and the strange drugs they synthesis. Not sure about C, and are a bit short from getting the E criteria. I also think that their main problem is S- the use of drugs to see the truth has some strong overlap with alchemy (see M:tAs Solifacti), which puts them as having much more sense as a spirit-focused chapter of the AO than its own conspiracy.

    Talbot Group: not really have a lot going for them, actually. Serving as a support group for monsters is a interesting niche, sure, but I'm not sure they have much in the other criteria. The main thing they have standing for them is B, yet their supernatural benefits are almost identical to the Bear Lodge- so if they get the conspiracy treatment instead of the chapter treatment, this compact has nothing in their favor. Not sure if they should be a chapter of the Survivors Club- they have some overlap, but maybe they'll work better with some other group...

    Night Stalkers

    Barrett Commission:a wasted potential in terms of A- like, seriously, outside of that they really have a lot going for them. Excellent C, E, F and S. I would have given them easy spot on the list, but the text claiming that they have been absorbed by TFV is annoying. Also, makes even less sense compared to Division Six, where the logic was already shaky. Really consider to give them a spot regardless of A.

    Maiden's Blood Sisterhood: their connection to witches and the occult gives them a decent score for B, which also answers D by extension. They also seem to have enough structure to answer C, and are around 66 years old so I am willing to give them E. They do have overlap with the Survivors Club, however, so I need to give it thought (even though they are a good excuse for adapting a Cruac version for Hunter).

    Night Watch: yeah, no. Community guards and all of that, really don't feel like they have something beyond of that.

    Slasher

    Hunt Club: yeah, no. Basically fail on S on being similar to the Abbey, as they only answer the rest of the criteria as good as the Abbey does and on the same metric- only being less player friendly. So yeah, no.

    Mortal Remains

    Habibti Maa: have some interesting things going on for them. There seems to be some supernatural benefit for their deprogramming techniques, which answers B, and they have Maat as their patron (which could indicate some involvement by the Judges), so we have D. I also think that C is also on the table for them. They are young, however, and not sure about vibes- but hey, we could also make the Talbot Group into a chapter of those if we want to. So I tend towards a positive answer.

    Reckoning: easy no, basically fail uniqueness for the Long Night and have little to no support for any other criteria.

    Yuri's Group: again, fail on S towards the Survivors Club, and all else falls apart accordingly.

    Utopia Now: succeed on A- 2e mentions them as a conspiracy, and while it might have been a errata issue, I have intentionally not submitted it as an issue just so that we will have a canon support for this claim, and it seems that everyone else have done the same. Yes, they also answer other criteria, but A is enough.

    2e Core

    Nine Stars: basically a re-iteration of VASCU with less resources, so even if they were to have any argument in their favor, they would still fall, at best case scenario, as VASCU's chapter.

    SWORN: interesting case. They seem to be on the edge of the E test, but they also seem to connect to the Keepers of the Weave, which shows a longer tradition. They do have global presence, so they have C to a sufficient degree, and they are unique enough, but not sure about vibes. Considering that the KotW also weren't a conspiracy, there isn't much support for B (even though I headcanon otherwise), so I need to think about it.

    Tending the Fire

    Crimson Halo: on one hand, they are community guardians. On the other, they answer the criteria much better than any other similar group. They have some answer for C, and answer E as good as any compact could, having more than 100 years on their side. They are also unique, but lack in the other fields. I would have to think about it, yet it might just be because I really love them.

    Crossroads Convoy: the main point we have for them is C- they definitely have some level of organization. About everything else... we have no date so we know nothing about E, and the only way to justify B for them is by stealing the original covenant benefit for the Gallows Post. Unfortunately, I don't think they make the cut.

    Eden Covenant: yeah, no. Best case scenario they are a Long Night chapter.

    Las Guadanas: a big fat yes. Medium merit at 3 dots gives them B, have structure through the cartels and such so C, Santa Muerta gives D, going all the way back to Aztec Death Cults gives E, they have some great vibes for F and their attitude towards death is very different from the CoB so they also get S. In fact, they remind me a lot of my own homebrew Ebony Fangs, so it would give me an opportunity to discuss them through this compact so.. yeah, definitely on the list.

    Recalibrators: feel like a bit of small scale. They have some organization for C, but not sure about the rest. Anyway, even as a conspiracy they'll move between TFV territory and the Sanitation Collective from, Light & Dark, so I tend to exclude them from the list.

    Dark Eras

    Ama San: their whole Whale Whistle thing gives some excellent B to work with, so even though they fail on most other criteria (I think that C and E may be argued for), I would tend to give them a spot here.

    Azusa Miko: while they definitely answer all criteria to a very satisfying degree, their attitude makes them feel very close to the CoB, which would likely make them more of a chapter of the conspiracy in this regard, and as such they unfortunately do not get to the list.

    Bijin: if they still exist, it is likely they do as a AA chapter, failing on uniqueness here.

    Followers of Mansa: while cool in principle, and while they have been presented with enough structure to answer C and likely have enough Age for E in their Era, I am not sure they have enough on the other criteria to stand, unfortunately.

    Gumi: while they have structure and some age, I am not sure they have enough on the other parameters. So yeah, did I just included cut material just to ignore it again? Yeah, but we should remember that those guys almost were in the book.

    Keepers of the Weave: same state as SWORN, being entangled with them.

    Les Voyageurs: fail on S towards the Bear Lodge and potentially S^2 if the Lodge is part of the Abbey.

    Protectors of the Light: community guardians who fail like most of those groups, unless they have some legacy in SWORN.

    Scarlet Watch: a almost definite yes. Their writeup heavily suggest that they are a fallen conspiracy, and all of the dream-fate-blood-bond thing they have going on is a strong base for B. E and F also work within their Era, and it seems that at least in the past they had C, even if they are more fractured within their Era. Also, very unique IMO, getting easy pass on S.

    Soldiers of the Forbidden Sun: having more than 1400 years on their side during their own Era, it is a wordcount-caused warcrime that they are not being presented as a conspiracy in the first place. Like, by E alone they should be a conspiracy, not to mention the vibes from F, structure from C, and some base with Taoism for B. It is possible to make them a eastern AO chapter, but they feel distinct enough to stand on their own.

    Other

    Ahl al Jabal: they have a lot going for them to be considered, including B (occult interests), C, E and F- yet they have issues with S. The fact that they are being based on the Hashashins, and the AO being based around the same concept, puts them in a awkward state when considered as a conspiracy. They may work better as a AO chapter in this regard, but subject for change.

    Heritage House: honestly, their writeup is so similar to the Scarlet Watch that they sound like their modern incarnation- so yes by extension.

    *********************************

    And that's for stage 0. I'll return later to this thread after giving some though about who actually makes it through the list, but feel free to give feedback about those estimations- note that I want to trim the list as much as possible, so a lot of edge cases may fall eventually if I won't see good enough reason to keep them (hence why I will be listening to suggestions, to a degree).

    Anyway, see you next time!

          Re: A: As previously, I have to point out that the text on the Barrett Commission is that they were split up and that there are theories that they were merged into TFV (the text simply stating that TFV is the only official government-sanctioned hunter conspiracy in an edition where being separated from the FBI didn't stop VASCU from continuing to exist), this being part of a discussion of how speculation in general is more widespread than fact among hunters and specifically that some hunters think governments and other ruling bodies lending their support to compacts and conspiracies in an uneven fashion is responsible for those groups' changing fates.

          Meanwhile, I can literally find nothing in the text saying that the Bear Lodge was absorbed into the Abbey — Hunter 2e has two books out, the Bear Lodge is mentioned in one paragraph of the corebook (right before the bit you're looking at about Div 6 and the Commission), and that paragraph is there talking about how the group might have an older pedigree than surmised and evidence suggests its hunters are still around despite rumors of its collapse.

          This is all from a section of the corebook talking about how hunters don't have the complete picture of their supernatural-adjacent culture's past and present, and while I have some issues with bits of 2e treating conspiracies like open secrets, "a shadow organization with notable resources and an agenda may have recruited or formed out of this regional association of hunters that we haven't heard from in a while" is a good continuation of all the stuff 1e established about hunters being rightly paranoid, hunter groups being susceptible to compromise by the supernatural, and higher tiers of organization tending to quietly watch and/or manipulate the lower ones to save their own resources and/or further their agendas.

          That this continued harping on about stuff that didn't happen to the stated degree (or didn't happen at all) is giving me powerful recollections of people thinking the Beast Kickstarter draft was comparing the Begotten to minorities because it mentioned the phenomenon of othering in an explanation of how the Temenos relates to factuality does not make me inclined to stop pointing out that the complaints are based in unfounded or exaggerated views of the material.
