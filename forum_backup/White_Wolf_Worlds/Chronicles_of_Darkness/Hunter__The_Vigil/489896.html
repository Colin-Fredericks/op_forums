                  Generally, if you want to keep a wound, you can spend a point of Willpower before you go into Daysleep, and it will stay there afterward until you spend the Vitae to heal. Normally, this is a way to keep from coming up empty before you sleep so that you don't go into Torpor from healing. However, I see nothing wrong with having tattoos and other decorations 'permanently' emplaced by spending Willpower.

                  I think at least one NPC in one of the books (Fall of the Camarilla?) is missing his eyes, and a related book (Requiem for Rome?) mentions that losing a limb or eye as part of a punishment should be kept, since healing the injury is taken to mean that you flaunt the authority of those who punished you.

                  As for hair regrowth, I believe the mending takes care of that as you sleep overday anyway, while your body works to restore itself as it was at the point of Embrace. No Willpower or Vitae needed for minor things like that. (Though, I guess, if you got a sweet new haircut, you could spend Willpower to keep it in place indefinitely.)

                                                  I'd personally rule that preventing healing and making a feature permanent are two totally different things for a vampire. The former is undone when you deliberately heal the wound. The latter never gets undone.

                                                  I don't really think it's necessary or necessarily RAI (though I think it's certainly RAW), but I'd enforce it that way for thematic reasons. The physical stasis of a vampire's body is part of the theme. Being able to easily change and un-change kind of cheapens the effect and feels kind of silly, really. I see the unchanging undead form as one of the (minor) curses of being a vampire. If they can remove a tattoo they get bored of more easily than a mortal, this hinders that goal.

                                                  Still, as I said, I'm not sure if that's RAI, and I certainly don't think it's the only way to do it. It's just how I'd do it, with my reasoning as to why.
