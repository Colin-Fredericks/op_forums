    Help With Simplified Templates for Players

    06-14-2016, 09:56 PM
    Hello!

    We tried to play with simplified templates in a mixed party, based in some NPC ideas in the Hunter's handbook (like vampires in NightStalkers, werewolves in SpiritSlayers and Witches in Witchfinders) but it was a DISASTER.

    The reason for that is even you adjust the XP cost tô acquire each new trait, it is not balanced for players. For example, blood potency and primal urge in the Requiem and Forsaken CoreBook, respect the same schedule about energy consumption/turn and Max dot/dot of power trait.
    But in the Hunter's hamdbook, this doesn't happens. The Vampire with Blood Potency 5(that now is a Merit) can increase attributes to 10, but werewolves with Primal, doesn't. And what the cost of this Blood Potency, if with 5 dots only you can increase traits to 10??

    But looking to other side, i don't think that have sense read all 350 pages from vampires and/or 350 pages from werewolves and/or 400 pages from mages (and the others templates) just to make ONE character. I guess these CoreBooks about templates is good to play a entire campaign-based on that creatures, but not in a mixed one.

    What is your experience in a scenario with players with mixed templates? How do you deal with it?

                                      06-16-2016, 08:42 AM
                                      Originally posted by winchester View Post
                                      We tried to play with simplified templates in a mixed party, based in some NPC ideas in the Hunter's handbook (like vampires in NightStalkers, werewolves in SpiritSlayers and Witches in Witchfinders) but it was a DISASTER.
                                      Hah. I was actually thinking about doing this for a Suicide Squad inspired game. Taskforce VALKYRIE captures some monsters and puts cranial bombs in them, or threatens to destroy their phylacteries or whatever, then it's all "Assemble Taskforce X"

                                      If you want to have your players be supernaturals but still only use Hunter rules, you'll have to do a lot more hands on stuff than usual. Here are my suggestions (I assume you have Mortal Remains):
                                      • Treat Potency as an Attribute. So you buy it for 4 Experiences (or you can go 5, if you really want). Refluff Potency as desired. A Vampire has Blood Potency, a Werewolf has Primal Urge. A robot might have Engine. A Mummy might have Sekhem, but you're not playing Mummy: The Curse, you're playing Hunter, so maybe it's got something else.
                                      • Instead of using Willpower, give everyone an Energy stat. Vitae (or Blood) for Vampires, Essence (or Rage) for Werewolves, Battery for Robots, Balance for Mummies, etcetera. Have them come up with their own term.
                                      • Have everyone come up with some way to regain their Energy. A vampire might have to drink blood and gets 1 vitae per turn he deals 1L of damage. A werewolf might regain his Rage by running wild and hunting, or even eating flesh. A Robot's battery might require meditating under the sun or charging at a wall outlet (or punching a circuit breaker and regaining Battery based on how much damage is done to him). A Mummy might meditate on her canopic jars.
                                      • Work to come up with a template for everyone. A vampire might not bleed, might take damage from sunlight, might downgrade all attacks due to having dead flesh, and might have the Drain Dread Power to represent how they feed. A Werewolf might have fast healing and the ability to transform by spending a point of Rage and rearrange their Attributes and get a Dread Attack. A Robot might have 2/2 armour and not need to breath or bleed or eat, but has to spend a point of Battery to stay active each day and has to be repaired because they can't heal. A Mummy might also be dead like a vampire. I don't know, Mummies are dumb.
                                      • Worth it to also think of Banes or Bans they might have. Maybe your Vampire has a compulsion to count grains, or your Werewolf has hairy palms and a unibrow. Whatever your players think might be neat. They could even take it as a Persistent Condition acting as a Flaw. "Photosensitive: You take a -3 penalty to all actions and need to spend one Blood every scene when in the sunlight; Beat: Your dislike of the sun causes you problems" or so on.
                                      • During actual play and as they spend Experiences, don't just let them take anything. Work with them to decide how they "level up", and what Dread Powers they can take, and how those Dread Powers might work narratively and mechanically. Have them be bought at 2 Experiences per dot, or flat out 3 or 4 Experiences, if you feel treating them like Merits is too powerful.
                                      • Feel free to completely ignore what the Power says. Some of the ones in Hunter and Mortal Remains are ranked, but the ranks don't do anything. Balefire could cost less and only do 2B damage. It could also be fluffed as, for instance, a robot's electrical field. Agonize could just be a flat Tilt that lasts as long as the monster does nothing else. Same for Confusion. Damnation, Dement, most of them, honestly. Just add Potency to the dice rolls. Maybe don't bother rolling Revivify. For Absorb Energy, maybe don't bother with a dot rating, and don't roll it. Actually, there are just a lot of Dread Powers where it's not really thematically appropriate to roll them, but then again it's good to have the roll and not need it than to wonder what you should roll and not have it. Keep the same method in mind when using the ones from the CofD core. You're the one making these dumb monsters your friends are playing, or at least, you're helping. You don't have to limit yourself to the book.
                                      • Do you know some of the cool powers that "Real" Vampires/Werewolves/Faeries/whatever get? Well, feel free to copy them. Does your Robot want to be really strong? Unholy Attribute works, but maybe Vigor or Resilience is exactly what you're looking for.
                                      The gist of it is that when you use Hunter only, you're going to have to do a lot of work. But honestly, I think it can be pretty rewarding. You're making your own monsters. Sure, the WorldChronicles of Darkness covers one type of Vampire and Werewolf and Mages and Changelings and Prometheans and so on. But you get to make up your own. You get to decide what Vampires are like. Maybe you think that it would be interesting to have a Changeling that's the faerie baby left behind when the faerie steals a human. Maybe you want to play a monster story about being a robot, so you take Biomechanical Nightmare and Ghost in the Machine. Maybe you've got the Mixed Blessings or Miracle Dread Power that you can only use three times on someone else's behalf, and your monster is a Djinni.

                                      I've honestly thought a lot about this, and if I weren't setting up to run Mage 2e, I might pull the Suicide Squad idea off the back burner. I may push myself and run two games...
