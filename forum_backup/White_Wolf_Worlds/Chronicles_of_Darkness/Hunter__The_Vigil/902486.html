    Challenging Human Supremacy in HtV Players

    05-29-2016, 12:45 AM
    Whether the setting is fantasy or science fiction, if it involves humans meeting with non-humans, there is a sub-genre which, at least in the eyes of its fans, is dedicated to emphasizing the superiority and unquenchable moral rightness of humanity. On /tg/ boards, this motif is called "Humanity, Fuck Yeah!", but to avoid getting banned by the mods for excessive swearing, I've gone for the hopefully less incendiary stand in of "human supremacy".

    Now, human supremacists interested in the Chronicles of Darkness setting just love Hunter: The Vigil, seeing it as basically a celebration of their ideology. This is often called into contrast with Hunter: The Reckoning, which, as part of the World of Darkness, essentially went with the opposite viewpoint of "humanity is weak" - take in note that Reckoning!Hunters were only made capable of going toe-to-toe with the creatures of the night because they were empowered by mystical patrons of good, whilst Vigil!Hunters are ordinary humans (for the most part) taking on the creatures of the night with grit, guts and guns.

    Now, I'll be the first to admit that the Chronicles do explicitly have a more positive depiction of humanity than the WoD did. However, it's not taken to the extent that "being human is being perfect; being nonhuman is an abomination". The gamelines that do explicitly have some strong human-positive or pro-humanity viewpoints are given valid reasons for doing so - Promethean, for example, isn't so much "humanity is perfection" as it is "humans are flawed, but still better than being an outcast with no place in creation", whilst vampires try to cling to their humanity since they're basically el cheapo knockoffs of werewolves, with predatory natures clumsily grafted to minds incapable of really handling them and given Kuruth with faulty safety valves.

    Even in Hunter itself, care is taken to avoid being too pro-humanity. Though others have complained on these very forums about, say, Les Mysteres being puppets of the spirits or the Keepers of the Source being misguided about the way that Essence works, I think groups like them have a very real place in the rulebooks. Even if their specific execution can leave things to be desired in my eyes, they still emphasize that being a hunter does not ensure you are in the right. Hunters will get things wrong, they will get blindsided, they will get subverted, swayed, duped or corrupted. In other words, hunters are not infallible.

    And yet, somehow, players seem to keep coming to Hunter from that human supremacist viewpoint. Why it is that this is so, I really don't know. Does the specific "you are humans who dare to bump back against the things going bump in the night" seem to just overpower the statements of the Chronicles being a "horrifying world of dark mystery"? Did I miss some memo that the Chronicles' general rule of moral ambiguity and "Black & Grey Morality" was suspended in Hunter games? I don't really know, and I don't really care.

    I confess a certain intense distaste for the human supremacy theme in general. But, in particular, it bugs me to sit down to a game of Hunter expecting an action horror game in the world that the rest of the Chronicles have sold me on, and then the game turns into a bog-standard "black & white morality slashfest with minor horror trappings", ala the stereotype of D&D horror games.

    So, I had a burning desire to ask: has anyone else ever noticed this tendency in their Hunter games? If so, how have you challenged it? How have you addressed the topic of conveying to players that "no, being human fighting monsters does not make you an action hero with Omniscient Morality; it makes you at best a Bryonic Hero and at worse a kool-aid drinking member of the KKK"?

      Hunter to me is a game about two things: being horrifically outgunned by awful monsters, and the massive personal cost of upholding the Vigil instead of keeping your head down like a smart/sane person would. I feel like any Storyteller that isn't stressing those points is missing something vital to the game, but maybe I'm crazy.

      I think part of the reason Hunter has that unfortunate reputation you describe is due to its role as many non-horror gamers introduction to the CofD; many folks jump straight from D&D to Hunter, and see it as more "more murderhoboing, but in the modern day!" rather than "Delta Green versus the Universal movie monsters." It basically boils down to narrative immaturity, at least in my eyes.

              Why challenge it? If everyone is having fun what's the problem?

              The Hunter setting doesn't demand moral greyness. The MM killing witches just for being witches, pretty dark. Killing vampires just for being vampires, pretty grey. The Union defending their community - pretty heroic*. If you want to play a humanity fuck yeah game, play a more heroic group and stick to lower powered antagonists like Thamaturges or Ghouls.

              And heck, if a group wants to kill Werewolves left right and center then who cares. If they're having fun they're having fun.


              Now if you're not having fun and your group is, just talk to them. Maybe you can reach a compromise, maybe you're just in the wrong group. But there's no one weird trick for this. You have to just talk to the group.

              * Howard Wood Ingham has gone on record on RPG.net saying that he wrote The Union as heroes. Later authors added some darkness like xenophobia, but corebook Union were supposed to be heroic.

But that combination of anti-thematic and setting-required says to me "put it in the antagonist chapter".

                  I have personally taken the stance that even at the most horribly flawed they can be, organizations in Hunter are still doing a just and noble thing. At lower rungs in a conspiracy, you can argue that the hunters are protecting innocent people with the caveat of having a bureaucracy to deal with.

                  Speaking as someone who honestly prefers hunters in Humanity Fuck Yeah stories, I don't see how any hunter gains some kind of unquestioned moral supremacy. Instead, it always feels more like hunters convincing themselves (For the more heroic groups) that what they do is the necessary evil. As mentioned by Kings, there are some groups that are unambiguously heroic. That doesn't mean they shouldn't do what's necessary (Which if I remember correctly is one of the bigger themes in Princess).

                  As for the Antagonist organizations, there are really two ways about it (For me at least). The first is that they are fooling themselves, like saying the Abe is really just desperately trying to find a way to give some sense of morality when they clearly are already way down the slippery slope as an organization. The second, and again this is just me, is to rewrite the organization. CofD is a toolkit after all. If I want Les Mysteries to be in actual confederation with spirits rather than being dupes, I can.

                  Finally, this all seems to be more of a personal preference question. Like I said, I prefer HFY. I like having stories where Union members break a witch's kneecaps for summoning demons, stories where the Malleus and TFV force themselves to work together in order to destroy the apocalypse cult summoning Cthulu's second cousin. And to be honest, HFY seems way less incendiary than the unfortunate implications of using "human supremacy" in its place when discussing the concept. It seems more like the difference between HFY and Van Helsing Hate Crimes, which is already covered anyway.

                                  Is excessive swearing a problem? How the fuck did I make it this long, then?

                                  EDIT Anyways, the problem.

                                  1) Don't let the hunters only see black and white. THe monster's they hunt are people too, and while the worst of the bunch are more likely to be visible, keep in mind the behavior of players characters and use that to inform your antagonists and misdirections.

                                  2) Hunters get used by monsters as much as not, and there's a variety of ways to do that. Find subtle ways to show they're in the game the monsters play as pieces rather than as players. It should still be to their advantage of course, but it should also demonstrate a sense of smallness.

                                  3) Never be afraid to scare the living shit out of them and to design scenarios where the only answer is to run. While it is a fallacy, people do conflate might with right, and giving players a few good no-win scenarios will give them reason to doubt, reason to fear. It also tends to be very memorable scenes for players, because it's high-tension, player-praying-over-their-dice action that gets the blood going. It also makes places where they win also more poignant by comparison.

                                  4) Keep other hunters in competition. Play up territorial behaviors, arrogance, greed, distrust, aggression, paranoia-run the whole gamut. Never let your PC hunters be the only ones in the picture, and always find a way to use them to make the PC's question themselves by comparison to their peers. Hunters should fight hunters almost as much as monsters.

                                  5) Also keep pushing the buttons of the internal dynamics of the group. While you might not want to push it so far as to be lead to permanent divisions unless you have a group that enjoys that, PC's flaws stand out the most and affect the presence of human flaws more than any other characters.

                                  Just some thoughts.

                                          As mentioned, I would say the issue is less with the players having a H,FY! attitude than them being disruptive when that view isn't catered to.                                               Originally posted by VoidDrifter View Post
                                              Did I miss some memo that the Chronicles' general rule of moral ambiguity and "Black & Grey Morality" was suspended in Hunter games?
                                              Nice! I love answering rhetorical questions where the person doesn't care to hear an answer, sarcasm aside and very sincerely, thank you for the opportunity.

                                              While it is possible to play the 'human supremacy' motif in the Hunter: The Vigil you'd have to completely ignore the themes covering descent into becoming Slashers and the numerous serial killer/Vigil!Hunter comparisons throughout the all books talking about monster hunters (emotes eyeing the Antagonists Core Hunter: The Vigil and Slashers books), or at least suffer some moderate mental dissonance.The whole Slasher phenomenon refutes moral blank and white 'Human Supremacy' as a theme intended for the game line.

                                              So you missed no memo, and 'Human Supremacy' is not a theme inherent in Hunter the Vigil, but rather something grafted on by those that favor that mindset in the first place. While there is room for Hunter's thinking of themselves as "heroes" initially, depending on compact, it should become clear at some point in the chronicle in the larger world that merely being human doesn't make you less monstrous than a supernatural creature, or even that every supernatural creature is even a monster. Cheiron Group and Ashwood Abby strike me as two of the easiest examples of compacts that can be painted in such a light that they seem worse than at least some monsters. Even if the specific cell the players belong to can maintain moral high ground itself, the fact other humans are more obscene than supposed abominations should remove the trope of the overall superiority of humanity.

                                              Also about problem players, I recommend pointing out the above out of character, and then perhaps give them a list of themes you intend to cover in your game so they have the option to buy-in, or opt out. I find the best games happen with people working towards a goal shared than adversarial, and I f you need to shift the cast of your players to have the game you want, please know you are the one investing Storyteller time and effort and recruit a player base that meets the needs of what you want to run.

                                              That being said, friendships have value and just because people don't share exactly the same taste in fiction doesn't mean relationships have to be lost, only perhaps the activity and who invests what may need to change.

                                              Of course this all happens in a white-room, and in my games supernatural creatures police themselves for the same reason humanity policies itself, it is made of real people, not mere caricatures, and real people care even though they are flawed and selfish. "Monster Hunter" is not a concept reserved in anyway for humans, and because of that my settings tend to be self-regulating. You may find having supernatural creatures hunting monstrous members of the various splats takes a good bit of wind out the bag of the supposed moral high ground of mortal hunters vs supernatural ones.

                                                  Originally posted by VoidDrifter View Post
                                                  has anyone else ever noticed this tendency in their Hunter games? If so, how have you challenged it?
                                                  Well, back then some of my players really liked Supernatural, and came with a similar mindset: If you are a monster that do even the slightest bad, like feeding only the bare minimum fresh brain to your children for it to survive until it grows enough to be able to eat days old corpse brain like you do, you are dead meat.

                                                  I saw that as an opportunity to play that aspect, and show how monstrous they were becoming, the He Who Hunts Monsters trope. Of course, my players are very mature and open to interesting, dramatic moments, and it was pretty dramatic when they said "Wait, what the F are we doing? We are supernatural-racists!". From there their characters started realizing how the other hunters and hunter cells acted exactly like they, the players, acted when they started the game. They realized that they were blinded by pride, fear, racism or pure joy, and they were actually pretty unhinged (at the time there was no Beast the Primordial, but in the end hunters started to look like beast's Heroes). It was a very deep, meta-level realization.

                                                  Maybe stand-ins for ethnical issues, racism and armed militia aren't the default themes that HtV tries to focus, but I focused on that together with the issues of keeping a normal life together with the Vigil (something Supernatural almost never tries to do, except sometimes with the almost non-mary-sue character of Bobby), and they enjoyed it a lot.

                                                  I think that in the end you don't need to shut down this player perspective. If it isn't just a immature power fantasy then you may do lots of things to this trope: You can play it straight or not, subvert it, deconstruct it, lampshade it... As long as it enriches your experience, why not?
