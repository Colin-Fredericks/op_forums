                    Well, firstly, the Knights are from Hunter, and I feel like they fit many of the Hunter themes, like fighting against long odds and struggling (with mixed success) to fight monsters without becoming a monster yourself, quite well.

                            I won't deny that it's plausible that there's some connection; I guess I personally prefer to separate the owl as a symbol of wisdom from the "bird of ill omen" archetype. That said, since the Strix were directly inspired by classical mythology, I think it would be a mistake to present the nemeses of Roman times from their later incarnations. Rather, I get the sense that the idea of the Strix has evolved with each book they've appeared in, and I prefer the current presentation of them as something timeless and intrinsically tied to the Kindred over the idea that they're some kind of supernatural servants or weapons that somehow slipped the leash.

                            In any event, the beautiful thing about the nWoD is that you can make whatever you want out of the pieces presented in the books, and the Aves are mysterious enough that they could be made into almost anything. It might be worth noting, however, that as far as I can recall they're described (in Night Stalkers, I think?) as having access to some kind of ritual magic, which would technically make them a conspiracy rather than a compact. I think there was some suggestion that the Cainite Heresy later co-opted some of their magical secrets, but I could be mistaken.

                                From one side, one needs to be blind to ignore the similarity between the Aves Minerva and the Strixs- the original presentation of the strixs was beings of Roman origin (during that time they were called striges, after all- the Latin name for Owls. The Aves Minerva means the Birds of Minerva, which are owls, and they are antagonistic toward vampires and use some weird blood sorcery. Heck, the original writeup of them even has a VII Endowment!..

                                But that writeup also denies any kind of connection between the Owls and the Birds. The Aves even clain to hate the Owls for taking the form of their goddess. Plus, why the hell would the strixs give so much help for those crazy humans without taking their skin and dance with it? Shouldn't they be bored already? And how they track down every member of the Heresy and give them their weird Blood Magics? And what they drink tea with the crazy demon and pervert ghoul in Italy?...

                                In sort, there is no canon connection between the Cainte Heresy and the Aves Minerva to the strixs any more then there is a canon connection between the Knights of St George and the Abyss- but it can be implied or read this way. Again, this is not written explicitly, and heck maybe that pattern is just like seeing faces on the Moon- but you can use it. Nothing in the text approve or deny it, so you can take it or leave it.

                                                Yes, Banishers are from broken awakenings, and exist to destroy magic. They are essentially crazy mages.

                                                Knights of St George have that neat balance that hunter does well, in that they're wrong but not completely wrong. There's a lot of stuff in the World of Darkness and a lot of things are depicted as having the entire picture, even when it doesn't really fit. When hunters are depicted as having a part of the picture and working with what they've got, it makes sense. Especially when that point of view can't be dismissed by readily-accessible proof. The Knights are wrong in thinking that Mages are actively bringing destruction into the world, but they're not completely wrong because that's what paradox and the Scelesti do. In addition they have found a way to take advantage of power from within themselves. Good on them.

                                                Back on topic, I think the Loyalists of Thule are a bit out of touch. It always seemed to me like it was put in to have an excuse to have some sort of Nazi-related group and I have a hard time seeing how it would last more than two generations and exist in modern nights.

                                                Also the Cainite Heresy. They have some nice background that hooks them into other settings. They have interesting abilities and a focus and reason, ample fodder for recruits among those harmed by vampires. But they're a suicide cult, and they seem so aggressive that they don't really fit very well unless you're playing a very specific kind of game. They seem more fitting for a vampire antagonist than as a vampire hunter group. Especially since their brand of zealotry is something you could slap onto an individual character from almost any other conspiracy and compact and not have it be that out of the ordinary before having a whole legion of secret suicide killers.

                                                On the opposite side of things, Network Zero always seemed to go a little too far. Taking a video of something and putting it up on youtube is easy. Everyone who sees it may not be shocked into sudden belief in all things supernatural, but at least someone out there will believe it. In Vampire the Requiem you don't have the world-spanning Camarilla there pulling strings alongside the Technocracy to get things shut down, so it seems like their job would be relatively easy, especially if they just kept on the road. This puts a huge burden on the storyteller. 'Why isn't my video of a coterie of vampires fighting the trio of wizards we lured them into getting anyone to believe in vampires or wizards?' The idea of full-on revelation is a potentially world-shattering concept, and that's a bit much for a compact.

                                                            1) It's worth noting that while there may or may not be supernatural efforts towards keeping the secrecy, Valkyrie's goal is to conflict with the supernatural while maintaining their secrecy, with an entire branch devoted to covering shit up. Between that and groups tendency to prefer being quiet and usual human interests, you might not need much more.

                                                            2) It really is that last part that's hard to push. Even in a world where everyone sort of knows that there's something in the dark, most people just aren't willing to invest in it.

                                                            THe big thing NetZo has to deal with indifference and 'disbelief', followed by the response time of groups like Valkyrie.

                                                            3)And for making it clear, NetZo has to attain three things to make their dreams come true: credibility, collaborative numerical mass(Or basically a large number of regular material that corresponds with one another and strengthens the argument), and popularity(essentially, because I can't remember a better word for widespread throughout the world). That's, by itself, problematic enough, and having TFV and other parties move against them doesn't help, BUT!

                                                            4)NetZo has one big advantage, and it's huge-they're riding the cutting edge wave of the communication boom. Every new way that the anarchic communication community is grabbed and used, and no force in the world can keep up with the sort of people NetZo has taking advantage of this. THey don't need to keep stuff up for long, just long enough to catch attention.
