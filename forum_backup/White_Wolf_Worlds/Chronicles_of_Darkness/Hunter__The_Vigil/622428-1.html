        Hototogisu

        Opening

        The rise of the merchant classes has been mentioned before, and now we come to the merchants themselves. The Hototogisu aren't just merchants though, they were merchants with an occult bent for about two centuries dealing with folk remedies and such. (Presumably they still have them, since those sorts of charms and herbs are common to all hunters).

        As the merchants grew in success they began competing with monsters, and since monsters didn't play fair they began looking for an edge against them. It's always nice to see a group who's motive for the Vigil reflects their motive in civilian life.

        Their Endowment comes from a guy named Inoue who discovered a way to steal supernatural powers, and like a good merchant he quickly found a way to make a LOT of money from this trick.


        The Enemy

        The Hototogisu don't see monsters as enemies but obstacle. If the expected rewards outweigh the cost of removing an obstacle they hunt.

        If the costs outweigh the rewards they apparently hunt even harder and try to weaken the monster and force it to work for them. Maybe any monster that well defended must have become so well defended by having skills valuable to the Conspiracy? I'm not sure.

        No mention is made of hunting powers because they're useful. An immortal sitting in a cave minding his own business isn't an obstacle to business, but who doesn't want immortality?

        Hunters

        The first Hunter is a merchant who guards a stretch of highway because his business depends upon it – and seems to be charitably defending other treaders.

        We have a merchant who just sees trading as a job, and is in the Hototogisu because she wants supernatural power. Ah, after it's absence from The Enemy the concept shows up here.

        Another Hunter who wants power. Or rather, hunter #2 wants power, hunter #3 is after prestige. He's also not a merchant, but a disgraced samurai who works as a merchant's bodyguard.


        Factions

        Organisers are the leaders of the Hototogisu and their job is to provide sales leads and the location of monsters with desired powers to other members of the Hototogisu.

        Merchants are more interested in making money than the Vigil. I get the feeling that in most cases they're more likely to buy protection and supernatural abilities from the Conspiracy than actually hunt themselves; though clearly there are some exceptions like sample Hunter #1

        Diplomats are, well diplomats, between the Merchants and the monsters. Presumably they're also the ones most likely to actually steal somebody's power.

        All in all I get a very clear picture of how the Hototogisu work. The Merchants pay money and pay Diplomats to provide powers or solve problems. The Organisers are the middlemen making sure everything runs smoothly.

        It's nicely done, though I would quite like to see a forth faction “Security”, who're hired muscle for people who have a lot of money and no combat training Vigor is only useful if your punch connects with the other guy. Do Security get Setto? Well that depends on their employer.


        Status

        Like most Conspiracies the first dot unlocks the Endowment. I've often felt that the first dot is too useful for flavouring an organisation to do nothing in of itself. Though I don't have any strong opinions about what the Hototogisu should have in a first dot.

        The third dot gives four dots between Contacts, Resources and Retainers. Another high dot value, I also think that the Staff merit should be on this list.

        The fifth dot says something extremely important about the Conspiracy that until now I've just been assuming is true. They can and do trade powers. At five dots Inoue gives you a power of five dots or less.


        Stereotypes

        Compared to the Gimu these read like more typical WoD stereotypes full of snark and smugness. I approve.

        It seems The Agis Kai Doru are around, I thought they would be and am glad to see them. The Hototogisu arrogantly assuming they'd outlast the AKD was a nice touch.

        They don't want powers from the monsters the Ama-San hunt. Of course they don't, Leviathan Channels are a flat seven dots so they're immune to Setto (sorry, couldn't resist )

        More seriously. I'm actually surprised by this. From sirens to Lovecraft sea monsters have a strong association with mind-affecting abilities and obviously sea related powers. Stealing Lovecraft powers would probably shread your sanity but what merchant wouldn't want a siren voice to lure customers into his shop or powers to make sea voyages full of cargo faster and safer? I imagine the Hototogisu hiring the Ama-San to hunt them powers; and laughing about how they can underpay peasants who don't know the true value of their catch.


        Endowment

        As Endowments go this one is simple. Despite being for a Conspiracy Setto uses the single 1-5 Merit usually found in Compacts (and LostLight homebrew). Not that this is a problem, since a single merit fits perfectly. Mechanically it's very similar to Anthropophagy with the key difference that the need to con a monster results in a unique hunting style.

        And really, I'm padding, there's not much I can say about a single Merit, maybe if I had some criticisms but I don't. I like it just the way it is.


        Moving on from the Endowment itself to a broader picture. I've been thinking and discussing the question of. What is a second edition Endowment. What's Hunter's equivalent to Vampire's “Monsterous out of the gate”?

        To me the answer wasn't just “more powerful”. I don't think that Hunters should match or exceed supernaturals in raw power.

        Actually let me rephrase that. Hunter is about diversity, and matching/exceeding monsters in raw combat power should be the purview of one or two Conspiracies. Task Force: VALKARIE in attack helicopters or the Aegis Kai Doru breaking out “the good stuff” and going toe to toe with an enraged mummy.

        But for the rest of Hunter. I would say 2E Endowments turn monster's into prey. Perhaps the Malleus Maleificarum have a funeral rite that curses a killer with the inability to hide their sins. Performed over a vampire's victim he is cursed with bloodstains that spell out “vampire” that cannot be hidden. When the inquisitors corner the Vampire the Armour of St Martin is still weaker than Resiliance so they need tactics and teamwork to take down a bloodsucker, but what vampire wouldn't be scared knowing that at any time the inquisition could take his Masquerade away?


        So. Does Setto fit my vision for 2E Endowments? Absolutely!

        With Setto in play monsters must act like paranoid recluses never touching (I'd hoserule that handshakes are culturally required regardless of history) or being persuaded by a merchant, or only doing business through intermediaries. The idea that the mere presence of a Conspiracy makes monsters adjust their habits out of fear is exactly what I want Endowments to do in 2E.

                Otodo

                We come to our last group the Lucif... I mean Otodo. Technically I read them already, but why let that stop me?


                Opening

                The opening section retells how the Conspiracy was formed. The most useful thing here is a description of just who the Oni are. They are not the red and blue folklore ogres, but “spirits of plagues and devastation”.

                In the WoD that could be many things. They could of course be Spirits but despite the word “spirits” Infernal demons are another likely option given their connection to the Lucifuge.

                Red and blue ogre Oni are probably some sort (actually, several sorts) of faerie. Oni are a 1E changeling Kith. But they're not going to turn up in this reading.


                The Name

                We next get an explanation of what the word Otodo means. I don't have any particular thoughts here.


                The Lucifgue Sidebar

                Two things are stated:

                The Otodo come from the “same stock” as their Lucifuge cousins.

                What the Otodo and Lucifuge mean to the other's dogma is up to the individual group.

                That said. Saying the Oni are some sort of Infernal Demon (remember that Infernal is a very broad category) would fit both groups dogma quite nicely. That's a good thing for any groups who've run the Lucifuge's dogma as being accurate or played certain Castigation powers (e.g. the summoning/binding ones) as specifically working on demons. I always ignored the sidebar about Hunters not caring what a demon is; hunters who don't care bring holy water against a Fae with the classical demon look and end up dead.

                Anyway, I'm digressing. Back to topic. One thing that's not brought up in the Lucifuge connection is that the Lucifuge aren't just children of demons, they're children of the Devil himself or arch-demons like Mammon. In other words, they're royalty to the point I'd expect them to have Honorary Rank. If the Oni are the same that's never mentioned.


                The Enemy

                Way back with the Ama-San I mentioned that The Enemy sections typically fall under targets or tactics. This one is all about the targets. So who's called out as Oni-prey?

                First off we have anything evil. The Otodo are said to be paying off their ancestors karmatic debt. Battle with evil, any evil, is the path. Even human evil seems to be on the menu as the Otodo are said to battle “corruption”.

                The Oni themselves are targets of course, as are other Otodo-blooded who don't hunt as they do. With the Oni it's just like putting out a fire, you don't blame the fire, but with their cousins it's personal.

                Next up we have Changelings; and here the Otodo are in the wrong both factually and morally. The Otodo think that Changelings are descendants of something similar to Oni and treat them like their own cousins. It's said some Changelings are welcomed into the Conspiracy, it's also said some changeling’s humour them, but mostly it's bloodshed. I'm surprised the humouring isn't more common, Changelings are the finesse splat after all.

                We also have a sidebar about their signature weapons. Interesting information in here: Changelings take Aggravated damage from iron in 2E. Or maybe that's just a Hunter thing? I hope it's in Lost 2E, with 2E power boosts a bigger weakness is appropriate.

                Finally Beasts are given a paragraph to themselves. The Otodo hunt Beasts, pity Heroes, but think that the best solution to just kill the Beasts first. This definitely doesn't answer any questions asked by the Beast game; but the Otodo are not Beast focused so it shouldn't. There's plenty of time for that later in the Beast Hunters book that's coming out. Honestly, I'm not sure why Beasts were given a special section here but no harm done.


                Hunters

                The first hunter is basically a witcher. Raised to fight monsters from childhood, wondering the road killing monsters for coin. (The Otodo even carry a signature weapon like a Witcher's two swords)

                The second Hunter is actually just a backstory. It talks about the hunter's childhood but nothing after starting the Vigil or to connect said childhood to the Vigil except the obvious oni-blood.

                The third is basically the Lucifuge's Truth faction. Someone trying to peace together the past of the Conspiracy.


                The Lonesome Road

                The Otodo's heritage is hard to hide and given how WoD Mortals react to normal supernatural powers they're unsurprisingly hostile to the Otodo's probably demonic powers. So they travel a lot.

                Another witcher like trait comes in – the Otodo bloodline is thinning and to slow it's descent they have lots of sex. Geralt of Rivera ancient Japan style.

                This section is more important than it appears on first glance because it highlights another very important distinction from the Lucifuge. Two actually. Firstly the Lcuifuge's bloodlines typically don't ever thin, presumably the Devil's seed grows deeper roots than some random Oni's. Secondly it seems the Oni only sired mortal children once. Human-demon liaisons are adding new blood to the Lucifuge all the time. Presumably some combination of the Vigil's mortality rate, the occasional Catholic Inquisition (or similar), and the Lucifuge's own efforts are keeping demonic DNA from spreading like Gengis' Khans.

                Anyway back to the Otodo.

                In addition to having lots of sex the Otodo keep notes of their children and when two meet they trade notes. This system isn't that robust and there was some discussion about it when the Otodo were first spoilt. I never minded though – I'm ok with in character mistakes providing it isn't fundamental to the entire organisation. (Looking at you Keepers of the Source).

                Oooh. Now this is interesting. “An Otodo that tries to hide her heritage from her family sets herself up for heartbreak, and puts her family in danger”. Take that Harry Dresden and every other superhero or urban fantasy character who keeps secrets to protect people.

                (Honestly it's not keeping secrets that's the problem. It's that so many of those characters are terrible at judging when secrets are appropriate and when they're not. Also, if secrets are appropriate than marriage obviously isn't. Still it's a refreshing break from Urban Fantasy cliché).

                So Otodo usually work alone or with other Hunters; and their non-powered family are support staff. I'm not to keen on mixed org games (I treat it as the same as crossover, which I'm not too keen on either) but Otodo + Tier 1 is ok in my book.

                It also seems like Otodo have lots of clauses in their Code.


                Status

                All that stuff I've been saying about Lucifuge descending from royalty of hell and Otodo descending from infernal commoners? It seems like it's cannon or at least has mechanical evidence. Compare the lifespans to the Lucifuge; much much shorter.

                In practice that won't matter much. How many games last long enough for even a mortal's age to change significantly.

                Status one: Unlocks the Endowment and grants unseen sense Oni. Yay.

                Status three: Resources and Contacts with other Otodo Hunters. An odd choice for such a distributed and small group. What happens when a player wishes to roll contacts – they wonder the roads for a month ask ask Otodo they bump into? Maybe a network of supporters and sympathisers, un-powered cousins and people the compact helped, would provide a more logical source of Contacts and money?

                Status five: universal unseen senses. I actually really like the thematics of the progression here. They start of with demon blood and thus demon related abilities but as they master it they claim/subvert a demon's true place as the lord of all that is evil and thus their demon powers can be used in more general ways. (This comment makes more sense if I point out I'd personally play Oni as a form of Infernal demon)


                Stereotypes

                Not much to say here. They're nicer than the Hototogisu but not as nice as the Gimu. Apparently the Inquisition isn't trying to kill them.

                A Conspicuous Lack of a Faction section

                Once again we have no factions and I feel their lack far more than the Gimu. When I read the Lucifuge core I read the three groups as a way for players to define how they felt about being the spawn of satan:

                The Denial: “I'm not a monster! I'm not!”
                The Reconciliation: “I like my heritage because <insert reason here>”
                The Truth: “I don't know what any of this means, but I want to.”

                Later on the groups got more rigid and I never liked that. The idea that the Reconciliation's “I like my heritage” was tied to “Kill em all and let god sort them out” never sat right to me.

                But the point for the Otodo is that I think subgroups based on what you think about your heritage, even if the Otodo don't realise they exist in character, would be a very good thing.


                Endowment

                In general

                It seems that the Otodo can replace gifts through meditation like the Lucifuge. Shame, I was hoping that would go away in 2E – as the Conspiracies are all about genetic legacies it seemed logical to me that your selection of Castigation / Seitokuken powers are the ones you're born with or with the potential for, not the ones you choose.

                Notably Integrity is often a good thing for Seitokuken. This reverses the way low Morality boosted Castigation – I don't have a problem thematically, Integrity is tied to the soul, so are demons in Christian theology so if that crosses over from the Lucifuge then we're golden. I do miss the low morality angle though and hope with more word-count the 2E hunter core will bring something like it back for the Lucifuge.

                As I read the individual powers I'll put a little mark next to each of them:
                2E – this power will make monsters change their habits by it's very existance.
                NA – this power does not directly target a specific monster (see my comments about the Armour of St. Martin under Setto and you'll see what I mean)
                1E – I think this power needs a boost to fit 2E levels.

                Hannya (NA)

                This power lets you see Ephemeral Beings as though they were viable and supernatural creatures for what they are.

                The roll is Integrity + Seitokuken vs Dread Power + Supernatural Tolorence. That's a very powerful dicepool in favour of the hunter. Even late game you should be pushing 8-10 dice and with high Integrity you can beet theoretical maximum dicepool for a Hunter rules monster. In the context of finding prey I approve of hunters having this kind of raw power.

                Special mechanics like Spoofing and hopefully something similar for the 2E Mask can redress the balance for monsters who's themes deserve a better chance of staying hidden.

                There's also a yes/no metagame about the subject's aura. I like the idea, but I don't like the fact this unreliability makes Hannya weaker than the generic Aura Reading merit in GMC. I'm presuming the list of acceptable questions is the same; a see page XX would have made that clearer.


                Jakaku (2E, with Caviats)

                This seemingly simple ability actually has a lot of surprising mechanical implications to it.

                To start with here's my interpretation of an ambiguous mechanical point. It says any demon or spirit the Otodo “can see or knows by name” and later on it says “the two characters must be able to perceive each other”. From context I think the later rule refers exclusively to humans with the Thrall condition. (I suspect a lot of people are thinking I'm being pedantic to call this ambiguous. I'm a computer programmer with Aspberger's. I am pedantic.)


                To start with the good. It's wraps up three Castigation rituals (Calling Forth the Pit, Shackles of Pandemonium, Mandate of Hell) into one power. About damn time; for such a core power to the Lucifuge's theme being a demon master shouldn't take three of your five power slots.

                It also combines awesomely with the Ephemeral Being rules. You can just say “Lilith come over here and eat that monster!” But chances are most if not all demons and spirits will need a Condition before they can teleport over; and that means you use the Summoning rules from GMC core.

                Making the power combine with the GMC rules like this is more powerful to making it a ritual like the 1E Castigation rules in a situation where I think hunters should be powerful. However it also has room for the coolness of performing occult research and summoning.

                The mere existence of this power also means that demons and spirits will be very careful with their names – so the Otodo's demon affecting ritual is a 2E power. As it should be


                Now those caveats.

                The dicepool is Integrity vs Resolve + Supernatural Tolorence, which I think will mean the Otodo will be at a significant dice-pool disadvantage. If you use the Ephemerial Being rules (which you probably will as they're corebook) for rank 1-3 Spirits/demons you're looking at about 4-12 dice too resist vs 3-8 dice to attack usually.

                The second one is that Aspiration rule means you can't use it to banish a demon back to hell or use it to foil it's evil plot. If that rule was meant exclusively for human Thralls then it would be fine, but as written you can't banish demons with Jakaku.

                All in all; great power and much improved over the 1E but held back by a far too week dicepool. Fix that and Jakaku would be an excellent way to support summoner characters.

                I'd also quite like to see the dicepool being extra strong against Oni/demons, an explicit mention of the corebook summoning/exorcism rules and suggested situational modifiers. But the only thing I think the power actually needs is a revised dice pool.


                Kaibutsu (NA)

                You turn into a red/blue ogre (so that's where the folklore came from. The Otodo not the actual Oni; I still think the faeries are involved )

                I didn't go through with a fine toothed comb but this looks identical to Mark of the Beast but without the time limits or the after effects. Since Mark of the Beast is a huge gamble, if you can't win the entire hunt in a few turns you're at a huge disadvantage, I think that's probably an improvement.

                I could see characters who's response to everything is Kaibutsu, but why not. The storyteller could always throw a few curve balls like intangible foes or curses to cure rather than monsters to bash.


                Kenshi (2E)

                The duration is currently listed as a week. Will it return to indefinite in the final version? I hope so.

                It's a neat power, very powerful, but against the Otodo's primary foe I'm ok with something this powerful. Ephemeral Entities are so dangerous that they basically need it and gifting amulets to people is a nice way to represent the Otodo's more caring side.

                Since the monsters don't get a roll to resist I'm assuming the power resides in the amulet's not as a curse upon the monster. This is how it should be IMO; I wouldn't let the Otodo run around making a ban like “hurting humans” on everything.

                I also like the way it synergies with Jakaku. You could use Kenshi to keep entities imprisoned until you want them to do something.

                I don't think this power would change monster's behaviour just by existing but it's powerful enough and synergies with Jakaku so I definitely think 2E.


                Kigo (N/A)

                The Mark of the Lucifgue, but without the cool mysteries surrounding it.

                Honestly I can't think of many practical uses for this, just like the Mark. The blind fighting is too specific, and if you can inflict the mark you probably don't need to track the monster.

                You usually have a dud power or two in a long list so I don't mind – and maybe someone's going to point out why I'm wrong in the comments – but my suggestion would be that if the Otodo find a victim (alive or a corpse) they can perform this ritual to curse the perpetrator.

                Otodo then get the tracking bonus or the ability to see someone with a glowing “murderer” kanji on their chest. And even if people can't see the brand they still subconsciously go “something's telling me this guy's a murderer”.


                Shinigami Buki (1E)

                You can't have Oni without an eating power. This one is useful, but not particularly powerful. To use it you already need to have a target restrained – which means you've already won.

                Sadly, it requires touching, not actually biting. I would like it to require actually locking jaws around the target, not enough to break skin but enough so the Otodo's spiritual teeth can sink into the spiritual monster.

                Now being able to eat a possessing entity makes for easy exorcisms and safe victims – so it is useful. The ability to create thralls you can then control with Jakaku is also a useful ability if hideously unehtical.

                So I'm putting this down at 1E but I'm not sure that's a problem. It's doesn't “turn monsters into prey” but as a utility power; I'm not sure it needs too.


                A 2E version of this power would be something like removing 1 random Numina from an Ephemerial Being per lethal damage as an in-grapple bite attack.


                Shonetsu Jigoku (N/A)

                Hellfire attack. Got to have a Hellfire attack.

                It's definitely a useful power, especially in the Edo period where weapons aren't as advanced and obviously very good against Vampires and Promethians.

                I'd have liked the damage rating to be one higher. You're paying experience and using up a limited power slot for this. I'm not too surprised that the Aggravated damage option is gone since 2E makes Aggravated rarer, and the multiple shots per Willpower is a much needed boost.



                Closing Thoughts

                What can I say? They're mostly the Lucifuge and I like the Lucifuge.

                As the first full 2E Endowment we've seen this gives me a lot of faith for the future direction. Mechanical niggles like needing three powers for a summoner character at Hellfire costing 1 Willpower per shot are gone. The Otodo are absolute terrors against their primary foes (assuming Oni are either spirits or demons) which is as they should be.

                    Final Thoughts

                    This was a good chapter, really really good.

                    Every organisation was good. Togeather they provided a nice breadth of different groups that hit all the broad points necessary to have vigil in Edo Japan but also managed to explore new territory that Hunter hasn't covered before (the Hototogisu especially, with their con-artist ways).


                    I've made a lot of comments as I went along but I'd say that there's only two things that, in my opinion, actually need to be fixed. That's an impressively low number.

                    The first is Jakaku needs a more generous dice pool [edit] And the Aspiration defence needs to be limited to humans[/edit]. It's far to low to go against the high stats given to Ephemeral Beings. If the assumption was to run Spirits using Hunter rules that should be made explicit IMO – as Spirits are in the core book I think a lot of groups will run them using the core book rules.

                    The second is more details on the Bijin; put it this way. I have no idea what resources the Compact has that allowed the third sample hunter, to lay down the law on the local monsters. I think I'd have trouble playing one without that information.


                    Of the changes I don't think are necessary the ones I'd like to see the most are:

                    Jakaku having a quick mention of the GMC ephemeral beings rules. Just something like “While many demons and spirits can appear instantly when summoned they require Conditions to do so. See the GMC core pXX”

                    Not being able to change Seitokuken gifts after you choose them. It makes them feel less genetic.

                    Seitokuken giving bonus dice to GMC Summoning/etc for Oni (maybe half dots; with Castigation giving +dots since I think I was right about the nobility/commoner split)

                    Better Factions for the Ama-San. The current ones seemed a bit too narrow and specific. I would quite like it if hunting monsters was one sub-faction and dealing with the outside world was another. The third could be village matrons maybe. Or perhaps something completely different, but broader groups would be nice.

                    A different stereotype for the Ascending Ones. Since the AO are based on the myths about the Ḥashshāshīn I'd like to see their Japanese branch be based no the myths about ninjas. The Japanese logically should be full of Japanese hunters though; they've been in the area long enough and are amazingly adaptable.

                    Some mention of the Inferno's heavenly opposite: the Empyrean.


                    And to end on a positive note. My favourite things:

                    The Endowments. Dear god the Endowments, they were everything I could hope for. Scary enough that monsters would change their habits because of their very existence – thus making Hunters into hunters and monsters into prey. Seitokuken fixes mechanical niggles I've had with Castigation for a long time and Jakaku combined with GMC mortal interaction rules is just a very streamlined way to handle the demon summoner archetype in the Otodo/Lucifuge.

                    The Ama-San. I really like the angle of a whole community where everyone's a Hunter. It feels extremely different to a normal vigil. No keeping secrets from those closest to you, more flexibility in regards balancing your commitments, and the compact's highly culture supports those differences. Plus they make good foils to Leviathans, I make no apologies for my biases.

                    The whole con-artist angle to Setto. It's a very different angle to the usual strategies hunters use. On a related point, the Hototogisu being a group who's primary mission is completely mundane (money) is nice.
