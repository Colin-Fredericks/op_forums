    Where I Read: Hunters of the Edo Jidai

    08-10-2015, 02:02 PM
    In this thread I will read through the Hunters of the Edo Jidai which can be found in this preview

    Ama San

    Opening

    In the start we are quickly introduced to perl divers and the sea whistle. There is nothing specifically Hunter in this opening, which makes sense.

    The average WoD reader can be assumed to know what a Trade Union is but not about Japanease perl diving (I did, but not about the sea whistle). This opening quickly introduces the real world concepts behind the Hunter Compact.

    Also. It's about damn time we got something on underwater monsters so I'm excited to read on.

    The Enemy

    I've found that "The Enemy" sections usually go one of two ways. Either they talk about the hunter's tactics, or about who the hunter's target.

    This section is mostly a who they target, but with a bit of tactics there too. Suprisingly we're told that they they have a revernce of sorts for the sea's eccological balance (I presume eccological balance is not unique to the sea, rather it's a unique trait of the Ama San too care about it).

    So who do they target? Sea monsters who grow too big and disrupt this ecological balance. I say suprising because they give me a bit of a Unionish feel (Hunters organised around a working class profession) and it's not like the Union to care about why a monster is killing thier own.

    Next we get to tactics, which are mostly "lure them to the surface, then kill them". Don't let the fact these monsters are described as causing tusnami's and earthquakes break your disbelief. Look up Isonade on Wikipedia, quite a small thing for having so much power.

    Hunters

    We have three hunters here:

    A sixty year old aproaching retirement but hoping to die in the hunt or be dragged of by a monster lover.
    A young girl who's got a psychic link to a monster, that the compact is promising to kill and free her from.
    A token boy.

    While I've often thought the "Hunters" section is not the best use of precious precious wordcount, these three show the section at it's best - giving not only character ideas but non-obvious ideas and some much needed hints of what sea monsters they could expect to find (the obligatory lovecraftian monster makes its appearance – are ningyo that lovecraftian in folklore?). The token boy in particular manages to provide useful information on the Compact as a whole (how they treat male members) without lessening the character’s usefulness as a character. I preferred this method to the sidebar used by the Maiden's Blood Sisterhood.

    Subgroups

    As always we have three:

    Deep Divers - scouts and bait
    Callers - use the sea whistle to make monsters surface (after they've lured into hearing range by Deep Divers)
    Okaa-San - Perform the actual killing, and also sell the monsters they catch.

    There's a lot more about their tactics embedded into these groups. Most importantly, the sea whistle lures monsters ashore before they kill them. Actually, that's a little disapointing, I was hoping for awesome underwater fights (also, how many sea monsters are amphibious? It can't be many).

    So what I like about these three is they make it feel like an organisation. I'd estimate with this division of duties you'd need at least 9 people per hunt which plays nicely to the idea that Hunter's greatest advnatage is numbers.

    What I don't like is that these groups feel far too narrow. If you take it literally a Caller's only job during the hunt is wait, make one roll, then get out of there. I don't see any reason why a single hunter couldn't do all three roles in a single hunt. It would probably be more interesting for players and free up the subgroups to explore different directions you could take the core compact like they usually are. For example there's a bit about the Okaa-San selling their kills to occultists - that would make a great subgroup all by itself; a country bumpkin (I think Ami-San are that) going into town to sell their catch to sinister sorcerers or hire out the compact as mercenaries to sceptical samurai is practically an entire separate game to hunting sea monsters but it would be awesome. That kind of story defining choice is what I like to see when choosing my subgroups.


    Status

    Status O: Small Unit Tactics at an enhanced level.
    Status OOO: You can use the sea whistle to force monsters to surface
    Status OOOOO: retainers show up when you're in danger

    Now at this point we start to see the compact suffering from word count. The first dot of Status gives Small Unit Tactics. Now this is an interesting choice because I always imagined Hunters would be getting that Merit for free; the core powerset of the Hunter line is called Tactics after all. Anyway, I said "wordcount" - well if every hunter has Tactics, what makes the Ami-Sama better than other hunters? What do they have that the military training of VALKARIE does not provide?

    Well a perfectly sensible answer is provided. They grew up together and learned to be a team from childhood; but that answer only has one sentence dedicated to it. The token guy's section in Hunters also touches upon it – but only barely through inference.


    Status three I don't like too much. There's no link or justification given to link Status to the sea whistle. The Sea Whistle would be much better as an Endowment or a Tactic (perhaps an Endowment that usees the Tactic mechanics)

    Status five gives you six dots worth of Retainers; who apparently have the ability to teleport (they turn up within nine seconds if you're in danger). The retainers make sense given the close knit nature of the compact but the teleportation doesn't. I'd strongly suggest dropping that bit and just giving them Retainers; players can easily ask their retainers to stay close.

    I also notice that compared to to other hunters the Ama San get a LOT of benefits from their Status. Small Unit Tactics is 3 dots, but two dots is unusually high for Hunters' one dot benefit. Getting a retainer at 5 dots has been done before, but never so many dots of it.


    Closing thoughts

    This Compact is bursting with ideas, most of them good, to the point that there isn't enough room to give all these ideas room to breath.

    Case in point. The idea of a family oriented Compact has been done before. But Ama San do it better than Heratage House – despite having only one sentence about family. And I'm saying that seriously. Having more than just “family” helps a lot, and the things that aren't explicit but easily extrapolated – like growing up together creating advantages in Tactics & Small Unit Tactics provide a superior take on family business to Heritage House's cultish tendencies. (The Scarlet Watch don't count. They not family, they're descendants).

    So imagine what we could get if there was room to devote three or so paragraphs to growing up Ama San.

    I'd say the only things that really suffered were the subgroups being awkwardly welded to tactics since using the Subgroups this way means they can't be used to provide more diversity and splitting their hunt into three separate groups resulted in them having exactly one tactic for a hunt; what happens if a monster is immune to the sea whistle or can't get close to the shore (e.g. a Ban)?

    The sea whistle being awkwardly made into a Status Benefit is, well it's merely awkward and with more wordcount it could easily be moved elsewhere or expanded with fluff to connect it to Status. I imagine the bit about the balance of the sea will mostly be ignored since there's no material on it. With an explanation of what it is and why the Ami San care it could have been good, but it's absence does no harm. Same for the family aspects, groups that notice it should be able to fill in the gaps and groups that don't won't miss too much.


    One final thought is that – obviously – these women (and token guy) are going to become more useful and more playable the more other books expand upon cool aquatic monsters to fight.


    The inevitable section on Leviathan

    So how would the Ami San tie in to Leviathan? Thematically they'd actually do great. Practically, a Transformed Leviathan with 2E mechanics (once they're written) has nothing to fear from them.

    Thematically they're draw power (namely Small Unit Tactics) from being an insular and close knit community. That makes a nice foil to Leviathans who's insular and small knit communities are unhealthy cults.

    But as I said. Even a starting Leviathan (if they chose combat powers) would probably wipe the floor with dozens of Ami San and depending on how the sea whistle works – Leviathans are going to be all but immune to mental influence.


    Other aquatic beasties

    There's a fair few monsters that are aquatic; how do the Ami San thematically tie to them? Mostly I'd say they don't. When I think what's the core thematics of the Ami San; it's that they hunt underwater and – despite it being barely mentioned; their family and tight knit community.

    The only other underwater splat presented where family really matters are Changelings – but Ami San's take on family is unrelated to abduction or the quest for a new life.

    (Someone's going to mention Beast. I pre-emtively disagree that Beast actually has family themes; and will leave it at that).

                          Azusa Miko

                          Opening

                          We start with some history about the the changing status of Miko's in the early Edo period, the shoganate is imposing organisation upon religion and it seems the Miko will be made to conform. Curiously the first paragraph ends by saying that the Miko know that they will become pawns of monsters even more than pawns of mortal government. Are we to understand that monsters are pulling the strings of the shoganate? Perhaps this will be clearer when we have the rest of the Dark Era for context.

                          Next we’re told that the Azusa are only one kind of Miko and that it's possible there are other miko compacts and definitely some miko that are the enemy. At this point it doesn't say if they're the enemy because they're monster pawns/cultists or "Witches" - but presumably the former given the first paragraph.


                          The Enemy

                          More details are given about the shogunate’s "organisation". The temple miko now work with to spirits and gods, as typical for the WoD gods is undefined. Given my very basic knowledge of Shinto Gods are most likely just high Rank Spirits - though much like Inferno portrays demons as a template that can be applied on top of ghosts, spirits and basically most ephemeral beings I consider it's Empyrean counterpart to be the same and will treat the Shinto gods as Empyerian-Spirits.

                          Hmmm. I just noticed that the write up hasn't actually used the word kami at all.

                          Returning form that digression we learn that our compact, the Azusa Miko, focus on ancestor ghosts and there's no place for them in the shogunate’s temples. Apparently this imbalance left the spirit/god focused Miko vulnerable to possession and manipulation by spirits/gods but also by other things. It doesn't say why. Were the ancestor ghosts protecting the miko?

                          Fortunately this imbalance doesn't apply in the other direction. The ghost focused Azusa Miko are safe and being unaffected by the corruption they realise it exists and decide to hunt the monsters behind it. This duty is explicitly secondary to their standard miko duties.

                          Thus far there's a fair bit of resemblance to Les Mysteries - with a blend of priestly duties and hunting. Unlike Les Mysteries they aren't a bizarre pan-shamanistic faith (that's good) or manipulated by monsters (that's very good). While both the Azusa Miko and Les Mysteries have strong community links, the Azusa have no supernatural powers which drastically changes the nature of their relationship and gives the Compact a nice unique niche. In fact I'm more excited to play a character who focuses on community links and regular miko duties like appeasing ancestors and exorcisms than one who hunts monsters on the side.


                          Hunters

                          We have two:

                          A con artist who's surprised to discover monsters and her rituals are real and now uses her talents for good.
                          Another token man, who's also very good at using his bow as a weapon as well as a ritual implement.

                          These two are well written. The con artist is a good use of wordcount by showing that yes, the popular archetype of a travelling grafter can be used with these travelling priests and fortunetellers. The token man isn't bad but adds little beyond a single character concept.


                          Subgroups

                          Ah, these are proper subgroups. Picking one is a character defining choice, as it should be, and if you play an entire group in the same subgroup that's a game defining choice, also as it should be. Each group introduces a traditional miko role and how that applies to the vigil.

                          Sayaniwa - social experts who mingle and keep an eye out for signs of possession.

                          Reibai - occult experts who keep the ancestors appeased, and receive their help (usually knowledge) in return.

                          Geki - An entire subgroup of male miko. Their talent is physical combat.

                          Honestly, I wasn't too keen on the way the Geki are handled. I'm fine with the idea of men being in the group; but the compact is written as a female compact their sorbette is literally "Bow Shaman Women", and under hunters the man is written as though he was an exception. Now that's contradicted by an subgroup of men. The Geki also fail to fit the design of the subgroups, being defined by their gender and not their traditional role.

                          I think they this contradiction could use clearing up with men either fully integrated into the compact write up from the start or fully externalised into a closely allied compact depending on what the history makes more appropriate.


                          Status

                          Status 1: The Medium Merit for both Ghosts and Spirits
                          Status 3: An adaptable dot of Allies/Contacts/Resources/Retainer
                          Status 5: Authority over rank 1/2 spirits and ghosts; and social bonuses with higher rank ghosts/spirits (does this apply to "gods"?)

                          Once again we see the Status Merit providing the Endowment; though the Azusa Miko have a big advantage. They put it at the first dot and so there's no oddness about skill with the endowment only being available to high ranked members since, unlike the Ama-san, none of the other Azusa Miko's Status benefits had a thematic need to appear at the first dot.

                          The Status 3 benefit is strongly reminiscent of the Union's Endowment: Your Friends and Neighbours. Which I'm fine with, I kind of feel that Your Friends and Neighbours (along with the Long Night's The Prayer) should be generally available Hunter Merits with the Union/Long night having a more unique Endowment to their name - and the Azusa Miko certainly qualify for Friends and Neighbours. It does however continue the pattern that the Edo hunters are getting a LOT more benefit for their Status. I'm hoping this is some sort of "1.5" design to bring their Status close to 2E rather than imbalance. Is it MachineIV?

                          The Status 5 benefit, while very impressive, actually feels like a 3 dot Merit to me (or maybe six dots if you count Ghosts and Spirits separately) so that's exactly what the Mystery Cult Initiation guidelines state.


                          Closing Thoughts

                          The Azusa Miko have a delightfully unique niche in Hunter. They combine the strong community focus of groups like The Union and parts of the Long Night & Ascending Ones with genuine occult abilities; but without having enough magic or guilty secrets to distance themselves from the communities they serve. It's definitely a niche that should be filled, and perhaps a second group more suitable for the majority of WoD games (that is, modern day and anglophone nations) should be created.

                          The sour note was that the compact contradicted itself on the presence of men. I don't mind if the menfolk are fully integrated into the compact or split off into a closely allied group but right now they seem to be both at once.

                          In play I could easily see the Azusa Miko going one of two ways - one is to go heavily WoD where the spirits they interact with are the predators of Werewolf and, essentially, take their Azusa Miko's goals, take the standard WoD monsters, put them together and see what comes out. The second way is to try and bring more Shinto into the WoD with supernatural beings that act something like traditional Shinto folklore. Since the second one would benefit from supporting material I'm hoping something on Shinto appears elsewhere in the Dark Era.

                          But then, I'm always hoping the Empyrean will be expanded beyond it's one sidebar in Inferno.

                                      Bijin

                                      Opening

                                      Three words in and I'm already seeing them Edo Japan's Ashwood Abbey. For the curious search tvtropes for "The Beautiful Elite", the trope used to be called "The Beautiful People". Lets see if my first impression is accurate.


                                      So it seems the Bijin are artists, and also servants of the merchants since the merchants have the money. As a result Edo art is designed to have mass appeal as merchants like to sell stuff to the masses. This appears to have informed the Bijin's internal culture, at least I assume it does. The final part of the opening isn't too clear.

                                      The Bijin want "access" which apparently involves "Iki" which is an innate and non-academic sophistication or a "city-sexy-sophistication". I interpret that something like beautiful renaissance paintings compared to a pure white canvas. So my interpretation of this is that they want the common man to look up to them, not the exclusive elite. In short, they're ancient Japanese Hollywood stars and not whoever is making those infamous (and possibly mythical) four hour black and white films of clouds.

                                      I'm still not sure what "access" has to do with it. Access to the best parties? Access to the best art distribution networks. Access to the cultural Zeitgeist?


                                      The Enemy

                                      Remember that stuff about The Vigil as a virus? I'm strongly reminded of it here. The Bijin start of as a social club and when gossiping they start to notice monsters. It doesn't say why they didn't go into WoD-Mortal-denial so presumably the art circle just had a fair number of unusually open minded people at the time. In short their founding was basically a fluke.

                                      I'm ok with this.

                                      Seriously. I'm ok with this. The WoD monsters aren't really that great at hiding beyond the individual level - my go to example is the "what have you forgotten" question for GMC breaking points that outright states that in the WoD just about everyone has encountered the supernatural at least once. Most people ignore the truth, a few don't, and so by the law of large numbers some groups will have atypically high numbers of people who believe. Hunter groups forming by fluke are probably fairly common, at least as a proportion of hunter groups.

                                      Finally what do they do. They kill dangerous monsters but make use of useful monsters. Oh, looks like I called it about the Ashwood Abbey.


                                      Hunters

                                      Once again we have three examples. These ones I found to be a little basic for my tastes, but I'm getting ahead of myself. The examples are:

                                      A painter who realises he's painted an immortal after comparing is work to his master's. Now he paints targets the Bijin ask for.
                                      A dancing girl who killed a monster in self defence and now works as bait in exchange for the Bijin's protection
                                      The owner of a tea house who lays down the law all nearby monsters must follow.

                                      While I liked the idea that a Hunter lays down the law and monsters obey or worse (I've always imagined the Conspiracies as the biggest players in the WoD apart from gods, archmages, etc) apart from revising my estimate of their power level up significantly I don't feel like I learned anything new about the Compact from this section. Nor do I feel that the examples were things I couldn't have thought up myself.


                                      Duties

                                      Like the Miko before them, these are the kind of duties I like to see. Broad character defining things. Our three are

                                      Ninki: In a word socialites. Gathering info on a monster requires access to that monster, so the Ninki lure them out with parties. I also get the impression that the Bijin just like parties.

                                      Geisha: Scholars. They learn about monsters and record what they find hidden in art work.

                                      Aware: The actual hunters, who use traps and assassinations. Curiously they are "rarely called" because monsters of the Edo Period police their own. That seems surprising in a hunter game, why have monsters that do the players job for them? The assassination theme also fits nicely with their cultured image (at least by modern standards, not Samurai standards). An assassin, not a SWAT team, is whom the rich and cultured call for their dirty work.


                                      Status

                                      We're told that being Bijin isn't about being rich, it's about being in the right place at the right time.

                                      The first dot of status seems like it would be a lot of fun. One per session you can ask the storyteller what gossip you know about a topic and get three rumours, two mostly true and one false. The second dot refines the technique so you get to know that one of the two true rumours is true for certain.

                                      Finally the third dot is fame three.

                                      One thing that immediately jumps to mind is that this seems far more balanced compared to the previous Hunter groups. The rumour merit seems like great fun, and fits in with the GMC design of merits like Common Sense - I would actually like to see it as a merit available to all (The Union and Network Zero in particular would do well with it) that of course the Bijin get free dots in from Status.


                                      Final Thoughts

                                      There's a lot to like here. They have some similarities to cannon groups; their information network of occasionally dubious accuracy harkens back (harkens forward?) to Network Zero while their primary weapon being social status reminds me of the Barrett Commission - though the Bijin have a completely different kind of social power.

                                      I would have liked to see something a bit more concrete on just how powerful and influential they are. The opening section left me with the impression that they're basically working for merchants but later on we see the tea-house hunter who seems to have the local monsters in her grip. Knowing how and why she could pull that off would be useful.


                                      The Inevitable Comparison to the Wilde Society

                                      Those who have read Dream Catchers will know about the Wilde Society. Artists who expose themselves to faerie Glamour to gain superhuman artistic talents at the cost of their sanity (side effects include insight invaluable to the Vigil, but they're in it for the artistic talent).

                                      So how do they compare; well actually apart from being artists they have very little in common. Earlier I described the Bijin as having traits of Network Zero and part Barret Commission yet being distinct from both. The Wilde Society's closest cannon comparison would be the Illuminated Brotherhood. A vast difference.

                                              Gimu

                                              Opening

                                              And now we come to Samurai, and naturally enough they're motivated by a form of Bushido that puts duty to Japan higher than honour. The Compact formed when the unification made Samurai almost redundant. No more wars, no more need for warriors. This free time let them see monsters pulling the strings. Those who chose loyalty sided with their leader, manipulation or not. Those who chose duty became the Gimu.

                                              Being Samurai they have influence and resources, which is why they arrange executions and send their retainers off to commit arson in the dead of night, as often if not more often than fighting personally. The write up says "their katana", since the bow and the spear more common Samurai weapons I am really hoping there'll be a trenchcoat Easter egg because that would be hilarious.

                                              Since the compact's primary weapon is their status and influence, and since you can't hunters controlling your boss without going against your boss. Gimu caught hunting are shamed by society, and the rest of the Gimu must cut ties to avoid association. Most save up coin for post disgrace but most also become death seekers who go after monsters waiting to die in battle. Presumably it's not that much coin then.


                                              The Enemy

                                              The shortest enemy thus far at only one paragraph. They focus only on the puppetmasters behind noble houses which means they face the greatest threats, and they use the utmost discretion lest attention destroys the Compact.


                                              Hunters

                                              We have

                                              A Samurai desperate to die in battle, and who fights monsters because he's too good for human opponents.

                                              A is-there-a-word-for-female-samurai? who killed the werewolf that murdered her husband and was approached by the Compact. (A werewolf as a secret puppetmaster, don't see that every day)

                                              A Buddhist who considers demons an exception to the rules of pacifism and has gnostic like views - though I understand that Buddhists came up with that idea independently.


                                              Of the three the Buddhist is definitely my favourite, as it shows potential in the Gimu I would never have come up with myself. I wonder if we'll see a Buddhist legacy in Mage any time soon?


                                              Duties

                                              The Gimu have no subfactions because being Gimu is too different from the samurai path to be taught. If I'm honest I disagree with this choice. Plenty of hunter organisations have personal philosophies rather than organised groups for their factions and given that the Gimu are going against everything they've known, using motivations as the groups would work fine.

                                              For example: Duty to Nihon, seeing the greatest opponents, and personal beliefs about monsters would make a good set of factions.


                                              Status

                                              One dot: Two dots of resources for vigil use only. Makes sense, Samurai are nobility, they have money.

                                              Three dots: Four dots split between Allies, Contacts, Status, Fame. Another example of Edo hunters getting larger bonuses but straight forward.

                                              Five dots: Three loyal samurai, who once again, can teleport. Giving simple dots of Retainers would make a lot more sense here.


                                              Stereotypes

                                              For the first time in Edo we have hunters with Stereotypes

                                              Reading these stereotypes the first thing that struck me is how nice they are to everyone. WoD Stereotypes are usually highly hostile to everyone. I won't go through all of them but I will mention the Ascending One's stereotype did make me go hmmm.

                                              The Ascending Ones were said to have spread across the great silk road which stats 200 AD while the Edo starts 1600. So The Ascending Ones were probably in Japan for around 1400 years. The Ascending Ones are also extremely adaptable with the modern group containing Hermetic Alchemists, all three Abrahamic Faiths and drug cartels. A group that can have Islamic extremists and Jewish extremists (Knife of Paradise section in Compacts and Conspiracies) side by side should easily have recruited native Japanese in 1400 years. So it was odd to see them refereed to as coming from the "stock" of a sub-Saharan African; when they originated from north Africa and probably look Japanese by now.

                                              It also struck me that he Gimu seem to know who the Ascending Ones are. Most stereotypes about them are something like "my cousins friend met this weird guy with potions" or "My mum's aunt's grandson tried this potion and died".


                                              Closing Thoughts

                                              The Gimu remind me of the Barrett Commission. Rich influential guys fighting monsters who want a piece of the rich influence pie by using their riches and influences. Being samurai the Gimu are obviously better fighters, and it seems a bit less rich and influential than the Commission, so it balances out.

                                              You can never get enough Barrett Commission stuff, so I enjoyed seeing them.
