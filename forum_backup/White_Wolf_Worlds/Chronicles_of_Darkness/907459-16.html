            Originally posted by 3Comrades View Post
            Guys you are both right to some degree. How to roleplay correctly(tm) some of these games as the one true way is a bit much to argue about.
            I'm not arguing about how to roleplay. I'm arguing about whether or not a particular approach to characterizing the broad themes of each gameline is valid.

            If the approach is useful for people planning out a chronicle or writing further supplements or homebrews, then that's seriously the only reason I bothered writing it down. Well, that and wanting to actually move the conversation away from the... other thing.

            I mean, let's presume I have a point. That's easy for me, because I think I have a point. What's next? Territoriality is interesting, because it's about seizing and holding ground. It's about occupied land. It smacks of a paternalistic imperialism. Which means that a useful thread for thinking about not just Vampire, but also Werewolf and Mage, is to think about how their basic mode of being, the assumptions and presumptions they make mostly subconsciously just being themselves, inflict collateral damage onto the world around them. It means that you can, for instance, characterize the Guardians of the Veil as pacifist, and create parallels between them and, say, the Vietnam War protesters who argued against American overreach. It means that, for instance, you can look at the Pure and the Seers as those who are too privileged to see or care about the collateral damage their actions inflict, which is another dimension to why they're antagonists that you can explore.

                    I was actually discussing this recently and felt like sharing.
                    When we're talking about changing up the core elements and mechanics of a game to make it handle other concepts better what we're basically asking is if we can "take it off road."

                    There's nothing wrong with wanting to take any car off road, but not every car is equally well designed to accomplish that. This isn't to say you can't modify a car to make it better at off-roading, but that's work you have to put in on your end if it's something you really want to make happen. By the same token there's nothing wrong with putting monster truck tires on a Ferrari, do what you want to do if it's your hobby, but some people are also going to look at it and wonder what the point was. Neither they nor you are wrong, but you look at the same idea and see differing levels of utility in it.
