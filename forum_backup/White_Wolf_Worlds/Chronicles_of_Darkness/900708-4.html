                                                    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                                                    Are you? Could it possibly be? Is the suggestion you're offering, could it possibly be also understood alternatively as...

                                                    I 'unno, "You're on fire" or something?
                                                    Rather than fire, I've been wondering about the metaphor of an explosive chemical reaction. Such a reaction is destructive to its components and potentially the vessel it's held in (Torment), not to mention its surroundings (Wasteland), and the people around it (Disquiet). But it's also energetic (Azoth and Transmutations). And while chemical reactions are usually over quickly, Prometheans are somehow stuck halfway through them. But chemical reactions also have a natural direction they're going in (Mortality). And if you're careful, you can analyse the components in the reaction, figure out what they are, and so more specifically what they "trying" to be (Pilgrimmage, and in 2nd Ed the connection between Roles and Transmutations). And maybe induce the reaction to finish (New Dawn). But chemical reactions tend to produce results that are less energetic than the components going in (hence loss of powers and weaknesses, because they're the same thing).

                                                    How this would relate to an alternative to Disquiet... barring the Azoth vs Pilgrimmage roll I suggested earlier. What if the power inherent in Transmutations is too much for others? Using a Transmutation on a person - no matter how otherwise harmless, or even benign, it might be - risks inducing a lasting sickness that gets worse at roughly the rate of a Wasteland spread? That would preserve the "powers and weaknesses are the same thing" element - since heroic Prometheans would no doubt regard making people ill as a weakness (that's not snark btw) - and encourage Prometheans to be careful about whom-on, when, and why they use their powers.

                                                    Originally posted by Mangle77 View Post
                                                    On a completely different note; I feel like there was another way to go with the inspirational material.                                                     Remember too that the Ulgan have an alterate origin myth based on Orpheus - they have Orpheus as the progenitor, which is more in keeping with their shamanic selves, but from this perspective, Orpheus could be the demiurge and Euridice the progenitor.

                                                    On the subject of ghosts, as of the 2nd Edition, Prometheans have to die at least once before the New Dawn. During this they visit the Underworld and encounter ghosts. As soon as we heard this, a lot of us immediately jumped to the ghosts of those who made up their bodies. For the take you're talking about here, perhaps they encounter the ghost of their body's donor (more likely to be just one in this version, it seems, if resurrection was the goal), and justify their use of it. If the ghost accepts them, perhaps their weaknesses are muted to an extent - such as a cap on how much Azoth can be counted to their severity - as the ghost's blessing makes their life more true and whole (conversely, if the ghost violently rejects the Promethean, the weaknesses might be exacerbated). (One question here might be why the Promethean should have to justify themselves to the ghost, to which the answer is, you're free to present that argument to the ghost and see how far it gets you).

                                                    I don't think that would work for Prometheans in the canon game (I can't see why the ghost's attitude would make any difference to the effects of the Divine Fire), but presumably in the necromantic version, the Prometheans would be animated by something more chthonic.
