            So here’s the advances I got since I asked for advice what powers to give the lists in. If anyone wants to suggest things to fill in the empty slots for Stewards, Scribes, and Diplomats, you’re free to do so.


            The ideas for Stewards is going to shift a little more into a more “selfcentered” fashion, being primarily administrators and managers of their realm instead of social butterflies (not just literally). They are the sort of people who ensure things run smoothy, maybe are the best builders.
            Maybe something space manipulating for them?

            Diplomats, I think maybe should be telepathy focused, all forms of it. Not sure what their 3 is.

            Scribes are outright the “wizards” and mystics I think.

            It may be self explanatory as to why I put Shades and Marshals with what I did.

            Also, though it isn’t said here: every Caste will have their own unique “perks”. All will start out with Form bonuses and one unique thing, maybe a gear or structure, a unique Servant template to force elite warriors out of. As they get stronger and gain more Authority, they unlock more such bonuses, such as in the case of the Marshals, they can eventually build Ultralisk equivalents to act as Officers. Unsure of what to give the other Splats
            So I'm personally against having one and only one X-splat that gets all the fighting powers at in-house costs, while no one else does. I might jump Bows to Stewards (since currently the other Castes have powers I can imagine with combat applications) or to Diplomats (if they're telepathy-based, they can be causing damage to other's minds) and give Marshals healy powers, for both fixing themselves and their minions, maybe giving Physical bonuses. Proposed name of Leeches? Or Leeches could go under Stewards, for fixing up themselves, their people, and their castle. Drums (shock-and-awe tactics, intimidation) could work for Marshals or Diplomats, maybe.

            Alternatively, changing the spread to use only ten powers (because coming up with unique spreads for fifteen lines sounds daunting):

            Marshal
            Blades - melee, intimidation, power
            Bows - ranged, lightning, finesse
            *Plates - armor, defense, resistance

            Scribe
            ​Quills - mind-reading, discovery, telepathy
            Records - postcognition, precognition, time
            *Spheres - scrying, warping, space

            Steward
            Cloaks - illusions, disguise, shapeshifting
            *Hammers - creation, repair, healing
            Records - postcognition, precognition, time

            Diplomat
            Bows - ranged, lightning, finesse
            *Crowns - commands, control, royalty
            Quills - mind-reading, discovery, telepathy

            Shade
            Blades - melee, intimidation, power
            Cloaks - illusions, disguise, shapeshifting
            *Veils - speed, flight, shadows

            Powers with a * are Caste-unique powers (might mean other Castes can't learn it without being taught; might mean that they're just the only Caste who gets it at in-house cost). Spheres subsumes Staffs, since both are about space. Records subsumes Clocks, because both are about time (and having both Clocks and Cloaks could be confusing). Dropped Chalices because I don't know if poisons needs to be its own thing, it might fall under Blades or Cloaks depending on how you spin it.

                        I get the societal roles idea, but from a gameplay perspective, I'd like to have more options, rather than fewer. Given that each dot is granting several powers at a time, I'd imagine they'd be costly, and more so out-of-Caste; if I want my Steward to be able to defend their castle, or my Shade to work as an assassin, or my Scribe to act like Indiana Jones, or my Diplomat to defend themself, I don't want to pay out the nose to pick up Bows or Blades. Further, my Marshal would get no powers not related to combat right now, which similarly limits their playability. (I know 1e Werewolf does this quite a bit, and I dislike it there, too; 2e seems to be doing better with giving each Auspice combat-powers and non-combat-powers, despite their similarly role-based X-splat.)

                        Expanding in-Caste powers a la Werewolf is a valid option, as is doing something like 2e Changeling, where every power is the same cost, but different Seemings get different bonuses/special effects for free (and others have to purchase them), or like Beast, where your starting powers are determined by Family, but after that everything costs the same.
