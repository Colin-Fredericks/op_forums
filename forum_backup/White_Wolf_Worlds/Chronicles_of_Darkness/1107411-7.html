                                    I'm assuming this stuff was in response to my post, rather than Jakondite.
                                    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                    You're right, those things are quite dangerous and would be a daunting threat... if the monsters didn't also have the capacity to access them.

                                    In the World of Darkness, humans aren't dealing with mindless, savage creatures who's only thoughts are hunt, kill, repeat. They are dealing with intelligent creatures who have blended into their society and adapted their resources for their own use. They have been doing this for hundreds, if not thousands of years and have become so proffecient at covering their tracks that the vast majority of humans don't even believe they exist.
                                    I don't disagree. In fact you can easily go one step further and say that the average monster could be more connected than the average Hunter. The kind of attitude that leads one to monster hunting probably doesn't also lead to a well-functioning social life. It's not exactly relevant though, for the reasons I'm going to detail below.

                                    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                    Let's say the monsters pull the same stunt and track the hunters back to their own homes and families. They scout the place out and find an entire room loaded up with military grade weapons and an itinerary of a list of people they plan to target.
                                    That's a big assumption, first and foremost. The point of indirect warfare is to prevent exactly that, the monsters ending up on the Hunters' doorstep. We already established that the Hunters probably lose the straight fight, that's why they're attacking the monsters in this fashion to begin with.

                                    More importantly though, we need to deal with the matter of profile. The monsters do monstrous things. That attracts attention, attention draws in Hunters. Monsters need to do what makes them monsters because it's part of their nature. The wolf hunters, the vampires feed, and so on. So their standard mode of behaviour puts them in a spotlight for those who know what to look for and also gives cause and grievance for random alienated humans to become their enemy.

                                    What this means is that there's virtually zero chance of a monster ever getting to a Hunter first. The Hunter may not even be a Hunter until they make the first move. There's no "tell" that a Hunter exists until the first monster is dead. The Hunter's equivalent to the monster's monstrous behaviour is killing monsters. And when the Hunters have the population advantage, and the monsters can't help but create more Hunters, that's the reason a smart monster lives in fear.

                                    Yes, Hunters can fuck up. A monster could realise it's under observation, or a Hunter could fail its first attempt at killing the monster and the creature could be alerted by that. But in game terms we're all familiar with, it's like Hunters have a permanent Surprise Attack advantage against monsters. They get a free round in which they can do something to the monster before it gets to react, except instead of being a single round of combat that "free round" could translate into any number of things.

                                    Even in your example, the monsters have to take the hit first and then track the hunters back to their homes and families. The monsters are always reacting, on the defensive. That's an oppressive burden to live under, to say the least.

                                    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                    The monster makes an anonymous call to the cops and suddenly the Hunters are being investigated on suspicions of terrorism. The fuck are they gonna tell the judge? That the people they were planning to blow up are shapeshifting wolf people? Or that their neighbor practices witchcraft in his basement on thursdays?
                                    For most monsters, the law is a lot more dangerous and disruptive than it is for most Hunters. You get a Hunter indicted on conspiracy to commit murder, and he's behind bars for a long time but he can still function as a human being. A vampire probably wouldn't even survive its court dates, and most kinds of monster behaviour are a lot harder to do once they've lost their privacy to the legal system.

                                    Worse, for a monster, being convicted of a crime raises their profile. It means more people will know of them and about them. That's more potential Hunters. And a Hunter getting convicted? That does the same thing. A man raving in court about wolf people and witchcraft may just give a few select souls who read about it in the paper or see the clip online the clues they need to take up the fight themselves. Less dramatically, it may mean that the details of the case catch someone's eye and in a more mundane fashion and they dig, and uncover the truth.

                                    In effect, the monster can use the same tactics. They often do. But due to the difference in nature between monster and Hunter they're not going to impact their respective targets in the same way.
