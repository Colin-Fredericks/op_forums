        Five Pounds Of Meat
        Let's start with something relatively mild (and quite long, apologies for how it turned out in that respect!)

        ​Toothcracker

        The pain starts as a dull ache nestled in the teeth - a throb of unpleasant pressure with each beat of the heart. It comes and goes at first but, over time, it grows ever more insistent, ever more acute. The pain's edge is sharpened, and it grows constant. A taste of sour rot sits on the tongue no matter what the victim does - no scouring brush or antiseptic fluids seem to fight the infection back. The pressure grows and grows until it feels like surely it must burst out. It winds down into the bone of the jaw as well. In the mind's eye of the victim, they see cracks and fractures worming through the enamel of their teeth, and imagine the thumping pressure of their heart reaching such intensity that, crack, they are left with a mouthful of blood and ivory shards in an orgy of pain reaching its crescendo.

        Some are driven to venture into the embrace of modern dental medicine, braving the glaring lights and whirling drills and harsh sterility and dripping needles. It's hard to call them the lucky ones, though they undoubtedly are more fortunate for their actions; doctors, aghast, find a boiling cauldron of infectious horror in the victims' mouths, their teeth split with worm-like burrows and their gums seeping ichor. It's like every worse-case condition in the manual decided to come throw a party at once. With whirling saw and flesh-piercing injection, treatment begins. The victim will likely live, at least.

        But people have a fear of dentists, or a fear of spending money they can't afford on dentistry, or have teeth already so rotten or numbed that the surging pressure never reaches the heights of actual pain that stir them to take action in time. And so the incubation progresses further, and further, and then one day, as the victim sits at home, wincing at the thudding pressure running through their jaw and teeth, there's that final, ominous crack as the pregnancy of their infection reaches its culmination and the fetid power hatches.

        Some victims die immediately as their heads are ripped apart from within, shattering fragments of tooth blasting out and spraying gore everywhere as if a grenade had gone off in their mouth. It's hard to call them the lucky ones either, although they are undoubtedly more fortunate than the others who make it this far. Chunks of enamel and dentine thud into walls, tear through cloth, bury themselves in the flesh of hapless creatures in close proximity. The teeth fragments thus seeded begin to flourish. They sprout their own little ivory roots. A human injured by a tooth fragment tries to pull it out, struggling against sharp barbs; if they can't extract it, in mere hours its anchors have grown, a hard invader connecting itself to their own cartilage and skeleton. In days, they die as their entire body goes into cancerous overdrive, teeth sprouting everywhere within their meat. Tooth fragments buried in walls slowly sprout out like bone flowers, fronds reaching forth with yellow-white spines and leaves.

        Some victims survive the initial hatching. The teeth don't rupture with enough force to kill them; they are left with wounds in their own mouth and throat, spitting tooth-shards out and gargling blood. Shock and blood poisoning tends to incapacitate these victims. Each tooth fragment in the wounds soon roots itself into their flesh, growing a garden of new teeth wherever it reaches over several agonising days. A few aren't wounded at all, merely vomiting out the stream of ichor and shattered teeth in a horrifying deluge. Hallucinations soon follow, odd compulsions to collect the pieces and plant them in soil where they can grow their jaw-branches that hang with succulent tooth-fruit.

        A very rare few undergo a more extreme transformation. The teeth don't come apart entirely. Instead, the fracture-marked little horrors start to grind and chatter amongst themselves. Enamel snaps and twists, sparking fresh agony, as vile faces tear out of them. They interlace with each other, a parliament of rotting hatred that loathes the meat-body they are bound in. Unless the victim tears them out with pliers or hammers them out, his own teeth dig roots down through the jaw and out, extending new bony growths and filling the veins with poisoned blood. They don't kill the meat vehicle they inhabit; they rebuild it. Flesh is overwritten by hard, nodulous matter. The teeth hate the rest of the face, the thing expressing the host, so they crush it; the hard cancer that erupts throughout the victim obliterates eyes, nose, any features that might show some sign of the person within, replacing it all with irregular budding growths of teeth and cartilage and bone. This takes time, of course, but if the victim does not act fast, the poisons coursing from the infections will leave him incapable of doing anything but thrash and moan as the Judas teeth overwrite the fleshy substrate of their home.

        Once they're done, the newly-grown toothwight is a hungry, mad creature - a mutated thing with a distended maw filled with fangs and teeth jutting in from every angle, its twisted limbs studded with bony growths. It wants more teeth, so it hunts for them. Anything will do - animals or humans - as long as it can rip the teeth from their sockets and crunch the little things down its own throat. It may then stop and eat the rest of the victim as well, if hungry - but what it absolutely craves are teeth.

        Toothwight
        Int 1 Wits 4 Res 3 Str 6 Dex 4 Sta 8 Pre 4 Man 2 Com 3
        Athletics 4, Brawl 4, Larceny 1, Stealth 3, Intimidation 4
        Size 5 Health 13 Willpower 6 Initiative 7 Defence 8 Speed 15
        Toothflesh: Toothwights have a natural armour value of 4. They regenerate 1L per round.
        Fingerbite: Toothwights can cling to ceilings and walls, and their brawl attacks deal +2L damage.
        Eat Teeth: Upon eating all the teeth of a victim, a toothwight regains 1 Willpower point and heals 1 point of aggravated damage.
        Grindsight: A toothwight doesn't have eyes and cannot hear conventionally. However, it can pick up the noise of teeth hitting one another, such as during talking or any other normal movement of the jaw. It can also effectively extent perfect awareness of an area up to fifty feet around it by grinding the teeth of its mouth together, akin to a ghastly form of echolocation; this is a reflexive action and quiet, but it does make a sound, and therefore has a possibility of being heard. A toothwight trying to stalk a victim will occasionally have to make a faint grinding or clattering noise to sustain its awareness.
        Tooth Cancer Overdrive: A toothwight who wounds a being with natural regeneration triggers that regeneration to go into a ghastly meltdown. Rather than knitting back together healthily, rent flesh furiously vomits forth bony structures and teeth, building nodules of gnashing fangs and molars or zipping up in hungry mouths. This effect lasts for three rounds after inflicting an injury, during which time any health levels that regeneration would heal are instead inflicted as additional health levels of damage.

        For all their hunger, toothwights are just a byproduct. A werewolf who investigates a series of horrific deaths where victims are devoured by an overgrowth of their own teeth might stumble upon a ravenous toothwright ripping incisors from a dead body and assume they've encountered the root of the problem - some sort of utterly bizarre vampiric process where teeth are substituted for blood. Such a werewolf would sadly be very wrong.

        Now, the Jaw Hags, they know something of what's going on; the adherents of the weird, small Lodge have encountered the cause before, and consider it a significant rival. Some of the Tribes have pieced together a little too - the Hunters in Darkness who fear this is some sort of bizarre Host infection, the Bone Shadows who prize the occult value of teeth enough to be intrigued, the Iron Masters who try and determine the threat that an outbreak amongst humans may represent - and whether or not it might have been incited by human efforts in the first place. Enough little gobbets can be sewn together to show the ghastly whole: the handiwork of the tooth harvester, the tusk harridan, the Maiden of the Maw.

        The harvester is an entity of unclear provenance. It - she? - looks humanoid much of the time; a gnarled, hulking thing with a too-wide mouth, wiry limbs that look too thin for her bloated body, twisted hands with iron-hard nails which taper into probocises and organic needles. She - it? - has skin covered in burns and sores and buboes and gnarled, cartiligionous lumps; her maw has too many teeth in it, innumerable teeth, from all manner of species and sources, all jammed into bleeding gums and sticking out at strange angles like a hedge of ivory barbs. It garbs itself in robes threaded together from sinew and hair, with particularly beloved teeth bored through and sewn in like beads. In places, large growths press so hard out of her body that the skin splits and parts, revealing new faces with grinning mouths that stare wildly from their meat prison. From her back, a fleshy growth or cord protrudes, an additional spine-cum-umbilical that ropes away onto the floor and drags along. After a little distance, it tapers out into nothing - it doesn't end, and is not severed, it simply seems to curl round a corner in the fabric of reality. The growth pulses disgustingly; sometimes sacs along its length billow and bloat briefly.

        The Maiden of the Maw is a creature that dwells in the world of Flesh, but it can slip in and out of a Twilight frequency at will. Sometimes it gathers a small entourage of spirits - spirits of pain, gluttony, teeth, and fear. Sometimes it catches toothwights and beats them until they accept its authority, a pack of whining, tooth-cracking hunting hounds that cringe with fear of it. Often, though, she operates by herself, happy in her isolation. She likes to hum and whistle as she works with dentine and flesh. The screams and the sickening crunch of ripping flesh don't seem to phase her.

        When the harridan arrives in an area, she chooses a base of operations. This will usually be a remote location - a cave or abandoned house in the countryside; the basement of a crumbling, empty tenement or the dusty, forgotten rooms in the top of an old tower block. Perhaps she breaks into a bricked-off stretch of sewer or metro system, or digs out a hollow under a hospital. Once ensconced, she will lay down some beds of fertile earth for her grisly crops; she needs grave-soil for this, so a sign of her presence is the disturbance of burial grounds as she steals away mouldering dirt in the pitch black of night. She seems to have some basic need to eat from time to time; her preference lies towards vermin and small animals, ideally alive as she mauls them with her tooth-studded jaws. She needs other tools, other furniture, which she steals from where she can find them, assembling her little workshop with remarkable talent and an easy understanding of modern electronics.

        Her lair done up to her satisfaction, the Maiden of the Maw cannot resist the temptation to begin her harvest for long. The thing stalks through the surrounding area in Twilight, hunting for just the right victims. What, exactly, qualifies a victim in the eyes of the tooth harvester are still not clear to those Uratha who have encountered her; there is speculation it involves some sort of lingering flaw in the victim that can be coaxed into 'sprouting', or a fear or anxiety. Without being sure what it is that drives the thing, her malison seems terrifyingly random, even as she mutters to herself that one human is not quite right, another too much. The nightmare itself is bestowed, once she finds a suitable target, by simply prodding them and muttering something. The victim, in the material world, feels nothing more than a faint shiver and then the throb beginning in their teeth. Werewolves who have gotten close enough to hear what the tooth harvester says have suffered catastrophic results, as the bones of their inner ear explode in a spray of razor-thin shrapnel; afterwards, they still aren't sure exactly what the words she utters are.

        And that's it - a victim cursed to a likely horrific death. The tooth harvester will perform this several times over several weeks, carefully sifting through the population for the right targets. Then she waits for the eggs she's laid to hatch, turning up as soon as she can after the victim has fruited. She gathers all the fragments and pieces up, plucks the bone flowers, and takes whatever carcasses she can; the hulking thing just slings them over her shoulders like they were game animals. Where there's a survivor, they are granted the phantasmagorical experience of a massive, crooning thing looming over them, tutting and muttering as it probes their injuries to yank out remaining lumps of tooth-shrapnel, occasionally making what can only be attempts at calming noises or brushing the human's forehead in a soothing motion. If there's too much embedded in the survivor to work through on site, she'll just take them with her back to the lair.

        Sometimes the Maiden of the Maw doesn't arrive at a fruiting event until much later; she sometimes has to scurry to deal with unknown matters, falls into something akin to sleep where her pulsing umbilical shivers with bioluminescence, or is simply delayed when she runs into hostile spirits or other beings that get in her way. She'll always try and make good on harvesting something she's laid; there's a few urban legends in police crime-scene investigation labs of a tech briefly seeing something hulking shuffle through a room or rattling through mortuary units, disappearing as swiftly as it came.

        The bodies she brings back - living and dead - are harvested. When gristly meat or tooth-infested-into-bone is too irritatingly tricky to pull apart with her monstrous strength, the harridan uses tools - meat-cutting shears, bone-cracking pliers, screaming saws. She pulls and yanks and tugs and plucks until everything she's come for is free. Living victims soon cease to be; she restrains them but doesn't bother with anything like an anaesthetic. A few, who have suffered only light wounds and have little to harvest, survive. She doesn't seem to care much if they do; she drops them back where she found them, or nearby, and if they live then they live. Dead bodies get shredded into compost for her garden. She doesn't eat the meat of humans, although from time to time as she works, she tosses a tooth or lump of overgrown bone into her mouth to grind it down with some pleasure.

        Whatever it is trying to achieve, the Maiden is never quite satisfied. The teeth and bones are planted and coaxed into a tangled overgrowth of enamel and cartilage - flowers of flesh and dentine that sprout tooth-fruit and curl with vines of sinew. The harvester takes the crops when they are 'ripe', and pries them open, monstrous face lit with hope. But that hope is aways frustrated; the sought is never found. Angry, bitter or despairing, she gathers some of her possessions into a great bundle of human-skin leather that she hauls upon her shoulders, and sets on towards the next stop on her pilgrimage.

        There are two factors that sometimes alter the course of the tooth-harvester's outbreak. The first are werewolves. The harvester sees werewolves as a great prize, but they are extremely hard for her to infect, and much more capable of defending themselves - thus she usually steers clear of them. However, if she does manage to subdue and capture a lone werewolf, she will drag them back to her lair where she forces the infection to a ghastly new height. The feverish horror that grips the Uratha's flesh turns its regeneration against itself in an overdrive of toothy growth. Within days, the werewolf utterly loses its original form, instead becoming a mass of oozing flesh that starts to grow over wall, floor and ceiling. It slowly splits and buds out with flowers of teeth and branches of bone, producing new dentine-crops at a rate far beyond that of the harvester's usual crops. It anchors itself with great spurs of bone; eyes occasionally emerge from the meat and stare wildly before popping as of under high pressure. A werewolf reduced to this sessile state cannot be restored, only put out of its misery. It will die under the weight of its own self-destructive metabolism after a while, stirring the harvester to seek another werewolf or move on.

        The second are human supplicants. There are humans who learn of ways to contact and invoke the tooth harvester, although in most cases they have no full understanding of the nature of what they are dealing with. Still, through a ritual of teeth pried from the jaws of the dead, a human occultist can call to the Maiden of the Maw. The harvester will divert from her seemingly random wanderings to answer such a summons, which sadly means she will almost certainly be active in the area of the occultist for a while afterwards.

        The actual call itself is a trade. The occultist offers up a tooth of value to the harvester - something unusual and meaningful. She's come to collect a tooth from the mouth of an assassinated President, a fossilized fang from a species unknown to science, a fake tooth from a set of dentures in which a KGB spy held the cyanide that he committed suicide with, and the tusk of the last of a now-extinct species of seal. If the harvester accepts the tooth, she offers something in trade, which varies depending on the exact occult ceremony the human uses - might be that no poison that passes their lips can harm them, or their teeth become unbreakable, or that they can command predatory animals that they feed raw meat to, or that they can gain the memories of anyone whose flesh they eat. Regardless, during the actual rite, the harvester does not manifest; she snatches the prize away into Twilight, leaving the occultist with the sense that something has visited and the proof of its agreement to the pact.

        Some occultists, ambitious or insane enough to think they can milk more out of such a deal, go beyond a simple ritual. With the horror already in the vicinity, they offer up more and more teeth - a crop harvested bloodily from screaming jaws - and call the harridan to appear. Those that don't panic and flee when faced with the enormous monstrosity try and bargain for more power from her. The Maiden of the Maw is usually happy to agree; she trades further blessings for their help in finding suitable targets for her infections, or covering up what is going on, or because they have easy access to the sorts of places her tooth fragments end up needing to be collected from, and so forth. Sometimes, if she has a toothwight to heel, she forces it to serve such an occultist. This is all only temporary, however. When she decides to move on, all the blessings past the one earned with a special tooth cease - including control of any bound spirits or toothwrights she has bestowed them with. She has no particular attachment to any of the would-be sorcerers she deals with.

        And the special teeth they barter with her? She shoves those into her maw with all the rest, where they take root. Some Uratha think every tooth in her mouth was acquired through such deals.
        Some werewolves think the Maiden of the Maw is a Host gone even more horrifically wrong. Some think she's the product of an idigam, or some sort of invader from another realm that she is still tethered to - and may be trying to return to. Some think she's a reality-cancer, a tumour of wrongness that's budded into the world around something that fundamentally corrupts it - a pearl of teeth and meat around a corrosive grain of sand. Some think she's one of Destroyer Wolf's actual teeth, smashed from his mouth during a battle and plunged into the world where its sheer power gave it independence and agency. Some think she's a last piece of Father Wolf's own jaw, driven to mad harvests by the fragmented nature of the Great Predator's remains. Some think it doesn't matter what she is, but just that her antics warp the Shadow, murder innocents and twist werewolves into tooth-blistered parodies, nightmares of flesh that just need to be granted the mercy of death, and that this is enough to know about her to understand she needs to die.

        There's plenty of reasons Uratha hate the Maiden, or want to harvest her mouthful of teeth and its occult potency and possibilities, or want to study her umbilical and see where, exactly, she may come from, but a particularly unusual agenda drives a small number of werewolves. They want to eat her, to crack her bones with Bone Gnaw, and see if they can't just steal the power she has accrued directly.

        Maiden of the Maw
        Int 4 Wits 4 Res 7 Str 14 Dex 5 Sta 18 Pre 8 Man 3 Com 3
        Crafts 5 (Tools), Medicine 3 (Teeth), Occult 5, Athletics 4, Brawl 5 (Claws), Stealth 1, Survival 4, Weaponry 3, Animal Ken 2, Empathy 1, Intimidation 5, Persuasion 1, Subterfuge 3
        Size 9 Health 27 Willpower 10 Initiative 8 Defence 8 Speed 22
        Ironhide: The harvester has a natural armour value of 5, and regenerates 3 points of lethal damage every round.
        Shredder: The harvester's unarmed attacks and grapples deal an additional 3L, and bite attacks during a grapple deal permanent Arm Wrack or Leg Wrack tilts at the harvester's choice.
        Monstrous Bulk: The harvester has the 8-again rule on non-combat Strength-based rolls, deals double damage to objects, and can spend 1 Willpower to shrug off any Tilt.
        Toothcracker: Any enemy the harvester wounds has their teeth immediately explode in their head. This deals an additional 5L immediately, and the victim is unable to use a bite attack until the damage has healed or regenerated. An enemy whose teeth have already erupted will obviously suffer no further damage from this effect if struck again.
        Infection: The harvester's touch can infect a victim. This can be done from Twilight, as one of the needles or probocis pierces through the frequencies into the victim's flesh for a brief moment, which usually goes unnoticed. Only certain victims can be infected and it isn't clear what makes a human suitable. Werewolves, if infected, have their metabolism go into overdrive, gaining the moderate Sick Tilt; they start to sprout tumours of teeth, and lose 1 point of Essence each day as their body struggles to cope. A werewolf reduced to 0 Essence while infected is incapacitated and, if subjected by the harvester to a greater infection at its leisure, will be reduced to a formless mass of tooth-spewing meat. Otherwise, the infection lifts after five days.
        Festermaw: Anyone who attempts to remotely affect the Maiden of the Maw with a curse, hex, or other supernatural malison or interference suffers the moderate Sick tilt as the teeth in their mouth begin to rot and their gums are befouled with infection. This doesn't prevent the attempt, but it seems that interfacing with the Maiden's corrupt spiritual essence taints the attacker in the process.
        Twilight Strider: The Maiden can enter or leave Twilight as an instant action at will, or as a reflexive action by spending a point of Willpower.
        Pilgrimage: Once her belongings are packed on her back, the Maiden of the Maw can move through half-hidden paths in the world, covering up to a thousand miles a day and unhindered by barriers such as oceans, walls or deserts.

        - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
