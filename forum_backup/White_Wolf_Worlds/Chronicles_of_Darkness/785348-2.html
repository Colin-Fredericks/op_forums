            Basically, if the copyright holder for World of Darkness (Paradox Entertainment, I believe?) knows about something like Subnet and doesn't do anything about it...

            Well, then someone can use that against them in a court of law.

            No, really - if I were to, say, reprint the rules for Mage: The Awakening as Wizard: The Waking-Up, and I was taken for court about it, I could point to Subnet as a legal defense.

            Now, normally, the lawyers on the other side could just shrug their shoulders and say &quot;well, we didn't know about that&quot;.                                                 Desired service doesn't equal needed service. You don't need a searchable meta-file (and you can create your own as noted above). Some people want them.

                                                How? By providing a service that is the same but requires accounts that know which books have been purchased and provides access accordingly.
                                                And how do they do this without causing other problems? The only ways to verify purchases is for them to start watermarking PDFs again, which was a giant PR nightmare, or start their one PDF store which will require everyone that purchases their PDFs from DTRPG to repurchase them in the new OPP store.

                                                On top of this, there's the issue of OPP not actually owning these things. CCP in the past, and WWP now, have to make this decision. Even if OPP wanted to provide this service, they could only do it of for their own properties (Triniy, Scion, Cavaliers, Pugmire).

                                                Sorry if that's a little vague, I am not a digital book distributor, but I do have faith that those who are can come up with something to solve this problem in ways that benefit everyone.
                                                They did. It's called the WOD Canon Index (and numerous similar projects that predated it). It lets you search a giant meta-index of CofD material, so you can find where various things are in individual books. If you own those books, you now know what pages to open up.

                                                This is, in many ways, vastly superior to some sort of a high-maintenance subscription service as has been discussed previously (and that generally bombed for WotC). It's free. It's a wiki, allowing for users to assist in content organization. As with any wiki, it can be downloaded for use offline (though obviously not updated after the point of download). It requires no verification services, because it is just a book reference finder; requiring you to have the books to look things up.
