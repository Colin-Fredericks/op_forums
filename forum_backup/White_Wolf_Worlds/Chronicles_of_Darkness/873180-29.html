                What I am saying is that you are literally not going to be in the situation where all four Beasts are encountering all twelve monsters at the same exact point in the narrative, unless your goal is to have the exact story beat of "the four of you each saw all twelve of these other monsters within seconds of each other, and it is significant that you all come to separate conclusions about each of them despite being in a context where they can communicate." Most pacing structures parcel out that many characters over the course of the story rather than dumping them all in front of the camera at the start.

                The roll for Family Resemblance matters when you need more information than the automatic basic success provided for unveiled kin. Anything that's not an Unchained or in the "fundamentally human" category can be pinged as a basic member of their species without a roll as long as they aren't magically hiding what they are.

                    If it is really going to be one single big moment than just do a pooled rolls system. Only roll once for the Beasts, use the highest and assume they will share what they notice with the others. That could cut it down a whole lot.

                    If you want to reduce it even more then you could group the others by supernatural type, again use the highest. If they succeed on the roll against the highest Composure+Wisdom mage then they notice all of them, etc. It may not be perfectly mechanically accurate but it works for that narrative moment when they start to notice how every third person seems to be a Mage.
