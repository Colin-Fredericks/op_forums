    nWoD general questions.

    06-03-2022, 08:55 AM
    I am a long-time oWoD player that has become tired of the clunky rules mechanics, and I hear tell that the nWoD mechanics are widely considered an improvement over the oWoD mechanics. As an example, in oWoD, combat is deadly and you are often killed in one hit without ST fiat. Fortitude in VtM is almost silly, and the Mage rules are... complex to say the least.

    If anyone can give their thoughts on the following it would be most appreciated:

    1) Are the nWoD rules really more streamlined? Is combat less broken than oWoD? If you can, give a quick rundown of how they differ.

    2) I already have the book titled "The World of Darkness", which I think is the 1st edition core rulebook, and Mage: The Awakening. Does the book titled "A World of Darkness (2nd edition)" replace the 1st edition, or is it more like an errata? Is it worth picking up, does it differ much from the 1st edition? For Mage: The Awakening, are there multiple editions, or is what I already have the only one?

    3) Any other thoughts or observations on what I might gain (or lose) by switching to nWoD rules. I do not know that I would switch the setting, I'm more interested strictly in the rules mechanics, though that doesn't mean I wouldn't mind the less strictly defined setting of nWoD.

    Thanks in advance for any insight you can provide!

      As a premise, nWod 1st edition and 2nd are two similar but different rulesets, and my personal experience is limited to the 2nd edition.
      As a former player of oWod i can say:
      Rules are more streamlined most of the time.
      Dice difficulty always setted at 8 is a great improvement.
      In combat the attacker makes only one roll for both striking and damage.
      oWod was obscenely unbalanced in favoring the attackers, new mechanics are slightly in favor of attackers.
      It must be said that in nWod if two fighters with similar attack vs defence dicepools clashes the combat scene could be very long, with little damage if any inflicted each turn.

      Mage awakening have two editions, i tried only the second.
      Magic rules are more clear than Mage the Ascension, but mages are still power houses hard to manage for the GM.

      In general if you like to mix monsters types, nWod is more friendly and balanced for crossover, but each monster is build with a specific mood and theme in mind and excels in some fields more than others.

      As a side note, nWod 2nd edition is now called Chronicle of darkness, each product that uses this names is usually compatible.

          1) Chronicles of darkness's (that is the official name of the 2nd edition line) rules are better in basically every way compared to WoD, the system is straight up superior, attacking for starter is resolved in only 1 roll or 2 if the target choses to dodge, there is no soak roll since you are directly subtracting your defense from the attacker's dice pool, there are no multiple actions (although you might make attacks that target multiple enemies in some cases) and the mathematics that went with it and initiative is as simple as rolling a dice, no need to wait for everyone to declare their actions etc. much quicker this way.

          2) The 2nd edition book is called Chronicles of darkness or "the blue book", it is the official manual for human characters but it's rules are usable with all the splats which is a good reason to get it even when you plan on using other splats. I have no idea what the world of darkness book you have is but it's definitely not part of the CoD line.

          3) Frankly, you are not losing anything by switching to CoD, it's better in everyway compared to WoD (especially 5ed vampire), the setting is highly modular and there are plenty of supplements that you can use (most of the 1st ed stuff is still canon), there is a defined setting but it's pretty lose so that you can make the type of chronicle you want, you don't have to worry about which prince reign in this city or which pack runs this side of the woods, you just make your own.

              Just before getting to your questions, since 2015 (2 years after the "God-Machine Rules Update" which kicked off what is now 2nd Edition), "nWoD" was officially rebranded as "Chronicles of Darkness" or CofD most commonly. Not to say that calling it nWoD is a faux pas or anything, just that CofD is a lot more recognisable and more commonly used now

              1) I played CofD first before touching oWoD, so take this with a grain of salt, but I definitely think the rules for CofD work a lot cleaner than 20th Anniversary WoD and V5, and I don't know enough about prior editions. Combat in CofD is definitely simpler, being reduced to a single dice roll per participant per turn (for attacks; your character/s might also have reflexive actions to roll for that don't take your main action)

              2) The book "The World of Darkness" is almost definitely 1e. For 2e, the book your looking for is "The Chronicles of Darkness". As for the gamelines, every 1e gameline now has a 2e rulebook available, and there are three gamelines that are 2e only, so yes, there is a MtAw 2e, and that edition has 2 source books as well, Signs of Sorcery and Night Horrors: Nameless and Accursed

              3) I personally highly recommend 2e if you do end up trying it, especially for each gameline's updates over the previous edition. Werewolf especially was a massive improvement over 1e, and Mage became a hell of a lot simpler to work with

              Good luck, I hope you do enjoy reading/watching/playing CofD!

                      The most important rules distinction between oWoD and nWoD is the elimination of multiple-axis difficulty.

                      oWoD had three meters to measure the difficulty of an action: the size of your dice pool, the Difficulty or target number for an individual success, and the threshold of successes needed for a given result. nWoD eliminates Difficulty and mostly removes threshold success. The relative ease or difficulty of a roll is measured by the size of the dice pool, with circumstantial dice modifiers being common to give out: -2 to take a shot in heavy fog, say, or +1 when using appropriate tools and equipment.

                      The target number for a success on an individual die is always 8. The number of successes you need, outside of extended actions and contested actions against other characters rolling against you, is always one. One success isn't a partial success or barely squeezing by, but full success. This is important to stress, because when the target number is always 8, the dice can be very swingy, and it's not uncommon for a character with high ratings for a task to still only roll one success.

                      There are two edition rulesets for nWoD.
                      Demon: The Descent, Beast: The Primordial, Deviant: The Renegades, and the Chronicles of Darkness Rulebook are second edition books; every other second edition core has "Second Edition" in its title. The World of Darkness Rulebook and any other monster corebook without "Second Edition" in its title is first edition.
                      The first edition rulebooks must be paired with the World of Darkness Rulebook. Vampire: The Requiem, for example, doesn't contain the full ruleset, but just the rules relevant to vampires. The second edition rulebooks do not share this quality. You can buy Vampire: The Requiem Second Edition without owning the Chronicles of Darkness Rulebook and you will get the full ruleset for playing Requiem.
                      Confusingly, Demon: The Descent is an exception to this pattern. Demon uses second edition rules but is laid out like a first edition rulebook. It expects you to own the World of Darkness Rulebook (and contains an appendix specifying what rules change in the second edition ruleset), but any of the second edition corebooks will work just as well for providing the base ruleset to use with it.

                      There are Translation Guides for running oWoD setting elements in the nWoD games or otherwise mixing and matching, for Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, and Demon. The first three of these are for first edition nWoD. How easy you will find translating setting elements differs between gamelines, with some gamelines differing from their oWoD counterparts more than others. Requiem, in either edition, can run Masquerade elements pretty well, with the base gameplay being largely a similar experience. Second edition Mage: The Awakening will fight you in trying to model Mage: The Ascension, having gotten rid of the concept of coincidental or vulgar magic altogether. Changeling: The Lost and Changeling: The Dreaming set out to do almost entirely different things.

                              If you especially like a blend of magic and science fiction elements, Mage: The Awakening is lighter on that than Mage: The Ascension, being more of a story of secrets and sorcery, ideology and hidden worlds, and not having much in the way of specifically technological aesthetics. (It is definitely possible to use magic to control or enchant technological devices, but it's simply one more dimension of the world to influence, without an industrially-flavored Technocracy faction. The Seers of the Throne are more likely to dispatch once-human servants with transformed and weaponized souls than machine assassins programmed with enlightened science.)

                              On the other hand, Demon: The Descent is heavier on it in a way Demon: The Fallen never was, mixing fallen angels living stolen lives in a gnostic world ruled by an uncaring demiurge with espionage, bending secret laws of the universe, and a view of angels that sees flaming wheels with multitudes of eyes as not unlike ferroliquid machine gods with voices like radio waves.
