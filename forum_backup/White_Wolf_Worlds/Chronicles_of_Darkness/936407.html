                  Thaumaturges from Second Sight are a minor template of magic users, divied up by magic tradition such as hermetic, shamanistic, folk witch, etc, since psychics in second edition are now people with supernatural merits, more than one person believes that thaumaturgy would/will get the same treatment.

                  But not really what you were talking about though.

                  More along those lines, there are options-

                  1) Mage chroniclers guide, has an option to allow even muggles to do ritual magic, although perhaps requiring reagents like a dragon's scale or the breath of a fish, that sort of thing.

                  2) A changeling book has an option in which the local rules might change depending on which court is ascendant. If the autumn court is ascendent, changelings can start collecting secrets, up to their power stat, which they can use to use contracts they don't know, it even includes the option that possibly other supernaturals and even muggles can do the same thing.

                  3) Witch book for Hunter, it has it's own magic system, and even muggles can use the magic therein after buying a merit for each power they want, or casting the magic ritually.

                  4) Use the crafting rules from CofD book, sure only getting dice bonus is rather tame, but hey it's free magic.

                  5) Mirrors, forbidden lore, in which people can buy and power their magical abilities with their own sanity, for a call of cthulhu sort of deal.

                  6) Spirits and other supernatural beings are valid targets for supernatural merits, so hey you could have a character with a ghostly ally or a spirit contact.

                  There might be more, but that's all I got offhand.

                      Magic-as-Endowments. The Malleus Maleficatorum's Benedictions are essentially Catholic-flavored ritual magic.

                      Also: the concept of “lesser templates” has evolved since it was first introduced in Second Sight. These days, psychics don't qualify as a “lesser template” anymore; they're “merely” mortals with Supernatural Merits (or, at most, “micro-templates”, which is another way of saying “a collection of closely related Supernatural Merits”). Second Sight's thaumaturges only barely qualify as templates, and then only because each Tradition has a Strength and a Weakness.

Second Sight's thaumaturges only barely qualify as templates, and then only because each Tradition has a Strength and a Weakness.
                              I only used the word template to differentiate thaumaturges from "normal mortals", not because of any mechanical implications. Although Second Sight provides the option that everyone can develop psychic powers or thaumaturgic ritual magic, it could just as well need special circumstances or an inborn trait that could be likened to a template. So even if mechanically all mortals could get supernatural merits without having to assume a mechanical template, narratively some special circumstance could still be required to develop these powers. But maybe we need another word than template to differentiate normal mortals from beings with supernatural powers, as that word already has too many connotations.

                              I like to treat Malleus Maleficatorum's Benedictions the same way. Not everyone could just pick up a book and learn them. But I know that is not necessarily how it is presented in hunter.

                              But I digress. Other things I thought of mortals can do:
                              • Geomancy: Mortals can still shape buildings or even cities to make certain areas more conductive to specific resonances or actions. More complex geomancy, as used in ancient Irem, could also create more complex effects.
                              • Relic making: In ancient Irem mortals used recipes to create relics, but even if most of those recipes got lost, dedicated mortals can still create a few relics during their lifetime.
                              • Some things just work: There are some simple (or complex) rituals that a mortal can find that just work. Maybe they are powered by a supernatural patron who likes the ritual and/or gains Essence from it. Some other rituals key into the arcane physics of the world, crudely mimicking the way the God Machine works its magic.

                                      amechra
                                      I've once considered the following: Within fiction, fire is a common weakness of many monsters. It is this way because it helped humanity survive in a hostile world and has become a potent symbol. What if the same reasoning was applied to the magic system? Fire wouldn't have been such a weakness had humanity never learned to use it. This would also mean that other symbols could be imbued with the same meaning. Should someone light a fire, generate electricity or create a weapon relying on their own expertise and focused effort (Willpower expenditure), and having a specific foe in mind, these assets become especially potent against entities responsive to human psyche. This includes Spirits, Ghosts, Goetia and possibly Fae.
