                I'm not liking a mage's chances fighting a werewolf in a small enclosed space like bar while unprepared for the encounter. Such a situation definitely favors the inherent advantages of the werewolf.

                I would recommend the mage first employ any available means to quickly escape and prepare to fight the werewolf later on more advantageous terms. Even if the werewolf could track the mage, just a little extra time and a better environment could dramatically change the odds.

                If escape was not practical (e.g., protecting innocents in the bar, etc.), and assuming the mage had at least one Arcana at 4 dots, she should attempt the biggest ranged aggravated damage she can muster, using any necessary mana at her disposal and risking Paradox, to try to kill or incapacitate the werewolf in one shot, or at least inflict significant damage that is immune to the werewolf's improved healing abilities. Even without an instant kill, the werewolf might not want to risk a continued encounter where more aggravated damage and death is even a possibility. A draw is as good as a victory.

                                        Acceleration, the spell, makes the subject always go first, too, or delay to reflexively interrupt anyone else then go back to first next turn. Although we do helpfully point out that if more than one person in a combat has an "I go first" effect (like a Time mage and a vampire with Celerity), you Clash of Wills it.

                                        An adept (so, four dots in an arcanum) can do aggravated damage to a target equal to Potency with no recourse, no resistance, and no allowance for being a supernatural creature. the only limit would be how many dice penalties the mage could take off her spellcasting pool while only using the one yantra she's allowed in a single turn of casting. It will do 4 aggravated wounds, each additional wound will reduce the mage's pool by 2. Against a hunter? probably a dead hunter, though the circumstances will probably invoke Paradox (that adept would only have one free Reach, so will be eating paradox dice for casting the spell instantly, for casting it at range, and for making the damage aggravated. That all adds up). But, as I say - most likely one dead hunter and one mage with some resistant bashing damage from absorbing the paradox.

                                        Against a Werewolf, though, it only works as long as they aren't transformed. Once a werewolf takes war form, they a) have an unexpectedly high number of Health dots and b) are no longer Size 5, so the mage has to take more dice penalties to cast on them to start with. They'll still do lots of agg, more than almost any other supernatural being is able to throw out, but it probably won't kill the werewolf.

                                        And the werewolf, already in war form, will immediately flip out into Death-Rage when they take those aggravated wounds. The mage will enjoy the moral victory of having hurt her foe badly, before her spleen goes on vacation elsewhere.

                                                Acceleration? No, it's Time 3 spell.

                                                If the mage knows she's fighting werewolves and has Spirit 2, she can Clash of Wills whenever someone uses a Gift on her, as well.

                                                Now, if the mage in our increasingly-unlikely scenario is a master (of any Arcanum)... Well... That's like the aggravated-damage adept scenario only more so. Instead of doing levels of damage, a master can Unmake or Make her Arcanum in the subject's pattern in fatal ways - if it's a Forces mage you're after, we'll say Unmaking Forces to remove all electrical activity from the poor sob that pissed off someone who lists "mastery of the universes' fundamental energies" on her resume.

                                                Unlike doing health boxes of damage, this one's actually withstood. Her Potency will be reduced, assuming she casts successfully to start with, by the subject's Stamina. If she has any successes left, the subject dies. Instantly. No matter how much health he has. Her Potency starts at 5, so she would instantly slay all but Olympic athletes, but we'll be generous and assume the person fighting her knows what she is so is going to spend willpower and raise his passive resistance to, say, 6 (assuming he had stamina 4). That's still only -2 dice, though, against a character who has a base dice pool of at least 10, and will probably be spending willpower herself on top of whatever the one yantra she's allowed in a single-turn spell uses. The Paradox will be about the same as the adept, but her foes' chance of survival is a lot slimmer. As with all passive resistances, Withstood spells do not give you more resistance for having a Supernatural Tolerance Trait. All that matters is your Stamina, give or take a +2 for burning Willpower.

                                                As before, however, if the werewolf's in war form he's probably got a superhuman Stamina, and like the adept she'll be bleeding dice to account for his massive size. If so, and the master casts her death spell but didn't build a potency high enough... Nothing happens. And then the werewolf kills her.
