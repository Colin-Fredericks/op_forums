    Something occurred to me recently. Mirrors was a very interesting book for pre-Chronicles nWoD. I think some of the concepts there made it to Chronicles, but I'm not sure. I know that the concept of Conviction and "skill tricks" which I remember there didn't, though. Has anyone tried to use those rules with Chronicles-era books?

      I know that some of the Skill Tricks are redundant or not very impressive when compared to 2ED stuff. They would need changes in order to keep up. Conviction itself is nice, but is likewise underwhelming when compared to all the power boosts that the splats have gotten so far in 2ED.

      For example: the Flashing Blades Skill Trick for Weaponry is covered in 2ED by Defensive Combat (Weaponry), the second Maneuver of the Heavy Weapons Style and the fourth Maneuver of the Light Weapons Style. For a 2ED version, Flashing Blades could instead give 8 Again to Dodging when you have your weapon drawn, increase the weapon's Damage Bonus by one, and increase your Defense by [your Weaponry/2 round up]. That does not make the aforementioned 2ED Merits obsolete, but also gives a new function to the Skill Trick which works well with Light Weapons, Heavy Weapons and Armed Defense.

                  *spins up arm sore from International Travel.* Well, first off, the main point of Mirrors is that its a tool kit, most of its material is as a result configurable or addable, such as revision initiative, trading Willpower to affect World Building (I love this one so much), and so on. that stuff is cheap and simple enough any revising is going to be simplistic and not really noteworthy and really acts as Addons or replacements to existing material.

                  Less easy is the "Mirrors" setting material. Woundgate, Bleeding Edge, and Infinite Macabre are all fascinating, but their mechanics often aren't often well defined, some exceptions exist, but in general, these things have issues due to being too sparse. They largely have good settings, but lack mechanics

                  Just going off of memory:
                  Woundgate needs more "stuff" plainly, and a good prefab list of Cities to draw from. I guess it's made for customization and to save costs.

                  Bleeding Edge just doesn't have enough Cyberware, I am so sorry and I understand that this was done to save costs, but having to reference other supernatural powers is a tad lazy and requires having other books to make use of it. It's especially bad because the cost of these powers was tied towards the cost of getting that power... ignoring that for many supernaturals the cost involves more than just paying Exp. I would also drop Willpower as a cost if you're going to charge Cyberware any resource cost and have universal batteries or something. I do like the Alienation effect though, that was pretty neat.

                  Infinite Macabre simply needs more detail with ships and a dedicated Ship to Ship combat system. I still feel upset fighters and battleships can get the same value on weapons and health was kinda shallow in a way since a fighter was 20 HP, but a battlestation was only 100. I'd prefer if there was different types of gear, largely to make more variety in spaceship combat.

                      Re:Mirrors

                      Mirrors is one of my favorite books in the nWoD/CofD line(but I am a sucker for game supplements that give you ways to tinker with base mechanics of the game). But in retrospect it is very much a book that was put out when the overall future of the gameline was really murky and they were trying to get some material out in case the line was canceled entirely. Many of the ideas presented could have easily been standalone books with development. Its pretty telling that many of the proposed mechanics in Mirrors (and other books like Danse Macabre) ended up in 2nd Edition.
                      That being said, I am going to go over my design notes and see if I can contribute some ideas that can give some of the presented ideas a bit more polish.

                      The Artificer's Workshop - A collection of Exalted Artifacts
                      Curios, Relics, and Tomes - A collection of Relics (Cursed and Otherwise)
                      The Horror Lab - A collection of Beasts, Monsters and less definable things.
                      Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
                      Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities

                          Going over the Dark Hero Template, two Masteries seemed especially inappropriate for 2ED: Powerful Success and Rapidity. Here are my proposed fixes:

                          Powerful Success: Reduce the number of Successes required for an Exceptional Success by (Arete). Each dot of Arete from 6 upward gives an automatic Success. For rolls where more Successes are their own reward, such as combat, add (Arete/2 round up) as bonus Successes, or (Arete) with 1 Willpower.

                          Rapidity x 1: If your Skill rating is equal or greater than that of an opponent, you may attempt to interrupt their roll, as long as they are within ([Arete + 1] x Speed) yards. You do not have to use the same Skill as the one that is being interrupted, but the ST decides which combinations are appropriate. Every Success you gain reduces their Successes by one. Should the opponent's Skill rating exceed yours, you may make up for the difference by spending 1 Willpower point. You may interrupt rolls a number of times per scene equal to Arete. Should the opponent have a power that would counter this, roll for a Clash Of Wills against the Dark Hero's Arete + Skill. If there is no opponent immediately present, divide the Target Number of Successes for Extended Actions by (Arete/2 round up) + 1 and divide the time taken per each roll by (Arete + 1), round down.

                          Rapidity x 2: Your Skill gains the Advanced Action benefit. If you reduce your opponent's Successes to zero, each Success you gain after that may be spent on your own counter-action. You may spend 1 Willpower point to immediately reduce his Successes to zero and then apply all of your Successes as a counter-action (this cannot be done if the opponent's Skill rating exceeds yours). If no opponent is immediately present, you may spend 1 Willpower point to make a number of rolls equal to Arete for Extended Actions take one turn. This may be used to complete one Extended Action much faster, or be distributed among multiple Extended Actions.

                                          Re: Augmentations

                                          I did a quick revision of my notes - these are fairly bare-bones mechanics that are light on examples and I'm still not certain what would be the best approach for blatantly inhuman capabilities. but have a look and lets see how we can fine tune these ideas.

                                          Augmentation

                                          Augmentations represent artificial modifications to a characters body that greatly enhance its capabilities, in certain cases to a superhuman degree. These augmentations typically represent cybernetic or genetic modification, but could be used to represent mystic alterations as well. The merits represent basic capabilities, how these manifest or are described is up to the storyteller and players to decide.
                                          The over-all number of dots in Augmentation merits a character can have is limited to (10 + the lower of Resolve or Stamina). In addition, if the total number in dots in Augmentation ever exceed the characters (Resolve + Composure), they suffer a -1 penalty to Breaking Point rolls. There is only so much strain the body and mind can withstand.

                                          Most supernatural creatures cannot take augmentation – their physiological and metaphysical natures often confound the complicated and delicate technologies needed to successfully apply augmentations. Supernatural beings that are still human (as least physiologically speaking) may be able to use Augmentations (at Storyteller’s discretion)

                                          Enhancement (••• or ••••)
                                          Enhancements improve an individual’s physical or neurological makeup. When this merit is selected, choose a physical or mental attribute. The character may spend a point of Willpower to gain a +1 increase to the chosen attribute, which effects derived values and may exceed the character dot limit in that attribute. This bonus lasts until the end of the scene. This merit may be selected multiple times, each selection applying to a different attribute. The 5 dot version increases the bonus to +2.
                                          Drawback: A dramatic failure while using the Enhancement causes it to malfunction. For the remainder of the scene, the Enhancement cannot be re-activated and rolls using the associated attribute suffer a -1 penalty.

                                          Implant (•+; Special)
                                          An Implant is an alteration that integrates an item or device into your physiology. This device can be nearly any weapon or technological piece of equipment. This merit may be taken multiple times, each time representing a different implant. The benefits and associated merit costs are as follow;
                                          Armor (•• or ••••): Implanted Armor gives a bonus the characters armor rating. All of these are considered to grant coverage of the legs, arms, and torso. The character gains armor 1/3 or 2/0 at two dots. The four dot version grants armor 2/4, but suffers a -1 penalty to defense.
                                          Equipment (• to ••): Implanted Equipment can be nearly any item that grants an equipment bonus or special feature. Such a device must be size 2 or less, and its cost in merit dots is equal to the higher of its Availability or Equipment Bonus.
                                          Merit: An implant can replicate the effects of a physical or mental merit at equivalent cost. It may also replicate the effects of the Striking Looks social merit. At the Storyteller’s discretion, an implant may be able to replicate supernatural merits as well.
                                          Weapons (• to ••): Weapon Implants are typically housed in characters limbs, but more exotic locations are possible. At one dots, the weapon may be a size 0 or 1 weapon with availability 2 or less. These devices are small and retractable, giving a +2 bonus to dice rolls when concealing the weapon. The two dot version allows size 2 weapons with availability 3 or less and reduces the concealment bonus to +1. The 3 dot version allows size 3 weapons with availability 3 or less; even with advanced miniaturization, such a device typically replaces the entire limb, is not concealable, and often impedes the use of that limb for actions other than using the weapon.

                                          Plugin (•to •••)
                                          A Plugin is an alteration that enhances the characters performance at a certain task. These often represent implants and modifications that greatly enhance capability within a somewhat narrow scope. When this merit is selected, choose an Attribute + Skill pair (such as Intelligence +Computers or Dexterity + Firearms) or an Attribute+ Attribute pair (such as Wits+ Composure) The character may spend a point of Willpower to gain an equipment bonus to the chosen pair equal to the dots in this merit until the end of the scene.
                                          Drawback: A dramatic failure while using the Plugin causes the enhancement to malfunction. For the remainder of the scene, the Plugin cannot be re-activated and rolls using the associated attribute + skill pair suffer a -1 penalty.


                                          The Artificer's Workshop - A collection of Exalted Artifacts
                                          Curios, Relics, and Tomes - A collection of Relics (Cursed and Otherwise)
                                          The Horror Lab - A collection of Beasts, Monsters and less definable things.
                                          Strange Places - A collection of Dark, Mysterious, and Wondrous Locations
                                          Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities
