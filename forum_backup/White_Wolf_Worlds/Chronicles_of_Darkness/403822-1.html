                        I actually wrote up a new Promethean line for the crossover game I'm running. I wanted an antagonist who wasn't out and out evil but still screwed with reality enough to give every splat a reason to care.

                        The Mender was originally a mage who died in surprisingly mundane circumstances. His wife stumbled on strange things while going through his belongings...grimoires, arcane symbols, notes on impossible things, things with ink that crawled and writhed. She understood none of it but knew it was power. She made up her own shorthand of the symbols, took notes from her own limited understanding, assumed things that were patently wrong and from what little pop-culture magic she knew. She recognized the power, but didn't know how to use it and furthermore didn't care. Body parts came from other dead mages, found by recognizing symbols and learning signs. Her makeshift ritual worked...sort of.

                        Supernal Magic apparently doesn't mix well with the Divine Fire.

                        From the wreckage of the building stepped forth the unfortunate progenitor of a new lineage (whose name I havent figured out yet).

                        To prevent this being a lonnnnnng post, I'll condense the necessary bits.

                        Disfigurements: The Mender is twitchy and looking at him too long gives people a headache. When his disfigurements are revealed, the reason is obvious. His twitchiness turns into something reminiscent of slow-frame filming. Parts of his body phase in and out, flickering between representations of Supernal planes of existence. In one viewing, an observer could be assaulted by visions of the Mender's bones and decay from Stygia or see bestial fur from the Primal Wild's from wrist to shoulder alone. When they move, afterimages are left, different versions of his body moving out of synch with one another.

                        Wasteland: Staying in one place too long, the Mender's presence starts to break down causality and normal laws of reality. Initially, its small things like disproportionately bad luck or things not bouncing in the direction physics would predict. Then matches start flaring into fireballs when struck and supernatural powers fizzle out or go wild. Visions similar to the disfigurements flicker over normal objects. FInally, the world seems to fall apart, despite the opposite happening: the Supernal realms are being drawn down simultaneously but separately. A tree is suddenly at the same time a skeletal dead thing and a wild dryad while the spirit of the tree writhes in angry confusion. It's as if we'd been wearing red-and-blue 3d glasses all our lives and someone suddenly took them away.

                        Disquiet: I actually borrowed this from one of the Mage antagonist books: the Mender's disquiet resembles the False Awakening. On the plus side, it affects slightly fewer people more slowly than most other lineage's disquiet, but the effects are disastrous. Those affected have a sudden revelation and Awaken into great and terrible power. They believe they have become Extempore on the Path of Pyros, the Watchtower of the Divine Fire. As their powers grow exponentially, they see flickers of the Mender, even if he has passed on, offering escape and greater power and freedom. They become compelled to enact a ritual to PHYSICALLY ascend into their Watchtower which is, sadly, patently impossible. The resulting explosion and firestorm wreaks havoc and leaves echoes in every realm, and the local Twilight and Underworlds and even the Hedge feel it.

                            Got sick of the Transhuman thread being top of the page (I know, I was last person to post in it; that just makes me even more so).

                            Anyway, I thought of a Lineage called the Flayed; I won't try to write them up properly until the second edition comes out, but this is what I brainstormed:

                            More than one Lineage has begun with the attempt to resurrect someone - in this case, a man sacrificed at an Aztec temple long before the Europeans came. Why the priest chose to attempt this is a matter of conflicting legend: more modern Prometheans, living in a Christian milieu, suggest he was a young priest wracked with guilt; others say he was a friend or lover of the dead man, or that he was a sorcerer with darker uses for a body brought back from Mictlan. Regardless, he stitched the sacrifice back together, replaced the parts that were lost, and called upon the Sun to grant new life. The answer came from something else, but something just as bright.

                            The Flayed are most "common" in Mexico, Central America, and the Southern US. Their generative act requires mutilating a body in a manner consistent with Aztec sacrifices, though fortunately, they can use one that's already dead (note that this is just the motions needed to make a new Promethean - none of them practice Aztec religion in any meaningful sense these days). A new Flayed can be made mostly from a single body, though the heart must come from another. While I'm calling them "Flayed" for now, their Disfigurement is actually that they look half-eaten. Their Humour is Sanguine, though their Element is characterised as Wind rather than Air.

                            Many Flayed come into the world with a sense of purpose - the idea of some concrete "quest" they must complete as part of their Pilgrimmage. In modern times, those who feel this usually associate it with the person who provided their heart, and believe they must complete some business on this individual's behalf to set their ghost to rest (whether this is true or just a superstition is a question to be left unanswered).

                            The Lineage began with the profaning of the body of a holy sacrifice, and this is reflected in their Disquiet and Torment. Those affected by Disquiet feel guilty and judged and resentful, and lash out at the Promethean for making them feel that way (and tend to be self-destructive as well). Flayed suffering Torment become antinomian, transgressing against every barrier they can - or maybe they should become bloody, revelling in spilled blood, excised hearts, and eaten flesh (basically, the surface violence of the old sacrifices, without the organised ritual or deeper spiritual meaning) - I'm not sure yet. I'm also not sure what their Wasteland should be, except that air and water should both have a rusty (aka bloody) tang.


                            And inspired by the slasher demiurge thread, the Untimely:

                            The Untimely were also an attempt at resurrection. Someone whose family had been murdered desired revenge, an obsession that might have made them an Avenger slasher, but took a different course. Instead of using their loss to fuel their revenge, they meant to bring their family back to exact their revenge for their loss. Thus the Lineage has more than one Progenitor (or else the demiurge only managed to make a Promethean from one of them). It would also likely be a very young Lineage - a PC might easily be the first.

                            Untimely must be made from the body of someone who's been murdered - less difficult than it might sound at first, given that the maker can murder someone herself - though this is considered the lazy option (and may be dangerous too; see below). Their humour is similar to melancholy, but with more of a deathly tinge - I'm unsure whether to call it Morbid or Elegiac. Their element is metal, the substance of which weapons are usually made. Their disfigurements are subtle: just an exaggerated version of their death wounds, constantly leaking blood and other fluids (though this doesn't harm the Promethean, it does normally mean the wound is visible even through clothing).

                            All Untimely must grapple with the question of how to relate to the murder of their body's former owner - some decide on their answer very quickly, others may take many years. There's no right answer either: one might decide she must avenge it, another might decide it has little to do with her, a third might even look for a way to reward the killer for the act that enabled her existence (the first of these options is why a Promethean is often wary of killing someone to make offspring).

                            Untimely Disquiet is direct: the affected begins to fantasise about killing the Promethean. Because they're unlikely to be a murderer at heart, they'll try to rationalise those feelings as being something else, but will still start following and stalking the Promethean nonetheless. Torment is about revenge: the Promethean will reflexively strike back against slights, her own or those of others, she won't care. [Functionally, Disquiet and Torment put those affected onto the edge of being slashers] The Wasteland corrodes and tarnishes metal, and nothing ever seems to get clean (bloodstains especially linger).


                            And a bit more about the two I posted earlier...

                            Many Dumuz believe they are an older version of the Tammuz (not all Tammuz disagree either, though some consider the Dumuz the younger Lineage, warped in the image of the Inanna legend, which had become attached to the Tammuz some time after their creation); others believe the two aren't related by anything but the coincidence of sharing an Element (some Tammuz who hold this view trace their own origin to the Golem of Prague, though tellings vary about whether their Progenitor was truly made from clay, or from the bodies of Jews who had been murdered by Gentiles - such a desecration of the dead would explain a lot about Prometheans).

                            Regardless of origin, Dumuz must be made from the body of a single person - the generative act requires it be but into eight pieces (thus seven cuts, representing the seven gates of the Dark House), and covered with a mixture of earth from a grave and a grainfield. Their disfigurement makes them look skeletal, crusted with dirt and mud, and rimed with grave mold - witnesses may mistake them for some sort of zombie, though those who have encountered actual zombies will realise the Dumuz is something else.

                            Many Dumuz believe that their soul already exists, and the Pilgrimmage is about finding it rather than creating it - this is likened to the Descent into the Underworld. [Whatever the 2nd Edition Osirians get that lets them visit the Underworld multiple times, if it's different from Revivication, the Dumuz should probably get it too]

                            A Dumuz's Disquiet is elegiac: she prompts thoughts of the affected's loved ones; the affected see these people reflected in her, and get disappointed and angry when she doesn't live up to them. Her Torment is still: she becomes inert and finds it almost impossible to rouse herself to any action at all, which may even apply to self-defence; as if she were dead, in other words. The Wasteland resembles that of the Tammuz.

                            The Delphine believe they descend from an Oracle of Delphi (one from before the period described in the great myths). Most of them believe that their demiurge and Progenitor were the same woman - that she called the Divine Fire into her own flesh to better understand the mysteries between death, life, and divinity - whether she thought it was worth it is lost to time, but those who pay too much attention to prophecy tend to suffer for it, so probably not.

                            The generative act of the Delphine must be performed during daytime - the body must be brought out from an underground place and into the light of the Sun - this respect to Apollo is more important than the specific treatments used to raise the body. Delphine disfigurements are more unearthly than most Prometheans' - their bodies seem to darken almost to shadow, and appear wreathed in gas and vapour - witnesses tend to be left with a headache. Their Element is Vapour, and their Humour Ecstatic (there's some debate on whether these are actually different from Air and Sanguine, or if the Delphine are simply trying to distinguish themselves from the Galateid).

                            I can't recall off the top of my head whether 2nd Edition Prometheans are getting Bestowments or similarly unique powers. If they are, then the Delphine's would be oracular visions, naturally.

                            Those affected by a Delphine's Disquiet are convinced that she knows too much - they might be convinced she's spying on them or following them, or the suspicion may be more formless - and of course it's shaped by the nature of whatever secrets the affected possesses (people who share secrets may suspect one another of betraying them to the Promethean). A Delphine's Torment is madly ecstatic, unfiltered by wisdom or judgement, blurting out whatever words or actions cross her mind. In a Delphine's Wasteland, the air becomes stagnant and thick, with almost visible vapours around any gap in the ground - shadows seem deeper, while dreams and the corner of the eye seem to display flashes related to whatever decisions the individual is grappling with (though they provide confusuon rather than clarity).

                                                    Here's what I managed to save from the Princess Promethean. Bestowment, when I get around to it, is about accessing the Akashic Record for information and postcognition, he's also an ex-Centimanus who Calcified the Solvent Alimbic; he wants to save other Freaks from themselves, and kept his ability to neutralize their powers so they stop long enough for them to listen. His Humor is Dreamstuff (giving him an acute sense of his own hidden urges and the web of fate that underlies the world), his Torment is recklessness and self-punishment, and his Wasteland is things losing their solidity and everything becoming uncertain-at extreme ends, creatures from the Dreamlands start unknowingly passing in and out of reality. Didn't decide on Disquiet, but I feel it manifests as a growing sense of him constantly judging you-and then rage at his "hypocrisy" when people inevitably realize he is flawed as they are.


                                                    Thevetat, The Mirror's Mendicant

                                                    Quote: "The true bearer of this body was starved and mummified alive to be free of karma. I am that which he left behind."

                                                    Devadatta was a monk. Devadatta was wise. And Devadatta was the cousin of Siddhartha Gautama, the Buddha, himself-but Devadatta did not have Gautama's humility or freedom from materialistic wants of fame. And as Gautama became famous and loved, Devadatta, who had spent his entire life as a monk and had awakened his spiritual power through long practice, he became jealous of the prince who had only known happiness until that fateful day where he saw a crippled man, and had found the enlightenment that eluded Devadatta anyway. Was he not many times his cousin's senior? Didn't he deserve to be rewarded for his faith and long suffering in search of inner peace? When the Buddha rejected his cousin's request for becoming the abbot of his monastery, recognizing the growing inner darkness and enslavement to personal wants, Devadatta snapped, and his jealousy made him a monster. Three times he tried to kill his cousin (once through assassins, once through a bolder, and a third by a monstrous elephant), and one time tried to fracture his own monastery into feuding factions to ensure that if he could not lead the others, then at least neither would Gautama. All failed, and eventually his karma grew so wicked and dark that it afflicted his current life, cursing him with a terrible and invariably fatal illness. But Devadatta was still wise, and saw that this was precisely what he deserved, to attack his cousin and teacher out of selfish spite. He sincerely begged for forgiveness, and the Buddha gave it-not nearly enough to purify his karma except through a thousand unhappy lives and more than one term in Naraka's deepest levels, but pure enough for him to be destined to find the elusive enlightenment at the end of his penance.

                                                    The Created who names himself after the Theosophical Society's own moniker for the envious monk sees in himself something akin to his namesake's repentant lives-to him falls the duty of balancing the evil karma left behind by his demiurge. For Thevetat was meant to be wise and a monk by a girl who felt she deserved a guide to elusive enlightenment-and because mummified a holy man alive to do that, he is a monster who is painfully aware he is neither.

                                                    Her name was Advika Desai, and the Crown's Custodian convinced her that her name ("unique lord", if read as given name-surname) was a sign of prophecy. She was not always a vain girl, or even a spoiled one as so many would be True Heirs of the Kingdom of Light are said to be. She was born rich, but her parents walked the tightrope between spoiling and neglecting their daughter admirably, and given how she was always a genius (she was capable of reading college-level textbooks at ten), her future seemed bright. Then the Darkness came and ruined her father's IT business with a Darkspawn virus, and so she had to become a crank author, disguising what she knew to be complete bullshit about indigo children and alien angels in actual scientific terminology and intellect to create several relative bestsellers that made just enough to avoid starving or darker avenues of income. She found something that overcame her growing cynicism though, in the form of a modern Theosophist's theories on the spiritual development of humans, and how older, more racist versions of the philosophy-religion were actually compounding the problem. This agreed with what she knew of her family's Buddhism, and set her heart in political activism-and making her brain a fertile ground for Blossoming. But she was still so young-barely sixteen when the Light settled around her, and with the emotional development to match. Overwhelmed by her sudden thrust into an endless war against a force of entropy disincarnate, she clinged to the comforting whispers of the Queen of Mirrors as the only way to hold on to her sanity-things would get better, the Child Queen promised. You yourself will do it, with your wisdom, she said. In time, Advika came to believe she was the fated herald of Sanat Kumara, ruler of Shamballah the grandmaster of the Great White Brotherhood who had revealed the teachings of Theosophy to the world, the highest of the Lords of the Flame who inspired the image of Lucifer and his allies as angels of purest Light (unfairly, according to Theosophy, but perhaps an understandable error given what she later did). In time, she would become enlightened enough to communicate with the Ancient of Days consciously, and become the World Teacher who would guide the destined metamorphosis of humanity into a spiritually aware people with no need or fear of the Darkness. The Queen simply smiled and nodded, saying nothing but that a Hierophant is a kind of teacher, too.

                                                    Five years after this revelation, in the wake of a most terrible battle, Advika became impatient. Yes, suffering brings enlightenment and all, but was enlightenment worth the continuing deaths of her friends? The Queen gave no answer but a familiar refrain; "Hard Choices Means You Aren't Looking Hard Enough". She did not mean to patronize, and somehow did not-rather, Advika took it to heart. The problem, she decided, wasn't that she would have to bear tragedy after tragedy in hopes of one day finally opening her sight enough to behold brilliant Sanat. No, her problem, just as it was to the initial students of the Great White Brotherhood, was that she did not have an enlightened teacher of any kind to begin with-no living human had been yet so free of karma to follow the inner pathways to Shamballah within the heart, and still remain capable of speaking with their less enlightened species.

                                                    Sokushinbutsu, the Japanese practice of living mummification, was a way of achieving the enlightenment so required tenfold. A monk nearing the end of his life would seal himself in a tomb and deny his own thirst while eating only a diet that would not feed any fat or muscle on his body, overcoming attachment in total so that his reincarnation would be that much more evolved. The successful ones died, of course-but if the mummy's soul could be brought back...

                                                    Of course, most sokushinbutsu were willing. Technically, the priest that Advika chose gave his consent to become her Light-reanimated mentor, but given how copious use of Speechio was involved, the "willing" part is dubious. Regardless, a much-loved prison missionary became the holy corpse his Princess needed, and in the caverns beneath a waterfall, his mummy was carefully impaled on three-faced phurba knives in image of the Crucifixion, and Advika's attendants sung holy songs as she bathed his body in the Light of her Charms, bidding the sun to impart its vitality to dead flesh, stopping only to dine on plain, raw rice and the purified water that splashed into the cave. Three days of prayer and magic later, in a flash of Pyros, the light congealed and settled across the seven major chakras, slowly fading into vital fluids as the corpse de-mummified just enough to live. Two tense, twitching minutes later, the Promethean that would one day be Thevetat opened once-dried eyes.

                                                    And it was two days later that Advika faced the disappointment that all demiurges must-yes, the mummy could access knowledge beyond mortal ken easily, yes he had an insightful nature, but his mind was not that of the priest's; in fact, were it not for his ability to walk and talk, he might have well been an ignorant newborn. His Disquiet fed upon her frustrations and hidden horror at the realization she had effectively murdered an innocent man via torture for nothing, and she came to believe her creation was a foul spirit sent by the Brotherhood's enemies, the Lords of the Dark Face, to steal her teacher's body and deny her birthright at the last moment, and she conspired to kill the failed teacher and try again. But she wasn't misguided in her beliefs that her creation would have great spiritual power-in particular, his ability to draw on knowledge from beyond to protect himself from mystics that intended him harm. By the time she arrived with fire and spell, the mummy was long gone, having taken advantage of his Pyros-infused biology to survive the waterfall's rocks and heal from a passing thunderstorm. Also gone was her notes on his creation-he knew enough about the priest's life to known one must first know himself before teaching anything, and that seemed to be the best source. Coming to the end, as Disquiet set in, he came to agree with her-he was a dark spirit who was never human, but faced with his demiurge's selfishness, he also believed it was her own ignorance and attachment to a dream based on nothing more than her own hopes and belief to be what called him and forced him into a flesh that was not his; a mindless tulpa conjured from her own misconceptions and ignorance, only given true thought and the capacity to believe in his own existence when the priest's dead flesh gave him a brain to more fully believe in himself. In other words-he was the product of a complete failure and a stillborn dream, the congealed avatar of spiritual ignorance.

                                                    He almost decided the most moral thing to do was to remove himself from the world right there and trouble it no more, but then his Elpis quickened, and in his first vision, revealed to him one final truth that Advika and her Queen had ignored-that darkness and spiritual ignorance is not the opposite of light and enlightenment, but its shell. By the existence of evil, is good defined and capable of evolving, the dance of Flux and Pyros. Was it really so hard to believe that a Promethean, born of the shell of humanity, incapable of of filling that shell with humanity-and then shedding it altogether?

                                                    And so did Thevetat, then named for what he thought was the dragon that misled the people of Mu and indirectly begin the ancient war that destroyed the previous race of humans (and as he became more familiar with actual Buddhism and its myth, he has seen no reason to change it) begin his Pilgrimage, the path from the ignorance of an existence completely defined by the physical and into the New Dawn of a soul-and with it, the potential for true enlightenment and wisdom.

                                                    Description: While the priest whose body became him was a devout Buddhist, neither he nor the new occupant of his flesh are of Asian ethnicity-he was actually a black Californian who converted in high school and never looked back. Still, Thevetat is a devout Buddhist himself, and takes as much pains to appear as the traveling monk he so desperately wants to be-when he can get away with it, he wears the distinct ochre robes that hang loose from his right arm, revealing a set of scars that he's sustained both from the natural dangers of Promethean existence and from his occasional flirtations with self-flagellation (a Christian and Islamic practice rather than Buddhist, but he's a practical man when it comes to purification-he will do anything that seems to work). When he can't due to weather or evading human hunters, he favors sturdy traveler's clothes what hide his appearance as totally as he can manage, but in all he wears a bindi, a red dot in the center of the forehead. This isn't due to any fashion statement on his part (in truth, he thinks of the bindi as a feminine item-any male wearing it is cross-dressing, he feels, which is all fine and dandy for them but he isn't a fan of not dressing your preferred gender), but a philosophical one-the bindi symbolically represents the beginning of creation, which he technically is; when and if his creation is finished and he becomes Redeemed, he fully intends to get rid of it, as it is unneeded as a marker.

                                                    When his Disfigurements are visible, Thevetat looks a great deal like an Osirian; his skin is pallid and leathery, his teeth are yellowed, and his nails black and uneven. He looks more alive, though-his eyes, the soft tissues of his mouth (including his lips), and his blood look exactly like they do when his disfigurements are hidden, giving people the uncomfortable sense that he is actually a living man wearing a skintight corpse. Here and there, cracks appear in his skin-when he charges an Alimbic, they release a gaseous blue light that drifts up to his head and forms a halo (though it takes him only a second's thought to cancel this, thankfully for his stealth).
