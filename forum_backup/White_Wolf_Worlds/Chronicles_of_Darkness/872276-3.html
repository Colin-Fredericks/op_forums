    Not sure the Jewish one should even focus on Jewish theology, per se.

    How about a Sect that is focused more on protecting and propagating the Jewish people, community and traditions in a vampiric context? Maybe a Carthian-related group that emulates mid-century Israeli kibbutzim?

    They could see themselves as both defenders of the Jewish community in the direct sense of fighting anti-Semites, but also providing a better environment for Jewish vampires.

    So, they would enter smaller cities where life is hard for a vampire and build communal systems of blood gathering, storage, and distribution that is done in as ethical a manner as possible. I can picture long, complex debates of what constitutes an ethical system for blood gathering, but that would become an interesting part of the story. Whatever the methods, it would be their version of making the desert bloom... and the underlying assumption for all these debates would be that one should live by the law, not die by it.

    They could help vampires establish havens that are ethically acquired and pleasant to reside in, not stolen squats or rancid sewers. This could be where much of the communal life of the community occurs.

    The other service they would provide is simple networking. Just because you're a vampire doesn't mean your mortal beliefs and practices vanish. For lot of Jewish practices a minyan is needed. It could be nearly impossible, except in a very few cities, to put together a minyan of Jewish vampires. For most Jews, even if you're a vampire, it's nice to know that if you have an unfortunate sunlight-related accident, somebody, somewhere will be saying Kaddish and sitting Shiva.

    On a pastoral level a community that is focused on personal ethics and maintaining ties to the mortal community could also be helpful with the retention of humanity. Even vampires from the goyim might find benefits from a fairly level-headed, practical community of vampires.

    Of course, it's hard to imagine all of this happening in the context of the more Orthodox interpretations of law, but the Reformed and Reconstructionist? Maybe.

    I guess I'm saying make it less a religious cult and more a late-night JCC.

                                        Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                                        Or add another vampire prophet with the justification that Muhammad was the final human prophet.
                                        Hmmm, not inconceivable given that we're dealing with evil, blood sucking monsters... but, for anyone who had even a nominal relationship to Islam in their breathing days, the idea that revelation is sealed is a pretty big deal.

                                        The idea is that everything that needed to be revealed was revealed, and within that revelation it said unambiguously that revelation wassealed. Anyone who comes along afterward saying otherwise might seem like they were calling the Quran (and therefore the divine source of the Quran) either capable of making major mistakes in what is revealed, or of lying in those revelations. This implication would be a non-starter for nearly anyone, anywhere in the umma, alive or undead.

                                        Now, that said, having a small group of individual vampires come together and form a new Madhhab, or school of interpretation of Islam, and, therefore, a new interpretation of fiqh, or human understanding of divine law, could be possible. There are Surahs that say the Prophet was sent to teach both humanity and the Jinn, so if the vampiric school of Islamic jurisprudence conceptualized the Beast as a personal Jinn... well, I don't know enough about Muslim folklore or scripture to take all that to its logical conclusion, but I think it could work.

                                        The main problem I can see is that, like any field of jurisprudence, new rulings are ordinarily based on precedent, and different mortal Madhhab have different existing precedent, though there are large areas of overlap. This means that they would first need to decide which school the Vampiric Madhhab grew from, so there was a body of precedent to work with. This choice would immediately tend to exclude, or at least discourage, vampires with sympathies to other schools. It's hard to imagine getting Sufi, Salafi and Ahmadiyya vampires to exist side by side in a single religion-based vampiric organization, even if they are all blood-drinkers.

                                            And the idea that Jesus didn't die for your sins is likewise a theoretical non-starter for many Christians who go undead. And yet, its a thing. Vampire religions don't have to hold onto even the central doctrine of human religions. That's not what they do.

                                            Having a vampire who lived at the same time as Muhammad could work. Or having Muhammad become a vampire after finishing his farewell pilgrimage. Or even when alive just having "secret teachings" that never made it to mortal ears. We could even just go neutral and call the final prophet, well, The Prophet and leave the exact nature of who this was a mystery and let vampires argue it out ICly. Lead to divisions and internal strife. That kind of thing is good for the stories, after all.

                                            From a game design standpoint, who delivered the teachings is frankly unimportant. Vampire games are concerned with the night-to-night affairs, and so what's important is the night-to-night operation of the vampires who follow this faith, not the details of historical theology. Given the nature of Vampire as a city-based game, and how each and every city should have a different take on the core system? Going into these details are actually detrimental instead of beneficial.

                                            Should there be a central prophet figure from the time of Muhammad, possibly the man himself? Yes. Even if its a different person, should the actions of this vampire-Prophet mirror Muhammad? Yes, but as a distorted mirror. And what did He teach to the jinn, who had set themselves up as gods? What actions differ? What precepts should there be? These are the important details that seem to be glossed over in favor of arguing about Muhammad or not.

                                            From a purely game design standpoint, we cannot have vampires following the exact same tenants as actual Islam. That's not how Vampire works, and it would be a disaster anyways. There has to be deviation. What's important is finding what those deviations are, and how.

                                                My personal approach has been to: 1) set aside the law-aspect per se considering that most Islamic norms don't work on vampires and they would even seem impure (blood in corpse) fiqh wise and I found it boring and plain weird to just invent a vampire version of Shari'ah; 2) focus on the concept of being tasked to remind mortals of God; 3) avoid to implicate the Prophet - peace be upon him - explicitly in all this, but that is my personal thing as a Muslim to avoid disrespecting (even in fiction) or lying about (even if fiction) what the Prophet (peace be upon him) did.

                                                Point 3 is obviously totally personal, so you may disregard it, and, on a note, in my take on the Longinus story I try to keep Jesus (peace be upon him) equally out of it, again, out of respect (even in running a game) for God's prophets (peace be upon them all). Of course, the story for the LeS is that it was through the blood of Christ that Longinus was cursed, but that can be dealt with. First, Jesus need not have been intending that to happen. It could be God's curse. Second, it may simply be the LeS belief, while in reality Longinus may have been embraced by someone else and mixed up things or lied.

                                                That being said, one could totally make a Muslim covenant whose founders CLAIMED to have received special instructions from the Prophet sall Allahu alayhi wasallam.

                                                I personally didn't want to simply repropose among vampires the same divisions humans have. I thought it nicer to think of the LeS as something so unique to vampires that it let its adherents put totally in second place the issue of which Abrahamic religion the vampire used to follow. It makes sense though that even Christians (non-Longinians), Jew and Muslim vampires have each their own covenants, but I just prefer avoiding that in my Chronicle, unless they're small, unorganized groups of vampires who don't reach the size and structure of an international Covenant.

                                                I was reading the Testament of Longinus recently though and there are explicit mentions of the divinity of Jesus, even as something that a Longinian MUST believe (see the Rule of Golgotha). Now, that's clearly a problem for Jews and Muslims. However, they could "neglect" that specific part of the Testament. Both Jew and Muslim Longinians for example could argue that Longinus used the term Son of God in the sense of an extremely dear and honored servant of God, or Muslim Longinians may say that even the Testament of Longinus, while being a revelation, was subject to alteration and that one should disregard in it those things that are denied by the Quran, while accepting the rest, thus putting aside the issue of divinity of Jesus. This would clearly create some tension in the LeS, but overall they would remain within the fold of the LeS, with the tensions being more or less intense depending on the city one is in. This is pretty much how the Iblic and Dammitic creed are dealt with in the LeS covenant book.

                                                Another alternative comes to my mind too, but that suits probably Muslim players more than others. There's a story (in the real world, not in WoD) about a Companion of the Prophet (peace be upon him) who happened to actually ingest the Prophet's blood. He had been ordered to bury it, like Muslims do with bodily parts that are detached from the body like nails, etc., but instead he decided to ingest it hoping it would be blessed (the Prophet's body is reported to have had miraculous properties, such as his sweat having a beautiful smell, his touch leaving things perfumed, his saliva having healing properties, ecc) and he was told that, due to having ingested the blood, even if he should not have done it (since he had been told to bury it), the Fire (i.e.: Hell) would not touch him. Now, this runs opposite to the Longinus narrative in the LeS: taste Jesus' blood and get cursed, even if there is a significant distinction: Longinus tasted the blood out of gluttony, laziness and perversion, while that Companion ingested it out of over-whelming love and faith (like the lady who touches Jesus' robe and is healed in the Gospels, just slightly weirder...).

                                                The two stories of Longinus and this Companion could have been merged by a uniquely Muslim vampire covenant (not necessarily the only Muslim covenant) who claims that Longinus' curse was a mercy from God to the Kindred, that they may be guided in their vampiric state, while they could have that blood of the Prophet (peace be upon him), transmitted through his descendants (so it could be a Shi'i inclined covenant, but not necessarily: many sunnis equally revere the Prophet's descendants, they just don't agree that they had to be the political leaders of the community by necessity), has the power to cure vampirism or lessen the curse somehow, provided one takes it out of reverence and faith and not meaning to harm the Prophet's descendants (who may themselves be immune to vampirism, dying automatically if they're embraced and not becoming ghoul even if force-fed vitae). So Longinus is there to guide the Kindred, while the Ahl al-Bayt (the Prophet's descendants) are there to lessen their curse or remove it if they're repentant and faithful. This Covenant would vow itself to protect the Ahlu l-Bayt through their vampiric might, until they are considered ready to receive pardon and taste enough prophetic blood to return human. While on the journey to redemption through service of the Ahlu l-Bayt, they could have benefits similar to the Coils of the Dragon (lessen the effects of the vampiric curse), but obtained through imbibing small portions of the descendants' blood, willingly offered to their protectors.

                                                This Covenant would obviously be seen as heretical by the LeS, even if it accepts Longinus' message for vampires who do not feel the call to redemption through service to the Ahlu l-Bayt.

                                                So, to recap, this Covenant sees two ways for vampires: either a vampire sticks to Longinus' way (be a wolf among the sheep to remind them of God) or he vows himself to service and protection of the Ahlu l-Bayt until he is pardoned and returned human (already in a state of grace for Paradise once natural death comes, probably). The Ahlu l-Bayt's blood, given as a gift and not taken forcefully, and in small quantities bestows different powers that somehow lift or reduce aspects of the curse (like the Coils of the Dragon does, even if it works differenty) until it is completely lifted. These powers could be called Barakaat (blessings) and they are imparted by the Ahlu l-Bayt themselves by some prayer done at the moment of giving the blood, and they also know through kashf (unveiling, a type of inspiration or visions that saints/walis have) when the vampire is ready to receive the ultimate blessing of returning human. In this sense, these Barakaat are more similar to the gifts of the Uratha (bestowed by spirits or by the Moon) than to vampiric disciplines (cultivated by the vampire).

                                                        Btw, if anyone is wondering why would someone need to bury the Prophet's (peace be upon him) blood... The Prophet used to practice and receive hijamah (wet cupping, where tiny cuts are done on specific parts of the body and then air-compressed cups are applied on those body parts to let the superficial stagnant blood seep out), so the blood was that left in the cup after the cupping, if I remember the story correctly. Wet cupping is still practiced worldwide, just like dry cupping (which comes from chinese acupuncture and is more popular). The difference is just whether one lets the blood out or not. Note that it's not the same as European bloodletting, as the amount of blood, the places where the blood is taken from, the methods employed to extract it, the medical theory behind it and even the frequency with which it is performed (hijamah is performed on specific days and not too frequently) are totally different. The only thing in common between the two is the idea of removing "old" or "stagnant" blood.
