    Attributes and skills conceptual reach: How to truly grasp their limit?

    05-11-2021, 06:21 PM
    Hello everyone!

    I doubt this has probably been discussed already, but I could not find anything satisfactory on the subject.
    I’m currently running a weekly campaign using the Chronicles of Darkness rules and I plan on jumping on Vampire the Requiem as soon as I’m done with the current one.

    ​I have recently realised that the more I think about some of the attributes and skills, the less I can find myself really interpreting how they differs. Of course, the physical ones are obvious, but the social and mental one are the one that makes me question my whole grasp of their conceptual reach.

    Before I lay out my actual confusions, I will try to present how I understand each of the following attributes that puzzles me:
    • Intelligence: As an attribute of power, I view intelligence as the raw computing power of the brain. It is about memory, logic and knowledge. When I think of high intelligence, I think about doctors, math majors, Engineer, etc.
    • Wits: As an attribute of finesse, I view wits as a way of adapting, react, picking-up on cues. As the stats of both defense and perception, I also view wits as an ‘’awareness’’ kind of stats. When I think of high wits, I think about investigators, social workers, or survivalist.
    • Resolve: As an attribute of resistance, I view Resolve as your inner strength, your determination, and focus. When I think about high resolve, I think about those who persevere despite hardship. For example, single struggling parents that keep doing everything they can to keep their kids fed.
    • Presence: As an attribute of power, I see Presence as your raw charisma, your assertiveness, your ability to be heard. When I think of high presence, I think about famous politicians, Celebrities, Orator.
    • Manipulation: As an attribute of finesse, I view manipulation has an aptitude with words, as an ability to make others change their mind without realising it wasn’t their idea and as a way of veiling your intentions. When I think of high manipulation, I think of skilled politician, car salesman, Lawyers.
    • Composure: As an attribute of resistance, I view composure has your ability to remain calm in stressful situation, has a way to keep a cool head with all hell is breaking lose. When I think of high composure, I think about movie heroes that always keep their cool, or the cool guy at school that can brush off any insult.
    Now, this all seems fine and good, but the more I think about it, and how they interact with skills in general, I feel like those attributes are too general or broad and that them seems to be conceptual overlap which makes me confused as to which attribute, I should key of certain actions in some specific moment. Lets go over some cases that confused me:
    • Intelligence Vs Wits: This one I though I had a grasp on, until I read some example in the skill description section.
      • Investigation: investigating a scene keyed of Intelligence + Investigation, but that examining a scene and identifying patterns called a wits+investigation rolls. Wouldn’t it be more appropriate to have ‘’investigating’’ a scene be based on wits instead?
    • Presence vs Manipulation: This one I’ve always had a bit of trouble drawing true distinction. When I think about the definition, I can definitely see how they differ, but when I apply them to skills, the differences start to get muddied a bit. At lot of the time, it seems like a matter of ‘’approach’’ rather than an actual difference.
      • For example:
        • Intimidation: The samples actions are clears and quite enlightening, a stare down calls for presence and subtle threat calls for manipulation but the interrogation action calls for manipulation instead of presence. Why? Wouldn’t both be of any use in that case? Is this because I should have a police kind of interrogation in mind where they use dirty trick to make you talks? Wouldn’t it also work by using presence to inspire fear? If ‘’Interrogation’’ just means getting information by tricking them, wouldn’t it be better to ask for a subterfuge roll?
        • Persuasion: That skill in its own puzzle me, but I’ll come back to it later. Why is seduction specifically manipulation, instead of presence? Why is an impassioned plea presence instead of manipulation?
        • Socialize: The action of ‘’Carousing’’ call for a Presence+ Socialize/Streetwise, but in Socialize, one of the sample actions that was given was ‘’carouse’’ and it calls for a manipulation + socialize roll.
    • Manipulation vs Composure: The difference in those two attributes is quite star, but both seems to overstep each other when it comes to hiding emotions. Under attribute, the sample action for manipulation is ‘’poker face’’, but in the subterfuge skill, it call for a composure + manipulation rolls.
    • Resolve vs Composure: That one seems mostly fine to me, I just wanted to make sure I utterly understood the difference. Would it be right to say that resolve is your mental fortitude while composure is your emotional fortitude? That way when skills and power affect the way you think it gets resisted by your resolve, while skills and power that try to change the way you feel, it gets resisted by composure. Am I correct?`

    For my final confusion: Persuasion. It’s clear, it knows what it should do, it’s to make people change their mind. How is it then that, so many other skills seems to accomplish what it does?
    • Expression: The ‘’Argument’’ action, which call for an intelligence + Expression, can convince people of the truth (or apparent) of your words. Also, why does it keys intelligence and not manipulation?
    • Subterfuge: The ‘’Fast Talk’’ action calls for a manipulation + subterfuge roll, but it’s a sample action of persuasion.
    • Empathy: Soothing someone’s nerve can be achieved through empathy.
    Am I alone in my confusion? Anyone willing to give me some guidance? I hope I’m not being too picky on examples; I’m really trying to get a firm sense of which attributes and skills should be used in given situation and I always feel like I’m struggling. I’d like to avoid solution that just end up letting anyone using any attributes they feel like whenever they can by changing a word or two as I feel it would encourage dumping stats over others. After all, why would I need presence, if I can almost always guarantee I can use manipulation to influence others? And vice versa.

    Well, this was way longer than I anticipated, so thank you for taking your time to read my wall of confusion!

      OK, so a big piece of general advice:

      Treat example rolls as illustrative, not prescriptive. There isn't a single correct Attribute + Skill roll for any given action. It's fine to say, "well the book says to use Manipulation, but this situation feels like Presence to me," or to let players make a case for a different roll.

      Originally posted by Algarik View Post
      ​I have recently realised that the more I think about some of the attributes and skills, the less I can find myself really interpreting how they differs. Of course, the physical ones are obvious, but the social and mental one are the one that makes me question my whole grasp of their conceptual reach.
      Your general explanations seem to be on point to me.

      ]Investigation: investigating a scene keyed of Intelligence + Investigation, but that examining a scene and identifying patterns called a wits+investigation rolls. Wouldn’t it be more appropriate to have ‘’investigating’’ a scene be based on wits instead?
      Intelligence, as you note is processing. When CSI goes into a crime scene, they're not looking for things they think are clues. They're looking at everything, then they process everything, and find the clues in that process. It can be slow, and tedious, but it's thorough because answers need data. The detective that comes to the scene and rolls Wits is looking for a starting point. As you note, finding patterns. They're trying to shortcut the thorough investigation to match the pattern at the scene to the collection of patterns in their brain. They can't wait for CSI to finish up completely before they start doing anything. They need something to give them ideas about what to ask the neighbors. They need to think about what databases to search or request access to. Etc.

      Presence vs Manipulation: This one I’ve always had a bit of trouble drawing true distinction. When I think about the definition, I can definitely see how they differ, but when I apply them to skills, the differences start to get muddied a bit. At lot of the time, it seems like a matter of ‘’approach’’ rather than an actual difference.
      Pretty much. However much it helps.... everyone runs into this one on some level.

      So, second piece of advice: don't be afraid to make approach matter. If the players need to get past a locked door, there's lots of options. Strength + (Stamina/Brawl/Weaponry) to bust it down. Dexterity + Larceny to pick the lock, or + Crafts to just disassemble the door. Wits + (Composure/Investigation) to look for a hidden spare key. Manipulation + Subterfuge to convince the building super to open the door for you. But with the door there's obvious complications to consider. A broken door is obviously broken. Circumventing the lock might not be as obvious, but still leaves signs. Finding a spare key assumes there is one. The super is a witness that you've talked to and might tell people about you.

      There's no reason to avoid the same kind of thinking with social problems. Presence and Manipulation might both convince someone to do something, but Presence means they don't necessarily think it's a good idea, and Manipulation doesn't necessarily mean they believe doing it for you is a good idea. You obviously don't want to undercut your players by sabotaging their successes or anything, but convincing someone to join you in a robbery with Presence might run into trouble when things start going wrong and the accomplice gets spooked, while doing it with Manipulation might mean someone thinks they can toss you under the bus and take the loot if the chance present itself. As the ST, use the Attributes your players lean on to give consequences flavor. Either they'll enjoy how their manipulative bastard gets in trouble sometimes as smug snakes tend to, or they'll try out options with lower dice-pools if they think they might want different stakes to the action. Both results are good.

      Intimidation:...but the interrogation action calls for manipulation instead of presence. Why?
      Reality more than fiction. Actual police interrogations are very much acts of manipulation. It's not some powerful move where the bad cops scares the suspect into answering, or the charisma of the good cop gets them to open up. Interrogations are calculated down to temperature of the room, what sort of food is made available, and all sorts of little details to sell people on the idea that the interrogator is your friend, and you should help your friend out. Quite a large number of civil rights activists think police interrogations (at least without a defense lawyer present) should be considered inadmissible evidence because of how utterly coercive they are.

      [quote][*]Manipulation vs Composure:
      Ultimately, resistance attributes are reactive, and the others are proactive in how the books tend to use them.

      Resolve vs Composure: That one seems mostly fine to me, I just wanted to make sure I utterly understood the difference. Would it be right to say that resolve is your mental fortitude while composure is your emotional fortitude? That way when skills and power affect the way you think it gets resisted by your resolve, while skills and power that try to change the way you feel, it gets resisted by composure. Am I correct?`
      I'd say so. It can be a hard line to see sometimes, but it's the essential line to draw. But again, don't stress it. The game doesn't break if you call for Composure and maybe it should have been Resolve.

      Expression: The ‘’Argument’’ action, which call for an intelligence + Expression, can convince people of the truth (or apparent) of your words. Also, why does it keys intelligence and not manipulation?
      Argument seem to be meant to be more formal. Winning a debate, making a case in court to the judge, and so on. The kind of thing where being right is ultimately more important than clever turns of phrase.


      So yeah, your confusion is understandable because the game wants to be flexible here, which means solid lines are hard to find. Don't worry too much, even consider talking to your players. Do they want to be able to have dumpstats, or want to have good reasons to spread out? That's more important than threading a needle the game doesn't really worry about. I think you'll also find that once you get into supernaturals, some of these problems get addressed through a far more controlling factor: what powers make you roll. Power rolls are, generally, not negotiable. If you want to be good at Dominate you get good at Intelligence.

              The difference might come from the difference from sample actions for Skills (where you try to leverage using a Skill in creative ways), and sample Common Actions (where they give out predetermined examples of what may be best used, in a given context)

              Anyways, my intuition is...

              Investigation: The sample common action of "Investigating (or Tidying up) a Scene" seems to imply you're taking time to logically working out what traces the culprit might have left, or what traces you might have left that investigators can look for. You have to simulate behaviors and information, thus, Intelligence. Both of the sample actions for the Investigation Skills seem to imply you're trying to perceive things that are already there, as opposed to creating simulations in your head. Thus, Wits.

              Intimidation: Reading the "Interrogation" common action, I do feel that yes this is using subtle threats. Using Presence to stare down someone looks more like a dominance/power play; good for making people shut up and look away, as opposed to spilling the beans.
              Subterfuge is probably reserved for con artists trying to weasel out private information out of you.

              Socialize: It feels like the difference between making the group as a whole like you and then using that likable-ness to approach someone in that group (Presence), and specifically circling around and gunning for an individual (Manipulation).

              Concealing emotions is Composure + Subterfuge, yes (I think you mistyped it as Composure+Manipulation?). However, poker face is exactly that; you're deceiving and tricking someone with guile, ie. Manipulation. It's a different context.

              Resolve and Composure, yeah they do look like that. Composure seems to have a bigger role in resisting general "change the situation I am in" rolls, though, such as for resisting curses.

              Persuasion &
              - Expression: The "Argument" common action sees you using logic and rhetoric, not just trying to leverage your personal charisma or way-with-words. It's a calculated formation of learned words and speech. Thus, Intelligence and Expression.
              - Subterfuge: Sometimes, you appeal to charm. Sometimes, you just lie your way out. The latter would call for Subterfuge instead of Persuasion, and that specific context seems to be what the Common Action "Fast Talk" seems to be describing.
              - Empathy: Changing what they want to do and changing how they feel do seem like different things.

                                      In my experience, a lot times you're not really calling for a specific roll, but asking your players the kind of roll they want to use.

                                      As Heavy Arms said, different Attributes and Skills define the type of approach used, which is something that most of the time you let the players decide.
                                      One player may state a very general intention, like "I want to interrogate the suspect", and the ST asks "How do you proceed?".
                                      The more the player describes the specifics, the more it becomes clear the kind of roll that is called for. If the player imagines the interrogation as dangling the prisoner from a rooftop, Batman style, it may as well involve Strength instead of Manipulation.

                                      (P.S. Heavy Arms provided an excellent explanation as to why realistic interrogation may involve Manipulation. But my suggestion is, don't be afraid to use movie logic if realism may slow down the pace of the game)

                                      Also, some STs may feel like more or less generous when calling for rolls. If the character is untrained in the Skill you're calling, you can think of letting him approach the same problem from a different angle using a different Skill or Attribute. The character may not have Academics to know the whole history of the city, but she has enough Streetwise to know all its secret and forgotten places.

                                      The possibility of coming up with different combinations of Attribute + Skill is something I really love about the CofD system and one of its strong points IMO, and it's always fun to call for unusual combinations.
