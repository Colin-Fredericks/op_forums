                                                I have played two Leviathan games now. Both over discord. I rather like it. I would be happy to discuss things that we liked or disliked or places we were really able to get into it.

                                                Within the first 2 scenes of either group we ran into the transformation rules being ridiculridiculously obtuse. It slowed down play added a lot of undesired weight to just wanting to go up or down a depth. Each group liked the idea of rewarding planned gradual change and making drastic depth change more taxing. The rules are already good at the latter The first group we trimmed down the rules but ended with something similar if simpler and easier. My current group completely threw out the rules and made the first depth change in a scene aitomatic without dice or ichor. This might not be ideal as an official rule but it made a huge difference at the table with the players not just thinking what they wanted right now but plotting with what they would need in the following scenes. We don't want to waste the oppertunity to change a depth without spending ichor or time to roll so the leviathans coordinate on what direction they want to manipulate events.

                                                It's also a game that works better with smaller groups, especially ones that communicate between sessions. There is a lot of managing and looking at what is happening in the background, even in other locations, for both players and storytellers. It's incredibly fun and unique to akways be having your character in one place doing one thing and their cult making something happen somewhere else. My first chronicle was ST + 4 and would have fallen apart very quickly with some players way more cult focused than others if the non cult focused players didn't have the foresight to involve themselves in the cult leaders cults in various ways. One player was a scholar of the Leviathan lore type and took a supportive role studying the others letting us flesh their networks out better. He was easily the most important role at the table while seemingly unimportant as a character.

                                                My current game is me, and other player and a storyteller. Plotting with the other girl between sessions and sharing full character write ups on our cultists is incredibly fun.

                                                        03-18-2023, 06:22 PM
                                                        Originally posted by Amethyst View Post

                                                        What did you like most about Leviathan?


                                                        Playing a dark god that was one of the wicked things that controlled the world behind the scenes. Both my campaigns went "Mythpunk" (Hellboy and American Gods) pretty fast over more lovecraftian themes. Both groups gave the players more starting exp, wishing to give the fledged Leviathan a higher floor. It feels nice to be the wicked thing giving the orders instead of the minion taking them. It has a very "Better to reign in hell" feel.

                                                        I liked how easy it was to make Leviathan interests intersect with other lines. We didn't use Marduk at all. They existed but for The first game The Paternostrum was the main influencers. The current one is pulling more would be divinity from werewolf spirit world with some hints of The True Fey being around.

                                                        When reading about the Y splat for Leviathan I was very unimpressed. They seemed boring and minimal compared to things like Vampire Orders. In play it became way more important than it seemed. School (along with undertow and upwelling) was about chosing what aspect of being an evil god-monster you embrace and don't suffer for. I was school of the sun and purposely making beloved and really getting into evil cult ruling would have normally destroyed my tranquility but recovered it instead. This was the case for everyone. School was the area where you really embraced your nature rather than worry about balancing it. So it turned from something I found dull to being very fun and important in play. I don't know if that was intended design but thats how it worked out.


                                                        Edit: The ritual system became a lot of fun once we started bring way more liberal in what we allowed. At first there was a lot of looking something up and getting a "You can't do that yet" then doing nothing with it. So we started being more liberal, especially with letting one Leviathan's cult use a ritual to give a different leviathan a temporary increase in how many channels, or dots of ardor or zeal a different player's cult had. As I probably implied before, times when players were comming up with evil schemes together were a major highlight of play. Everyone started looking on each others sheets and brokering supernatural favors.
