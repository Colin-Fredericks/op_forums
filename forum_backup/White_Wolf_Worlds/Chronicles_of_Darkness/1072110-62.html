                                    My real issue with the Star of Bethlehem is that anyone who plays them, barring a large change to the setting, is setting their charachter up for outright failure.                                     I've always liked the idea that the Cold Guard are more about managing the darkness rather than fighting it. The idea that they believe (not without some justification) that this is the "world of darkness". By cosmic law will always be darkness.

                                    So rather than try to kill the monsters and wait for them to inevitably be replaced by more monsters somewhere else who'll eat innocents before the Cold Guard find them again.                                                         Originally posted by The Kings Raven View Post

                                                        I've always liked the idea that the Cold Guard are more about managing the darkness rather than fighting it. The idea that they believe (not without some justification) that this is the "world of darkness". By cosmic law will always be darkness.

                                                        So rather than try to kill the monsters and wait for them to inevitably be replaced by more monsters somewhere else who'll eat innocents before the Cold Guard find them again. They use their Endowment to trap the monsters, so they always know where they are and it's somewhere far from people.

                                                        Mix this in with a philosophy that they don't try to overcome their darker personality traits, but accept them and put them to good use and you have a foil to Princesses.
                                                        Hm, the wardens of evil. I like that. They're the people who go about putting evils into cans, because they don't think it's possible to kill them (and sometimes, it's the safer and less costly option-a Dethroned will reincarnate if killed, barring some specific circumstances, so they honestly have the smart plan barring a Noble willing to be symbiotically possessed after fighting a posthuman demigod of despair.).

                                                        Once I see the Endowment, I'll see what I can do. Also, idea-their "pseudo-Caliganes" (which I'll call the Malebranches for now, after the demonic guards of Malebolge-the Evil Claws) use their prisoners as a "filter" of sorts to draw on the Dark without being corrupted by it; the self-same act of developing a Malebranche is simultaneously an act of defilement and purification, since it is done by using the entropic aspects of the Dark to make their prisoners suffer for what they have done. They do not expect their prisoners to mend their ways (especially because, as creatures of the Dark, they can't), but on the rare occasions it does happen (because they...aren't creatures of the Dark, say a powerful vampire in semi-permanent torpor or a godlike Amanojaku), it is a time of great celebration, since that both means they can divert resources somewhere else and because they have proven their philosophy-"Only once one knows the darkness of self can one truly find the light."

                                                        Which also flips their portrayal from unthinking zealots to conflicted, well-intentioned philosophers, but I consider that a benefit, the previous Cold Guard didn't even strike me as love-to-hate scummy like the Magisters.
