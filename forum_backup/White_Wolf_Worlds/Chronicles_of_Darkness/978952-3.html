        I think it's definitely a generational thing.

        The WoD is about seeing injustice and being angry about it. It's about us vs. them: good (but conflicted) heroes lashing out at a corrupt machine. That's very reflective of the sort of youth political philosophies of the 80s and 90s. It's fun, but it misses a lot of subtleties about how injustice and corruption actually insinuate themselves into society.

        My generation (at least from my experience) tends to focus more on the roots of these problems. Racism is less about mustache twirling KKK members manipulating politics and more about the subconscious and internalized prejudices everyone, knowingly or not, perpetuate. It's about profit-based incarceration. Millenials seem more apt to recognize a philosophy where we all contribute to that machine. They look at this year's presidential election and the question isn't Trump vs. Clinton. It's whether the disproportionate cost disenfranchised groups would have to pay for a Trump election is worth the cost of perpetuating an unfair two party system that we've all helped perpetuate. And I think ChroD does a good job of reflecting those kinds of shaded questions.

                                Personally i agree with the interview part of NwoD was conceive as game first and a world second. So NWoD never felts as organic as OWoD, it took Chrod and lots of work from the DM to make it so. The justifications of 1st edition of why there is warrior auspice and a warrior tribe is flimsy so is the fluffy pieces as to why the unecesary paths of mage are there.

                                Not saying the other approach was better mind you. Mage ascension had the big problem that the "player party" didnt fit in the setting. Like the mage "society", game didnt really factor a cabal of various traditions. Vampire coteries always felt weird, and out of place in masquerade. There is sweet middle point between the 2 extremes of NWoD (1st) and OWoD.

                                I would never understand the "limiting metaplot" angle of OWoD though, i dont see either line (Chrod and OWoD) as limiting with the exception that OWoD gives me a default setting to fall back into when i wanna change it when Chrod doesnt. I mean if i wanna screw the metaplot and make a caitiff city i can just do it, i would have to pitch that game to my players beforehand but i dont see it how the metaplot would limit me?

                                Aside from that i just dont like the direction they say they are taking OWoD. Example i dont agree with their definition of werewolf. To me apocalypse was never about fighting the wyrm or ecoterrorism. The wyrm wasnt never the true antagonist but the garou nation. The Wyrm is too impersonal to be so, its like the zombies in walking dead. The zombies arent the true antagonist but the narrative excuse for your character to having to deal with the ugliest part of the garou nation and try to work with them and slowly change them.

                                Same with Ascension, which is to me as whole is a perfect analogy for the 90s. You start (1st edition) all full of piss and passive aggressive vinegar to fight the MAN! because like you know the MAN! want to kill imagination and magic and all those things that are incredible important to people in semigood economic status and the MAN! is evil, fascist and faceless sellouts. Then as the edition/time passes you realize that the MAN! wasnt as faceless or fascist and you though and they were just trying to fix the world as they know how, and you little cliche of fellows dissenters wasnt as pure and righteous as you though.

                                Then you grow up and find out that fighting the MAN is not gonna change the world and the world doesnt wanna be change either it like its mediocrity as its stand and the MAN! neither does know what to do because its made up fallible people just like you and is as lost as you are. So you face a choice, do you grow up and try to work with the man and try the hard work of improving the world a little (meaning cooperating with the technocracy not joining)? Do you just give up and live your life without bothering anyone? Or do you stay in your teenage fantasy and keep fighting the man as manchild that can accept the world is more complex (rogue council)?

                                Then i found out that that whole thing was completely by accident and the writers always intended for the MAN! to be faceless and authoritarian. The players saw deeper meaning and the steps were taken for it to never happen again. Kinda of sound like the Star Wars special editions doesnt it?

                                So honestly i dont care if they "ruin" the setting as long as they give us a good rule set. Its all i ask for. I will ignore the setting they sell me and keep the good rules because while i like WoD and Chrod, their math on the systems leaves a lot to be desired.
