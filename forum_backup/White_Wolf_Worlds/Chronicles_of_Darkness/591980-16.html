                Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post

                Hm, I understand the reasoning, and I like Incepts - them being a thing makes sense. But I don't see how the Incepts > Go Loud connection holds.
                I'll break this down into pieces and talk my way through the thinking process. Apologies if any of this is unclear, it's late here. Ask questions if what I've written below doesn't make sense.

                Although DtD is an espionage-themed game, demons are more than spies, they're hackers. In fact, demons are first and foremost hackers. They hack reality to get what they want, obscure their identities, and keep themselves one step ahead of the competition. Demons hack together Covers from whole cloth or fragments of actual code (ie, human lives), and they use these to hide from the system. When scrutinised, they spoof their authenticity, hoping that the legitimate agents of the system will think they're normal code and move on. Embeds are their use of back doors and inadvertent vulnerabilities in the code to force outcomes with minimal risk to themselves. Exploits are more brute force attacks where they pit their own hacking skills against the system and force it to bend in ways it's not really meant to work.

                The DSTG tells us that Exploits are the demonic extension of Incepts. 'A demon’s Fall cancels her Incept privileges. She must hack together Aether-fueled Exploits in crude imitation of what she lost.' This is subtle, but important. When an angel falls, the God-Machine removes her Incept privileges. It scrambles her access codes to keep her out of the system, but it can't uninstall the essential hardware of the Incept component. Quintessential reality hackers, demons find workarounds that allow them to force Aether through the Incept 'gateway' to create the more limited Exploit effects. Even these severely curtailed effects cause noticeable Incept-like ripples, forcing the demon to test for compromise of her Cover.

                Hopefully that makes sense so far.

                Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
                Incepts are something that comes from the God-Machine, something which may be inherent to the framework of every angel even if they're often not authorized to use it; they equate to their master system's acess codes to reality. Going Loud is a demon sacrificing a piece of infrastructure extremely vital to them, the one kind of infrastructure that they are able to use and build in such a way, for a burst of aetheric energy that momentarily expands their awareness of occult physics and integration with reality.
                This is where our interpretations differ, because this is a 'chicken-and-egg' scenario. The tl;dr version of the next couple of paragraphs is that you're saying 'the demon sacrifices her Cover to access power', whereas I'm saying 'the act of accessing the power destroys the Cover'. A subtle, but important difference to the thesis behind Incepts.

                Let's examine what Going Loud actually is from the point of view of what it does. You can read going loud as presented in DtD (p113) as 'demon drops Cover, gains massive power', but this kind of gets it backwards IMO. The key sentence on p113 is 'In a burst of raw, aetheric energy, the demon casts off her mortal shell and, for a brief, glorious moment, remembers what it was to be connected to the entire universe in a way she hasn't experienced since she served the God-Machine.' The key part of this sentence is 'in a burst of raw, aetheric energy'. It doesn't say she gains the energy from destroying the Cover, and I'd argue that since it's possible to discard a Cover without going loud (or making any sort of ripple), it's not the act of destroying a Cover that initiates the going loud benefits.

                The demon forces the universal machinery to accept her as one for a gloriously short period of time. She connects to the system in the most obvious way possible, and she knows it's going to be noticeable, and draw attention. She can't maintain a Cover under this, she's naked before the God-Machine. But even here her connection is an imperfect hack. Her power shoots up, maximising her potential possibly beyond what she was an a legitimate angel. She's filled with ephermeral fuel straight from the mains system, just like angels, but it's a quick top-up. She gains even more access to the system than angels, but because the hack is imperfect, her greater access is more limited than an angel's. An angel's version of 'going loud' is using whatever Incepts it has auhorised access to, even if it doesn't necessarily have tactic approval to deploy them. A demon's going loud gives access to every Exploit, even those that would logically have been hacked from Incepts she never had access to as an angel.

                To help visualise this, assume that the God-Machine installs every Incept into every angel when it is Outputted, but due to metaphysical limitations of the angelic form, it can only hold access codes to a number equal to its Rank. Any more would destroy it. (For a bonus bit of chronicle paranoia, this could logically mean that if an angel returns to refitting Infrastructure between missions, the God-Machine can swap out access codes to make the angel more suited to each mission. This means that an antagonist angel will not only return each time with a different power tool set, but may become more creative and closer to falling the longer the demons can string it along.)

                Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
                On a somewhat different train of thought, isn't going loud by its own definition something demons alone can do, since the G-M spoonfeeds angels anything it thinks they need to know (how they make use of what they have and their degree of compliance to mission parameters nonwithstanding)?
                You're right that by definition only demons can go loud, but that's because going loud is a specific term for the demonic version of tapping into power and using it, and it has a few side effects that other creatures don't experience. Other creatures have their own versions of tapping into power, with their own side effects. These aren't called going loud, but they're the equivalent. Angels use Incepts - they tap into power, they're noticeable, but they don't lose Cover or suffer the other problems demons do. To look at it from the other direction, demons go loud by activating the hacks that allow them to emulate angels as close as possible - they do this is by flicking every switch and connecting every circuit that taps into their residual Incept components and briefly lugs them back into the universe.

                Originally posted by YeOfLittleFaith View Post
                Even if they could do something truly comparable to going loud, wouldn't they instantly fall, making the point moot? It's been said that Incepts are a conspicuous temptation for Angels who hold thoughts of acting independently or deviating from orders, because they're always there as an option, and so are a very likely catalyst for the Fall. Is the relation drawn from that in some way?
                Incepts are a temptation, but not an instant Fall trigger. Remember, Incepts are tools the God-Machine does install in its angels. It wants them to have the capacity, for its own inscrutable reasons. In today's word, the God-Machine's works are more subtle, and it doesn't want attention drawn to it. But in the (possible) distant past, Its angels used Incepts to move planets into alignment, alter orbits, and geo-engineer what It required. If you want to equate human misundersanding of the God-Machine with another deity, it authorised angels to use Incepts to flood the world, or transform entire cities into salt.

                Even in modern times, the God-Machine may authorise angels to use their Incepts. If a demonic ring becomes too obviously successful, they may find themselves tracked by a hunter-killer angel with orders to get the job done by any means necessary - including raining down fire on entire city blocks. Additionally, if a guardian uses Incepts to terminate a group of demons from disrupting a painstakingly constructed occult matrix - and it doesn't damage the Output - the God-Machine probably won't care. If the matrix is disrupted and the angel uses Incepts to track and destroy the guilty parties to ensure they can't do it again, the God-Machine may turn a blind eye. Similarly, if some working requires the collection and harvesting of supernatural creatures, and the angel tasked with this just uses its Incepts to bring the components to it, the God-Machine will probably be satisfied by a job done efficiently.

                And sometimes, an angel will use an Incept out of desperation, or oversight, or stupidity, and the God-Machine does nothing. What does that mean for its existence? Is it tacit permission that it should use the Incept again, or did it just get lucky this time. As much as it's a life of clarity, an angel's life can also be one of underlying confusion.
