    Supernatural enemies that make good crossover antagonists?

    06-15-2016, 11:54 PM
    So, I've been thinking, any antagonists from certain lines that you think would make great baddies for other lines? Some examples-

    - Idigam vs. Promethean: Atamajakki had a great idea for a 1950s Promethean dark era based on 50s and 60s atomic horror b-movies, so why not pit them against The Thing From Outer Space- in this case, an Idigam that's causing bizarre mutations. Maybe it's even taken a liking to flux and the Centimani, and wants to transform into some kind of pseudo-Qashmallim? For bonus points, set it in either New Mexico or one of the poles.

    - Fae vs. Demons: Mysterious thorny growths have been cropping up over local Infrastructure, causing it to act strangely. The Ring might initially think this is a good thing- it's fucking with the GM- but then the damage to the infrastructure starts causing dangerous reality glitches. And then they find out that the GM's plan to solve the situation involves "cauterizing the wound" by setting off a massive natural disaster and razing entire city blocks to the ground.

    - Strix vs. Beasts: A well-intentioned Begotten "patron" of the local Kindred is seduced by a Stirge, who wants to use him to push the vampires into barbarism. What will the players think of this new threat- assuming they can detect it. And when they do, how will they react to an entity that claims to embody their guiding principles even more closely than they do?

    - Tremere Lich vs. Sin-Eaters: A very old and very powerful Lich is on a quest to refine his flawed immortality, and his resources have notified him of the existence of the Bargain. The possibilities are almost endless, but he's decided to start by hunting down as many Geists are possible and binding them to himself and his servants. If the Krewe has dealt with abmortals, they might be in for a surprise when they find out just how powerful this one is.

    - Pandoran vs. Sangiovanni: One of the most prominent, wealthiest, and skilled Sangiovanni, owner of a sprawling estate and laboratory complex, suddenly drops out of contact with the rest of the "family". When the players are dispatched to investigate, they find out that their experiments on creating even more perfect undead servants have created a brood of Pandorans led by a Sublimati, who have claimed the estate as their own twisted lair.

    - Kerberos vs Mummies: Yet another Atamajakki-inspired hook. So, you can draw a lot of interesting parallels between Geist and Mummy, especially with the whole "brought back to life and maintained by a minor god of death" thing giving some interesting similarities between the Bargain and the Rite of Return. Mummies looking for some answers could likely end up in the underworld...only to find that its monstrous overlords don't take kindly to outsiders snooping around. Perhaps they'll even seek an audience with Polydegmon himself...what would he make of such venerable deathless beings?

      Vampires have to be absolutely terrifying to the Arisen. Sure, you can kill them easily enough... but an Elder has had all the time you've been in henet to scheme and leave nasty surprises for you. Fail to take care of one during a Descent, and they've possibly got centuries to conspire to keep you asleep and to subvert your Cult.

      I keep seeing arguments that Changelings get along well with Beasts, but it's for me to imagine anything more terrifying short of one of the Gentry themselves. Everyone else sees a normal person, but you see a walking, talking nightmare; are they blind, or is your Clarity starting to slip? When a living piece of the story starts stalking people and dragging them off to places that don't exist, things start to get uncomfortably familiar.

                                      I've always felt that the best way to handle things in crossover is by using Hunter's example: Just take the vague concept and stat it however you want. Your players may never even know that mechanically your Vampire is using Spells because you don't want to bother remembering how Vampire works. Having Dread Powers in the corebook makes this all the easier. I once had Kuchisake-Onna show up in a game and because I don't like how the Slasher templates work, I gave her five dots of Physical Disciplines from Requiem 2e and I think two Numina.

                                      So don't limit yourself to directly using the things from other gamelines exactly. I feel like that's an easy mistake that gets made often.

                                                          If you're looking for fodder here, go look at the stuff from Dragon, both 2nd and 3rd edition: I've pulled Hydras and Deep Ones from those before, and there are a few other cool antagonists in there. Hydras being good for balls-out crazy-monster attacks and Deep Ones being good for that subtle "is it evil or just creepy" kind of monster. I also know that Vampires make for good fakeout-villains, and I tend to use them for that on alot of newer players, because they see Vampires running some massive conspiracy and don't know enough to realize that that's just status quo. I also enjoy tossing anything from Inferno in. Obviously, Hunters are the best antagonists, because they're probably the closest thing to a "bad guy splat" which was made with PCs in mind. If you want more, you should go look into a book called Antagonists. Also, look up the SCP Foundation for some great monsters.

                                                          Most importantly, remember that all templates have their "evil" Y-Splat. Vampires have Ballai's Brood (and Invictus, who are less evil and more stuck in outdated ways), Werewolf has the Pure (3 unique kinds!) and Bale Hounds, and I pulled imagery from the Black Spiral Dancers into the campaign I ran when I first got my copy of 2nd edition, Mages have Seers and a few other nasty things... Every book mentions something.

                                                          Also watch the stuff we're releasing for Dragon Rekindled. The Deep Ones, Knights of the Bloody Chalice (coming this week), Nightmares, and Nihilists are being written with Crossover in mind.
