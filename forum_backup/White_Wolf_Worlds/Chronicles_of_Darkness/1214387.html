      Pretty much different in every way.

      Different setting, different game mechanics, and different themes. While both are about supernatural monsters that have lived among humanity from the beginning, World of Darkness is about how the world is coming to an end and with massive stakes, while Chronicles of Darkness leans more and the supernaturals' day to day life among humanity and with their respective supernatural societies with (relatively) much smaller and personal stakes.

          Some sister games share relatively similar premises. Most notably, Vampire: The Masquerade and Vampire: The Requiem are both recognizably about similar visions of vampires clinging to their humanity, forming dynasties of blood, and squabbling for power over one another as they manipulate the living to ease their unlives and achieve their goals.

          Things diverge from there. Werewolf: The Apocalypse and Werewolf: The Forsaken are both about people who awaken to a half-spirit heritage and become hardy, shapechanging world-walkers who wage battles across both the body and soul of the world. But Apocalypse's war, its enemy, and its counterorganization are all global in nature, while the Forsaken often face more personal threats and are more likely to splinter in local interests. The spirit world of Apocalypse faces a deadly evil taint which cannot ultimately be reasoned or compromised with, threatening the natural balance, whereas Forsaken's spirit world is muddier, red in tooth and claw, mostly cautious and opportunistic if you get too careless, but without such a cosmic threat to taint it.

          Some other gamelines diverge wildly in basic premise. Demon: The Fallen and Demon: The Descent, while they share certain influences, are wildly different games about fundamentally different conflicts.

          One contrast that can be drawn between the two gamelines is in the titular darkness. As a general trend, the World of Darkness leans towards an inner heart of darkness, a sort of corruption of the soul. The World of Darkness is an impressionist world where violent crime rates are higher and horrifically cynical businesses, like snuff film companies, operate concealed within society. The Chronicles of Darkness tend more towards darkness as mystery and the fear of the unknown. Both settings have their share of pessimism about human behavior, but where the World of Darkness deliberately exaggerates to make its point, the Chronicles lean more towards portraying down-to-earth humans in a dangerous and isolating world. Ordinary life goes on in the World of Darkness because the monsters have the power, and they exercise it to erase their presence from human knowledge. Ordinary life goes on in the Chronicles of Darkness because even the local monsters often don't know what's lurking in the fog on the outskirts of town, but everyone shares the instinctive sense that whatever it is, they're probably safer and better off leaving it alone and pretending it's not there.

                                  This was a perfectly good place for it. If you have questions about a specific game line you could ask in that game’s subforum. Most lines also have a “ask a simple question, get a simple answer” thread as well, which are a good resource.

                                  Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post
                                  So basically it's about scope and tone, world is the end, you are just another cog, while chronicle is a personal, solitary experience. Though, each have their horror elements
                                  That’s one of the key tonal differences, yes. Although I wouldn’t necessarily describe Chronicles as solitary - most of the game lines have a focus on group play. It’s just that CofD focuses on more intimate stories of those characters’ interpersonal experiences, where WoD focuses on grand scale stories concerning the fate of the world.

                                  Looking at the werewolf games as examples, since you were interested in those, in
                                  Apocalypse you play the soldiers in a losing war to save the planet from physical and spiritual devistation at the hands of corrupt humans and spirits tainted by disharmony. There is a heavy focus on rage, and the game plays up the futility of your struggle, while simultaneously emphasizing its importance. You’ve lost the war, the world is ending, but at least you can use these last waning days to fuck up the ones responsible as much as they’ve fucked the rest of the world.

                                  In Forsaken, you play memers of the keystone species in the spritual ecosystem. As descendents of the ultimate predator, you are driven to hunt, and in hunting, to maintain the delicate balance between spirit and flesh, within your territory and within yourself. There is no grand conflict or great enemy to war against, though there are many threats to you, your pack, and your territory. Rage is still present, apropos of werewolves, but rather than righteous indignation at the world’s sealed fate, the rage is a consequence of disharmony. Deny your predatory nature and it seeths under the surface until the moon, or the taste of blood, or some other trigger sets it free and the rage takes control. Embrace your nature to the exclusion of your humanity and you may have control, but you become unable to lead a normal life, as you lose the ability to resist becoming the wolf at the slightest of urges.

                                  So both game lines play with similar themes - harmony between the material and the spiritual, the balance of nature and natural ecosystems, the werewolf as a metaphor for uncontrolled anger, etc. But Apocalypse handles it through a global and fatalistic lens, where Forsaken handles it through a more personal one.

                                          RE: W:tA vs. W:tF

                                          While I can't speak to how other St's approach the game, for me, the central Them of W:tA was "Rage against the Dying of the Light". The battle of the Apocalypse was truthfully over except for tiny glimmers of hope. The Garou Nation was determined that no matter how bleak the situation became, they would fight to the bitter end. It generally framed the Garou as Warriors - the mightiest of the mighty..and yet that might was rarely enough, and often caused more problems then it solved. The gameline had a deeply fatalistic thread that was spot on appropriate for the World of Darkness at the time.

                                          For W:tF , the Theme is "The Wolf Must Hunt". The Uratha are the descendants of a predator god , and predation is a central conceit of the setting. No matter what situation a Uratha finds themselves in , they view just about every interaction as Predator or Prey. The setting also plays up the themes of Pack - the family you choose - against all the forces arrayed against you.
                                          The Uratha are framed as the supreme Hunters - they aren't fighting a war as much are trying to reconcile that predators nature with the world around them.

                                          There are of course conceptual overlaps, but at their hearts they use the imagery of Werewolves to tell very different stories.

                                          As far as their prowess is concerned, its almost apple and oranges. Each is relatively powerful compared to the rest of their respective gamelines, but the Uratha are less overwhelmingly potent than the Garou (at least in terms of sheer physicality).

                                          Twilight Menagerie - A collection of Ephemeral Entities

                                                  Originally posted by Primordial newcomer View Post
                                                  Ahh ok

                                                  In terms of theme, what is the difference of the two? I only understand apocalypse is about controlling what you are, not giving into bestial temptation, but forsaken seems to be also focused on that, so I'm confused.
                                                  I’m only passingly familiar with Apocalypse, so I can’t help you much on that. With Forsaken though, I wouldn’t say it’s about not giving into bestial temptation, and a character who did see their lycanthropy that way would be very likely to end up like the werewolves of classic media like The Wolfman - an ordinary human most of the time who uncontrollably transforms into a monster and kills indiscriminately for a few hours whenever he’s faced with a certain stimulus (the full moon, in this particular case). Rather, Forsaken is about owning your bestial nature, without letting it own you. See, in Forsaken there’s a stat called Harmony, which is rated on a 0-10 scale. Doing werewolf-y things like hunting, spending time in the spirit world, spending long periods away from other people, etc., makes that number go down. On the other hand, going too long without hunting or entering the spirit world, or otherwise trying to resist your werewolf nature makes that number go up. At higher Harmony levels, it’s more difficult to shape shift, and the triggers that can set you into a death rage get more pervasive and harder to resist, and when you go into death rage it lasts longer. At lower harmony levels, shapeshifting becomes so easy that sometimes it just happens by accident, and you gain Bans - behaviors that you are supernaturally compelled to follow, just like a spirit. So ideally you want your Harmony to be as close to 5 as possible. The goal is not to deny your predatory urges completely, but to manage them carefully so you strike just the right balance between human being and predator spirit.

                                                  Originally posted by Callishka View Post
                                                  Also are the forsaken werewolf forms more powerful than apocalypse ones
                                                  That’s difficult to measure, since the two games use fairly different rules systems. Apocalypse’s forms do tend to give bigger bonuses to physical attributes than Forsaken’s forms do. However, as of 2nd editition, each of Forsaken’s forms has a unique ability according to its role in the hunt. Human form is supernaturally good at blending in. Near-human form is supernaturally intimidating. Wolf form is incredibly fast. Near-wolf form can inflict free debuffs on its victims. Wolf-man form regenerates at a ridiculous rate and can kill lesser enemies in a single roll, but has a time limit.
