                                Something I haven't seen played with a lot is service to humanity. One of the unifying themes of CofD is that humanity is an unalloyed good thing, for all its warts, and doing a light subversion of that seems like an interesting direction to go. It overlaps a little with Deviant and Promethean, but the tone is significantly different from revenge or redemption... but I'm not really sure what that tone is. But make a splat with some created purpose, where it's clear that being subservient makes sense and is a good thing... but... it's ultimately pretty terrible for you and you need to figure out how to balance that. The antagonists would be those who go all-in on service, to make it more clear that that's not what you're actually supposed to do.

                                Hm.

                                I could also see something interesting playing off the kind of domain-management that Beast has, or Mummy's cult-management. I'd start with Ents or Dryads, and make it about putting down semi-metaphorical roots in the community. Have some "Law and Order" versus "Chaos and Growth" things going on. Have some significant problems when important people decide to move away from your community, such that it's actually a big power-loss for you, so it's very tempting to entangle them and make them stay, but that has its own problems. What happens when you make people breed, because that strengthens you, for instance? Can reflect on whether or not some forests are the result of going too far and stagnating everything into a non-city, perhaps. Definitely some Beast there, so probably needs another something to differentiate.

                                I'd kinda like to see a mechanic where the power stat gets crossed with the subjective stat into a 2d graph, but I'm not even sure how the math would work.

                                There's also Moai statues, and sun worship; I feel like there might be something there to mine. (I may have been reading Erfworld lately.)

                                Hm. Also, Greek Muses seem like a neat little seed, though I think there's a fansplat that tried to tap into that already. Kinda want to reference Simmons' muses in Iliad, Gaiman's Calliope, and Plato's daemon all in one there. The push-pull between creating inspiration and creating mania. Not really sure what they'd do when they're not tinkering with mortals, though, or what the power spectrum would really look like.

                                I would like to see a mature take on religion. Not really sure how to go about that.

                                Also wonder if it'd be possible to do economic horror. I know Red Markets is aiming somewhere around that; don't know where exactly it'll land.

                                            Not entirely sure that it counts as a "monster", but what about something involving time travel, or rather the paradoxes therein? Be it from carelessness, malice or the good intentions that paved Hell's paths, careless actions in the past create the problems people were send to eradicate if not worse ones. Alternatively, you go back in time to change something...and you have the power to change so many wrongs. What kind of person would only do it for a certain set of gains, and how would they feel if they wanted to go and help everyone but couldn't due to some limitation? How would it feel to go back in time to fix your loved ones' fatal cancer, only to give them a rapid flesh eating disease that evolved thanks to your time travel equipment? Worse, messing with your own past to prevent your own time travel?

                                            Perhaps not a monster story, but mucking in time's backwaters (thank you Big Finish) can definitely be a source for horror. I know that Demons, Mages and to some degree Changelings can affect time, but in a limited fashion. Imagine giving the reigns of that horse to someone who wouldn't know how to get it to walk and how to get it to buck and throw them off?

                                            Alternate timelines and space aliens seem appropriate, but I'm not too sure - the Mirrors supplements rather handily showed how any gameline could be edited to such a game.

                                            Perhaps a game idea where someone goes about their lives and then, out of nowhere, they come to the startling and undeniable fact that not only are they not human, nothing about them was ever human? That they were made to fit in with humanity for some purpose, but now they are breaking down and can never be human again? That the world was a fine puppet show, except now the audience is realizing that they are not actually part of it? I admit, it sounds like Fetch: The Game, except in this case there was no life that they took the place of. The life was fabricated out of nowhere...

                                            I dunno, these are ideas I've had before for fansplats and it just never really worked. Just thought I'd throw my two cents in.

                                                Maybe we should think less about which archetype doesn't have a gameline yet and think more along the lines of what storytelling themes haven't been explored yet that a new gameline could revolve around in a unique way. So far we have..

                                                Vampire: Addiction and lust
                                                Werewolf: Violence, savagery, animism, companionship and a carnivorous cosmos
                                                Mage: Hubris and capital M Mystery
                                                Promethean: Isolation, birth, rejection, and the meaning of humanity
                                                Changeling: Identity issues and living with trauma
                                                Hunter: Gazing into the abyss and taking a stand against overwhelming odds
                                                Geist: Second chances and what is owed to the dead
                                                Mummy: Memory, civilization and slavery
                                                Demon: Paranoia and living a lie
                                                Beast: Family, the unconscious, fear, and the myth of "heroes" vs "villains"
                                                Deviant: Vengeance and transhumanism

                                                I am over simplifying these but with all those games you can sum up what they are about in several words or a short sentence. So now let's look at the most common unused archetypes in myth and horror and see if whether a game could focus around them or not.

                                                Mad Scientists: Failure, bitterness, broken dreams.
                                                Sea Monsters: I don't even know. The unknown?
                                                Genies: Slavery, be careful what you wish for.
                                                Angels: Obedience.
                                                Ghosts: How lives never "end" and echo throughout history. Memory.
                                                Aliens: Being an outsider in the most literal sense of the term.
                                                Dragons: Glory and hubris.
                                                Zombies: Hunger and greed

                                                As we can see pretty much every single one of the themes these archetypes are associated with have already been done in previous games. I'm not coming down on fan games. Personally I enjoy reading them and consider Genius and Leviathan as part of my head canon. But I think for future Darkness games we shouldn't try to force things. Beast and Deviant are creating entirely new supernaturals drawn from a massive array of inspirations. I expect any future games will be similar. Taking themes, archetypes, and fears that have yet to be used and creating something unique.

                                                To be honest though I still want my Reptilian: Why Won't Anyone Believe Me and Sasquatch: The Blurry Footage.
