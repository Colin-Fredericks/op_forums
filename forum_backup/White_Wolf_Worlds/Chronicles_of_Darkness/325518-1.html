                The Slasher recognizing its "failings" is a considerably improbable thing to come by. And such unexpected events can't be so easily determined. Take it as that:

                A Slasher is a somewhat rare phenomenon. On top of that, the Sentinel is somewhat rarer among them. We are talking about someone so deranged to become a killer, and on top of that to become a superlative killer. On top of that, it isn't just good at killing, but ultimately defined by the kill. A Vampire have a condition. A mercenary kills for a living. A slasher doesn't have much left at all but the kill, and the madness around the need to kill.

                Now you go one step further, so this person actually acquire an Undertaking. This is the rupture point. Remember that no external transformation makes a slasher. At this point, the need for killing just went far enough to make this person more than human. Not inhuman on a moral standpoint, but truly so. And on top of even that this particular person did ended, between all possibilities, as a Sentinel.

                Given how rare Sentinels already are, imagine how rare it is for any Slasher to get lucid enough to evaluate what she's doing. I'm not saying it isn't possible, but simply that it is rare, rather unique. It isn't precisely simple to answer. This would take a lot of development just to actually happen, so probably the outcome would derive from that development.

                On a later analysis, I think the link between the Warden and other Supernatural phenomena in the flavor text didn't bore well. The description should focus more on how the condition of the Warden evolved, and in other aspects of how the killing method changed, if it did, without directly considering supernatural phenomena beyond the Slasher, proper. I got the idea, but it don't sounds right to go that way so suddenly. Let any supernatural link be at the ST's discretion.
