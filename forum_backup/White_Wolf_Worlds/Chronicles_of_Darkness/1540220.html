    Fire & Revolution: Carthians (Coming Wednesday!)

    08-12-2024, 04:52 PM
    Edit: Get it here! https://www.storytellersvault.com/pr...iate_id=321925

    Hello again! Time to break out the Little Red Book once more.

    Some of you who follow the doings of None More Dark Publishing might know that we have a covenant book for Vampire: The Requiem Second Edition in the works: Fire & Revolution: Carthians. Well, I’m here to tell you it’s coming next week as a little late-summer gift to all you CofD fans (i.e., Wednesday, August 14th). At 12 PST, that’ll be dropping on DTRPG and the Storytellers Vault. We had wanted to drop this in July, but sadly, we had some delays, but we're basically ready to go now!

    If you don’t know who/what NMD is, we’re a collective of RPG authors and artists who’ve spent the last three-ish years creating supplements for Requiem. Because the current IP owner has stopped approving new official releases for the Chronicles of Darkness (to the best of my knowledge; I have no other information in that regard beyond what everyone else has seen), we’ve been trying to carry the torch, at least with Vampire and Changeling.

    So, what’s in this book? Let me quote the back cover. Fire & Revolution includes:
    • A complete guide to the Carthians, reformists of the All Night Society.
    • Concordats, factions, and ghoul families, ready to light up the world on the Revolution’s behalf.
    • New Archetypes, Devotions, and Merits; optional mechanics for debates, elections, and improvised Carthian Law; and errata for systems in Secrets of the Covenants.
    • Radical schisms and reactionary horrors, including the return of the Commonwealth, the Movement’s sinister globe-spanning shadow.
    Why do covenant books if we have Secrets of the Covenants? Well, as cool as SotC is, it's a very specific kind of book. I wanted to provide something a bit more big-picture. I also wanted to cover more systems than SotC had the space for. Plus, SotC is getting close to 9 years old now, and I think it's worthwhile to provide the covenants with a fresh coat of paint.

    The other reason was that everyone really wanted us to do covenant books, and we're not ones to fly in the face of public opinion!

    We started with the Carthians mostly because of alphabetical order, but I also felt they needed the most work in terms of adjusting popular perceptions. The Firebrands sometimes get painted with a, shall we say... Anarch brush that I don't care for. The Carthians aren't Anarchs (the Carthians aren't even really based on the Anarchs), and they're not even always left-wing. I think some folks read them as kinda default progressives (in human terms), and while that's true in contrast to the Invictus — everything is progressive compared with feudal gerontocracy — the Movement has a much sharper edge than "vague vampire socialism." At least to me, the Movement is more compelling (and weird) if it's ideologically diverse. All that said, the idea of the Movement being Revolutionary yet the wielders of literal Law has sometimes stuck in people's craws. I don't think there's really a contradiction there, but I see why folks sometimes perceive one; thus, I wanted to really dig into dispelling that perception.

    I also wanted to get away from the idea that the Carthians are the good vampires. Some Carthians certainly try to be the lesser of five evils when it comes to the human equation, but the Carthian Movement (at least the way we conceive it) doesn't give one whit about humanity other than as a source of ideology. Do all Firebrands agree with that? Definitely not! And that's a dramatic angle for you to explore.

    I’ll get into the specifics more in the coming week (like how this book relates to Secrets of the Covenants and why we chose to do it), but for now, I’ll leave you with a short preview of our big system innovation in this book: concordats.

    On Monday I’ll be back with another preview. What would you like: A concordat or some new Carthian Laws? Or should I talk about the Commonwealth...?

    Also, I hasten to noted that NMD has a Patreon: https://www.patreon.com/NoneMoreDarkPublishing. There are already a bunch of Carthian previews on there, and most of them are available for free supporters. We’re going to be adding some new tiers and content soon!

    So, if you'd like to support us in other ways than just buying our books, we'll have more about that soon.

    Concordats

    Covenants are pacts made in Vitae. Most Kindred take this as a social contract, but in truth, this promise is also sealed in the Curse, though it’s hard to say when or where. Not all sects of the Damned are true covenants of the Blood, and while that might seem like a fine distinction, covenants can employ “practices” minor gangs of Kindred can’t. These include Carthian Law, the Coils of the Dragon, Invictus Oaths, and the various forms of blood sorcery — but there are other perks as well.

    Concordats are one of these perks. Similar to bloodlines, these are fraternal groups the dead can forge, albeit based on political or religious persuasion rather than family ties. Like covenants in miniature, they’re dedicated to a particular purpose, albeit narrower than those of the five great sects. As such, concordats are rarer than true bloodlines; many Kindred can’t tell the difference, and considering the mutable qualities of the Blood, some may have even become true lineages over the years.

    Concordats are less defined than bloodlines. They’re more like templates that get passed from city to city, sometimes dissolving when they feel they’ve fulfilled their vows. A few have more storied histories, especially in local settings, and some have been reformed through spontaneous invention. They might even have different names depending on where they operate, though they tend to share a common one. They’re also not unified by any particular ideology beyond what their local covenant has set down.

    In theory, they’re functional, not political, working for the good of all members of the covenant. In practice, it’s more complicated than that. Concordats can attain a great deal of power even beyond their factions, but they’re also bound by their vows, and if they move against their comrades for selfish reasons, they lose access to their special abilities.

    Systems

    A vampire joining a concordat needs a dot of Covenant Status and a willing Fidelis (see below). Any member can act as Fidelis if she has higher Blood Potency than the initiate. The only other restriction is that a vampire cannot be part of a “true” bloodline when she joins or forms a concordat.

    Concordats are open to all clans. A handful may restrict membership to certain Kindred families for political or social reasons, but none are supernaturally restrictive (that anyone knows of).

    A character can only have one concordat at a time, though nothing says she can’t join another at a later time.

    The Vow

    Concordats are always created with a purpose in mind, something that can only be accomplished by twisting the Blood so much that it creates a mystic link with their covenant. This purpose is called a vow, similar to both a bane and a Touchstone.

    A concordat member must follow her vow to the best of her ability. Fulfilling it and incurring a breaking point before the end of the night grants a +2 on detachment and an additional Beat. However, breaking it instead applies a –2 on detachment and grants no Beat for risking Humanity. These effects apply once per night; if a character fulfills and then violates her vow, or vice versa, the most recent action applies to any breaking point.

    A vow must be feasible and can’t be inherently fatal: A concordat can’t vow to sunbathe,. Vows are also usually open-ended, but some are time-sensitive, sworn only to deal with grave emergencies. The Storyteller adjudicates all ambiguities, and she’s free to make decisions based on a given character’s personal beliefs.

    Disciplines

    When a vampire joins a concordat, she replaces one common Discipline in her clan spread with two new powers associated with each group: a common one (Animalism, Celerity, Obfuscate, Resilience, or Vigor) and a signature one (Auspex, Dominate, Majesty, Nightmare, or Protean). The player chooses which common Discipline to replace. If a vampire already favors one of these powers, she gains a Discipline of the player’s choice as an alternative, though it must be from the same category (common or clan). If she favors both Disciplines, she gains an additional clan power.

    For example, a Daeva Red Eye (p. XX) gains Auspex and Resilience. Her player drops Vigor, leaving her with Auspex, Celerity, Majesty, and Resilience. Conversely, a Mekhet recruit gains Resilience, and because he already favors Auspex, his player decides to replace Celerity with Dominate.

    Concordats never gain access to unique Disciplines like Cachexy, Crochan, Dead Signal, etc. These are the province of true bloodlines. However, concordats in the Circle of the Crone, Lancea et Sanctum, Architects of the Monolith, and other covenants with access to blood sorcery can bestow Ritual Disciplines as favored powers, though their vows are stricter.

    Gift

    Concordats always gain an inherent supernatural gift, the effect of which increases as a vampire gains Kindred Status in her covenant. This gift is loosely tied to the concordat’s vow, at the very least providing members an advantage in keeping it. Concordats can sometimes access special Devotions or Merits, but unlike bloodlines, these aren’t always exclusive to the group.

    Creating a Concordat

    To form a concordat, a vampire in good standing with her covenant must make the concordat’s vow to a character with five dots of Kindred Status in the same faction; in cities without such characters, concordats in that covenant aren’t possible. If this is a concordat of her own design, she must have Blood Potency 5. If it exists elsewhere or previously did so in the city, she only needs Blood Potency 3, but she must have tasted the blood of a former/current member or have a blood tie to one. She must also have five dots of the concordat’s favored unique power at at least a dot of its common one.

    Once the vow is agreed upon, the high-ranking vampire sacrifices a Humanity dot, and the other sacrifices a Blood Potency dot. The latter serves as the concordat’s first Fidelis, the equivalent of an Avus. A vampire cannot make a vow to herself.

    The first Fidelis has no power over her concordat beyond the blood bonds she might form with converts. If she dies or falls to torpor, the concordat won’t end, as she’s a political leader, not a mystical one. As long as one member of the concordat exists, they can pass on their vow.

    Breaking the Pact

    Concordats are indefinite groupings, not permanent ones. If a vampire joins a true bloodline or sheds her last Covenant Status dot, she incurs detachment at Humanity 2 as if she had broken her vow; succeed or fail, she’s no longer a member. Her affinity powers revert or change as appropriate, and she’s no longer bound by the concordat’s vow. She keeps any Disciplines she purchased at a cost break, but she loses that discount and can no longer access the concordat’s gift. She keeps any Merits or Devotions except abilities that tie into that gift; Sanctity of Merits applies, and lost Devotions are refunded in Experiences.

    A concordat can voluntarily disband. If a majority of members in the city agree and spill a Vitae each in a short ceremony, they can declare their vow fulfilled. They lose all benefits as described above, but they suffer no breaking points.

                          Sort of! We initially set out to talk about the ins and outs of vampire socialism/liberalism/conservatism/etc. in first drafts, but what we ended up with was, frankly, rather boring, and ultimately not that different from what you might read in a Wikipedia article (turns out you can change "money" to "blood" and cover a lot of Kindred political economies without much heavy lifting).

                          Instead, we talk about Carthian appropriation of human ideologies in more practical terms, with five example "experiments". For example, we have one called The Welfare State, which uses a social democratic model, and another called The Impartial Meritocracy, which is pretty much what it sounds like. We also have one called The Uneasy Compromise, which is effectively the default Carthian domain. In ideological terms, we talk more about how Carthians differ in changing the All Night Society rather than where they fall on the left and right. We also include several factions, which are very diverse ideologically.
