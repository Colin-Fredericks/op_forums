            Nitpick - modern psychology gives more weight to the idea that power doesn't corrupt, but rather just enables. The overwhelming majority of one's personality is fixed by one's genetics and early formative years. Power changes your environment, and grants new reactions to environmental stimuli, but it doesn't fundamentally change you as a person. Someone that respects the privacy and innate rights of other sentient beings isn't going to change. Studies show that people with a strong sense of compassion and empathy respond positively to gaining power, rather than negatively. Power does nto corrupt. It heightens pre-existing ethical leanings.

            The larger factor of mental shift in Mage actually comes from the Orders and their cult-mentality, combined with peer pressure and group think effects. Which has nothing to do with their magic but rather basic human psychology. But, putting that aside for a moment - the majority of horror in Mage doesn't come from corruption. It comes from messing up with your power because you didn't realize the full implications of your actions and making things worse. Hubris isn't corruption. Hubris is thinking you know best for people, and things spirling out of control even when you have the best intentions. Its trying to be a good person and effing up because you know just enough to get into trouble. "Power corrupts" is not a theme of 2e mage (unless we're talking about the Abyss, which is a different can of worms).

            I also feel compelled to point out that a mage that doesn't cast spells is also a part of the game. Its literally built into High Wisdom.

            ------------------------------------------------------------------------

            So, where does the monstrocity of being a mage come from? My personal take focuses on Obsessions and the Nimbus. Now, Obsessions don't have a lot of mechanical kick to them, but I personally play it like the Obsession Condition, where you get constantly distracted and upset and frustrated away from working towards them. And the Nimbus Tilt? It spreads like a disease, infecting people around you. You alter reality around you just by existing. That's kind of scary when you really think about it. If you are a Mastigos with a Nightmare-ish Nimbus, then you spread misery around everyone you spend time with. Think about it. That's sickening. Moros could be making the world around them rot even with being perfectly Wise. Even if you have a more benevolant Nimbus, you're still messing with people's lives just by existing in a way that vamps and wolves and lings don't. Now, add on the spells spreading that Nimbus.

                                    Well, here's the thing. Whether or not you personally believe power corrupts, the saying is true in the Chronicles of Darkness. Combine that with the Supernal Realm's Blue & Orange morality and the positive reinforcement mechanics behind Obsessions and Arcane Exp and corruption becomes second nature to the Awakened.

Most power sources in the core CofD gamelines do that pretty well to. Point is, a Genius' mind and personality is forever altered by the power that courses through their minds. Pare that with the nihilistic undertone and academic exceptionalism subtext and you have a witch's brew of corruption.

                                            MCN,

                                            While I disagree with your counter argument you do make two very good points. The first is that I should clarify my point further when I state that "power corrupts." The second is that you're entirely right when you say their monstrous nature drives them towards corruption. I feel this stems from the same core premise that we are both hinting at, and that is that all facets of our own world (be it modern day human institutions to mythological beings) are cast as a dark reflection within the Chronicles of Darkness.

                                            In regards to my statement about power corrupts within the Chronicles of Darkness. Let me clarify by saying that I don't mean corruption as evil, I mean corruption as distortion and transformation into something monstrous. Corruption is less about succumbing to a moral shortcoming and more about supernatural and temporal power twisting and changing a person into something else due to it's nature within the Chronicles of Darkness. A monster's behaviors and powers are a core part of their nature, to illustrate further all of the Core Gamelines power states (Blood Potency, Primal Urge, Gnosis, Azoth, Wyrd, Psyche, Sekhem, Primus, and Lair) all mechanically reinforce the idea of slowly metamorphosing into an even greater monster or aberration (yes, even Azoth. If that weren't the case then a Centimani wouldn't be able to increase their Azoth.) And all of those states empower a supernatural creature's powers, further distancing themselves from the humans they were (in the case of Vampire, Werewolf, Mage, Changeling, Geist, Mummy except in reverse as Sekhem decreases, and Beast) or the humans they wish to become (in the case of Promethean, also Mummy, and Demon) as well as enforce the supernatural banes and taboos associated with being a monster.

                                            Side Note: My earlier statement about the Supernal Realms Orange & Blue morality is in reference to the inherently alien nature of the Supernal and it's denizens. It's so beyond human understanding that conventional morality has no sway, and this is explicitly illustrated in the Supernal Being creation section in 2nd Edition describing that a Supernal entity does have a virtue and vice, but they don't adhere to any human virtues and vices. (Ex. Virtue: Orange and Vice: Blue.) This is also illustrated again nicely in Demon the Descent since their Virtues and Vice are completely alien compared to humans as well, or by the Temkhat's (might have misspelled) Immortal Virtue from the Deceived in Mummy the Curse (One literally being Corruption mind you.)

                                            And as for modern psychology being broken down when literal magic is involved...well I'll just name off a couple of things that might just do that to a human mind: Mind Arcanum, Life Arcanum, Legacies, Prelacies, Artifacts, Paradox, the God Machine, the Lower Depths, and of course the Awakening itself.
