    Lemurians are the main antagonist splat of Genius. They believe that their personal paradigms are either how the world really works or how it should work. They actually did secretly rule the world in the past, but a massive war with the Peerage in the twentieth century that involved large amounts of time travel broke their hold on humanity, and even retroactively erased their control. The Lemurian Y-splats are known as Baramins, and they represent where they believe history and science went wrong. Lemurians generally believe in working towards some sort of utopian future where the Inspired rule the world openly, and their Paradigms are openly accepted and no longer hampered by Havoc. So, basically, the Ladder's goal, except the Lemurians are all mad scientists and are the antagonist faction. And the fact that their mythical lost city is Lemuria rather than Atlantis. I merged the two, making Lemuria be an imperfect attempt at resurrecting Atlantis that went horribly wrong. This plays into some meta subtext- the devs have said that they moved away from Atlantis in 2e because they didn't want to have the game so heavily tied to hoary turn of the century western pseudoscientific gentleman's club occultism, so the Lemurian legacy here represents the Awakened who still hold on to that idea and refuse to leave it in the past. Despite being mad scientists, they're meant to be a very old and traditionalist faction. Their love of clockwork hardens back to their origins in the Renaissance, and is also a reference to Unknown Armies' Mechanomancers. It also differentiates them from the Transhumanist Adepts and the Free Council in general. They're very much ivory tower philopsher-king wannabes who hold into older ideals of science and technology rather than embracing modern technology.

                        I could flesh out their backstory more, but I already have it pretty well outlined. Their ideological forerunners emerged in Renaissance Italy, as a group of early technomages and mad scientists who latched on to the popular notions of cosmology and the spirit of progress. Ironically, while the Free Council currently sees them as hidebound past-dwelling elitists, when the first "clockworkers" emerged they were very much like the Free Council of their era- open-minded and willing to embrace new philosophies and technologies, and seen by their more traditionalist contemporaries as sleeper-loving libertines. It did help that prevailing Renaissance notions about enlightenment and the idealization of the classical era fit in very well with Awakened ideals. They continued to grow in power and influence over the years, and the rapid pace of technological and social development seemed to be proof of their ideals. However, they inevitably started to wane. Decades turned into centuries, and their promises utopia still seemed very much out of reach. Humanity didn't seem to he getting any more enlightened. The clockworkers continued to plan and scheme and gather power and materials. When the clockworkers embraced Theosophy, it was out of desperation more than anything else, a fervent desire to believe that their ideals were still relevant and powerful. This grasp for relevance spurred them to muster all their power and resources and attempt to finally create their new Atlantis- which ended up almost destroying them. While the Great War made many Sleepers question the wonders of science and technology, the Lemurian Crisis made quite a few Awakened in the Pentacle start to doubt their utopian dreams. But the clockworkers survived. They say that after the crisis, the only people left were the mad, and that's why the legacy has the goggles-and-death-rays reputation it has now. That's the thing about mad scientists- they don't give up. No matter how many times they fail, they pick themselves up, sew their limbs back on, and build an even bigger robot. The clockworkers, despite all their setbacks, aren't going anywhere.

                        To be perfectly honest, I don't care that 2e made Legacies Path first. I'm still going to handle Legacies on a concept-first basis. Though, I am considering changing it to a set of five legacies, one for each path, each with a different emphasis. Together, they make up the Lemurian "faction". Hmmmm, maybe a Legacy with a different emphasis and attainment for members of each path? I'd still keep that there are more Obrimos than any other Path in their ranks. I'd say that technically Pathless can join, but the Legacy's obsession with order means that they're rather distrusted. I also had the idea that there was a clockwork legacy for each Pentacle faction, but in modern times only the Lemurians and the Austere are left.

                        So, I think there's potential for that from a homebrew standpoint. Create a new type of superlegacy that encompasses a legacy for each path under its umbrella. That could work for the supervillain legacy, as well.

                        Let's see:

                        For the Lemurians:
                        Obrimos: Pretty obvious. Pretty much what's been described.
                        Acanthus: Focusing on the "machinery" of time, and using their affinity for luck against it- using Fate to quash uncertainty. Not sure what their new arcana would be.
                        Thyrsus: Nature's just one big machine, after all. They'd be focused on seeing patterns within the seeming chaos of life. I'd give them Matter, too.
                        Mastigos: I dunno, they focus on the machinery of the mind? Their version of the attainment focuses on leading crowds of people and helping groups work in unison.
                        Moros: Something something alchemy.

                        For the mad scientists/supervillains:
                        Obrimos: Mad engineers
                        Acanthus: Mad physicists
                        Mastigos: Mad psychologists/evil masterminds
                        Thyrsus: Mad biologists
                        Moros: Mad chemists

                        You know, it was surprisingly hard to come up with a concept for each one, so I'm not sure where to go from here.

Their focus on clockwork isn't for its time-related aspects. Matter, Forces, and Prime all have clear connections to the idea of a mechanistic cosmos.
