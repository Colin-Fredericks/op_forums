    Telekinesis stands out as a Numen which an entity can use without being Materialized, that allows for the same kind of effect (damaging a target), and is most definitely supported by the fiction and literature. See basically every representation of a poltergeist ever.
    Telekinesis costs Essence and relies on there being objects to manipulate; I'd allow that for the same reason I'd allow an entity to use its Influences, but Blast is literally just a costless ranged attack originating from the entity itself and there's precious little to support "this thing can strike you but you can't strike it" as an intended effect of the Manifestation Effect whose most obvious application is providing a framework for "as overtly supernatural as a mortals-story seance gets without something going completely wrong" — this should not be a normal outcome for a Manifestation effect that takes less investment than attaining a physical presence in the material world out of the hazy sensory fog of Twilight.

                                        Originally posted by Satchel View Post
                                        "Entities in Twilight can only attack or be attacked by other ephemeral beings of the same type, unless the attack utilizes the entity’s bane" feels salient to the discussion, given that the effects of the Image Manifestation are "the entity's Twilight form becomes visible to material entities."
                                        It seems to me that it’s clear from context that this paragraph refers to the preceding paragraph, “Ephemeral beings roll Power + Finesse to attack. Their attacks inflict bashing damage unless the nature of the entity a spirit with metal fists, for example) indicates that it should inflict lethal wounds instead. Some entities use weapons, in which case roll Power + Finesse, then apply weapon damage on a successful attack.”

                                        That describes what an “attack” from an ephemeral entity is, which the following paragraph then explains can only be done to other beings of the same type. Blast, on the other hand, is never described as an attack.

                                        “The entity may wound opponents at a distance. Range is equal to 10 yards per dot of Power, and the entity does not suffer range penalties. If the activation roll succeeds, the Blast wounds as a lethal weapon. The entity may increase the lethality of its Blast by paying Essence — every 2 Essence spent increases the “weapon” by one lethal damage. The maximum weapon bonus is equal to the entity’s Rank.”

                                        Blast does not allow the entity to make an attack at any point. It, like other Numena, requires an activation roll, and if that activation roll is successful, it wounds a target at a distance of up to 10*Power yards as a “weapon”, which is written in quotation marks, presumably to distinguish it from an actual weapon attack.

                                                        I will be frank: the Blast Numen is an attack in every way that matters and insisting that it doesn't count as one just because "the entity can deal lethal damage to a target at a distance as though it had a ranged weapon" is not outright called an "attack" (when Numina include things like "this ephemeral entity is armored and uses a different Attribute to calculate its Defense" and "this ephemeral entity is easy to overlook" as similar innate costless abilities) reads as an obvious bad-faith interpretation.

                                                        If Blast is supposed to be able to affect material targets from Twilight, there is little point in Numina like Telekinesis or Firestarter (which have the R tag and can therefore be reasonable assumed to work from Twilight if they can textually work from the Shadow) being printed in 2e in general, since a simple "depending on the nature of the entity, this may have additional effects on the target or the surroundings" rider like we've seen printed in the writeups of specific entities would cover much of their practical function.

                                                        Blast is already a very useful ability that is expressly described in terms of its utility within the game's systems for combat; it does not need the ability to break the standard limitations of "physically hit something from Twilight to the point of Health damage" to earn its keep.

                                                        I can’t see why anyone who agrees Blast can affect material objects from Twilight would think Image would be required in order for it to do so.
                                                        That's where the problem is coming from:

                                                        1. I do not agree with this supposition, because, again, "an encounter with a spiritual entity does not have to have any telegraphing of its presence whatsoever for it to immediately grievously harm you" is neither a common media touchstone for Chronicles nor in line with 2e's higher incidence of explicit and implicit establishment of the supernatural typically having reasonably visible connective tissue between phenomena (e.g. the whole reason we have Manifestation Conditions as a system of built-upon narrative cues instead of continuing to run Materialize et al as Numina with no use prerequisites).
                                                        2. This discussion is literally happening because of the forwarded supposition that an angel using Image is sufficiently Manifested to attack with the Blast Numen.
