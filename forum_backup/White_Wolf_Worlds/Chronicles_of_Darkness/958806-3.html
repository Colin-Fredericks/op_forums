                    Originally posted by Prince of the Night View Post
                    It was removed because of Balance considerations? or because it was seen as saying potentialy undesirable things about theban sorcery.
                    Because of thematic concerns. When the merit was first released, it was highly controversial because (in the eyes of many people) it effectively turned Theban into a watered down version of Cruac instead of something within its own right. In general, there seems to be a desire to have Theban and Cruac have distinct power sets and abilities with little overlap, each standing on its own abilities. This merit kind of threatened to undermine that difference, as well as remove the potential for Livian heretics and other cross-covenant shinanigans.

                    At least, that was my take away from reading the comments and thread when it came to complaints. You can see all of those still if you check out the Acolyte blog for that particular release.

                                                I think it's got a lot to do with fluff and aesthetics (a reader prefers how one covenant is described and depicted over the other), the goals that characters of either covenant can have (the Acolyte's "I'm a vampire who has no hangups about it" vs. the Sanctified's "I'm tempting and punishing mortals for the Lord"), and the powers that you get for joining either one (Crúac vs. Theban Sorcery).                                                     From what I've seen with the various players I've had, it's not as clear cut. I live in Europe, nutty religious upbringing and roleplayer witch hunts aren't as common as they seem to be in the US from what I read sometimes. Gamers can easily be percived as immature manchildren, but nobody would actually go on tv and seriously call them satanists. I dunno, maybe they do that in Norway or Finland, but I haven't seen or lived that here. That doesn't mean the Lancea is seen as a great thing, but it's nothing personal usually. Lots of people I game with are atheists and don't see a church like group as an enviable form of government. We also probably easily liken it to Catholic Chuch which, depending on your point of view and/or what historical period you're talking about, can be a pretty decadent, tyrannical and corrupt body with a lot of political power, so while the crazy zealot bunch is part of the Lancea imagery, it's also easy to see it as a kind of more spiritual Invictus.

                                                    From what I've seen, people seem to consider the Carthians the default "good guys" and the Ordo, a nice option. I've had one player I threw into the Lancea Sanctum's arms and who made his next character part of it when that one died, another one who wanted to play in it from the get go. One character joined the Circle after I revealed his sire was the head acolyte in town and another one could have joined, but then got tired of her character and made a new one. So when it comes to my players, they seem to prefer playing Lancea than Circle, but that may be also because of how I play them.

                                                    As a GM, I actually tend to prefer using the Lance et Sanctum. It allows me to easily defy player expectations. To me, the decadent, tyrannical and corrupt thing is pretty cool (like it is for the Invictus) because it doesn't mean the covenant is necessarily evil or good, but that it's a mess of potential internal conspiracy to be the one who's seen as the cool priest. I don't like to make them villains or good guys, just very ambiguous and let players decide if they're willing to side with them occasionally. One of the biggest dignitaries of the Lancea, the Cardinal (or former Cardinal) of Rome is actually a pretty decent guy. Shady and perfectly willing to order a hit on someone if he thinks it's necessary, but usually fair and willing to help when it can improve the image of the convenant. Sometimes, it's just funny to let players be paranoid towards a religious figure they would instinctively distrust only to shock them with the fact he's not that bad.

                                                    I also tend to have trouble portraying the Circle of the Crone for a variety of reasons. For one, while I can easily picture (even if it's inaccurate in the end) a corrupt church, a corrupt aristocracy, a shady mad scientist/freemason cell or whatever the carthians can appear as, the pretty alien nature of how the circle of the crone seems to function is hard for me to translate in game. Usually, I kind of protray them as a ragtag bunch of various pagan cults or one local variant of that. It's still kind of shallow so I usually try to throw some flavor at the players and hope they don't dig too far. I just usually don't really see how that thing would work while not looking too much like the other covenants. Reading the covenant book actually helped a bunch. I really liked the whole mythological roles thing with the maiden, hero, lady in red, etc. I thought that was great stuff and I find it's a good basis for creating NPCs.

                                                    The other reason is their whole being monsters thing, and the covenant book didn't really help with that. I mean, wow, if a city group of, like, 15 to 20 acolytes did half the stuff described in the book, their humanity would plummet like crazy. They're barely better than Belial's brood. Maybe it's just me and I usually do softcore vampire in terms of how cruel and dismissive towards killing my NPCs are, but still, There's just something borderline caricatural in the way the Circle is depicted sometimes. From the book : "If all were perfect, they could build a grand sanctuary in the middle of the city, as dark and magnificent as the gods themselves. The light of the moon would shine upon pillars of black marble laced with fire-red carnelian." I mean, wow, I didn't know losing your humanity also meant losing your sense of good taste.


                                                    So yeah, I guess whatever the covenant, I always take a step back from the official books so I can portray them as actually working. Plus, I dunno, but me and my players can only take so much stumbling upon baby being sacrified by a mustache twirling witch. I like to make my NPCs act for actual reasons and not be unnecessarily cruel (even a vampire that's mostly surrendered to the beast, to me, will be violent because someone is in the way, not going out of their way to murder kids for kicks), and I guess that makes them not that monstrous (the other side is the actually raving and more "evil" NPCs stand out more). From experience, I'd say that fits me and my players just fine.
