    Just because someone else wrote something up doesn't mean a good alternative isn't welcome.
    If you think it's a good start, unique take, or whatever else makes it worth sharing, go for it.

    Same goes for alternatives for official legacies: If the original is good, a new take expands on that; if it's bad, the new one could replace it.

    The only unwelcome things, as far as I'm concerned, are ideas that basically do nothing unique and interesting. Even if it sucks, a legacy might include some neat ideas to scavenge.

        The Tamers of Blood are largely unknown and fairly notorious where anyone has heard about them. They have an intimate understanding of Sympathetic connections, generally they talk about it in terms of family. Blood is thicker than water, family first, these are their ideals. Families can be wonderful, wholesome and nourishing, but they can also be warped and twisted, stunting growth. Those related to you can be your greatest source of help in reaching your greatest potential and they can also be your worst enemies manipulating you for their own ends. Some say you can't choose your family, the Tamers of Blood know better. The fact that a family can go either way is one of the biggest hindrances to the Tamers of Blood. The good ones just want to be able to help those closest to them, the bad ones get suspicion cast on the rest of them. It's true after all, their attainments allow a high level of control over those they're Bonded to, and therefore can be abused.

        Many Tamers fall somewhere on the periphery of the Awakened society. Seers don't have a problem with them for the most part because the Attainments can easily be used to further their ultimate goal of oppression, but the family unit itself is too small a focus for the globally minded Seers most of the time. The Pentacle on the other hand hears about the bad apples and worries about the Tamers being Left-Handed. Like any Tamer legacy they can be found in any Order in very small numbers, but the ones who gravitate to them the most often are the Guardians and the Arrow. The Guardians because it can give them greater control over their labyrinths and the Arrow because it can allow them to select some of those they've dedicated themselves to protecting and giving them a greater level of security. Silver Ladder sometimes join and use the Attainments as a way to keep tabs on Proximi and to ease the process of keeping the lines pure. Free Councillors might join in order to Bond with Sleepers they have attachments to that they're unwilling to forsake simply because they have Awakened. Just as frequently the Tamers are Apostate or Nameless building up cults of personality or fear. Because of their Attainments the Tamers are able to frequently operate via middlemen which can make them very elusive targets for enemies.

        To take advantage of any of their Attainments the Tamers of Blood require a ritual in which they and the person they are bonding to each take a point of lethal damage and exchange their blood. This ritual must be engaged in voluntarily, there might be coercion involved in getting that voluntary consent, but they have to be aware of what they are doing (Though not necessarily of all the consequences) and able to say yes of their own free will. This is important, if it's ignored the ritual will simply fail to create the appropriate bond. The exact technique varies, the blood can be consumed, or the wounds can be pressed together to force the blood to commingle. Some really forward thinking Tamers even go ahead and do blood transfusions. The lethal damage from the ritual must be allowed to heal naturally, and always leaves a scar as part of the process. I don't think there should be an upper limit on the number of people that a Tamer can Bond, but I do think that if they're not doing it for cabal mates then anyone who is Bonded should be represented by Merit dots appropriately. Allies, True Friend, Retainers, Mystery Cult Initiates, maybe even Contacts can all be appropriate, but it shouldn't be free. I think that with these two limitations, the necessity of voluntary involvement and the requirement for representing the Bonded with Merits can cut down on making this legacy too ridiculous. The merits can also help determine the kind of familial social focus the Tamer wishes to embody. Further limit on their power is that they can exercise their Attainments on only one given individual at a time, and for the Optional Attainments Resistance is allowed.

        All Tamers of Blood are bonded not only by the mentor/apprentice bond of a Legacy, but also by the Bond created and used by the Legacy itself. Experiencing the bond oneself, and having its influence exercised upon you is how the Attainments are passed on. This allows for a very closely knit and insular Legacy that has very regular internal communication. Of course, depending on one's Mentor/Apprentice relationship this can be just as oppressive as the worst Tamer relationship with any of his Bonded. The Tamer at least has some means of fighting back.

        Note: Many of the spells you can use to affect yourself seem to be constructed in such a way that with a dot higher you can affect someone else. I assume then that the inverse is also true. Because of the fact that I place requirements on Bonding and who you can be Bonded too in order to limit the reach of this legacy and because the Tamers are supposed to be able to use their Attainments on Bonded as easily as on themselves there are some Attainments which will appear to be a dot lower than they are supposed to be.

        Attainment 1: Gnosis 3: Space 2: Upon the successful completion of a Bonding ritual the Mage and the Bonded share an Intimate Sympathetic connection. If for whatever reason some level of anonymity was desired and true names or the like were not shared it does not negatively impact the strength of this connection. Furthermore, this Bond does not wane over time from lack of use, though typically they see quite a lot of use. These Bonds can be concealed, or frayed as per normal but they will regenerate or reappear regaining one step of strength per dot in Space per day. The Mage who created the bond may relinquish it, at which point it will proceed to operate as normal. The Mage may now slip into the senses of the person he has bonded with as an instant action using Presence+Empathy+Space. This is similar to Scrying but operates only through the senses of the Bonded, the Mage sees what they see, feels what they feel, etc. At Space 3 this duration becomes Prolonged, at Space 4 it may use the Advanced Prolongation factors.

        Optional Life 2: The Tamer once slipped into the senses of a Bonded may exercise some influence over him. The effect is similar to Body Control or Control Autonomic Functions and successes on an Instant action Manipulation+Persuasion+Life roll can be spent towards effects similar to either of these spells, the roll can be contested by the Bonded as normal. This can be a great boon, granting the Bonded the ability to heal small injuries more quickly, regulate his breathing, ensure a sound night's sleep or a feeling of satisfactory fullness after a good meal. The positive effects of this Attainment can even justify the Bonded being able to buy dots in Physical Merits as the Tamer tweaks their bodies in small, subtle ways to make them hardier, healthier individuals. On the flip side it can be a curse, the Bonded can't sleep, can't feel comfortable, constantly sneezes all at the whim of another. Of course the Mage can benefit himself as well, quelling hunger, resting peacefully, being more alert, etc.

        Attainment 2: Gnosis 5: Space 3: The Tamer becomes metaphysically closer to the Bonded whose senses he has slipped into. With an Instant action of Presence+Subterfuge+Space he may assume the Sympathetic connections of the Bonded. This allows him to mask his true identity when casting a Sympathetic spell, it also can mask the location of origin of the spell appearing to originate from either the Tamer or the Bonded at the discretion of the Tamer. Particularly cruel Tamers will use their Bonded as unwitting catspaws, while more benevolent ones will attempt to lure the enemies of their Bonded to them in order deal with them in person. Paradox if it occurs is outside the scope of the Legacy's control for now and has a tendency to appear at whichever location it can harm the most. As a side note, though the sympathetic connections might conceal who really cast the spell the Resonance of it can still give the Tamer away. As a result many Tamers study Prime as well in order to further mask their casting.

        Optional: Life 3: The Tamer may Trigger the Lizard Brain in the Bonded he has slipped inside of, or himself. He rolls Manipulation+Persuasion+Life as an Instant action which is contested by Resolve if the Bonded wishes. I probably don't need to get into the ways that this can be used to benefit or manipulate someone.

        Attainment 3: Gnosis 7: Space 4: The Tamer co-locates into the body of the Bonded and Space warps him slightly to fit within the confines of that body, generally by shrinking the Tamer if need be in certain dimensions. This Attainment cannot be used to create a violent collision between two bodies occupying the same space, though further use of Space magic could. While the co-location is Transitory as the spell and requires an Instant action and a Presence+Expression+Space roll penalties to the roll can be used to increase the duration slightly. At Space 5 this can be Prolonged as normal. The greatest use of this Attainment of course is that the Mage may conceal his presence almost perfectly and cast spells as if they were at Sensory range. This allows him to exert a significant amount of influence in protecting or manipulating his Bonded or those around the Bonded. As per the co-location spell the Mage may Reflexively choose where to end it. If he chooses to end it at the location of his Bonded he appears next to him.

        Optional: Life 4: The Tamer not only co-locates into the Bonded he can use him to act in a fashion similar to the Human Minion spell. The roll is Manipulation+Persuasion+Life and is contested by Resolve+Gnosis if the Bonded wishes. The Bonded might not want to fight it, being fully one with the Tamer can come with benefits. If they cooperate then the Mage may aid physical actions the Bonded takes as if the Mage had chosen to use an Assist action and had rolled successes equal to his dots in Life. The Bonded also gains access to any of the Fighting Style Merits the Tamer possesses if the Tamer chooses to share them.

            Path: Obrimos
            Nicknames: Sparkle idiots, Wonders

Only related lineages ever claim the same patron, so they seem to be coordinating on some level. They do act mightily to fight the Lie, as a rule, though they have really idiosyncratic definitions. Whether or not they are sane, they are receiving direction that coordinates them and makes them further their patron's goals---usually in ways compatible with Destroying Followers of the Lie.

            They dress up in gaudy clothing, and call on their patron's powers in showy and overt ways. When multiple claim the same patron, they call on different ascpects. The Lunar Team is six strong and have a member for Waxing, Waning, Half, Crescent, New, and Full. The Solar Trio is Dawn, Noon, and Dusk.



            1st: Flash Dance
            Reqs: Spirit 2, Forces 2
            The Nimbus of the Wonder adds color, tones, and textures that are very cheerful to represent their patron. If they represent a specific aspect, the changes specifically reflect that..
            The character may activate this attainment by calling out to their patron for the power to transform. Beginning this is an Instant Action which unveils their Nimbus, and grants them the same effects as using a Dodge action. The transformation takes two rounds after this to complete.
            A Wonder may perform one transformation per dot of Spirit before they must recharge by some sort of communion with their patron. The effect lasts a scene, though can be extended for a Willpower.

            From initial activation until the transformation is complete, a defense raises which converts hostile energies that are directed at or near the Wonder into a map of the Nimbus, creating a phantasmagoria of color and cheery tones. The effect is quite flashy, provided attacks are coming in. Treat this as Armor rated equal to Forces.
            The energies released into the Nimbus apparently act as some sort of mystical effect, which empowers the patron. The Wonders believe that they can recieve direct intervention with enough of a display.

            The effect provides the patron and/or any of it's related servants essence as if the effect is an example of their type. This is not creating or converting essence, though, as much as generating it through an appropriate mundane act. Much in the same way, sufficient energy must be converted by the shield to have any appreciable effect. Storyteller's discretion.

            Any time the Wonder Dodges, the defensive effect and Nimbus return for that round in addition to the normal effects.

            Optional Prime 2: Even hostile spells are affected by the strange defense field effect. Subtract Prime from hostile spells or attainments while dodging.


            2nd: Cleansing Celestial Prism
            Reqs.: Spirit 3, Forces 3
            While "Transformed" the Wonder can already Dodge to defend against most forms of harm, but this attainment takes that a step farther: Their patron's protection from spiritual threats is granted at any time they are Transformed. This applies Numinous Shield exept that it costs Willpower to use.When Dodging, there is no cost.

            When countering Numina, the effect seems to channel the essence either to the patron or any servants nearby. This attainment activating also counts as an event that attunes a location to the patron.

            Optional Prime 3:
            The Nimbus gains partial solidity, allowing Sleepers to sense it. The resonance of the area is dramatically pushed towards the Patron. Treat whatever occurs in the scene because of the Wonder as occurring once per level of Prime. This literally multiplies the rate at which the effect takes hold.


            3rd: Star Beam
            Reqs. Spirit 4, Forces 4
            The Wonder calls on their patron to dish out some sort of damage. This is an attack spell, and almost always fires a glowing symbol of the patron.

            The specifics are chosen when the power is learned, though it's possible to change this power by visiting the patron. The effect will deal harsher damage to the specific foes that the Wonder is "called to fight."
            Grant Materialize to an ally of the Patron, a fetish weapon (usually ranged) appears upon transforming, the Wonder may use Blast, or some other effect are all possible.

                    A mage I have in a Dark Ages chat game has been developing the following Legacy, meant for interactions with the Hedge and such. It's a bit of a niche, but it works for games where it might come into play.
                    Children of the Thorns
                    Mages aren't supposed to be in the hedge. That's what nearly any Changeling thinks that knows what they're dealing with when they see some crazy human studying a goblin fruit with an eery stare. Most mages happily agree with this sentiment, the risks of the thorns far outweighing the rewards. However, a choice few Acanthus feel an odd sort of sympathy with the hedge, for one reason or another; perhaps they Awakened near or past a gateway, perhaps they learned much of their own magic by studying changelings, or maybe they've seen a bit of the bounty found within; tokens, oddments, and goblin fruits, amongst an infinite number of unique locations and mysteries.
                    Any spellcaster quickly learns that the Hedge is one of the most dangerous realms a mage has access to, however. Even the Shadow, Underworld, and Astral can generally be considered 'safe' when one is dealing with packs of Briar Wolves that aren't responding to a disciple's Life magic, or upon feeling the terrible sense of loss when the thorns rip out a portion of their very Soul. Those that can find a way to not only deal with these threats, but to attune themselves to the realm that births them, however, may find a niche no other mage can possibly follow.
                    Primary Arcanum: Life
                    Parent Path: Acanthus
                    Appearance: Survivalists tend to be in outdoorsy clothes a lot, though on a minimalist approach (They don't often carry around a lot of equipment), but also have minor bits of the hedge decorating them; a square of a briar wolf's pelt hanging off a belt, for instance, or a necklace made from Hedgebeast ivory (Although in either case, these trophies are usually gained from self defense, not a tendency to hunt the creatures wantonly).
                    There's also a supernatural aspect to their appearance; their nimbus uncloaks and affects the surroundings when they're in the hedge. This isn't quite the same as a Changeling's Wyrd affecting the hedge, or even how a low Humanity Vampire affects it, but it still makes them a bit more obviously 'inhuman' to others in the Realm.
                    Nicknames: Tattered Druids, Hedge Survivalists, Those idiot wizards (usually by changelings)
                    Sample Oblations: Spending an hour in the hedge, eating goblin fruit, studying an oddment, trifle, or token.
                    First Attainment: Survival Instincts
                    Requirements: Gnosis 3, Life 2, Fate 1, Prime 1, Survival 2, 'Hedge' Specialty in either Occult, Survival, or Investigation.
                    Effect: The mage gains an instinctual understanding of the odd flora and fauna of the place, and can, with an instant action, inherently determine what a Goblin Fruit or Oddment's effect would be on him (Goblin Fruits, per Equinox Roads, affect mages like they were changelings). This requires a Wits + Survival + Life roll, and an exceptional success is needed to determine more discrete details, like infection from spores or discerning between two very similar hedgefruits that do different things. Since this becomes an innate ability, not a spell, it is not limited in dice pool by a mage's Wisdom. The mage also becomes better able to handle these fruits to begin with; treat his effective 'Wyrd' as half his Gnosis (round up) when determining the number of them he can carry.
                    Similarly, his connection to the fauna means he can detect nearby hobgoblins and other fae nearby him; similarly to Sense Life, but attuned to Fae, the mage can sense any creature of Glamour within Gnosis*2 yards of himself, as well as their relative location within that space. For piercing obscuring effects, assume a Potency equal to the mage's Gnosis + Life.
                    Optional Arcanum: Fate 2
                    By combining basic knowledge of Prime with a connection the same threads of Fate that interweave through the Wyrd, the mage becomes able to protect himself, to a small degree, from the Thorns of the Hedge. Like Changelings, they instead lose a bit of power whenever the thorns would reach for the soul; 1 mana is lost per hour spent in the thorns. However, if they run out of mana, their Wisdom begins to be stripped away as normal.

                        Hmm, well, yeah, with the Life primary, having them gain features akin to a kith would be interesting. Something that helps them actually navigate the realm would also be very useful. I do like the idea of Key for the second attainment optional.

                        With Fate 4, Life 4, an effect to banish the tearing of the thorns for a number of scenes seems interesting. (Roughly analogous to an Amnion in Dreamtime)
                        Alternately, and related, the attainment could grant them some ability to create paths and waypoints in the Hedge, though costing Willpower and Glamour. This could also grant a moment's respite, and would still require power expended.

                        This would fit the more fae-like relationship with Fate that Mages have than the Changelings, and still require expenditures of power to remain in the Hedge. (I'd prefer that Mages who figure this out would do so in a manner that is different than that of Changelings.

                        One thing that might serve is using Sympathy rules somehow, as part of the scheme. Magi who achieve a certain level of familiarity with a region of the hedge cease to be seriously harmed by it, and even come to navigate it simply, since that story becomes old hat, and newer challenges await. Not sure exactly how to run with that theme, just now, but I like it.



                        Re: the Tamers of Blood:
                        I'd consider shifting away from Space as the primary, and moving towards Mind.
                        Yes, normally only Space can build up bonds of Mystical Sympathy, but Mind and Life do present reasonable ideas of how to link to something. "My blood" and "My friend" fit Life and Mind, respectively, and certainly fit mystical (and literal) notions of Sympathy. A legacy could tap that, even if Supernal magic divides things a bit differently.
                        (The Familiar bond is a precedent of using Spirit to create a tight sympathetic bond.)

                        You'd need rules for how these bonds work. I was thinking exactly as natural bonds, except the link improves them Indefinitely by one step. The effective Sympathy is maintained at a level higher, so even severing the bonds with magic never fully works, and an Intimate relationship leads to "Sensory" connection. Not literally Sensory, but no penalties for casting. This would also make an emotional bond between the parties more obviously reasonable.I was thinking that a blood tie could allow a character to cast on the linked party as if on himself, but with any sympathy penalties.

                        I think the link should be bidirectional and not severable until a later attainment, so even Tamers of the Blood don't lightly become blood of someone else. At some point, it seems to me that they should be able to link two other parties by blood, and just their own. Rules for severing blood ties should also be present, and make them of potential cross-over interest.

                        This would make mental projections reasonable, which could use the normal rules. If you want the projection at the second attainment, though, I was thinking that the projection itself could bear any sympathy penalties on all actions, including sensory input, but qualify as fully present otherwise. If you have that Sensory link, no penalty exists.

                        Just some notions. I like the rough shape of it.

                                (Well, I'm trying not to be argumentative, here, in case it seems that way.)

                                I consider the brief descriptions as what mages think they know about any given legacy, and guidance as to themes, so I'm up for changing anything as long as the results would still seem fitting. If a legacy is known to create and manipulate Sympathy, the assumption it's a Space thing makes sense. (Most magi wouldn't have even heard this much, and deeper research could reveal, shucks, maybe it isn't Space, so I'm comfortable with such a shift.)

                                Switching to Life as the channel that establishes the link instead of Space (in a manner vaguely similar to a Familiar, through blood ties) makes it the perfect Conjunctional arcanum. Making Mind Primary cleans things up fairly well. When I sketched it out, mostly following your outline, I used Life as both Optional and Conjunctional arcana, and declared that they can target their linked others as if targeting their own body, though with Sympathy penalties. Their control over their personal physiology, then, also represents manipulation of that of their linked bodies, as well. This grants access to more powerful effects through Life as a direct consequence, but doesn't make these the attainments themselves, which I like.



                                Just because a legacy is left-handed does not mean that the legacy is evil, intrinsically or otherwise. Going with powers that are clearly sinister is one approach, but not the only one.

                                Imagine a pre-Free Council conflict between the Tamers of the Elements and Diamond cabals. Some of the Tamers of Blood might well apply their attainments to root out Guardian infiltrators and to assassinate Silver Ladder dignitaries. (not to mention, remove any particularly successful Adamantine Arrow generals or champions and extract any useful secrets from the Mysterium) That's a fantastic way to get yourself declared Left-Handed, even if they started the fight. It's also a great way to save your allies and friends.

                                A Lichtor abruptly has a change of heart, and declares the mage war over. Investigation reveals his beloved Sleeper daughter explaining to him that the mage war will lead to ever more tragic deaths, so he needs to end it. Unsurprisingly, he dedicates himself to hunting down the Tamers of Blood. Was that maneuver clearly evil? It prevented loss of life.

                                Of course if you have the ability to kill, you are probably going to attract those who want to.

                                If this isn't an intrinsically nasty thing, one interesting line of thought is in how the various lineages either embrace, reject, or regulate the darker aspects of their legacy.
                                As old as they are supposed to be, there could be martial, philosophical, peace-maker, and family building branches. The Tamers may well accept that the Left-Handed forms exist, possibly calling them the "Tainted." ("Bad Blood" and "Black Blood" have a good ring to them, too.)

On top of that, I don't think too many people would disagree if I said Mind is basically one of the strongest arcana, and although I also really like Mind it gets its due in a lot of Legacies and spells already.

It's the Life stuff I'm having the most trouble with. I'm currently toying with the idea of expressing "body control" that the Tamers are known for as the ability to switch Physical Skill and Attribute dots around by draining them from a Bonded and adding them to the Tamer and also allowing for the reverse to happen so a Tamer could create a super-Bonded by moving his own Skills and Attributes into the vessel. That could be the first and second Optionals respectively. The third Optional I'm pretty happy with, but might be hard to justify from the way I think about the Legacy in general since I can't think of terribly many situations where a Mage would puppet his own body with magic. I also have an additional idea for allowing damage to be shared among the Bonded and the Tamer similar to the Rose of Eden legacy, but that might be kinda bland since that's less in theme with "body control" and Life can manage to accomplish a lot more interesting things.

                                    I believe it should be a double-edged sword. It's not inherently Left-Handed, but the way the Attainments have been developed should stand as the kind of thing that can do potentially great good and great harm. For instance, with the Space stuff I've got up there, the Tamer could be either spying on his Bonded pretty much whenever he feels like it, and interfering to manipulate and control them like the meat/blood puppets they are. It's also set up so that they can be highly aware of what's happening to these people who are supposed to be so important to them so that they can better protect them. So yeah, while I'm totally with you on the fact that perhaps some Mage was just a jerk and started prosecuting them as Left-Handed, I'm also of the mind that maybe that jerk of a Mage was -right-. I'm not going to outright answer the question myself, that's for individual players and STs to do, but that means that I should create a setup where it's possible that it could go either way.
