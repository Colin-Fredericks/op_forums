    Alternatively, they could just be a bit better at maintaining a balanced harmony. If they're having a breaking point towards Spirit, they subtract one die for each point of Harmony below 4, and toward Flesh they subtract one die for each point above 6. This rule would, of course, replace the rule for other werewolves where they subtract dice based on their distance from 5. That way it's just a little bit easier for them to not get flung to the extremes of Harmony. Since they're possibly the werewolf-iest werewolves, it would make sense for them to just be more balanced than those who grew up as something else.

                    Originally posted by Thorbes View Post
                    I consider Conditions to be an advantage even when negative.
                    Hm, I assume you mean because of the Beats you can rack up. Well, I'd rather not give Hishus more advantages to make up for the others being too advantaged. Then it'd just be Werewolf: the Forsaken Except Now You Have a Couple More Advantages. In that case, I'd just nerf the others harder.

                    For Urhanu, I think it's fine as it is actually. They don't start with the native language for free, they'd have to buy a dot of the Language merit for that, and they can't start with Mental Skills as their primary. I think that's enough of a starting penalty to have an extra dot of Primal Urge (although maybe I'm wrong and again, I'm not really happy with this as their boon). Their Condition is one that they start with and can eventually get resolved. I think it's balanced fine as is with the Hishu, though feel free to offer alternatives.

                    Ulle'u's boost comes from their more balanced Harmony, and they start with a permanent condition that deforms them in some way. Both of these would last throughout the entire game. Easy enough fix, you just don't get Beats for their Condition. Consider the Harmony boost the replacement for that.

                    And for Uralathu.... I don't know if starting at a lower harmony is really a disadvantage enough. Like yeah, you have a Ban, but it's the same distance from 5 as 7. You can eventually lose it. Plus, they have the ability to get into the Hisil easier. I have two ideas for a solution to this:
                    1. As easy as it is for them to slip into the Shadow, the Uralathu find themselves having a harder time leaving. -2 modifier on rolls to enter the Flesh.
                    2. The spiritborn struggle to adapt to the ways of the Flesh. It just doesn't stick with them as easily. They gain an extra die on every roll on breaking points toward Flesh.

                    also considering naming my Spiritborn something more appropriate like Ulludib (bootleg sumerian translation meaning 'those that cross', after their habit of breaching the gauntlet upon changing and their affinity for it as werewolves).

                        Sorry to go gravedigging, but I'm afraid I'm gonna need this thread again, as I'm actually going to be trying to run a game with this homebrew very soon, so I need things at least in somewhat of a functional order before we start the game. They'll be starting as cubs, not yet a part of any tribe, so the players will be experiencing their introductions to the new tribes both as characters and as players. While we're on that topic, this is what I have at the moment.

                        I'm planning on having 10 Playable Tribes, two for each aspect of Renown, each dedicated to their own ideals and sacred hunt. I'm not entirely sure if I should have it devoted to prey, as I'd need to find a way to divide up the prey into more specific things probably, but I'd be happy to hear alternatives as well. However, the fallen tribes are already complete!



                        There are three Fallen Tribes, one for each part of the triad. Each tribe is quite large in comparison to the others, each having both new initiates and fallen members from the other 10 tribes. However, due to their aims conflicting with not only each other, but also the playable tribes, their power is not very consolidated, with each 'tribe' for itself. However, they have many allies both in the hisil and the flesh, and can be dangerous foes regardless.

                        Moon-Touched
                        Hoping vainly to appease their mother, they sacrifice their own sanity and stability of form to protect their mother's beautiful creations. They ally themselves strongly with lunes, and have more spirit-born members than any other tribe, fallen or otherwise. They aren't often seen, as they often are fortified in the deepest, most mysterious reaches of the Hisil, only rarely treading on flesh's lands, but when they do, they wreak havoc, devastating the gauntlet and spreading chaos in their wake.
                        Due to their near-universal madness, it is difficult for outsiders to comprehend their motives. They seem to follow their own whims, and those of their closest allies, the many spirits of the Hisil, in particular Lunes. They have a close working relationship with the Beshilu, who also strive to tear down the gauntlet.
                        It is believed that in the days of old, before humans had spread the solidification of the gauntlet beyond what Helios had already managed, the Moon-Touched were much more widespread, tearing open the gauntlet into massive, gaping wounds, being only held in check not only by the Uratha, but also their fallen counterparts. These days, however, they seem to be more on the retreat, only occasionally moving on the offensive, often directing their attacks in places they can cause the most havoc, often spreading mass lunacy in the hearts of cities by doing so. For them, Lunacy is a sacred part of their forms, to spread the insights they've found in their own madness, and sometimes, to even spread their curse. Indeed, many of their numbers not directly born from spirits are Uratha and wolf-blooded created from such raids (which ironically, can create their greatest enemies as well).
                        They are patronized by Silver Wolf, one of the two Firstborn who abstained from hunting Urfarah. It is said she had an important role in convincing Luna to offer the rest of the Uratha some form of amnesty, however, her brood were the only ones completely exempt from her punishment, being completely unphased by the presence of silver, and indeed, use it often in the name of their mother. However, Silver Wolf and the Moon-Touched proved an object lesson for the rest of the Uratha, as their close relationship with the Lunes kept them from their duty to protect the balance and eventually caused the insanity that now permeates their tribe. Silver Wolf is now known to the rest of the tribes as the Mad Wolf, and few of the Uratha outside of her own tribe can recall her true name, or her previous aid.

                        The Crowned Ones
                        The Uratha are almost universally shunned by their father the Sun, with one exception-- the Crowned Ones, children of the Sky King himself. Populated mostly by the sons and daughters of Helios, the human-born, they are the bringers of order and control. Unlike the Moon-Touched or the (OTHER TRIBE), they operate more silently. Their battles are not fought with tooth and claw, but with cash and cutting wit, bringing the world to a pristine, bright, and pure order right underneath the noses of the other tribes. When they can help it, they prefer to avoid the other Uratha, as they hold them in disdain, as unclean, messy things unfit to rule. However, their ideals for the world are directly opposed to the Uratha's and when they are able to, they will capture, drive out, or put down their cousins in much the fashion they would to a stray, rabid dog.
                        They take a scientific, clinical view of the Hisil, as a threat to the world they call home, having observed the danger many spirits can wreak. To them, the way to ensure the safety of both sides is to solidify the gauntlet, that none may enter or exit. Inevitably, this stifles and chokes out the life on both sides, yet in the eyes of the Crowned Ones, this is good: a pure slate, wiped clean, on which they can create a utopian society alit by the rays of the sun.
                        They do know how to use spirits to their advantage, aware that not all are a threat to their cause. Helions, of course, are an obvious ally, but other spirits include those of technology and human origin (provided they can be brought to heel) and lastly, the Azlu and their spider kin, are closely allied with the Crowned Ones, both working together to solidify the gauntlet. However, tribal culture often dictates using spirits more as tools than allies.
                        It is said that their patron, August Wolf, was one of the two Firstborn who refused to hunt Urfarah. While Silver Wolf fled to the deep Hisil, howling her grief to the stars, August Wolf remained witness to the shame of his brothers and sisters, and some believe that it is he who first brought the word of his father's death to Helios, disgusted by his murderous siblings. His own nobility in the face of the crimes of his siblings, according to the Crowned Ones anyway, allowed him and his brood the continued favour of Helios, and they continue to carry out his will to this day. To the other tribes, August Wolf is now known as Ice Wolf, for his cold indifference, inaction, and betrayal of his own siblings, and for the cold touch that sears the lands under the domain of their sun.

                        The Destroyer's Blood
                        Though all the Uratha carry the sins of the murder of their father, and all continue his duty, none take this role more seriously than the Destroyer's Blood, to the point of severe fault. To them, the forces of creation are already beyond control, beyond redemption. The only thing left to do is do as what they claim Father would have done. Destroy, devour, burn it to the ground.
                        So it was back when the Destroyer's Blood first began, the Uratha under the banner of Bloodied Wolf, the strongest and most fearless of Urfarah's get. Bloodied Wolf would sink his teeth in his prey and never let go, even if it may mean his death, for he knew the purity of the hunt would only be found in destruction, be it of himself, or his quarry. Almost all legends agree, both within the tribe and without, that it was Bloodied Wolf who led the decision to slay Father Wolf when his time came, and it is said that it was his teeth that found Urfarah's throat at his last breath. As the new leader of the forces of destruction, he led his brothers and sisters on the warpath, to subdue the onslaught of maddened creation and stifling order.
                        Perhaps that is their claim, but in truth, the tribe of today is now under new management. No one has heard from Bloodied Wolf in any form in countless years. Perhaps he finally died in battle as he wished, only finding peace in his own destruction, or perhaps he saw the weakness in his own tribe and chose to abandon it. Whatever the case, Bloodied Wolf is gone, and the Destroyer's Blood, believing themselves the heirs to his duty, have taken on new patrons, the Maeljin, which has ultimately been the killing blow in thoroughly corrupting what claimed to be the 'purest' of tribes, as there is no purity in the hunt of the Destroyer's Blood. A wolf does not hunt out of malice or vice, but out of necessity, taking what they need to sustain themselves and the world around them. The werewolves of the Destroyer's Blood hunt because they can, and in thorough excess at that, reveling in the atrocities which they commit.
                        They draw in a surprising amount of members, which is necessary, considering their swift rates of membership turnover on account of their violent lifestyle. Part of this is just a matter of rapid reproduction, as killing is not their only avenue of vice any longer. However, the sad truth of it is that they also embody what may be the darkest thoughts in every Uratha. With the incredible power and lack of control in their form, werewolves are able to commit terrible things that they could never get away with as mere mortals, without compunction except for the laws of flesh and spirit binding them. Those who cannot resist such temptations are often drawn in to join the Destroyer's Blood, and continue along their warpath of destruction, of both their self and the world around them.
