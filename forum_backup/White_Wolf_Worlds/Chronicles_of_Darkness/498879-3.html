                    Originally posted by ArcaneArts View Post
                    Dedicated but not wholly successful.
                    Actually, they are fairly successful often, at least for the PCs (rate of NPC success at achieving Hell is up to individual Storytellers). Or turn into an exile. The whole "achieving your goal for Hell" is a major theme of the game that is stated to be achievable as the basis of the game.

                    Plus, being turned back into an angel is a result of being caught by a hunter angel, not just being scrapped or recycled. Happens quite frequently. You might end up losing your free will, but there are times a good chunk of your memories remain instead of being completely scrubbed.

                    So, bouncing between demon and angel can be frequent, technically. Or divert to Exile and stay there (could actually be a reason why someone was Exiled, in fact).

                    More on topic, I'm wondering about the new Mage stuff revealed in that preview ( http://theonyxpath.com/supporting-ca...the-awakening/ ). Mainly, the idea that proximi are supposedly equal to revenants, and implying that sleepwalkers are somehow the equal of ghouls. Considering that sleepwalkers don't seem to be using Awakened Magic, while ghouls use Disciplines, I really have to wonder if that's accurate.

                    With the revenants pasted under "Point of No Return," I'd have put proximus equal to ghouls (half-template), sleepwalkers equal to mortals with the Mortal-only merits in the vampire book as well as blood bound thralls, and then the generic herds as equal to hired sleeper staff. Will have to see the book for final call, but I really think that mortals with Protected, Producer, or Clear Sighted really resonate more with how sleepwalkers are portrayed in the blog than sleepers.

                    But, at the moment, I would put Sleeper as an notation under the Any Human block, then Sleepwalker equal to Sons/Daughters and Fed with Vitae, with Proximus on par with Wolf-blood and Ghoul.

                                                        I used Excel 2007, a lot of customized "predefined shapes" and arrows. The idea came up when I was toying with ideas on how to allow the players more explicit choices in what they could play (saying it's "allowed" doesn't mean people will go out of their way to try it if it's kind of hidden in an appendix chapter). Since the only cross-over I will allow in my own game is Werewolf + Vampire, when I make additions to the initial diagram, it will be for completeness's sake and educational purpose. I strongly doubt most players really need to be "spoiled" of all the supernaturals, but it would be useful for GMs.

                                                        At the moment, since there seems to be many things in motion, and I'm not super familiar with the other lines, I'll wait for 100% sure / concise explanations.
