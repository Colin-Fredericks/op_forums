                                Originally posted by Kaimuund View Post
                                That's great advice, thank you! I appreciate it a lot.

                                Acrozatarim

                                I'd love to hear some examples and scenarios if you don't mind. I feel that would be a great benefit to the community and myself, having not had a chance to play with the new rules quite yet, and still kind of mind blown by the updates.

                                If you are in a sharing mood, I'd love to hear how two uratha were whacked in just as many turns. I'm really against player killing, but know that a playtest is a completely different environment, and the 'how much can they take' has to come up. I'm curious about how much they took.
                                Well, just to start with the tale of the two dead Uratha. Turns out it was actually three turns, not two.

                                So, the four Forsaken of the pack versus four fairly new Pure. The Pure have something to prove, heard of the new Forsaken pack and came in to try and provoke a fight with them by going for their Locus. Their aim was to steal Essence, have a brawl, hopefully beat up the Forsaken a bit and prove their dominance. The PCs, for their part, have pretty much the same idea but from the other side - they want to wound the Pure and drive them off.

                                The actual tactics they went for, though, caused the whole thing to turn into a bloody clusterfuck.

                                The Pure characters are slightly more combat-tuned than the PCs - most of them have honed a form (usually Urshul) and their natural weapons, and have slightly more Gifts. The mainline fighter has buffed his Stamina in Gauru form and has some appropriate fighting style dots (the one focused on all-out assaults, iirc). That said, they're also built more on thematics than optimisation so the pack leader, for example, has some stuff around using Urhan a lot for ambushes. The PCs themselves are fairly normal starting characters, with every Auspice apart from an elodoth present.

                                How it goes down is that, rather than lots of posturing, choosing targets to spread damage around and generally beat the other side up in a healable fashion, the PCs decide to focus fire and take down the person they believe to be the most dangerous target - the Gauru specialist. Legit thinking, but completely taking a werewolf out of the fight like that - especially someone they know is focused on being the heavy hitter and Gauru-user - generally involves killing them.

                                As such, when the battle starts, the Pure initially think it's a regular 'I'm harder than you and I'm going to show it' fight, but the player characters all dogpile one victim, the Gauru specialist. He flicks to Gauru asap, but the sheer volume of damage rolling in from four attackers is too much - especially the Rahu whose 8-again kicks in nicely and, more worryingly, the ambush from the Irraka. The Pure try to shift their own tactics and pile damage on the Irraka, who was already carrying a little bit of Aggravated from an earlier encounter.

                                Both sides are lugging various arm and leg wracks at this point from the Urshul form attacks.

                                Round 2, both the Gauru specialist and the Irraka get the crap pounded out of them The Irraka flicks to Gauru but it's too slow versus the pile-up of damage he's taking, including a fair amount of Aggravated before he ever hits the killing form; the Gauru specialist just gets severely injured despite the full health-track clearance of Lethal he's had for two turns. The Rahu also flicks to Gauru to help speed the killing.

                                Round 3, both the Irraka and the Gauru specialist die.

                                After this, both sides have also wounded the survivors of the other enough that more people could well die or be maimed unless (or even if) they all hit the killing form. Both sides realise this has already gone far further than they ever wanted it to, and the Forsaken allow the Pure to recover the body of their dead packmate and retreat.


                                - Chris Allen, Aberrant Line Developer, Freelance Writer
