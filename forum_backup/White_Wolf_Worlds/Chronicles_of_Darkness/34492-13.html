    182) The city is a powder keg. The Carthians came to power decades ago after wiping out the Invictus, but the only things they have accomplished is instituting an only-somewhat functioning democratic system, and splintering into three separate factions who all hate each other's guts. Too bad they have this nasty habit of dragging the mortals into their squabbles. Crowds of rabid communists clash with mobs of out-and-proud fascists, and moderates vindictively play the system to make sure they stay comfy. Homelessness and unemployment is rising. Minority neighborhoods are one more bad day away from rioting. The Sanctified Bishop didn't leave his haven in the Puerto Rican neighborhood for six months, and only emerged to start meeting with the local Crone, who loudly proclaims dire portents for the city and all of its citizens. The Dragons just hope their bolt holes are deep enough, that their vaults are sturdy enough, to weather whatever storm is coming.

        183)

        The prince's pet crafter of whimsical lethalities has made a wonderful new thing. It's a thing of elegance and beauty. A poem of engineering and ergonomics and occultism really- a rosewood, rowan, and figwood stake, driven by cunning little brass and tungsten innards. Once planted in a victim, it smoothly unfolds itself deeper into the wound, inexorably towards and through the heart, unless removed. More amazing still, the runes exposed on the interior metallic surfaces compel the victim's Vitae against its own sympathies, and spill to the ground in rebellion.

        Everybody but its creator is more worried about the fact that the prince's prototype fire-and-forget stake has been stolen, and the fact that it can put you in Torpor and slurp you dry at the same time.

        Followup Twist- things become much worse when an Amaranth'd vampire's leavings indicate that the extraction point was directly through the ribs, from the heart. Apparently the thing was also secretly designed to be... basically a powered straw, for Diablerie.

            184) It was supposed to be an iron-clad alliance. The Invictus drove out the Carthians when they tried to launch their little coup in the 50s, and they declared the triumph of order and hierarchy over anarchy and democracy. Less than a decade later, the Ordo Dracul threw down with the Circle over some theological dispute that turned ugly, and the Kogaion himself personally slew the Crone; when the dust settled, the Defiant said it proved the superiority of reason and secularism over the lunatic gibbering of a mad goddess. The Lancea et Sanctum saw its numbers shrink to less than a dozen in the aftermath; it was a new order, one ruled by secular logic, with no room for any gods or prophets muddying the waters.

            The First Estate cozied up to the Defiant quickly, congratulating each other on vanquishing their respective philosophical rivals. High-ranking members of the Invictus started encouraging childer without the necessary aptitude for cutthroat politics to pledge themselves to the Dragons, as a safer alternative to being a flailing sardine in a pool of barracudas. The Dragons, for their part, began performing Scales of the Dragon for Invictus members in exchange for boons. The Lancea et Sanctum muddled along, taking in any neonates who were cast aside, or ancillae who suffered a fall from grace that would otherwise be fatal.

            All that changed a couple months ago, when important texts disappeared from Ordo safe houses. The Invictus publicly scolded the Defiant for such carelessness, and it seemed like the Ordo had suffered a permanent diminishment in standing. Then, six nights ago, the Sanctified Bishop marched into Elysium, with the culprits at hand: two members of the city's police department, ghouls by the Sheriff, and ordered to steal the texts by their regnant. Elysium exploded into finger-pointing and dire accusations. The Dragons swore dire repercussions against the Sheriff and any who had knowledge of the plot; the Invictus, for their part, have remained tight-lipped, and mixed coteries are suddenly bereft of their Invictus members, replaced instead by Sanctified who shake their heads at the consequences of godless ambition, and hint that the theft was but the first act of a string of betrayals decades in the planning.

                185) One and a half centuries ago Patrick Brighton rode to war for the Union against the forces of secession. He was issued a warhorse named Chestnut by his regimental quartermaster. At the killing fields of Shiloh he was Embraced by a vagabond Kindred who saw something of worth in the wounded and dying young officer. His first act after learning about the power of the vitae was to ghoul his loyal warhorse. From that night onward Brighton and Chestnut remained together through wars, Kindred politics, the Depression, and societal upheaval. All throughout that century and a half Brighton has remained dedicated to Chestnut, making sure the horse gets plenty of exercise, feeding her a living meal whenever she's feeling the urge for blood, and making sure she is treated with a level of decadence most Daeva would balk at.
                And now Chestnut is dead. Murdered.
                Brighton is livid as only an elder can be. He's made a city-wide announcement to the Kindred as well as the lupines in the countryside; 20 million dollars, a mansion in the suburbs, and a favor for the capture of whomever did this deed to his beloved ghoul.

                186) Patrick Brighton has gone off the deep end since his ghoul was killed. He's been taking out his aggression on the kine, giving his mind and soul to the Beast with each atrocity he commits. The Prince approaches the coterie, and tells them to find a way to curb the worst of Brighton's excesses before his friend becomes a draugr with the all the strength and cunning of an elder.

                187) Involving the lupines in Kindred politics was one thing but now Brighton is sending messages to the merlins, changelings, and stranger beings. The Prince is livid, seeing this as an open invitation for other factions to try to take over his city and disrupt the carefully constructed society of Kindred politics. The coterie is deputized by the sheriff to patrol the city and make sure the any other supernaturals get packing or end up in a grave.

                188) Brighton's reckless actions have caused enough disruption. Kill him. Brighton has connections outside of the city and they may try to retaliate if they find the Prince has had a hand in Brighton's death, so make it quiet. The Prince even offers to conduct a ritual to remove any black lines on any coterie member's aura after the deed is done.

                                191) The corporate headquarters of a powerful and far-reaching company are located in your city. This particular corporation is also completely vamped up- its board and executives are made up entirely of vampires, ghouls, and blood dolls. So when a federal investigation uncovers massive corruption and criminal activity, the Kindred of the city must rush to cover up as much of their involvement as possible, lest their secret world be exposed by the IRS. But how did the Feds get all this information anyway? And how did they get their case pushed through so fast, when it's usually notoriously hard to nail corporations for shady business practices?

                                        193) redroom.com is a schoolyard legend, one of the "death sites" that pops up in modern web-savvy folklore. Supposedly, you can type in the name of someone in this website that appears only at midnight, and the next night they'll be attacked and drained of all blood. Not only are these hits being carried out, but the younger kindred find the website to be even more disturbing. It really does only appear at midnight and it's impossible to hack or trace, meaning that whoever is running it isn't just a merciless Beast-ridden serial killer, they're also one hell of a programmer. And why would a vampire set a site like that up anyway?

                                            194) There's a young crusading politician running for office, and he's getting the Prince worried. His rhetoric about cleaning up the town and fighting corruption is familiar, but something about the way he phrases it makes the kindred fairly uncomfortable- he talks about "burning out the infection", "taking on the leeches and parasites sucking this city's blood", and "driving a stake through the heart of the forces who think they can control us". Not only that, but his staff has proven impossible to penetrate or corrupt, and a Mekhet elder using Auspex Five swears he went to spy on him but ran into some sort of invisible wall around his hotel room. What's more, the Kindred could swear there's something...off about him. The people at his rallies don't look right, the way they stare at him all glassy-eyed, never looking away, always falling silent as soon as he talks.

                                                    196) No one in the city remembers. In times long ago, before even the american revolution, the city's Kindred launched all out war against the Rotgrafen, driving them from its ports and haranguing them across the east coast of the americas.                                                             198) Surveillance is bad news for vampires (and, really, any other non-ephemeral supernatural creature). It hasn't quite progressed to the point it has in our world, and that's partially because vampires use their sway in politics to push through plenty of anti-surveillance and anti-drone legislature. But the governments of the world are only going to take this for so long. One group of vampires believes they've found a devil's deal solution, and have been in covert meetings with powerful politicians: various national governments and even the UN itself pass powerful anti-surveillance laws, but in exchange groups of vampires have to agree to act as secret police for the various governments.
