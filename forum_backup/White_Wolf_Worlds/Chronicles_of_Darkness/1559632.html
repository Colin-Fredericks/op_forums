    Thinking about Patterns...

    04-26-2026, 07:02 AM
    Tass, mana, resonance, and magic.

    And patterns.

    I think there is some confusion about what Tass is, and I wonder if a theory of mana can expand into a theory of magic...

    But first, I am curious what people think about patterns. In 1e core there is a spell which allows you to tear apart a material pattern and get mana. I think a Matter spell, which suggests that basic material objects contain mana, or at least are held together by mana. Such that the act of tearing them apart releases the mana. This was not republished in the 2e core, either because the metaphysics has changed, or because game balance suggests that limiting access to mana is a good thing. But you can use Death to do something similar to a living pattern (a la Devouring the Slain). That along with blood sacrifices - killing a creature is described
    It's death releases mana from its pattern.
    Then you have Tass seemingly forming in material patterns based around the matter near a Hallow, and (alongside resonance, the general vibe of the area).

    Platonic Form describing "mana behaving as it does within Tass"

    And Eidolon forming mana into the complex "Prime patterns within living beings and environments".

    So do think there is a Create Tass spell, possibly as a Compelling spell (at a Hallow where there is untapped mana) or a weaving(?) version with mana from another source... Maybe as a reach effect of Channel mana? Lasting by default (like Destroy Tass).

    But is there also space for a Weaving/Patterning effect Transform Tass? Changing either the resonance or physical nature of some Tass, possibly making it into food, or something safe (if it came from a tainted Hallow).

    On the other side, Spells as mana, not solidified as Tass but dynamic expressions of pure mana, like a plasma to Tass's crystalization. Prime already has effects it can do to spells... Supernal Dispellation, but what about the patterns of non-supernal magic, can mana be transformed into the pattern of Cruac? Or the Alchemy of a Promethean? Would it require other arcana aswell? (Since you clearly wouldn't allow a Prime spell to create a Time effect just because magic is in the purview).

    Magic as mana suggests the ability to drain a magical effect of its power by sucking the mana out, or otherwise disrupting it. Would Prime be able to disrupt Alchemy? Would Vitae be a solid type of Tass whose pattern can be destoryed (perhaps with Death+Life) to release mana? What other possibilities does looking at things in terms of pure patterns evoke?

      I wouldn't personally go with the idea of a spell being "made" of mana just from a general preference for spells to not have some kind of materialist intermediary component that creates their effect. The spell doesn't exist in the Phenomenal; it is an act of arrangement in the Supernal that locally changes the default state of what the Phenomenal is. You don't make a thing hotter by taking hold of its atoms and shaking them up, you alter the platonic basis of how hot it is.

      Because mana is described as the palpable bleed of the Supernal into the Fallen World, that might have instances where a bit of mana is evident in a subject with an active spell upon it (although I would argue insufficient to constitute a point of mana to be transferred into a mage's pattern), but the mana is not the substance of the spell. It's more like waste heat from a machine in operation.

      (Also not a case where the mana is a thing that gives the spell away under most circumstances, because Sight can just skip straight to seeing the Supernal process that is the spell. At best, there might be cases where a spell that has been veiled happens to leave waste mana that can still be picked up, and otherwise it's an observation made by mages obsessed with the mystery of how mana even exists in the Fallen World.)

      Sacrifice and scouring are described as drawing mana from a pattern, and I'm inclined to reconcile this in terms of "something about the nature of life as a phenomenon inherently makes it a subject of Supernal bleed" and "something about the nature of deliberately destroying life makes that a subject of Supernal bleed".

      With mana understood as a phenomenon of the Supernal expressing itself in the Fallen, and spells considered in terms other than being matrices of mana inducing physical results, you wouldn't be able to emulate effects of other non-Supernal sorceries.

              Some general notes:

              1. Siphon Integrity, the core 1e spell for getting Mana from things that don't have discrete points of Mana invested in them from spells or other processes, only allowed a mage to use it to take Mana directly into their own Pattern once a day, only gleaned one point of Mana per three full points of Structure damage inflicted, and was a Prime 4 spell that always rolled for Paradox. Given that Devouring the Slain still exists and engages with the limits on Scouring, I think it's less likely that it wasn't ported for game balance reasons and more likely that it either got cut for wordcount in an early draft, wasn't considered a priority for the writer in charge of Prime spells for the corebook, or was considered to need conjunctional Arcana in an edition where the corebook's focus was on spells that only needed one Arcanum to cast.

              2. Patterns are described in 1e as the mystical "body" of a phenomenon describing the Arcana of which it is composed and in 2e as the aggregate Supernal symbols corresponding to a phenomenon (that aggregate itself made up of individual Forms). Tome of the Pentacle introduces the concept of lacunae, which (under normal circumstances) prevents temporal sympathy from being used to look further back in a subject's past due to that subject not existing in its current form even in a vastly different state — the straightforward example provided is that you can't simply take a rubber object and use Postcognition to look at the tree it was cast from, so in a practical sense destroying anything necessarily destroys its Pattern.

              3. The Mage/Mummy crossover material in the Dark Eras Companion's Princes of the Conquered Land describes Mana as shaping the world (in comparison to Pillars being the world). Scrutiny in Focused Mage Sight allows a mage to enhance their ability to understand a Mystery by spending Mana and observing "the shapes it makes as it sublimates into Fallen reality." As a phenomenon, Mana falls under the purview of Prime, and Prime is paired with Forces as an Arcanum because both are manifestations of power in the same sense of acting as positive presences in reality — connecting back to the Pillars and the first point above, they're related to the Ka, the vital force that determines if something is alive. Mana bleeds into the Phenomenal World from Hallows until they scab over from hitting their tass capacity, and Supernal beings in the Supernal World regain Mana daily simply from "being" there (as opposed to being summoned into reality).

              4. Tass is Mana that fills material forms, which is why Platonic Form describes its effects the way it does; given the third point above, there is no real contradiction in the lateral/more refined conceptual move described in Eidolon, and given the first point above, we may reasonably describe the overall situation as "amounts of Mana too small to typically count as full points flow through the Patterns that describe a given phenomenon." Signs of Sorcery implies in its section on Mana Batteries that mages can create tass by simply channeling Mana into an object, which causes it to crumble away once its reserves are fulling withdrawn (when using tass normally involves consuming it anyway), so changing the physical form of tass could be managed either by moving the Mana into a new object or using Matter to change its shape and other qualities. Changing the Resonance of a given amount of tass is likely as straightforward as using Prime Weaving a la Geomancy, albeit it's probably less efficient to do so instead of changing the keywords at the source.

              5. Spells come forth from the Supernal, such that any form of Active Mage Sight can see a spell as it is being cast, though after that point the appropriate Arcana are needed to detect and scrutinize them. Spells that are cast without the benefits of Praxes, Rotes, or Ruling Arcana require Mana regardless of their effects, but within those umbrellas Mana is only needed for particular spells with extreme effects like indefinite duration, healing or dealing aggravated damage, rote quality or automatic dice results, or breaking the natural limits of Traits. Given the fourth point above and the connection of Prime with the Ka, coupled with the limits of magic with regard to souls and the Abyss, disrupting spells is typically limited to turning them off or temporarily blotting them out — the closest exception that would allow you to meddle in greater detail is Primal Transfer, which only swaps the control of a spell and which does not allow sweeping changes to what the spell does because of how spell control works more generally.

                          The simplest way I would describe the Platonic Form spell is that the nature of mana as a Supernal energy and the Prime Arcanum as covering the purview of Truth is that it's possible to mould mana into a framework of the essential Form of a thing in a way that, by virtue of existing in the Phenomenal World, can be used to accomplish the functions of that thing. I view the fact that points of mana need to be spent for those otherwise mundane actions in terms of action itself being a transitional thing that sets mana in motion, or perhaps by a property of a thing that the framework can only effectively emulate with mana flowing through it.

                          (I tend to view that "mana as framework" thing actually kind of literally, as basically "Platonic Form makes things that look like Dr Strange magic from the MCU", as the way to realise the point that the subjects are obviously magical unless masked with holograms. Even with regards to the idea that the semiotics might differ between mages, it still comes back to a point that it's not so much being able to say something about how mana relates to objects as it is how mana can be shaped around the idea of objects, will still being pretty obviously a handful of manifested mana.)
