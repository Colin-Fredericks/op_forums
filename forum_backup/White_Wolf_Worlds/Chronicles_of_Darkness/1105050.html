    Bloodline Conversion- Carnon

    06-18-2017, 09:09 AM
    Greetings, everybody! Haberdasher here with another bloodline conversion. This week, I present the Carnon, a young bloodline who take cues from horned deities of Indo-European mythology who are associated with the Crone.

    A quick note on the Carnon: their 1e lore says that they really hate the Sanctified, but then the lore turns around and points out that often, the Sanctified aren't the ultraconservative boogiemen the Carnon think they are. So I've altered their lore to make it so that they just go after those who are enemies of the Circle, kind of a wandering band of Acolyte do-gooders who show up wherever the Circle of the Crone is up against the ropes.

    I was in western Virginia on an internship for a week and a half without internet, so no internet+need to occupy my free time= lots of progress on bloodline conversions. I have a backlog of bloodlines, enough to post one weekly for about a month.

    As always, leave your thoughts and comments!


    Bloodline Conversion- Carnon
    Listen up, shithead. We've had it with you calling for our circle to be purged. You've already seen what we've done to your car and your house. Keep this up, and we'll go after your ghouls next. After that, we're doing it to you.

    A vampire who leaves behind a helpless childe in the All-Night Society is already tragedy enough. But what about when a sire survived by multiple childer who now find themselves the eldest members of a brand-new bloodline? These were the circumstances of the Carnon, a bloodline whose eldest members are scarcely ancilla, trying to create purpose for their Requiems when their sire Embraced them on impulse.

    Background: John Carnon was a Gangrel Embraced in San Francisco during the mid 60s. Born to an evengelical Christian family from the Deep South, John became disillusioned with his family's beliefs and moved to San Francisco to learn “the truth.” He threw himself into every act his former faith had condemned: he became a fan of rock'n'roll, took drugs, engaged in debauchery with both female and male partners, and became involved in occultism and neopaganism. It wasn't long before his desire to break more and more of his old taboos brought him into contact with vampires, and was Embraced by a Gangrel.

    John immediately took an interest in the Circle of the Crone: their belief in multiple deities and the powers they wielded convinced him that he had been on the right track. Carnon was never accepted by the Acolytes of San Francisco, but he did have frequent contact with them. One favorite topic of conversation was the concept of the Horned God: Carnon had become fascinated by the imagery and myths surrounding gods like Cernunos, Pan, and Kronus. Incomplete readings of Joseph Campbell (and a mistaken belief that academic interpretation is fact) led him to believe that the Horned God was a real god deserving of the Acolytes' worship. The Circle tried to correct John, but he would have none of it. He struck out on his own, living much like he had in mortal life. He discovered a new drug: diablerie. Experiencing a high that surpassed any drug, John became an amaranth addict, and in a haze of sex, diablerie, and drugs, John transformed into something strange and wild: a true Horned God. Combined with several impulsive Embraces, John's already tenuous grasp on his Humanity shattered, and he became a draugr.

    The existence of a crazed, horned vampire was a breach of the First Tradition that not even the most liberal Firebrand could tolerate: not only was John's appearance obviously supernatural, but his behavior was drawing vampire hunters from across the country to San Francisco. Pursued by mortal and undead hunters, John Carnon fled as far north as Washington, where he died screaming and snarling as he was consumed by flames in 1970.

    Back in San Francisco, John's five errant childer presented the domain with a new headache. John had Embraced without permission, and one sireless vampire was difficult enough to handle. It seemed like the unfortunate fledglings would be destroyed when the Acolytes- perhaps regretting that they had not done more to save John- offered to teach his childer in their ways. These five vampires took John's name as their own, and swore to serve and protect the Circle of the Crone in gratitude. They set themselves against the Lancea et Sanctum after learning that John had despised the covenant (John had seen the overtly Christian trappings of the Sanctified and assumed that they were simply the vampiric version of his old creed). All of his childer were dead by 1980, but that could not stop the popularity that the bloodline had.

    The Becoming: Most Carnon are men in their late teens or early 20s, though an increasing number of women have joined the bloodline. Many come from backgrounds similar to their founder: young people who reject their Christian roots in favor of neopagan practices, outdoors types, or rock musicians. The common image of the Carnon is of tall, muscular men and women looking like modern-day vikings with intricate tattoos and elaborately braided hair and beards, though they are just as likely to be wiry backpackers and hikers. Nevertheless, joining the Carnon always bestows a certain animalistic poise and magnetism to members. Carnon usually rely on power, whether it be their intellect, physical strength, or force of personality, with most having Physical or Social Attributes as their primary attributes.

    In the Danse Macabre: While the Carnon set themselves against the Lancea et Sanctum, they soon found that there was not necessarily any quarrel between Acoyltes and Sanctified in San Francisco. Since the 1980s, the Carnon have gathered a reputation for being something of a roving band of Acolyte crusaders: whenever they receive word of Acolytes and their followers being persecuted, Carnon will converge on the domain to lend aid their their brothers and sisters in faith, whether it be as soldiers or preachers. When the threat is gone, some Carnon might remain, but most will move on to the next crisis. The appearance of Carnon in a domain signals that war is coming, if it has not arrived ahead of them. Because of this, most Carnon are nomadic, or work as Hounds and Sherrifs in domains controlled by Acolytes; these domains will often make it a point of including whatever horned deity the Carnon model themselves after. A handful of Carnon also hold membership in the Invictus or Carthian Movement, especially in domains where these covenants are friendly with the Circle of the Crone; others flirt with the Ordo Dracul; but no Carnon has yet joined the Lancea et Sanctum, though several Gangrel have joined the bloodline after defecting from the Lancea et Sanctum, especially in domains where the Sanctified who exercise absolute power.

    Clan: Gangrel

    Nickname: Horned Gods/Goddess, Bucks/Does

    Bloodline Bane: All Carnon are twisted by the blood of John Carnon, slowly molded into the image of his beloved horned god. Upon joining the bloodline, all Carnon begin to manifest traits that betray their supernatural nature: some might start to leave hoofprints instead of footprints in sand or snow, others might seem to have the eyes of goats, others' shadows might appear to have horns that resemble those of deer or rams. Mechanically, all Carnon treat their Humanity as two dots lower for determining penalties or bonuses for dealing with humans. When a Carnon reaches 2 Humanity, his form physically alters so that he has actual horns, and sometimes even real hooves, which do not fade even if he raises his Humanity back above 2.

    Bloodline Disciplines: Animalism, Nightmare, Protean, Resilience

    Bloodline Gift: Blessing of Cernunnos
    The connection that Horned Gods have with horned animals like deer and goats grants them the ability to use Animalism on such animals: deer, antilope, sheep, and goats can be influenced by a member of the Carnon. Additionally, a Carnon can use Protean @@ to grow horns that deal 1B damage that use Brawl to attack, can transform into one of the above listed animals with Protean @@@, and choose to have their horns deal 2B damage and inflict the Dazed Tilt on any opponents who do not reduce the damage dealt by the Carnon's attacks with his horns.
    Primordial Visage
    The appearance of a Carnon invokes supernatural reverence and horror in all who behold it, and this is manifested most intensely when the Carnon warps his form to more resemble the Horned God. Once per scene, when a Carnon uses a Protean power, he can choose to use the Nightmare power of equal or lower dots to the Protean power free of cost on the same turn.
