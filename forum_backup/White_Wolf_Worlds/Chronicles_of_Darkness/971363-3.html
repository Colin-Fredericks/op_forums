        I've already given my position earlier which is that having a "I know you're guilty" button is a significant constrainer on a GM's plots. And that attempts I've seen to get around it become increasingly circumstantial and convoluted. So I removed the button. And that's pretty much the sum total of my position on this to be taken or left by other GMs as they wish. However, I'm still reading this thread out of interest and I feel like I have to weigh in to defend overload's position here.

        Maybe the Prince doesn't regard the lowly PCs as trustworthy enough to take their word that someone is a diablerist. Maybe the Sheriff doesn't have the power or influence to act on their own use of Auspex because it isn't proof. These are all valid circumstances in which the ability to detect in a foolproof way that someone is a diablerist isn't a show stopper to a plot or character concept. And they could be true of any given GM's campaign. But the question of whether being able to detect diablerie is a big constraint on games doesn't depend on whether there are cases where it isn't; it depends on whether there's a wide range of circumstances where it is. And I'd pretty confidently argue that there are.

        Off the top of my head for general ideas:
        • The PC group is trying to decide whether to trust friendly new NPC. It doesn't matter whether they can convince the prince of something or not, you've immediately ruled out any given NPC hiding their nefarious deeds from the player group and for a very wide range of plots, that's all that matters. They might not be able to secure a conviction but goodbye to pulling the wool over the PC's eyes.
        • The powerful and influential prince is a staple of Vampire games. Such a figure may be absolutely fine taking their own word for it and not having to prove it in a court of law to their lessers. So you lose out on plots any plots that combine such a prince with another vampire being a diablarist.
        • The Prince scenario pretty much holds true for any other elder who is in a position of power over the diablerist. If Diablerie is this heinous crime, then it doesn't matter so much if the prince / sheriff / powerful elder can prove it - they personally know it for a fact and will act accordingly. In vampire society, a court of law is not typically the thing a vampire most fears - a powerful enemy knowing you're a diablerist will be sufficient. Rumour, personal influence, two or three Auspex users agreeing and backing each other up. All is sufficient to bring down a diablerist.

        I could come up with plenty more. Having an "I know you're guilty" button is very constraining on a GM or even a player. It cuts out a tonne of character options unless Auspex 2 is uncharacteristically rare. Certainly for all of the above you can twist and turn and find ways around it, but I want to be able to just stick in a character without or campaign event without having to twist around all sorts of things to justify someone not being outed, exiled, hunted, socially excluded / whatever because it's easy to see that they're really a terrible person.

        That's my view on it. It's bad because it is constrains the campaign a lot.

                    Uh. Okay. So.

                    1. If a Vampire in a position of power doesn't care what other people think and are going to persecute whoever they want without giving a damn about evidence or swaying others to their cause, then the Domain has far more serious problems than Auspex and is going to be toppled soon regardless. Auspex isn't going to tip the scales in that regard. It doesn't matter if they have Auspex or not, acting like that is going to have every Vampire in the domain wondering if it'll be them next. And they'd be right to wonder.

                    2. Even if they do have Auspex and know someone is guilty, as we've been saying, nobody else knows. Even if they believe the vampire with Auspex this time, and that belief was right, who's to say that the vampire with Auspex won't take advantage of this? Sure, today they point out a Diablerist. Tomorrow, it's their centuries-old Rival who they claim is a Diablerist. He isn't, of course, but this is what happens when you believe an Auspex user on their word and their word alone. Maybe we've been focusing too much on the "it can't be verified" angle because that's only half the problem. The other problem is Vampires lie. Trusting an Auspex user today means he can accuse you tomorrow of something you never did.

                    3. For the "but you can't hide it from players!" argument... why can't you? If you're okay with homebrewing content, have them use a Devotion or Sorcery to mask it. If you aren't, you could have them not be a diablerist -yet-. Either way, do your players just randomly Auspex everyone they meet with the Diablerist question? If so you have problems with your group beyond the scope of this thread. (Edit: Because of Diablerie in specific, not Auspex. Auspex is kind of intended to be used frequently. Diablerie is just oddly specific and makes me think metagaming is occuring) That's like Googling everyone you meet the moment you meet them.

                    The way I see this? One option is hindering players by taking something away from their established niche (making the Secret Gathering power arbitrarily unable to gather some secrets). The other option is encourages stories by providing obstacles to overcome and doesn't take anything away from anyone except the ability to commit a perfect crime, and perfect crimes only shut stories down.

                            Vampires are often described as paranoid and power-hungry. Using Auspex regularly would fuel both of those tropes. So, not sure I agree with Maina that PCs using it regularly means your group has a problem. If they're just talking about power-spamming as a meta-concept, that could be a different argument, but considering other disciplines have free always-on abilities, I'm less convinced.

                            I'm not a fan of diablerie as a common concept - seen it abused in too many group games - so I tend to be sympathetic to the idea that it is always something that's detectable. In 2E, though - there have been a lot of ways to make Auspex more powerful:
                            • No base cost.
                            • No inherent resistance for the victim
                            • No more penalties for time (It was harder to learn old secrets in 1E)
                            • Obfuscate can no longer hide your Aura, forcing a Clash of Wills like 1E did.
                            That means it is far, far easier to detect a diablerist (however you define that). I would hazard that 75% of Mekhet PCs I've encountered through all games I've been in have started out with Auspex 2. NPCs will fall lower than that, but still. When something's that easy, I agree with MCN - you have to get pretty convoluted in your power structure to write off that chorus.

                            Personally, I think 2E has skewed a little too far in Auspex's favor, but that's personal preference.

                            If I wanted to fix it (not sure I do), I might consider a couple routes:
                            • Uncanny Perception only reveals secrets from the past X. (X probably would be 1 year. Telepathy would let you dig for older memories, but can be countered by Dominate.)
                            • Modify the "Are they a diablerist?" question to, "Are they a diablerist, like me?" (Takes one to know one. Now there's a weird political/effectiveness reason to NOT just destroy all Amaranthines automatically and becomes a cold war of sorts in the accusation realm.)
