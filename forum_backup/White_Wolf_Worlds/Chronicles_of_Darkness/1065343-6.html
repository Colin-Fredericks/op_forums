        Originally posted by Elfive View Post
        Ok, the thing about the prerequisites is that a score of two is average, so just about everyone has a score of 2 or more in most attributes.

        If you reduce all the prerequisites to two then they stop being things that guide a certain character type towards a style and just end up not mattering 90% of the time. It just loses all thematic weight.
        As well, while the prerequisites can seem high:

        1) The vast majority of them are in traits you want to have high for a combat focused character anyway.

        2) The vast majority of them are in traits that are useful on their own.

        3) These are not creation only mechanics. Some Merits are better for advanced characters, and some are better for starting characters.

            I consider a lot of these merits kinda clunky in execution. I feel a certain dissonance between them and Corebook Rules, and sadly it isn't with favor for the Hurt Locker's material. It didn't feel very good on paper, and ended up feeling not very good in practice too, so currently we removed them from our material pool.

            The thing about violence in this game is that it's really overpriced. "Sensible" competent physical fighter needs a lot of redundant skill dots, often is forced to have awkward attribute dot placement at chargen, and investing into combat related merits is insanely costly due to the fact that styles need a lot of dots to "click" together and have a lasting impact.

            Which is kinda weird, because these things might not come up at all in 3 or 4 sessions (especially if you play with assumption that "combat is a failure state" that should be avoided), while investing into something like Status or Resources could give you plenty of benefits to use each session.
            Physical combatants that can't "cheat" by using supernatural powers to gain advantage are really kicked in the balls by the system. It's easier to be a polyglot socialite than a punchperson.

                            Other thing I disliked about these materials were drawbacks in the Merits. If they were mentioned as a optional rule it would be fine, but they feel really out of place to me for the same reason why "Small Framed" does - they just feel incoherent with the rest of system. Ripples and dissonance, kinda like suddenly encountering alien random object inserted into the game from another game following different design principles. That, and they forcefeed you a specific "character concept" for given merit. Air of Menace would be fantastic for a intimidating, charismatic "Mafia Boss" type of character...except you are forced one specific interpretation of the "so scary people are afraid to attack them" making you scarred brute who is socially disadvantaged.

                            Instead of forcing a drawback on me, maybe put in a sidebar about "hey, some merits might have interesting drawbacks, if you accept them you can decrease the cost/requirement/whatever by X! Here are some examples for drawbacks, feel free to experiment with previously published merits and adding your own drawbacks to them!"

                            "Carrots, not sticks", something something. I hope this design direction will not appear again, honestly.

                                            Yes, which would open up multiple stealthy concepts that don't have to rely on being literally small or throw dice at me for taking away their health box while still asking them to give me two of their STARTING merits, which are more valuable than XP spent on merits during the game (because there are certain things that can be bought only at char gen, or things that can be *traded for* Merit dots and often are better than Merits; thankfully, both of these seem to be slowly but steadily culled and extirpated from the game)

                                            By the way, you know what would be awesome for Hurt Locker? A big list of Persistent Conditions modeling different and interesting problems that can hover around the character and fit the theme of the book. Something like Lasting Trauma section, but expanded to include other things that aren't necessarily restricted to bodily harm or a specific supernatural template.
                                            Show me how to model being a Gang Member using a condition, show me a "Stalked" Condition so a *thought of playing such character* can enter my mind, give me a Condition woven with the stuff from the Menace of Air that makes my character visibly belonging to the life of violence and fear;
                                            Each such Condition could also double as a story seed, and provide a teaching tool to the buyer: "here are multiple ways to write and balance an condition, analyze them with these guidelines in mind and you will amaze your players with Improvise-A-Condition routine meeting their exact needs!"
                                            If there is one thing I really dislike about a lot of CofD material, it's how a lot of it is "here is a cool idea seed about a mechanic or something, it's totally cool, we don't have any examples of it or any guidelines about how you can write them yourself, but don't worry, you are a big trooper, you will certainly figure something out, gotta go write something else".

                                            One of most memorable moments about my GM learning these books was a desperate, angry spontaneous outbreak of "IF I WANTED TO HOMEBREW EVERYTHING WITH NO GUIDELINES TO COMPARE AND FOLLOW, I WOULDN'T BUY THIS BOOK FOR A PRICE OF A COMPLETE VIDEO GAME".

                                                    Dominate doesn't have that drawback, because that drawback is a result of Humanity mechanic, which is a different part of Vampire template. It's a Low Humanity Drawback, not "specific gameplay option you spend XP or chargen resources on in order to improve your character" drawback. It's like saying that falling unconcious due to damage is a Prime Arcanum drawback, because Mages fall unconcious when damaged enough.

                                                    Anyway, to avoid the rabbit hole of discussing if it's a Dominate drawback or Vampire drawback or Humanity drawback, replace the "Domination Discipline" with "Mind Arcanum". Or add a line to Firefight, "You are a gunfighter, which means you are a gun nut, gun nuts are scary and will shoot you to death, suffer social penalties for it!". Or add a line to Hobbyist Clique "As a member of hobbyist clique, you suffer from a reputation of being a nerd, so take a social penalty and decrease your First Impression against people who don't share your hobby". Or add a line to Iron Will, "Because you are so adamant in your way of thinking, you are kind of a jerkass who only cares about their own opinion, take a social penalty and decrease your First Impression against people who dislike people strongly entrenched in their opinion"

                                                    That's how I perceive Air of Menace.

                                                    You could easily replace it with "Your character is scary! Maybe they are big and imposing, or their smiles and soft words strangle you like sneaky snakes! When you call deer a horse, no one in the room objects! People with less Intimidation than you need to spend Willpower in order to take an action against you!"
                                                    Why ignore +2 to Intimidation rolls? Because you can already do that with Striking Looks. In fact, current Air of Menace allows you to grab both Air of Menace and Striking Looks ("scary as fuck"), to get +4 on Intimidation dice rolls.

                                                            Ekorren interesting to see a fellow Martial Artist take a peek at this book, though I'm a little sad you got saddled with a "spirituality and performance" instructor who acted as if they were teaching you genuine self-defence skills. My master is very good at the demonstration and performance aspect (he likes to tell a story about how almost fifteen years ago someone wanted him to be the Red Ranger's stunt double on the then-current iteration of Power Rangers), but he's also no nonsense about about practical application, has trained with very scary people, and has no trouble bringing in those very scary people (some of whom are also very nice and laid back) in order to teach the more dedicated students some stuff, including me.

                                                            I like the Fighting Style Merits section because it lets you pick and choose which maneuvers you want from a Style, which I feel is more representative of how actual martial arts works in terms of picking techniques that work for you, and also a wide variety of maneuvers that I actually recognize and can perform myself. What I learned from Hurt Locker is that, apparently in the CofD, I'm actually a very scary person, which I find somewhat hilarious.

                                                            Probably my favourite non-fighting non-Supernatural Merit definitely has to be Object Fetishism, because it makes you not only more combat viable (Willpower is a great way to boost stats) but also because it gives you the opportunity to have mortal psychics with objects they've tied to their soul and draw on for magic. It certainly makes it easier to use Supernatural Merits, considering the most powerful burn through Willpower quickly.
