    Tips or suggestions for my first GMing/Storytelling experience?

    04-13-2015, 05:35 PM
    Hello everyone!

    So, I've been wanting to play Changeling for a long time now, and I finally gave up waiting for other people to run it and decided to run a game of my own. However, I've never been in charge of a game before, and I'm a little nervous about a few things:

    *NPCs
    I guess I'm most nervous about dialogue. I don't think I'm particularly great at doing voices and accents and whatnot. Male characters are going to be particularly difficult because it's hard to get my voice to be deep enough. Is there a good way to practice this so that I might be a bit more confident?

    *Rules
    All sorts of information seems to be spread out amongst many different books. It's a bit overwhelming, especially when I also want to use the new God Machine rules. The other players are all aware that this is my first time, and I know they'll be patient while I make mistakes, but I don't want to start until I feel like I have a good handle on the rules. The thing is though, I will probably NEVER feel like I'm at a good enough spot (just how I am with things), in which case I'll never even have one session. I guess I could mostly wing it, but does anybody have any suggestions as to how to deal with this, or perhaps a benchmark for starting comfort with rules?

    *Setting
    Beyond writing up detailed descriptions of locations and possible items of note ahead of time, are there any good techniques to help ensure that players can have good experience?

    *Shyness
    Do any of you who do the Storytelling also deal with shyness? If so, do you have ways of putting that aside when you go to run a game? Power poses? Yelling loudly before a game to make sure your voice still exists? Lots of energy drinks? To me it would seem that Storytelling wouldn't work well for someone who's rather shy, but I'd like to give it a go anyway.

    *Other general Storytelling tips?

    ---

    As long as I'm asking for help, I may as well describe the setting we're going to be starting off with. This is more of a 'test setting', where we can figure out the rules a bit better, and it was originally planned for the characters to be mortals, but I guess I'm being impatient.

    The players, and several NPCs (I'm thinking some mortals as well, perhaps each changeling will have to pick a mortal partner) have been selected (a.k.a. kidnapped) to participate in a reality tv/game show where they'll have to compete in various challenges to win points or face various consequences when they fail. During their downtime, they'll have access to explore the mansion and grounds that they're being kept at (something like Big Brother, perhaps) The players will have to stay alive long enough to figure out how to escape, either through winning the final challenges or through other means, and they'll have to decide how much they're willing to work with the other contestants and if they want to try to save them as well.

    The contests are a way of trying out various skills and rules in the game in many different scenarios. The players might be teleported to a location where they have to find cars (their choice on how they manage to obtain one; persuasion, theft, etc.) to have a street race in the middle of the city.

    I want to try out a variety of situations so that Mental, Social, and Physical skills all get a good shot at being used. I was planning on asking for ideas from my players, but I was wondering if you guys would have any suggestions as well, especially if you have any that would be particularly good for changelings.

      Originally posted by Irinde View Post
      *NPCs
      If you are not that good at making voices don't worry about it. Just announce who is talking and let the players use their imagination. One tip when speaking as characters or entities where your actual voice might cause unintended humour or is simply not adequate to show the enormity of the speaker, simply speak in 3rd person and use appropriate descriptions.

      If you do want to practice you should probably hop onto YT and listen to some people and try to mimic them, but I don't really think it is necessary.

      Originally posted by Irinde View Post
      *Rules
      If this is your first time I would strongly recommend keeping it easy. Focus on the Core. Even using the stopgap GMC rules for CtL might not be a good idea. Focus on the Core and expand the books you draw from as you get more comfortable. If you are worried about limiting player choices maybe allow players *minor* retcons (if necessary) as you include more books.

      Originally posted by Irinde View Post
      *Setting
      Music is great. I am not so sure about writing up the detailed descriptions. Since you are doing CtL (a game of beautiful madness afterall), it might be better to have a general idea of what a location should be like and then adapt it on the fly instead of nailing it down precisely.

      Originally posted by Irinde View Post
      *Shyness
      I think that comes with experience. Just remember: You are the GM, which automatically makes you the awesomest person in the room. Also, players are usually much more forgiving about fuckups than most GMs think or expect.


      As for other tips: Never be afraid of calling for a break when they pulled out the rug from under you. Taking a few minutes to catch your wits when you have to improvise is way better than trying to pull something out of your... well... on the spot.

      Politeness is the lubricant of social intercourse.

          1) I would not worry too much about voices/accents. They CAN help, especially if there are a lot of NPCs in a conversation, but are not required. I sometimes use them, sometimes not. I may say someone specific is speaking ("Chandra says..."), Now, I print pictures for most major NPCs (eventually all of them, but sometimes they are introduced before I have them) and also frequently point to a specific picture to indicate who is speaking. (I highly recommend using pictures regardless, they help immersion and if you have a cast of like 50-70 NPCs having a face helps, and also helps encourage romantic side plots).

          2) If you encourage cooperative play, it can take a lot of pressure off. For one thing, don't pretend you know all the rules. If you know one, great, if not, ask if someone else does. If running a conversation where you don't need it immediately, you can have a player look it up while you talk. I often use times players are talking to each other IC to check rules as well. But I find a lot of stress GMs have is when they feel that they must be infallible and don't instead approach it as a group project. But that does not work with every group. Also FWIW I find using Changeling with GMC rules pretty easy.

          I would also say try and find the rules, but if you can't, be up front that the group is going to do this this way this time, but it may change if you find an exact rule later. Or, of course, if you prefer how you did it, you may stay with that.

          3) Have a decent idea of places and items ahead of time. If you are set in a real city, pictures of locations are often easy to come by, though I don't usually do that. I often try to keep a master list of NPCs, updated often, and a sublist early on of where many can be found. For example, X Y and Z NPCs go clubbing a lot, so if the PCs go clubbing they are more likely to run into them.

          4) I am shy in general, but less so with my friends. Going along with what I said in #2, it may help to not try to be the big authority figure but more the coordinator, running the story, being final rules arbiter, and getting a lot of input from players. It can help if you all feel you are on the same side and are trying to make an awesome game together.

          Well, other tips...I have my Actual Plays (linked in sig) partially for people to read and get ideas for NPCs or plots from. Feel free to mine them and steal shamelessly from them.

          As much as possible, communicate as openly as possible with your players. Get a good idea of what they want for their characters and the game. Communicate back your expectations. Figure out how much, for example, romance or sex they want to occur. How much combat? How much politics? Get a consensus of comfort levels with any of those things. Find out if there is anything that makes them uncomfortable. I have a player who I will not have anything involving child molestation as either a plot or background element if she is playing. Give them the opportunity to email you with anything if they don't want to say it in front of the group. make the game a project, and everyone participants. You are lead designer, but need your team.

          Decide if you want to run something plot intensive (you generally control a plot they follow) or sandboxy (the PCs initiate many plots and decide when/how to follow them). A combination may even be what you want. Discuss this with players. If going heavy sandbox, they should make characters who want to go out and do stuff without needing a hook in their back. Many players make different PCs for different sorts of games.

          Ask questions. Feel free to ask them questions, ask us questions. Solicit feedback.

          Does this help? Can I explain anything further or give examples?
