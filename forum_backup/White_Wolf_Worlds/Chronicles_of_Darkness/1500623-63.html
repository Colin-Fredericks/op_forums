        See the "Obvious magic" sidebar on page 298 of the corebook. The spell's effect has to be clearly, openly supernatural in effect and the Sleeper needs to be both conscious and cognizant of the magic's impossibility, which affects whether the effect is subject to Paradox (at the time of casting) or Dissonance (if encountered later). If there's not a Sleeper present and aware that something that shouldn't be mundanely possible happening, the Curse doesn't make things any worse than it would be if the mage was casting in an isolated mundane area.

        Quiescence means the Sleepers either forget or misremember what they see of Supernal or Abyssal phenomena.

                                    I take Withstand as just being a case of some spells have binary effects that still require a certain Potency to effect change the way some scaling effects do.

                                    Like, if you want to use a Ruling of Forces to make a size 1 flame into a size 4 flame, that requires Potency 3 in all circumstances. The only difference is that you have options for lower Potency that you don't for certain other effects on other targets.

                                    So it's not resistance to change so much as the level of applied force necessary to cause change. Take a steel bar. The bar isn't resisting you if it doesn't bend beneath your hands, it's just that the composition dictates certain minimum requirements.
