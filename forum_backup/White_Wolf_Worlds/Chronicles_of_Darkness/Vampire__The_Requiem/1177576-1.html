                        Originally posted by Malkav's Weird Cousin View Post
                        I think it's probably bias because the owls as a unified antagonist remind me too much of oWoD and its convoluted... well, everything.
                        The strix really aren't a unified force; they don't necessarily stay close to each other because they don't really need to like the Kindred do. They're immortal, nigh unstoppable creatures that can't be explained by most of the supernatural laws that govern the night.

                        I've always used strix as more of a phenomenon than something for the group to fight against (because honestly, there is absolutely no way that Kindred are going to be killing more than one strix, at best).

                        Individual strix might mess around with kindred for various reasons, but there are also strix that aren't actively antagonizing the kindred every waking moment. So I've always found it best to play individual strix as something that when you think you have them figured out, they will do the complete opposite.

                        However, I've found lots of fun in having strix gather in mass and not have them actively do anything. Strix are often believed to be omens of misfortune, and a few of their powers help reinforce that. By actively gathering in mass, and by not doing anything, the Kindred of that domain will become so paranoid that they'll cause a disaster themselves or start killing each other off because it'd be a good time to get rid of rivals or because of legitimate fear of strix possession.

                                                    Originally posted by Malkav's Weird Cousin View Post
                                                    Pijavica - Obfuscate, Protean, Resilience. The folklore "leeches" (that's literally what "pijavica" means, go figure) were shapeshifters and it was said that stakes couldn't penetrate their skin, so Protean and Resilience work great. I replaced Animalism from the official material with Obfuscate because it's specifically stated that they usually walked around invisible to mortal eyes.
                                                    The problem with this is that Obfuscate can be developed by any Kindred without special investment and "invisible to mortal eyes" is the entry-level power in a Discipline that means a lot more than personal invisibility.

                                                    Sharing a name doesn't necessarily translate to direct emulation of that entity, and as extremely bloody and alien vampires it follows that the most visceral Discipline of the core ten would round out their focal capabilities — a Gangrel and a Pijavica look very different from each other even with the same Discipline spread, and the latter's difficulties are part of why they're practically extinct.

                                                    For contrast, I genuinely don't know why Animalism would be thematically appropriate for the Julii — it's bloody business. The Ventrue leverage it most naturally in its capacity for broader control of their surroundings, and all the accumulated narrative of the Lords makes the dubiousness of their title fit with the unclean qualities of consorting with animals even among the subset that leans into the beastmaster angle.

                                                    The Julii are associated with a cosmopolitan city that is in many ways the big reason cities as we understand them took off as a concept — differentiating them in a way that tilts them closer to a middle ground between the two big Societal Clans follows a bit more naturally from the fiction.

                                                        Yeah, the Pijavica bane in Thousand Years of Night is just bad. I'm not sufficiently familiar with the actual folklore in question to be a good authority, but when I was at homebrewing at one point, I gave them:

                                                        The Uncanny Curse: The Pijavica exude an alien air. Crowds unconsciously part before them. Bystanders see the light cast against their faces and feel the intrusion of another world into their own. When a Pijavica presents himself as typical, natural or to be expected in a place, he does not reroll tens. When the Pijavica rolls an exceptional success before witnesses, the Storyteller may choose to treat a witness as Spooked: something about the success strikes the witness as not merely exceptional, but (whether literally or not) in some sense magical.
                                                        Optional additional mechanics: Pijavica can feed from direct skin contact through any part of their body. They use the Feed Move as normal, but "bite" by flexing their flesh into an abrasive, stinging surface of tiny needles. Even when feeding with a normal bite, however, Pijavica cannot inflict the Swooning Condition through the Kiss.                                                         The Uncanny Curse: The Pijavica exude an alien air. Bystanders see the light cast against their faces and feel the intrusion of another world into their own. Once per chapter, when the Pijavica makes use of a Discipline before witnesses, the Storyteller may ask the player to set one of their dice aside when rolling. Count this die towards success as normal, but if its rolled face value equals or exceeds the vampire's Humanity, occult omens occur: the vampire's shadow writhes, light casts an eerie and unnatural glow on him, or an animal spooks violently. Whatever happens, it's enough to disquiet and raise suspicion, and a mortal witness may become Spooked and moved to investigate.
