    Marcus is right. Order of operations is: Activate Face in the Crowd as an instant action, spending a Vitae as you do to enhance it with the effect of Cloak of Night. As noted, not having an action means it's not really a discrete effect so much as an add-on to other powers. The "also" in "...can also spend a point of Vitae..." is in relation to the previous paragraph, which outlines using Cloak of Night to enhance Touch of Shadow so it can affect other people, as opposed to just animals and objects. It also says a point of Vitae, which doesn't really make sense if it really means "spend 2 Vitae." I guess the phrasing is a little imprecise, but it's definitely not meant to indicate turning on Cloak of Night requires spending 2 Vitae when using Face in the Crowd. It would say 2 Vitae in the cost if that were so. You do have to spend 2 Vitae to use Touch of Shadow on another person/vampire, though.

                                Originally posted by Vampire, p. 142
                                Cloak the Gathering enhances the effect of Touch of Shadow [1 Vitae] and Cloak of Night [1 Vitae]. By paying two extra points of Vitae [Cloak the Gathering costs 2 in and of itself], the vampire can expand the effect of Touch of Shadow to encompass a number of people equal to his Blood Potency, though subject to the normal Size modifiers of Touch of Shadow. He can also opt to activate Face in the Crowd [0 Vitae] at the same time. If he also pays the extra cost for Cloak of Night to vanish completely rather than fading into the background, the effect applies to everyone masked by Cloak the Gathering — so for four points of Vitae total he can vanish from sight along with a number of people equal to his Blood Potency.

I've freelanced on Vampire and know this to be true.                                         Originally posted by totalgit View Post

                                        (at no point does touch of shadow extend FitC over others, rather CtG allows you to pay extra via CoN (without paying for CoN) to extend FitC to yourself and others.
                                        "With just a touch, the vampire can extend the muted attentions of Face in the Crowd to an object or animal, rather than himself."

                                        That's quite literally what Touch of Shadow does. That's from the text of Touch of Shadow. It's what Cloak the Gathering even says it does: "...the vampire can expand the effect of Touch of Shadow to encompass a number of people equal to his Blood Potency." Touch of Shadow is the same effect as Face in the Crowd, just on other people.

                                        However, I was a tad imprecise there, because the Devotion text does just say you're using Face in the Crowd for the Cloak of Night effect. So, the most accurate way to express it is:

                                        Cloak the Gathering to expand the effect of Touch of Shadow so you can use it on multiple characters at once. (2 Vitae)

                                        Touch of Shadow to use the effect of Face in the Crowd on other people. (1 Vitae)

                                        Face in the Crowd to make yourself forgettable as well. (0 Vitae)

                                        Cloak of Night to enhance Face in the Crowd and Touch of Shadow to make yourself and everyone else invisible. (1 Vitae)

                                        I'm struggling to understand what isn't clear here. I understand your logic, but you're coming at it from a false premise: i.e., that there's normally a 2 Vitae cost to use Cloak of Shadow on yourself, but then for some reason it only costs 1 Vitae when you use it in conjunction with Cloak the Gathering.

                                        Like, I'm not trying to be snippy with you, but the word "extra" really isn't as significant as you think it is, and I don't want to get into a drawn out semantic argument over it. I promise you, it's 1 Vitae to use Cloak of Night. If you don't see it that way, cool, but let's just drop this then, because it's not the point of the thread.

                                        Edit: Okay, so I did a bit of digging, because I remembered there were some errata changes to the way Cloak of Night works. In the very first release of Blood & Smoke (not Vampire: The Requiem Second Edition), Cloak of Night automatically enhanced the effect of Touch of Shadow so you could use it on other people. You didn't have to spend a Vitae. If you wanted to make a victim disappear completely as opposed to just making them harder to notice, you had to spend an extra blood on top of the cost of Touch of Shadow. However, after some errata, this was changed so you always have to spend a Vitae when combining Touch of Shadow with Cloak of Night, though the text implies you can be selective about how deep the invisibility is.

                                        As for Face in the Crowd, here's the relevant text from the alpha of B&S:

                                        "The vampire can also spend an extra point of Vitae when activating Face in the Crowd or Touch of Shadow to vanish completely."

                                        Presumably, that extra was the cost listed in the power, which was indeed 1 Vitae. Now here's the same text from VTR2E:

                                        "The vampire can also spend a point of Vitae when activating Face in the Crowd to vanish completely."

                                        Specifically, the word "extra" is removed so that it doesn't seem like you have to spend two blood to disappear. In short, the reason it says "extra" in Cloak the Gathering is because it's referring to the way the system worked in the first place; that is, it was free to use Cloak of Night in some circumstances, but you had to pay a Vitae to totally disappear other people or yourself. You never had to spend two Vitae to disappear yourself, however. The change doesn't really affect Cloak the Gathering, it just makes the wording a little odd.

                                        Alright, if that's not enough, I don't know what is.
