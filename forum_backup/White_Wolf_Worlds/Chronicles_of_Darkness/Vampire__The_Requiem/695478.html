              The book originally titled Blood & Smoke: The Strix Chronicle was rebranded with a new cover as the Vampire: the Requiem Second Edition corebook. (The Strix Chronicle Anthology is a separate book, that's the short story collection.) It's still got all the adventure hooks, domain layouts and antagonist writeups the original had, but they're not quite a full premade adventure.

              Reap the Whirlwind, the second edition quickstart, does have a premade adventure for 2e VtR. 1e VtR has several short adventure writeups in the SAS format, and a few other 1e supplements have prewritten adventure material, including Requiem for Rome/Fall of the Camarilla and The Danse Macabre. It shouldn't be too difficult to use adventure material from one edition in the other edition; the core concepts are mostly close enough.

              Damnation City is a one-stop shop for various optional systems, example writeups and walkthroughs on building a city domain to run your game in, and developing stories and hooks in that domain.

                  Vampire: The Requiem Second Edition was originally released as Blood And Smoke: The Strix Chronicle - it includes example settings and antagonists, including a chapter on the Strix featuring examples ready to use, but it isn't an adventure book. The Strix Chronicle Anthology is a collection of fiction.

                  There are a number of published adventures available, mostly designed for the first edition rules, although the free quickstart Reap The Whirlwind updates the adventure "Into The Void" for Second Edition. There's also a multi-part adventure in the free first edition demo.

                  If I were to recommend one supplement, it would be Damnation City, the guide to creating urban chronicles, with enough examples that you can use it as a city sourcebook and collection of adventure hooks as well. It's great for urban-centred games beyond Vampire too. See the free map segments supplement as well.

                  The Clanbooks are also excellent, mostly in-setting information and inspirational fiction, while The Danse Macabre was designed as a rules and setting expansion for the first edition that saw early examples of various Second Edition rules, but also contains new covenants, advice on playing at different tiers of influence,a nd alternate playstyles.

                                      It's already been backed up enough, but I'll totally reinforce the utility of Damnation City. It's not just a great VtR supplement but also an all-around fantastic toolkit for any modern day game. I'm planning a game taking place in my hometown, and while I know the city like the back of my hand, DC provides plenty of on point advice to translating that raw information into narrative gold. Bits and pieces of it are a bit crunchy for my tastes, but luckily you can take everything a la carte. The writing is also well beyond what I'd expect from a typical RPG supplement. A lot of it, not even just the little interstitial fiction segments, borders on legit literary prose.

                                      I haven't dug too deep into the game's catalog (I've been wary for concern out of how bits and pieces convert to 2E and have plenty of material with the core and DC), but it seems pretty well to stand on its own. I've browsed through a couple of the covenant books and they seem pretty solid. I'd suggest picking one or two up if particular covs are going to have a particularly salient impact on your game. All-in-all, the game seems pretty modular and the writing seems solid altogether, so I suspect it's more about picking the books that fit your particular setting and mood rather than there being a de facto list of essentials.

                                                      For 2e? Actually, my recommendations are based entirely on what you, as an ST, would need. I don't actually think any of them are really useful for players.

                                                      The clan books are primarily about stories behind the clan. While interesting reads, I don't think my players actually found the books impacted their actual play in any way, and they're more interested in moving forwards with their own views. Especially the Daeva - with their clan bane so different, they actually come off as a wildly different clan now. The Mehket clan book was all about the secret cults, and a character suffering from the Hollow condition, but the cults are primarily an Ordo Dracul thing now, and Hollow isn't a thing so much as the accumulation of occult banes. Ventrue madness is no longer a thing either. Actually, now that I think about it, I feel like the clan books might be slightly outdated already.

                                                      Requiem for Rome and the clan books helped redefine the game from the 1e core book, but the 2e core book went beyond what was covered in what came before. Same thing with the Blasphemies and Sacraments book - its the halfway point between 1e and 2e for Theban and Cruac. Danse Macabre and Ancient Mysteries/Bloodlines is another in that same vein - a book with lots of lore for pushing 1e towards its new shiny 2e world.

                                                      All the above are great for mining ideas for the lore behind the world. Which can be really good... if that's what your game wants, and you take it all with a grain of salt as you convert all of it to 2e. If you're not interested in playing those kinds of stories, more power to your group. If you're not, then the books are less than useful, and you'd be better served saving your money for the supplements already in the works coming out.
