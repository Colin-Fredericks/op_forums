        Some creatures are rare even for the standards of the supernatural, and so special that you can't find a place for them as given in the world. Admonitoria, or any other similar creatures conceived here, shouldn't have a single place were they are common, or their lore can be found. Their lore is found in the mouths of madmen, every case so unique that you never heard about. To find the simplest answers on their being, one should travel to the ends of the world and beyond. Or just wonder with the practical learning of night after night.

        Geist-possessed werewolves, or any other Geist-possessed Major Splat, isn't something that should be lightly thought of. Those creatures, if rare, still exist in relative good numbers and, well, dye a lot. If it was without cost, they would be naturally preferred over mortals by Geists. What the Geists could fear in those creatures, able to thwart their existences somehow without simply being prohibiting on the concept?

        Maybe the spirit half of a werewolf can affect the Geist in a way so extreme that most wouldn't chose that out of fear of the completely unknown?

                                            Going in the constructive method, I think we have something here we can use. The Claim Manifestation isn't in the equation, so lets get rid of it at once in this discussion.

                                            Now, I agree that World Police isn't an aspect of Werewolf anymore. Or, to be precise, it was clarified as a consequence of Territory and Hunt. But this idea already gives us a good measure of Hunt.

                                            The Geists are strange ephemeral entities, and never were as well defined as the others. It can be observed, though, that they seem to be a transition from Ghost to Spirit. And them we have the themes on werewolf, or, to be more precise, on the Shadow.

                                            Spirits are cannibals by nature, and when they consume other spirits they incorporate the essence, power and nature of the victim. This isn't a problem for them, just a part of their life. Geists, on the other hand, came from a world where violence and decay are common, but there are rules, and where cannibalism too frequently a taboo. They still bond with mortals in some way, and that reinforces this view on cannibalism.

                                            A werewolf, being part spirit, can draw power from cannibalism, but as part human can't lightly choose to do so with their own kind, including their human side. Consuming a Geist, another kind of human-spirit hybrid, to stave off death, seems like a powerful enough theme for me, burning the Uratha with stranger essence that is so close at the same time, and making her something new in the process, nor Werewolf, nor Bound, nor Spirit, nor Ghost, maybe not even too much matter.

                                                        This brings to my mind the Banshee and the Dulahan, two favorites of mine.
