                            Okay, let's go deeper down the rabbit hole:

                            Daughters of Cacophony

                            Nicknames: Sirens, Maenads
                            Disciplines: Majesty, Melpominee, Resilience
                            Clan Bane (The Discordant Curse): Daughters of Cacophony constantly, maddeningly, hear music running through their heads. Driven to distraction, a Siren's dice pools are limited by her Humanity when making Perception rolls, or when trying to recall or memorize information (World of Darkness Rulebook, p. 44). Treat any failures as dramatic failures.
                            Favored Attributes: Manipulation or Presence

                            Notes:

                            Melpominee is easy enough to just translate into NWoD rules. Fiddle with some numbers, determine dice pools, throw in some Conditions, etc. You can probably do this on your own. The thing is, it's...a strange discipline. Truth be told (YMMV), it's not even very good. It's a hodgepodge of abilities better represented with Majesty, Nightmare, or Dominate (or devotions of those powers), and the last tier is just "Two for the price of one!" All this is fine in Masquerade, where a bit of whimsy and oddity are par for the course. I wouldn't do all this translation stuff if I didn't love me some wacky ole VTM. But basically, Melpominee as written isn't a power that I could see predators using on the hunt. Yeah, the same can be said of the several proprietary disciplines from the Big 13 -- looking at you Ravnos -- but that's neither here nor there.

                            So, my favored solution is thus: use the mechanics from Mimetismo from Bloodlines: The Chosen. It's a Nosferatu power, yes, but it fits really well with the Daughters. It has much more utility, too. I don't have my copy of Chosen on hand, but I might write up a second edition conversion when I get a chance, or at least expand on what the power ought to do in this context.

                            If you're more interested in a flavour translation rather than a literal one, you could also just give them Nightmare. It can produce aural psychological illusions, albeit ones based around fear.

                            Kiasyd

                            Nicknames: Weirdlings, Moths
                            Disciplines: Mytherceria, plus two disciplines from the sire's clan
                            Clan Bane (The Changeling Curse): Kiasyd are burdened with the mercurial blood of the fae. When a Kiasyd reaches Humanity 6, she gains a single Bane not tied to Humanity, a ban or anathema reflecting the compulsive behaviors of the fair folk (see p. 108 of Vampire: The Requiem for examples). This bane counts against the three banes a vampire is allowed, so Kiasyd are less able to mitigate detachment: coming from two supernatural worlds, their connection to humanity is flimsy. In addition, weapons forged from iron always do lethal damage to Weirdlings, even blunt weapons.
                            Favored Attributes: Intelligence or Wits

                            Notes:

                            I went with the V20 Dark Ages version of the Kiasyd rather than "Lasombra Faeries", because David Hill and co. came up with a really cool, plot hook oriented take on fae vampires, and I prefer it. In NWoD terms, a Kiasyd could be what happens when one Embraces a Fetch, or perhaps an enscorcelled mortal. Or a changeling...

                            If you prefer the Lasombra bloodline version, then use their discipline spread as presented in V20 (though if you really want to go nuts, use their spread from Second Edition).

                            As you can see, this Bane is similar to the Mekhet curse. I was going to write something up more closely reflecting their iron allergy, but I'm ultimately not a big fan of damage banes (Setites notwithstanding, but at least theirs enforces behaviour, to a degree). I also didn't want to go down the road of "What is Cold Iron?" I did end up leaving the iron weakness in on a smaller scale, because it fits with the flavor and the spirit of the Kiasyd, and being true to the source material has been important to my design aesthetic. But to me, modeling off the Mekhet curse fits because so much fae folklore revolves around them having odd bans and anathemas and the like (Changeling being a great example). That said, the minor banes presented in Blood and Smoke are more oriented to vampire legends, so you might want to come up with some custom banes that are more "fae".

                            As for Mytherceria...

                            Like Melpominee, Mytherceria is a kitchen sink discipline. If I were building the Kiasyd from the ground up in NWoD -- as a bloodline, say -- I'd probably want to do Mytherceria as Blood Sorcery. Five tiers just doesn't encompass the number of things you could do with something that's supposed to reflect fae powers.

                            Anyway, disclaimers aside, options:

                            As with Melpominee, it wouldn't be difficult to directly translate the mechanics of the Classic version. For version, I (again) prefer V20DA. You can have a look at the raw manuscript via the Kickstarter page: https://www.kickstarter.com/projects...-v20-dark-ages

                            If you want to use the mechanics of a published discipline, which riffs on an appropriate theme, Sunnikuse for the Bohagande in Bloodlines: The Hidden fits with the idea of fae as fate weavers and luck manipulators. It's not that similar to Mytherceria, but I don't think there really are any powers like that. I know Sunnikuse has a bad reputation for being "broken", but I don't have any use for that term, and I think if you're going to have weird fae-pires in your game, you shouldn't be afraid to let your hair down. Do as thou wilt.

                            Another appropriate theme you could play with is dreams. Contract of Dreams from first edition Changeling: The Lost is a good model, but it doesn't have the oomph that Kindred disciplines have, so a power boost might be in order.

                            Taking Contracts as a model further, it might be interesting to just embrace Mytherceria's kitchen sink quality. Each tier of the Discipline could be a player-chosen clause from a Changeling Contract. That way, each Kiasyd is it's own unique creature, not quite Fae, but not quite a Vampire.

                            That's it for now. I'll have more later, when I get home from work. As always, thoughts and suggestions are welcome. : )

                                    Baali

                                    Nicknames: Demons, Devils
                                    Disciplines: Daimonion, Majesty, Obfuscate
                                    Clan Bane (The Infernal Curse): The Baali fear true faith. When faced with a religious symbol (a cross, a Star of David, the Takbir, etc.) wielded by a true believer, all of the Baali's dice pools are limited by her Humanity. A symbol held by a vampire has no effect. In addition, upon reaching Humanity 6, all Demons suffer the Plague of Purity bane (Vampire: The Requiem, p. 108), which counts against the three banes a vampire is allowed.
                                    Favored Attributes: Composure or Manipulation


                                    Notes:

                                    Not sure how I feel about the basic bane with this one. It has some teeth, but I'm not sure about the mechanics yet. I may grow to like it better as I think about it, but we'll see. I do like the extra Purity bane, though. My other idea for the main weakness is that when faced with a religious icon, even just a passive one (e.g., a church cross), they have to roll Humanity. If they fail, they gain Spooked or some custom Condition. Let me know what you think.

                                    As for Daimoinon/Daimonion (take your pick), I think it's actually pretty good as written, so I'll write up a quick translation when I get the chance. You could also give them Nightmare.

                                    Samedi

                                    Nicknames: Stiffs, Zombies
                                    Disciplines: Obfuscate, Resilience, Thanatosis
                                    Clan Bane (The Putrid Curse): Samedi rot as they fall to the Beast. All Physical dice pools are limited by the Stiff's Humanity. This does not apply to the passive bonus gained from Vigor or Resilience. In addition, Samedi are considered one Humanity dot lower than usual for the purposes of socializing with humans, as the smell of the grave follows them wherever they go.
                                    Favored Attributes: Resolve or Composure

                                    Notes:

                                    Thanatosis                                     as written is neat conceptually, but a bit boring in terms of output. Mostly it's just a fancy way of wounding people and making them ugly. The turning to ashes stuff is cool, though. I'll see about translating it. I can't think of any published Requiem material that would make a good stand in, if that's your preference. You could just be done with it and say they have Necromancy, but in that case I'd replace Thanatosis with Auspex, and give them access to Necromancy for out of clan costs.

                                                            For those interested, I have a 2nd Edition compatible Merit to represent Generation. It doesn't replace the Blood Potency system, so those who want that kind of system, should enjoy it. This is based off some of Yossarian's ideas, my first iteration of Generation, and the various Generation-as-merit that have floated around since the Translation guide and before.

                                                            Generation (• to •••••; Special, Character Creation Only)
                                                            Generation is Kindred's mystical bond with the first vampire -- its curse is strong, but so are its gifts. Supposedly, there are Thirteen Generations of Kindred from their progenitor. The Third Generation, the Antediluvians, were the progenitors of the Clans.

                                                            Effect: Unlike other Merits, Generation can only be acquired or increased through diablerie, but the diablerist gains nothing if her victim has fewer dots than her own Generation. After diablerie, the Merit automatically increases by one dot if the victim was of lower Generation than the diablerist. Childer usually gain one less dot in this Merit from their sires.

                                                            Those that have this merit accrue Blood Potency faster than normal. If their rating in the Blood Potency trait is less than their rating in this merit they can purchase the next dot in Blood Potency by spending new 3 experience points (instead of the new 5 experience points as would be normal) and their Blood Potency will automatically increase by one dot every twenty five years until it is equal to their rating in this merit.

                                                            In addition to this a vampire gains addition benefits from the list below. He acquires all the abilities in sequence. Those with Generation •••• gain the special abilities of the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd dot. Although this Merit decreases after Embrace, automatically lowering by one.

                                                            Drawback: For the first dot, you suffer under your Clan Bane at humanity seven. Additionally, your soul draws Diablerists to you. Auspex 2 can identify a Kindred's Generation.

                                                            0 (13th Generation) Your Blood Potency starts out at one at the Embrace, and can only ever increase to five, and you have a greater chance to sire Revenants and Catiff's.
                                                            • (12th Generation) You possess an Iron Will, once per story, the vampire may reflexively add double her dots in this Merit to any Resistance Attribute, or add it as a penalty to an opponent's own Resistance Attribute.
                                                            •• (11th Generation) You can spend one additional point of vitae each turn regardless of your normal Blood Potency determined allotment. Additionally, you apply a -1 to your Blood Potency for the purposes of determining whether you are restricted to human or vampire blood, +1 to your effective Blood Potency for purposes of Viniculum.
                                                            ••• (10th Generation) You start the game with Blood Potency two and regardless of how long you spend in torpor your Blood Potency will never drop lower than two. If you acquire this rating in Generation through Diablerie and your Blood Potency was one before you performed that terrible act you first increase your Blood Potency to two and then, gain Experience from their Blood Potency. Additionally, you have a Calloused Soul, allowing you take take one extra Bane.
                                                            •••• (9th Generation) You add a further +1 to your Blood Potency for the purposes of determining the establishment of Vinculums . In addition, the smell of your vitae is especially enticing to addicts and its taste is even more compelling than a normal vampire’s. Individuals that taste your vitae suffer a -1 penalty to their rolls to control vitae addiction and anyone who is currently addicted to vitae (suffering any penalties to those rolls due to previous tastes) who can smell your blood suffers a -1 penalty to their roll made to resist attempting to imbibe it.
                                                            ••••• (8th Generation) You start the game with Blood Potency three and regardless of how long you spend in torpor your Blood Potency will never drop lower than three, and your maximum Blood Potency increases to six. If you acquire this rating in Generation through diablerie and your Blood Potency was one or two before you performed that terrible act you first increase your Blood Potency to three and then, if your victim had a Blood Potency higher than three, increase it by a further dot. In addition, you count your Blood Potency as 2 lower for purposes of feeding restrictions. Finally, you possess an Apex Beast, Vampires with Blood this potent may add dots in this Merit when Lashing out with Predatory Aura. In addition, vampires of higher Generations must spend a Willpower to fight back, regardless of Blood Potency.
                                                            ••••• • (7th Generation) You start the game with Blood Potency three, your maximum Blood Potency is seven, and regardless of how long you spend in torpor your Blood Potency will never drop lower than three. If you acquire this rating in Generation through diablerie and your Blood Potency was one or two before you performed that terrible act you first increase your Blood Potency to three and then, if your victim had a Blood Potency higher than three, increase it by a further dot.
                                                            ••••• •• (6th Generation) You can spend two additional vitae (a total of three due to the 10th Generation benefit) each turn in addition to the allotment provided by your current Blood Potency, and your maximum Blood Potency is Eight. In addition you apply a -1 to your Blood Potency for the purposes of determining whether you are restricted to human or vampire blood (this stacks with the 11th Generation ability for a -2 total modifier for this purpose).
                                                            ••••• ••• (5th Generation) Your minimum Blood Potency is now four, and your maximum Blood Potency is nine. Those vampires Embraced into this Generation awoke from their deaths with a Blood Potency four and no matter how long they spend in torpor their rating in that trait never drops below four. If you acquire this rating in Generation through diablerie and your Blood Potency was three or less before you performed that terrible act you first increase your blood potency to four and then, if your victim had a Blood Potency higher than four, increase it by a further dot.
                                                            ••••• •••• (4th Generation) You add a further +1 to your Blood Potency for the purposes of determining the establishment of Vinculums, and your maximum Blood Potency is ten. In addition, the addictive qualities of your blood are heightened even further. Now, instead of the modifiers applied by 8th Generation blood, individuals that taste your vitae suffer a -2 penalty to their rolls to control vitae addiction and anyone who is currently addicted to vitae (suffering any penalties to those rolls due to previous tastes) who can smell your blood suffers a -2 penalty to their roll made to resist attempting to imbibe it. In addition, you add your current Blood Potency to the maximum amount of vitae you can have in your system at once. Lastly, Kindred with this potent blood have a Methuselah's Mind and are immune to the effects of a single mental or social discipline of her Clan (e.g., Animalism, Auspex, Dominate, Majesty, Obfuscate, etc.). These powers, or the effects of these powers, fail outright when used against her . If her clan has no appropriate Discipline, the player may choose one, though generally she should have dots in it. Drawback: This immunity does not apply if the discipline originated from a vampire with equal Generation. Also, the character's Blood Potency is considered two dots higher for the purposes of determining time spent in Torpor.
