                        At the end of the day, when it comes to themes, set up expectations first. Storytellers should lay out what themes and topics could or will come up in the chronicle, and things they won't allow. Players should bring up anything they are uncomfortable with, so that the Storyteller can avoid those subjects or present them in ways that aren't going to cross a line. Honestly, communication is key.

                        As far as themes go, I think the important thing is that things like Integrity and Humanity probably exist on something like a bell curve: a handful of saints and monsters, and most people sitting right in the middle, most people being kind of okay or kind of jerks.

                                        Meh, I always preferred 1e Requiem's "Masquerade Lite" approach to the metaplot-heavy madness of Masquerade or the approach 2e Requiem took. It's a well known fact around here that I am not a fan of 2e Requiem so I won't go on about that too much, but I love 1e Requiem (with some hacks, of course), and I never understood why people think that being "Masquerade Lite" is a bad thing.

                                        ​To me, 1e Requiem kept the best parts of Masquerade and dropped a lot of the bad parts such as the metaplot, the cultural stereotyping, and a lot of other baggage. I think 2e tried to reinvent the wheel too hard and had issues of its own, but other people like 2e and that is fine with me. So, yeah, I kind of like the "Masquerade Lite" approach that Requiem initially took, never cared for the themes of personal horror, but as an ST, that can be amended easily via Rule Zero. Otherwise, Requiem is an awesome game. Masquerade is decent too, but Requiem is excellent for an entirely different yet similar reason.

                                        ​I never liked the hardcore Masquerade fans who won't take off their rose-tinted nostalgia goggles long enough to give Requiem a proper chance. Maybe it's because they dominate the WoD fandom and LARP scene in Roanoke, Virginia, but either way, nostalgia isn't what it used to be.
