        So, the Anvari. Here's how the sausage gets made.

        It's not an exaggeration to say that we've been circling the drain on this bloodline for quite some time. I briefly considered them for Bloodlines: The Resurrected, but I didn't really have a hook for them, so they just ended up in the quick conversion appendix. I also considered them for Bloodlines: The Devoted, but there were a few too many Daeva bloodlines in that book already. Then, they were absolutely, positively going to be in Bloodlines: The Ageless... which, obviously, they are, but that was back when BTA was going to be, like, an omnibus of all the remaining bloodlines, including those from the 1E clan books and Ancient Bloodlines. My original outline for this book was kind of a beast, and it quickly became evident that it was Too Much. I also had some authors drop off for scheduling reasons, so that original, chunky version evolved into the 2E clan books; keen observers may note that Bloodlines: The Devoted had a "coming next" teaser for Ageless in the early version, which I sheepishly removed eventually. If I recall correctly, someone wrote a few bits and bobs for the Anvari in that proto-version of the book, but I didn't ever see the drafts. I believe it was something about them having toxic blood, which, in hindsight, I don't think would've really fit.

        Anyway, when the first version of Ageless collapsed into the clan books, the Anvari were absolutely, positively, definitely going to be in Sin Again: Daeva. And then, I finally took a good long look at their original write-up and kinda got turned off, lol. I didn't find them as icky as the Children of Judas, but I found the idea of opium-addict vampires deeply inessential. I also felt we had enough old bloodlines in the book, and I wanted at least a couple of new ones. So, they got the chop.

        However, when we decided to take another crack at Ageless, I gave them one last chance. I still didn't particularly like them, but I also really wanted to have full translations of all the 1E bloodlines with unique Disciplines, and they were the only ones we hadn't in some form or another. But, I was really stuck on their concept, which was so monofocused on opium, and I didn't know how to make opium powers interesting or pointful. That got me talking to one of the other authors, because I was getting close to just writing them off completely. Fortunately, that conversation led to a much more interesting take on them, one that fits with the original concept but expands it beyond the very narrow niche it was stuck in.

        In Second Edition, the Anvari are addicted to addiction, not just opioid-tainted blood. Sex addicts, gamblers, alcoholics, Vitae fiends — all these and more can fulfill an Anvari's appetites, though, in the case of that last one, they can't get high on their own supply. So to speak. The Pushers use their special Kiss effect to temporarily relieve addiction, forcing human junkies to rely on them to get their fixes. They're like walking, talking, omni-methadone, and by purveying vices, they ensure a steady food source. They also, rather infamously, use this ability to "help" ghouls and blood addicts escape Kindred (read: their rivals), weaning them off blood addiction. Of course, they can also help you out if you're jonesing for diablerie, so the rest of the dead haven't quite got around to dealing with them. Yet.

        All that said, this is probably one of the nastier bloodlines we've done. They spread human misery and then lap it up for good measure. I think there are some really interesting stories you can tell with them, but they're not something everyone's going to want to bring to the table. Of course, I could also see someone playing them very altruistically (speaking of the Salubri), serving as harm-reduction specialists.

        So, here are the Anvari, with a brand new drug. Tomorrow, let's end the week with a Nosferatu bloodline: the Adroanzi.

        Anvari: The Ones You Crave

        “I can take the pain away. Just takes a little pinch...”

        Addiction is everywhere. From opioids blighting the streets to the smartphone in your pocket, the monkeys clinging to our backs bear many masks — vampires among them. The Blood is more addictive than heroin, and the dead are habit-forming in their own awful right. The Daeva call that quality “Majesty,” but it’s not a stretch to say all Kindred possess a certain allure that’s difficult to resist. Some more than others.

        The Anvari have product to move, and that product is themselves. Any Lick can make a junkie, but the Lotus-Eaters supply a more specialized service. Rather than creating legions of Vitae fiends and blood dolls, they can fulfill any habit with nothing but a nip, and in the nights of the Persian Empire, the Anvari Kiss was a thing of legend, a panacea for all addictions physical and mental.

        Traditionally, they cultivated poppies (hal gil, the joy flower) or any other substance they could use to lure customers — but no one’s got time for that these nights. Drugs have too much overhead, whether financial or legal. But you know what the pigs don’t give a shit about? Gambling. Porn. Social media. Hell, booze is way worse for you than smack, and the government will sell it to you in ABC stores. Some Pushers run wineries for the rich and permanently soused, while others are sex workers with very loyal clientele, and others still deal habit-forming cure-alls for whatever makes you tick.

        They’ve smoked hash with the Assassins and supplied the Romantics with laudanum; they’ve cultivated harems for emperors and run casinos on behalf of riverboat barons — and they’ve ruined them all. Anvari require the disgraced, destitute, and doomed to feed their addiction to addiction. People who are comfortable in their habits tend not to see a vampire’s bite as an appealing alternative, so a Pusher makes sure her customers reach rock bottom before she takes her first drink, replacing addiction with an altogether more constructive habit in a perfect closed loop of parasitism. Any Serpent can make you want her, but an Anvari makes you need her.

        For the All Night Society, the Anvari are simultaneously a necessary evil and a source of conflict. A Lotus-Eater’s bite can relieve the addiction of even the most strung-out ghoul or blood groupie, and while it can’t break the Vinculum, it goes a long way toward clearing heads. Of course, in the same way, Pushers can also provide therapy for Kindred Vitae addicts, providing a rehab program that’s exactly as sinister (and effective) as it sounds. A few princes employ Lotus-Eaters as personal physicians, whether to treat their cravings or to punish those who fall to wanton cannibalism. Some mistake their gifts for altruism, but that’s a dangerous mistake. It’s not like methadone wants to drink your blood.

        One would think relieving their subjects of the very thing that sustains their bloodline would be self-defeating, and one would also be incorrect. Sure, maybe an Anvari can untangle your brain from its psychological or chemical dependencies, but addiction isn’t about the hit. It’s never about the hit. It’s about whatever circumstances brought you to a goddamn vampire’s doorstep for help. She can’t fix your life for you (not unless she wants to), and nine times out of ten, you’ll be back again within a week. Month, tops.

        Then, the Anvari will really have you hooked.

        Why you want to be us

        I mean, look at us. We make bank like Scarface and get to live forever. You don’t get into the life because you want to be a dealer: You want to be a kingpin.

        Why you should fear us

        Everyone has something they can’t live without, something that gets them through the night or pushes them over the line in the dull day-to-day. If you think you don’t have one, well, we already know what it is, and don’t think for a second we can’t take it away.

        Why we should fear ourselves

        I mean, look at us. All vampires are junkies in some fashion, and if we aren’t on the cutting edge of that particular flaw, we don’t know what is. All highs dull in the end. The satisfaction pops like a soap bubble on your tongue, and that’s when the Beast decides it needs a bigger taste. Ain’t no rehab for that.

        Bloodline Origins (Excerpt)

        Once upon a midnight darkly, a lost little girl in gingham came upon a wonderful field of poppies. Because she was pure of heart, she marveled at the rich crimson of their petals, the scent so sweet she couldn’t help but breathe in every bloom. However, these were no ordinary flowers, but the prized poppies of a wicked witch, and those who sniffed their pollen would fall to endless sleep. Yet the girl had great courage too, and she fought the lure of slumber as she soldiered on through the field. At the edge of the clearing, the witch awaited, and the sorceress offered to open a golden road back home if the child would only give up her heart and courage. However, the little girl had brains above all, and she blew a flowerful of pollen in the hag’s face, then ran down the golden road to Kansas. They say the witch lies among the poppies still, dreaming dead dreams of the things she can never have — addictively ever after.

        Nicknames: Lotus-Eaters, Pushers

                            Adroanzi: The Ones Who Answer Your Prayers

                            "Miracles aren't free."

                            There are many gods in Africa. From well-known fixtures of mythology such as Anansi the Spider to all but forgotten deities who watch over single villages or lonely crossroads. The spiritual ecology of the continent and its peoples is rich and vibrant… but not all gods are good. Some prayers whispered in the night are not made in the hopes of health and fortune, but rather for old and hungry things to pass the supplicant by and feast upon their neighbors instead. Some prayers are made not in awe and wonder, but in bitterness and rage, blood spilled upon black earth while wishing death and ruin upon those who deserve to suffer. For prayers like that, many things in the dark still listen — and answer. But when they do, you may regret ever asking at all.

                            The Adroanzi are an old bloodline in an ancient land, but one that has lost much of its power over the centuries. Once, these Nosferatu ruled openly over living tribes, their divine lineage granting them domain over the plants and beasts the people needed to survive. They tended their vast human herds as Gardeners tending to a grove: planting new seeds, pruning problem growths, and harvesting a price in blood for their “benevolent” guidance. Times change, however. Colonization eroded, and then finally collapsed, the Adroanzi’s powerbase, just as invasive Christianity dismantled their herd’s faith and fear in their old gods.

                            Modern Adroanzi are much diminished from the heights of their power, but they can’t forget they were once as gods to their mortal thralls, and their bitterness over having their rightful role stolen away drives them to recreate it. Indeed, the bloodline’s stubborn clinging to their faded glory has earned them the unfortunate moniker “Pretenders” in modern nights. Unlike most Haunts, many Gardeners surround themselves with cults of mortal followers, enforcing loyalty with Vitae, fear, and brute force. They become urban legends in the domains they lair in, lurking within old shrines and cursed groves, answering prayers in exchange for blood and other favors.

                            The Adroanzi are far from divine, however, and the miracles they grant to those foolish enough to seek them are harsh and terrifying in equal measure. A young man desiring success in business witnesses his competitors and their customers swarmed by hissing, biting snakes, leading to dozens of hospitalizations and an entire building condemned due to infestation. A jilted lover wanting retribution on the woman who spurned her is horrified to discover her drained corpse hanging from the washing lines the next evening. A father wishing for a more obedient son finds his child now terrified of him, convinced “the pale-eyed man” will take him away for horrible punishments if he so much as looks his parent in the eye.

                            Despite their methods, there are always desperate people willing to accept the help of monsters if it gets them what they think they want. However, these interactions strain the Masquerade, which is why Adroanzi usually operate on the fringes of domains, far from the prying eyes of Kindred courts. One might wonder, then, why any prince would suffer this arrogant and troublesome bloodline to operate anywhere near their domain, and the answer lies in why the Adroanzi are called “Gardeners.”

                            The bloodline has a unique affinity for producing a rare substance known as lacrima. This ocher sap is a delicacy among the Kindred, one of the only substances besides blood they can consume for pleasure. It can only be harvested from the vines of mandragora, twisted, Vitae-fed plants that crave blood like other flora crave light. Mandragora, also called “mandrakes,” are difficult to grow and cultivate, with most attempts ending in piles of fetid rot. Not so for the Gardeners. Where other Kindred might struggle to create a single viable mandrake, a lone Adroanzi can make an entire garden flourish with relative ease.

                            The lacrima produced from such gardens fetch a high price, and holding the keys to the supply is what buys the bloodline tolerance for their more problematic behavior. Some Gardeners devote themselves wholly to the creation of lacrima, preferring to withdraw from a world that no longer recognizes their divine heritage. Hidden away in vine-choked havens, they emerge only to feed or sell their little black bottles.

                            However, for most Adroanzi, lacrima is simply a means to an end. The sap gets them money and favors, which they use to cultivate worshippers for their (no doubt inevitable!) rise back to their proper place as divine rulers. One garden feeding another.

                            Why you want to be us

                            Let the other Haunts crawl in the sewers, too cowed to raise their heads. We walk unseen because others aren’t worthy to look upon us. We have the blood of gods within us, and our gardens drip with ruby ambrosia. To be one of us is to know your place in this world, and it’s upon a throne of thorns and blood.

                            Why you should fear us

                            We command the fears of men, we command the beasts of the land, we command the very grass beneath your feet and branches over your head. We are the descendants of divinity, and if you cross us, your body shall serve as fertilizer in our gardens.

                            Why we should fear ourselves

                            The worst fate for a god is to be forgotten, and it’s been a long time since we were truly known. We scavenge fearful prayers like jackals and drip lacrima in the eyes of our detractors so they don’t see how deep our sins run. It’s a poor Gardener who needs his plants more than they need him.

                            Parent Clan: Nosferatu

                            Nicknames: Gardeners, Pretenders

                            Bloodline Bane (The Sanctimonious Curse): The Adroanzi are either no longer gods or never were, but something deep in their blood still compels them to assert their assumed divine superiority over their victims. Pretenders all suffer a variation of the Blood of the Unwilling bane (Vampire, p. 108). However, instead of consent, the vessel must pay respect to the vampire without direct supernatural coercion, whether recognizing her as a deity or at least some form of superior. The Sanctimonious Curse does not apply to Kindred until Blood Potency 6, but it always applies to ghouls.

                            Disciplines: Animalism, Nightmare, Obfuscate, Vigor

                            In the Dark Eras (Excerpt)

                            Princes of the Conquered Land — 1501–1568 CE (DEC, p. 116): As the Grémio dos Corajosos (p. 45) spreads its influence across the world, the bizarre Tenth Choir covenant (Dark Eras 2, p. 352) sends a coterie to travel through Central Africa to sabotage the Christian and Sanctified missionary work spreading across the continent. There, they find unexpected but eager allies in the Adroanzi, especially those who wish to make inroads into Mutapan territory to the south. While only a small number of Gardeners formally join the covenant, they take on the mission of destroying God with a zeal only those who felt personally wronged by Him could. The Gardeners turn the land itself against the missionaries, planting hungry mandragora in newly founded church grounds and summoning hordes of savage wildlife to torment priests and converts alike. It’s rumored that one Adroanzi even learns to wield Therion, and when she finally awakens from torpor, she intends to spread its power to the rest of her bloodline…

                                    Welcome back! Let’s talk about the Kinarree Kinnarree Kinnaree.

                                    This was another bloodline that had a strong hook in the original that mostly needed a bit of finessing to fit in 2E. In general, that involved giving their dancing/fighting abilities more heft mechanically. In the original, they had a couple of Crúac rites and a Devotion. The rituals weren't much to write home about, and the Devotion is more or less just basic Auspex in 2E, so we focused on giving them movement-based powers. We considered updating the Muay Thai Merit in the sidebar on p. 119 of Ancient Bloodlines, but we felt it was pretty thoroughly covered by various Fighting Style Merits in CofD and Hurt Locker. We also wanted to give a bit more systems for them to actually hunt "demons" (i.e., ephemeral beings).

                                    The other big change was their bane. I actually find that their old weakness (i.e., riding the wave is a Vice) ties well into the First Edition Daeva weakness, though it occurs to me now that it may have inadvertently let you ride the wave without spending Willpower if you played your cards right. I might be missing something in that analysis, as it's been a while since I really engaged with the First Ed. system. I digress. We still liked the idea of frenzy as addictive, and I think the author did a great job of expressing that mechanically.

                                    This bloodline had some refocusing in development. Mostly, I asked the author for a better sense that they were karma assassins rather than just warriors; they have some overlap with the Amara Havana, who are also fighty Daeva with a Hindu mythos, and I wanted to make sure there was a stronger distinction between the two. I'll talk about that overlap a bit when I get to the Amara Havana, as it's a developmental problem I found quite thorny (and one I don't think I quite solved to my satisfaction, but that's a conversation for another day). I really like this bloodline, though. The origin section in particular is a highlight.

                                    The art for this one was also a lot of fun to do. I think the skull looks pretty metal, if nothing else. That said, convincing myself that I could elegantly fit that character within the text in layout was pure hubris on my part, but I feel like it was worth it for the strikingness of her pose.

                                    Alright, time to fill your dance card. Tomorrow, I'll be back with another new bloodline: the Kanaim of Clan Bekaak.

                                    Kinnaree: The Ones Who End You

                                    "Mind if I cut in?"

                                    Everything ends. This is not only true, but necessary. Without endings, there can be no beginnings, and this cycle is vital for the Wheel of Creation to keep turning and balance the world. Most things meet their end naturally and are sent back to the Wheel to be made ready for their next life according to their karma, as intended. Some things, however, need a little help.

                                    Kindred are static by nature. Beyond immortality, their forms are restored to the moment of death each night, their blood forcing the body to reset itself. Left to their own devices, vampires will stagnate for centuries, and the schemes they spin catch and hold everything around them in a never-changing web of influence and blood. Or, they would, but the Kinnaree are here to cut the threads.

                                    Claiming to be the descendants of asura, the demons of Hindu mythology, Kinnaree believe they have a sacred duty to bring an end to beings who are due one. Most commonly, these are ancient vampires who’ve grown bloated with power, but the Dancers also target more exotic creatures such as lingering spirits, patchwork immortal men, and human sorcerers determined to cheat death. “Kinnaree” is a name taken from the half-bird women of the Himmapan forest; they were said to have protected and succored Shurpanakha, the line’s founder, after she received Durga’s blessing.

                                    Obviously, the Kinnaree’s practices make them rather unpopular among elders, who’ve called several blood hunts upon the bloodline in a variety of Thai domains throughout history. Among their many detractors, Kinnaree are called Kin-Slayers, and most princes will not suffer them to operate in their courts, which means they’re rarely open about their lineage. They’re not so easily rooted out, however. The Dancers are a truly ancient bloodline, one that’s woven itself deeply into Thai culture, including the ritual dances so important to national holidays. The bloodline draws many of its childer from traditional performers, as they believe a master dancer physically experiences the history of the people of the land.

                                    Despite the heavy opposition they face, the Dancers aren’t without supporters. Many traditional festivals and superstitions involve humans aiding asura, a role that the Kinnaree enjoy taking on in order to manipulate kine into serving them. What’s more, many young Kindred — particularly among the Carthian Movement — celebrate the Dancer’s mission as justified and necessary, all too eager to have the Kin-Slayers topple a local tyrant so they can race to fill the ensuing power vacuum.

                                    Perhaps the most terrifying aspect of the Kinnaree, if you’re a potential target, is their beautiful, violent dances. A sensuous sweep of the leg enthralls the watcher so they never see the dagger descend. Steps so graceful the audience weeps tears of blood even after their hearts stop beating. Victims begging not for their lives, but to be allowed to see the dance to its completion before they die. Based on Thai ritual dances (or perhaps the Thai people modeled their dances after the Kinnaree), to watch a Dancer perform is a sight that stirs even the oldest hearts. To witness such a sight and live is a gift rarely bestowed.

                                    Despite their grisly work, Dancers are far from being grim executioners, or even righteous crusaders. Rather, the Kinnaree are as affable and charming as the rest of their Daeva cousins, which allows them to walk within domains largely undetected for what they are. They take joy in their work — not from the killing, but from the new beginnings they enable by removing that which is static and unchanging. When possible, the Kin-Slayers offer their targets a quick and painless end, with assurances that they shall have a better next life for having accepted the end of their current one.

                                    They are inevitably disappointed that so few take them up on their compassionate offers.

                                    Why you want to be us

                                    Of course you want to dance with us! Each step keeps the Wheel turning and preserves this wonderful world. You’ve seen what happens to our kind when we exist too long and forget what it was to live. Help free your kin from that existence, knowing that they’ll accept your help with gratitude — in the next life.

                                    Why you should fear us

                                    The last thing you see before your end is beauty. Accept that gift and die with dignity, or deny it and meet your maker in terror and agony. Your choice.

                                    Why we should fear ourselves

                                    Ours is an ancient dance taught by holy demons, and we’ve been dancing a very, very long time. Does that make us as old and unchanging as the things we hunt? One night, we must end, as all things must, but then who will do our holy work? These questions fester in our blood, but we dare not speak them aloud. Easier to dance on… as we always have.

                                    Bloodline Origins
                                    • In times of myth, when demons still walked among men, Shurpanakha was a demon created by the All-Mother to slay others of her kind. She was also a young girl, and the paradox of her existence as both an innocent and a weapon caused her great pain and loneliness. One night, after more asura died from her actions, she called out in grief to Lady Durga, scourging herself fiercely as she begged for answers. Moved by the girl’s suffering, the goddess answered her, explaining for the first time the girl’s purpose and gifts. Shurpanakha was necessary; she needed to serve as an ending to creatures who required one in order to foster new beginnings. In her mercy, however, Durga decreed that Shurpanakha would no longer need to bear her burden alone. From the half-god, half-demon blood that the girl had shed, Durga created beautiful, joyful children for her, ones that would never age so their mother would never grow lonely again. The Kinnaree claim Shurpanakha still watches over her children, and when one of them requires an ending, it is she who appears to deliver it with all the warmth and tenderness of a mother’s love.
                                    • The Kinnaree are liars. They don’t kill their targets; they sell them. Chaiyo Palathai was an old diablerist who managed to kick the addiction just in time to stop herself from becoming a draugr, but by the time she mastered herself, her thirst had already cost her all her worldly wealth and connections — her skills at hunting Kindred were all she had left. Having learned many strange abilities from the souls she devoured, Palathai searched for others like she had been, and offered them a deal. She would capture and deliver Kindred to them to feed their black hunger in return for money and influence. She became a dealer to a very select set of addicts, but not just them: mortal sorcerers, powerful in-the-know, corporate interests… There are many different parties who would pay a fortune for giftwrapped elder Kindred. As Palathai sired childer to keep up with an ever-expanding client-list (addiction is such a dependable business model) she invented her bloodline’s mythology as a smokescreen. Kin-Slayers actually go after tyrants and monsters because no one really misses those sorts of vampires when they’re gone. Much younger than they claim, the Kinnaree have been in business for only a couple centuries now, outlasting the disappearance of their founder and continuing to sell torpid Kindred to the highest bidder.
                                    • The Kinnaree are one of the Owl’s greatest triumphs. Durga ignored Shurpanakha’s prayers in the woods that night, as gods often do, but the Strix heard her. They came drifting out on the branches to watch this wailing, powerful thing, and in her, they saw an opportunity. Slipping into the corpses of women buried in the soft earth, they came to Shurpanakha’s side. They comforted her with words of smoke and held her in dead arms. They whispered to her a new purpose, that she was to kill monsters, old and terrible things that cause suffering wherever they go, ancient and powerful monsters stubbornly resistant to the Owls’ meddling. They showed Shurpanakha how to spread her blood so she wouldn’t be alone anymore, and ensured she taught her children as well. How the Strix laugh now, to watch the Dancers so dedicated to carrying out their will, removing the greatest threats to their schemes without so much as a nudge from the Birds. Indeed, by shadowy agreement, no Strix who knows of the grand joke will ever claim a Kinnaree. They don’t want to risk a Kin-Slayer being seen with yellow eyes and spoiling their lovely game.

                                    Nicknames: Dancers, Kin-Slayers

                                                Alright, let's take a look at Les Gens Libres.

                                                This was a tricky one.

                                                To start, I wasn't that familiar with the original version. I had some assumptions about what they were based on their name, but I didn't know anything beyond those assumptions, and the fact that they were Gangrel who didn't have Protean. As I've mentioned above, I wasn't originally the writer on most of the bloodlines I ultimately ended up doing, and I was in for an unpleasant surprise when I finally sat down to update them.

                                                For those of you aren't familiar with the old version, they were anarchistic Gangrel who emerged from the Haitian revolution...except that's a lie. They were actually feeding stock for colonial elders who had their clan Discipline bred out of them so they're more docile and easy to trap. They're the descendants of slaves whose true purpose is to be fed upon by high-Blood Potency vampires because they can't create blood bonds. There's even an artifact that you can use to track them for that purpose.

                                                So! Our version of Les Gens Libres has a murkier background; there's still a connection to Haiti (a very different one!), but there's also one to witches' sabbats in medieval Europe...and one to Aulus Julius Senex in Rome. Members of our version are still Gangrel, but the bloodline has partly removed the normal restriction on clan, and can induct other Kindred into their movement. They discard many of the trappings of lineage, including their signature Disciplines, to exist in a truly egalitarian All Night Society. First, however, they'll need to fight for it. Les Gens Libres are too radical for most Carthian Movements, and it's written in their very blood that all authority is illegitimate.

                                                Apart from reworking the background, the big change was opening them up to all clans. Because we already had the Sta-Au, and because we already jacked the Mystikoi for Wild Hunt, I wanted to keep them as default Gangrel. As such, I made the barrier of entry for other clans slightly higher (ironic given my complaints a few pages ago, I know, but I contain multitudes). That said, if you want to lift that restriction and make them truly open to all clans, that definitely won't hurt anything.

                                                Les Gens Libres: The Ones Who Set You Free

Even the sire–childe bond is just another imposition of power, an artifice as hollow as a vampire’s soul. All the structures and customs and moves of the Danse Macabre are designed for one thing: keeping you down and all the other fuckers up, and Les Gens Libres aren’t going to take it anymore. No, it won’t be easy. True freedom means dispensing with things like mercy for those who have none, and even for “bystanders” content to sit back and accept the status quo. No one is innocent, and these Libertaires are ready to show you how deep the rot really goes. With a flamethrower.

                                                Les Gens Libres are anarchists in the purest sense. No power is legitimate according to their philosophy, and even groups like the Carthians are too authoritarian for their sensibilities. Community is the only structure worth preserving in the All Night Society, and anything that places one Lick above another is just asking for a good toppling, to the point that they shun the Vinculum as the ultimate taboo, a precept that’s enforced by the curse of their blood. Hierarchy is anti-freedom, and anti-freedom is fascism, so anyone who would perpetuate it is worthy of death until proven otherwise.

                                                The cause isn’t limited to vampires, either. Les Gens Libres see no distinction between Kindred and kine artifices of power, and while they’re careful not to antagonize mortal governments, they have plans. Permanent revolution is the name of the game, a quasi-millenarian belief that they have the right and obligation to change the world. They’re not ready to discard the Masquerade just yet, but human oppressors are the next target once they’re done with the Kindred, and they don’t care if that makes them pariahs and criminals. Though some exist on the margins of the Cacophony, content to carve out their communes as long as the dead keep their corruptions out, many are guerrillas, freedom fighters, and even terrorists. “By any means” is both theory and praxis, and more than a few Licks have learned that the hard way.

                                                Near the top of the Libertaires’ shitlist, the worst of the All Night Society’s oppressions, are clans. Clans are a line in the sand based on a load of bigotry dressed up as supernatural pageantry. Vampires aren’t Daeva or Gangrel or Mekhet or Nosferatu or Ventrue: They’re Kindred. Even the so-called “gifts” of the blood perpetuate more inequality. Dominate, Majesty, Nightmare — these are just different names for the jackboot. Thus, the first thing a new Libertaire does is dispose of her clan; with the new blood coursing through her veins, she becomes part of a universal fraternity, one where each member of the whole is as worthy as the next.

                                                If murdering anyone who sniffs in the direction of hierarchies in order to achieve an egalitarian utopia sounds like a paradox, well, you’ll probably be next against the wall. Force in the hands of the oppressed against the powerful is acceptable as long as it doesn’t become institutional, and the Clanless are still figuring out just where that line might lie. In the meantime, they’ve developed a reputation that rivals Belial’s Brood for violence, and some Kindred whisper they’re one and the same as the Worthless. In truth, the Libertaires consider the Brood a perversion of their cause, all the more as they carefully study their tactics. Waste not, want not, after all.

Where they keep coming from is another question altogether, as is why they always return in greater and greater numbers…

                                                Why you want to be us

                                                You want justice. You want to live in this midnight world as a true equal. You want to take your despair at the nature of death and forge it into a sword to wield against those who would claim to be your betters. You want to destroy them all.

                                                Why you should fear us

Now you want to talk it out? I’d sooner cut out your fascist tongue and feed it to my dog to shit out. Negotiations are over; now comes the pyre.

                                                Why we should fear ourselves

                                                Bloodline Origins

As the people of Saint-Domingue overthrew their European oppressors, the island’s All Night Society experienced a similar tumult. At the vanguard of this conflict was a group of Gangrel who had been freed from bondage before their Embraces, and it’s said they fought well into the daylight hours to win their liberty once more. Eventually, these revolutionaries and their followers managed to take most of Port-au-Prince for their own, purging colonists both living and dead. Their movement rejected authority of all kinds, making a pact to never suffer tyranny as long as Vitae flowed through their veins. Soon, members of all Kindred lineages made the same promise, calling themselves les sans-clans. Few of the original Libertaires survive tonight, having either fallen to the fangs of their enemies or factional infighting, but their legacy sparked a fire in the Danse Macabre that might never burn out.
                                                • As the Inquisition made its way through Europe, a sabbat of witches came together to hold a great rite. They were, one and all, the children of those shunned and abused by society, or those who had escaped the flames with nothing but char left for flesh. They beseeched the old gods of the land — themselves discarded for the Christ and his saints — to make them strong against their abusers, to answer for the oceans of blood they had lost. Reaching through the rage and sorrow in their hearts, the gods answered back, but in their diminished state, they required great sacrifice. And so, each witch cut her own throat and drank from her sisters, their many deaths feeding the gods to make manifest their dreams of revenge. When they woke the next night, they were sisters in both blood and deed, blessed and cursed to walk the world to lay low the powerful.
                                                • When Julius Senex conquered the night, he brought forth the dead of Rome to legitimize his twilight republic, asking each of the Kindred gens to swear an oath to his rule. Each paterfamilias pledged their loyalty — all but one, a freedwoman of the blood clan I must not name. She made the manu fica at the dread lord and declared she would not be ruled by any nation of corpses, nor any nation at all.                                                     Good afternoon! Let’s have a look at the Bak-Ra.

                                                    This was always a cool bloodline. Mekhet who claim to have been sunwalkers is just the right level of audacity; it’s a concept you can do a lot with in ways that are both typical and atypical of the Mekhet.

                                                    The big changes were largely mechanical. Their bane, in particular, is completely different; the old version had them paying an extra Vitae per night to wake, which is not that interesting, nor particularly meaningful at higher Blood Potency levels. We felt it was more thematic if they had to “commune” with their god, Ra. That is, the sun. In first drafts, this required them to take damage, but I felt that wasn’t necessary. Now, they just need to view it live, taking whatever measures they desire to prevent too much, uh, rapture. Rather than making life hard for Bak-Ra players, we wanted their bane to be more about the ways you avoid it rather than being "die, lol". It's also something you can shirk if you don't mind your supernatural powers being a little duller, though at low Humanity, that's very dull indeed.

                                                    The other mechanical shift was their Discipline spread. Normally, we avoid shifting out more than one power, but Resilience and Vigor fit very well for their Devotions, and they’re an oddly un-sneaky manifestation of Shadow blood (are the Bak-Ra are so overt they’re covert?), so we didn’t feel they lost much without Obfuscate. We also wanted just a smaaaaaall hint that maybe their sunwalking claims aren't complete BS; see the Merit below.

                                                    You’ll notice that their powerset forms something of a parallel with the Usiri, who also possess a pretty atypical spread (three signature Disciplines), and I’ll let everyone draw their own conclusions on that, given our discussions about the… mutability of the Blood over the course of history.

                                                    The other change was giving them a bit more to do. The original version focuses rather narrowly on their obsession with uncovering their history, and while that’s still there, we felt it was limited on its own. So, now the Bak-Ra are (for lack of a better term) try-hards. They need to prove to their god that they’re worthy of walking in his light again, so they do… good… in the All Night Society. Each time they do… good… they hope they make up for whatever sin they may (or may not) have committed. Good doesn’t just have to be morally correct things; maybe a Bak-Ra becomes the master of a sport or the greatest scholar in the city — above all, they strive to shine. That ties into their Shadow Cult a bit, too.

                                                    On a side note, the third Bloodline Origin for this one is probably my favourite in the book, and is a perfect example of how you get weird in Vampire in a thematic way.

                                                    The art on this one was (I believe) the first I did. It's a little more (overtly) cheese-cakey than we'd normally go for, but I wanted the "sun-bathing vampire" image to be as explicit as possible. I also wanted a wee reference to one of the original illustrations.

                                                    So, don your sunglasses and take in the Bak-Ra in all their glory. Tomorrow, let’s check out another new bloodline: the Nehebkau.

                                                    Bak-Ra: The Ones Who Shine for You

                                                    “The only deluded one is you. Trust the proof before your eyes and see the light.”

                                                    The Bak-Ra must stalk the night beside lesser kin, but they know they belong to the light. Once, these Children of the Sun were leaders among the Great Covenant of the Kindred of Egypt. In this power structure, each clan and bloodline had a distinct role and was associated with one of the many gods of this land. The Bak-Ra (“Servants of Ra”) are Shadows who had the responsibility of guiding all Kindred of Kemet according to Ra’s will.

                                                    But those times are long gone. The elder Bak-Ra of tonight must learn how to play the All Night Society not from a position of leadership, but as senile ancients, delusional and out-of-touch. Those unable to adapt prove their enemies right. They build Shadow Cults to Ra and other ancient divinities; they may dazzle mortal cultists with ancient secrets, but trap themselves as much as the kine. Ignorant mortals won’t return them to the glories of the past, when they reigned from the top of the Kindred pile, so their neonates and ghouls are vital links to the current age.

                                                    It’s bizarre enough that any group of vampires would have ever been associated with a sun god, but that even doesn’t scratch the surface of the bloodline’s outrageous claims: According to them, they were once able to walk the daylit world without blistering their skin, without burning to ash — able to enjoy the sun’s rays like mortals. However, if this ever was the case, it’s no longer true. A Bak-Ra burns away in the light like any Shadow. What changed? They don’t know for sure, but they blame a curse. Most Children of the Sun only recently awoke from millennia of torpor, but they remember how hateful Roman vampires drove them underground and left them no choice but to escape into history. The Romans envied the Great Covenant and the blessings its members received from the gods, and so they destroyed it in the name of their One God.

                                                    Through the ages, only a handful of Sun-Stroked have awoken early from what the bloodline refers to as the “Long Night,” the period the world had to endure without Ra’s chosen, as well as the void of seemingly endless torpor these chosen suffered (the irony that vampires bemoan an endless night is largely lost on the Bak-Ra). The most common theory is that the Roman vampires cursed the bloodline with their vicious rites out of spite. However, the suspicious blame their fellow Kindred of Kemet instead, and even those who don’t blame their compatriots find their lack of support suspicious. Where are the Usiri with their wisdom? Where are the Khaibit with their blades? Not only are the Bak-Ra left without allies after centuries of guidance, the others outright deny their claims. Indeed, only their own histories back their version of events, and this only bolsters the ridicule they receive from modern Damned — not even their former pawns play along with their “daydreams.” This ridicule strengthens the Bak-Ra’s resolve to prove their detractors wrong — and perhaps even rebuild the Great Covenant while they’re at it. Forcefully, if necessary.

                                                    To this end, the Servants of Ra take up an odd niche in the All Night Society: doing good. As one might expect, the definition of “good” is… different for an ancient blood cult in comparison with modern morality, but it’s an ethos all the same. The Sun-Blessed have no illusions about the dead things they are, but they believe they must, in some way, prove themselves to their god so he might deem them worthy once again. Perhaps this is through personal achievement, becoming the master of a craft or the fairest in the land, or maybe it’s just community service, like, say, throwing some reprobate Lick into an oubliette with no roof. This redemption-through-good-works philosophy wins even fewer friends among the Kindred than their sun-walking claims, and it’s earned them a deserved reputation for sanctimony and overachievement.

                                                    Most Bak-Ra fit into two groups: The ancients who were long dead before Rome fell, and recently Embraced neonates created to help their sires make sense of the modern world. Those who rose early from the Long Night and their childer are a small but important minority, and currently, they’re the Bak-Ra with the most potent blood and connections in the All Night Society. Nonetheless, both factions, ultimately, have the same two objectives: Break the curse and return to a position of leadership in the All Night Society.

                                                    They’ll not be denied, no matter how many have to meet the sun.

                                                    Why you want to be us

                                                    We are diminished, true. Any Servant of Ra must bear a great burden. However, think of the future that awaits us! We will regain ancient glories and take our rightful place above all Kindred. Ra himself will once again welcome us with his warm embrace while the others suffer in eternal darkness. Accept this struggle now and reap eternal reward.

                                                    Why you should fear us

                                                    We refuse to be confined to the night. We know our memories are true; so many wise Kindred could not err. It’s a long way still, but eventually, we will rule both Day and Night again, and when that time comes, we will remember those who paid us respect — and those who did not. We will introduce them to our god.

                                                    Why we should fear ourselves

                                                    What did we do to deserve such a curse? And what terrible things will we do to get rid of it? We could be wrong. What sins will we commit in pursuit of a lie? Some truths are too terrible to accept. Perhaps it would be better to burn everything down than find out…

                                                    Bloodline Origins (Excerpt)

                                                    The Sun-Stroked aren’t vampires as this world knows them. These poor wretches come from a parallel universe in which, among other things, the rules of vampirism are reversed: They’re welcome to the sun, but darkness swallows them, body and soul. What they now remember as the Long Night was actually their minds being catapulted through spacetime. Of course their memories are jumbled. That’s only natural after such a traumatic event, but the real mind-bender is that their Blood (incompletely) adjusted to the Curse of our world. They speak the truth, but they and other Kindred simply aren’t talking about the same thing. They should be returned to their world as soon as possible, before the tear in spacetime causes a catastrophic collapse of reality.

                                                    Nicknames: The Sun-Blessed, Servants of Ra, Children of the Sun, the Sun-Stroked (derogatory)

A Bak-Ra must witness the sun in person (not remotely) every (11 – Blood Potency) days. If she fails to do so, [REDACTED].

                                                    In the Dark Eras (Excerpt)

                                                    One Thousand and One Nightmares — 832 CE (DE2, p. 124): Perhaps the strange dreams and nightmares that come over the kine during this era are what shake the first Bak-Ra out of the Long Night. This is the first time a significant number of Sun-Blessed rise, and they find a world very unlike the one they left behind. Shunned by the other bloodlines of Kemet and the “Mohammedan” covenants, these few struggle for every scrap. Their only advantage is the connections these elders have with the other ancient supernatural creatures of their land. Despite the animosity they face from their former peers and servants, the hope of recreating the Great Covenant does not die, and many Bak-Ra commit themselves to creating a united front against the Kindred of the One God. In the end, it will be the lack of sway among the mortals of this age that will doom their ambitions — a lesson the survivors learn well.
