    Understanding the nWoD current state of mythos (Vampires, Mummies, Rome, Egypt, Strix ...)

    04-20-2015, 03:24 AM
    TL;DR : What is the closest equivalent to a unifying mythos/story across game lines, especially in Vampire, Werewolves, Mummies, Hunter and such? Any link you would recommend?

    While reading other threads (such as http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...-and-the-strix, and http://forum.theonyxpath.com/forum/m...ascending-ones), I had the vertiginous impression of not understanding anything about the implied storylines of nWoD, including Requiem's.

    I thought I "got" it when I read the Vampire translation guide, that the scheming of the powerful, their conspiracies of silence, webs of lies, added to the Fog of Eternity, and even (in our own world) very real historical distance "make proving or disproving any given creation story impossible", that "the system treats none of them as more or less valid than any other", "many stories exist, but less evidence points to one being true". And I already know that I can dismiss what I don't want and create my own, it's all fine and dandy, even to my modernist objectivity-hungry brain (post-structuralist French Theory has to be a personal Bane dealing aggravated damage or causing me derangements...).

    Of course, Vampire the Masquerade's unifying meta-plot is easier for me to get a grasp / intuitive understanding of...

    But despite being different from Masquerade, signs point out to a certain linked, semi-unifying meta-plot, at least for the world's past even in Requiem and the other nWoD game lines. While I can easily keep or dismiss whatever I want, cobbling cross-over games mixing Vampires with Egyptian names, a roman "Fall of the Camarilla", Mummies, Khaibit vampire bloodlines, etc. Dracula, etc. ...it basically begs the question of, basically, what is THE "story so far".

    There is necessarily a story, because there is stuff you can't deny exists. Mekhet being a word requires an Egyptian origin of some sort, or at least an interaction. The Ordo Dracul requires a Dracula or some sort. What about the Strix? The Invictus implies a Camarilla and a relation to Roman history... and Egypt, which at some point was part of the Empire, which is not very far from Palestine where Longinus speared Jesus on the cross... etc.

    Is there a good timeline of events, summary, or global explanation of the various events? (I am asking because I actually want to know, not because I feel forced to know it.)

    I know for a fact, especially as a player, that "not knowing" is a pleasure in and of itself, "Mystery Box" and all. But the fun in Mystery is being confident that behind all the false leads, there IS actually an explanation, even (and especially) if it's a totally unexpected kind of explanation! As a prospective GM this summer, my players are going to expect some degree of truth to be found behind the many layers of falsehoods and red herrings. (Or else, what's the point of searching ?) I want to be able to decide what truth there will be, after having had a good look at what nWoD has in store.

    Basically, I know I can make my own stories or my own rules, I fully intend to be creative, but if someone uses weird egyptian words, mentions Rome or some historical time, they better have some idea of what was going on then so I can know if I want to use it or not.

      I'm afraid, that there is no thing as a storyline. There are a lot of myths, but they don't have to be true.

Every clan has different originstories, they all could be true, they all don't have to.

      Vampires exist and they do pretty much as long as mankind. In Rome one now lost Clan started to create a vampire society known as Camarilla. This Clan had betrayed the Strix, who are, or who are not the creators of all Vampires. The Strix believe that and hunt vampires for that, but it don't have be true.

      Every covenant has his own story, they are roughly believable but propaganda as well.

      In the last decades the Strix, who where rather quiet in the meantime came back to punish the Vampires.

          Lemme see what I can come up with:

          Distant Past - the first vampires appear.

          The first vampires seem to have been the result of a sort of symbiosis between early humans and something else. There is no singular moment of creation; the clan probably arose separately in different places and times. The Mekhet clanbook seems to suggest that that clan is descended from some kind of primordial, predatory being that encountered humans somewhere in central Africa early in the history of our species. Some or all of the clans may have been created by the Strix, either deliberately or by accident; the Strix certainly like to claim as much.

          c. 1835 - 1739 BCE - the rise of En Isiratuu

          A powerful Daeva known as Isiratuu becomes En ("lord" or "god") of Babylon's Kindred with the aid of a mysterious "Alu" or "Dark Stranger." Through the strange powers granted to him by the Alu, he will rule for around 12,000 years and extend his influence throughout the Near East.

          1352 - 1280 BCE - The reign of Akhenaten.

          By this time, vampires of all 5 of the major clans were present in Egypt (though the Ventrue were a relatively minor group), and had organized themselves into bloodlines covenants connected with the temples of the major Egyptian gods. Akhenaten's attempts to break the power of Egypt's traditional temples and replace them with his own brand of monotheism causes a lot of trouble for these early vampire organizations.

          1279 BCE - The rise of Ramesses the Great

          The leader of the Bak-Ra, Mekhet worshipers of the Egyptian sun god, makes Ramesses II into a ghoul, facilitating his long reign as Pharaoh, which brought great prosperity to Egypt. Around this same time, the Bak-Ra use the chaos following the reign of Akhenaten to consolidate the various vampiric temple factions into a "Great Covenant" under their leadership. This power structure remained in place, largely unchanged, until some time after the Roman conquest of Egypt.

          12th Century BCE - The Aenead

          According to traditional histories of the Ventrue clan, Aeneas carries the ancestors of the Ventrue from the ruins of Troy to their new home in Latium, the region that will one day give rise to the city of Rome. Some Ventrue possibly remain in Anatolia.

          c. 758 - 738 BCE - The founding of Rome

          Rome is founded by Romulus, who slays his brother Remus for obstructing his work. The Strix appear to Remus as he dies, and transform him into a vampire. Later generations of kindred in Rome will claim that he was the first vampire to call the city home, and his descendants will come to rule over the vampires of the Roman Empire.

          750 BCE - The Rape of the Sabine Women

          The men of Rome abduct women from the neighboring Sabine tribe to be their wives. Following this event, Remus embraces his first Childe, Aulus Julius, who would go on to become the patriarch of the Julii clan. Romulus sets him to work digging a network of tunnels under Rome in which their descendants will live. The Nosferatu of Rome claim that five of their clan were already in the area at the time, and aided Romulus in building his Necropolis.

          635 - 539 BCE - the Neo-Babylonian Empire

          By this time, members of all five major clans are present in Mesopotamia. At some point during this period, En Isiratuu is destroyed through the machinations of the equally ancient Mekhet sorceress, Nanshi Iltani, who unleashes seven destructive spirits known as the Edimmu (who bear some resemblance to the Strix) upon the kindred of the Babylonian Empire. The Edimmu proceed to tear the existing kindred power structures of the Near East apart, and by the time the kindred have exterminated them, they have come together into a single covenant known as the United in order to ensure their mutual survival.

          c. 600 BCE - The disappearance of Remus

          Remus disappears from the Roman Necropolis around this time; his fate is unknown. Aulus Julius becomes ruler of the kindred in Rome.

          509 BCE - The Fall of the Unnamed Clan

          The Strix appear to Aulus Julius about 100 years after the disappearance of Remus to demand a tithe of mortal family members from the kindred of Rome. Aulus and the Julii find this demand too much to bear, but another, unnamed clan opposes them, insisting that Remus made a compact with the Strix, and that any demand made by the owls must be met. Aulus condemns the unnamed clan, and the other vampires of the city turned on them. The whole of the clan is exterminated on the same night that Tarquin the Proud, the last king of Rome, is overthrown in a popular uprising.

          Some time afterward, Aulus Julius declares that the vampires of Rome should have a senate of their own, and establishes the Camarilla.

          c. 450 - 400 BCE - Aulus Julius enters Torpor

          Some time during this period Aulus Julius voluntarily falls into Torpor. As far as history records, he never re-awakens.

          c. 200 BCE - The Daeva Arrive in Rome

          Following the second Punic War, Daeva from north Africa probably start taking an interest in Rome and her kindred.

          52 BCE - The Gangrel Arrive in Rome

          A group of Gallic Gangrel follow Julius Caesar's army, and eventually come to the city of Rome itself.

          c. 46 BCE - The Mekhet Arrive in Rome

          Cleopatra visits Rome with a luxurious entourage, charming the citizens and sparking a period of fascination with Egypt in the city. Mekhet from Egypt first rise to prominence in Rome during this period, though there may already have been some of the clan in the city.

          c. 30 CE - Crucifixion of Jesus

          Jesus Christ is crucified in Rome. According to the Testament of Longinus, the centurion Longinus is cursed by God with vampirism after driving a spear into the side of Christ and tasting his blood.

          c. 62 CE - The Vision of Vahishtael

          According to the Testament, Longinus is visited by the angel Vahishtael, who reveals to him his holy purpose.

          64 CE - The Great Fire of Rome

          Four-fifths of the kindred in Rome die during the great fire and in the chaos of it's aftermath. The Camarilla is forced to more-or-less rebuild itself from the ground up.

          72 CE - The Siege of Masada

          The Monachus, the first of Longinus' disciples, is supposedly present at the end of the Siege of Masada with his four childer, where he embraces the fifth, Daniel. This, however, does not seem to agree with the chronology presented earlier in the text. Regardless, Longinus presumably embraced (or recruited, since some argue that Longinus never embraced a childe) the Monachus some time before this point, and by this time has already disappeared.

          262 CE - Thascius Hostilinus Arrives in Rome

          Thascius Hostilinus, a Numidian vampire whom the Testament mentions as being one of the vampires converted by Daniel, childe of the Monachus, in Egypt, arrives in Rome to preach to the Roman kindred, according to the Testimony of the Plague Angel. Hostilinus is possibly the founder of the Morbus bloodline, and is perhaps the most prominent leader of the Lancea et Sanctum in Rome prior to the fall of the Camarilla.

          286 CE - The Martyrdom of the Theban Legion

          The legendary Theban Legion, a Roman Legion originally station in Thebes, Egypt that had converted en-masse to Christianity, is executed on a battlefield in Gaul for refusing to sacrifice to the gods of Rome. Daniel has been with them since they were in Thebes, where he learned Theban Sorcery with the help of the angel Amoniel. Daniel turns on the remaining Roman soldiers, killing many before being consumed in a pillar of divine fire. Other kindred hiding among the army convert on the spot.

          Note that modern (mortal) historians doubt that the legend of the Theban Legion has any basis in fact.

          Some time after these events, the Monachus' two remaining disciples were martyred, one in Thebes (by a "worshiper of Seth") and one in the Italian Alps. The Monachus subsequently went on to found the Black Abbey somewhere north of the Alps, but the Abbey itself is a topic of myth and speculation, and its history is largely unknown.

          410 AD - The Fall of the Camarilla

          During the sack of Rome by the army of Alaric I, the Strix emerge in great numbers to prey upon the kindred of Rome, apparently as revenge for the slaughter of the unnamed clan and the violation of the compact they had made with Remus. Within a relatively short period the majority of Rome's vampires have been destroyed, the Julii clan has been apparently entirely exterminated, and the Camarilla is no more. The Lancea et Sanctum steps in to fill the power vacuum.

          1142 AD - The Founding of the Invictus

          Aelfgifu, a Ventrue Elder from the Rhine valley, persuades a group of secular princes from across the Holy Roman Empire to assemble in Rome, in the underground council chamber once used by the Camarilla, to establish a new ruling body for the vampires of Europe. Although the influence of this new alliance initially only extended to a handful of domains in the HRE, the philosophies and principals it established quickly spread, eventually growing into the modern Invictus.

          1204 AD - The Sack of Constantinople

          The crusader army of Boniface I, Marquess of Montferrat, captures and sacks Constantinople during the fourth crusade. A group of western European vampires affiliated with the nascent Invictus swoops in to seize power, breaking the strength of the Adamantes Athanatoi, an eastern offshoot of the Camarilla that had ruled over the vampires of the Byzantine Empire. Although they cease to exist as a governing body, the survivors of the Adamantes Athanatoi will exert a great deal of influence on the subsequent development of the Invictus.

          c. 1476 - Dracula becomes a Vampire

          According to the Rites of the Dragon, Vlad III of Wallachia rises as a vampire with no sire, having been cursed by God after falling in battle with the Turks.

          Late 15th - Late 16th Centuries - The Inquisition

          The early modern "Inquisition", more properly a witch-hunting craze that swept over Europe without any real central organization or planning, had a surprisingly profound effect on the vampires of the day, effectively demolished the web of alliances that had defined the European Invictus during the middle ages, as many elders met their final deaths and those who remained were forced to be subtler in their machinations. This marked the end of any serious attempt at a centralized vampire "government" in the west.

          Early 17th Century - Birth of the Ordo Dracul

          By this point the Rites of the Dragon are in fairly wide circulation, and just as occultism has come into vogue with the mortal nobility of the day, the Ordo Dracul finds many recruits, who establish the structure and titles used by the covenant to this day.


          ---

          There's more, but I just noticed what time it was, and I need to get some sleep. Hope this helps, though!

                          I'm totally amazed by that timeline! This is the kind of stuff I'd like to see, as a Storyteller.

                          While I relish the freedom to refuse a given "origin story" or explanation, and thus not feel constrained in my imagination, this doesn't preclude there being a default story in the book in the first place from which I could depart or base myself. The problem with multiple seperated / unrelated origin stories with no overarching / encompassing logic is that you might end up in a messy kitchen sink experience if you mix too many things together. It's much easier to justify many supernaturals if you make them related to a common (set of) metaphysic(s) that if you have to start anew each time.

                          To be clear, if each bloodline, each clan, each covenant, each werewolf tribe or lodge, each hunter conspiracy or compact, the mummy book, etc. if each of them have a different set of origin stories that are neither true or false, imagine the number of variables you have to take into account if you want to include any of them...

                          Which is not to say I would like to be handed a unified setting, such as Star Wars (which now has One Canon To Rule Them All). I think there is a margin between the two.

                                                      While Mummies are probably clear on when things happened, they weren't the ones who performed the Rite of Return, their knowledge of the whys and wherefores would be limited even with perfect recall.

                                                      Back to BarbeChenue, if you're interested in an absolute truth about things, you're going to have to make it yourself. You can use one of the many possible origins or make one up yourself. No matter how interested your players or their characters are in the history of the world, they won't find the 'right' answer until you decide which one you want to be right in the first place. Though if they're reading the books for the fluff they've probably already made their minds up.

                                                      The only real consistent thing throughout all the gamelines is that their histories tend to be entwined with the history of western civilization, from the fertile crescent and onward, sort of glossing over eastern Asia and the majority of Africa, except for a few stand out bloodlines. The more recent an event, the better the records of it, such as the founding of the Dragolescu bloodline, the farther back in history, the more unclear it is. The only standout might be werewolves, who might have evolved parallel to humanity in the Border Marches.

                                                      Also, since we have the Rome books, and the Dark Eras stuff is out, you can see what things were like at certain times. Neolithic Mage and Werewolf is likely to shine some light on some of their origins, and will have a sidebar saying what everyone else is up to.

                                                      There's a simple sentence in the Mekhet clan book that sort of puts it all in perspective, as one of Francis' past lives or past sires is feeding blood to a high priest and using what I think is dominate to influence him. Once Dominate popped up, you can't trust anything. The Ventrue in their own clanbook assure you they're descended from gods and kings, to the point that it extends to the Gangrel book, where a Ventrue is reaffirming this to Alice. But it really seems like they are just lying about it.
