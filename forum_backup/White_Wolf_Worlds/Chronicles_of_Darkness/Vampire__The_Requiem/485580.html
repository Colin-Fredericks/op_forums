    2E Blood Sorcery and Mage: The Awakening

    04-19-2015, 03:46 PM
    So, for those of you that have the 1E Blood Sorcery book, I'm sure you recognize a lot of elements from Mage: The Awakening's Sorcery in the 1st Edition.

    For the 2E of Mage, they've updated and streamlined a lot about the Magic System to make it a little less funky with numbers, like doing away with extended action sorcery and raw success pools.

    If you were going to update Blood Sorcery to the 2nd Edition, with its ritual factors et al., would you consider altering the system for adjudicating Spell Factors? Or keep it as it is?

                  I think one important aspect of Blood Sorcery using Extended Actions is the fact that the target of hostile Sorcery gets a chance to intervene. If it was a single Instant roll then this would either mean the victim would always have a specific set time to react which would never change, no matter how well the sorcerer prepares (to get the required successes faster) or does not even get the chance to counteract (if the sympathy to the sorcerer only kicks in at the moment the spell goes off). As such, Extended Actions work well to keep that aspect in.

                  That being said, I like that they are moving away from using Potency with the new rituals and instead simply use flat numbers or the sorcerer's dots in the Sorcery Discipline. With Potency straight from the 2e Core, rituals were wildly unpredictable. Depending on your luck with the dice (which meant not just your overall successes, but also *when* you rolled them) rituals could have been extremely powerful (instant-torpor Touch of the Morrigan, for example) or really weak. You always wanted to roll as close to succeeding on the second-to-last roll without actually crossing the threshold and then roll a lot of successes on the last roll. That was a really awkward system.
