    No tutor, no problem

    07-19-2015, 02:28 PM
    So I'm starting up a campaign which may include new players. As such I don't want to toss them into the deep end of vampric politics. So I have an idea for a less social and more personal campaign, but I'm trying to come up with a good initial justification for it. Essentially I want to have a group of players who are turned then dumped by the wayside without learning so much that sun bathing is a really bad idea. I had an idea about a group of anarchistic vampires trying to swell the ranks of unaligned vampires to undermine the Covenants through sheer numbers. But that still doesn't square to why they wouldn't stick around to tutor them.

                              One of the quieter motives behind putting revenants in the 2e core was to provide a way for groups to be Embraced. Your characters could have all been fed on by vampires, then "died" on the way home in a car accident or something. The revenant rules are a bit punishing to hit an entire coterie with at once, but you could just borrow the metaphysics and start the player characters as regular neonates.

                              The first edition demo also provides a justification for a diverse coterie of newly Embraced vampires, and in general works as a crash course in the setting for new characters and players.

                                  Okay, so an idea that I will need to flesh out. It borrows ideas from both the Malkavian disease and VII. It involves a secret covenant that is slowly spreading beneath the notice of Kindred society. It swells its ranks by means of a ritual that creates bonded coteries. The members of these coteries are amnesiac. They are left to their own devices by their sires. This may seem callous, but another effect of the ritual that is part of their embrace creates a subconscious mental web connecting the members like a twisted pyramid scheme. The neonates have flashes or dreams that urge them to the bidding of their sires. The stronger and older the members grow, and the more initiates they put beneath them, the stronger their connection and control over this psychic web grows.

                                  When members of this covenant come across "proper" vampires, their beast reacts in an alien way. It creates an inherent bubbling feeling of xenophobia that makes blending in with one of the major Covenants difficult. (though there are a few secret agents who manage to do just that).

                                  Only the eldest members know the end game. Swell the numbers, then rise up and replace the others.

                                                      Originally posted by LucidFeverDream View Post
                                                      I had an idea about a group of anarchistic vampires trying to swell the ranks of unaligned vampires to undermine the Covenants through sheer numbers. But that still doesn't square to why they wouldn't stick around to tutor them.
                                                      Sounds like a plausible Carthian plot. Swell the ranks of the undead to put stress on the domain. The established powers would look down on such byblows, shutting them out of proper vampire society. This would make them easy pickings for Carthian recruiters who come back a year later to see who survived. Even if the youngsters are just killed out right by the powers-that-be, their deaths would symbolize the indifference of the establishment to the well-being of the average Kindred.

                                                      Alternatively, a member/pack of Belial's Brood would also work as the Sire(s). They would do it to sow chaos, as part of their Pursuit.

                                                      Or a group of callous elders might to it as a test for their childer. Surviving a year as a newly made vampire, without any guidance or protection from your sire would be a brutally effective test of character/worth. In a Domain that strictly forbids the Embrace such a method would also be necessary to protect the status of the Elders in question, by concealing their identities.
