                Originally posted by wyrdhamster View Post
                How many of those vampires would return to it? With all the damage and low mortals population to feed off.
                In my game they had a rough patch in the couple of months after the storm, where it was almost post-apocalyptic. A number of vampires died. Others left. Those that remained were desperate for blood and took to poaching as the viable feeding grounds shrank to a sliver by the river. Vidal granted broad feeding rights in the Superdome (hence the horror stories about stuff that went on there). Over time things normalized, so by early 2006 things had reached a sustainable equilibrium, but only because a number of vamps had passed on (making room for the PCs in terms of territory and influence).

                The only named character I killed offscreen was Gus Elgin, whom Birch apparently burned as a Strix. The upshot was that the Sanctified lost a number of priests during the storm and its aftermath, undermining their hold on the city, particularly in the Uptown/Carrolton and lakeside neighborhoods. As a result Vidal was forced to grant certain areas to Invictus and Carthian interests, in order to make sure someone was in charge of major neighborhoods. Pierpont McGInn got much of Uptown, while my newbie PC Carthians (headed by a Ventrue) got the university/Audubon neighborhood, which they later expanded through judicious plotting.

                I don't have a hard number, but I assume there are around a hundred vamps currently in the city proper, possibly twice that in the metro area. I imagine that's as few as half, maybe two-thirds of the previous number. My assumption is that vampires have always been thicker on the ground in the Big Easy than just about anywhere else, since it's so much easier for them to blend in. The thing is, most of the dead vamps were Sanctified, which has led some to wonder if all of those were really just because of the flood. (They weren't.)

                Also, taking in consideration Vidal "Papal State" of Lancea et Sactum domain serounded by Circle of Crone voodoo cults in it. How in this would Invictus, Ordo Dracul and Carthian Movement work in to?
                The Ordo Dracul is basically illegal. They operate out of a mansion in the Audubon neighborhood by Tulane at the sufferance of the aforementioned Carthian Ventrue PC, with whom they've made a mutually beneficial pact. The Ordo is mostly apolitical and dedicated to survival and research, but Kendall would like to pull them towards the Baron, whereas John Polk (a name I swiped from the 1st edition core) is looking to veer them towards allegiance with the Invictus.

                The Invictus in my game started from a position of ascendancy, having gained ground post-Katrina against the Sanctified rulers. However, my PCs worked pretty hard towards McGinn's undoing, at which point the Carthians gained ground and Pearl Chastain experienced a sort of renewal and took the reins of the Inner Circle. That wasn't to last in my game either, as she overplayed her hand in destroying Savoy and underestimated my Carthian PCs' resourcefulness and hatred of the First Estate. However, my game aside, I see Pearl as a major long-term player once McGinn is removed from the picture. She'll almost certainly outlast Savoy and make a play for the Quarter on the pretext of her relationship with its previous lord.

                The Carthians in my NO are rising fast, mostly due to PC activities. They've set up a kind of autonomous commune in Uptown and laid down laws that grant feeding rights to those who put in a certain amount of work for the cause. Much of that work has taken the form of trying to render the Invictus obselete, which means setting up things like clean-up crews, fake IDs, and all the other little quality of unlife services that the First Estate is used to monopolizing—and offering it at a much lower price. That has led to some cold-war-style clashes, where at least one Invictus Young Turk tried to use the post-Katrina real estate market to torpedo the Carthian resource base. Like many of their enemies, he's not around anymore.

                For Vidal's part, he's been playing the Carthians and Invictus off each other while he can rebuild his own power base. Both are ostensibly loyal to him, but he knows the First Estate has ties to their allies in Baton Rouge and would love to see an overthrow of the Sanctified Papal States in NO. The Carthians have heretofore been mostly reformist rather than revolutionary, but that's not likely to last forever, and the storm and fierce competition for food has radicalized a number of young Kindred. Coco's still in his camp, but her influence is waning as the radicals look to other leaders. Vidal's best bet is to stall for time by letting the Carthians and Invictus go after each other. Meanwhile he tries not to let either side control too much of the city, and he only authorizes Sanctified Embraces.

                It didn't take long for Savoy to bite the dust in our game, thanks to a combination of plotting by Vidal and Chastain (who didn't realize Vidal's part until it was too late). Donovan is now lord of the French Quarter, having claimed it by right of inheritance and by killing his sire by his own hand once Savoy was officially branded a heretic for his dealings with the vouduisante Rosa Bale. My PCs arranged for Pearl to be undone immediately thereafter, so she didn't get to enjoy her victory and ended up a casualty of an even greater plot. Otherwise she quite possibly would have challenged Donovan's claim and won. That sent the Invictus into disarray, from which it still hasn't recovered.

                The only reason the Baron didn't come out on top of all this is that he was waging constant gang warfare with Savoy's camp for months after the storm and dealing with attacks from rival voodoo queen Rosa Bale, who still remains at large and still wants to unseat him. But for the most part New Orleans is once again a city with two power centers instead of three (i.e. Vidal and the Baron), and we all know how stable that setup is. As for Vidal, he ought to be taking his rest, but he's just growing more reclusive and taking ever harsher measures to keep his kingdom together. There's nothing to force him to go torpid, except that soon his dietary needs will be common knowledge, and the Kindred of the city whisper that something's not right with him. The PCs are wondering whether that thing that happened back in Storyville (which they found out about) didn't change him in deeper ways, or if maybe that's not even Vidal in there anymore...

                        Several of the clanbooks have some reference to it. Ventrue has some correspondences regarding a covert action against New Orleans from another city (probably Houston) during the chaos caused by Katrina. Gangrel has a brief reminiscence by one who left the city after the storm to become nomadic again, having decided it was too full of bad memories. Mekhet has a first-person account of a long-torpid fellow who is awakened by the flood and shares some of his impressions of the city in the immediate aftermath. Seems Vidal and the Baron are still around but no mention of Savoy, who is presumably out of the picture by that point. Nosferatu deals with the retrieval from New Orleans of the Glass Harmonium, an artifact that also shows up in Gangrel and elsewhere.

                        The New Orleans stuff is on the level of Easter eggs and some hints about how some of the bits might tie together, but it's more likely to tease your imagination than offer any specific details.
