    Custom character creation xp-scale [opinion wanted!]

    08-09-2017, 04:10 AM
    Hi everyone,

    I'm getting ready to start my next VtR 2E chronicle. I ran a test game during this summer. Based on the experiences from that game and reading the example characters given in the books I have created a custom XP-scale for my next game. I would like some constructive criticism. This feels about right to me, but I haven't run a lot of VtR 2E games.

    Neonate: 10-15 xp
    Ancilla: 25-35 xp
    Elder: 80-100 xp

    Link to a mock up of an Elder created with this XP-scale --> https://drive.google.com/open?id=0B-...1BzU2NfMklxWkU

                          I like to build cities where there's only one or two NPCs (per' clan) who actually get a signature Discipline to 5. All the Neonates are stuck at 1-2 dots, while Ancillae and Elders are rocking 3rd and 4th level powers with the ultimate bad asses getting access to the 5th level powers. It's a simple way to build power fantasy for the players. Also, having three dots in a skill is supposed to be enough for a successful professional career. Having four dots is supposed to be rare, while anyone with five dots in a skill is supposed to be downright legendary. That's what the books say, anyhow, so it's how I build the NPCs. Player characters are a different matter altogether, of course.

                          That way when the player characters get to a fifth dot in anything it means something. They can do things that only a handful of others in the entire city can do. Sadly, the Thousand Years of Night didn't come with detailed descriptions of Disciplines past the 5th dot, unlike Guide to the Camarilla or Guide to the Sabbat (it's been a while, so I don't quite remember). I know, poor example, but the only ones that I can think of when it comes to details of higher level Disciplines. Instead I give powerful characters a wider selection of Disciplines and a few Devotions to go along with those.

                                  VtR 2e presents a relatively smooth transition from neonate, to ancilla, to elder. Your average ancilla might be no much for your average elder, but the two groups are supposed to "touch" at the extremes.

                                  That's why I'm trying to get some clarity here. If you just don't want this, that's a very different thing than if you feel the chart in the books doesn't reflect what the books say.

                                  I feel like Elders are supposed to be the exception to the rule.

                                  The bonus XP are their for building PCs, not NPCs. Inflating where the PCs start only inflates were antagonists have to be to give the PCs dramatic tension. Upping the ante has appeals, but it shouldn't go to the NPCs, because the NPCs aren't going to grow like the PCs are.

                                  As well, if you're planning an elders game and want to boost things, using the Thousand Years of Night creation rules (and Spinning the Web as per Charlaquin's post) with a base of 25 XP on top of all that, creates some pretty heavy hitters. Are they one on one matches for some of the book's NPCs? No. But those NPCs are meant to be potential solo (not counting underlings) antagonists to a coterie characters created with those rules.

                                      You only need one century to qualify as an Elder by RAW. Even if you change that, you're an Ancilla until you are an Elder, so unless there is some special super-powerup that happens when one becomes an Elder, it should be more of a gradient. Probably with overlap, since an active Ancilla could have more XP than an Elder that survived so long by keeping their head down and not exercising their abilities much.

                                      Maybe the fiction Elders are more potent, but you bear the Elder title from 100 years to 1000 years, whereupon you become an Ancient. They should probably have a huge range of power to account for that. A new Elder shouldn't really be much stronger than an old Ancilla, once again barring some mystical powerup.

                                      I feel like people get hung up with what are, essentially, in-game titles that indicate respect for vampires that survive a certain amount of time. It doesn't indicate some fundamental change in what that vampire is.

                                      And I feel like once you start getting to where older vampires are just flat-out better because of mystical reasons rather than intelligence and ability, you start going back to Masquerade design principles rather than Requiem. Maybe that's what you want, I don't know. You still seem kind of vague on why you think this is necessary and what your design goals are for making the change. Nobody can really help you if your only explanation is "the current method feels wrong."
