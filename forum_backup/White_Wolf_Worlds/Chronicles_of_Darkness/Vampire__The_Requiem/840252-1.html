    Thing is, hypnosis doesn't star until you give them the command - being Mesmerized isn't equal to being commanded. Mesmerized is scene-long *potential* for being hypnotized. You need to issue an actual command first for the victim to get all YES MY LORD. And I'm talking about the space between you using your first dot and issuing the command.
    I mean, you can go meet a dude, shake hands with him, quietly Mesmerize his two bodyguards between one business proposal and another, and then - if you find that you need them to break the dude's legs after he decides that he is taking both his moneys and your drugs - order them to break the guy's legs instead of killing you. Or you might complete your deal without problems, and the condition will short out harmlessly after the scene ends.
    You cannot resist the command, but you are not a vegetable waiting for the command to happen, either.
    EDIT
    Like, drooling vegetable is probably the Exceptional Success resolution for the power.

                    Originally posted by WHW View Post
                    If you know how Dominate works, it's super easy to dodge it. You have at least one opportunity to act between Vampire Dominating you and declaring their command. Taking any amount of damage from any source removes Mesmerized, so if your target is hurt in the space between you completing the power and commanding them, the spell is broken.
                    Using combat turns, it looks like this:
                    Round 1:
                    Dominator: Dominate as a Instant Action
                    Everyone else: Still have their actions
                    Round 2:
                    Dominator: Issue the Command as a Instant Action
                    Dominated person: OBEY

                    This means that particularly determined vampire, or even mortal, can make your players go O__O by using his action to punch or stab himself to break the spell.
                    Nevermind if Celerity is on the field.

                        As a rule, it seems like the intended design of 2e is for mortals to effectively be at the mercy of any vampire that wants to do something to them, barring exceptional individuals who happen to be rolling lucky.

                        The primary weaknesses of Dominate have already been mentioned. Memories need wiped, for one, unless you're careful. Damage can/does break it. There are limits to what can be done with the lower dots. There might be witnesses outside of your target (a camera sees the security guard unlocking and opening the door for someone; even if the Beast keeps the Vampire from being recognized in the film, the guard will likely end up fired before any longer-term commands can be followed).

                        But, in general, you should expect a Ventrue to do what Ventrue do with common mortals. If you don't want it to work, it'll either be an exceptional individual (high stats and/or resistance merits) or another vampire.

                                I think that, when in the description of the mesmerized condition it says: "You’re not obviously hypnotized — you’re a bit quiet and reserved compared to normal, but nothing out of the ordinary.", it is written with the intention to not let the use of mesmerize power, obvious to other observers. For exemple you could use it in an elysium or during a dinner with other diners, and mesmerize a particular person without other think something strange is happening.
                                In the example of the combat, using Mesmerize on the opponent, according to my personal interpretation of the power (after the appropriate roll to catch the eyes of the victim), lets him quiet and he stops to attack anyone unless, someone else, attack him dealing any damage.
                                Probably I'm wrong but I like the idea that the vampire's gaze lets the opponent.....mesmerized and in his power even before the mechanical act of speacking and impress the order.
                                So probably 1v1, if the opponent is not a vampire (or have a low resistance pool), it could be a strong power, but there are a lot more effective power to stop the fighting...
                                Just think about Obfuscate, disappear and hit the enemy with a backstab that ignore defense and deal a Killing Blow. And this power acts immediately not in two turns. Even the simple celerity, that allow you to interrupt the enemy action or put you on the top of initiative.
                                So I don't see why Domination should be out of these range of effect. It's absolutely not unbalanced in these way I think.
