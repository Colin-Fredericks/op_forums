    151) The Damp

    Over the course of several days, water is revealed to have been getting in through a window beside a kindred's daytime resting spot. Sunproofing is uncompromised, but the water gets in no matter how tightly sealed the window is, possibly through the wall or lintel. Without physically tearing apart the window, which would defeat the purpose, there's no way to tell without professional evaluation where it's coming from. Over the several days this takes to schedule and investigate, the damp spot in the carpet slowly spreads wider, to touch the owners' bed.

    That day, while the vampire is sleeping, they wake in darkness to the feel of someone embracing their back, spooning them. The intruders' body temperature is somewhere between hypothermia and room temperature, and they are drenched with wetness. If provoked, the entity vanishes, leaving only a matress and floor damper still. If unprovoked, it continues to lie motionlessly until your concentration wanes and you return to sleep, waking to find nothing but more dampness.

    As a standalone story: The expert finds nothing wrong and the existing wetness evaporates as per usual physical water, although damage might have been done. The entity never returns- what do you make of a creature that spent days of effort to spend a few hours embracing you quietly, then leaving forever? Did it do something to you? Take something? Was it just that lonely??

    As a seed: How long do these events continue? Who escalates them? Do you find it's feeding, trying to possess or kill you, and have to stop it? Was it a messenger, and what was the message? Is this only a prelude to communication? A new ally? God, is this an OLD ally?? What did you DO?!?

        152) Earthquake!

        The worst one on record for the entire country. Where no one expected it, right in the players city.

        Vampires across the domain are awakened in the middle of the day to the psychic death cries of their clanmates, some closer than they ever expected, crushed by buildings and swallowed by the earth. For long hours they can only wait in their havens, corralled in by the sun and chaos. It doesn't take long for communications to fire up, some of the harpies arranging contacts and shooting out group texts, noting deaths and warnings.

        No one's heard from the Prince.

        In the hours before sundown, centuries-old plots are scrapped, new plans made in haste, coteries organize, waiting patiently to strike at bitter enemies, some vampires mourn over their lost touchstones and ghouls, while others see the chaos on the news, hear that help will take days to organize, and see only feasts and opportunity.

        When nightfall comes, fires are ravaging the city, along with the moon the only source of light with the power lines severed, emergency services are paralyzed, humanity is flailing. The scent of blood rides the smoke in the air. Danger. Cries of pain in the distance, punctuated by joyfully sadistic laughs. Vampires aren't the only things crawling through the dark ruins tonight. It's time to act.

            #153: Cobwebs in the Attic

            A favored ghoul has been complaining about a spider infestation in his home. He also complains about headaches which you might have mistaken for Vitae-born hunger pangs. Then one day he didn't answer your calls or messages. You went to investigate and you found him, acting strangely. He's colder now, more distant, and his eyes are those of a dead thing. Your Vitae seems to bring him around a little, but he has still grown disjointed and distant. Even Disciplines can only reveal so much. Auspex says that this is a supernatural creature, a human body sustained by Kindred Vitae—a ghoul, surely? But it tells you that this creature is also being controlled by someone else, and yet you cannot find any hint of the vampiric mind magic that haunts her steps. Perhaps one of your enemies has made a move against you, seeking to use a favored servant as a weapon or spy.

            Things get stranger still when Lupine activity occurs in your territory. Stories, rumors among the Kindred, "dog attacks" in the news. Someone's pit bulls, they say, perhaps dogs bred for fighting. You know better. Something has agitated the Lupines and now the court is growing tense. You notice that the cobwebs seem to be getting worse in the places you frequent. What could be causing it?

            The ghoul is in fact host to an Azlu Crawler. The Crawler found its perfect host body: perfectly preserved as long as it consumes blood from its vampiric master, protected as part of that same vampire's brood. It's using the shadowy world of the Kindred to spin its webs, relishing in their stagnant unlives, supping upon the blood of those they victimize. The Azlu has become addicted to the power of Vitae just as surely as any mortal would. What's more, it realizes the sheer supernatural power of the Blood and grows ever hungrier for it. Perhaps, the Azlu thinks, ensnaring and devouring a vampire will provide it with greater power, even spark its horrid metamorphosis. This process is not unlike diablerie, but it may in fact be creating something neither vampire nor Azarath. It may be on the verge of birthing a new race of horrors into the world, a bloody genesis of vampiric spider shapeshifters whose fangs pierce the very flesh of the spirit world and suck it dry.

                #154 How It All Fell Apart (This is a add-on to #149 to tie into a larger arc of my Obscured Lines story to explain some further lore of that story, but would work great on its own and start your own lore reasoning to the fall of Julii)
                Requirements: Requiem for Rome mixed with elements from Changeling and a touch of Mage

                Julius Ceasar has just returned from Gaul, but with lesser mentions of a secret attempt to reach Britannia, a mysterious Isle of Fog ruled by a coalition of three siblings. What was unknown was Senex (Aulus Ceasar) himself had gone along with his blood descendant, and was thought missing all those years while he was on campaign. Things for the Julii has been fragmenting, strained relations with the other clans are starting to come ahead... but Senex has been put on a special task from the nefarious Striges to try and redeem and gather favor once again as Remus had done.

                However, upon his return, the Striges are furious with Senex and have awoken forces Romans have not faced, and they are calling for blood. He had failed them, and the consequences will be dire. His mission was to bring back a being from Britannia simply called The Witch Queen for the Striges. A mage of power who can call upon Fire itself, and has a gift of Prophecy. However, in spite of the Striges, Senex has murdered her in cold blood, disposing her body in ways not to grant honor and damn her soul to wander the Earth.

                Aid Senex in fighting off the rage of the Striges along with enraged Changlings after the murder of a powerful ally, the Witch Queen, and fend off the fall of the Julii for a little longer, or help the Striges with taking down Senex for his betrayal while paying retribution to the Changlings and Fae, and aid in the fall of the House of Julii.

                In our story Arch we have modified some placements of real people in that time period and their fates obviously. If you wish to do something simliar as we have, the spoiler in this story is that depending on the choices of the party, they can aid or try and stop a horrendous fate for two innocent beings. The Striges, seeing that Senex has no regard for what happened, have targeted Julius Ceasar's daughter, Julia. In our story she is pregnant with twins, as she is the wife of Pompeii who flees the city after Julius's return.

                While on route to capture Pompeii, word returns to Rome that Pompeii has been killed, triggering a sudden labor for Julia. She has twins, a boy and girl, but dies in child birth. The children are taken by the Striges, one given to the Fae and Changlings for the repayment of the Queen's blood, and one kept hidden by the Striges for their own devices, which can be up to the digression of the DM. This story is easily to deal with customization depending on how badly you wish the Striges to lash back and vice versa if you chose to go against Senex.

                The key point of the Witch Queen in our story line is that the Striges wished to test the mixing of blood with mages of certain elements to see if something may stick as a sort of half breed, but Senex knowing this, decides on himself, even if it will help vampires against the weakness of fire, this is not something that should be done. Thus, he murders the Queen, knowing it will set them back years, if not centuries to find a powerful enough mage again.

                    155) High and Dry

                    After an instance of the fire department suffering an outrageously unlikely setback during a routine fire-fighting excursion, property in the city burns to the ground. Investigating, the Senechal is alarmed to discover that this is only the tip of the iceberg- several stations have been having serious financial, personnel, or equipment problems. The characters are deployed to determine whether this is innocent infrastructure failure, an attack on the city, or an attack on the parts of the city that conspicuously fear fire.

                    If they don't get results in time, or if this is just the seed to a larger encounter, an extremely high-ranking kindred has their Haven utterly destroyed via a combination of competent arson and yet another fire department blunder (was the elder killed, or is somebody playing houseguest with the PCs?) Now it's urgent, and it's a fight to keep the locals from panicking. Worse, it's still not clear whether this is an unknown kindred attacking their foe(s), or whether it's an outside player like a Hunter, or whether it's both, like a collaboration or Strix possession.

                                    159) Another Court Altogether

                                    At a minor kindred social function, the characters are approached by an awkward, milquetoast Elysium outsider, who is clearly trying not to look like he's hemming and hawing over an awkward subject to broach.

                                    Once relaxed or intimidated, he admits to an ulterior motive and pitches a job- his Touchstone is a basketball trophy on display in a local high school, and he wants the characters to safeguard it for him for a sizable cash reward. If the characters tell him to do it himself, or scoff at the ease of the task, he slumps (or bristles) and admits that safeguarding isn't quite the right word. His old high school got a new coach a few years ago, and since then their sports programs have improved enough to get state-level attention.

                                    Which wouldn't be a problem, but His actual Touchstone is, in fact, the trophy, while it's still inside the display case. The client's old high school achievement is about to be bettered, and the trophy replaced.

                                    That's right. If they take the job, the characters are basically committing to using their sinful vampire powers to quietly fix a children's sporting event. At which there will be at least one college scout.

                                        160) The Piper of Pubtown

                                        A regent is having trouble with their feeding grounds. A string of disappearances at the local bars is stressing the herd and making it harder to feed or contract out the grounds. So far they have it under wraps with the mortal authorities, but the ramifications for the Masquerade are worrisome as well. Four disappearances over the course of two weeks.

                                        The culprit is a Gangrel nomad from a despised medieval bloodline known as the Coppenbrügge. The line is known for creating pseudo-revenants called Waisenkind from children killed in the wilderness.

                                        Using a mortal honeypot in the form of a shot boy/girl (or any mortal that can blend in to multiple bars on a weekly basis), the victims are given an alcoholic shot/concoction which causes them to hear faint music in their heads, and to follow that sound wherever it leads. Of course, it leads to their devouring.

                                        The Red Herring
                                        If the players scope out the bars or manage to get a hold of security tapes from the nights of the disappearances, early on their radar is a local singer-songwriter who's had contact with two of the victims. A serial tail-hound, the musician is known for one-night stands and maybe-kinda taking advantage of drunken paramours. They ply their shtick somewhat aggressively in front of (or on!) the PCs. A roughing up, in addition to discipline use, reveals they didn't seal the deal with those two, and never met the other two victims. They can prove they were playing other bars on those two nights.


                                        The Master
                                        Parent Clan: Gangrel
                                        Bloodline: Coppenbrügge
                                        Nickname: Whisps, Pfeifers
                                        Heritage: Medieval German (Saxon)
                                        Disciplines: Animalism, Dominate, Protean, Resilience

                                        Bloodline Bane: The Coppenbrügge must sleep for the day in ground or water in the presence of special herbs important to the creature. If the Coppenbrügge’s slumbering area is located, and its herbs are collected, they can be crafted into an oil or poultice (Medicine, Science or Survival roll, 'doses' = successes) and applied to bladed weapons to change the damage to aggravated - each hit uses one 'dose'. Further, Coppenbrügge are tied to old medieval contracts in which mayors (regents/princes) would pay them 100 guilder (gold pennies) to leave their hunting territories alone. The gold coins would be buried near the hunting sites of the bloodline. If a Regent buries one-hundred golden coins in their feeding grounds, the Coppenbrügge cannot gain any sustenance from mortals who have visited the territory in the last 24 hours.

                                        Signature Devotions:

                                        The Piper's Drink
                                        Pre-Req: Dominate 2. Resilience 2
                                        Cost: 1 Vitae

                                        The Coppenbrügge mixes their blood with an essence of their 'bane' herb along with some sort of flora from a specific location. If the mixture is imbibed or ingested, roll contested Wits + Crafts + Dominate vs. the victim's Resolve + BP. If the Coppenbrügge wins, the victim hears a faint ethereal music in the back of their head. They gain the Mesmerized condition, with an active command of going to the location where the extra flora was harvested for the concoction. On an exceptional success, the victim even risks direct danger to follow the music to its source. Despite the name, the concoction can be food or drink.

                                        (Depending on your setting, there are usually foraging guides on the internet that people use to go herb/plant hunting IRL. Wild mint and Elderberries are decently common across the states, and often used for drinks / sweets.)

                                        Create Waisenkind
                                        Pre-Req: Animalism 2, Protean 2 (or 4), Resilience 1
                                        Cost: 1 Vitae, 1 WP

                                        The Coppenbrügge feeds its blood to the corpse of a child who was killed by the 'elements of nature.' Roll Strength + Survival + Resilience. On a success, the child rises as a Humanity 1 Revenant (see p94:VtR2E) with an additional benefit: one Predatory Aspect from Protean. If the vampire has Protean 4, the bonus is upgraded to an Unnatural Aspect. What aspect is granted depends on how the child died. Creating Waisenkind is a Level 3 Sin. See Minions Entry for build details.

                                        General Methods:

                                        At start, the antagonist is in the cycle of feeding themselves and their pack of Waisenkind. Using The Piper's Drink to create alcoholic beverages and using a mortal minion through Dominate or the blood bond to disperse them to loner-type clients of the bars. These victims then return to a secluded spot where they are preyed upon. If you want to prolong the encounter at this stage, only the Waisenkind are present and waiting for the victim. Otherwise, the whole group can be there. If the Waisenkind are destroyed or captured, the Coppenbrügge changes tact to begin rebuilding their pack. This takes the form of now targeting children in the feeding territory, luring them into the the closest wilderness/natural area, and letting nature take its deadly course.



                                        The Minions
                                        Waisenkind: Feral Children created by a posthumous embrace. Rising as revenants with Humanity 1, these creatures are very close to animals themselves, wrangled only by the Coppenbrügge's blood and disciplines.

                                        Disciplines: None

                                        Bane: In addition to the rules for Revenants, Waisenkind can be affected by certain Animalism powers (Feral Whispers and Summon the Hunt) of anyone they hold Vinculum towards.

                                        Ability: Waisenkind do receive one aspect of Protean that is always on display, determined by how the child died.

                                        Fashion of Death: Predatory Aspect p139-140 (Unnatural Aspect p140)
                                        Cold/Exposure: Patagia (Wings)
                                        Wildlife/Poisoned: Claws (Horrid Talons)
                                        Drowning/Suffocation: Aquatic (Rubbery Flesh)
                                        Falling/Crushed: Wall Crawling (Hooked Hands)
                                        Thirst/Starvation: Pack Vocals* (Pack Threat **)

                                        * Pack Vocals: The Waisenkind develops the capability to howl, screech or sing across long distances. These can only encompass very basic primal communications (Danger, Help, Food, Pain, Trapped).

                                        ** Pack Threat: These vocals can now also be used to threaten or intimidate other vampires. The Waisenkind may lash out using the bestial aspect, rolling Strength plus the combined BP of all Waisenkind in the immediate location.

                                                    164) Heart in Your Mouth

                                                    When one of your characters gets a Covenant-granted holding, some kind of long-established but minor property asset to utilize or guard, the place is an unexceptional example of its kind... with the exception of repeated minor items of architectural wear and tear. If repaired, the damage reasserts itself- there's something wrong in the building's orientation. If investigated, this eventually reveals a long-escalating sublimation in the surface the building rests on. If left alone, during a major rainstorm part of the basement folds up and drops into a hole in the ground. Regardless of how it's reached, eventually a long-secreted series of non-blueprinted chambers below the ground are revealed.

                                                    The chambers were added after the building's construction, secretly, but the damage has been so severe it's all but impossible now to tell how anyone got in or out. Virtually nothing salvageable remains; decades of gradual exposure to the city's flood-drains have washed away or rotted beyond recognition almost everything. Most of what's left are piles of storm-dross, warped fragments of furnishings, and waterlogged bricks of pulp that used to be boxes of files.

                                                    Oh, and the heads.

                                                    A recessed steel shelf contains a half-dozen withered heads, with what turn out to be a strip of their own spine and viscera wrapped around their hearts, then stuffed into their mouths. An attached credenza has protected enough of the notes to make broadly clear what happened, and how- the heads are stripped-down kindred, possibly still in Torpor. Why anyone would want to be able to fit a city's worth of slumbering vampires into a single broom closet is anyone's guess, though. There is no discernible hint as to who would do this, except probably "anyone who had access to this Covenant asset long enough to build a secret room."

                                                    Leaving alone how the characters choose to respond to this , there's one last twist; if they reveal the discovery, the Covenant rapidly tries to bury the whole thing. The site represents fairly solid circumstantial evidence that one of their own was, at one point, not just involved in grisly, illegal research, but research in the wrong kind of blood magic; a little brick chapel reveals reams of broken alchemical glassware, or that little Mystery Play theater has tattered parchment ribbons inked with pre-Druidic runes, or a private corporate parking garage contains what are, unmistakably, Night Doctor implements. Whatever insight the Covenant could gain from investigating this madman's experiments come with a potential side of serious and unpredictable political consequences, from without and within.

                                                    If the characters are willing to risk losing their shiny new asset and brave that powder keg for the sake of valuable or levergable information, though (or, hell, for the sake of the poor bastards on the shelf), hey, more power to them.

                                                        165) Getting Tanked

                                                        A socialite is holding a Mandrake tasting, and taking the opportunity to present a new product.

                                                        It's... different. Not bad, certainly, but peculiar, with tones of what more experienced tasters describe as acid, swampy, gamy, or salty. It's crazy cheap, though, and no weird side-effects.

                                                        It's from coral*. A huge, rusty-colored, fast-growing, aquacultured, obscure Indian Ocean coral, in a series of tanks filling a whole basement. It's ridiculously delicate and expensive to maintain, but it also produces an order of magnitude more Mandrake than equivalent expense for another variety. The vintner obviously doesn't want the secret getting out, both to protect their investment and their innovation, and to keep the competition in the dark, and because your product getting nicknamed Chum-Juice is bad for business.

                                                        The fact that the whole business venture could also be described as "basically ghouling a large colony of invertebrate predators with stinging tentacles and bodies covered with rough, horny blades, then leaving them unsupervised all day" probably won't occur to anybody for a while, though.


                                                        *specifically the unusually aggressive symbiotic algae making up part of the coral
