                                                    10-14-2016, 02:27 PM
                                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                    It's not. It's the start of an investigation. You found out someone is guilty of a crime, but you have nothing but your word against theirs to prove it,
                                                    Which is fine if that is the adventure you wish to run, but immediately creates major headaches if you want to run a wide variety of other archetypal adventures such as a murder mystery. Want to have PCs in doubt as whether someone is a villain or not, know whether their new ally is a traitor or not, basically any adventure where not knowing if someone is X or not, becomes much more difficult to pull off. An adventure where you know someone is guilty but have to convince others could be a very fun adventure. But it doesn't alter that Auspex 1 as written constrains the sort of adventures you can run a lot. No more whodunnits for you!

                                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                    same as if you witnessed a murder but had no proof to show for it. A lot of thrillers start off that way. Granted, other Auspex users can corroborate your claims just as other eyewitnesses can but it's still not hard evidence.
                                                    And again, we're back to the constraints on the stories you can tell. You presume a setting where there's some rigorous legal process amongst the vampire community or that the person who sees it has to prove beyond doubt to others that it is the case. The powerful prince whose word is law is a staple of Vampire games. If they have Auspex 1 (not a big ask), job done. They pronounce that the new vampire is a diablerist. Doesn't even have to be a powerful prince. Anyone whom others either trust enough or don't wish to challenge will do. If Diablerie is this terrible crime then simply having elders able to spot it is sufficient to bring down punishment. You don't have to have punishments or problems imposed by community consensus. If Diablerie is this unforgiveable crime then simply having random powerful elder know you're guilty of it is sufficient to invite destruction.

                                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                    And we're talking about vampires here. Creatures paranoid to no end, and intrinsically self-serving. You think anyone is going to back you up without a little quid-pro-quo? You think anyone is going to take you on your word when your word could just as easily condemn them? Everyone in the Domain is guilty of something, who gets popped for what has more to do with politics than actual guilt. You want to catch your culprit, you're going to need better leverage than "my spirit senses told me".
                                                    Putting "my spirit senses told me" in quotes as if it would be treated in-game the same way people would treat it in real life is disingenuous. A lot of vampires have those senses and experience them intimately and even those that don't know that they are real. Furthermore, others can verify. Any remotely respectable vampire saying someone was guilty of such a terrible crime would get the others all gathering together to check for themselves. And when they all agreed, job done. Either Diablerie is not this terrible crime that other vampires all find repugnant, or you don't make it easy to detect. Anything other than this leads to action against the diablerist. I don't have to get on with my neighbour to help them deal with someone who is eating people on our street.

                                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                    First of all, unless the crime is specifically Diablerie, that's not a question I'd allow with Auspex. Second of all, for the reasons given above, no, not end of story.
                                                    The write-up of the power offers a wide range of options beyond just Diablerie. As written it could very well tell you whether someone is guilty of other crimes. In fact as written it should.

                                                    Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                                    I agree. Auspex shouldn't be the solo evidence used to condemn criminals because that would be stupid. It would be comparable to human courts condemning criminals solely on the basis of eyewitness testimony. Which, as I've said before, is how you end up with witch hunts.
                                                    And are human courts comparable to vampire society? If you're a community of a 100, most of whom have met each other and who organise themselves by who has the most power, witness testimony (what this effectively is) is going to be a common approach. Especially when a sizeable portion of your community (quite possibly including yourself) can become witnesses on request and the judge / ruler / police can also be witnesses.

                                                    You can certainly build settings and adventures where the ability to easily detect Diablerie is not a problem. But it certainly limits a number of popular settings and adventures from being viable.
