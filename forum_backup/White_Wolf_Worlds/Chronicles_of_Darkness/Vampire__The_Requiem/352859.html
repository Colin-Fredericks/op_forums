    No Humanity Variant?

    12-28-2014, 01:54 PM
    When we updated from 1st edition Requiem to 2nd edition, Humanity was much improved as a concept and a mechanic; however, I find it still just doesn't work well for me and some of my group. I'd therefore like to try out a rules variant where there just isn't a humanity stat at all, and see how it goes. Of course, in 2E humanity is interwoven into many mechanics, so it's a bit more involved than just removing it. Here are my current thoughts on mechanics that would need some adjusting:

    The Basics: Blood Potency would probably take a lot of the functions of Humanity onto itself. Each age category of vampire has an average humanity (the book speaks of things like "most ancillae fall in this category" and stuff), so tying some of this stuff to the other stat that's influenced most by age seems to make sense. Humanity was also one of the big limits on the Embrace and diablerie, and I can see plausible ways to tie this into BP instead. (Diablerie does it on its own, and a connection between the Embrace and how fast your blood thickens doesn't seem too far-fetched.)

    I'm considering using Blood Beats in some form to supplement BP growth, sort of making those who act more vampiric thicken faster, and thus getting to the problem points faster. Like how those who act more vampiric drop humanity faster, too. Way too fast if you ask me, if I played my players through 50 years of night to night play, they'd never manage to still be at the "ancillae" level of humanity. I'm toying with the idea to make BP cost 5xp times your current BP rank to go up to the next level, so that it doesn't shoot up too fast.

    Social Penalties: Truth be told, these flat social penalties low humanity gives you is one of the things I and some of my players just don't like too much. If I do want to keep them though, it's simple to tie that to BP. You just invert the table: penalties rise as BP gets higher, rather than rising as humanity gets higher.

    Touchstones: I like touchstones. In fact, I love them. I don't want to throw the child out with the bath water and get rid of them too, but their main purpose is somewhat diluted without the humanity mechanic. A bonus to resisting frenzy for having touchstones, perhaps? Their willpower regaining properties are still useful of course, and maybe just making it a bit easier to get wp back from them (it's quite hard to get wp back in our group, so there's room for improvement there) might keep them useful. I truly detest the way dropping Humanity has my touchstones automatically detach, especially since I've frequently had a bout of bad luck turn two necessary evils into a quick 1-2-punch detachment of an interesting Touchstone (yeah, they're still there, but it's not the same), and then you have to face the xp tax... Over half the people in another game I'm in just don't have touchstones anymore as they dropped so fast in a short period of time that the xp tax it'd take to reach their level doesn't seem worth it to them, with the risk that they detach right away.

    Players will have to work to keep their relationships to their touchstones up, and they can detach if they mess up or estrange them somehow. I've always preferred running things like this purely through cause and effect rather than a tangentially related failed dice roll. I suppose that it would be possible to attach them again too, maybe with Social Maneuvering and some effort. Might be a good place to put the Ventrue curse in place, or penalties form high BP.

    Clan Banes: The gap between BP1 and 2 is higher than Humanity 7 and 6, so I'm thinking of having it in effect right away. At most maybe a delayed onset of 1d10 weeks could be considered. Mekhet would count as higher BP for banes rather than lower humanity.

    Sunlight: Either make both interval and damage based upon BP, fix one of the two at a steady rate, or have one of them decided by something else. I'm thinking of just doing something like this base don BP:
    0: No sunlight damage.
    1: 1 lethal/10 minutes
    2: 2 lethal/10 minutes
    3: 2 lethal/1 minute
    4: 2 lethal/turn
    5: 1 aggravated/turn
    6: 1 aggravated/twice per turn
    7: 2 aggravated/twice per turn
    8: 2 aggravated/thrice per turn
    9: 3 aggravated/thrice per turn
    10: 4 aggravated, 4 times in a turn.

    A different progression than normal, but it works. In general this means vampires can be "ok" at being in sunlight for a bit longer than in your average game, but I'm fine with that.

    Banes: I'm thinking of redesigning these a bit, making both "lesser" and "greater" banes. You would then automatically acquire banes at certain BP levels, like a minor at BP2 to start, and go from there. Minor banes would probably give penalties to certain actions after encountering or sinning against the bane (the size of the penalty equal to BP, probably), while greater ones likely do damage or have other equally bad effects. I'd like to get some simple guidelines together so GM and players can make up a bane for a character easily and have it fit them, the current list is a bit hit or miss.

    Daysleep: Replace humanity with Sta + Resolve, with a bonus for low BP (small) and then -1 from that for every BP you go up. Considering not giving the bonus from Resilience to this, similar to how Vigor doesn't give a bonus to Lashing Out with the Monstrous Beast. Speaking of those...

    The 3 Conditions of the Beast: Bestial, Competitive and Wanton have some resolutions dealing with Humanity. I never liked those anyway, so those were already house ruled to not use humanity resolutions. "Indulge a vice at a personal risk, or..." and stuff like that. Not too hard.

    Torpor: Just base it off BP. I've never been in a game where the exact length of a lengthy torpor was important anyway. Perhaps make torpor degrade BP a bit faster, making a dynamic where vampires both go up faster in BP and go down faster. Maybe.

    Diablerie: I believe more in social repercussions to keep people in check, so I'm not worried about losing breaking points for this. It makes BP go up, which will also give more banes, make them affect you more, and the like. That seems sufficient to me next to the social stigma and risk of discovery.

    Embrace (and Ghouling, to some extent): Same as Diablerie, I believe more in social repercussions. Go ahead and Embrace away, but be prepared for your sudden flock of neonates to set half the city on fire, piss off every other Vampire and bring several Hunter conspiracies knocking on your door. I'm toying with the idea of making the Embrace cost 1 or more Blood Beat XP; it sounds plausible that Embracing another makes your blood thin slower. I'm not sure if this wouldn't just be seen as a slow-bp-gain-down exploit though (but obviously, high BP makes you quite powerful, so maybe not.) Thoughts?



    So yeah, aside from some smaller things like changing some merits or specific powers, I think that's about it. Anyone notice something big missing? Got ideas on touchstones or other elements, done something like this yourself before, etc... ? Let me know!

                                      The original post appears to be a riff off of one of the options found in World of Darkness: Mirrors. For reference purposes, Mirrors proposed the following changes:

                                      • Base Time spent in Torpor: use Stamina + Resolve instead of Humanity
                                      • Remaining awake during the day: use Stamina + Resolve - Blood Potency instead of Humanity
                                      • Interacting with mortals: use Blood Potency as a penalty to suitable rolls

                                      Of course, Mirrors was dealing with VtR 1e; and in 2e, Humanity gets used for more/different things; so Unahim’s proposals need to address those changes as well. Which, apparently, it does.

                                      There are also the “Atrocities” and “Hell Is Other People” options mentioned in Danse Macabre, the latter being a spiritual predecessor of sorts of 2e’s Touchstones. That said, neither really addresses what to do with cases where your Humanity score is referenced directly by the rules (such as being the basis for a dice pool, or being a resource you must expend); rather, they act as replacements for the detachment rolls: instead of having to roll against Humanity and risk lowering its score when you do something monstrous, the Atrocities rules give you “Atrocity Dice” for such actions that tend to poison appropriate rolls until you rid yourself of them; and instead of losing Touchstones whenever your Humanity drops below a certain threshold, “Hell Is Other People” has a tracking system where your relationship with your “anchors” gets corrupted and twisted into something depraved by such actions. In this regard, they’re worth looking at; however, the question of what to do without a Humanity trait is still something that needs to be looked at: with Atrocities and/or HIOP in play instead of Humanity, how do you determine how long you spend in torpor or how well you’re able to stay awake during the day?

                                      My hacks: Trinity Continuum, Scion, Exalted, World of Darkness, Chronicles of Darkness
