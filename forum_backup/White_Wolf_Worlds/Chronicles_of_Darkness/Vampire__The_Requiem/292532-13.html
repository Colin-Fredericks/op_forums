            Ok, since this is still technically in Open Development, and therefore still open for questions and feedbacks, I'm guessing this is not a breach of netiquette.
            --

            About the Coil of Živa:

            First off, I love it. Denying the Bane and Buttress the Soul are amazing, and so is Bleed the Skin. Funny thing is that, unless you are willing to run away from the Ordo Dracul the rest of your unlife, you basically need Twilight Judge and Status: Ordo Dracul at three dots each. Which is a pretty steep requirement from the get go.

            Denying the Bane, as mentioned earlier in the thread, would benefit from being clearer, with examples of sort. Does it apply to Torpor duration and Sunlight damage on the charts? How does it apply to the different clan Banes (do you substract the Mekhet's -1 from Humanity for banes like Sunlight and then add +X afterwards? Does this mean you can end up... with a negative penalty, thus a bonus (I'm stretching it on purpose here), or does the effect already apply to Torpor duration and Sunlight damage, etc.). This would be a very powerful Coil, and synergize with Coil of the Ascendant very much, but I think it would be balanced by the fact you just paid 6 Experiences for Twilight Judge and its prerequisites in order to avoid a blood hunt against ya.

            Buttress the Soul, on the other hand, seems 100% clear. The temptation of spending too much blood is inevitably going to trigger breaking points to regain said blood, which is interesting narratively. This is an interesting gamble. Though if you already have multiple touchstone and really chug through vitae like crazy, this could lead to almost guaranteed successes on Detachment rolls, and tons of Beats because of high humanity. Something the Storyteller needs to watch out for.

            (table shows the % chance of failing a Detachment roll)
            8d--- 5,76%
            9d--- 4,04%
            10d-- 2,82%
            Bleed the Skin: if you can apply this on yourself, this can be interesting. (Can it be applied on one self?) Let's say you stay at crazy high Humanity, you keep having to roll for Detachment for so many little things, and gain tons of Beats. If you fail, simply purchase Humanity back at discount, and keep getting Beats coming. This could be abused by a smart and subtle player, something to watch out for.

            Anyhow, the cost of entry in all of this is still pretty steep, but I can see how it could be mechanically employed by a clever player.

                        Actually thinking about it more...I think my issue is something I've been noticing more as VtR2e has gone (it may have been prevalent in 1e as well but I barely played 1e). There are a lot of merits that interact with other passive merits. Such as Herd, Feeding Ground, Notary, Twilight Judge, etc. A lot of these probably come up as very specific applications of broader merits like Status or Contacts or come up as relevant for a single plot but they don't let my character do something new and cool.

                        Let's say I am a Notary. More people pay attention to me, I can trade a new kind of favour, I have some more clout and importance in the political game, so it definitely has value but I don't do much with it that is unique to it. Having a high Status would give me a similar kind of approach to things, even if the specifics were different.

                        Similarly with the Wyrms Nest. Ok, my character has a nest and their Haven and Safe Place are linked to it an surrounded with a Feeding Ground. I now have what amounts to a well protected home base of operations. I can trade some other new types of favours so again, has value. But I'm not really doing anything new when I have them. Day to day I;m still going to Elysiums or protecting my turf from invaders or hijacking the procurement department of a museum.

                        I like the Dracul as they are represented in some ways as a group that politics less and does things more. They go out and do monstrous things for 'science'. The Lance sacrifice sacraments but those are often not too onerous to get ahold of. The Crone spend blood for their unique thing and the default state of vampires is acquiring blood. The Carthians and Invictus play the politics game that I wanted t move away from. The Dracul however go out and create vile concotions of blood, salt and rendered human fat and force others to drink it and vomit out their vinculum. It's that side of them that appeals to me.

                        This particular set of stuff feels less like it plays up the aspects of the Dracul that made me want to play as one.

                        Starting a session with an Aspiration of 'find a victim to peel off their skin to scrape off the fat layer underneath' feels distinctly unique to a Dracul character while an Aspiration of 'bribe James with access to my nest to get him to overlook Monica's indiscretions' feels like it could slot into any of the Covenants, just swap Nest for Notary or Safe Place or Herd or Feeding Ground or...etc.

                        I now that these kinds of merits are needed for verisimilitude, for world building and for those players who do enjoy that kind of game play so I in no way would want to see them removed. I just want to see more Flesh Grafting, more reasons why I would want to reenact the scene from the core book with the kindred strapped to a table with their flesh peeled back to expose their rib cage. Give me Scales and Coils that require me to engage in some form of activity/experiment/physical thing to use them.

                        Sigh. I know this isn't particularly useful, I'm basically saying to give me less of the stuff I don't like and more of the stuff I do like and also rather late into the process as this went up in 2014!

                            Actually, there's a bit more of a difference than that. Notaries are required to be present for the use of any Oath to take effect.

                            A notary can make personal oaths with no one else present. While the core book oaths are often between a liege and follower, there's a number of the new ones that can be made in a vacuum if you have the Notory merit. For instance, they could swear the Oath of the True Knight at will to avoid being forcefully blood bonded. Oath of the Refugee for a nomadic cotorie establishing safety. In theory, the GM could allow a spontaneous new Oath to take place as well.

                            A lot of the things you can do with these merits aren't always obvious at first glance. Chapter House, for instance. If you're a Majesty or Nightmare user, you'd love it for making it less likely for someone to resist your powers. Having what amounts to a highly effective Elysium where your powers are uncontested is pretty nice.

                            Many mortal supernatural powers use skill rolls (usually an Occult roll). Adding in your Occult Alignment dots could be helpful when you're investing in the Coil of Ziva.

                                        THE UNDEAD RISE AGAIN!

                                        Sorry for necroing this, I just made some alterations to Živa, thought you guys might like it:
                                        Soul of Life •••
                                        This coil allows the vampire to fool both herself and her beast with the mimicry of life. Upon learning this coil, the vampire chooses a virtue, a vice, and a ritual exclusive to mortals (such as a family dinner, drunken partying, etc.). So long as she benefits from the blush of life, she has the chosen virtue, vice, and ritual, in addition to her mask and dirge. The ritual takes at least an hour to complete, restores a point of Willpower, but can only be performed once each night.

                                        Denying the Hold ••••
                                        Dragons that have mastered this coil can deny the hold of the Beast over the Man through sheer tenacity. When subjected to a condition uniquely experienced by Kindred, such as Jaded, Tempted, or Competitive, she can spend a point of Willpower to attempt a Resolve + Composure + Coil roll. On a success, the condition is not applied at all. On an exceptional success, the condition is not applied, and the next time she is subjected to the same condition, she needn't spend Willpower to reject it.

                                        Shedding the Beast's Skin •••••
                                        The final spiral in Živa’s Coil has the Dragon literally shed her flesh, and with it, her vampiric nature. She spends three Willpower and three Vitae to activate Shedding the Beast’s Skin. Shedding the skin takes five turns. Shedding the Beast’s Skin lasts for twelve hours, minus one for each dot of the Dragon’s Blood Potency. During this time, she becomes effectively human. Her heart beats. Her skin sweats. She can get hungry, and she can eat. She can engage in sexual activity as if human. She easily passes medical scrutiny. She can walk out into the sun. This comes with it a loss of her vampiric advantages (except for those granted by this mystery; she retains the virtue, vice, and ritual granted by Soul of Life), Disciplines, her ability to ingest, store, and expend Vitae, and her immortality. She no longer suffers any vampiric bane, has no predatory aura, and does not experience blood sympathy. At the end of the period, she becomes Kindred again. For nearly a half an hour, she undergoes the sensations and transformations of the Embrace again. This leaves her functionally incapacitated during this time. She can hold off the transformation for one minute for an additional point of Willpower. During this transformation, her Vitae drops to 0, possibly causing a hunger frenzy. She immediately takes damage from any exposure to the sun as if her Blood Potency was 1 and Humanity was 7, and she vomits all food and drink consumed during her time as a mortal, possibly causing an anger frenzy. At the end of the transformation, she takes aggravated damage equal to her Blood Potency, possibly causing a fear frenzy. This damage leaves no scars. After all is said and done, the vampire regains all spent Willpower, and gains the Inspired condition.

                                        Bleed the Sin
                                        Prerequisite Coil: Buttress the Soul ••
                                        Procedure: With this procedure, the Dragon bleeds a subject of all their blood or Vitae. With human subjects, this generally requires a dialysis machine or other technological assistance to keep the subject alive. This flow of blood carries with it the weight of their sin. Outcome: The subject must remain effectively bloodless for at least ten minutes in order for this Scale to take effect. Reduce the subject’s next Humanity, Integrity, or similar trait purchase by one Experience per dot of the vampire’s Coil of Živa.

                                        Siphon the Soul
                                        Prerequisite Coil: Denying the Hold ••••
                                        This ability acts as a sort of pseudo-Amaranth. The vampire drains a victim completely, then continues to do so further. She spends Willpower and rolls Composure + Resolve (instead of the Strength + Resolve called for normally, see Vampire: The Requiem, p. 101). On a success, add the successes of that roll to the victim's Humanity or Integrity. If this exceeds the vampire's Humanity, she steals one dot of Humanity from her victim. He loses it; she gains one. She may only ever strip one such dot from a given victim. A mortal victim drained this way falls into a coma and takes the vampire's Blood Potency as aggravated damage. A vampire victim only reaches torpor as result of this. Regardless, the attacker suffers Detachment at Humanity 1 (possibly losing the stolen Humanity dot), and suffers the Tainted Condition (with Blood Potency as 1 for mortals). This act can be extended into a full Amaranth.
