                        I feel like whenever these discussion come up we go to a little white room and forget some serious considerations. Let us assume that Blush of Life allows for hair, fingernails, muscle development, and even aging (as in wrinkles and gray hair, not physical development) at the normal rate while the effect is on. It would take hundreds of vitae and willpower points to grow hair, fingernails or "get toned". It would take thousands for your hair to grow long. Aging a decade would be many thousands. Even in the best case scenario, this would take about twice as long. Generally, it would take "forever." All of this would occur in the background. Unless the chronicle has serious time skipping, then these issues don't really come up.

                        If there is lots of time skipping (or long periods of time) then you can figure out based on a character's resources roughly how long it should take to reach the desired effect and assume it happens. The huge amount of character resources expended and the time it took illustrate just how difficult it is for a vampire to change from the point of Embrace. I'm in the group that says it should only take a few vitae and willpower to revert back to the state at embrace no matter what physical changes have occurred, short of final death. Those two together make vampires practically, as opposed to completely, as unchanging as just saying you can't change at all.

                        False nails, wigs, and makeup are the way a vampire is going to go if they want to change their appearance with any regularity. If it is just once every 50 years to keep up with the times then I say let it happen. The movie Dracula from the 90's has him go from young to old and back to young again after becoming a vampire. It isn't the only point of reference for this type of thing occurring either. There is no harm in allowing "permanent" changes to be undone or "impermanent" physical traits to change over long periods of time. It fits with the general idea of a vampire doesn't appear to vary once embraced. It would only be niche cases where that level of stuff is occurring. Wigs and makeup would be the norm.

                        I would keep "Claudia's curse" by not allowing physical maturity without magic. Even Blush of Life doesn't mean a vampire suddenly requires food again. There would be no basis for the body to mature. Aging is a different process. A child that is embraced may appear to grow older over time, but they would appear as an old person that never actually grew up into an adult which happens.

                        This is just a game and we probably think way too hard about some of these things.

                                            Well typically, it seems to be that you are stuck in the state you were in just before your embrace. I can't remember where, but I think I read something that said a vampire can will their vitae to heal pre-existing conditions, but these conditions revert themselves while they sleep the day away. For example, an amputee who becomes a vampire could use their vitae to grow back the limbs they lost for the night, but they would fall off again during the day and they'd have to do it again when they woke up.

                                            The prince of New Orleans in the 1st edition book is described as needing to spend the first 2 hours of every night cleaning himself up because he was embraced in a disheveled state and didn't have time to make himself more presentable.


                                            It also mentions that when left to their privacy, elders prefer to surround themselves with things from their era. Even when in public, the elders are known to dress in clothing that seems decades out of date with the rest of the public, because they don't adapt well to the times. Some of them prefer to use written messages because they don't understand how email works.



                                            The thing is; how can vampires claim to be immortal or have eternal youth if their body is allowed to undergo permanent changes or signs of aging? The answer is that they can't. If your body can age, you don't have eternal youth. If your body can be permanently transfigured, you're not immortal.

                                            If you want vampires who can change their appearance, I'd suggest reading a bit of the Cirque du Freak series (not that god awful movie they made). For every 10 years, the vampires age by 1, unless you're a half vampire (partially embraced) in which case you age 1 year for every 5. One of the oldest vampires in the world is only about 700 to 800 years old, which makes him between the physical ages of 70 to 80.

                                                Continuing to age after the embrace is actually the bloodline weakness for the seriously creepy Oberloch bloodline. They're still as immortal as any of the Kindred, however; "Old Alice," the matriarch of the insular lineage, is rumored to be somewhere on the order of 150 and 200 years old, and looks every day of her age.

                                                As for the static nature of Vampires, second edition has departed a bit from first in that it places more emphasis on vampires being well adapted to their condition. On the one hand, vampires see well in the dark (which is something I always house ruled in Masquerade and Requiem 1e anyway; I'm not sure how creatures that burn in the sun and are afraid of fire are supposed to have survived most of human history without really good night vision), and can hear heartbeats and smell blood from afar, and can even track prey by scent if they're bleeding.

                                                On the other hand, vampires are also well adapted to blend into human society. The lost visage is a much more subtle effect than in 1e; instead of having weird reflections and showing up blurry on film (which is far more of a liability than a boon in terms of hiding out), they just have a preternatural combination of instinct and blind luck that ensures that their image is always somehow concealed. In addition, the strange dreams vampires suffer during torpor have an adaptive function, keeping the mind sort of plastic so that awakening elders are surprisingly quick to adapt to the changed circumstances they wake up to.

                                                I imagine that many elders might still find it comforting to surround themselves with the trappings of their mortal life in private - some of these trappings might even be touchstones - but an elder who entered torpor in 1815 will usually be fairly quick to figure out cellphones and the internet when he wakes up in 2015... or at least, to figure them out well enough that he doesn't immediately stand out as strangely inept and out-of-touch.

                                                Likewise, the Kindred body is receptive to minor cosmetic changes. A vampire embraced in the Danelaw in the 9th century might have been quite the lady's man in his time with his long, bleached hair and beard, but he'd have a much harder time hunting in 19th century England if he couldn't adopt a more "conservative" hairstyle.
