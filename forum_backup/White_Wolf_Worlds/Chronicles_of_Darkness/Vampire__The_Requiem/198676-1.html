                                Yes, makeup would stay on your skin just as it would on a mortal, barring that your skin exudes no greases or sweat as a Vampire so perhaps slightly better even.                                         The Ordo clan book has a lot of info on the in-narrative processes a Vampire must go through to make physical changes "stick"". The Willpower is just a mechanical representation of the fact that it takes a great effort, but really you should have to actually do something in character. Just like you can't necessarily buy a Merit just because you have the xp for it unless you've earned it in character, you can't necessarily make a change permanent just because you have a point of Willpower to spare unless you've worked on it in character. Maybe you use meditation to access your subconscious and hold off your natural instinct to heal. Maybe you tear your own skin down to the bone and painstakingly regrow and tattoo one layer at a time. Maybe you create some kind of chemical compound that halts your regenerative processes and apply it to the area you want to pierce before doing so. As with developing Coils, the exact process will vary from one Kindred to the next, but the results are the same - you exert a great force of personal will towards maintaining a post-embrace change to your body (whatever that looks like for your character), spend a point of Willpower, and that day your Vitae does not reverse that change while you sleep.

                                            It's the same thing as spending willpower to avoid healing too grievous wound during sleep, avoiding the risk to remain with no blood to wake up. You spend willpower to make a wound permanent untill you have the blood to heal it back (otherwise it autonomously heals as you sleep). Basically, vampires physical changes are actually treated as wounds by the vampire body and they "heal back" with the automatic blood point spent to wake up. This could actually mean you can willingly go back on every change you made by spending point to heal them back just as you do with normal wounds.
