        Originally posted by the_1990 View Post

        I think it's more a question that there is just so much more content out for 1E already, if you want to use a lot of content from the 1E sourcebooks it may just be easier to just do the whole thing in 1E rather than spending the time translating all of it to 2E. And again, like I mentioned before, there is the problem of books like Inferno, which just don't work with the new rules.



        In regards to the God Machine and Demon. I get the feeling it was thought up in part to avoid drama. You see, the original Demon: The Fallen was criticized for being too &quot;Christian-centric&quot;, in other words; the lore drew too heavily from Judaeo/Christian theology as-opposed to other faiths/mythologies for some people's liking. Something tells me these complaints would be more vocal (but probably still a minority) if it were published today. This left the publishers with a dilemma: risk having these complaints with the new version or try to expand the background lore to be more inclusive.

        The problem with things like demons/angels/God, as opposed to other supernaturals, is that they are far more relevant to things many people believe in today, often very strongly. There's often a paranoia about offending these beliefs whenever you portray them in fiction. If they expanded the base of real world religions they drew on it would, in their eyes, run the risk of upsetting more people.

        It's only a theory of mine, but I can't help but wonder that the writers went with the weird techno-thriller angle because they were afraid of navigating the minefield that comes with addressing religious themes: &quot;Does this mean God exists in WoD? if so, which God? How will our religious readers react if we get things wrong? Will our non-religious readers be put off by the suggestion some religions might actually be true?&quot;

        I hope this isn't the case and they were just being creative, but I still wonder if that was part of the reason they chose such a different path than what people like me were expecting (and hoping for). In any case people who would complain about the above things would be a very small minority.
        Sorry to negate your theory, but avoiding controversy was never a factor in creating Demon. Not directly using Judeo-Christian symbolism was a creative choice by the developers because of how they wanted to frame the game.

                Yesterday i had my usual weekly Forsaken game in which one of my players constantly complains on Chrod in favor of NWoD and might as well make my weekly annoyance into something useful and he does bring a couple of good points:

                Point #1: Putting mechanic to things that shouldn't have mechanics. For example, interdisciplinary specialty allows you to use your medicine specialty of say, anatomy, when doing targeting attacks.

                In NWoD this doesn't exist, i think, but in a game with reasonable people a player can say to the DM "hey look, i got a specialization to anatomy, can i get a bonus when i am attacking a specific body part"

                Now in Chrod, there is merit that does this and a cost with requirements so now is more difficult to do the same. Because as DM, if i allow this i am cheating by giving the merit for free to a player and the one that did pay for it feels rip off. As DM is one thing having to make a judgement on the fly on things that dont have a mechanic for, and quite another going using the same when the mechanic is actually there.

                I used the example of Interdisciplinary specialty but there quite more of them like mentor, which makes you arbitrarily choose 3 skills (or resource) that the mentor can help your character which is something than in his mind should not have rules and be handle it on a case by case basis.

                Point #2 The door system feels unnatural and non intuitive.Again putting mechanics in something that should be handle with 1 roll and RP.

                Point #3 Aspirations. They are hard to grasp and not for everyone. In my case i am close to just drop the entire concept and just assign 4 beats per session flat because they are a handful to handle on both the DM and Player side.
                • Player side: Some players are passive, they dont know or cant think about what they want to happen or are more gamist and prefer to "win" and thus both prefer the approach of DM throws plot and you deal with it. Other many players also dont read the rules, the just come to play and asking them to think about were the game is going is too much work at best.
                • DM side: Is hard to fill in the space of 4 hours of gaming, assuming 3 players, 6 aspiration + the plot that are already in motion and come to fruition on that particularly game + the ones that also come to fruition but the player dont know about. And as aspiration are tied to XP then you, as DM, feel obligated to include them first because otherwise the player will feel cheated out of XP.

                                            Originally posted by Charlaquin View Post
                                            And it's always easier to ignore mechanics you don't like than to invent mechanics for things you feel need them and don't have them.
                                            That is a great way to put it. Even in non-modular RPGs this is the case. Just make sure everyone in the group knows what's been removed.

                                            Originally posted by LokiRavenSpeak View Post
                                            I am thinking 4 beats per session + all the Beats they get from conditions + all the beats from Converting failures to Dramatic ones.
                                            Aspirations are supposed to grant an average of 2-3 Beats each session so that would be the amount of end-of-session Beats if you want XP gain to be unchanged. (Not accounting for Obsessions or Totem Aspirations from the specific gamelines.) However, my group wants a quicker XP progression so we use both Aspirations (2-3 Beats/session) and end-of-session Beats (most commonly 3 Beats/session, but sometimes 5 if we do a lot during the session or if we didn't get much opportunity to roll dice), meaning we'd grant ~6 end-of-session Beats if we did away with Aspirations.
                                            My point is that you should worry more about how quick you want your players to gain XP.
