              Thanks for having a look!

              - I do have a tendency to write out things a little too explicit, yeah. Which might make the wording a bit complex. I'll look into it. Perhaps it'll help tremendously if I switch to OPP's standard he/she alternation, instead of the neutral singular/plural alternation that I'm currently using with regards to pronouns.
              - I fixed the Humanity roll mistake, which you spotted rightly.
              - With regards to your second (or actually, your first) comment):

              Originally posted by Griautis
              so it's best to just have a lot of sites at 1 as it always counts as One Asset Skill dot?
              No, not necessarily. Each actual Asset Skill dot counts as 1 Asset Skill dot for the purpose of determining a character's Influence. So, if Halina's Primacy character has the Asset Skill (Rutger's Weaponry) 3 and (Rutger's Survival) 2, these two merits would tally up to 5 Asset Skill dots for the purpose of determining a character's Influence, even though they refer to one and the same Subject.

              In contrast: a Site always counts as 1 Asset Skill dot for the purpose of determining a character's Influence. So, if Halina's character has the Sites (DIY-store) 1 and (Meth-lab) 3, these two merits would tally up to 2 Asset Skill dots for the purpose of determining a character's Influence.

              Now, if I understand you correctly, you suspect that Halina would be better off buying three 1-dot (Meth-lab) Sites. Her total score would after all be 4. This is true, per RAW. But take into consideration the following:

              1) not all Sites can be bought at 1 dot,
              2) buying a Site at one dot makes it very easy for rival characters to perform a hostile takeover of the Site or to destroy the Site,
              3) if a Player would only invest in Sites, the Player's Primacy character would have no Subjects to perform Asset Skill actions with during a round. The character could only perform the 4 personal Asset Skill actions. Furthermore, if they have no access to Subjects, they cannot promote Subjects to Henchmen, which in turn means they have no Henchmen to promote to Agents.

              Having said that, Primacy characters with access to Animalism, Dominate and Majesty may rely less on Asset Skills and more on Sites. That does seem like a valid strategy.

                              Dear Howalt,

                              I find your product and/or service intriguing, and wish to subscribe to your newsletter.

                              In all seriousness, while reading through I was picturing the Assets and Sites on index cards, so you could organize who's where doing what.

                              As a broad suggestion, you might consider explicitly the effects of available time on the vampires, in terms of what is left after hunting. The quality of a herd or hunting ground not only gives more vitae, but also does so with less mess, fuss and time taken. So in a given turn the vampire could spend more time issuing orders or other Actions.

                              Each turn the vampire has 1 personal action, translating to 4 A.Skill Actions or 1 Scene. If she stays up during the day, she has three more or 1 Scene. That means to me that A.Skill Actions translate 3/1 into Scenes--you may as well make that an explicit conversion in order to simplify your text, subject to number of Scenes per turn.

                              You could dispense with "personal actions" entirely: make them "Actions", combine with A.Skill Actions (your action is to activate the asset skill). Start each turn with 1 "Action", which may be placed into Herd or Feeding Ground. The quality in dots of Herd or Feeding Ground then gives that many vitae and Actions; Actions may be received in this way only once per turn; if a vampire has both Herd and Feeding Ground then another action may be spent to obtain vitae from the unused Site.

                              Why do these give Actions? Because vampires need time to hunt during each "time slice" that a turn represents (default--one day). The easier the hunt, the more time they have for doing anything else: directing Assets, calling up Scenes, etc. Influence Actions then represents either an easier time ordering things around ("I text one word. Just one. And your haven burns.") or dedicated staff or something like that. Theoretically you could have an Agent act to bring food as well, making a third source of vitae (and thus Actions 1/turn between all sources). Why is all this important? The influence game is only fun until you start going after someone's food supply--then it's deadly serious; if you want to make your opponent uncomfortable you start squeezing how they eat. If you DON'T squeeze how they eat you may as well be sitting in Elysium playing Mario Kart.

                              Elders/city powers are "untouchable" not just because they're physically secure behind a wall of lackeys with valuable skills, but because they have their food supply mapped to the N'th and multiple ways of avoiding the distraction of unfamiliar hunting. (Which is what you have represented by starting with 1 Action/Turn.)

                              Moving on:
                              You might have Sites provide a bonus to certain Asset Skills if used there. For example a Library (Research). Gun shop (Firearms). Police Station (Investigation). And etc. Sites could then provide 1 or 2 to Asset Skill level when occupied by a Subject, and contribute something to Influence the next turn thereby. Then, take the Subjects off at the beginning of next turn. Leave Henchmen or Agents on. So if you have enough loyal followers you can camp sites for greater influence, but if you just have a network of Subject contacts for skills they will be too busy with their lives to give you lasting Influence from Sites.

                              You might also, in this fashion, have some Sites provide a monetary income for maintaining Assets (as you allude to in your text), making the finances less of a narrative handwave and more explicitly attackable. I'd suggest if you include this that Finances (note name not the same as "Resources") per Turn be equal to total of Resource merit + Finance score from Sites and Agents. All Finance not spent in a turn is lost, so it also represents a cap on activity tempo. Maybe include a Bank Site if you want some saving. Anyway: expend Finances along with action that increase Asset Skills, recruit Sites, Henchmen and Agents. Henchmen and Agents could have upkeep costs, but I don't suggest it--just make them expensive to recruit and you do away with bookkeeping.

                              Again, everything here could be represented through 3x5 cards and markers (i.e. glass beads).

                              --Khanwulf

                                  I have added some sample Sites.


                                  @Khanwulf: Thank you for reading. I'm glad you like the project. You could certainly work with index cards and beads. I imagine that would be very helpful.

                                  As for some of your other comments:

                                  - a previous write up gave a Primacy character 1 free hunting roll per round and the choice between 3 personal Asset Skill actions or 1 Action Scene. For the sake of ease and for the sake of freedom of choice I've simplified this. Primacy characters can still use their personal Asset Skill actions to 'hunt and gather', they may also instruct their underlings to 'hunt and gather' on their behalf.

                                  - the reason why the use of a Personal action allows a Primacy character to personally perform 4 Asset Skill actions, is because there is only so much anyone can do in the time available. Every character (Subject, Henchman, Agent or Primacy character) can only perform up to 4 actions during the time they are active (nighttime, for most).

                                  - the reason that the use of a Personal action allows a Primacy character the choice for either 4 Asset Skill actions or 1 Action Scene (performed by themselves or by one of their Agents) is because playing out an Action Scene might cost quite a bit of game time. It's to keep the game moving, the same reason why there is a per week limit to the activation of Action Scenes.

                                  - the reason why staying up during the day can net a Primacy character up to 3 extra Asset Skill actions (or 1 Action Scene) is to reflect that even when pushing their limits, vampires can simply not be as effective during the day as they can be during the night.

                                  - I agree that a big part of the Influence game is the struggle of access to blood supplies. I think this is best handled by the ST restricting the availability of Feeding grounds and Herds and by having characters such as Princes and Sheriffs enforce the Domain's laws and rules that concern poaching and keeping up the Masquerade. There are also Asset Skill actions to allow Sites (such as feeding grounds) to be the subject of destruction and hostile take overs. One could also massacre a rival's herd, if so inclined.

                                  - elders might be as powerful as you describe them to be, if they have sufficient Influence to maintain such a position. And even then, they might have the rug pulled out from under them. I think no character (npc or pc) should be untouchable.

                                  - I am weary of adding yet another new Advantage, like Finance. I think this can be covered through (temporary) dots in Resources.

                                  - I have now added some sample Sites. Some give bonuses to Asset Skill actions, some provide temporary dots in merits such as Resources. Take a look. Tell me what's missing.

                                          Thanks Howalt. I think we're in complete agreement, but let me rephrase:

                                          I see the Personal Action as unnecessary from a mechanics standpoint, in the sense that you can simply translate it into other things and it only exists to be translated.

                                          Example:
                                          1. Start a turn, receive 1 Personal Action (PA).
                                          2. Decide to turn it into up to 4 Asset Skill Actions (ASA), or 1 Action Scene (AS)

                                          That means, mathematically, PA = 4 * ASA and PA = AS. So, AS = 4 * ASA.

                                          You can discard Personal Actions entirely as a mechanic and just use ASA--reskinned as "Actions (A)" as the currency for Turn activity.

                                          That gives you 4 * A = AS.

                                          If you want to downplay the time that Hunting and Gathering costs, then just give each player 4 A at the beginning of the Turn.

                                          If you want to make Herd, Hunting Grounds and Agents more valuable, give players 1 A each Turn and make a Hunting and Gathering action grant additional A once/Turn. That simulates just how much time Hunting and Gathering takes, and thus how much time is left for other actions.

                                          And yes, excellent Policies for each Elysium turn would be "Condemn Poaching" and "On the Sanctity of Blood Dolls" or "Enforced Truce on Hunting Grounds"--all of which would logically impose penalties for jacking with another character's feeding sources--and thus their Action economy.

                                          Elders, being high Influence, would be hard targets but vulnerable to players working together to make their lives hell. Concentrated effort (and destruction of assets and sites) would wear them away simply because a group would have more Actions to spend on attacking the Elder. A neonate (low influence) vs. and Elder (high influence) should end predictably, so long as the Elder pays attention.

                                          Your wariness of adding another, Finance, mechanic is well-founded. Twas a possibility, only.

                                          Keep in mind I'm approaching all this from a boardgame perspective, essentially.

                                              I get confused when symbolic abstractions enter the equation, algebra and modal logic was never my thing But unless I completely misunderstand what you are getting at, I think there has been a misunderstanding. Which is probably my fault, as I should have been more clear in this, perhaps.

                                              To clarify: 1 Action Scene is the equivalent in narrative time as 1 Asset Skill action (both take up a scene or a couple of hours). So, in fact a character playing out 1 Action Scene should have enough time left to perform up to 3 Asset Skill actions. In fact, Agents have this option. However, Primacy characters (the Player characters) do not. They choose: either 4 personal Asset Skill actions or 1 Action Scene (performed by the Primacy character or one of his/her Agents) during their night-time activity. They may of course attempt to stay active during the day, earning them either 1 personal Action Scene or up to 3 personal Asset Skill actions.

                                              The reason for this (apart from pacing) is because I want to force Primacy players to not do all the dirty work themselves. If they do choose to take up an Action Scene personally, they'll have to be at such a point (ideally) that they grind their teeth and say some cliche line like "What is it they say again, if you want something done right..."

                                              The way you describe an Elder/Neonate struggle is how I hope the system will work, indeed Ditto for the possible application of Policies. And yes, an ST should also apply circumstantial modifiers to feeding (or covering up feeding) based on the kinds of general laws and rules a Prince enforces on the Domain (apart from the Policy of the week).
