            In the 2Ed Werewolf it is mentioned, that even not all Werewolfs believe the Story of Father/Mother Wolf and Father/Mother Moon. It is mentioned as the most common, but there are still Werewolfs who believe a variant or totally reject the Story as myths or metaphor.

            The thing with the truth is, that there is no thing like that. There are just viewpoints. When I am leading VtM (Yes, the Game with the huge Metaplot) I constantly let my players figure out “the truth”. After that I let them play with the knowledge of it. They make decisions, act in the way they think is the right one and fight against people who are enemies of the truth. After that, I pull away the curtain and reveal a more “true” truth and they have to realize, everything they had done with the best intentions was wrong, stupid or even meaningless. And so on and on and on... They love it, and I am too!

                The second edition brings in the possibility that becoming a vampire is a simply possible part of the human condition, just as a human can become a Mage. Maybe several "Daeva-like" beings arose over the centuries, dancing their way into the condition with an extreme lust for the (shallow?) aspects of life powerful enough to overcome death. With enough interchanging of blood any line of descent that is "Daeva-like" simply becomes "Daeva". In other words the five major clans are common themes, even an independent lineage typically converges around one over time.

                Doesn't have to be this way of course, but I prefer it. It makes the vampire "tree of life" a tangled mess, five big branches resulting from many smaller ones weaving together, branching off and folding back in again.

                        I have a few theories regarding some of the big "mysteries" of Vampire, though whether they're "true" or not is probably mostly immaterial in any modern Chronicle I'd run.

                        *Remus was never a vampire, and probably never existed. Aulus Julius made him up in order to give his brood of Roman vampires an origin myth that legitimized their rule over the kindred of the city. Also, he probably wasn't as old as he claimed to be. However, it is true that the founder of the Julii (Aulus, in this case) was not embraced in the traditional manner, but rather became a vampire when a Strix merged with his body. The unusual nature of his embrace accounts for the small differences that separated the Julii from the Ventrue.


                        *The "Unnamed Clan" of Rome were the Latin Ventrue, who had been in the region for some time, although they probably weren't brought there by Aeneas. They might have come there following the ancestors of the Etruscans, who are believed to have come from Anatolia. There are a number of possible reasons that they might have aligned themselves with the Strix, but in any case they were exterminated in the area and did not return until after the fall of the Camarilla.


                        *Longinus never existed. The vampire who would become known to history as the Monachus was a devout early Christian who received the embrace, and subsequently dreamed up the dogma of the Sanctified in order to justify his unnatural existence. He was probably at least a little bit mad. He penned most of the Testament himself, though other early Sanctified added to it to create its "modern" form, and he cobbled the plot of the text together from bits of existing Christian folklore and his own diseased imagination.


                        *Theban Sorcery doesn't actually have any particular link to the Judeo-Christian cosmology; it's an ancient form of blood magic developed by the Great Covenant of Egyptian vampires, much of which had been lost in the upheavals following the Roman conquest of Egypt. The "angel" Amoniel may have been a member of the Bak-Ra using a devotion to project his consciousness and image out of his body, in order to impress the Monachus and his disciples. His reason for introducing them to Theban sorcery is unknown, but it's likely that he intended to in some way bend the young covenant to his own will. He may have succeeded.


                        *Dracula was one of the kindred, but he was the result of an unintentional embrace by a Turkish vampire and not a unique creature cursed by God. His philosophy was significantly influenced by the beliefs of the Mystikoi bloodline of the Gangrel, who adhered to a strange, Gnostic version of the Sanctified doctrine. He likely encountered members of the bloodline while in the Ottoman empire, early in his unlife.

                        ---

                        Most of these are ideas implied, to one degree or another, in the text of various supplements; there's nothing terribly groundbreaking about them. I am rather pleased to have come up with the idea of a Bak-Ra elder trying to hijack the nascent Lancea Sanctum, and that his plots might continue to have consequences in modern nights. I also think it might be interesting for the Monachus to still be out there, somewhere. What he knows could shake the Lancea Sanctum to its core, but his mind is so broken by millennia of lies and self-loathing that he's no longer capable of telling it.

                                                OK!

                                                Maybe it would be clearer with examples, it's all still early stages:

                                                In the game I'm preparing, I want to offer players many options, but for each option, I want them to be embedded into a cohesive tapestry rather than a kitchen sink. Of course, the difference between the two will be my capacity to achieve the desired mélange. My challenge is that we are all social science postgraduates who have delved into questions of myth, history, literature, politics, etc. at some point in our lives. We are also geeks, and MST3K mantra applies, but part of the fun is in the suspension of disbelief.

                                                Vampires (as the primary playable option)
                                                So, it will be a Vampire the Requiem game first and foremost, set in a fictional US East Coast city (and since I'm from Québec, Canada, the US East Coast is a fun little "exotic" setting to explore), not unlike the Sunnydale, Mystic Falls, Neptune, East End, or Bon Temps of TV Series. (My players are huge fans of these shows, heck I have a player who's doctoral project involves Buffy the Vampire Slayer.) It might very well resemble the fictional city of Newcastle, from Damnation City, or Portland ME, Providence RI, and similar-sized cities.

                                                Werewolves (as the secondary playable option)
                                                Secondarily, but still present, many elements of Werewolf the Forsaken will be integrated. I wand to avoid them being "there" without their spirituality being sufficiently explored, so I need to understand how their history and mythos intersects with Vampires in different historical periods and how that might shape contemporary politics. There will be more than one tribes, and at least one is going to be sympathetic towards the party (for interested reason), and at least one other tribe is going to be outright hostile, though smart about it.

                                                Covenants
                                                To that end, the local Circle of the Crone group is going to be somewhat close to one of the tribes, I'm thinking this would have to be covert. I know my players love Rituals, Blood Magic and the idea of magic having a cost, drawbacks and being somewhat unreliable sometimes.

                                                I want to Ordo Dracul to be an important covenant, researching ancient secrets and all that. Of course, the other groups might not like all that snooping around, despite the well-mannered members of the order acting so innocuous.

                                                I intend the Invictus to act as the overarching "Camarilla-style" masquerade enforcer, a greedy powermongering capitalist social club, but an undeniable necessary evil.

                                                The Carthians will be at odds with the Invictus, but also their extreme-left Anarch rivals. I'm thinking Anarchs in requiem are not very complicated to integrate.

                                                The Lancea et Sanctum will be aligned with the Religious Conservatives, Evangelical churches and such. Of course, in its own wicked way.

                                                Clans
                                                The Mekhet clan, and its Egyptian background for that matter, needs to be clarified in relation to Mummy the Cursed. I will not integrate playable mummy characters, but I want the mythos to be cross-compatible. Same goes for the origin of "Theban" sorcery, and mentions of goddesses like Isis. I will not do any unadvised name-dropping of Egyptian deities, cities or words without having done my homeworks properly.

                                                Gangrels will have relations with the local werewolves more than other clans, as well as many members part of the Circle of the Crone.

                                                Daevas, Ventrue and Nosferatu are going to be mostly where one would expect (meaning: I still have some work to do to think of how to integrate them more, though I don't see them as that complicated).

                                                Antagonists will likely include the Strix, possessing demons, VII, groups of hunters (including slashers), vampire politics, quite a few werewolves. (And, depending on events, even Judges and Mummies.) That's quite a bit of stuff to tie together, and I want the storyline to have the players guessing who their real enemies are. I'm thinking the role of werewolves as guardians against evil spirits helps a lot to make them want to work with vampires against the greater evil.

                                                I know I don't intend to use changelings, sin-eaters, geists, wraiths, ghosts, or prometheans much. I might if their metaphysics can integrate seamlessly with the rest. I'm thinking prometheans would be good candidates, if not for their masquerade breaching looks... Changelings, I'd like to integrate, if it doesn't necessitate too many last-minute adjustments and doesn't cause a dirty kitchen sink mess. I just know that for each element I add, my players will have innumerable questions and will expect answers. Of course, not only do I have to find my own hidden answers (or Schrödinger's Gun my way through), but I have to craft all the lies in a narratively interesting way for the plot to be multi-faceted enough. Which is why I'm asking all those questions.

                                                        Originally posted by BarbeChenue View Post
                                                        Werewolves (as the secondary playable option)
                                                        Secondarily, but still present, many elements of Werewolf the Forsaken will be integrated. I wand to avoid them being "there" without their spirituality being sufficiently explored, so I need to understand how their history and mythos intersects with Vampires in different historical periods and how that might shape contemporary politics. There will be more than one tribes, and at least one is going to be sympathetic towards the party (for interested reason), and at least one other tribe is going to be outright hostile, though smart about it.
                                                        Lets start with this.

                                                        First, while vampires and werewolves might bump into each other a lot, they don't have a set perception of one another. Most vampires haven't had the pleasure, and those that do have likely had a wide variety of experiences with werewolves. Unless there was a war of some sort, this likely wouldn't color the perception of an entire court of vampires. If you want there to be a history between the vampires and werewolves in your town, you're going to have to find a reason for there to be one, like the aforementioned war.

                                                        Second, vampire and werewolf mythos is likely to be vastly different. Some werewolves might believe vampires are undead werewolves. Some might believe they're claimed by spirits of hunger and blood. Others might think they're just another kind of supernatural. Werewolves have an entire different level of reality to work off of, that of the Hisil. Werewolves are spiritual hunters that are descendants of Father Wolf and Mother Luna, and patrol the boundaries of the Hisil and the real world. Unless a pack or protectorate or other group has a personal grudge against vampires, they're likely to leave vampires alone until they start earning a hunt. There's currently a Lodge based out of Mongolia that likes to hunt vampires, and the Black Moon eXtreme, though they are pretty bad at it.

                                                        Third, Werewolf the Forsaken tribes aren't like they are in Apocalypse. The core unit of werewolf is the pack, which has members of many tribes and auspices. Outside of a tribe's sacred prey, which they tend to view as a more favorable opponent than others, and the following of the tribal ban, there's not much connecting individual members of the tribes, like there were in Apocalypse. If you wanted to fit into the current World of Darkness, you would want to cut that down to a pack or protectorate that might be sympathetic, and another pack or protectorate that might be hostile. Protectorates are an alliance of packs.

                                                        Fourth, werewolves are divided into four groups. The standard Forsaken, members of the five tribes, they exist to exalt in the sacred hunt and become great hunters, also following the Oath of the Moon and protecting the two worlds from the depredations of their denizens. The Pure, three tribes who hate the Forsaken and have a low view of humanity in general. They exist to tear down the barrier between the worlds and rule, sort of vaguely like the Sabbat. The Ghost Wolves, who are not a member of any tribes and either follow the Oath of the Moon, stick to their own, create cults, or try to form new tribes. And the Bale Hounds, who are serve the Maeljin, demigods of vice and evil.

                                                        Again, there is no metaplot, but knowing a bit more about the individual game lines will help you mix them into your setting without things looking out of place.


                                                        Gangrels and Circle of the Crone
                                                        If you want these two to be in touch with one another, you're going to have to come up with a reason for it. Werewolves can let vampires into their packs, so why are they letting these guys in? And why is this Circle of the Crone involved with werewolves?

                                                        You might want to keep in mind the political advantage werewolf allies will bring to the table. They are predators that can work in the day and scent out a vampire's haven in moments. If some vampires have them as allies, they're going to have an advantage compared to those that don't.

                                                        Also keep in mind that the clans are not usually like they were in Masquerade. Members of a clan not by default all working together, and may not even be related.
