                    Originally posted by Marcus View Post
                    Yes and a valid command, citated also in the book in aninalism 1, is: Attack.
                    Yup. Rats have individual stats, as do birds. You're not sending a bunch of normal rats after somebody hoping to do significant harm to them unless they're especially vulnerable — you send a bunch of small animals after somebody to make things difficult for them and wear them down, which is generally better accomplished with small groups or singular attackers if your goal is real damage.

                    Furthermore the specification of the possibility to splash the blood directly on a target, what other meaning could have if not to let him beeing attacked without a direct command, in the attempt of consume blood by the animals?
                    "What other meaning could this have?" is kind of a silly thing to ask when you have the entire breadth of possible commands to apply to the animals that arrive and you can command them to attack a person without getting the blood on that target — "keep other people away from this person" is valid, as is "follow this person, then come back to me," as are more involved effects like giving an animal an object painted to attract a different kind of animal and sending them to leave the object in a particular place to prompt a particular order on a delay.

                    The primary use of Summon the Hunt is to expand the ways in which you can use Feral Whispers. Inflicting the Distracted Condition on people caught amidst a bunch of small animals is an option, and in fact that is the main consistent effect of the Swarm Environmental Tilt as represented in the mechanics of gamelines whose 2e books do not reproduce the entire Tilt as a specific printed game mechanic, but nothing about Vampire's mechanics for swarms necessitates messing around with diameter and condensing.
