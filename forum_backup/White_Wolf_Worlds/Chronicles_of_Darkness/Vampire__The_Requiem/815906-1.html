    Originally posted by MCN View Post
    Actually, a player that wants an secret-seeking shadow vampire will pick the Mekhet because of the bonus to mental stats, dream-uncover-secrets merit, and the disciplines. Its kind of their entire archetype. Auspex, Celerity, and Obfuscate all lend to that archetype as well, not just Auspex.
    Which is part of what I am trying to avoid. The entire 'kit' is strong in this regard, and players are likely to follow just that, which isn't really that much different from Masquerade or the original Requiem.

    Originally posted by MCN View Post
    And if someone wants to use Dominate to help them do all this shadowy secret uncovering... then they just start learning Dominate? This isn't Masquerade where clan disciplines were restricted to those clans. Covenant secrets are the restricted powers. Nothing stops a Mehket from learning Dominate. Or Protean. A Gangrel can easily learn Obfucate, and nothing stops a Daeva from learning Animalism.
    But that still means clan powers take precedence over the character's traits. The fact of the matter is that it's interesting to have some of the power reflect what the character is, not that 'okay, you're now a Daeva, so you have a supernatural affinity to being stronger, faster and more charming, despite the fact that you've been a geeky librarian all your life who doesn't really care too much about social interaction, exercising or whatever. You're still a Daeva, but none of the powers suit you well. They can be useful, but they won't suit the character. Plus, the very thing about 'the clan chooses you' in a way is just helping make stereotypes stronger.

    Originally posted by MCN View Post
    Actually, Daeva are the fighter types by mindset in the game. They lend themselves to the rebellous Carthian mindset, and they get access to Enfeebling Aura, which turns Majesty into a combat power. The Daeva are the signuture knights of the Invictus (Spina). So, from a lore standpoint, its a perfect fit.
    Are they? They are the signature knights of the Invictus because of their disciplines. It makes them fit the archetype more easily. That is another thing I am trying to avoid.

    Originally posted by MCN View Post
    They already get Resilience, Dominate can be powerful in combat, Animalism is good for retainer-swarms in a fight, and Vigor isn't hard to pick up as a cross-clan ability. The Ventrue can also go Ordo and pick up Coil of the Wyrm for more ability in combat.
    And that is exactly the situation I'm trying to avoid. 'A Ventrue can fight like this', 'a Daeva will fight like that', 'a Gangrel will do this and that'. That is only the case because of the powers available to them. And it is the very thing I'm trying to keep away from.

    It is not a matter of 'so I pick this clan because it usually plays like this'. It is more along the lines of 'I like the mood, I like the tone, I like the role, but if I love the Gangrel fluff and I want to have a character that can be better at mental powers. Going by the rules as they are, a Gangrel wouldn't be good at that from the get go. It wouldn't reflect the character's personality or background. But the character would be stuck with Reslience and Animalism despite not being interested in those in the slightest. Or, worse yet, the player would be more inclined to choose a DIFFERENT CLAN just because of the powers alone. Of course players can get out of clan disciplines, join a bloodline or something similar, but that still means that the character's personality has very little impact on their 'curse'.

    As for bloodlines and covenant books, we are staying away from those from the time being. Most of the group is new to Requiem, but half the players are familiar with the Masquerade, and they don't want clans dictation too much.

    Thanks for the opinions, loving it so far. It always adds a new perspective which I hadn't considered. Gotta love it! :-)

                                        OP - A mechanical suggestion for what you maybe want (avoiding the why you might want it).
                                        1. Players Choose a Clan. They take the Bane of that Clan, and choose one of the Clan's three disciplines to cost 3 XP per dot (i.e. - In-Clan).
                                        2. Players choose two other disciplines of the basic 10, they then cost 3 XP per dot as above.
                                        Alternative (weird) system:
                                        • All disciplines costs 3 xp per dot. Any character that takes a signature discipline from a Clan (Auspex, Dominate, Majesty, Nightmare, Protean) also gets that Clan's bane. They can take multiple signature disciplines at the cost of another bane. If you want to force scarcity, count this against the "three optional banes" limit per character.

                                                        Clans don't inform who you are, but how you act. They're methods of being a vampire, and each one has more than one methodology. The one thing I advise is to not dismiss how Clan can affect a person's worldview entirely. It's not a matter of a "culture" per say, but how do you view people when you just don't really feel attached to even the most important people in your life (Ventrue curse), you can look people in the eyes and tell them what to do (Dominate), animals yield to you (Animalism) and people that try to hurt you just find that you shrug it off (Resilience)

                                                        Your disciplines and your clan bane give strong behavioral cues for characters. How they react to it will vary from person to person, but the one commonality between members of different clans is that they will experience those same stimuli. Maybe even react to it the same way. Those Kindred uniquely understand the perks and stressors of your existence, which is one reason why you might come together as it were.
