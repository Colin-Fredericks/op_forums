                                            I think that Ventrue Life's comment was that you could mechanically reuse the Lancea, not that the Abrahamic origins made it appropriate to dump the three major religions and various minor sects with millions of theological man-hours behind them into one convenient bucket.

                                            And to reinforce the point, Lancea is the Covenant that helps define a vamp's relationship with a higher moral authority. (Circle cults help define her relationship with pseudo-peer if perhaps more powerful divinity figures/self.) A Jewish-centric Covenant or mystery cult will have a similar role, or it's not really more than a social club. There are other Covenants that serve as clubs better, and wield mystical power stemming from that role.

                                            To me, one of the key systemic factors in the exercise of Judaic religion is the expression of separation through purity. The avoidance of certain things or practice of them served to draw a line between the Jewish people and their neighbors, with the purpose of ensuring they remain distinct, hold intact the story of Yaweh's interactions with them as a group, and enable the Messiah to clearly trace lineage back to Abraham of the promise. The Levites were further separated and purified as a group and individually so they could serve as intermediaries in ways that would literally kill less-pure people.

                                            I'm certain I'm oversimplifying, and don't wish to argue the broad points, but this framework could help assign a suitable role for Jewish vamps: not as tempters of the flock, but as vicious watchdogs of its purity in ways even the priestly caste could not. They might draw significant inspiration from the story of Phinehas (Numbers 25, do not know Torah reference), who violently purified a key transgressor of the non-fraternization strictures, saving the people as a whole from a judgement plague that had killed 24,000 already. Interestingly, the promise to Phinehas went along the lines of (again, Bible not Torah excerpt) "Now tell him that I [Yaweh] am making my special covenant of peace with him. In this covenant, I give him and his descendants a permanent right to the priesthood, for in his zeal for me, his G-d, he purified the people of Israel, making them right with me." (Note that in the remainder of the passage the instructions on what to do to the peoples who tempted Israel to worship Baal in this incident, through intermarriages was... thorough.)

                                            Now historically speaking we can assume a "permanent right" ends with death, but CofD being what it is, perhaps permanent is permanent? Or, at least argued as such by those for whose interest it serves.

                                            --Khanwulf

                                                            Vampire the Requiem is a game, not a theology lecture. So of course it's going to be dumbed down. If you and your players want to turn your games into a theology lesson, then by all means, it certainly isn't my intention to tell you how to run/play your games. But the average player isn't going to care about any of the nuance you just brought up. Especially because the Lancea et Sanctum isn't any of these religions.

                                                            I think that's the most important thing to understand. The Sanctum isn't Christian. The Sanctum also isn't Jewish, nor Islamic. The Sanctum is its own religion, it's a new religion created specifically for the Damned, because quite frankly Christianity, Judaism and Islam doesn't apply to them (those religion apply to mortals, not to the undead Damned, who's state of being is arguably inherently incompatible with Christianity, Judaism and Islam). The Sanctum's creed appeals to ex-Christian vampires, ex-Jewish vampires and ex-Muslim vampires. That at least was the intention of the writers. The Lancea et Sanctum was never supposed to be exclusively ex-Christian. And if you want your version of the Sanctum in your game to fit a specific type of ex-Jews, you can easily reskin the Sanctum to fit your needs. The core of the Sanctum is being God's monsters to test the faith of the mortal population. That doesn't necessarily mean a Sanctified has to scare mortals into being proper Christians/Jews/Muslims, quite the contrary. It simply means they test how strong a mortal's faith in God is, and whether they'll stay on the straight and narrow even when tempted by evil. If you're familiar with the story of Job you'd understand that fear (of God) has absolutely nothing to do with this.

                                                            But Khanwulf was right, even if you disagree with what I just wrote, the point still stands that from at least a game-mechanics perspective, the Lancea et Sanctum is very compatible with Judaism. Just rewrite the lore but keep (most of) the Sanctum merits and Theban Sorcery. That should easily be doable, at the very least it's an easier and more logical fit than trying to reskin Cruac and the Crone merits to fit your Jewish ideology. That's just my opinion though. If you think Cruac is easier to rework into your homebrew Jewish covenant, then by all means, go for it. But you asked for ideas and advice, so here I am, advising to use the Lancea et Sanctum stuff as a basis for your Jewish covenant.
