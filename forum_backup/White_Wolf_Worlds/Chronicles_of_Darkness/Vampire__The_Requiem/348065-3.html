    Aura Perception is actually better at detecting lies if the social scene takes a while, since you can use it all the time. There are only so many questions you can ask with Uncanny Perception without draining yourself of Vitae.

You have to take the dicepool penalty for "subtle shifts in the mixtures of color and pattern", but Aura Perception can show relatively detailed information, including who is the target of the read person's emotion. It wouldn't be much of a stretch to ask "who here does this person want to hurt most".

                    Those are only sample questions. You are allowed to narrow down the possible answers to only people present, but that does not mean you -must- do so. Two very different things. Examples are only examples, we should look to the actual power description for its limitations.

                    "The vampire focuses on a single victim, peeling back the layers of lies and misdirection to reveal the truth underneath. The Beast sniffs out the victim’s dark secrets, things that she doesn’t want anyone else to know. What the vampire does with that information is up to him."
                    "This level of Auspex focuses on questions concerning the secrets and weaknesses of a single character."

                    That's the only real guidance given. Who you hate most in the world can definitely be constituted both as a secret and an exploitable weakness; it checks out.

                    Nobody is saying that Auspex 1 lets you know things about people not present, that's true. Not even sure why you brought that up. They're different powers in their entirety. Notice how Auspex 1 specifically points out that only immediate stuff can be perceived, while Auspex 2 does not. Your assertion that Uncanny Perception is only about things that are immediately relevant seems a bit shaky when they found the space to specifically mention that in one power, but not this one. Also notice how Beast's Hackles says that "the sample questions below cover much of the information the Beast can provide.", while Uncanny Perception doesn't (again). Why? Becauce Uncanny Perception is much less limited, and the examples there are a far smaller grasp of what is possible than is the case for Beast's Hackles, and its examples are thus not representative of the full scope of the ability. Trying to determine the scope of Auspex 2 based upon Auspex 1 is quite meaningless.

                    On the topic of the descriptions: "[Auspex shows the user] visions that range from the direct to the hallucinatory." I would consider all the visions in the example to be of the direct variety. They are immediately overlaid onto reality; however, when somebody isn't present I could easily see an image representing them in a metaphorical way appearing in the Mekhet's mind's eye. It is no surprise that they represented it so one-sidedly, since direct visions tend to require less context to get it, while more metaphorical ones would possibly confuse players outside of a context they had no room to create.

                    It is of no surprise that the current Auspex is seen as not much improved or even inferior to the last one when people choose to add limitations to the text that were never written there based only upon a non-inclusive list of examples.

                                    Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post
                                    This sounds like a very poor mechanic in world where Vampires have to contend with Hunters, Werewolves, and who knows what else trying to kill them on sight! Like Jack said "Self preservation is a vital part of humanity!"
                                    You don't lose Humanity for defending yourself. You lose it when you experience something that reminds you that you're not human. Humanity breaking points are also still scaled like 1e (though a very different scale), so dropping below Humanity 7 means surviving what would put a human in the hospital stops bothering you (unlike Integrity where breaking points don't go away with lower Integrity).

                                    Humans don't suffer moral degredation from surviving near death experiences...
                                    2e has moved away from these traits representing moral degradation.

                                    Integrity is about how well you keep your sense of self in the face of emotional/mental traumas. You can most certainly end up losing Integrity from surviving a near death experience; especially if from a supernatural source.

                                    Humanity is about how well your vampire maintains their ability to connect to being human. That's why things that remind you of your inhumanity are breaking points. Lasting 100 years as a vampire, or gaining BP 6 are both breaking points (and worse ones than getting beat to shit and walking away). There's no inherent moral difference between Humanity 10 and Humanity 1. Though low Humanity vampires would appear less moral than high Humanity vampires, because their morality would not communicate as one that humans would understand.

                                                        Originally posted by Nyrufa View Post

                                                        This sounds like a very poor mechanic in world where Vampires have to contend with Hunters, Werewolves, and who knows what else trying to kill them on sight! Like Jack said "Self preservation is a vital part of humanity!"
                                                        It's a trying challenge to maintain a high level of Humanity, that mental and emotional link to the living being you once were, if you're living the natural unlife of a vampire. It is, however, pretty manageable to level out comfortably at a medium or lower level of Humanity while doing all the things that are thematically common and expected of vampire PCs who might have to get into shootouts or deal with rabid werewolves.

                                                        Humanity isn't meant as a punishment mechanic. There's some intended tension of give-and-take, but losing Humanity is not supposed to be something that results if you make the wrong choice or do things you shouldn't. You're supposed to end up in stressful, taxing situations, and just as you might limp out of them with physical wounds, you might stagger out of them with spiritual or emotional wounds. Unlike your physical health, though, your psyche can callous, becoming inured with experience towards certain levels of stress; that's settling at a lower Humanity rating. But that's moving away from the human baseline. Humans don't weather catastrophic damage calmly and unbothered. You let yourself become a little more of a monster in order to endure and thrive.
