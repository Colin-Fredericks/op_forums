                                    I like to think that the term "clan" is used by a group that is large enough in a given area to constitute a major extended familial relation, with similar blood powers and afflictions. While "bloodline" is the polite term for a minority distinct in powers and afflictions from the major "clans". Depending on the scholarly bent of the Kindred with whom you converse, they might admit that all family lines are technically "bloodlines"--or not. Should you wander far enough, to where a minority group is numerous enough to form a local majority, they'll probably call themselves a Clan. And if (for example) you stand before the Keeper of Alamut, it would be wise to call the Akhud a clan--and not just a Bloodline--in case you get another night out of it....

                                            Correction: A Bloodline always has four "in-clan" Disciplines. In the vast majority of cases at least two of these are carried over from the parent Clan.

                                            I can also add that everyone belongs to a Clan regardless of their Bloodline-status (which affects bloodties and can be determined by tasting their Vitae), and that Bloodlines doesn't necessarily carry over from Sire to Childe (which makes Bloodlines and bloodlines two different things). It would seriously kill any Bloodline's claim for Clan-hood if it turned out those from the Bloodline can induct members from one of the Clans after the embrace, or could embrace Childer without inducting them to the Bloodline.

                                            I guess that in more isolated domains it might be possible that a Bloodline styles itself as a Clan, but there'd had to be unique circumstances for that Bloodline to actually be believed, such as little to no communication with other domains (which would make at least four of the five big Covenants impossible in that particular domain).
