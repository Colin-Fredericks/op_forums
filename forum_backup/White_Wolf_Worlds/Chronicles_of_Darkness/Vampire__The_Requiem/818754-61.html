        Hard to say.

        It offers you a +1 to Physical rolls when Frenzying. Being able to spend 2 Vitae a turn allows you to use an ability and heal, or spend more to heal. Vampires are sort of glass cannons in that they don't heal very well, but can do a lot of damage. It really enhances senses a lot. But most things you'll only be seeing a +1 bonus to your die pools with. Things like Lashing Out. Which can be nice but it's only one die. Though if someone wants to fight your Lashing Out, they have to spend a point of Willpower if you're of higher Blood Potency. It also gives you some better resistances.

        That said, I found while playing that the Power Traits are basically the most expensive thing you can get, not counting 5-dot Merits, Rituals or Artifacts which can get ridiculously high. And in my game whenever I ended up trying to save for Primal Urge, I usually got swayed by something more useful immediately. That's Werewolf, though, and vampires don't have that much in comparison. So starting off at 2 can give you a little leg up in that regard, and it certainly fits if you want a character who is 'more' vampire than your standard feeble neonate.

            Originally posted by Gellydog View Post
            How useful is an extra point of Blood Potency for a new character?
            The difference between Blood Potency 1 and Blood Potency isn't huge, but it isn't insignificant. The big thing is it allows you to spend an extra point of Vitae per turn, which significantly expands your options in combat or other turn-based situations. You can store an extra point of Vitae, which is a nice but small bonus. You get an extra +1 to physical dice pools in Frenzy which is a mixed blessing unless you take advantage of it, like with Riding the Wave or Coil of the Wyrm. It gives you +1 Supernatural Tolerance, which is actually great to have. It gives you +1 to Lash Out dice pools, which is cool. And it gets you one step closer to Blood Potency 3, which is one step closer to Blood Potency 4, etc. and at higher levels of Blood Potency it becomes one of the most useful things you can spend experiences on.

            Ultimately though, it depends on the character. A combat wombat who rides the wave at the drop of a hat, Lashes Out at everything that moves, and is constantly spending Vitae on physical intensity and healing will get a ton of mileage out of every dot of Blood Potency. A more reserved, mental or Social focused character who pulls all of the strings and has minions to do the dirty work for them will still find an extra dot of Blood Potency useful, but perhaps a bit less so.

                                        It's technically just flavor description. The average person is very reliant on sight to navigate and it is disorienting to have the scenery visually changing every time you touch it. I wouldn't automatically require someone to roll just to get out, they would have to describe how they're getting out/dealing with the shifting imagery. They'll need to do something that shows some thought as to how they'll overcome the illusion. (Using a stick to tap along the floor/ground or wall ahead of you at a slowed movement rate to find the exit or door knob works.) And of course if they lash out, the illusion goes away for good.

                                        D&D has the concept of disbelieving illusions for saving throws (which you normally don't get for illusions) but technically the dissolution warp effect would constitute auto success. If you want to go that route, you could give them a perception check with a -5 penalty and add an Occult or Intelligence bonus to the perception check if they know it's an illusion or application of Obfuscate.
