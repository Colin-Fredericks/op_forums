    Alright, let's dig into some Chapter Three stuff.

    I don't think I've talked about this before, but the "chapter 3 toolkit" concept has probably changed the least from my very early conception of this project (i.e., the whole series). I even just dipped into my very jury-rigged outline for Strange Shades to see if that was true,* and sure enough, the basic idea is more or less intact. While the basic structure has expanded slightly, it's always involved Devotions, Merits, banes, and a little discussion of the Clan Curse. Wild Hunt breaks this format a bit by including a long section on Protean.

    Protean was always a power I was a bit indifferent to in Masquerade and Requiem 1E, mostly because it was very limited for a shapeshifting power. I was always a more a fan of Vicissitude and its more (perhaps too) freeform system (the influence of which you'll see below). So, it shouldn't be surprising that Protean in 2E went from being the Discipline I was least interested in to being right up there with my favourites—for the record, Auspex, Dominate, and Obfuscate.

    Anyway, I thought it was important to talk about Protean from a player and Storyteller perspective. Not that we couldn't have talked about the other clan powers, but Protean is so wonderfully weird. It's the only physical Discipline with discrete powers, and it's the only one that you can actually see. It also makes the Gangrel the odd ones out among the five clans, in that their unique power isn't socially or mentally influential (Auspex is kind of an edge case, but you see what I mean). More than anything, I wanted to get into the Gangrel as shapeshifters: how that affects their psychology compared to the rest of the Damned, and how it makes them absolutely vicious hunters. All Disciplines are tools of the hunt, of course, but Protean is the most overtly so.

    Another thing I wanted to cover in this chapter was the Red Surrender, from Savage and Macabre, which went through a LOT of reworking over the course of writing the book. Originally, we conceived it as a stepping stone to gaining a Risen Beast, but we quickly realized that didn't really make sense. The Red Surrender is about integrating with your Beast's instincts, whereas a Passenger is about providing the Beast with a bit more of the Man. Eventually, we decided they needed to be separate, though it then took us a while to figure out how to even present the Red Surrender as a separate thing. Our first idea was to leave it as a thing Gangrel can do, but I didn't love the idea of having two "signature systems" in one book, and, upon having another readthrough of the original, I felt it was ultimately a pretty simple concept, one that didn't need much coverage. The author's first draft included a lot more bells and whistles, but I edited a lot of that down, to the point that I realized it felt like I was creating something more like a Merit...so I turned it into one! However, I did make sure that, if you prefer to have it still be an inherent quality of the Savages, you can really easily do that. Not everyone's going to want to use the Risen Beast, and if you prefer something a little more brutal, the Red Surrender's got your back. I'm a fan of simple, blunt systems that do a lot with a little, and I think this one has those qualities in spades.

    Which is all a rambling way of introducing a bunch of excerpts from the systems chapter. Tomorrow, I think it's time we get into the Horrors, starting with the Blood Mold...

    Chapter Three: Tools of the Hunt

    Devotions

    Awaken the Horrid Form
    (Animalism •, Protean ••)

    Some Gangrel learn to command the flesh of others like they do animals, pulling horrors from their victims’ skins.

    A vampire must be touching her subject to use this Devotion.

    This Devotion costs 2 Experiences to learn.

    Cost: 1 Vitae per adaptation (1 Willpower, optional)

    Requirement: The victim must consume or be anointed by 1 Vitae (included in the cost).

    Dice Pool: Dexterity + Crafts + Protean vs. Stamina + Blood Potency (if unwilling)

    Action: Instant

    Duration: Night

    Roll Results

    Success:     The Gangrel applies adaptations from Predatory Aspect to a human, ghoul, or another vampire, up to a limit of her Protean dots divided by two (rounding up). This does not have to be one of her own forms, but inflicting a form she doesn’t know requires her to spend an additional Vitae. This effect stacks with any forms the subject can already access through Protean, but the vampire can’t double up the same effects.

    If the subject is a ghoul, the vampire can spend a Willpower to make his transformation permanent, but she cannot use this Devotion on him again unless to remove or swap out adaptations.

    If the vampire knows Uncanny Aspect, she can apply a single form from that Discipline for two Vitae.

    Exceptional Success: The Gangrel grants a part of her essence to the subject, who counts as being twice-removed in terms of blood sympathy with her for the duration.

    Failure: O, that this too too solid flesh would melt!

    Dramatic Failure: If he’s a vampire, the subject’s Beast recoils at this alteration, and he enters frenzy with the Gangrel as his target. If a human or ghoul, he suffers cardiac arrest or another catastrophic medical event.

    Consume
    (Auspex ••••, Protean ••••)

    This horrific Devotion allows a vampire to absorb her prey, slowly feeding on him until he withers away to nothing. The vampire’s body merges with her victim’s, mystically containing his mass without any outward signs.

    This Devotion costs 5 Experiences to learn.

    Cost: 5 Vitae

    Requirement: The vampire must drain a mortal victim of blood to the point of death.

    Dice Pool: None

    Action: Instant

    Duration: One week

    The vampire draws the victim into her body, completely absorbing him, and knowledge flows between the two. She gains all stored Willpower the victim had and, for the duration, can use any of his Mental and Social Skills if they are higher than her own. If the vampire wishes to consume a new victim before the duration expires, she must spend a Vitae to messily regurgitate the previous victim’s bones and partially digested viscera beforehand.

    Forced March
    (Celerity ••, Resilience ••)
    A product of Clan Gangrel’s frequent need to cross large expanses of the wild before the sun catches up, this Devotion allows Kindred to move at top speed over long periods. Forced March is most often employed by Carthian Savages, especially couriers, preventing the Revolution’s enemies from zeroing in on important communiqués.

    This Devotion costs 2 Experiences to learn, or 1 if the vampire is a member of the Carthian Movement.

    Cost: 3 Vitae

    Dice Pool: None

    Action: Instant

    Duration: Hours equal to Resilience

    The vampire can move at the maximum Speed allowed by Celerity for the duration. Except for reflexive actions such as Perception, she can do nothing but move in this time.
    If the vampire stops for any reason, she must reactivate this Devotion and repay all costs. However, any potential assailants must match her Speed or else they have little chance of catching up to her.

    Merits

    Aspect of the Predator (••)

    Prerequisite:     Gangrel

    Effect: Sometimes, the trick to getting rid of the competition is just looking like you’re the bigger monster. When lashing out, your character’s Blood Potency is always treated as one dot higher than another vampire’s. This means she never has to spend a Willpower to respond to another Beast, and other characters must always spend one to respond to her.

    Drawback: Failing to back up your bark with sufficient bite can be disastrous. If your character loses a Predatory Aura contest with a vampire of higher Blood Potency, she must take the Bestial Triad Condition even if she would normally be immune to it, and its duration is doubled.

    Atrocious, Advanced (••)

    Prerequisite:     Atrocious

    Effect: Your character’s Beast is a thing of legend, a feral demon that resides within her veins. Once per chapter, gain a Beat whenever she invokes the Monstrous Beast in someone who has attacked her within the same scene.

    Drawback: When lashing out with the Competitive or Wanton Beast, any failure becomes a dramatic failure.

    Flesh Focus (• or •••)

    Effect:     Your character is more in tune with the shape-changing aspects of Protean than other Savages. At one dot, she can memorize and assume a fourth adaptation with Predatory Aspect. At three dots, she can assume a specific second trait with Unnatural Aspect by spending an extra Vitae.

    The Red Surrender (•••)

    Prerequisites:     Gangrel, Beast is not Risen

    Effect: Gangrel are closer to the Beast than other Kindred — too close, some say, but well out of hearing of any nearby Savages. Still, some believe it’s not close enough.
    With the Red Surrender, your character can blur the lines between the Beast and her own personality. Unlike the Risen Beast (p. 79), this is a communion rather than a division, and as such, those who’ve chosen the path of the Passenger can never use this technique.

    While operating under the Red Surrender, your character enjoys a variety of benefits from the Beast’s power. Once per chapter, she can spend a Willpower to take the Tempted Condition. As long as this Condition lasts, she gains the following benefits:
    • +1 on Athletics, Intimidation, Stealth, and Survival rolls; this applies to Disciplines and Devotions;
    • +1 to Initiative;
    • +1 to Perception;
    • If she spends Willpower on dice pools involving Animal Ken, she gains a +5 instead of a +3.

    Each time the Tempted Condition compounds, the trait bonuses above increase by one, to a limit of +5. Once per scene, your character can spend another Willpower to compound Tempted a step, or she can provoke the Beast and hope she wins.

    Drawback: Letting the Beast so close to the surface means the Man falls into the depths. Any breaking points suffered during this period suffer the Tempted Condition’s penalty as a modifier.

    In the Flesh: A Players Guide to Protean

    Our only security is our ability to change.

    —John Lilly

    Razor sharp claws. Limbs warped to run faster, climb higher, and dig deeper. Grasping tails, spreading wings, gaping maws and things less easily described. Any or all of these images might come to mind when discussing Clan Gangrel. With the Beast made manifest in their very flesh, is it any wonder the Savages have such an intimate connection to it?
    Possessing the Discipline of Protean alters a vampire’s Requiem in fundamental ways that no other power of the Blood does. This section examines how it affects not only the physicality of the Damned, but the very core of their identities.

    Skin Like Quicksilver

    Even among the unique powers granted to each clan, Protean is special. Every other clan Discipline has some parallel that can be connected to human abilities, or even thought of as normal skills magnified.

    Auspex? Who doesn’t make leaps of intuition now and again?

    Dominate? There are entire workshops and books dedicated to making people do what you say.

    Majesty? Charm is cheap! So many people are effortlessly enticing, with smiles that open doors and legs alike.

    Nightmare? There are those who can silence dissent with a raised eyebrow, who project menace in every move they make or thought they think.

    All these powers, unnatural though they may be, can be thought of as simply “human… but more.” And, indeed, they often are by Kindred clinging to the past, but there’s nothing human about exploding into a cloud of mist, or melding into a block of granite, or shedding your body like an old coat in favor of one with wings, claws, and scales. Protean, more than any other Discipline, announces its user’s inhumanity to both herself and the world. To possess it is to hold constant, undeniable proof that one is no longer mortal, and the impact of that fact can’t be overstated. Daeva and Ventrue submerse themselves in the herd of humanity, pretending to still be one of them. Mekhet and Nosferatu lurk unseen among the masses, desperate to still be a part of them. The Gangrel who soars above the crowd on wings she didn’t possess yesterday knows, unequivocally, that she’s something fundamentally different, and that there’s no going back.

    Bit by Bloody Bit

    The powers granted by Protean are so diverse that it’s worth going through the Discipline’s various abilities to better examine how it alters the experience of its users. Below you’ll find a dot-by-dot discussion of each level.

    Unmarked Grave

    Protean doesn’t ease anyone into its use. Right off the bat, young Gangrel find their whole bodies merging with the earth while their minds remain active and aware, a feeling indescribable to anyone who hasn’t experienced it themself. This is often enough to cause dysphoria and other mental trauma as the neonate is forced to grapple with their radical alteration of space.

    However, Protean’s earliest gift is also one of its most potent. To possess even the barest understanding of the Discipline is to be untethered from domain or haven in a way that Kindred without it simply can’t match. Savages new to their Requiems might not appreciate how powerful this gift is at first, but all come to be deeply thankful for it in time. The knowledge that safety from the sun (or, indeed, most threats) is just a patch of dirt away helps instill the confidence the clan is known for.

    Predatory Aspect

    Perhaps the most iconic Gangrel ability is being able to take on a variety of animal-inspired features at a moment’s notice, and it’s available relatively early in their Requiems. It’s hard to overstate the sheer versatility of Predatory Aspect; it turns a Gangrel’s body into a Swiss army knife, from extra limbs to multiple senses. Still, it acutely throws a Savage’s monstrous nature to the fore. Sure, that Haunt over there might be sort of creepy, but her Gangrel friend just grew ten thousand insect legs and is crawling around on the ceiling — which one is less human again?

    The obvious nature of these alterations also means that a vampire must be conscious of their surroundings and any potential witnesses when calling upon their gifts. Many Kindred abilities can be dismissed as tricks of the light or passing mania, but watching someone radically alter their physical form right in front of you is hard to dismiss. This Masquerade-induced need to hold back helps explain the isolationist nature of many Gangrel, and why they often prefer more secluded environments where they don’t need to worry about prying eyes.

    Beast’s Skin

    A less strange but much more versatile transformation than Unmarked Grave, a vampire with Beast’s Skin is only limited by what animals she can sink her fangs into. While there are many options for both stealth (rats, house cats, snakes) and combat (wolves, bears, lions) granted by this ability, perhaps none are more Requiem-altering than taking on a form that can fly. Personal flight is a dream long held by humanity, and many Gangrel report feeling something akin to true joy the first time they launch themselves into the night on their new wings. Be it in the form of a bat, owl, eagle, crow, or something stranger, the ability to enjoy a whole new dimension of movement is a mind-expanding experience. If nothing else, Savages who can fly note how infrequently Kindred or people actually look up.

    Not only does this facilitate travel across vast swaths of terrain unseen, but the potential for hunting is significant. If Unmarked Grave is the first taste of freedom, then Beast’s Skin is the full course meal. What does domain mean to Kindred who can travel between cities through the air? What meaning do a prince’s laws have when you can leave when things get too complicated?

    The idea of a totally untethered Requiem is seductive, and this is yet another reason so many nomads are Gangrel. However, a vampire without ties is also one without Touchstones, or other tools to keep the Beast at bay, a problem Savages already struggle with. A wise Gangrel keeps a few roosts to land in, lest they become little more than a gory urban legend on a lonely stretch of highway.

    Unnatural Aspect

    If the tools granted by Predatory Aspect are the most iconic, then the weapons given by the penultimate Protean ability are the most notorious. Easily the greatest contributing factor to the wariness other clans regard the Gangrel with is their propensity for sprouting awful claws that can rend even the resilient flesh of the Kindred asunder. Often, just the sight of these horrid talons is enough to stop fights before they begin, and it’s at this point that those with Protean understand how much power they really possess.

    While some become swaggering brutes, many Gangrel who reach this level actually become more reserved and willing to resolve conflicts without violence (especially those who bear the Unholy’s infamous claws). They know how much destruction they’re capable of — particularly if they fall to frenzy — and they feel a sense of responsibility to not use their power recklessly. It helps that, at this point, most Savages feel they have very little left to prove.

    Primeval Miasma

    Protean’s final gift is a potent one. As a miasma, a Gangrel has the safety of Unmarked Grave coupled with the ability to move almost anywhere, as little can bar her entry. What’s more, one doesn’t even have to resume a solid form to feed, and some Savages are known to spend nights on end in this shape, simply luxuriating in the unfettered feeling.
    The only weakness of this shape is its lack of speed; any healthy prey can easily outpace the miasma, and moving anywhere in the form takes significant time. Still, this Discipline can impart a true sense of invincibility upon those who achieve it, and that feeling is hard to argue against, especially when you happen to get caught up inside it.

    This is the ultimate expression of the survivalist nature of Clan Gangrel, and it leads many of them to become foolhardy. After all, what’s to fear when you can dissolve into a nigh-invulnerable cloud at a moment’s notice? It’s at this point that older Savages, the ones who’ve existed for centuries and have seen all the terrible, dark corners the of world, will offer a smile without humor and mutter, “You’ll see.”

                    Alright, let's look at some Horrors!

                    The horror section of Chapter 4 probably had the most changes from conception to final execution, and the Blood Mold in particular went through a lot of reworking. Initially, this concept was a disease that affected vampires, turning them into cannibalistic, body-horror mutants driven to feed on their Kindred. Not a bad idea, but if you've read our books before, you're probably thinking of the Noctuku Strain right now. Cannibalistic, body-horror mutant Kindred are a fine concept, but we'd rather emphatically done it before. One thing I strive not to do is repeat ourselves, and this was a case where there just wasn't enough difference.

                    So, the big shift here was changing the mold's vector from vampires to humans: Humans who rise from the dead "spontaneously" and develop a hunger for blood that grants them greater and greater power until they explode into spores to begin the cycle all over again. That worked a lot better as both a difference from the Noctuku, and as a more unexpected element to throw into stories. Maybe the coterie thinks they've got a poacher on their territory, only to discover it's some mortal is feeding on their herd...only to then discover he's not mortal in any way that counts.

                    Another change was in the basic ways these antagonists work mechanically. Initially, there was a whole suite of dread powers for them; again, not a bad idea, but another thing I strive to do is to not reinvent the wheel. Protean already has plenty of adaptations that cover a similar design space, and it also felt more thematic to imply that the mold was sort of... infected with Protean. We leave the backstory somewhat open-ended on that front, but there are a few hooks you could play around with if you wanted a closer connection to the Gangrel in particular.

                    So, here's the Blood Mold! Tomorrow, I'll return with gargoyles...

                    Blood Mold: The Creeping Terror

                    "It's not as bad as it looks!"

                    AKA: Ophiocordyceps lucifuga

                    Fungus is something of a paradox: simple but complex, neither plant nor animal, a harbinger of entropy yet a branch of life all its own. Fungi are adept at returning dead, inert matter into the food chain, and what’s a vampire but a walking, talking, thirsting pile of compost?

                    It was only a matter of time before a fungal network got a taste of undeath and evolved. In the end, nature devours us all. The Kindred exist comfortably at the top of the food chain, but beneath their feet lies a hungry and ever-expanding predator that craves the brittle flesh and sanguine ichor of the dead. It is a patient and subtle killer, and if you’ve noticed it, no antifungal will save you.

                    Background

                    Back in the 70s, a Savage Carthian called Marguerite Samson founded the Second Earth Revival Fellowship, a commune and cult of personality she used to protect her territory in the local state park. Then, in the late 90s, corporate logging interests associated with the Invictus rolled into her turf. Marguerite couldn’t have that, and her particular style of dispute resolution left six human loggers and a First Estate ghoul dead. She planted the bodies in an unmarked grave deep in the forest, hoping that nature would take care of the rest.

                    In a way, it did. The topsoil around the bodies was laced with fungus from the parasitic Ophiocordyceps family. Steeped in the rich, profane nutrition of Vitae-infused human flesh, the fungus drank deeply. Slowly, it evolved to feed a new hunger, and to do that, it borrowed the bodies it had been provided. Imagine Marguerite’s surprise when the Invictus flunky and his goons wandered back into her commune three days after she killed them all. Visualize her shock as they began to grow in size and appendages whilst they drained her coterie. Picture her horror as they ate her alive. From there, the creatures headed to the city, slaughtering four more Kindred before the collective might of the domain’s elders destroyed them.

                    Yet fungus is pernicious. It doesn’t constitute a discrete being, but rather an assemblage of many. One of the creatures managed to enter the end stage of its bizarre life cycle, bursting into a miasma or spores as its host was destroyed. Hitching a ride on nothing more than the wind, it’s been spreading ever since.

                    Waking Up Dead

                    Occult medical experts call this new species lucifuga (“day-fearing”). It can grow on just about any surface, taking on the appearance of a fuzzy carpet the color of deep red wine. This appearance is the source of its other, more common name: Blood Mold. It enjoys the dark, and those without the knowledge to differentiate would say that it resembles a bad case of black mold. Its brittle mycelia allow it to particulate airborne spores, which, ideally, migrate into any nearby lungs.

                    In living tissue, the fungus is no more dangerous than any other spore or mold. Mortal blood lacks the essential nutrition of Vitae, so O. lucifuga instead uses human bodies as a transmission vector for its rotting infection. Living victims develop a wet, wracking cough and flu-like symptoms, as well as a sensitivity to light, but while those with weakened immune systems or asthma may develop pneumonia, most recover with the aid of drugs. Infected Kindred, on the other hand, find their blood supplies grow thin, though this never poses much danger beyond inconvenience, and the infection will burn itself out by consuming too much of its host’s precious Vitae.

                    The real fun begins when the fungus finds a corpse. Without an immune system or Blood Potency to fight off the invasion, within a few hours, the fungus will replace any damaged tissue with its own, resparking the corpse’s nervous system and raising the body from the dead.

                    In a few nights or weeks, the Blood Mold pushes its host to feed. Each time he does, the parasite colonizes the corpse’s body more and more, mutating it into a monstrous entity best suited to prey on as many victims as possible. At first, patches of mold spread across his flesh, starting at the mouth and lower orifices, followed soon thereafter by sprouts of woody, fibrous growths with a shriveled texture like morel mushrooms. When the victim speaks, his breath carries the stale sweetness of rot. By the time these growths spill from his mouth, nose, eyes, and skin, the colonization is complete. From there, he becomes a grotesque and massive beast with only two drives: feeding and breeding.

                    Systems

                    O. lucifuga seeks out animal and human corpses to propagate its existence. Like any fungus, it lives in dark, dank places, but without a host, it cannot reach its full potential.

                    Blood Mold will infect any cadaver that’s been dead no longer than a day or two, but once a corpse hits the “active” stage of decay, it’s no longer viable. Living humans infected with O. lucifuga experience mild cold symptoms, while vampires gain an unsightly, sweet-smelling skinrash and suffer a reduction in Vitae capacity, losing an additional point per night. Kindred clear the infection whenever their Vitae pools next fall to zero, utterly starving the fungus in a matter of moments.

                    If a carrier vampire feeds on a mortal victim to the point of death, the victim will always rise as a host.

                    Host Traits

                    O. lucifuga hosts share the following qualities:

                    Alien Urgings: When faced with reasonable opportunities to feed, the host must resist the urge with Resolve + Composure. Unless he gives in of his own violation, failure means he enters a frenzy-like state, which the fungus might still attempt to induce if he doesn’t drink his victim dry. The host’s mutation stage (below) acts as a penalty to this roll.

                    Blood Sense: Hosts gain the Bloodhound Merit, ignoring the Wits prerequisite. Furthermore, while they don’t have Predatory Auras, hosts can recognize Kindred from the sweet scent of their Vitae.

                    Sanguine Hunger: Hosts can feed as Kindred do. However, instead of a Kiss/Assault effect, the fungus inflicts victims with a paralytic poison, rendering them immobile for each turn of feeding (treat this as the Stunned Tilt if necessary: Chronicles of Darkness, p. 286). Humans have no resistance to this chemical, but vampires can resist the effect per turn with Stamina + Blood Potency, taking the host’s mutation stage as a penalty.

                    Vampire Traits: A host has Blood Potency 0, which cannot be increased. Being ambulant corpses, hosts also downgrade damage like Kindred, though wounds still heal over time. However, natural healing always adds more fungal matter, making the infection’s presence more obvious as time goes by. They also do not pass out from full bashing or lethal damage in their Health tracks.

                    Unkillable: If a host “dies,” the fungus will resurrect him within a day, healing all wounds. The only way to kill a host is to burn his corpse to ash or to expose it to sunlight at later stages of mutation. Resurrection involves replacing more of the creature’s body with fungus, and this automatically advances the host’s mutation stage.

                    Mutation Stages

                    Blood Mold hosts go through five stages of mutation. At each stage, the fungus replaces more and more of the victim’s body with itself. These stages progress based on how often the host feeds: Whenever the host consumes blood, roll his unmodified Resolve + Stamina. On a failure, he mutates into the next stage. If the blood came from a vampire, apply the total Vitae taken as a penalty on this roll.

                    The effect of each stage is cumulative.

                    Stage 1

                    The sudden influx of repaired tissue leaves the subject feeling energized despite his recent demise. He looks like he’s put on some muscle and lost some fat, partly due to his desiccated flesh and partly due to the masses starting to build up under his skin. Upon rising, the host gains a dot of Strength, which can take this Attribute past the human limit.

                    Stage 2

                    Fungal tissue swells in the victim’s muscles, granting him another dot in Strength but riddling his skin with rashes and distending his form as fruiting bodies begin to press against his flesh. This stage also grants limited shapeshifting abilities: Choose two adaptations from Predatory Aspect. The host can spend a Vitae to manifest these forms for a scene at any time with an instant action. Furthermore, sunlight deals him a bashing damage per minute of exposure, which cannot heal until he seeks shelter or he spends Vitae to repair the damage manually.

                            Hello again! Let's talk about gargoyles (have you talked to your doctor about gargoyles?)

                            Gargoyles are an odd feature of both iterations of Vampire. They're not something that comes to mind in more generic vampire fiction(?), and I feel like the trope of living gargoyles has much more to do with the cartoon, though I also have a vague sense that White Wolf's gargoyles predate it, so who knows.

                            Anyhow, gargoyles in Requiem have a spotty history. They're mentioned in the original core rulebook (along with hell hounds and homunculi), but they're more or less an afterthought: they don't have special powers, and it doesn't explain how you create them. They don't appear again in any significant fashion until The Danse Macabre, where they're given a complete overhaul.

                            Obviously, our take on gargoyles is based on TDM's presentation, though we expanded their concept and simplified their systems. Now, they effectively work more like non-human vampires, at least at base. Our big change was making them viable as player characters, dividing them into two distinct types: Automata and Rampant. Automata are effectively barely sentient robots, whereas Rampant manage to (in one way or another) attain a semblance of Humanity.

                            Why the change? As with anything I do, for the drama. Requiem's 1E gargoyles are neat, but one thing I think the Masquerade version has on them is consequences, the danger that they might turn on you. That said, I wasn't hugely comfortable with the baked-in slave/master dynamic that's core to the VTM version, so how you treat your awakened gargoyles is dependent solely on what kind of vampire you are. There are still definitely ethical questions you can delve into about creating mindless automatons to do your bidding, but that's down to you and the Storyteller.

                            Gargoyles: Children of the Stones

                            AKA: The Graven

                            Vampires are creatures of flesh, but the Blood doesn’t always limit itself to the organic. Vitae spares little from its corruption, blighting even stone and steel, and with just little effort and hubris, the Curse can take hold in more solid things than corpses.

                            Since time immemorial, vampires have birthed beings both dead and never alive, hardier than ghouls and far more loyal than childer. Gargoyles make ideal servants for Kindred with no need for a personal touch: The Children of the Stones are devoid of drive or desire, and they’ll follow their creators’ orders unto Final Death.

                            Or maybe not. Some of the so-called Graven can gain a touch of the Humanity their unholy births denied them, stealing into the night to make their own way in the All Night Society, whether for freedom, revenge, or a better offer.

                            Background

                            The gargoyle looming over church eaves is an indelible image. These sneering grotesques carried a power all their own, and before they became mere waterspouts, they were wards against the Devil, a practical application of holy terror to protect the house of God. While the righteous slept, the gargoyles stood vigilant, and for their trouble, they were treated with a superstitious respect. The Children of the Stones have similar origins, and undoubtedly this is why they share a name with these figures. Ancient Kindred were just as prone to ritual and magical thinking as the mortals they once were, offering blood sacrifices to idols, lucky charms, and other fetishes shaped in the rough form of a man. What they thought when these objects first started coming to life is unknown, but it didn’t take long for them to learn how to use them.

                            Graven in the Roman Camarilla were carved of polished marble in the form of griffins, gorgons, and other mythological beasts to patrol the halls of the Necropolis. In Viking Scandinavia, they were crafted with wood as wicker imps who would sabotage their masters’ enemies in the daytime. And in China, Kindred nobles would imitate the emperor, commissioning terracotta armies to protect themselves for their journeys through death.

                            Tonight, gargoyles have shed much of their mythological and spiritual baggage. More akin to multitools of the Damned, most Graven live up to stereotypes as guardians glaring down from castle keeps, or as menials designed to keep havens in efficient order. Some designs — especially among elders with more blood than sense — range into the esoteric. A vampire might create a flock of single-use Graven, each a key to successive doors through an arcane vault. Or maybe the gargoyle has no practical application at all, instead serving as a semi-sentient art installation, nothing more than an unliving symbol of its master’s power.

                            Automata and Rampant

                            A gargoyle is a blunt, bespoke instrument; nothing more. Most can communicate with their masters, though spending time with a talkative one isn’t unlike typing at an above average chatbot. For this reason, Kindred are even given to calling them Automata, a term that gained currency as the Industrial Revolution helped humanity develop its own advanced technology.

                            However, it has no agency beyond its master’s wishes, and for some Kindred, that in itself is worth the heavy blood investment. Yes, a ghoul or a childe can understand the spirit of a command, but most gargoyles will never deviate from the letter.

                            The key word here, however, is “most.” In rare cases, a gargoyle can earn a scrap of the Humanity it never had, achieving an intellect beyond impulse and devotion. Kindred call these creatures Rampant, Graven who escape the confines of their design perimeters to become something almost like a real vampire. Some Rampant abandon their creators to make their own Requiems, while others come to new terms with their progenitors, offering services on more equal ground. Still others watch and wait, keeping up a façade of docility for the chance to take revenge on years of mindless toil. How this transformation takes place is unknown (or at least inconsistent), but the possibility is often enough to put vampires off using Graven servants altogether.

                            In the Covenants

                            As with everything else, the five great covenants have differing viewpoints on the creation and use of gargoyles.

                            The Carthian Movement: Many Firebrands ban the creation of gargoyles. Whether Automata are sapient or not is beside the point — building creatures with no purpose beyond service is no better than any other feudalistic practice in the All Night Society. However, Rampant gargoyles do sometimes find their way to the Movement, though their inability to maintain a Masquerade makes remaining difficult. Even among the most liberal Firebrands, a gargoyle can’t contribute to the cause in the way a normal vampire can, so many are limited to grunt and wet work.

                            The Circle of the Crone: The Acolytes view gargoyle creation as holy. Spawning life (even a facsimile) from inanimate things is surely sacred to the Dark Mother, and so the Crone rewards her faithful servants with faithful servants of their own. Gargoyles are regarded with a certain reverence among the Mother’s Army, though that’s no guarantee they’ll last long: Sacrificing a gargoyle for a Crúac ritual is rumored to infuse the magic with incredible potency…

                            The Invictus: There’s nothing this covenant likes more than new toys, especially ones that can protect their assets. The Invictus uses gargoyles as technology: weapons, security systems, amenities, and any other functional roles its elites can think of. Most First Estate Automata are outsourced, crafted by Dragon or Acolyte builders for considerable sums or favors, but some lower-ranking Invictus members will create their own Graven, even though many elders look down on nouveau riche who would use gargoyles as a quick fix to make up lacking for a proper powerbase.

                            The Lancea et Sanctum: Many a Sanctified church has a gargoyle installed in the eaves, watching and waiting for anyone foolish enough to violate holy ground. The Church’s theology views gargoyles as less than animals, inherently sinful things they can use as they please to effect the Dark Father’s will. This belief extends to the Rampant, though the Sanctified don’t really recognize a distinction. Coincidentally, Sanctified gargoyles are the most likely to turn on their masters if they achieve sapience, seeking revenge for years of thankless tasks.

                            The Ordo Dracul: No other covenant produces as many gargoyles as the Ordo Dracul; even the Mother’s Army seem like dabblers in comparison to the Order’s assembly lines, and in some cities, it’s unheard of for Defiant not to maintain at least one Graven servant around their chantries. Gargoyles and their never-living state provide all sorts of opportunities for research and development, an endless source of fruitful experimentation. Defiant are also the most accommodating to Rampant, as long as they’ll submit to some testing now and then.

                            The Birthing Ritual

                            To create a gargoyle, a vampire must conduct a special ritual similar to blood sorcery. This rite has different trappings depending on the vampire’s clan and covenant, but whatever the surface details, the core mechanism is the same across the All Night Society.

                            Before conducting the ritual, the vampire must fashion an appropriate chassis for her creation. This need not have moving parts; it just needs to take the shape of the intended gargoyle, as well as the primary material that will take up its form. Once the ritual is complete, the Blood will provide all the necessary logistics of mobility. If other vampires contribute to the gargoyle’s creation, the creature will follow their commands as well, but its creator’s orders override all others.

                            Successfully creating a gargoyle is a Humanity 3 breaking point.

                            Cost: Vitae equal to five plus the gargoyle’s intended Blood Potency. A new gargoyle’s Blood Potency cannot exceed more than half its creator’s, rounding up.
                            Dice Pool: Resolve + Occult + Blood Potency

                            Action: Extended (target is ten plus the gargoyle’s desired Blood Potency, and each roll represents an hour of time)

                            Roll Results

                            Success:                             The ritual accumulates successes. If the target number is met, a new gargoyle is birthed.

                            Exceptional Success: The vampire makes great strides in completing the birthing ritual. Her player can choose one of the usual effects of an exceptional success on an extended action, or she can take either the Ecstatic Condition or the Raptured Condition.

                            Failure: The vampire accumulates no successes. Her player can choose to abandon the birthing or take the Stumbled Condition.

                            Dramatic Failure: The birthing ritual fails, and the vampire takes the Tempted Condition.

                                            So, Hunting Grounds.

                                            Hunting Grounds: The Hungry Earth

                                            <a low rumble followed by muffled screams and the sound of branches breaking>

                                            AKA: Badlands

                                            The Savages share many legends around the fire. While some are just ghost stories to see who can spook their Kindred the most, these yarns often illustrate useful lessons, especially to fledglings. If a vampire sheds her essence too often, some say, her haven will take on her curse. The candlelight dims in her presence, the spiders weave words in the webs, and the floors creak almost like a moaning voice. Or so the tales go. Mostly, this is superstition, coupled with an animal inclination not to shit where you eat.

                                            Still, Vitae is nothing if not adaptable. Sometimes, the taint of death pools in low places, and the pain refuses to fade away. Sometimes, the land goes bad. Stains like this exist throughout the world, and left to fester through neglect or ignorance, this seeded corruption can sprout. Hunting Grounds are the end result of this awful bloom, and while not all are the product of Kindred carelessness, these places all share the Beast’s mindset without any of the Man’s self-control.

                                            Background

                                            Hunting Grounds are vampires. They feed on blood and manipulate the living to get their fill of it, hiding in plain sight as the masses walk on by. They don’t cling to covenants or Traditions or Humanity or discrete bodies, but maybe that just makes them a purer strain of death than the Kindred. Strix sometimes roost in Badlands for this reason, providing the creatures with fresh victims in exchange for shelter during the daylight hours.

                                            Hunting Grounds form when too much blood spills in one place. Usually, this is due to a vampire lairing in the same area for many years, letting her prey’s viscera dribble into the earth. Draugr often create Badlands because of their habit of leaving kills to rot in their nests, but any vampire who doesn’t bother to clean up after a meal can spawn one of these creatures. At other times, this spark of hunger emerges from human influence, like a killer storing hundreds of victims in a single grave, or a battle flooding the soil with gore.

                                            However it takes shape, bloodlust leaks from a Hunting Ground like radiation. It works its way through the air, the earth, the groundwater, and into all the creatures that live within its reach. The influence spreads first through wild beasts like birds and rodents, then through domesticated animals, then, finally, into humans. And as this influence grows, so too does the Hunting Ground’s thirst.

                                            Most Kindred have never heard of Hunting Grounds, but Gangrel have encountered them many times over many centuries. Whether this is due to the sort of places the clan chooses for havens or because they give rise to Badlands more often than other vampires is a topic for debate, but it was undeniably the Savages who named the phenomenon, and it’s the Savages who are most often left to deal with it.

                                            One of Us

                                            The primeval cunning of a Badland isn’t intelligence, per se, but it knows a predator needs a pack, especially one without mobility. While Hunting Grounds have an inherent mastery over the low beasts who burrow and nest within it, most recognize humans are apex predators in their own right, and they can tap into that strength for their own greedy ends. Like Kindred, a Hunting Ground can grab mortals by the lizard brain through the addictive power of Vitae, and once a person belongs to it, she becomes its “willing” agent in the world. She becomes its Hound.

                                            Despite the name, Hounds need not be feral brutes on leashes. Humans have always been hunters, and a good hunter adapts to its environment and prey. A Hound can just as easily kill with a knife in her victim’s back as she can with an “accidental” misfire during deer season, or a box of poisoned muffins at a PTA meeting. In some Badlands, Hounds are hulking, hairless monstrosities born from humans merging with forest animals; others are filled with beautiful people whose lovely eyes belie their soulless, mindless interiors; and yet others are populated with “normal” people who carry the sickly sweet scent of tumorous interiors like walking, talking rotten fruit.

                                            Where the Heart Is

                                            At the center of each Hunting Ground is its Heart, the spiritual seat of its hungry urges. Like the Hunting Ground itself, the Heart can be anything: a forest pit lined with thousands of bones, a bloody altar in an old church, or even the fountain inside a shopping mall. The Heart is the only place where the Hunting Ground’s thirst can be quenched, but, like any vampire, it’s also the part that’s most vulnerable, and even while stalking prey, the Hounds mustn’t stray too far away from it. It’s the seat of their community and the ritual focus of their lives. Should an interloper manage to damage the Heart, either by destruction or exorcism, the Hunting Ground dies, and any gifts bestowed upon its servants end with it.

                                            Three Badlands

                                            Local legend says an epidemic closed the sanitarium, but in truth, it was the thing growing beneath the floors, supping on the blood and trauma of its residents. However, when the place was shut down, the creature lost its food supply, and so it fell to dormancy. Eventually, an enterprising Lick acquired it as a bolt hole, a place she could bury her prey without fear of scrutiny. Unfortunately for her, the Invictus was watching, and when they came to put her down, the bloody fight awakened the structure. Now the vampire and the sanitarium work together, using Kindred victims to grow their mutual territory, exponentially.

                                            The Tri-County Shopping Mall is dying. Years ago, it was a magnificent monument to 80s consumerism, a home away from home for weekend shoppers, teenagers, and elderly walkers, but today, it’s succumbing to wounds dealt by online shopping and other modern conveniences. When a bankrupt shop owner hurled himself to his death from a third-floor railing, the land beneath the mall awakened. Though small in number, its Hounds are composed of a healthy mix of youthful bodies and families who’ve begun to lure others into their burgeoning cult. In a way, the mall hungers for community. It longs to be full of life again, and if it can’t have that, the blood will do just as well.

                                            Everyone in town knows the wishing well in the middle of the park. Once upon a time, it was the town’s original water supply, but over the years, a lack of maintenance left it as little more than a relic and an eyesore. That is, until a little boy drowned trying to skim coins from it in the middle of the night. The body was never recovered, and the well woke up. Now, rumors are spreading that the old wishing well can really make your dreams come true if you make the right offering. The well hungers for sacrifice, and the larger the trade, the greater the reward. As long as the sacrifice holds value to the person offering it, the Hunting Ground will accept it, and as the sacrifices build up, so too does its ability to grant wishes to its faithful.

                                                        The Narya (aka, the Colossi) are non-Kindred vampires, kind of Wicked Dead-style. They have a lot of similar traits to the more typical vampires, especially (suspiciously) the Gangrel, but their archetype is more about hubris than predation. No longer able to make more of their kind, they walk the night making trouble for the Kindred by imposing all sorts of pointless, baroque schemes on them to pass the time better, and, maybe, in the hopes of one day finding a way to breed again. They're also far more vulnerable to the sun than Kindred, instantaneously petrifying into statues with even a glint of sunlight.

                                                        Obviously, they're something of an homage to another vampire-adjacent property, which shall remain nameless. Ironically, the thing they're a reference to is like the one thing I know about that property; I was once forced to watch half a season of it for reasons that are too arcane to go into here.*

                                                        In terms of development, the big changes came in the form of making them less referential. The first draft was a tad too nodding and winking, so I upped the Tzimisce-ness of them, giving them an ambiguously Eastern Euroasian origin and making their major weakness into a kind of hyperfocus on their vices: more Greek tragedy, less anime. I also made their connection to the "void" a bit more prominent, and implied a few Werewolf connections you might be able to suss out...

                                                        So, have a look at the Narya. Tomorrow, I think I'll do a quick excerpt of the Clan Bane section, and after that, I think our journey might be at an end. Maybe I'll see if I can dig up any cut material. There was some fun stuff about "Project Shaterglass" for the Mystikoi that I had to chop for space, for instance.

                                                        Narya: The Colossi

                                                        The most potent warriors are patience, time, and me.

                                                        AKA: Antediluvians

                                                        Kindred were not the first vampires to rule the night. Those who count themselves among the All Night Society are merely the most successful. Long before the Kindred drank their first draft, other horrors stalked the dark in search of prey.

                                                        The Narya are one such horror. These deathless monsters — vampires in all but name — say they once ruled a golden empire, yet now their numbers have dwindled to barely a handful. Scant evidence of their past exists in eerie and intricate statues, murals, and reliefs, but little does the waking world suspect that these immaculate works house the monsters they depict. And when these ancients rise, it is with a deep and desperate hunger.

                                                        Background

                                                        The Colossi claim their human ancestors ruled over a long-lost kingdom in a remote part of the earth, long before the time of the biblical flood. However, this kingdom was nearly shattered when a sudden catastrophe befell it. The ground shook, the moon swallowed the sun, and the sky was sundered. In desperation, the royal family of this land offered up a sacrifice to the “void” spreading from the wound in the sky. When the calamity passed, the nobles were changed into gods, not only masters of their people, but masters of the physical form, whether their own or anything they touched. What they called themselves is lost to time — even they do not recall — but Kindred records use the term Narya, a Sanskrit word for heroism and potency. The Narya themselves prefer Colossi, for they stride across the world as giants among lesser beings.

                                                        Now more than mere mortals, the corrupted royal family raised temples to their own egos, devouring sacrifices in the thousands and waging proxy wars against each other for their own amusement, all hidden in the shadows. For although they were eternal, their pact with the void had cursed them to turn to stone under the wrathful gaze of the sun.
                                                        All of humanity might be under their wretched yolk tonight had the unthinkable not happened: Almost overnight, the Narya became sterile. Some speak of a plague that passed silently among them, the symptoms only apparent long after it was too late, while others believe they made some slight against the void, punished for their hubris.

                                                        The Colossi had always limited their bloodline, but without a means to breed, the rigors of time, ennui, and the threat of a hateful sun devastated their numbers. Now those who remain have embraced the long sleep of torpor in hopes of emerging in more interesting eras, or perhaps to find the key to their survival in human innovation.

                                                        Blood and Stone and Flesh

                                                        Each Colossus can reshape her body at will, similar to the Savages but with far more versatility. Unfortunately, this does not include creating more of their kind. To the Colossi, the Kindred are an inferior strain of vampirism, but not in how they reproduce. For all their power and functional immortality, the Narya breed as humans do, but none have successfully mated since their golden age. Some have tried to conceive with ghouls, dhampir, or even Kindred, but the results are always the same: failure.

                                                        Maybe that’s for the best. While the Colossi can savor food and drink, they cannot survive without the blood, and they often have a thirst that can match any methuselah’s. However, even that would be more manageable than the sudden and capricious moods that possess them. These emotional jags come on like hurricanes, driving the Narya to acts of selfishness that would make a draugr raise an eyebrow. While in these states, a Colossus might consume an entire city in one night, slaughter his own armies, or uncontrollably weep until the sun rises and seals his fate.

                                                        No known mortal weapon can slay a Colossus, but they do fear two things. The first is the sun their ancestors betrayed to the dark; the second is the loneliness of eternity. With no prospect of a future for their dying race, many have lost themselves to despair and embraced the daystar’s judgment.

                                                        Now that only a handful remain, this loneliness has consumed them. The Colossi long for the companionship of their own kind, whether as friends, lovers, or rivals, and they scour the world in search of one another, especially on the off-chance that a coupling might produce heirs. When these efforts fail, many take their frustrations out on the ever-changing world and its inhabitants. Some orchestrate power fantasies and mystery plays, drawing unwitting actors into their webs to play heroes and villains, often with themselves at the center of the narrative. And when this too grows wearisome, the Narya returns to torpor. Within a tomb of living stone, the vampire sleeps away the centuries and dreams of nights past until she wakes to repeat the blood-drenched cycle all over again.

                                                        Sidebar: Lies, Damned Lies, and History

                                                        Kindred who’ve heard of the Narya and their empire wonder at the latter’s veracity, which isn’t to say the Colossi are lying, but also they’re predisposed to self-aggrandizement, and, if nothing else, vampires know having a long memory doesn’t mean you have an accurate one. Some Kindred have heard stories of this fallen kingdom and sought its remains, though if any Narya still recall its location, they’re keeping mum, at… least until they get a good offer.

                                                        Systems

                                                        The Narya function like Kindred except as follows:

                                                        Anchors: Colossi see no need to hide their natures, and they have even less use for the All Night Society. As such, Narya have Virtues and Vices rather than Masks and Dirges.
                                                        Banes: Narya are no more vulnerable to fire than humans, but even minor exposure to sunlight turns their bodies to stone. Once petrified, they can never be restored, but they remain mentally aware until something or someone destroys their remains.

                                                        The Beast: Narya lack Beasts. Instead of frenzy, they suffer Hamartia (below). Furthermore, a Colossus who loses her last Humanity dot does not become a draugr. Instead, she gains a persistent form of the Cathectic Condition (p. 165).

                                                        Blood Sympathy: Narya are considered once-removed from their parents and children and twice-removed from all other Colossi. They treat Kindred who know Protean as thrice-removed, though this relation only goes one way.

                                                        Disciplines: While they can learn blood sorcery, Narya have no Disciplines of their own (with the exception of Protean; see below). Instead, they can manifest Discipline powers by spending Vitae equal to the total dots they wish to access. This lasts a scene and is limited to the ten powers common to most Kindred. Narya cannot improvise Devotions or Merits that tie into Disciplines, but they can learn them, ignoring prerequisites. They must manifest these prerequisites to use these powers, however.

                                                        The Embrace: Narya cannot Embrace and do not spawn revenants through careless feeding. In theory, they can reproduce naturally, but none have been able to do so for eons.

                                                        Ghouls and Blood Bonds: Narya can form Vincula, but they cannot create or foster ghouls. Colossi are immune to Vitae addiction and add their Resolve dots to any rolls to resist blood bonds.

                                                        Physical Intensity: In addition to the usual effects of Physical Intensity, a Narya can spend Vitae to increase a Mental or Physical Attribute by a dot, up to her trait limit. The vampire can do this as many times in a scene as she has Blood Potency dots.

                                                        Physiology: Narya bodies have human autonomic functions, including natural healing. Instead of a blush of life, a Colossus can spend a Vitae to seem corpselike. Furthermore, they sleep like mortals and do not need to spend Vitae to rise for the night, though they also do not experience a Cleansing effect. By spending a Vitae and a Willpower, a Narya can heal all bashing damage in her Health boxes. She also does not need to rest to heal aggravated damage, though she can only mend a point per scene. Furthermore, only full aggravated damage can send her into torpor, at which point she can don her Stone Chrysalis (below).

                                                        Gifts of the Void

                                                        The Narya differ from the Kindred in their mutable forms and command over matter. However, the darkness also exacts a heavy price upon their souls, inflating their egos to tumorous proportions as they fall further away from Humanity.
