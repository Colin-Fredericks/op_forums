            One day it will be possible to make a criticism of a game system without somebody responding that the critic just needs to "be a better GM".

            The corrollary to saying "this isn't a problem if you're a better GM" is to say "if you're not, then you have a problem". I've never liked the "You must be this high a GM to ride this game system". Not if it's a needless restriction. I think it's fair to say that my post was correct - it IS constraining to have an easily available "I know you're guilty" button in the game. That's undeniable because I list circumstances all day long that it constrains a GM or player from doing. "I want my character to be a secret diablerist / sorry, it's not possible to keep diablerie secret".

            I'm not even convinced you can put some simple ranking of better or worse on people because of their game tastes. Is it fair to say that a GM who wants a tyranical prince with Auspex is a "worse" GM than one that wants a city filled with laws, councils and judicial process? I don't think so. You can claim that one GM is putting more effort into inventive solutions to get around the constraints, but I don't think you can say they're "a better GM" just because the other wants a despotic ruler. Someone criticized the idea of a powerful prince not caring what others thought. Well that's what power is for. Anyway, I don't want to get too specific in my responses here because this conversation seems to be getting heated, so I'll summarize: if someone is saying that the solution to a constraint is being "a better GM" then they're acknowledging that it's a constraint. And lets be blunt - it's a big constraint. There's a terrible crime in vampire society and there's an easily accessible way to tell whether or not someone is guilty of it. That closes a lot of doors.

            Now I don't think anybody has said that this is a problem for everybody nor that it should be. But if it closes a lot of doors and creates a big problem for those of us who are not "better GMs", then it is by definition constraining.

            And if it's constraining without some good stuff to offset that constraint, then I don't personally see it as a good bargain.

            Someone asked for a reason why this is bad but Invisibilty isn't. Let's answer that. Invisibility is far more easily worked around at a campaign level than detecting secrets like Diablerie. It throws up problems for a GM like "just having someone stand guard wont be enough to stop them getting inside" or "they'll be able to follow someone home". The thing is, you're hard put to think of problems Invisibility throws up that can't also be achieved other ways. So invisibility lets you sneak into the party? So would forging invitations or scaling the walls or gas form... Invisibilty lets you walk right behind that elder on his way home to find out where their haven is? You could use a drone or a tracking device or a few mortals doing hand-off tailing. Invisibility throws up practical level scene challenges. And there are lots of ways of mitigating it that any GM can use. Making invisibillity engaging and a challenge is mostly just a question of resource - give them a dog, give them a fancy security system, etc.. And resource solutions are easy for a GM. Knowing someone's secrets is a campaign-level problem. It can't usually be solved with resource solutions, it has consequences way beyond adventure level abilities such as invisibility. And what do we get in return for these constraints? The difficulties presented by invisibility are small and in return you get "I'M INVISIBLE! WOO!". I mean it's really cool to become one with the shadows. The difficulties presented by Auspex 2 are large (i.e. people in this thread are talking about requiring entire city concepts to be used to deal with the issues it throws up) and in return you get "I know if someone has committed a terrible crime or not". That's a bad bargain. And that's the difference between Auspex 2 and invisibility.

            It may not be a problem for everyone in this thread (and what is universally considered such?), but it's a big constraint for many of us. And I really don't think someone is a better GM than another because one wants a friendly seeming vampire to be a secret diablerist and the other wants the PCs to be her first ever victims. Maybe the first GM doesn't WANT their character to be the latter. Maybe the secret diablerist is what they want. The button is a big constraint and for some people that is legitimately going to be a problem and they're not wrong to say it is for them.

                    All powers are "problematic" from one point of view or another. The problem is that people are trying to make an objective argument about something that is subjective.

                    What I see here are people trying to black and white something that is 1000 shades of grey and black. A diablerist is not automatically a demented soul sucking monster. He is somebody that committed a terrible act. You should be wary of him, but then again you should be wary of any vampire, right?

                    Truth and knowledge don´t always matter. Impotence in the face of stagnant procedure and social power dynamics are a facet of reality and are greatly present in standard vampiric society.

                    And when truth and knowledge matter it doesn´t have to matter in a positive light. You could live terrified in the presence of people you know committed heinous acts that are punishable by law and yet there is nothing you can do about it.

                    The game espouses a dark atmosphere.

                    First idiot neonate that starts spilling the secrets of other vampires will soon learn when to leverage their knowledge when they can and shut the fuck up when they cannot. The ones that learn to tell the difference between the two (pray to the Lord), will be the ones most likely to keep existing. "Like me so much that you been spying on me, eh little kitty? So, lets get to know each other better."

                    The self-righteous and judgemental will soon isolate themselves in a society where, by default, all are bloodsucking predaory unholy abominations. Careful not to be left alone in the dark. There ARE monsters under the bed.

                    There isno objective value in thinking that players being able to detect dark secrets is more "constraining" than them being able to turn invisible.

                    Why are people trying to establish something as universely "constraining"? There is no such thing. Aplples and oranges, sorbets and juices.

                    Any Discipline can be plot jarring depending on the plot. There is nothing wrong with that. Either rethink the plot in question or adjust the power so that it isn´t able to disrupt the plot.

                    The use of a discrete power is a narrative statement. Narrative statements are a building block of storytelling. There are no "better" one than the other. Each and everyone one eliminates countless stories.

                    Being given advice on how you might let the power be and still keep suspense on a plot is not being "told to ST better". It is just giving advice on how to keep suspense using the directives as written. Don´t like the advice, don´t use it. Don´t agree with it, and care to, discuss it. Don´t act like people are trying to put others down.

                    Chipped shoulders and thin skins abound. But this is the internet.
