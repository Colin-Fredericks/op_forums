    Originally posted by Tessie View Post

    As far as I'm concerned, Clan politics is basically absent by default. There's the Clan Status Merit, but very little fluff and no other mechanics to back up any sort of Clan organisations.



    It's 25 years.
    For PCs it's a complete non-issue*, but it means that whenever you want an ancient elder to wake up and disturb the peace or just have the players find the resting place of such an elder, they'll always wake up with BP1.

    *An exception is historical games where time skips are used to fastforward through large time spans. We're planning such a game right now and we'll have to completely rework everything relating to BP changes so that we have a good progression from year ~410 to current day.

    Clan politics popped up in a couple of games I was in although the majority mostly paid lip service to it and focused on the covenants, the exceptions were in cities that had a predominance of a certain clan in a covenant (one game in particular had the vast majority of the Crones being daevas).

    I was in a game that started in the 1500's and gradually progressed to the modern day (lasted about two years in real life), after the first months when the ST decided to have the first time skip, we had another session zero where we discussed what we would have wanted from the game and how we should approach the whole BP thing, we decided to transition to the elder template (a thousand years of night was the basis for everything elder related) and basically do what was more in character. My character was a nosferatu and he went into voluntary torpor to keep his BP at 5 and so did our Daeva, our ventrue had the highest at 7 but had to get into the graces of the ordu to get the scales for allowing him to subsist on human blood because it was getting harder to feed, our gangrel went on a crusade against a large werewolf pack when he was at BP 6 but ended up in torpor following a last stand against a ridiculously badass Rahu and decided to drop at BP 4 for his "repentant monster" phase.
    The game was very character focused without the PC focusing too much on their power level and preferring to pursue their in character objective, it was far less unbalanced than you would immagine (although don't expect for humans or even most other supernaturals to be a real threat to you, both physically and powerwise).

            I can speak to this, since I'm running a Requiem game right now. It's not really any of these styles. In our game, the Coterie have recently graduated from being Ancilla to being the leaders of their city (Bath circa 500 AD, using the Arthur's Brittannia Dark Era) and of their respective Covenants. The player characters aren't directly at cross purposes with each other, and there are a lot of outside factors that are causing them to need to work together, including an army coming to besiege their city.

            Nevertheless there's an entertaining amount of backstabbing going on. In our game, I would say about 50% of scenes have two or more characters together in the same scene, and about 50% are just one character interacting with NPCs. This means characters have a lot of opportunities to lie or misdirect. Even better, sometimes two or more characters are in on the same lie, and honestly I'm pretty impressed that the players are able to keep their character knowledge straight and remember who knows what.

            So while a lot of the time the players are working broadly in concert with each other, there are also lots of political shenanigans happening behind the scenes. For example, the former Prince of the city recently "went missing". The players took the opportunity to seize power by forming a council. Now, the player who is leader of the Circle of the Crone knows what happened to the Prince and knows that she's not coming back. But he also didn't want the Lancea et Sanctum to get a spot on the Council. So he argued that while they could fill the empty Council positions (such as Sheriff, Augur, and Keeper of Elysium) they couldn't create any new ones since only the Prince could do that and the Prince is currently missing. The compromise they came to is that the Lance could send a representative (the Lancea et Sanctum player character) but they are only an observer and can't actually vote on anything.

            I'm looking forward to wrapping up this story and doing a time skip into the future, since I think that will lead to a lot more opportunities for the players to directly antagonize one another. But I still don't see it ever becoming a full political intrigue game since there's always some external force or threat that the Coterie have to deal with.

                                                It feels like VtM assumptions about how the Masquerade works is coming into this. VtR's idea is not some giant vampire conspiracy, though vampires work towards this end, that creates the Masquerade: humans do. Humans in the CofD all, on some instinctive level, know the supernatural exists, but telling the supernatural that gets you killed (or worse). It is a "soft" Masquerade enforced by dozens of thousands of years of evolutionary pressure favoring humans that are in a perpetual state of subconscious denial about the world they truly live in.

                                                Also things like The Lie in Mage that can further explain how humans have become more and more blind to the truth over time.

                                                As has been noted, repeatedly, when discussing this for the CofD, there's a reason why the 2e core book's character creation section tells people playing normal humans to still come up with supernatural events their character has brushed up against.

                                                    The thing is, they already know.

                                                    Most people, deep down, know vampires are real. You can't live in the Chronicles of Darkness without having a sense that something is deeply wrong with the world, and that supernatural beings are part of it. No, you don't say it out loud, because A) there's a literal machine god that maintains a vicious false status quo and B) your life will be a lot better if you don't piss off the vampires who might be listening in. Why go asking for trouble? And like, they're rare, right? Probably all died out a long time ago, or they only come out on a full moon...or maybe they all just live with the homeless people, snacking on derelicts. They certainly won't come for you, with your dead bolt and your security system. Why worry about it. Why even talk about it.

                                                    The Masquerade is about who is a vampire. Steve down the hall? Definitely not a vampire. You had coffee with him that one time! And vampires don't eat, so he can't be one. Vampires don't watch football with you on Sunday (they have to sleep on Sunday, right?) They don't bring sick guac to game night (garlic! Garlic is in guac!) No. Steve is NOT a vampire.*

                                                    Does everyone know vampires are real? Well, most people know sharks are real, but we don't spend a lot of time thinking about them. However, you also know not to go swimming alone with a bloody wound, just like you never patronize that night club on 7th Street.

                                                    But shark attacks still happen, no matter who tells us to never swim alone.

                                                    * Steve is the prince.
