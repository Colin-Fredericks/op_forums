    Revenant characters?

    06-13-2016, 04:33 AM
    I will be trying my hand at playing a Revenant character within the next 2-3 weeks. I'm expecting a rough go of things, but fully intend to get uplifted somehow, as quickly as possible.

    I'm interested if anyone has any experience with Revenant player characters, interesting Revenant NPCs they've had included in their chronicles, or any other general advice on the subject.

      I've had a Neglatu PC that raised revenant Retainers. Started off as a revenant, was uplifted, and went through that same process myself.

      My end thoughts, mixed in with some comments from the developer Rose, are thus: Revenants are the way we get to play spontaneously Embraced vampires. You just wake up one morning with an insane hunger you can't control and new banes and a hair trigger temper. I had fun playing as the complete newbie in a way that I don't think that a freshly Embraced full clan member ever did. This is also a good way to get PCs of (eventually) different clans in play, but all become vampires at the same moment.

      The story I had revolved around a few things. First and formost was survival. My character had no idea what was really going on - hungry, and afraid are two bad combinations when you're on a hair trigger. And I didn't want to kill anyone. I had no support structure other than other revenant PCs. We ended up killing from hunger more than once - frenzy checks when you only have a little willpower, every night? Its very easy to lose control. We ended up hiding out in an abandoned home's basement we boarded up to sleep. As for what we did for food? Well, it was pretty amazing how far we went to get a hit of that red, how much we were willing to go - prostitution, muggings, breaking into homes and feeding on sleeping people. We spiraled very quickly into crime to survive.

      The second story point was being hunted. We didn't know it at the time, but the PCs were being tracked by Kindred. The Kindred, when they found us, eventually helped us; at the time, we didn't know that, however, just that something was after us and there were dead bodies. Mostly dead, anyways - one got back up. This led to a fear of discovery and constant paranoia. When you're this close to the ground, and you don't have a lot of power? The importance of the Masquerade really hits home.

      Eventually, we were found and introduced to Kindred support structures, covenants, and the like. From there, it was pretty simple to be upraised into a full clan. Suddenly, life was a lot easier, and easy to feed without fear, more control over the Beast, everything. Everyone was eager to join the All Night Society and joined a covenant at this point - it was a huge relief. After that, I ended up joining the Neglatu bloodline, which unfortuantely wasn't as interesting as I thought it was going to be. I felt like I simply was playing an alternative ghoul minion-master, with a stronger combative/tracking focus. It felt odd to have revenants without uplifting them - cruel even, so I eventually just did that. Revenant NPCs are too pitiful and too dangerous to not uplift - unless you're a controlling sociopath, that is. I had never hit that point.

      In the core book, Chapter 2, "All These Things I've Become," it talks about the basic needs of a vampire, needing to hide out in ice cream vans or in the sewers. This is the perfect type of chance to highlight these basic needs for a vampire, before "Moving Up." Revenants really hit home on the basic necessities of being a vampire without the politics or family swaying. Just pure vampire. Once you get into the All Night Society, though, a lot of that pressure goes away, and it feels like the point of playing a revenant goes away. Revenants are a great way to really hit that "just starting out and have no control or allies" vibe, but once you get into the Danse Macabre, there's no point to being one.

          I played a Revenant for a while in a Chronicle and I can concur with MCN's points, especially the first one.

          Being a Revenant isn't only jarring but also very dangerous. Waking up every night with zero Vitae means that the Frenzy checks will start coming with every heartbeat you hear. Eventually the odds are going to be against you, and every Kindred will realize that once you're discovered.

          While Revenants can be useful due to their unique ability to track down their creator, most Kindred won't risk sticking their neck out for such dregs. This can lead to some wonderful "Caitiff" moments in Requiem where the Revenant is on the run and must find some benefactor to protect them before the Hounds find them and put them down.

              MCN nicely sums up a lot of the salient points about playing a Revenant. The lack of blood every wake-up combined with (-4) to frenzy checks means a nightly struggle with frenzy just to avoid torpor. I've got three PCs playing the Revenant game at the moment (The City has been struck with a blood curse by Sanctified renegades which is part Judgment Fast plus an increased occurrence of Revenant creation - a rough combination.)

              The PCs are:
              • Morgan - An attempted suicide who ended up committed in a survivors counseling center directed by a Kindred. Morgan was attending a meeting with the Director hoping to be released when the Judgment Fast was enacted. The Director suffered the effects (Not being able to hold vitae in greater amounts than humanity) and vomited multiple points of Vitae. The Director's ghoul responded to the commotion, saw the situation - his Regnant suffering the curse effects, this mortal seeing through the Masquerade - and panicked. The guard attacked Morgan, knocking him out and slitting his wrists to make things look like he actually succeeded in committing suicide. Morgan was discovered in the Morgue by an ex-Retainer medical examiner, nursed back to 'health' and has now begun learning to hunt and survive as a solitary vampire.
              • Straum - A streetwise type character who looks out for the underprivileged in his neighborhood. Tries to help one of the young gutter-punks get out of a drug deal gone bad, and winds up negotiating with the Kindred who was Regent over his particular district. Straum trades his own services in place of the street-kids, but before the deal is done the building is attacked by a being who uses a horn to blast down the walls, has on some sort of supernatural mask, and seems to be accompanied by apparitional angels. Straum witnesses the fight between the Kindred - with the team he was negotiating with running like hell and leaving him to get slaughtered. He comes to in his coffin, frenzying and succumbing to torpor multiple times before he can claw out. He tries to survive and frenzies on his first victim. Eventually, he meets another Revenant trying to survive, who brings him underground to an abandoned part of the Undercity where a small community of Revenants are trying to hide from the 'topsiders.' Straum learns that there is a society of Vampires who control the city, who view them as a threat to their 'Masquerade' and are trying to kill any of them who don't allow themselves to be taken. Straum struggles with and eventually confronts the leader of the underground community who had been becoming more and more militant and paranoid.
              • Simon - A musician and occultist playing a gig on a boat party which was attacked by one of the campaign's bigger antagonists. A low humanity Daeva/Gangrel merged bloodline (who had frenzied because of the Judgment Fast) tears into the boat, killing twenty-three of the party-goers and staff. Simon wakes up down river to find that his shop, his home, his family have all been shut-down or sidelined by other Vampires (Results of the Invictus stepping in the clean up the Masquerade breach.) Luckily, a Carthian is looking for ways to poke holes in the Invictus effort and tracks him down through Social Media. Simon is more naturally introduced to Kindred society and is on track to joining the Carthians where he learns about the underground community. Wanting to help, he agrees to be a diplomat to the group and is sent to try and convince them to officially join the All Night Society.
              All three have been dealing with the every-day frenzy in different ways. Morgan has just been rolling every time and tried to be efficient in his feedings. Straum frenzied twice and killed his victim both times, losing humanity on the second. He took a Bane for impassioned murder as a result. Simon actually used his occult knowledge and philosophy to create a Kindred style merit to help Revenants deal with the beast. We worked on it together and came up with these mechanics:

              ---
              Thin Blood, Thick Mind
              Pre-Req: Revenant Only

              A meditative technique that takes advantage of the incomplete fusion of Man and Beast within Revenants. A mystical tradition centered around the opening of Chakras, the style assists its practitioners with staving off the negative effects of the Beast.

              All rolls for this style use the Meditation rules:
              Action: Simple or Extended (30 mins per roll)
              Pool: Resolve + Composure
              • Anahata (●): As your last action before entering the daysleep, engage in an instant or extended Meditation action. On a simple success, gain the Steadfast Condition which can be used after you wake the next evening on any single Frenzy check. On an exceptional success, the Steadfast Condition may be applied to any single roll that evening.
              • Manipura (● ●): Engage in an instant Meditation check. For each success, lower the duration of a single Bestial, Competitive or Wanton Condition affecting you by one day per success. You may only focus the Manipura once per Condition inflicted. On an Exceptional success, remove the Condition completely, and you may not be the target of that Condition for the rest of the month as if you had Resolved it normally (don't take a beat).
              • Sahasrara (● ● ●): Upon emerging from the daysleep, pay one vitae as normal to avoid falling into torpor. If you have any excess vitae still in your system, rather than purge the excess, convert it into a single point of Willpower (this may not take you above your maximum).
              ---


              As for NPCs, many of the Revenants underground are victims from the barge-party. The Cacophony have started referring to them as The Ferryman (including Simon). Right now they are a paranoid community that has been running from the Scourge on a nightly basis and aren't sure if they can trust the topside people. Straum has become their temporary new leader after getting in a frenzy fight with their old leader and placing him into torpor.

              The other big Revenant NPC is actually the Priscus Harpy. A themed character known as "The Smith" - they present a different face to each person they meet, and one that seems neutrally trustworthy. Basically, Familiar Stranger enhanced to appear as a default 'trusted' type of individual, that gives a minor Social bonus. I actually have each player pick out an image that represents the Smith for their PC and use that. The Smith takes advantage of obfuscate to impersonate the 'smallfolk' in the Domain, has met people as bartenders, beggars, lawyers, etc. I try to play them like an old Hollywood reporter, ferreting out juicy rumor and gossip on the city officers and people of status - showcasing and sensationalizing current events. Most recently, the Smith has actively put out to the Domain that they will look favorably on any Kindred who uplift and Account for any Revenants.

              The one base-mechanical switch I did for the Revenant PCs was allowing them to 'manifest' three dot-one disciplines on the fly during scenes. These are only the base-five, but they can do any three of the five and can do so as a reflexive action. I've enjoyed tempting people with different results. "Do you let the person rush inside and shut the door, or do you manifest Celerity and flash inside just behind them?" If/When they get uplifted, I'll allow them to keep any manifested disciplines, or convert them directly to the new in-clans of their adopted Sire. So far the choices have been Obfuscate, Obfuscate and Celerity, and Resilience.

                      Ok, finally got the chance to play this character...

                      I usually try to have characters start a campaign with at least 1+ years already under their belts, because as a long time fan of the WoD, I find it redundant at this point.

                      However!

                      From the moment this character opened her eyes on the coroner's table has been a total blast so far: In just 2 nights, she's already killed 3 people (including her lover). Her Humanity is in a complete tailspin. She's had her face burned and blistered by the morning sun, had to resort to chopping up one of her victims in the bathtub-- only to have a bag full of body parts burst open while throwing it into a lake. She's earned 2 XP in a single session (which is crazy to me), yet because I'm bookmarking that XP for a new touchstone, she's actually running at a net loss.

                      Basically, I highly recommend giving it a try!

                                      I am under the impression that Revenants start with the same three dots as a regular vampire, except that they're all out of clan instead of requiring two to be in clan, for obvious reasons. If you have a Mentor or some other appropriate merit tied to a full blooded vampire, you can start with a signature discipline. Given the nature of revenants, that's understandably going to be a very rare thing, but its worth mentioning. Otherwise, we're restricted to Animalism, Celerity, Obfuscate, Resilience and Vigor. No covenant magics.
