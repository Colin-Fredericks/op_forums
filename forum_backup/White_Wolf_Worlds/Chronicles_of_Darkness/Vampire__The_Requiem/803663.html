    Problem with religion based covenants (Lancea and Circle).

    01-08-2016, 07:59 AM
    In the last 18 months I have organized a long term Requiem campaign and 3 other introductory 2-3 sessions mini chronicles for different groups. All games took place in a big city with about 60-70 kindred. Combining all players, and considering their chosen covenant I had this character distribution:

    Ordo: 7 (1 defected to the Invictus)
    Invictus: 2 (plus 2 deserters from other covenants)
    Carthians: 2
    Lancea: 1
    Circle: 1 (defected to the Invictus after selling the cult to the Lancea)

    Well, as we can see the Ordo is pretty popular among players, perhaps too much for a lesser young covenant. But that’s ok, no problem. My problem is with the 2 religious Covenants. I literally had to sell them to the players because they were discarded in the first round, not even considered. After some marketing I got a lone Lancea player and a Crone one.

    The Lancea player at least was happy with his character and the covenant. He understood what they were doing, liked Theban sorcery and were in good standing with other Lancea NPCs.


    But the Crone player... never liked the cult (and she played the long chronicle). I tried to sell the Circle as better as I could, I made the witches able to work spells and summonings much more powerful than the ones you can find in the book. And also after a few moths made the covenant much more politically aggressive. Nothing worked, the player never felt involved, in fact I wasn´t able to convince her to spend a single experience in cruac. They were “a bunch of crazy ladies in the forest”, my attempts to make the five witches appealing were all in vain.
    A lot of wasted work.

    Right now, I´m developing another city, a much smaller one, about 15 kindred. And I´m thinking in completely ignoring the Circle and reducing the Lancea to a small cult of one priest and a few Invictus and Carthian sympathizers. I don´t know if the problem is the non religious times we live, if religion and vampires don´t mix well or if the problem is mine, because in my deep soul, I don´t like those covenants and I´m unable to make them alluring to the players.

    I feel the Ordo got all the appealing “supernatural secret lore” thing the Tremere had in Masquerade, leaving the circle with the bloody hippies concept. And the savage predators stuff, well, all covenants are composed by vampires and all of them are true badasses. And If a vampire don´t want any rules, he doesn´t need to follow a forgotten summerian god to be free.

    I can discard the Lancea, but I really like the “blood magic” concept, even if the rituals in the basic rulebook fail to attract players. So I have this options:
    1. Give the sorcery to the Ordo. But would be a bad idea because it already have the coils and has enough sexappeal.
    2. “Tremerize” the circle. Remove the cult roots and change them a for groups of kindred using the power of the blood and the beast to practice secret sorcery. Change the ritual names to make then more arcane and bookish. Change the bloody forest bacchanals for bloody rituals in a runic magic circle. They wouldn’t pray for magic, they would steal, study and corrupt it. The leaders wouldn´t be priestess and hierophants, but master sorcerers.
    3. Ask for help in the forum. Anybody has found the same problem?, do you have some more modern looking cults? (all this greek, celtic and voodoo stuff hasn’t worked for me). Perhaps more “God machinish”.

    I wouldn’t like to reduce the game to 3 covenants, but I also don’t like to work in vain designing characters and plots that will be ignored by the players. All comments and insight will be wellcome.

                  There's a simple way to run the Circle to be more player-friendly, especially in a tiny location.

                  Let the players decide on the cult structure. The amazing thing, to me, about the Circle is the variety of it. At it's core, it's a religious group that exalts the Beast and uses blood sorcery. You can frame it as witches, animists and pagans, you can even frame it in terms of existing religions. Haitian Vodou has a lot of Catholicism in there, for one thing. They could take a more philosophical 'way of living' approach rather than an explicitly religious one. Cruac rites will always involve blood, but they can be ecstatic or somber. As long as they still with the absolute core principles of the Circle and Cruac, let them make whatever crazy cult they want.

                  I reckon this approach with really appeal to certain players and is also a way to really integrate the Circle into your city, since the 'default' flavour might not fit..

                  You can do something similar with the Lancea, somewhat, but you can't really drop the 'Vampire funhouse mirror reflection of Christianity' thing, which some people flat-out won't be interested in. But there's scope for corrupt, 'God is on my side, so I'm right' zealotry and nastiness, sympathetic (and possibly politically neutral) 'Beasts we are lest beasts we become' guides and gurus, tight-lipped and insular Christian-magicians who appear more like a Masonic Lodge. Again, if you want to use them look at your city and what role you think they'd fit best.

                                  I wouldn't go out of my way to make any covenant more attractive rules-wise. Either the players can relate to the underlying philosophy or they can't. But I would refrain from any changes just to get a player to play a member of a specific covenant.

                                  I have similar experiences with the Circle. None of the players really wanted their characters to belong to the "crazy" blood witches. I continued to portray them faithfully to the fiction. They are my covenant after all - and not because of Cruac, but because of the philosophy they espouse. They are the least civilized and rational of all the covenants, prone to decisions guided by faith and believe not political calculation. It's something rather hard to grasp for most players today, I guess. Especially compared to more easily understood covenants like Carthians and Invictus or the Ordo Dracul who offers tangibel mechanical benefits in dealing with the unpleasant side effects of being a vampire.

                                  My players chose the Ordo Dracul most often, Invictus second, then Carthians and only rarely Lancea Sanctum and never Circle of the Crone.
