    Help me decide to play Requiem instead of Masquerade

    09-30-2016, 09:09 PM
    So I realized while looking at house rules and such that I wanted for Masquerade, I was effectively making it more like Requiem, and since I like Requiem more and Masquerade primarily has nostalgia value, maybe I should just go ahead with Requiem.

    I am also somewhat reacting to Shane's comments about Chronicles of Darkness during the White Wolf Q&A at Grand Masquerade.

    What makes Requiem cool?

    Stuff I like immediately is that there's explicit mechanical support for vampires as a group having enhanced senses, with Auspex covering more ESP-type stuff. This was what I was trying to adapt to Masquerade. I also like the smaller number of clans and the diverse covenants in contrast to the sects of Masquerade.

    Unfortunately, I haven't played it yet so there's a lot of stuff I am probably missing. I mean my few things listed are minor.

    Also, which books work best with second edition?

      You can't go wrong with the Clanbooks. They're very much the foundation for the Second Edition, and they add a wonderful, dark flavour to the world, as well as a palpable sense of history (whether you believe it or not).

      What attracts me to Requiem is its atmosphere. Vampires are parasites who play at politics and religion to keep themselves from devouring each other. Humanity is both your food and the one anchor that keeps you from falling into the maw of the Beast. You can be an aristocrat, a revolutionary, a witch, a mad scientist, a priest, or something else entirely, but at your core, you're a monster, and you have to figure out how to live with that. That's the Requiem. But as you grow older, as your blood thickens and your power grows, your challenge becomes not how to live with being a monster (there's no denying it anymore), but what the best monster you can be is.

          In my opinion, Requiem - and Requiem 2e in particular - does an incredible job of showing the descent into monstrosity. Losing Humanity makes you take more damage from Sunlight, and makes your Clan Bane (along with any additional Banes you've gained) get worse. Humanity is lost not by committing sins, but by experiencing things that distance you from the human experience. You can protect your humanity to a limited degree by maintaining personal relationships with humans who remind you of what it felt like to be human yourself.

          I also love the background and worldbuilding details of Requiem. Without having a metaplot (which I personally find intrusive), it manages to have a rich history and background details, and still leaves a lot of space for the ST to fill in. Like, to use video games as an analogy, if Masquerade was a BioWare game, and Requiem would be a From Software game.

          The local scale is a plus for me. I generally like personal stories more than epics.

          As for what 1e books work best with 2e, definitely get Damnation City. That might be the best resource in all of 1e's run, and most of it can be ported to 2e with little to no legwork. The Clan books are also fantastic, they're 90% story material, all told through in-character doccuments, with rules appendicies at the end. Not all of the rules can be easily ported to 2e, but some of them can, and the others are worth the extra work (or you could use one of the fan conversions you can find in the homebrew hub). But really, you want the Clan books for their fluff anyway. They paint a fantastic picture of the Clans' identities, their place in the setting, and they were some of the first hints of setting details that would continue to get fleshed out across 1e's run, and at this point are regarded as more-or-less canon in 2e, including the Clans' origins and the existence of the Strix. I would recommend the Covenant books as well to get a more in-depth look at the Covenants than the core book gives, but the Covenant identities got a bit of a facelift for 2e so it wouldn't all be relevant, and besides, Secrets of the Covenants might be coming out soon (fingers crossed), and it's going to be written in the same style as the Clan books, which has got me excited. Blood Sorcery: Sacraments and Blasphemies is also worth a look. It significantly expands the blood sorcery Disciplines, in a way that is fully comparable with 2e (in fact, 2e's revisions to blood sorcery were based on the system in that book).

          Going by Willow now, or Wil for short. She/Her/Hers.

                      The big thing for me is that there's more story potential in five uneasy Covenants than there ever was in open Jyhad between two Sects; while the Sabbat were your monstrous vampires and your Camarilla were your ostensibly humane vampires, there was no time to debate that because you had kill-on-sight orders or huge swathes of territory apart from each other. I love that Requiem lets each and every member of your coterie be pulled apart by factionalism and ideology, but you still have to play nice because these assholes are the only thing keeping you safe from everyone else.

                      The biggest shift in going from Masquerade to Requiem is probably in clans going from distinct lineages to loose archetypes, but that's incredibly freeing to me. Whereas "I'm a Ravnos" was the core of your Masquerade character, all a Requiem clan gives you is means to be and do whatever you want; there are Bloodlines if you want to fit into something larger, but by default your character stands alone rather than defined by a dozen generations of context and stereotypes. That they're also more flexible (Nosferatu that aren't physically hideous, but still somehow off and disquieting!) and less racist (Bye-bye, Assamites amd all the baggage that comes with belief-driven Middle Eastern killers!) is just gravy on top for me.

                      Other than that, the cleaner nWoD/CofD rules are a boon and so is not having to race alongside a metaplot or know a mountain of canon characters. The best book by far is Danse Macabre and while a lot of the optional rules in that have been folded into 2e by default (though not the brilliant Atrocity Dice, which I hope have a place in Hunter) there's some amazing advice on what makes Requiem tick and how to make it your game. Requiem For Rome is a blast if you're a history nerd like me and it nails the cinemativ feel of bloody, decadent, globe-spanning Rome. VII gives three contradictory truths behind the shadowy antagonist group that haunted 1e and all of them are brilliant, but special love goes out to Stolze's take in a lost demon-haunted clan that hates the Kindred. Ancient Bloodlines/Ancient Mysteries are great for showing the crazy setting diversity that Requiem allows, the clanbooks all come highly praised by Mekhet is the only one I read (and adored), and then the Night Horrors books are disgustingly great.

                      Welcome to the other Vampire game! If you're like me, you'll never go back.

                      Remi. she/her. game designer.

                                      Basically, it's Requiem's take on Malkavians, as a contagious illness rather than a Clan. Malkav was the first Kindred diagnosed with it, and was a Ventrue, so although it can affect any Kindred, the stereotype is that it's a Ventrue disease, much like the "AIDS is a gay disease" stereotype.                                                   Just my 2 cents.
                                                  Requiem mechanically smoothed vampire to a much more playable system.

                                                  It's easier to change the names of the players in metaplot than rebuild the wheel, if nostalgia is tugging at your brain

                                                  2e is even better imo. Added lots of neat fiddly bits to play with. Also made vampires feel more like classic monsters than school yard bullies 1e left them feeling like. And don't neglect the storyteller section in 2e, lots of great toys to make the feel of the game really immersive.

                                                  Charlaquin nailed the books dead on, but if budget is an issue maybe wait for secrets of the covenants.
                                                  Oh and one of my personal favorites The Blood. More for setting the feel.

                                                  If you are still conflicted maybe try a one shot type game in each system and see which is more your taste. Cuz sometimes you just have to say system be damned, this feels more like what I want.
