                                    You know what method wipes out vampires faster and cheaper than maintaining a prison city?

                                    Killing them on sight!

                                    But that's just the threat to the tax payers. The soldiers guarding them face a more immediate threat. Protean doesn't just let you turn into a bat or wolf, those are just the most common animals. A protean user can turn into any animal. What if a vampire manages to escape by turning into a mosquito and grabbing some TFV soldiers for a quick bite? Speaking from experience you don't feel every mosquito that bites you, at least not until after they're already gone and the bite starts itching. Also, vampires are more durable than humans.                                                     That does sound like a fun campaign, and we had a similar session in a multi-splat game where a sensitive piece of information (that one of the players, playing a werewolf was the reincarnation of father wolf) got out. I pointed out of game to the GM that this would probably break the setting (which he didn't want) since there was no way that PC was going to be left alone and would have to contend with attempts to make him world alpha, to assassinate him, to discredit him, etc. So in the next game, we had to go to the Temenos (collective unconscious) to delete that information and retroactively undo what basically ended as a werewolf world war.

                                                    I mean, I don't know, there are games that have a metaplot, written campaigns (my best roleplaying experience was as a player in a game with a set and very detailed background), and even mechanics that are so closely tied to the background touching anything messes with everything. I don't really consider that Requiem has a "cannon". Sometimes I'm even annoyed at how Requiem books are on purpose, you never really know anything. The bright side is that with so little incentive, you can really do anything you want, and if an idea comes up, but a few rules get in a way of it, decide which is more important.

                                                    I stand by the campaign I've built.
