                                            I believe a mekhet bloodline of 1ed was morevsensitive to sunlight than normal (prob khaibit). I'm pretty sure it was specified they also took some moderate damage from full moon light. I would assume that this is because moonlight is actually just indirect sunlight, so indirect sunlight would be an issue. Now, how much of an issue is it ? One could argue that a mirror is indirect sunlight and mirrored sunlight would kill vampires (I guess everyone would agree on that). On the other hand, an opaque black wall would reflect a lot less light and I don't think both would hurt a vampire as much. There's also the problem of diffraction a ray of light that hits an obstacle rebounds a little in all directions. In that regard, an opaque obstacle would cause a lot more diffraction than a clean mirror (though it would also absord more light). That said, since we're talking about the energy of a unidirectional beam, it would lose a great deal of energy when diffracted and would keep losing energy as you would move away from the spot the beam would hit.

                                            So now comes the question. Should indirect light matter and to what extend ? I dunno. It's a lot more dramatic if it doesn't and if it's only direct rays and reflected rays (by, say a mirror, a window, a piece of chromed metal...) that hurt the vampire. It means they're safe as long as they manage to get behind an obstacle. It's more cinematic to picture the vampire that just made it behind the door, while the rays dart a few centimeters away from him. Obviously, in the end, it's useless to debate about a precise rule. If it's really ONLY direct and reflected light, then, a vampire could walk in a street as long as they keep in the shade and avoid windows reflections, and that's be stupid.

                                            Having diffracted, diffuse light kill vampires could add an element of danger, it's not just the rays, but like a wall of hurt that you have to get away from, and a closed room with a sufficiently large window becomes a death trap no matter in what corner you hide. Thing is it'll start getting annoying if you try to get more accurate with that. Whar color are the walls, how opaque, ok, I'm trapped in a room with a window, but the walls are black right ? Even if light fills the place, it's not gonna be nearly as bad as standing in the beam, right ? Then, as a GM, you find yourself tempted to make charts with environment color, opacity and distance from the spot hit by the beam instead of thinking about how to emotionally toy with the PCs and nobody really wins.

                                                In common parlance, direct and indirect light have specific meanings. Direct light can be diffused or reflected and remains direct until it becomes indirect. Indirect light is diffuse light which can't be followed back to a source by direct observation. A closed box placed out in the sun with completely opaque walls will have no light within. Poke a hole in the box and a "beam" of light would appear within the box. That beam could be reflected with a mirror or diffused with a crystal to make a larger beam. That would all be direct light. The overall light level within the box would increase because light continues to travel until absorbed. The light level increase outside of the beam would be indirect light. The text is ambiguous as to whether any of that matters. However, moonlight is not a problem so the issue must be from the fact that it is a curse and works via supernatural methods.

                                                As it is supernatural the laws of nature are not limitations. If a reasonably strong light that comes from the sun during the day falls upon a vampire, I believe it affects a vampire. The text is ambiguous.

                                                The text is more clear on the damage question originally posed. If only a small part of the body is uncovered then BP is considered one lower than normal. The legs are not small enough so the full effect occurs. Thematically, the legs would appear more damaged and likely tend more to the burning rather than the decaying the rest of the body would like be the reverse. The legs would not get burned off. The final consideration is the legs might be covered with clothing. That should be worth treating like only the eyes uncovered or complete covering depending on the clothing.
