    Noob questions about Touchstones and their relation to the Languid condition...

    03-27-2015, 10:46 PM
    Okay so some friends and I decided recently that we wanted to play Vampire. A friend of mine and I are studying the source material and plan to co-dm the game together.

    I will probably have more noob questions in the future but right now,

    I'm currently reading the second edition and wondering about Touchstones and why losing their last touchstone would cause a Kindred to have the Languid condition. I understand the mechanics, but the text fails to identify why exactly this process happens.

    The text lends some context as to what a touchstone means to a kindred, mentioning something like "a touchstone has a fundamentally positive affect on a kindred's requiem" and "a touchstone reminds him of the man he once was". But as far as I can see, the text gives no incite into why this phenomena occurs.

    In addition to being generally curious about the lore, the main reason why I'm asking is because I'm working on fleshing out Paths of Enlightenment mechanics for the NWoD system and I'm trying to figure how Touchstones might affect a kindred who is trying to adhere to a Path.

    For example, the main character I will be playing is a Gangrel who is following the Path of Harmony. I've worked out that there will be different breaking points for him and I've decided that most Kindred who follow paths of enlightenment, for the sake of social interactions with humans, will be capped at the Weathered humanity level (humanity 4, -2die to relating to humans, ashen pallor). That mindset makes the most sense for a Kindred who has utterly abandoned his humanity in favor of an alternative paradigm and way of life that is ultimately used to keep the Beast at bay.

    I'm trying to figure out why a kindred would slip in to torpor due to the loss of a touchstone. I can see a kindred on the Humanity system basically falling into such a profound depression that their blood would actually slow, after all depression has physical effects on humans too.

    However, in relation to a kindred following a path, Touchstones inevitably are going to mean varying things to them depending on the path they follow. For now, lets examine possible touchstones for a kindred following the Path of Harmony.

    For those of you who are unfamiliar with it:
    Let's start with the Hierarchy of Sins for this path so you all can get a general overview of the path before we take a slightly more in-depth look.

    10.Attempting to alter the natural world. 9.Indulging in the Jyhad(the Danse) or playing too effectively at being mortal. 8.Failing to hunt when hungry. 7.Pointless cruelty or kindness. 6.Not respecting a stronger vampire's domain. 5.Allowing gross disruption of the natural world. 4.Acting too human or too bestial. 3.Refusing to kill if necessary. 2.Feeling guilty about killing when necessary. 1.Grossly upsetting the natural world (vandalism, arson, etc).

    Hamonists believe that Good and Evil are human concepts that are used to codify events and have no bearing on their Unlives. They also believe that everything that exists in the world has a place in the natural order. To them the natural order is a shifting ecological paradigm that cannot, and should not ever be altered intentionally. Even humanity and all it produces is viewed as an expression of nature (If this is true is it possible that human made pollution, even pollution that is extremely destructive to the environment, is justifiable under the pretense that it is pollution that was produced by an aspect of the natural order?). Kindred are not monsters, they are predators. Any monstrous tendencies of a kindred existed in them before the embrace (this idea has especially interesting implications considering the way the new humanity system works). Harmonists value making smart decisions in the interest of survival as one can see by example of the sixth sin on the hierarchy. The most distinctive feature of this path is the emphasis put on not being too human or too bestial. The harmonist exists somewhere in between these two ends of the humanity continuum and interaction with humans is actually encouraged, if for no other reason than to satisfy one's boredom.

    Enough of that.

    My main point of this post is to look at what kind of touchstones would be acceptable for followers of such a path and why would losing such a touchstone would be so devastating to a creature devoid of humanity as to cause the Languid condition.

    Any thoughts or feedback are welcome

    Thanks in advance~

      I think you're losing something in attempting to port the Paths of Enlightenment to New World of Darkness, you're already in homebrew territory with that, so I don't really know what to tell you metaphysically about why losing a Touchstone would make your character Languid.

      Now, as for Touchstones and Humanity, I think it's important to keep in mind just how important a touchstone is in helping Kindred maintain their Humanity. Having no Touchstones is an active detriment to the maintenance of your Humanity. While I think the way Kindred experience Languid is probably particular to each individual, I imagine it's characterized by the absence of anything to hold onto and care about. Touchstones are an anchor for you, and without that anchor, the Man's only real recourse is to sleep. It sounds to me like the same sort of feeling as when I've experienced Depression, and just found myself unable to even get out of bed. An emotional distance and lethargy. It's easier to just sleep and ignore a world around you that you, and I mean the human you, has no stake in.

      I don't know why it would affect someone on a Path, as I don't really feel Paths are appropriate for 2nd edition world of darkness, since Humanity has been decoupled from Morality. It's an Integrity stat, and if you wanted to create something like a Path, I'd rather suggest it to be a unique merit with certain behavior prerequisites that functions as a Touchstone. I'm not sure the path you've described is substantial enough to actually warrant something like this. I'd look to the Lancea et Sanctum book, which suggests as an optional rule degeneration bonuses when a character is acting in concert with their Sanctified Faith. I think that's a good model for what a Path would be like for 2nd edition vampire.

              So, context! In Masquerade, as well as in 1st edition Requiem, Humanity was something of a moral code, and degeneration was caused by sins against this set of rules. In this paradigm, it makes sense to replace the traditional sins with different set of moral mandates; after all, they were essentially arbitrary rules to begin with, so why not replace them with a different set of arbitrary rules more thematic to a certain type of character?

              Humanity in 2nd edition Requiem is quite different. Instead, it measures the Kindred's actual connection to the human experience. a high Humanity vampire still remembers what it means to be human, while a low Humanity vampire has forgotten the pleasures and pains of mortality. They have become jaded and indifferent to the lives of people they were once one of. It is lost not by breaking rules, but by being reminded that they are unambiguously monsters. Living through things that would kill humans, or seeing nations rise and fall, or exsanguinating an innocent man and feeling not horror but pleasure in the act. These things give the vampire undeniable proof that they are no longer human beings, and can drive them a little bit mad. It might seem similar in concept, but I think the key difference is that previously a sin against Humanity was something you did. Now, a Humanity Breaking Point is something you experience.

              So the reason Touchstones protect Humanity is because they give the vampire a connection to the human experience. someone who reminds them what it felt like to be driven by a conscience instead of a Beast. Losing that connection drives them into Torpor because they just don't care anymore. They have no more connection to life, to the world, not to mention the fact that someone truly important to them, maybe even the last living person they see as anything more than cattle, just died. That kind of loss would make anyone tempted to just go to sleep and never wake up again - or, in a vampire's case, maybe wake up again in a few centuries, after everything from the world that took the only person they still cared about away from them is in ashes, and they can start fresh in a new time, a new world.

              It would be very difficult to apply either concept to a Paths of Enlightenment type mechanic. By the time a character has grown alien enough from human nature to completely embrace such a worldview, they would likely have precious little Humanity remaining, or even none at all. They would likely have no need of a touchstone.

              I think a more appropriate mechanic for Paths rather than a replacement for Humanity might be a Merit that replaces or supplements Touchstones. A code that you keep to that keeps you in check despite having turned your back on human nature. Werewolf 2e has a Merit for keeping to a sort of honor code, I bet some of those mechanics could be borrowed to replicate the narrative function of Paths in a manner more mechanically appropriate to 2e Requiem.

                  Seconding Charlaquin's excellent response.

                  If I were to implement a Path Merit, I think I'd give it two functions:
                  • Have a ritualistic or philosophical action that when taken (and dramatic) allows the Kindred to stave off the Languid condition for a set amount of time (month, say, or maybe weeks per dot of the Merit). this would allow the Kindred to function without touchstones as long as they perform the base functions of the Path. For example, one of the ethics to Path of Harmony is "Do not sully your haven." Maybe allow the character to perform a cleansing, patrol or some other dramatic and confrontational scene in his hunting grounds. (Get rid of the drug dealer a block over, beat the s* out of a poacher, etc).
                  • Second - Customized based on the Path, swap some of the generic breaking points under humanity with alternate breaking points for the path. Ex: one of the tenets of Path of Harmony is 'Refusing to kill if necessary." Allow the player - with justification - to avoid the "Committing Murder" breaking point in justified cases. But in exchange, whenever they refuse to kill when it seems necessary, they get a breaking point instead.
                  Make it about modifying breaking points and touchstone mechanics rather than replacing them whole hog.

                                  That sounds really needlessly complicated (relatively speaking) but if you guys enjoy it, go nuts.

                                  One of the benefits of Touchstones is how they help to facilitate some very natural, personal drama for players, and the nature of the systems that surround it and Humanity being what they are helps to not over-complicate things when it comes to what you're rolling and why. It means more chances of failure and beats from testing and Conditions and other dramatic difficulties. It's the peril of the vampiric existence. If you have to have Paths or whatever, you really, really don't need to make any new systems for them or anything like that. It's seriously as simple as declaring adherence to those habits or whatever as a Touchstone in and of itself.

                                  The best thing you can do with Storyteller and the 2nd Edition of WoD (In my opinion) is keep it clean and simple. The systems that are already present are more than sufficient to create dramatic situations, it's all about just using the tools the game gives you, rather than attempting to make additional tools that are ultimately redundant and a sore fit for the themes of 2nd edition Vampire.

                                              I feel like there's a misunderstanding of what Humanity is in the system, though. Like, it isn't actually your ability to be humane to other people and to not be a dick to them (though it's certainly related) Humanity is actually more about your discrete identity as a person. When you have no Humanity, you cease to be, so turning your back on Humanity seems kinda funky as a radical shift in thinking. Nobody in Requiem counsels the divorce of Kindred from their Humanity, because it's not about being nice to people, it's about actually being able to be a person. When you lose your Humanity, you don't become something alien. You are your Humanity, and the Draugr that's left behind isn't actually you anymore. So a Path shouldn't be about not being human anymore, because Humanity is already about that, and Draugrdom is what happens when you actively abandon your Humanity. The group Belial's Brood are the guys that do this, and they're infamous for their rapid plummet into draugrdom and the fact that they desire it.

                                              If you're going to make Paths in 2e, they need to focus on creating a strong sense of individual identity, a core that you can rest your hat on. It shouldn't actually change the already fluid breaking points of Humanity, but rather, provide you with a means of coping with them more easily. It's simple stuff to make it a dice bonus on a degeneration roll, and demonstrates an important core conceit of Vampire: You can't just wish your Humanity away completely, no matter how much of an inhuman vampire you are. It's actually the thing that keeps us grounded, and it helps produce characters who are sympathetic and interesting.

                                              I realize as I'm writing this that I'm not really against the idea of a Path, but I think you want to implement it in the sleekest way possible. I've GMed 2e Vampire a bunch at this point, and the 1st edition, and I promise you, creating too many subsystem issues for any one player becomes a tiresome activity. If you keep it sleek and simple, everyone is playing by the same rules, you can satisfy your deviant player's desire for something which incentivizes the behaviors he finds interesting while not cutting him off from the compelling elements of Humanity, and you save yourself the extra rolling and logistics of having a whole subsystem dedicated to something other subsystems already accomplish.

                                                      Well, the very first step we consider is to critically evaluate the Path itself. Does the behavioral prohibition make sense? If so, then I'd treat it as a Breaking Point.

                                                      The beauty of 2e's Humanity system is once again that its list of breaking points are suggested and not comprehensive, so it's very accomodating to introducing additional onces/moving things up and down the hierarchy as story dictates. Imagine, if Impassioned Murder is usually a Humanity 4 sin, you might make it Humanity 2 or 1 if it was REALLY bad, like your best friend for life or your sibling, stuff like that.

                                                      Also, in some situations, it might just not be dramatic or cool enough to warrant actually rolling a breaking point, and luckily, we have discretion in that, too.

                                                      I suggest this, offering "Path of Enlightenment" as a 1-3 dot merit, potentially offering a hefty bonus of up to +3 on degeneration checks that are in line with the Path's ethos, because of how strongly you've tied up your sense of self in the self-conception of the Path. I'd require either a specific Dirge or a relevant Dirge in order to take it. so for instance, if the Path of Harmony is your Path, it might require a Dirge of "Monster" or "Predator" or "Beast" or whatever, or it might just have the requirement of "In-theme Dirge" for it. I'd offer a dice bonus commensurate to the level of the dots you have in the merit to breaking points that apply to the merit, and I'd consider one of two drawbacks: That it lowers the amount of benefit you gain from Touchstones as long as you're on the Path, OR that things that violate the ethos of the Path incur the dice bonus as a penalty to the check. I'd examine what counts as sins carefully in the original Path, some things might just flat out not fit VtR 2e, while others might be great sources of drama.

                                                      The key with breaking points is that, while a character might regard them as bad, a player should regard them as exciting, cool and dramatic. Stuff like "not saying hello to someone before you kill them" isn't a terribly exciting breaking point, and doesn't necessarily lead to a more interesting dramatic situation, so it might be ill-suited as one to keep. You've got to maintain a discerning eye towards what fits and what is fun, something older editions/previous games didn't always keep in mind when designing these things.
