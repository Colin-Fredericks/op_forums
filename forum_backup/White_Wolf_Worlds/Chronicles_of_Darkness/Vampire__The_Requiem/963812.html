Lovecraft is a great way to run these games and want to go with it. However, most of the characters, are well...Vampires. Is it still possible to test the Integrity of a Vampire? Would they still be shocked by the H.P. Lovecraft Mythos? Since most Vampires have powers, how can you make them feel powerless to world that they already know?

      I think the Chronicles of Darkness book suggests things more for mortal takes, but it's certainly possible to delve into cosmic horror with a vampire. They do have powers, but there's a limit to them, and there's no limit to the things a vampire can face in the World of Darkness.

      Just because you have powers doesn't mean they're going to be of any use, or worse, might become used against them. Ancient Bloodlines and Mysteries has the Edimmu, for instance. En-Isiratuu was a tyrant vampire king, and in order to fight against him the vampires of what would be the Circle of the Crone summoned spirits from beyond the stars and put them into a body, making what was essentially a star vampire. When it confronted En-Isiratuu, he found himself taken by an uncontrollable urge to drain the vampire, going so far as to commit diablerie, which he was known for doing at the time. Unfortunately, this was how the Edimmu 'Embraced,' and so he became the first subject of a vampiric scourge that spread across the land.

      Alternatively, going directly to Lovecraft, you have something like the Insects from Shaggai or the Mi-Go. Alien beings that 'possess' humanity, some for no other reason than their own hedonistic pleasure. Imagine the fun something like that could get up to with a vampire's body. It wouldn't even have to take full control, but what if after their daysleep the vampire wakes in strange places, with bodies living or dead, finding their body surrendered fully to some other being that didn't think enough about the Kindred to even try to communicate to it. Sure, you could tell another vampire, but that's likely to get you laughed out of your covenant, unless you're in the Ordo Dracul where it will get you put on a slab. You have some of this with the strix, though their reasons are more understandable.

      Basically, just because you're a vampire doesn't mean you can't be ignorant or taken by surprise, and just because of you have powers doesn't mean those powers will be universally useful.

                          That sounds like an awesome basis. Honestly, the reason Vampire is my favourite line of WoD is because despite being immortal monsters, vampires are probably the most human of the creatures and the most normal. Their morality scale focuses on acting and feeling human (it used to be exactly the same as the human scale). They're not here for a reason, they don't have a designated enemy they can base their exstence around and they don't have a place to them, they don't belong anywhere. The closest thing they can call home is humanity and the default occupation for them, besides surviving, is to try to fit in. While powerful, they're also not THAT strong for most of them. Finally, they're numerous enough that they're nit really unique enough for that aspect to be a huge huge deal. Werewolves have a sacred duty to be super border patrol and, at least used to be supposed to run a territory, Changelings know Arcadia exists and that they have to be prepared for when the True Fae inevitably shows up. I don't know much about mages, but don't they have the thing with Atlantis ? They also have lots of realms they can go into and I think they're also pretty OP. Prometheans have a set goal (with differences of how to reach it) and they're generally too rare and messed up to form societies. Demons work against the God Mmachine. I don't know enough about the rest.

                          I like vampires because they're the secret society that lords it over humans but actually doesn't know that much about anything other than themselves. Their only common enemy is the strix, but their number vary from what the GM decides and who knows what they really are/want. That gives them the most potential for discovery and, I think, maybe the widest range of possible stories (with Changelings being a close second I'd say). So if any supernatural template is perfect for Lovecraftian horror, I'd say it's vampires. They don't have any reference to ungodly poweful entities that they could compare elder gods and the such to and they're much more likely to feel lost and helpless against them. I mean, a Werewolf who takes the Luna/wolf mythos seriously could suggest that "Ok, that Cthulhu thing sounds pretty powerful, but we've heard about Idigams and stuff like that. Even if it's more powerful than that, we're backed by one of the most powerful spirits ever, so what's there to fear ?"
