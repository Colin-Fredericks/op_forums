                                Originally posted by NineDaysDead View Post

                                I went back and re-read all your posts. As near as I could find, you did not specify living animals. But as for living animals, I suppose if a vampire owns cattle, they can safely feed from that cattle all they like. Still doesn't explain how you get every non-Citizen vampire in a city to own their own cattle, either in a ranch or a slaughterhouse. I live in a city of 100,000 that's pretty much defined by its cattle industry (seriously, we're nicknamed Cow Town in local news, and you can smell the stench of either cow or meat processing over the entire city when the wind blows right), and we still only have one slaughterhouse.                                 In the interest of completeness, I looked up some statistics of my local slaughterhouse.

                                It processes up to 5,500 cattle per day. Frankly, this was a somewhat life-changing number to learn. Took a while to wrap my head around the scale. Therefore, even if each cow only produced one Vitae, I will admit that this is easily enough to feed all the vampires in a city.

                                            There is a certain amount of suspension of disbelief available when running a game, so I wouldn't worry about ratios. I would focus less on feasibility and more on "what stories does this idea allow me to tell?"

                                            If the player go all "WTF. There would be a rebellion" then stroke your chin, hrm loudly, nod, and say "Yes. You should get on that."

                                            In terms of Framing, I'd have The Primogen, a council of 5 Elders that have the for realzies power. Then I'd have ~25 of the "Haves" with a "Prince" chosen from their ranks that is technically in charge. Then have some number of the "Have Nots" (anywhere from 50 to 125). I would also note that strict feeding rules are probably meant to address the overpopulation problem as much as the "Malleus Malefcarum is in our back yard" problem.

                                            Nearby slaughterhouses or owned animals become excellent examples of the Herd merit and prime real-estate for elders to own and rent out. As do animal shelters and other facilities that collect stray animals.

                                            I would allow the "Haves" to temporarily extend feeding rights to the have nots. Make this an affair if it comes up. Maybe dope the mortal up on some narcotics to make it feel even better. When you get to this scene, it's going to do a lot to set the tone of your game, so you want to do it right.

                                            I would allow the "Have nots" to be allowed to feed from vampires freely, especially each other.

                                            In terms of enforcing... turning in your peer gets you feeding rights for a month. I mean if you want it to be hard enforcement, just use Oaths, but I'd recommend against it. Underground and black-market mortal trading can definately be a thing.

                                            You really want to decide how you want to handle Ghouls, since it's a work around. Ultimately a 2-3 Ghouls can keep a Vampire running at minimum fed indefinitely, until something goes wrong. I'd make a really huge deal to be allowed to make one, and having a Ghoul being a huge status symbol (probably Have or "just about to be a Have" or "Performed some legendary service" only.)

                                            A key to "soft" enforcement, which is what I'd recommend, is detection. Have Mekhet be the prominent enforcers.
