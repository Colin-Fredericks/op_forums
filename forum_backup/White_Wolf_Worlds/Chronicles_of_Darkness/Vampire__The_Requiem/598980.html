              Put your NPCs with mutually exclusive goals. Bishop Birch wants to have everyone go to the Sanctum mass. Hierophant Mahala has quite a few problems with that. Carthians want to establish a better life in the slum neighbourhood, so that it's easier for them to feed, but the Invictus lords want to promote their own businesses, which means they must keep abusing the neighbourhood. And this is just a very big view of covenants. Ancillae Johnson has the neighborhood to himself for the past ten years. Neonate Tom's touchstone and still living mother is living in the neighbourhood. She's also stubborn as all hell in her desire to keep living in his house. Johnson wants to keep his lands, Tom wants to keep his mom safe and accessible to him. Johnson forbids other vampires from entering his domain.

              You can't have tidy if different people want different mutually exclusive things. One of them get's what they want, the other is plotting how to get what he wants and so forever the Danse Macabre spins.

              Couple that with the fact that NPCs don't know everything and chaos will ensue.

                      For inspiration you might try looking up some Noir fiction, or even just the tropes associated with it. I'd also say stop trying to make things characters do always "make sense", instead give the character an irrational flaw (extremely greedy, bigoted against a certain clan, selfish to a fault, etc.) then keep their actions consistently "in character". You can invent something in the character's history to justify their flaw (came from poverty, a Gangrel killed their sire, etc.) but you can assign the flaw arbitrarily.

                              The thing to also keep in mind is that large-scale events don't just happen. Let me give you an example from a chronicle I'm currently running.
                              Alecto, in Athens, is governed by a secretive cabal of elders (few know of their existence): the Inner Circle (a group of Ventrue that survived, not only the purge, but even greater disasters in Athenian history). They want to discredit Ypochreosi to the point that the Kindred of Athens demand that a tyrant seizes power to protect them and the city's prosperity.

                              To do this, Alecto slowly creeps in with the local media and financial corporations. On the larger scale of things, they do this through the powerful mortal capitalist dynasties they control. A firm with innovative security solutions (including facial and GPS tracking) is acquired by the Bobolas conglomerate (traditionally involved in media). If the Sheriff or the secret police of Ypochreosi are clever enough, they'll start snooping around the takeover.

                              On a smaller scale, a journalist known for his network of whistleblowers and anti-corruption investigative work, goes missing. This is a small but crucial step in someone's grand plot. Then there's that trespassing incident in the Pendeli underground government facilities. Supposedly, they've been abandoned since the 1980s. Why is everyone freaking out, then? Perhaps because a certain valuable relic belonging to Aegis kai Doru (whose armory and reliquary is located beneath Pendeli Mountain) was stolen during the break-in. Urban explorers? Sure, if you believe the mainstream media, now controlled by Alecto.
