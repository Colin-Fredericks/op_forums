                            Originally posted by DarkKing View Post
                            What's up with the Ordo Dracul? I've been told that in second edition they went from trying to overcome the weaknesses of vampirism, to instead focus on becoming the perfect monster?
                            Some people may have taken the functional benefits of some of the Coils of the Dragon in 1e as an indication that they were the most ethical and/or humane covenant, a perspective not helped by the fact that the Coil of Blood immediately cutting out the logistics issue behind extracting Vitae dealing lethal damage and feeding restrictions being hard limits and the Coil of the Beast allowing the character to simply Stop a frenzy by spending Willpower if they failed to resist it normally, which game the impression that, in the absence of purely narrative safeguards, the covenant who takes Dracula as their role model in the pursuit of having the advantages of vampirism without the drawbacks would allow dabblers in their mysteries to learn a few useful tricks for pretending they aren't still vampires.

                            In 2e, by contrast, the first tier of the Coil of the Wyrm (replacing the Coil of the Beast) allows you to spend Willpower to voluntarily enter frenzy toward a specific goal (which can be used to avoid an involuntary frenzy) and enhances the benefits of being in frenzy, the Coil of the Ascendant (replacing the Coil of Banes) roots its enhanced daytime activity in the blush of life, and instead of the Coil of Blood messing with Vitae efficiency, the Coil of the Voivode tweaks how Vitae addiction, blood bonds, blood sympathy, and the Embrace work for the Dragon. In addition, Coils are part of Mysteries that each Dragon can only specialize in one of and needs to increase their Covenant Status to learn higher-level Coils, and each Coil has procedures called Scales that allow the Dragon to share aspects of their (possibly altered) vampiric condition with others in ways that risk their Humanity to research but can be learned without the prerequisite Coil rating and can accomplish things like reversing a vampire's sleep-wake cycle, removing blood bonds, or imbuing Physical Intensity into a consumable solution that enhances a mortal's physical abilities by the same amount for a longer time.

                            Retaining one's humanity is not the goal of the Ordo, and though there is some argument within the covenant about what the end target of transcending their limits is it's sufficiently clearly not just "turn off all the downsides of vampirism and chill as a normal human" that the Coil based around increasing your effective Humanity and lessening the effects of your Humanity-scaled banes is considered heretical. They're not trying to cure vampirism, they're trying to refine it, most notably by experimentation and exposure to supernatural phenomena that mortals tend to avoid.

                                    Originally posted by DarkKing View Post

                                    1 Vitae per [Resolve] nights, which at least doubled the value per Vitae spent on waking up. The fact that the 1e covenant sourcebook introduced a term in its lexicon and a related sidebar for the feedback loop produced by two vampires with the "feeding on supernatural vessels gives you double the normal Vitae" level of the Coil suggests maintaining Vitae scarcity without extraordinary methods or dedicated resources was not something they wanted to maintain in 2e.

                                    But overcoming the weaknesses of vampirism doesn't mean you become a normal human.
                                    No, but it sure makes it easier to pretend, which raises the question for a certain subset of readers of "why don't they?" in a game whose central mechanics included pretending to be a normal human at least well enough to discourage serious investigation.

                                    Even in 1E, they weren't presented as that being their goal. They wanted to evolve to a higher state, keeping all the benefits of being a vampire, with few or none of the drawbacks.
                                    For which again see "Some people may have taken the functional benefits of the Coils as an indication that they were the most ethical and/or humane covenant" despite these being the folks who poke around haunted houses and have a dedicated faction whose practices include doing things to their bodies that mortals would not survive.

                                    The books aren't obligated to address egregious misreadings, but it certainly helps to be up-front when presenting a new edition whose focal goals include specifically demonstrating the gameline's specific angle of monstrosity and presenting its own take on vampirism that isn't self-consciously stepping out of the shadow of Masquerade.

                                    But from what you describe, it sounds like they turned the Tzimisce into their own faction, minus the flesh crafting aspect.
                                    I'm not going to pretend I have the Masquerade expertise to know what gave you this idea, but I recognize a reductive reading when I see one.

                                    The Ordo studies the vampiric condition and the supernatural more generally, especially where the two overlap. They meditate and perform geomancy on Wyrm's Nests, which is the generalized term they use for any sort of supernatural real estate. They study the particulars of how their Disciplines manifest. They impale themselves and experiment on others with procedures that would be called torture by many standards. They have an institutional mandate against offering subservience to gods and spirits and ghosts as part of their goal of self-refinement as one of the more naturally bastardly monsters in the setting.
