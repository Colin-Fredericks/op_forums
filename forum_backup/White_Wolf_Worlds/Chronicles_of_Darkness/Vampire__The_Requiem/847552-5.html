                    Having character goals and backstories does come naturally. Codifying and setting those thing in stone (or just pencil) is the problem. Not using aspirations doesn't mean the PCs have no goals, it means nobody writes the goals down.

                    Like, let's say I need to go to the bathroom. I don't first declare out loud "I need to go to the bathroom" before I go. I just do it. They don't codify their goals and aspirations, but they still pursue them. And it is obvious what their goals are based on the actions they take in the game. So, I don't need it written on a sheet to see that they want to find a haven. When I say, "Ok, what do you do tonight?" and they say, "I start looking for a Haven by..." I am made well aware of what their goals are.

                    It's not having goals that is a problem. It's not having a personality type that bothers us. It's not having people you turn to in a crisis that's an issue. It's mechanizing those things. It's making them something you write down ahead of time, then get rewarded for following through with.

                    I don't understand why this is so hard to grasp. Let me try an example. One of the characters in the group is snarky. She just is. She doesn't have the word "Snarky" written down anywhere because she doesn't need it written down to remember that she's snarky. She doesn't need me to reward her when she remembers to be snarky because it's not something she could forget. She just is snarky. That's it. Simple.

                    Last session, the PCs wanted to investigate some children that went missing from their territory, just in case it was a vampire poaching from them (and breaking the law, since feeding from children is illegal in this city). Nobody had to write down on the sheet, "Find missing kids." They just went and did that. I said, "What do you guys do?" and they described how they started investigating. Specifically, in this case, they went to local parks near where the majority of the kids were last seen. At no point did anyone require a meta-reward to do this. They just did it.

                    But see, leaving that reward in there could have led to problems. What if, at the beginning of the session, they had written down "Find missing kids" only to discover during the session that there was a more pressing problem (just call it "Problem X"). Now, there's a conflict of interest. Do they stick to what they decided before the game started for the reward, or do this other thing that's more important to the character, but carries no reward? There's no reason to create this cognitive dissonance. Just remove the reward and they will do what their characters want without any carrot/stick.

                                Other mechanical ideas for tweaking the Ventrue Bane sans Touchstones I had today while stuck in traffic.
                                • Allow characters to spend Willpower on Detachment to gain the (+2) bonus they would normally get from having a touchstone. Then you can hit the Ventrue with the (-2) penalty to make it a little more robust at base, and they can still spend WP to break even.
                                • Require Ventrue to check for Detachment on Breaking Points at one above their humanity. In otherwords, a Humanity 6 Ventrue still has to roll for Breaking Points on the chart at level 7.
