    Okay, okay! Let me ask another question

    In 1E, there's Humanity, right? And some of it's basic sins are similar to mortal Morality. If so, then... how come that most vampires aren't extremely low on it?                 Well, by the rules, learning Cruac is what gives you the Humanity cap. Some practitioners might encounter Breaking Points as a result of things they do as part of the rituals, and they might be able to regain that lost Humanity when they walk away. But you don't have do perform a single ritual to get the cap, you just have to buy the dots, and there's no way to get rid of that cap by the rules. Of course, you can always make some kind of quest for redeption into the focus of a Story and raise or remove the cap through ST fiat, but I was talking about the standard Kindred experience.

                Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                Okay, okay! Let me ask another question

                In 1E, there's Humanity, right? And some of it's basic sins are similar to mortal Morality. If so, then... how come that most vampires aren't extremely low on it? I mean: feeding might be considered a planned, serious crime akin to murder. Wouldn't that cause quick Humanity degeneration to all vampires..?
                Well, most vampires who live a long time do end up with pretty low Humanity. A lot of things that vampires naturally do causes Degeneration/Breaking Points, even more so in 2e where the scale is based less on Morality and more on doing inhuman things. This is by design; vampires are not a human, so maintaining their Humanity is naturally an uphill battle.

                As others have pointed out, most vampires don't feed their victims dry, so it's usually not on the same level as murder. That said, feeding can be (and often is) a Degeneration check or a Breaking Point, the level just depends on how you go about it. But ultimately, you will lose Humanity eventually, it's in your nature as a monster. That's a big part of the personal horror of the setting.
