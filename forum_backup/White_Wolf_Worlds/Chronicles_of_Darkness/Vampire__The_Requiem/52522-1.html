                            Not the way I understand it. The Masquerade is the whole collective effort of the vampires to not let mortals know vampires are all around them. The way I see it, The mask isn't just the face a vampire shows humans, it's the part of themselves they consciously or unconsciously show everyone, vampire or human. The Dirge would be what they keep for themselves or what they only allow themselves to be around people they trust. Regaining willpower from your mask is just a matter of how mcuh you're willing to risk to keep people from seeing beyond what you want them to see. Like, if a vampire has mask : mastermind, he'll get his kicks from people seeing him as a clever dude who's really difficult to outsmart. If one of his plans start to completely fall appart, he might regain all his willpower if he risks his unlife doing damage control and tying up loose ends so that nobody ever knows he messed up.

                            People might disagree on the exact meaning, but I'm pretty sure it's not about the masquerade. One of my player's character has the Mask : Harmless and the Dirge : Vindictive. She's a former vampire hunter turned vampire herself. She tries to appear pretty harmless to everyone she meets but has a pretty mean streak when she feels people have crossed her, including (in my game, mostly) vampires. She's mostly gained willpower by being vindictive so far, but if she, for example, let herself get beat up by someone without really trying to fight back, she'd regain willpower because she wouldn't look like a threat.

                            This is an example, but the mask and dirge don't have to be opposite or really different. Again, though, I wouldn't be too attached to that. These are two ways to see your character, but any believable character won't be described by just two words.

                            This is beside the point, but I think it's also pretty normal for character to change their mask or dirge occasionnally if it's justified. One of my players who had the dirge "humanist" (she would always try to favor humans, seeing vampires as monsters that needed to be killed if they didn't try really hard to not feed on humans) asked to change it after she found out about the Strix, how they were potentially way worse than the vampires and that vampires were maybe one of the few things that had a shot at fighting them.

                                Originally posted by S. Brechen View Post
                                Not the way I understand it. The Masquerade is the whole collective effort of the vampires to not let mortals know vampires are all around them.
                                Eeeyyyyhhhh, that's kind of an outdated concept of the Masquerade at this point. That's certainly what it means in VtM, and 1e kind of held onto it, but 2e has really doubled down on the idea that the supernatural doesn't need a massive concerted effort to keep itself secret. Humanity's tendency to ignore and deny things outside their experience does most of the work on its own. One's Masquerade is the personal effort a vampire makes to maintain a facade of humanity. The Julii claim credit for inventing the concept, and whether or not they were really the first vampires to pretend to be human, they certainly played a large role in popularizing the practice. The closest you'll really see to The Masquerade as a conspiracy rather than an individual practice in 2e is the re-envisioned Invictus, who style themselves the "Conspiracy of Silence" and work to cover up vampiric influence (and in particular, Invictus influence) in human power structures. This may all seem rather tangential to the subect at hand, but it serves an important role in properly framing the question of "Does Masque = Masquerade?" Heck, during open development Masque and Dirge were called Masquerade and Requiem respectively. I still think those would have been better names for the Traits, but ultimately they were changed to avoid confusion, since those words do refer to broader concepts than the Traits alone (and, you know, the two vampire gamelines). But the Traits are tied to those broader concepts, definitely.

                                Originally posted by S. Brechen View Post
                                The way I see it, The mask isn't just the face a vampire shows humans, it's the part of themselves they consciously or unconsciously show everyone, vampire or human.                                 Agreed. The Masque isn't just the face you wear among humans, it's the face you wear no matter who you are interacting with. I think the same is true with The Masquerade - it's not merely an effort one makes to keep one's vampiric nature a secret, it's the effort one makes to appear human. And if you think about how vampires behave among themselves, they're still trying to keep up that veneer of humanity. Sure, they don't waste effort trying to hide the fact that they're undead and that they drink blood. But they have laws forbidding the use of Disciplines against each other, or attacking each other in fits of Frenzy. They make just as much effort to control their Beasts among other Kindred as they do among Kine, if not more. They construct and engage in elaborate social structures that are as removed as you can get from their true, Bestial natures. They want to convince each other (and ultimately, themselves) that they are basically just humans suffering from an unfortunate condition that makes them highly averse to sunlight and require regular blood transfusions. So, the Masquerade is just as much a practice you engage in among other vampires as it is one you use to keep your secret safe from the muggles. Accordingly, the Masque is the persona you present to the world to distract it from who you really are, your Dirge.

                                That sounds like a fun character, and a great example of how Masque and Dirge can interact. Don't get me wrong here, I think we're ultimately agreeing on what Masque and Dirge represent and how they are meant to be used. We're just disagreeing on what the concept of Masquerade means to vampires, and I would argue that one's Masque plays a big role in her Masquerade.

                                Originally posted by S. Brechen View Post
                                This is an example, but the mask and dirge don't have to be opposite or really different. Again, though, I wouldn't be too attached to that. These are two ways to see your character, but any believable character won't be described by just two words.
                                I agree for the most part, although I think it's important that they not be exactly the same. This is where Masque and Dirge differ from Requem's Nature and Demeanor. Your Masque is the side you want people to see, as well as the person you want to believe yourself to be. Dirge is who you let yourself be when you think no one is watching, it's the unlife you lead that you wouldn't want people to find out about, the reason you participate in The Masquerade in the first place. If they were exactly the same (apart from giving you effectively half as many ways to regain Willpower), you would have no reason to take part in the Masquerade. You could simply present yourself as you truly are to everyone around you, vampire and mortal alike.
