    The Ordo Dracul in 2E

    05-23-2015, 04:23 PM
    So, in the thread Non-Super Evil VtR2 I posted this:

    Originally posted by ErikModi View Post
    Requiem 2E really stepped full-throttle on the "wholly evil" aspect, which was a bit of a turn-off for me. A lot of the new powers, especially Covenant-related, seem to revolve around varying levels of human sacrifice (I honestly don't think I read a single Scale that didn't involve leaving a human dead or severely injured, for just a temporary effect). Really clashes with the semi-optimistic view of Vampires I have in my Chronicle. I mean, yeah, a lot of them are bastards, but a sizeable group are just doing the best they can (or at least want to be seen that way.) Yeah, my Gotham City is run by a Mafia-styled Invictus who keeps the bad people rich and the good people scared to stay in power, but there's a Carthian Movement that's trying to take them down to restore some semblance of freedom and justice. The Carthians in my Minneapolis have a council with representatives from the other supernatural groups, specifically so everyone can try and co-operate, or at least not step on each others' toes. The Invictus of St. Paul aren't really bad, they're just Invictus.

    Thing is, any of the Covenants can really be portrayed as good or bad, depending on your own prejudices and how you want to run the story. Are the Lancea Sanctum really a good vampire church, offering meaning and comfort in an unlife of Damnation? Or are they complete religious zealots, crusading against anything that offends their doctrine and putting all nonbelievers (breathing or not) to the sword? Are the Circle of the Crone just vampiric pagans, seeking to get closer to the truth of nature? Or are they worshipers of dark, possibly demonic spirits that demand tribute in blood? Are the Ordo Dracul learned scholars, delving into the mysteries of the vampiric condition, or are they monsters ascendant, overcoming the few weaknesses that can slow unstoppable juggernauts of blood and death? Do the Invictus represent order and stability, or control and stagnation? Do the Carthians represent freedom and democracy, or anarchy and chaos? It's all in how you choose to interpret them.

    Which I guess is my biggest gripe with a lot of the new mechanics. Playing the Ordo Dracul as learned scholars seeking vampiric enlightenment is a tad difficult when most of their powers stem from research that would make Dr. Mengle say "Dude, that's fucked up."
    A few people posted in response to say that I was being a bit uncharitable. So, I took another look at the 2E book and. . . well, no, I don't think I am.

    The Ordo really seems to be a bunch of really sick bastards in 2E now. I'd like to share a few things that give me this impression:

    First, in Mysteries of the Dragon, the last line of the first paragraph: "They call the results, refined through rigorous self-control and clear-minded study." Sounds good so far.

    Then they start talking about Scales, where it says "But these procedures can be brutal, tantamount to torture, and carry side effects both physical and psychological." Uh, okay. . . so, the Ordo's "clear-minded research" leads to torturing people?

    Again, talking about Scales of the Ascendant, there's the line "Direct exposure to sunlight and fire, sleep deprivation, and medically induced death are common practices." Oboy. Now, granted, I misread a lot of these statements about Scales as generic research methods in the Coils, and am realizing now they're intended to be two separate things. . . but still. As for the Scales of the Ascendant themselves, Day-Wake conditioning isn't that bad, but Epidermal Shielding Bath requires "blood and pulped human fat." Now, while you certainly might be able to get those ingredients without killing someone, seems more like the expected route is through, shall we say, human resources. Likewise with Flesh Graft Treatment, requiring (presumably, though never stated as such) live human skin and (explicitly stated) fresh human blood.

    Scales of the Wyrm are stated to be "savage, even by Ordo standards; subjects are dissected," which sounds plenty evil to me. Augmented Vitae Draught isn't so bad, again, although it could kill a mortal with the aftereffects. Kindred Sense Endowment requires "blood and bone," and while the source is never specifically stated, by this point, I think we're all again thinking "human resources." Oh, it also makes a mortal want to drink blood. Groovy. Surgical Heart Removal is. . . actually pretty cool.

    Scales of Voivode specifically calls out "Initiates of the Mystery commit psychological torture." Which, honestly, makes them sound like Initiates of the other Mysteries, except they do it en route to purposes while for these guys, it is the purpose. Blood Cleansing Ritual is again called out as "tantamount to torture," but since it "cures" Blood Bonds, I'll give that one a pass. Fealty's Reward is basically straight-up human sacrifice, except the human doesn't stay quite dead. They may become a Draugr, though. Mass Embrace is just terrifying, probably more so for the person doing it. Sanguinary Invigoration. . . again, actually pretty cool.

    But, really, a lot of this seems to say, with brutal clarity, that Dragons are really nasty people who do really nasty things, and how do they maintain a Humanity over one?

    Now, I really do like the Ordo Dracul, and I want to keep liking them in 2E, but with the way Scales are advanced, I find that difficult. I understand that you don't necessarily have to learn or use Scales, but that they are there, and not specifically called out as being the "dark underside" of Coils, leads me to feel the Dragons are characterized less as potentially noble scholars, and more as bloodthirsty butchers using science as an excuse.

    I'd really like to be proven wrong.

                                          My impression of the Ordo Dracul is mostly informed by the 1e information on them, so let's be clear about that potential bias up front.

                                          One of the complaints I'd seen regarding the OD is that they weren't really "mad scientist" enough. There was discussion of their varying methodologies in the covenant book, but there was nothing to back up what that becomes in terms of playing the game, and putting that research to use. Except for the Coils, sure, but those are long term, and low-impact in the sense of having a character trait that you then put to use. The 1e Coils were almost uniformly passive. That doesn't scream vampire scientist tinkering with the workings of the very Curse. There were a couple merits that were mechanical misfits (it eventually came out that the merits relating to the Impaled were from an older draft of the rules, or some such), and the rules for Crucible Nests were neat, but it was missing something, mechanically, to capture that vampiric, transcendent scientist feel. The Mind of the Devouring Wyrm merit chain was excellent, though. I loved that one for the introspective, scientific, almost mentat-like quality the Ordo Dracul could invoke.

                                          As far as flavor, there were definitely some inhuman elements intrinsic to the OD even then, see Chasing the Dragon's Tail, and any Fontal nest ritual. But overall it, like all the covenants, has room for whatever type of character you want to play. Diverse motivations fit under the same general "goal" umbrella.

                                          Take that as context for what I saw when I first skimmed the drafts at Gen Con, and then read in depth when Blood and Smoke/2e released. Suddenly the majority of the fluff, description, and night-to-night workings of the OD were the same (mostly), but the mechanics now have this incredible, dark science, active component in the Scales of the Dragon. You can absolutely go full Mengele with it, and the description, as you've quoted, warns that the Scales are grisly business. The 2e write up of the Ordo Dracul is certainly dark. But, I read that as keeping in line with the tone of the game itself. The Ordo Dracul are about results in the general sense, but how far a given Dragon is willing to go to get those results will vary. Just like what any given Invictus will do to rise in the ranks.

                                          I suppose I read the Scales as another facet of what the Dragons do, but not necessarily the sum total. You could have a squared-away, scientific Dragon who insists on informed consent for his subjects, and gathers materials from willing donors or via medical waste or cadavers as Leetespeak suggested. Or... ya know... Mengele.

                                          My early experiences with Ordo Dracul players were of mitigating the horrific demands the Curse placed upon them, mostly for reasons of morality, even in the face of a more utilitarian view of the covenant at large. I enjoy the juxtaposition and challenge of trying to maintain Humanity, and being expected to murder a person as an object lesson in observation and unintended consequences.

                                          Does that make sense at all? Having a hard time pinning down exactly where my impressions are coming from. This is an interesting exercise.

                                          Edit: Oh, one other thing. I think my personal impression is that all the covenants are basically "evil," if you want to boil it down. I think it's an oversimplification, but they're large groups of vampires. In my estimation they start in moral debt and have to work their way out (again, oversimplification, but I hope my point is coming across). Many don't even try. Many give up after a while. Some manage to stay the course, in their various ways.

                                                          I don't believe applying human ethical standards to undead monsters who generally wish to transcend their condition without actually becoming human is a particularly productive exercise.

                                                          Simply, the Ordo are not immoral "evil scientists." They are, however, frighteningly amoral inhuman scientists, who follow a insular, selfish and egocentric philosophy which largely supports the ends justifying the means. The Ordo don't develop and use Scales because they needlessly cause suffering and death. They employ them because in the horror setting that is the nWOD, these gruesome Scales are what actually accomplishes the vampires' goals. The Scales, no matter how unpleasant, work.

                                                          I also hardly find it surprising that Ordo (and most other) vampires would have no compunction against experimenting on or sacrificing the human sheep that serve as little more than their food. By way of comparison, look at what humans are willing to do to various laboratory animals in the name of science for extremely laudable goals. Scientists don't infect animals with disease, observe and then dissect them because the scientists are cruel, they do so because there's nothing more efficient and effective in preventing and treating disease in humans. Of course, suffering is minimized whenever possible, but the research and experiments continue, with objections raised only from only a tiny minority.

                                                          Nevertheless, the Ordo's experiments could certainly take their psychological toll on the participating vampires. The Humanity stat is a reflection of this effect.
