    I... think you might have 1e Humanity in mind here.

    In 2e the higher the Humanity the PC is, the more often they're going to roll. Watching people eat is an example of a high Humanity detachment check, which is going to happen a lot if you're walking down an average city street. The frequency of human contact necessary to upkeep high humanity is very high. Low Humanity detachment checks are linked to very rare events, or very purposeful actions.

    The Coil of Ziva is very useful for mid-range Humanity vampires that want to be able to use their Disciplines and powers for "good" without detaching from the fact that they have supernatural powers fueled by drinking blood. If you want to use your vampire powers to be Batman, Ziva is going to make that a lot easier on you.

            Originally posted by Iguazu View Post
            I guess a, "Good-vampire", is one that survives as a vampire and ends it's unlife at its choosing.
            That's a pretty fatalistic reading.

            A vampire is certainly a source of strife and harm in their own right, and the odds of a vampire degrading into dragur-dom over a prospective infinity is almost certainly a one-but that's a lot of space of time between the Embrace and the end their humanity, and in that time there's a lot of room to commit their powers and resources to doing good, whatever "doing good" means to them. A Nosferatu or Gangrel is a more effective intercessor against an asshole landlord than the average tenant, and the same powers that lets the Daeva and Ventrue play with hearts and minds for a meal can also sway mortal authorities towards policies and actions that serves their neighbors.

            The question is if a person who is a vampire can do enough to justify it night by night, or can be comfortable with the means they use to get to their ends. Reconciliation with that asides, though, a vampire could get up to a lot of good in their unlife.

                Yeah, there's no Salubri, and the Carthian/Brujah similarities can be a little shaky at times, but it's certainly possible to do a messy kind of good with your gifts. You'll have to struggle to retain your Humanity and avoid giving into the Beast (or the Other, if your group decides to use that). It's hard, you're never going to be sure if you're doing the right thing, and maybe it's better to chain yourself to a wall and see a sunrise, but you can potentially be a decent person.

                Although the idea of using Majesty and Dominate to 'do good' is utterly terrifying, and probably a great starting point for a Deava antagonist.

                There's no such thing as a good vampire. But it's possible for a vampire to be a good person, even with the deck stacked against them. It might seem like a strange distinction to make, but oWoD having vampire Paladins, even if extinct, is a bit weird. Then again Masquerade is a lot more weird than Requiem, so they fit well enough for me to make them work for me (the Tremere purge wasn't complete enough to stop them eventually becoming a key part of the Sabbat, they very explicitly have always ranged from vampiric saints to thugs, warriors are the majority caste, and they're all consentualists).

                    Originally posted by TwoDSix View Post
                    There's no such thing as a good vampire. But it's possible for a vampire to be a good person, even with the deck stacked against them. It might seem like a strange distinction to make, but oWoD having vampire Paladins, even if extinct, is a bit weird. Then again Masquerade is a lot more weird than Requiem, so they fit well enough for me to make them work for me (the Tremere purge wasn't complete enough to stop them eventually becoming a key part of the Sabbat, they very explicitly have always ranged from vampiric saints to thugs, warriors are the majority caste, and they're all consentualists).
                    Within my usual refrain of "It's important to weigh monsters within the context of their circumstances, but to not forget that they used to be human too", the "No Good Vampire" argument feels a little weighted in judging vampires as you would a human and not giving them the due of being a vampire. There is, to a certain extent, an unfairness in doing so-an unfairness that can vary based on how up to the game a person was when they became a vampire*, but an unfairness all the same. THe Kindred play with the hand they're dealt, and it's important to keep that context in mind when approaching the subjects of morality and ethics as explored by vampires, and monsters in general.

                    Of course, it's also important that most monsters, with the exception of demons who have a semi-opposite problem, is that they had experience as humans, and it is fair to weigh that ground for empathy with the people that they will be some kind of strife and harm to against them as well.

                    *There's a difference between a vampire who received the Embrace as a curse imparted by the sire, and a vampire who received the Embrace as their everything they ever'd, of course.
