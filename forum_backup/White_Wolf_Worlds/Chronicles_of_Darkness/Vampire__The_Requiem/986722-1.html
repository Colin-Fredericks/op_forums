                That's what the ministry of sleep and it's Mausoleum are there for. Obviously, there is also a police force controlled by the ministry of interior, as well as a prison (vampires in there are only fed in their private cell by a small tap before sunrise in so they can rise the next night so they are bloodless for most of the time, and falling into voluntary torpor only suspends your time, you get back in there when you wake up, diablerie is common in the prison, but those who become specialists of it usually keep doing it and don't come out at all). The vast majority of vampires are BP 1 to 3 (with the feeding scale being pushed back on rank) and the rest either have special privilege or are criminals. Feeding on another vampire is not technically illegal, although it is frowned upon (like in many domains where powerful vampires will sometimes go to sleep in order to avoid a disaster), but assaulting another for blood is. There is always disciplines to hide that, but one way or another, people notice when they're missing vitae and liberal use of disciplines like dominate is obviously mostly illegal. Obviously, if the vampires collectively decide to fuck shit up, it is going go collapse, but i's not like the only thing refraining them is their being reasonable.

                Most vampires also spend about 15 minutes doing a routine every night after waking up. It involves a composure+one skill the character has above 3 and is a sort of meditation. Some do katas, most do more mundane things like yoga exercises, reciting a content from a medical treaty, etc. Not doing it causes a small penalty on frenzy rolls because the setting is just bad for vampire minds There are stickers and posters in public places that read stuff like "Have you done your waking routine ? It is important for you to keep a clear mind at all times."

                Look, at some point, I asked myself : Do I want to give my players a noir cyberpunk game in a prison City-State with only vampires. My answer was fuck yes. The next question was how could I somehow justify that.

                            They get willpower from sleeping. It's how it was in 1ed if I remember, and I liked it just fine. Dirge and Mask are also fine as long as they're not "I'm a complete monster and I get my kicks from beating people up". I just consider the majority of active vampires in the world are relatively recent, twenty years or less so they're mostly regular humans who are still getting used to what they are. A good number never asked for any of this and just want to be left alone.

                            I don't see why they'd have to frenzy all the time. They are fed. Animal blood, but they are fed and they usually try not to do stupid things and use their powers which would lead to them running out of vitae and becoming starved. They're rarely maxed out, but also rarely at a dangerous level. There's basically a system of ID linked food coupons. Everyone has the right to an amount of vitae that's enough to wake up and regenerate the occasional wound because they hit their small toe in the door frame. Getting more means having a job which gives you a certain amount per month that you can go "collect" in the various farms. I mean, yeah, vampires are violent creatures, but they're not irrational, and most don't want to die, so they rarely go out of their way to do things that will make them starve. They're also aware their neighbours are just as dangerous as they are and there are things they shouldn't do unless they want to risk dying. Add to that a government that's close to a police state. Add to that the various philosophies. The Invictus likes to have a feudal system and a well organized society, and people starving and frenzying left and right would end up making for a pretty lonely rule. The Lancea Sanctum is split and generally depressed, but it's still pretty against killing other kindred in general so they tend to insist on that when they can. The Carthians, I guess it depends on how you interpret them, but they want a working society, and while the Circle could find it fun to have a giant frenzy party, they also want to get out eventually, so everyone dying would not make it easier. Obviously, it's not sustainable very long term, it would probably collapse before it lasts a hundred years, probably even fifty, but the campaign will be over before that.

                            Finally, maybe I'm nicer with frenzy rules than most GMs. I don't ask for frenzy rolls very often. Mostly, it's when the players just spent a lot of blood and go to refill at a farm. They start drinking the cow's blood, roll, fail, then couple guards jump on them and hold them on the ground until they calm down (one PC is getting a reputation for that now). I don't generally ask for an anger frenzy roll unless it's really personal, and fear frenzy is mostly fire and sunlight, and predictably, they tend not to see a whole lot of those. One PC had a fear frenzy when they went to a Circle ceremony and someone busted out a torch to test the crowd's spirit.

                                        I see the numerous flaws on the setting, but no reason to point them out, since the end is the setting itself. By myself, I would play it gladly, but mainly to figure a way out. That would be fun.

                                        On my take on the proposition, things had a lot of ramifications, but changed less than most people maybe think. Mortal society was shocked and confused at first, it was a great turmoil. But to be frank, mortal society is already in turmoil because of religion, sexuality, politics, economy, migration, etc.. Sooner or later it will get used and adapt, with extremists to both sides in the middle, as it get used to everything else in some way.

                                        So, in my game it was pure confusion for a couple of years until the first workable laws to deal with the vampires were issued. As a rule of thumb vampires were granted full civil rights in most countries, with some metaphysical discussions on the meaning of life in the meanwhile. As a rule, more severe actions against vampires were doomed by extreme costs and total ineffectivenes. A lot of vampires went hiding anyway for ease of unlife, and so most things actualy changed very little.

                                        Hunters didn't had that great time for long on most places. Even if knowing, most people don't have the strength of purpose to risk life against such creatures, so they numbered almost the same. It is also easier to voice against this to your authorities than to take things in one's own hands, so for each potential hunter that took to the Vigil because of the sudden knowledge, other actual hunter stepped down because didn't saw it as necessary anymore.

                                        A lot of hunters became members of actual official and well known special forces to take care of supernatural threats, and the old Conspiracies actualy took a harder blow for their methods whyle being substituted by more adapted ones (both legal and illegal). Curiously, as many as hunters, vampires and other supernatural things comprised the numbers of such forces, being as they are, the most apt to deal with criminals of their kinds. Other supernatural phenomena, not necessarily other splats, soon got out in the light since the first did it, as it became more easy to suspect, reason and accept such things now.

                                        The entire thing was fun, and very interesting. I'll probably run this again, but the most interesting for me was how much it didn't changed all that, but simply lessened the Masquerade focus.

                                                        But that's fine, they would just frenzy and kill each other because they're "starving". Look, there are times as a GM, where the potential stories you can get from a setting/scenario far outweigh the few tweaks and theoretical inconsistencies the premises cause. Besides, vampires wouldn't "actually" turn on each other, vampires don't exist, they can't actually do anything, we like playing them because they're an extreme representation of some of our own aspects as human beings. None of my players had any problem with the basic premises of the setting and we had a pretty good time playing in it. I'd thought I'd just share something I worked on and had fun with, so I don't see the point in continually jumping on it to nitpick the very same point over and over.



                                                        Again, for like the fourth time, they're not starved. For those vampires who "don't care to be nice to humans", chances are they just got killed or sent into torpor through damage. Task Force Valkyrie never expected everyone to turn themselves in anyway. Many vampires were destroyed at the time. A few really powerful ones escaped capture, most not so powerful ones could be captured, turned themselves in so they wouldn't be hunted, were shot/beaten to torpor and woke up inside the city. A bunch of mid range BP with enough power to think they could just escape easily and enough ego to try fought back or ran away for a while, generally to end up getting destroyed eventually.

                                                        Nobody expected the masquerade to go down so quickly, so nobody really had time to prepare. Some descriptions of the Invictus have it sort of hide in plain sight, so they didn't have their money in fifteen different banks, through five fake societies. The human governments relatively quickly identified the companies that were controlled by the Invictus, and seized a share.

                                                        The Ordo Dracul did give some problems, and their goals were soon known of the hunter orgs. For this reason, one of the rules the humans imposed on the city's government was that they had to outlaw the Ordo Dracul. Since it's the smallest covenant, and the others had no particular problem with that, Ordo Practice was banned. What that means is that you can't open an official Ordo Dracul lab in the City. Those who learned coils could keep them, but not be caught studying more or doing scales. A bunch of them became underground scientists for hire, and of course, the government has a secret program and keeps a number of Ordo Researchers who can keep working in exchange for their security.

                                                        The good thing about controling the Circle and the Lancea is that that they hate each other, so they're generally more busy mutually pointing fingers than working to topple the government and replace it with an entity that wouldn't last a week since it'd be a minority in the city. Their higher ups, the bishops, arch-bishops, cardinals, the high priestesses and priests have ties to the government. Some of the ministers are Lancea or Circle. The current government is composed of one Prince and twelve ministers, almost all of which belong to a covenant or have sympathies to that covenant. They managed to hit a balance point. The more hardline members of each covenant consider them traitors to be dealing with the enemies like that, but most realize that at least, that way, there is someone to negociate the humans sending cows so people don't starve. The Prince is pretty good at throwing everyone a bone to keep them satisfied enough that they won't go on a rampage.

                                                        The Lancea and the Circle could do rituals to mess with the government, and some did, but that has the risk of being identified because of the blood sympathy it creates, it takes effort in a city where you don't want to waste vitae (and you can't just go murder clubbing to refill your willpower). Plus, and that's important. The covenants aren't monolithic entities. They generally have a tendency to dislike their traditional rivals, but a Circle cult who thinks they're being smart by fucking with the government with rituals has a pretty good chance to have other Circle members who'd rather not start a civil war come and kick them in the teeth or call the police.

                                                        The Lancea Sanctum, for example, is very split. Basically, since they're locked up, they can't accomplish anything pertaining to their mission. Interpretations of their situation vary. Some see this as an obstacle to their mission and that they have to survive while looking for a way out, some figure they've failed somehow and this is their punishment, so they'll just have to take it until god decides to release them, some other figure that the reason they failed and were punished was because they didn't understand Longinus' message and so they need to go back to the drawing board and potentially come up with a new interpretation of the testament. Finally, there are those few who think that they have to keep straightening humanity, except if they can't do it directly, they'll have to show how monstrous they can be, so their plan is to cause as much chaos in the city so that everyone will either kill each other or the military will decide to nuke them.

                                                        The Circle has different views altogether, and it's even less of a coherent group from the start. I really like how they encourage creation and attract artists within kindred. A certain number of them, while wishing they could just get out, think this is actually a fascinating opportunity. Vampires are, at last, completely cut from humanity and only have each other to draw inspiration from. They're hoping that a form of society, expression and art uniquely kindred will emerge from what they see as sort of a giant crucible.
