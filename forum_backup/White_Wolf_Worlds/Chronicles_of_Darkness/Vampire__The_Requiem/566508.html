    The Three Heads of Kerberos Clarification

    06-24-2015, 12:07 PM
    Hello , this is actually my very first post here. I have recently started playing a Kerebos in my local game.

    My question broadly involves "Lashing Out", I know this is suppose to be a social taboo or something amongst Kindred. The Kerebos , as the masters of lashing out, get all sorts of advantages with The Three Heads of Kerberos merits but must constantly be breaking this social norm just to not suffer their bloodline bane.

    So my broad question is " Is there a subtle way to apply Predator's Aura to someone?"

    Secondly I am confused by the fourth level of the merit, the way it is written:

    Striking (••••): Your character infects with her Beast through other actions beyond lashing out. By spending a Willpower point, she can lash out reflexively, so long as she’s affecting the victim with something else simultaneously. Most commonly, this means slashing a victim with her claws while infecting them with the Beast. But it could mean using it while investigating them with Auspex, shooting them with a pistol, while entrancing them with Majesty, while feeding them her blood, while seductively touching them, feeding from them, or any other number of effects.

    Make seems that it is allowing you to lash out in addition to attacking/ using a discipline. But it also mentions "seductively touching" someone. Can not all Kindred do this, touching someone , in a non combative way, does not take an action does it? If so this makes sense but if not why bring it up? That combined with the wording at the beginning "Your character infects with her Beast through other actions beyond lashing out." makes me feel as if THIS is a way to inflict your aura on someone subtlety. My ST says it is not, but I feel it is.

    Am I completely wrong? Anyone who could clarify these questions for me would be a huge help to me. Thanks!

      The action of Lashing Out is normally an instant action, which means you can't lash out and do something else in the same turn. With the merit you can lash out as a reflexive action in conjunction with another instant action.

      Seducing someone requires a persuasion roll (usually extended). Mechanically you and your seduction target take adversarial roles to determine success/failure. Same as shooting someone.

      There's nothing subtle about lashing out. The other person knows you did 'something' to them and in the case of it being another vampire they know you flashed your Beast at them in order to incite a specific reaction from their own Beast.

                                                          I think the Predatory Aura suffers slightly from the text using two different interpretations. I can only assume it's a relic of 1e. It's stated that the perception is based on smell, touch, sight, and that it can be one-sided where one vampire perceives the other and not vice-versa. But then we also get talk about it being an aura that precludes civility between vampire, painting Kindred as wild animals barely reigned in by the rules and codes and Traditions they live by.

                                                          I mean, reading the section a few times over, it barely even seems supernatural at all. Only the fact that the conditions seem disproportional to the mundane actions performed gives any indication it's magic.

                                                          I mean, you shoot a flirty wink across the bar, and the target gets to either get flustered and bails or become more pliable to further flirting. It's a subtle power. The effects are well within the realm of mundane possibility. They only nudge the target, they don't force anything.

                                                          Aside from about two lines that seem to reference to 1e's "frenzy on sight" mechanic, it really seems like the Predatory Aura refers to how vampires move differently, smell differently, and look differently, rather than anything supernatural.

                                                          This leads to some confusion as to how one can intensify "breathing unnaturally shallow and smelling like mortal blood" into "make everyone want to sleep with me with a glance."

                                                          I suspect this is the root of the confusion between people who think it's subtle and people who think it can't be. The text seems a bit conflicted.

                                                          Of course, it also talks a lot about Lashing Out being the Beast itself. The text also seems a bit conflicted as to whether or not the Beast is a literal entity sharing a Vampire's head or a metaphor for the unique needs and desires that a bloodsucking parasite has.

                                                          Personally, I run it as both subtle and subtext. If you Lash Out and they turn it back on you, there's nothing more to say. They've established dominance. If you Lash Out and succeed, there's nothing more to say. You've established dominance. Vampires just don't talk about it, even when it happens. It doesn't really even have an IC name in the book (who's going to go "you lashed out at me!"?).

                                                          The sheer fact that you can lose and have it turned on you is enough punishment without really needing to make it taboo, in my mind. Might makes right in Vampire-land, and might doesn't just mean strength of arms. If you win, you win. If you lose, you lose. Both cases may or may not end up in allies getting called against you if you wronged someone in the process.
