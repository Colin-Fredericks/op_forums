    Vinculum as a Breaking Point

    04-20-2015, 09:31 PM
    I always found it odd that making a ghoul (and thus likely soon inflicting a vinculum) was a Humanity 8 sin. I know that the book doesn't say that the vinculum is Humanity 8 (can temporarily ghoul someone to save their life with only a one step blood bond) and I can pretty readily understand *why* Onyx Path didn't weigh in with a sin placement for a vinculum as it's a whole big can of worms. I'm just curious as to where other people would place it on their chart.

    Personally, I see vinculums as deeply corruptive things. They immediately place a wedge between a human and everyone around them as everything is shuffled down a rank. Their efforts to please their master is likely to ruin their marriage, leave their kids in trouble, etc. as they're hardly likely to (often) plead off seeing their regnant and doing tasks for them in order to go to their kid's concert and may get so wrapped up in the task that they forget to even pick their kids up from the After School Care. Naturally they'd never bother their regnant with such things, because if their regnant does care, it'll make them feel bad, and who'd want that?

    Besides all of that, it's also a deeply vampiric act that utterly changes a mortal - vampire's relationship in a way that utterly subverts the person's own individual instincts, beliefs and desires, in terms of the vampire, and causes the rest of their world to realign. This realignment may be in an utterly unexpected and hard to control direction, but it's there. The vampire basically gets to experience how an intrinsic part of their nature, their own blood, can turn someone else into a devoted slave, and that's got to be at least the same level as suffering massive damage that would hospitalise a human, if not lower.

    With a well-picked individual, especially with consent, it could be that little bit higher up the chart, but in my estimation, going to that honest cop and corrupting him to your goals absolutely (thus destroying everything he cherished about himself) or your friend who was starting to freak out and considered leaving (betraying them in an eternal trap) would be lower because there's something intrinsically vampiric, again, about negating another person's will permanently for your own desires.

    Especially as the blood is also addictive and so it's not like they can walk away from it just fine if you Forgetful Mind their memories away or die and leave them without a wrist to tap.

    On the other hand, the new devoted slave will attempt to convince the vampire that this was the best thing that happened to them, even if they don't quite believe it themselves, which is something you won't get from murdering a person.

    So yes, do you have it as a Breaking Point below 8? If not, why not? (I can see an argument that the slave's devotion is so absolute it can trick the vampire). If so, why so?

              9 sounds right. It's not really about how horrifying it is, but how inhuman it is. Ghouling is at 8 because it turns an ordinary mortal into a potentially immortal being with vampire powers that can sustain their existence on the blood of vampires, a pretty inhuman concept. However objectionable a thing a vinculum is, it's an idea that's been around in humanity's mind for a long time, and you can certainly have similarly twisted relationships without the benefit of magic vampire blood. So it makes sense that it would be higher than ghouling.

                      I mean, if you're used to objectifying humans, thinking of them as toys to be played with, then vinculum would seem perfectly natural. "Of course I can innately make them subservient to me, that's what they're there for".

                      It doesn't really matter that it's horrifying to you as a human, to a vampire it's likely going to become second nature very quickly. One of the first things you learn as a vampire is to dehumanize the living. You have to in order to survive, because they're your food. So right away, you're naturally going to get very comfortable with the idea that you can make them act like the sheep they are with just a small sip of your Vitae. As an everyday part of a vampire's unlife, Vinculum should definitely be a very high Humanity Breaking Point, because only the vampires who try very hard to be human are going to be as horrified by it as you are.

                      Remember, in 2e, Humanity Breaking Points are not sins against an objective moral standard. They're just things that are jarring to a vampire and remind them "oh yeah, I'm a fucking monster." The fact that it's horrifying to you indicates that it would be a harsh Integrity Breaking Point for you to witness or experience. The fact that the vampire is so nonplussed by this atrocious act probably makes it seem all the worse to you, but it's actually an indication that it's a very high level Humanity Breaking Point for a vampire.

                      Thinking on it further, why is the Vinculum so horrible? It comes down to the fact that it violates the victim's free will. It removes their ability to refuse the vampire because their will has been altered so that they want to do what ever the vampire says, despite the fact that they probably wouldn't want that were it not for the influence of the Vinculum. In effect, it deprives them of consent. That's actually covered on the sample Breaking Points, at Humanity 8 - it may not be a Discipline per se, but it is a power of the blood, and I think it should absolutely be included under the umbrella of "depriving another of consent with a Discipline."

                              It seems like, by default, one might consider Majesty as Humanity 9 and Dominate as Humanity 8. I would place Vinculum as worse than both. One, it's (practically, if not literally) permanent rather than temporary. Two, and this is more subjective, it's a more visceral sort of monstrous behavior. There is no real physical feedback regarding your monstrosity when using a discipline. Vinculum involves bleeding yourself and having someone else imbibe it. Humans don't do that. It's inhuman, so probably should count as a sin.

                              On the other hand, it's heavily implied that most vampires of any standing at all will have some measure of bound human servants to act as eyes and ears and hands and feet to maintain their standing. If it were a terribly damning experience, this couldn't be the norm without vampire society being much more dominated by the Beast than it is already.

                              So while my personal stance on mind control would put it as something only an Ancient bordering on Draugr would do, it just isn't reasonable. From a fluff/mechanics/playability standpoint, I'd probably have to put it at Humanity 7 at worst. The drop from 7 to 6 kind of symbolizes a neonate's dive into actual vampire life, as evidenced by being both the first drop for a new character and the one that activates the Clan Bane, and it seems like vinculum is a core part of actual vampire life. Placing it lower would seem counter-thematic to me.

Of course, depends on if you're doing it with their consent or not, but breaking points are explicitly meant to be fluid guidelines.

                                              While uncommon, you can also ghoul a character without giving them a full vinculum (i.e. so they can heal a wound). It's ... improbable that they would place it there for just such a specific incident, though, but it is a good reason for those of us who put it below 8 to use.

                                              Re-reading the list, I wouldn't place it at Humanity 9 because the full vinculum doesn't so much as urge as re-shape it in my mind. Also depriving a human of consent is a sin at 8 and the vinculum very much does that. Even if they consent to the bond, once the full vinculum sets in, they'll pretty much "consent" to anything if it's put to them right by the regnant and that puts the very idea of them being able to consent into question.

                                              Of course, part of our decisions on where to rank it will also likely connect with just how "far gone" we run those under full vinculum. If it just means that they want to please the person, sure, it's no worse than Confidante, but if we run it like Idol where otherwise well-attached and compassionate parents would suddenly and gladly kill their own children or offer them for feeding just to make the vampire happy, and even thank the vampire for the privilege of killing their children, then yeah, that's going to be as clearly vampiric as getting hit by a car because it's not so much "urging" as a "personality rewrite to suit the vampire". It's a pretty heavy enforcement of will in the latter case and there's no human equivalent -- or even equivalent among the other monster types.

                                              The other option would be to sit it at 8 (or 7 in line with it being a "Welcome to Vampiricism" sin), and then just hit the PC with breaking points if the person suffering from the vinculum does something terrible in the vampire's name that they wouldn't ordinarily do because of the blood bond*. This would probably be the most interesting option for me as it allows for situations where the vinculum (i.e. with ghouling) plus ghouldom seems to me like something that could disturb a very new vampire. So after all this conversation, I'll probably lift it to 7. Welcome to being a Vampire. Your blood is a drug that enslaves humanity. Once you hit Humanity 6, you won't look twice at it.

                                              *NOTE: This isn't to say that a GM should lay in wait to spring such a trap, only that it might make good story if the PCs get lazy in their choice of who to vinculum, such as using to barrel through humans without any real further interaction to back it up and smooth it down. It makes me think of the injured girl from Vampire: the Masquerade Bloodlines who eventually traps a man in the closet for you. Also if this type of reaction is likely, some kind of Empathy roll to give the vampire fair warning and an OOC talk about just how hard core blood bonds are in your game are in order.
