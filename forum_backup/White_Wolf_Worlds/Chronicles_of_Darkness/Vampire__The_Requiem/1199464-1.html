                                    Something to consider about all this:

                                    The addiction rules are easy to break... for player characters. That's pretty much on purpose to avoid addiction taking over the game. It lets you tell stories of overcoming addiction if that's what you want to do. For most characters, it isn't going to be easy. They're not going to have Resolve + Composure high enough to resist (esp. multiple doses), or the WP to keep spending on it for it. They're not going to be regularly experiencing breaking points, let alone something life changing enough to just increase their Integrity.

                                    It's also worth considering that vampires with BP 1-5 have it exceedingly worse than mortals when it comes to vitae addiction. They don't have Integrity to go up or down. Regaining Willpower is harder for them. Older vampires all know about it, and the effect it has, and will use it against neonates. VtR was written for playing vampires, not mortals, so the temptations of blood getting harder to deal with as mortals get wrapped up in vampire existence and then become vampires is sort of going with the themes of the game. Kicking vitae addiction with ease is a thing of the past, one more thing the Embrace denies you for what it gives you.

                                                First, your game is excellent and you might consider doing a write-up and serializing it on a forum someplace. RPG.net perhaps? Anyway, even your synopsis came across as a good read.

                                                You're doing everything right. Why? Because you're planning on the characters becoming vamps. Everyone knows this as they sit down at the table, so the process ahead of time is an appetizer for the main course. Do most "average" mortals encounter such patient/tolerant shadowy bloodsucking monsters before becoming one? Perhaps not... but they also aren't PCs!

                                                Kicking the addiction mechanics, temporarily, just makes the PCs more interesting and puts them in greater trouble if their future sires understand they are not, in fact, so tied down.... Vamp society runs on "ties you can count on"--especially Invictus society; so, having flexibility is actually a liability from the PC's perspective, or can be.
