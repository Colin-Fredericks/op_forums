    Several rules questions - Humanity,defence to disciplines,defending others,safe house

    07-27-2016, 09:42 AM
    Hello, me and my best friend are starting a vampire game he is going to GM it and I will assist when needed as we have a large group of 8 players and there are several rules that we are debating about. We both have characters that will be involved in the game.

    1 - Humanity breaking point - We are trying to work out where self defence killing would go on breaking points, we see at 4 is accident 3 passioned 2 premediated. He is saying it is 2 because you kill someone on purpose I am saying lowest 5. View points being 4 is accident which could be your negligence such as speeding in a car and killing someone. Self defence killing is this guy just stabbed someone and is coming at me with a knife I hit him with my sword and he died. Or theres this guy about to kill an innocent person/group so I shoot him to stop him he dies from it. My friend thinks that because you are chosing to kill them its a 2 breaking point you could just ignore it and walk away.

    2 - Defence to disciplines - He is saying to get around dominate his character is just never going to look anyone in the eye, I said that is stupidly cheap and if you have an audience with the prince/sheriff and refuse to look them in the eye you'll end up having no eyes. He said no every vampire will do it as a norm to protect against dominate even the prince will do it. Is it really that easy to just negate a whole discipline? Just say to the GM just so you know my character never looks anyone in the eye? I'll walk around with headphones in and never looking anyone in the eye and I negate dominate and majesty with no effort or xp on my part.

    3 - Defending for others - No rules for this but if I made a bodyguard or had one, how would it go them defending for someone? I was thinking they would need to go first and withold their action and then use it when someone attacks the person they are protecting to get in the way? But you act do your attack/move then they could just side step around you and hit the person your protecting as they act after you? Any rules for this anywhere?

    4 - Lastly safe house - We were wondering it is very non descript about what you can have and how to get around it etc, so can you just say you have 20 dogs 5 traps 12 security guards 17 sensors 30 cameras etc? Only asking as my friend said that if you do some research and hack the cameras and chuck some drugged meat over the walls for the dogs to eat you can lower the defence rating the safe house gives you. So if you can through just mundane means lower the security a safe house provides how do you govern how much stuff is in your safe house? Also he said you can have loads of fire traps so it does 5 aggravated to vampires trying to break in, as the merit says you can have traps that deal lethal damage equal to its safe house rating so he said chose a fire trap then it does 5 agg. I think this again is bending what was intended but we decided to have it run like that but just wanted peoples thoughts.

    Sorry long rants but needed to clear these up and explain view points, any thoughts would be great thanks.

          1. I'd say to just forget if it was in self-defense or not. I think that's meaningless - Humanity is supposed to measure how much empathy you have for your fellow humans. It is not a measurement of your morality. So, while self-defense is a strong moral and legal argument, it has no relationship to how a person relates to humans. If you bust out the Claws of the Unholy everytime you're attacked, that's showing a callous attitude towards life, even if its only done in self defense. Meanwhile, someone that perfects choke holds or use of stun guns to immobilize attackers in such a way that no one ends up seriously hurt? They're much more careful with life.

          2. This is nothing more than trying to be a munchkin. And there are plenty of tricks to catch someone's eye and draw their attention, even without magic. I'd just tell the player "No" and be done with it - I think of this as an OOC player attempt to game the system, so I think that it should be adressed in the same way. Alternatively, if you prefer an IC method, they get a Blind Condition when related to perception of other people as well as a social penalty for being so obvious.

          3. I personally treat a bodyguard as Cover, making it hard to reach or hit the protected person, as well as reducing the potency of all attacks. I may switch things up depending on the situation. As well, there's actually a pretty cool Temple Guardian FS in the Secrets of the Covenant CotC preview that works as a bodyguard for others. I highly recommend checking it out.

          4. Nope. IC descriptions are justifications for purchasing the regular merits, not getting free merits. Descriptions on how you're breaking in don't modify someone else's merits, but provide you equipment bonuses to your rolls. You want to deal aggravated damage, then, like Vitalis said, you could come up with a new merit that deals that kind of damage. Though, for the record, I wouldn't allow it with the justifiaction "Its fire!" Ghouls and hellhounds don't suffer agg from flame, after all, so this is a bit questionable, as well as the fact there's Resilience or armor or other damage reducing measures that would apply. And I would suggest kindly that any vampire installing fire into their home is rather suicidal, since if someone breaks into the home in the middle of the day (most home robberies happen during that time, when people are usually away from home at work) and lights the place on fire, you've basically set your own haven on fire with you sleeping inside. Death trap anyone?

                  Mechanically, I would just ignore the fact it was self defense. No bonus dice, no changing the level, nothing. I don't think that self defense applies in this situation, and should have absolutely no mechanical impact.

                  Shooting someone because they kept stabbing you is, honestly, something I would qualify as either impassioned (you panicked) or a deliberate murder. Being stabbed is just bashing to a vampire. Pulling out a Predator's Aura, some grappling with a physical, or anything other power else could easily solve a fight against a mortal with zero casualties. You chose to go for a deadly weapon against a mortal in some way.

                      I run self defense as a +1 to +3 dice modifier to checks based on the circumstances. You kill someone who attacks you first. Impassioned killing. You provoke someone into taking a swing so you can hit back. (Either by verbal insults or vandalism of their property etc.) Premeditated.

                      Gaming the eye contact wording. Nope. Doesn't fly with me unless your character is blind, which includes an inability to use Auspex and permanent penalties. If you're going to stare at the floor the entire time you get perception and combat penalties and the Dominate user still only takes a -2 to mesmerize you because their power oozes from their voice and body language as well.

                      Bodyguards I have function as cover, armor, bonus to surprise checks etc. Depending on the circumstance.

                      As noted by others, drugging the dogs counts as a one shot situational bonus to break in. You throw poisoned meat over the fence? Maybe the dog is trained to only eat food from their handler. I know some military and police dogs are trained like that.
                      Firetraps would do aggravated damage to vampires. But it's kind of stupid for a vampire to sleep in a firetrap.

                              Secondly, you're equating morality with humanity here. This has nothing to do how good it makes you feel. It has everything to do with how well you relate to people.                                   1. Killing (or doing anything else) in self defense isn't a breaking point by itself. Depending on the nature of the killing, I'd say it's a Humanity 1 or Humanity 2 breaking point. If you want how guilty a character feels to figure into it, shift it up or down a level. Keep in mind, though, that the struggle against detachment based on mitigating factors is what the roll is already meant to measure.

                                  2. Attempts to avoid eye contact are already part of the dice pool to resist Mesmerize.

                                  3. I'd let the bodyguard add one Attribute or Skill to the defended character's Defense. Depending on the context, this might be Stamina (I'm protecting you with the sheer bulk of my body), Weaponry (I'm blocking attacks on you using a tool or weapon), Dexterity (I jump between you and your attacker with lightning speed), or even Resolve (I resist my fear of fire to stand between you and the torch-wielding hunter).

                                  If that pushes Defense too high for your taste, consider something like substituting the bodyguard's appropriate stat for the defended character's Athletics in calculating Defense.

                                  A character should not be able to benefit from a bodyguard physically blocking attacks while also making attacks themselves. Unless maybe they have a cool new description for how they do it, in which case go to town.

                                  4. Safe Place is primarily meant for player characters. If you're attempting to lay siege to a non-player character's home or bolt-hole, and want to cover it in detail, you're better off designing it by statting traps and opposing characters individually. When player characters are being attacked in their own Safe Place, follow the damage guidelines on p. 123. If a character wants to buy security, consider making a Retainer, using Resources to get equipment/services like Surveillance (p. 189), Bodyguard Service, or Security Consulting (both on p. 185).

                                  If you want to get deep into building your own security measures (animate or not), try the Building Equipment rules from the Chronicles of Darkness Rulebook.

                                                      1 - I'd put killing in self-defense at Humanity 3, same as impassioned killing. The need to defend yourself is the thing flaring your passion.

                                                      2 - The sidebar about eye-contact for the purposes of Dominate says the victim needs to see the vampire's eyes. You don't need to be locking eyes with the vampire, you just need to be able to see their eyes. So unless you're looking at the floor or something, no, you can't avoid what constitutes eye-contact for the purposes of Dominate. And good luck getting through (un)life never looking at anything but your feet.

                                                      3 - I would allow the bodyguard to voluntarily act as a human shield (grants the person they're protecting Cover, with durability equal to Stamina + Armor, and takes all the damage from the attack).

                                                      4 - The Safe Place rules are intentionally abstract. The fact that you need to go to the trouble of disabling security cameras, drugging guard dogs, etc. is represented by the dice penalty to your roll to break in. Hacking Software and drugged meat and such could maybe grant an equipment bonus to the roll to break in, but it's not going to allow you to bypass the roll entirely. Personally, I would probably not allow fire-based traps on a Safe Place because of the way fire works. The damage is dependent on the size and intensity of the fire, and continues to deal that much damage every turn that the victim is in the affected area. The intent of the trap damage from Safe Place is clearly a single one-and-done amount of damage, and fire just doesn't work that way.
