    Feeding grounds, territories and boundaries

    03-21-2014, 02:07 PM
    As I brainstorm to eventually try out Blood and Smoke, my suspension of disbelief was shattered. I can't seem to wrap my mind on how to establish domains anymore. I can't get to grasp how feeding grounds function in practice.

    I mean... If each of your vampire has a designated feeding ground, any other vampire on it is a potential poacher. Do the owner forbids entry/passage into his territory to "protect his blood"? If that's the case, won't all vampires get stuck into their own city block because crossing that street means stepping into another vampire's territory? Isn't that a little boring, being imprisoned in your domain for eternity?

    And if there are resources in a vampire's territory, does he have a claim on it? Say there's... an hospital. If another vampire ghouls, blood bounds or starts pulling strings on members of that hospital, is that a breach of domain?

    I'm tired and maybe I'm just overthinking things...

      Feeding grounds are not the same as tenurial domain, and they are not necessarily officially assigned. Feeding grounds are an area where hunting is easy that your character knows and utilizes.
      You could also be offiicially assigned a feeding ground but not necessarily be entitled to use additional resources. For example: Your character is allowed to hunt in a gated community but not necessarily be allowed to establish a haven, set up an elysium etc.
      Alternatively, you could also have tenurial rights to an area but not necessarily allowed to feed. Your character could be granted tenurial rights in a 5 block radius but the Prince maintains a ban on feeding from active public safety servants so feeding off the firefighters living at the firehouse is off limits. But you could feed off the employees and customers at the 7-11, throw kindred parties, charge a toll/fine on other kindred or their minions to pass through and so on.

          Originally posted by Mr Iki Iki
          If each of your vampire has a designated feeding ground, any other vampire on it is a potential poacher. Do the owner forbids entry/passage into his territory to "protect his blood"?
          I would say no. Being granted a feeding ground grants exclusive access to the blood that feeding ground produces; nothing more. Unless or until another vampire feeds from your feeding ground, they are not trespassing.

          However, some vampires are granted more comprehensive control of a territory. If a character is granted Regency/Domain, then they are for lack of better terms Master of that domain. They decide who is and is not permitted to travel there, have a haven there, cultivate mortal influence there, create ghouls there etc.

          Originally posted by Damnation City
          “Domain” is similar to land, but eclipses it — a Kindred granted domain is entitled to whatever the land within that domain generates (provided he can claim it from the mortals therein, which is an entirely different issue and outside the scope of relationships between Kindred), as well as any other resources that domain generates. A Kindred whose domain is the downtown neighborhood is the master of that domain, answerable only to his neo-feudal superiors, and has the right to take whatever downtown generates, and act against anyone who would usurp that right from him.
          The "neo-feudal" system has been in the books since the beginning of VtR. It was fully fleshed out in Damnation City. Although its true that B&S has made a point to show some examples of other types of kindred government, neo-feudalism is still represented.


          This system may test your suspension of disbelief, but I feel the opposite. After all, if every vampire is allowed to travel where ever they want, feed where ever they want, ghoul whoever they want, etc. Then... how does the Prince hold onto power? Why should a vampire serve as sheriff or hound if their opportunities and privileges in the city are identical to every other vampire, even fledglings who contribute nothing to the court?

          The neo-feudal system assumes the Prince gives out the juiciest feeding grounds and domains to his allies, who in turn promise to support his authority.

          That's just my opinion on it.

                  Well, if you are using B&S there are 2 merits in which you may have interest.

                  Lex Terrae: 100% effective at dealing with poachers. Zero effort required.
                  Oath of Serfdon: Your spider sense tingles when a vamper enters your territory, and you know from what direction they entered. If you have encountered their predatory aura before, you even know exactly who it is.

                  I personally think these merits make the game less interesting, not more. So I don't use them in my game. However, Vampires have other ways of catching trespassers. Some instant, some delayed.
                  1. Animalism 5, Lord of the Land: The user of this effect will always be aware of trespassers in his territory.
                  2. Auspex 1, Beasts Hackles: Whenever you go to Elysium or any other kindred gathering, make a habit of asking your ST "has anyone here been poaching on my Land?" or some such.

                  In the end, when you are designing your city/chronicle, do so in a way that you think will be fun for you as ST and fun for your players.                           There is a reason the Feeding Grounds merit specifies "your character has a regular feeding ground, whether legally or otherwise" (or something to that effect). Poaching is in fact common enough that the book acknowledges regular poaching as a legit way to justify a feeding grounds Merit purchase. But the poacher has to constantly live in fear of being caught. Every Mekhet is a potential enemy because they can learn of your crimes with just a glance and blackmail you to keep it secret. You'd better watch your back poaching on a Gangrel's land too, because every dog, every rat, and every crow could be one of their spies. You have to be especially cautious when talking with Daeva because they can charm you into telling them your secrets. You can't look into the eyes of any Ventrue, because they can search through your memories if you do. And the Nosferatu? Anyone who would risk crossing those freaks has some serious balls. Basically, Kindred society is so incredibly low-privacy, all it takes is the threat of severe enough punishment to deter just about any crime. Because if you honestly think NO ONE knows your secrets, you're a fool. It's just a question of who knows, how much they know, and what they want from you to keep it quiet.

                              Just like in real life, there's no one answer for these things.
                              VampireA may refuse to let anyone in his territory. Yes, it is hard to enforce, and depending upon the laws of your local vampire society, it might be unfeasible (if you can't raise a hand against your fellow vampire, you made a stupid decision saying you'll try and punish people for invading your territory).
                              VampireB may just say that the Mynt Lounge her hunting ground. And the management and workers are all blood bound to keep an eye out for poachers.
                              VampireC may have his territory invaded by VampireD. From there it depends on VampireC's capabilities. Can they stop VampireD from coming in and annexing part of their property? Can they look to other vampires for help, or will they be too afraid of the public backlash if they go pick a fight with VampireD? Maybe VampireE disapproves, but isn't willing to (or can't, because of the no fighting law, or simply because if VampireE joins in the fight, everyone will see it as unfair and dogpile them). But VampireE has some sort of power. Maybe they're a bigwig in the Circle of the Crone or the Lancea Sanctum, and VampireD finds ghosts and spirits popping up in their domain, causing trouble. Maybe they find themselves shut out by the harpies, and wild rumors start spreading over the Cacophony, but they don't know cause the harpies aren't talking to them, and VampireD is now alone and isolated with a neighbor he just made an enemy of.
                              And VampireF has literally everyone in the insane asylum blood bound to him, and will hunt you down and kill you for stepping foot on its grounds without asking for permission.

                              It depends on the laws of your court, on the vampires involved (and their relationships), on the time (maybe there's rumors of strix around, and people don't take kindly to strangers), on the city (one of the stories in the Strix anthology had four counties split apart by wilderness, which allowed for very clear boundaries).
                              Even without Majesty, vampires can create masses of people loyal to them through ghouling and blood bondage.
                              And of course there's the coterie. Why would vampires ever work together? Maybe because they have to, because they're the only ones living (ha) in a certain area and the only way they can keep it theirs is by working together, until some of them decide to screw over the others.

                              Vampires aren't the trusting types. But vampires who gather renown for being untrustworthy aren't going to be given any passes without a hefty fee.

                                  The methods for countering poaching are the same as any underground group uses to keep enemies off their turf--networks of informants, hired thugs keeping an eye out, lurking in the shadows and keeping an eye out for suspicious activity. Poaching does happen, though, and it's what sparks a lot of conflicts. This is good, conflicts are fun and lead to stories.

                                  Safe transit: It's something that's mentioned in one or two Mage books, where the territorialism makes a lot less sense. The idea is that some domains will have 'safe routes' marked through the territory to get from Point A on the border to Point B on the border. That's the sort of diplomatic arrangement that has to get made for the sake of a functional society, and probably something that the Prince has a hand in to make everyone play nice. Stay on the Cacophony-marked road, maybe pay a toll, and you're fine; stray from the path, you're a trespasser and will be deal with as such. ...Also, on a more basic level, if someone passes through your domain on the freeway at 70 miles per hour, it's way too much trouble to try to enforce strict territorial bounds on those routes. Similarly, subways are likely to be controlled by Kindred with special arrangements to limit overhunting and let vampires get around for a 'fair' price.

                                  Resources in the domain: That would vary a lot from city to city, I think. In one city, the regnant of the Upper West Side has a say on everything there; if they have a hospital there, they control who has access to it, and the only way anyone gets a hook in without their permission is if the intruder does it sneakily (and that's important to note--you can always branch out into others' territory legally if you make the right deal with them). In another city, the Prince might have declared hospitals as their own type of domain, given over to another Kindred or held in reserve for the Prince alone. At that point, the one with domain over hospitals probably has a lot of deals to make to get access to their realm, or they're going to be a lot of subletting.

                                  That's a major part of how the vampiric feudal system works. A city district is technically under the control of a single vampire or coterie of vampires, but that regent is going to have a rough time controlling the whole damn thing, and keeping poachers from screwing up the good hunting spots. So they keep the best part, and let other vampires live in the other parts in return for payment in service or goods. Those vampires then sublet to others, and so on and so forth until you get the low-down neonates who really can't afford to let anyone else get in on their turf. Those neonates are the ones who do the dirty work of keeping poachers out in their tiny territory, and in the process help out their chain of overlords. Delegation is the key to maintaining this sort of arrangement. Of course, those feudal vassals are the sort of ambitious jerks who want to take their overlords' spot in the hierarchy; again, that's what stories are made of. No one likes having to lend power to anyone else, but no one can run everything.
