      1. It depends. For example, Resilience can be reflexively used to negate damage by spending blood after a wound is inflicted. There isn't really a hard and fast rule I can think of, other than if it makes sense one way or the other, go with that. That being said, I would err on the side of allowing the Reflexive action.

      2. Without seeing the specific build, it's hard to say, but generally the effects of fighting styles don't stack. Using the tactics of light weaponry, a function of the Weaponry Skill, doesn't work when you're street fighting, which is a function of Brawl.

      3. I'm not sure I understand the question? If you use Auspex on that character, asking if he committed a crime against the city, it would fail, yes.

          Originally posted by Wunderkid View Post
          1. Suprise and reflexive. Now from what I've see suprise denies a player their action. But I've also noticed in a lot of reflexive Abilities it says 'must be aware of the attack to use'. So if you are suprised can you use things that are reflexive? Or are they denied to you? Page references would be perfect as well.
          In general, you can use a reflexive action/ability whenever it would be applicable unless something says otherwise. Surprise is one way to deny a suite of reflexive actions. Ultimately if it isn't clear remember that reflexive actions contain both "automatic" things like breathing, and "purposeful" things like moving a limited distance in a turn. Automatic stuff isn't denied lightly, while purposeful stuff is more likely to be denied.

          2. One of the players has come up with a build that nets him around the 25 dice to attack while denying the opponents defence. This is through a combination of high strength, Dex, weaponry, vigour and the light weapons merit which allows you to sac your defence and add it to your attack. Cheap shot to deny them their defence if his stealth attack doesn't pan out.

          Street fighting 3 let's him inflict the knocked down tilt which makes a person lose their action. And with this many dice he can comfortably aim for even the smallest gap in armor. And then the Kindred fighting style thrown in on top to be able to inflict lethal damage.

          Well, first off, is someone's invested this many Merit dots into being good at hurting people, they're going to be good at it.

          That said, Light Weapons is a Weaponry style, which means it only works with Weaponry based attacks. Street Fighting is a Brawl style, which means it only works with Brawl based attacks. So Thrust (LW 2) and Kick 'Em While They're Down (SF 3) are not compatible moves.

          This tactic also requires being able to either reliably sneak attack and/or Cheap Shot. So characters that have good rolls to counter these are already in a good place to stop this in its tracks by not having their Defense removed (and if they're equally invested in defense they can Dodge with lots of dice, and can use the defensive Merits to mess up this sort of alpha strike).

          If they aren't surprised any character with Celerity can just use the active interrupt power to move away from this assault, and then run.

          There's also the ultimate trump card: friends. This character might be great against one opponent, but these sorts of tactics are going to fail fast against multiple enemies. Sacrificing your Defense - esp. in the 2e combat rules - is nigh suicidal if you're up against more than one competent opponent.

          There's also Predatory Auras which can be used to send someone into Wanton, using Dominate, Majesty or Nightmare, and other such tricks to just not get into combat in the first place.

          Ultimately though, remember that the game is not about stopping inhuman monsters from being scarily powerful if they dedicate themselves to being so. It's about what happens next. How does this sort of character maintain their Humanity when they've accepted their existence as an undead killing machine? How many political bridges does their violence burn? Etc.

          3. The merit plausible deniability. Now it says "Any attempt to use a Discipline or other supernatural power to prove your character’s guilt in breach of city law or Tradition automatically fails."

          Now does this mean if a character with auspex asks a question about this character regarding illegal going ons it won't work. Or does it only work when it is used to try and generate 'proof'?
          Auspex doesn't generate "proof" The Merit protects you more from things like being Dominated into confession. All Auspex would do is allow the Auspex user to be protected via the Merit's Drawbacks. They know what the Carthian did even if they can't prove it.

          4. And finally. I bought the god machine chronicle, VTR and a copy of the WoD core book, however I'm not sure exactly what copy of WoD I need to be using, could someone tell me how to tell which book is the most recent, or provide a link, just because I want to ensure the book I have is the correct one.
          Anything that says "Second Edition," or "Chronicles of Darkness," is the most recent (except GMC and Demon). There aren't exactly a lot of second edition books out yet. The general core (Chronicles of Darkness), Demon, Beast, VtR 2e, WtF 2e, The Pack, and Mage 2e are the current 2e books.

          If your copy of the WoD corebook says, "World of Darkness" on the cover, it's the 1e one, not the 2e one.

                  1: Dropping prone isn't a reflexive action, it's a defensive action that's an exception to the normal order of action rules.

                  2: Punch daggers and shivs are weapons that can be used with Brawl. If you look at the beginning of the Merits it says specifically that Light Weapons works with Weaponry and Street Fighting works with Brawl. A shiv could be used with Light Weapons one turn, and Street Fighting the next, but not both at the same time.

                  Can you describe how he's both getting 25+ dice, and winning initiative so easily, esp. at start? Heck, how is he even managing all these Merits at start?

                  Also, if he doesn't get a surprise attack, Celerity trumps this via active use, even if he'd have the higher initiative without active Celerity use. The Celerity user can just interrupt him to hit first or run away. And Celerity passively adds to Initiative, which is a very mathematically lopsided roll off. What looks like a small edge in Initiative is generally a lot statistically.

                  3:

                  Well, Auspex 1-3 doesn't give proof in any shape. It gives impressions. Auspex 5 isn't relevant. Auspex 4 is the only real sticking point of whether or not it might be "proof."

                          In general, Vampire fights are slapfights; most of damage sources will deal Bashing Damage, which in turn allows very easy regeneration and tilt removal. Your typical Vampire brawl might look brutal, but unless someone flips out, it will probably will end with both sides deciding to stop this shit now because they are going low on blood. Most of these tricks would be relatively pointless against vampires, because again, you being blinded isn't much of a problem if the wound that blinded you is 1 Bashing damage that you are healing anyway.
                          The thing you should be wary about is the fact that he can ignore the basic assumption of vampire combat and go straight for Lethal. It has following effects:
                          - It, effectively, skips a whole health bar. Normal vampires have to punch through Bashing Health Boxes, which is about 7-9 health; it gives a lot of time for damage control by spending blood to regenerate, and makes conflicts relatively low on lethality
                          - By jumping straight to Lethal, he fucks up the basic assumption of regeneration being able to match or even outpace the damage dealt. It cuts their healing in half (you get 2 Bashing damage healed for one Blood Point; you get one lethal per blood point), which will mean that he will be able to outdamage their healing.

                                                  CofD combat is violent. Arm Wreck, blinding, magic powers, retainer/familar swarms (or Protean insect swarms), mind control. These are all devistating tactics. My personal favorite tactic at the moment is from my Acolyte Khaibit. Use Gaze of the Gorgon, negating the need for a eyesight with the free Gift from the bloodline. I'm at 16 dice without willpower (Presence 4, Occult 4, +2 specialty, Cruac 3, +3 from Mantle of Amorous Fire); the opponent rolls Stamina + BP, subtracting my successes. Each success is a point of aggravated damage, and turns one limb to stone; Resilience might prevent the damage, but not the turning-to-stone limbs. That is going to really put a wrench in any combat tactics. And the best part? I haven't even used my action - just opened my eyes to meet someone's gaze. And this isn't a combat-focused character.

                                                  Combat, in my experience, is rately about who's got the larger dice pools. Its all about breaking the other person first. If you can Arm Wreck or Blind someone, they're screwed. Attack from Celerity distance with a bow for staking. Dominate someone and force them into a docile state. This isn't D&D and a struggle to reduce someone's HP total to zero. This isn't a heroic battle. This is fighting dirty and hurting others before they can hurt you. Its about breaking someone's will to fight. I don't honestly care if a player has a dice pool of 45 with Vigor 5. That's not enough to consistantly take down a vampire with 2 stamina and 1 Resilience in one round. Because, I promise you, any decently combat vampire will destroy the ability of that 50-dice character to fight back in the next turn.

                                                  Like Charlaquin said, this is cripplingly overspecialized to do one thing, and one thing only. Like WHW said, vampire combat with this character's specialty is basically "slap fight" territory since everything is bashing damage. Vampire combat, at its most brutal, involves staking, grapples, fire, Dominates, Obfuscates/Celerity evasions, and other one-sided, heavy handed methods. A starting Protean specialist can turn themselves into a swarm of insects - you can't beat that with just a shiv. Your options are to outrun the swarm, use magic, or be bitten into torpor. The character has no defense or ability to fight against anyone other than another swordsman, and even then? Don't mess with Daeva using Enfeebling aura, Vigor and a stake.

                                                  Frankly, "challenging" this character would be difficult because I would likely end up one sidedly slapping this character down. My vampires believe that, short of Kindred Dueling, there's no real reason to go after each other with guns or blades. You go for the magic, you go for staking, you go for grapples with bites to tear the other's throat out, you go for mind control, or you just Celerity/Obfuscate away and avoid fights altogether.
