( Dominate 5 ) or a Protean devotion to negate damage ( Resilience) while in... ooze form or something? Why make a devotion to mezmerize yourself so you can dig up memories you might have forgotten or so that you can know if you have been messed with because that's Auspex...

                        Sure! Why not? When someone suggests "make a Devotion" the first thing that springs to my mind at least is "combine two+ Disciplines to achieve your effect". So want to dominate yourself? That'll be including Auspex 4 please. Want to control someone else's body? Dominate 5 + Protean 5 to physically possess them with full control. Protean to negate damage? Protean 4+Resilience to halve bashing like good ol' Flesh of Marble from VtM (for example).

                        Why do all this? Because Discipline powers as written are narrow and intended to be utilized as such. That's why we have Devotions--to break the mold and permit characters to create their own, custom requiem with blood-powers that reflect their personal needs and outlook. So arguing "I can use X discipline to do what I want!" is really not in spirit; the answer should be "burn that saved XP to make a Devotion." Or, get help from another character to work your edge case.

The Unholy at BP1 is still going to rip your coterie in half, because she's planned for people like you....

                                You miss my point, sir. Duplicating the word-for-word effect of an existing power with the same plus another Discipline, plus extra cost (XP or otherwise) is simply silly; doing something different is not. If, say, we wanted a Dominate+Protean Devotion I would expect it NOT to, for example, leave the vampire's body behind--said body would be merged with the subject, removing a key weakness of Dominate 5.

                                Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
                                Resilence already lets you downgrade damage and there is a devotion to negate it completely. Digging up memories is Auspex 4. Avoiding attacks is Celerity 1
                                When you rewrite things that already exist, to make it easier to get, it feels like cheating to me. ( or if you are making it harder to do, it just feels stupid, like requiring protean 5 and dominate 5 to take over someone's body... )
                                All subject to the specifics of their mechanics. So does your new Devotion do something differently? Does it trade off a disadvantage of the base power for a different one (likely more severe, in many cases)? Does it enable you to target something the base power does not--for example, Auspex 4 lets you rifle through memories, but memories of someone else; if you want to search yourself, you'll need a Devotion to swap the target.

                                This is why I note that Disciplines are narrow and should be interpreted narrowly. They're quite powerful within their narrow sphere, and if you let that focus off you can players pulling all kinds of silly stunts. Like mesmerizing themselves in the mirror, which has been beaten to death, staked and burned in the past two pages of this thread. Again.

                                Originally posted by Vitalis View Post
                                I am 110% in on making devotions to create NEW things for your disciplines to do, but not creating one for something that is already written.                                 I agree that people often don't ponder the full implications of their discipline. And "narrow" can cover a lot of ground. Celerity is an excellent example of that, insomuch as it gives a variety of useful effects for vitae cost regardless of level. I do recall the other thread, and even posted a contribution, which pursued a purely situational benefit otherwise also obtainable through Instant Transformation and Celerity 1 (for initiative). That's actually spurred a rules question I'll post next in this thread.

                                Your example is an excellent Devotion, and practically powerful. Did you realize that the old "summoning" effect from Majesty was axed in 2e? Seems so from my read, so you could reasonably do a Majesty 1 Devotion to let you attract a specific person in the area with Awe, without drawing the attention of the entire room. This is an example of using a Devotion to alter the narrow scope of a Discipline power and achieve a specific effect: bringing *that guy*, without being a rock star.

                                Now, this will probably offend your sense of order, but I would happily consider a heavily concept-based offer from a player to use another Discipline and duplicate an existing effect (such as damage reduction)--so long as the cost involved is equal or better and we have an understanding on how it will interact with the other Discipline. Questions at work need to be: Is this driven by concept, or meta-gaming? Is this going to break systems if combined? And the like. (Example: Player doesn't want a high-Resilience Gangrel, but wants to take less damage by turning briefly into stone... ok.)

                                Players making their lives more difficult and expensive for the sake of concept is EXACTLY what I want them doing.

                                    Quick question:

                                    Surprise
                                    Characters who don’t realize they’re about to be on the
                                    receiving end of bloody violence have a chance to notice the
                                    ambush by rolling Wits + Composure, contested by the attacker’s
                                    Dexterity + Stealth. Any character who fails the roll cannot take
                                    an action in the first turn of combat, and can’t apply Defense
                                    for that turn. Determine Initiative in the second turn as normal.

                                    This obviously will apply during the first turn of hostilities, and certainly so if a given character is oblivious to any threat (buried to the armpits in an engine block repair, for example). But:

                                    1. Does surprise apply if a given character is aware of the tense situation, ready to fight, but not aware of a specific, hidden opponent (such as through Obfuscate)? Would they lose defense on failed roll to the sole, hidden opponent? [Logically, yes, I assume.]

                                    2. On subsequent rounds are opponents able to re-hide themselves (e.g. through Obfuscate) and gain the benefits of a surprise check again? Or is the character too keyed-up to fall for that trick again?

                                    3. What about powers that re-position an opponent, or disorient them? Could those be used to re-apply surprise rolls? (E.g. Protean 5 with Instant Transformation, or a short-range teleport effect, or something that instantly spins/reorients a target)
