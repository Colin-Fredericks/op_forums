            Okay, I went to look:

            Chicago by Night, Chicago by Night 2e: Nope. There are two published adventures that are set in Chicago (Ashes to Ashes and Under a Blood Red Moon), but since they're adventures they're not metaplot, and they're not included in the Chicago by Night supplements. Chicago by Night 1e has a chapter devoted to random encounters, however.
            New York by Night: Nope
            New Orleans by Night: D'Jabbic. I remember nothing about this story, as I never ran it. Apparently there was something about a house having a basement/cellar in the adventure which is clearly pretty silly given that it's New Orleans, however.
            Berlin by Night: The Ascension of Caine, which is again not metaplot. I mean, it's a bad adventure, but the events are pretty contained to Berlin. I also didn't run this, although I did read it.
            Cairo by Night: Nope.
            DC by Night: Nope.
            LA by Night: Nope.
            Mexico City by Night: Nope.
            Milwaukee by Night: Psychomachia. I don't recall what this is as I never ran it.
            Montreal by Night: Nope.
            San Francisco by Night: Nope.

            12 by Night sourcebooks, and three of them have included adventures. Sooooo... the implication that they were included in most by Night sourcebooks is not accurate. It probably is true that they're railroads, but that doesn't make them metaplot. No supplement relied on you having played those adventures, nor did any supplement refer back to those adventures. They were self-contained pieces. You could play them and move on, and you got to decide what form the fallout took after they were over.

            Can we not keep expanding what constitutes metaplot to the point of uselessness? Stick to what they are - overarching story arcs with mandated events that will turn out in a singular predetermined way whether player characters are involved or not, and either become the basis for future supplements or are at least referenced in future supplements. And while character writeups can be involved in metaplot (such as happened with multiple Children of the Night character writeups) they're not metaplot by default. Otherwise, Vampire: the Requiem is swamped with metaplot.

                                    I think there's disagreement over the meaning of the term metaplot here?

                                    Metaplot is (in RPG terms) a living setting where the circumstances of the world change with each publication. Lucita joins the Sabbat, the Assamites break the Tremere curse, the Ravnos get blowed up real good; the official setting changes as time goes by. That book you bought where the Ravnos were the dominant clan of Desmoines, Iowa? No longer true. If you look at one book, and the given circumstances are distinguishable from those of a book published later on (as opposed to a new edition that changes the setting by fiat, rather than using the setting to justify that change), that's metaplot. Metaplot is what's to come. Setting is what came before. If any established event in the before of a setting can be defined as metaplot, metaplot isn't a useful term.

                                    I wouldn't call Fall of the Camarilla metaplot. It's an established piece of the setting (VtR1e mentions the fall of the Camarilla) that the players can interact with and shape; there's no canonical ending beyond the Camarilla falling. The adventure might be railroady to some, but it's not progressing a plot that's going to be continued in a new book -- there's no City of the Damned: Constantinople catching us up on Corbulo's true fate. To be fair, there are plenty of instances in Requiem where we get little updates on certain characters and events (Felix is a good example), but those rarely bear any weight on the setting as a whole. Like, to compare the closest equivalent, Dark Ages: Vampire has a metaplot. There are lots of plot threads that run through that line.

                                    All that being said, there's a lot of wiggle room. I just would argue that an adventure with a predetermined ending (however unsatisfying that might be) isn't metaplot.
