        Originally posted by nicodemus View Post
        When it comes to some of the cool powers available to Bloodlines, I think more often they work better as new Covenants with their power as Covenant perks, or integrate them into existing Covenants and powers, like the Gethsemani and LeS integrated into Theban, or the Architects of the Monolith could be integrated into the Ordo Dracul.

        When I do like to use bloodlines, I prefer to use local bloodlines that are more like family broods of sires and childer, only using the 10 standard disciplnes to mix things up and add flavor.
        There's a lot to be said for all that. I think the Architects of the Monolith work well as a separate covenant rooted in a fusion of Ordo and Invictus ideas - vampires becoming the very currents of power that flow through cities. The interesting thing about the Tismanu is their cultural role within the Ordo Dracul, so they could be a faction as much as a bloodline. The Alucinor archetype could be a fascinating addition to any clan, which makes them a feasible covenant. The Toreador could be considered "socially local" even if they're not geographically so.

        I like the Annunaki, Malocusians, and Morotrophians - along with Tenure, Domus, and Institutionalise (three very atmospheric Disciplines) - and all three together seem like a similar kind of change expressing itself in different ways for each clan. It would be interesting to see what the Daeva and Mekhet analogues would be.

            I think there are many cool bloodlines that has me much more interested than most Legacies or Lodges, but I have a problem with bloodlines as a concept. New quirks of the blood, blood borne curses, dynasties with little to no mechanics, and Covenant sub-factions all share this single slot on the character sheet. Those are all cool things I want to see in VTR, but by making them all fit into the same mould they all get the same mechanical status and are directly compared despite filling wildly different roles in the game.
            Since only a single bloodline has been published in a book in 2e I have a faint hope that Spilled Blood will revise the system for bloodlines.

                    Toreador, Taifa and Galloi

                    Daeva are passionate, for sure, but that passion is not always focused on arts and art patronage like the Toreador of Masquerade, having a bloodline which focused on that is a nice touch regardless if it was a nod to the previous game or not. Also plenty of concept ideas.

                    As an ethnic Arab I'm very grateful for the Taifa, really, for the first time I'm glad to see Muslims and Arabs depicted as art and culture patrons rather than bloodthirsty terrorists (cough cough Assamite).

                    I didn't like the Galloi when I first read about them in Bloodlines: The Legendary, though I completely changed my opinion when, in one game, there was a family of Galloi who were a powerful line of Harpies in Venice; the snark, the snobbery, the way one of them completely eradicated my character for making a simple social faux pas in Elysium; absolutely amazing!

                        Originally posted by Tessie View Post
                        New quirks of the blood,
                        The Morbus - in both Discipline and weakness - draw from a definite vampire archetype, which can refine the Mekhet's (diseases are invisible) (it could refine the Nosferatu too, as diseases are fearsome, but doesn't really make sense for the other three).

                        blood borne curses,
                        The Nelapsi are like a clan or clanlet beginning to break down - a terrible weakness that drives them out of almost any vampiric society. It makes sense for it to be confined to one clan, but it could be any of them.

                        dynasties with little to no mechanics, and
                        The Toreador are built around a shared cultural impulse that seems like something that would pass from sire to childe - in the sense that it would be a strong influence on the kind of person the sire Embraces - but is more or less orthogonal to most of the things covenants offer.

                        Covenant sub-factions all share this single slot on the character sheet.
                        Cf the Tismanu in my post. Spiritual counselors for a covenant that regards God as an obstacle is a great concept, and one of the ways the Ordo's history is appropriately messy. But I can't remember what clan they are, or anything about their Discipline - and it makes little sense for them to be confined to one clan.

                                    Melissidae and Morbus. Morbus are just cool, and are a great example of Z-axis change to a character that completely alters the way they play, while still staying in the themes of the game.

                                    The Melissidae are just excellent. It's the Ventrue need for control tuned to an excessive degree. As a PC, even if your character wants to run from their fate, they are forced, night by night, down the path of their predecessor. As an NPC, great for throwing your players for a loop, and an excellent Big Bad, with some nice body and psychological horror thrown in.

                                        I like Macellari, Galloi, Taifa, Morbus, Lygos, Deucalion y Nelapsi. Anything I can use as a terrifying NPC.

                                        Once I had a setting prepared for a VTM/VTR mixed game that included over 30 different "bloodlines" (the word "bloodline" would substitute "clan" in-game). Of course, the vampire couldn't convert from bloodline to bloodline, only by embrace or in weird cases of diablerie... Anyway, the point is, I used many of the Requiem Bloodlines to create other clans, but in some instances I would mix and match bloodlines together that I consider thematically redundant to create historical narratives for some of them. For instance,
                                        -- Lygos+Badacelli were one, Lygos were a bloodline of blind light averting pale-translucent underdwellers with tales and rumors of their existence stretching back to the Grand Covenant of Egypt and ties to Anubian cults.
                                        -- Orlok- was the name for VTM Nosferatu,
                                        -- Kerberos and Taifa were the same line of Social Predators but spit into two groups when they assimilated themselves to greek and persian culture respectably.
                                        -- Mabry are the lost descendants of a long lost Gangrel linage of hunnic warlords who traded Protean for Vigor. After Rome's fall these vamps joined the legio mortuum and the gallow posts, spreading throughout Europe settling isolated spider traps. and eventually almost died out. One lone survivor with low humanity, got to the US and embraced the remaining Mabry family abandoning them right after.
                                        -- The Anvari and Ishtarri share common ancestry
                                        -- The Impundulu were actually an odd type of Striges that the african witches were able to blind into servitude, looking like lightning birds instead of owls... that's all I remember off top of my head. )

                                                While I dislike the concept of mechanical bloodlines, especially with peculiar disciplines, I do like some bloodlines individually:
                                                • Norvegi: a vestige of a near exinct clan that's assimilated into another. I think that I'd interested in seeing a Jiang Shi bloodline for a similar reason.
                                                • Ordo Dracul ones in general: The old ones show a bit of regional witchcraft, spirituality and so forth that apparently forms part of Ordo Dracul's background. The new ones tend to be focussed around new avenues of reasearch. But I prefer them as Y-subsplats. However, I do think that Ordo Dracul has an excuse to make new bloodlines because changing their state of vampirism is what they do.
                                                • Moroi: Sort of the two reasons above combined.
                                                • Melissidae: One of most instantly scary features of V:tR in my opinion.
                                                • Bron: Basically because they might have been the Welsh clan (I'm bit biased towards other Welsh things sometimes, very much including Beddnerys) at some point before anyone around there had heard of Ventrue. Or maybe it's a load of reconstructed b******s based on the Matter of Britain that makes them feel special, but either way, I'm interested.
                                                • Players and Carnon: I don't how to explain it, I just like them.
