    Originally posted by Vyrkolash View Post
    The conceptual difference between the Tremere and the Odo Dracul isn't superstition vs science. The Tremere were mystics, but research and experimentation were part of their mystical learning process.
    I see the Tremere as taking the Discworld approach to their powers: dress it up in mysticism so everybody thinks it's harder than it is. When they research they are still as Scientific as the Ordo, but they then dress it up in mumbo jumbo and pretend it's even more difficult.

    The Ordo is a secret society with a mystical mission and pseudo-scientific methodologies reminiscent of parapsychology and victorian era spiritualism. The typical dragon has more in common with Dr. Frankenstein than with Albert Einstein.
    Nothing in the book implies that Victor Frankenstein was a bad scientist. He used alchemy and didn't repeat his experiment, but that was partially due to the attitudes of his time, and his experiment was the result of at least months of research. I see the doctor as a good example of what an Ordo Dracul researcher should be: a long time spent researching and gathering materials until he finally tries the experiment that will prove his theory works. If you wanted to create a patchwork person and create it by the way, I'd suggest Coil of the Voivode as the basis for such a scale.

    Also, the amount of science and pseudoscience will change depending on the specific vampire, I have younger members working in groups and using less pseudoscience in order to conduct research that takes longer, but is much more likely to produce useful results. The older members of the covenant don't really see it as real research, to which the neonates respond that the world has moved on, and can you please explain what Mr Magus meant by 'align the crystals in a counterclockwise rotation towards the orbit of mercury?' You still get the oddity and psudeoscience is more common than real science, but the covenant is changing, slowly, and is only slightly more out of touch with the modern world than the Carthians.

    Both groups study the occult, and both rationalize their approach. The difference between them is that the Tremere cultivate control over the world around them, while the Ordo develops control over their own state of being. The Tremere make sense as part of the Ordo if they are considered renegades or degenerates. Mainstream Dragons would view them with mistrust or disappointment, because they "lost the mission" by focusing on cultivating mystical power rather than overcoming the curse. At best they might be seen as a necessary evil, if they make themselves useful by helping to secure Wyrms' nests or fighting other covenants.
    I agree with this, but I would say that they study the occult with a mix of science and pseudoscience, and the Ordo would prefer to downplay the role pseudoscience has in it. The Ordo does occasionally admit that the Coils probably are a strange form of blood magic, but they prefer to promote the Science side, just hide the other 70%. The Tremere prefer to say that they use rigorously developed magic in my version, and throw a few crystals, skulls, stuffed crocodiles and pentagrams into the mix to convince the other vampires that it can't be done with three short sticks and 4cc of mouse blood.

        The disparities between science, pseudoscience, and mysticism are less clear cut than most people think. All are attempts to explain the world via internally consistent systems of thought. The real difference is just the assumptions you are allowed to make when formulating theories, and the language you use to describe said theories. Secular skeptics claim science makes no assumptions, but in reality it assume the absence of the supernatural. Pseudoscience uses the language of science, but is willing to make limited assumptions about the presence and relevance of supernatural influences (often while denying their supernatural nature). Mysticism tends to assume the significance of supernatural influences, makes untestable claims about the nature of such influences, and uses archaic language to describe such influences.

        For example, a Crone cultist assumes their goddess is what grants power to their blood magic. A Dragon pseudoscientist accepts that blood magic works, but either doesn't claim to know what powers it or describes the power source in terms of "energy". A scientist assumes blood magic is impossible and must be some kind of trickery. The problem in the WOD is obvious; the scientist is wrong. A Dragon, as a vampire who frequents haunted houses and spirit loci, does not have the luxury of being a strict scientist.

        When Mr Magus tells his students to meditate in a circle of crystals aligned "in a counterclockwise rotation towards the orbit of mercury" it may sound crazy to a modern secular neonate. In the WOD, however, that alignment of crystals produces a resonance associated with spirits of light. A resonance whose influence on the vampire makes him better able to resist daysleep. So the students of Mr. Magus would learn the first Coil of the Mystery of the Ascendant faster and more reliably than scientific students who assume it's all just a matter of conditioning.

        I don't mean to be That Guy; it's just a game and you can play it however you want. It's just that when the stereotypical Dragon is a mix of Dracula, Dr. Frankenstein, and the Ghost Busters, focusing on the scientific side of the covenant writes off a lot of their core themes.

                Originally posted by Vyrkolash View Post
                The conceptual difference between the Tremere and the Odo Dracul isn't superstition vs science.
                Just so's we all knows: THIS is a difference that *I* invented for MY game, not something that comes out of the 'canon' of either game, Requiem or Masquerade. Here's what I wrote about this for my own game:

                So, the Tremere are mystics who dabble in science and the Ordo Dracul are scientists how dabble in the mystical, but what does that mean? The answer is twofold.

                First, the ENDS to which each Covenant applies their arts differ tremendously. The Tremere seek power for the sake of power, wishing only to harness the potential of their Vitae as vampires, all in order to achieve individual goals. On the other hand, the Dragons seek to transcend their undead state, and only after that has come about do they expect a reward for their altered nature. The Tremere think in the very short-term – at least insofar as vampires see it – while the Order plays a much longer game. Of course, a character in either Covenant shall approach these truths however she sees fit, but we’re speaking in generalities here.

                The second part is the APPROACH that each Covenant takes. The Dragons see themselves as investigators, as rational beings who must sometimes use irrational methods, but the Tremere claim a legacy of magic, which they may augment with modern scientific tools. The net effect of these differences is negligible on the street-level, but membership in a Covenant ultimately boils down to accepting and acting out the party line. Calling a mystic a scientist or vice versa sounds like fighting words to any Kindred who takes her work seriously.
                ...As far as how MUCH of a difference there really is, that's debatable. I wanted some kind of REASON why the Dragons were different from the Warlocks, and I did that by cranking the contrast between them up to eleven.

                I don't offer the above to invalidate anything that rest of you have stated or suggested or whatnot. I'm only making my OWN version clear, not saying that anyone needs to agree. Heck, I quite like it when I'm able to debate stuff that I'm actually wrong about, so please do carry on!

                    Oh, and since someone asked, there's the 'elevator speech' for how my Requiem Thaumaturgy blood magic works:

                    Standing somewhere between the witcheries of Crúac and the examinations of the Dragons’ Mysteries, Thaumaturgy uses Vitae to focus power through a vampire, something only possible with a strong will, a disciplined mind, and proper study.
                    • Uses a series of elemental Paths that increase in potency
                    • Each Path allows access to a series of Sendings, effects that must be meticulously prepared and cast; functionally more akin to Coils and Scales of the Dragon, respectively, than other Blood Sorcery, but far more mystical in nature
                    • Thaumaturgy is alien and anathema to the Blood Sorceries of Crúac and Theban Sorcery; the two types of blood magic cannot exist within one Kindred, and tales of what comes from those who attempt such are as gruesome as they are secret
                    • Due to similar roots, Thaumaturgy Paths and Coils of the Dragon may have SOME limited compatibility, but since the two factions who use these respective arts are dire enemies, no known collaboration has ever been recorded
                    • Thaumaturgy demands great focus and attention to use, and the consequences of failure are often spectacular
                    I'm still hammering out solid mechanics with playtesting, but that's where it starts.

                            He (or She, no idea really) asked: What is the best Bloodline/Clan to represent what used to be a Tremere Lawyer? I want Auspex Dominate & Blood Magic with strong political/social leanings, but I'm kinda lost on the Requiem lore. There seems to be a lot of ways to do blood magic now & most of the clans are gone or have been assimilated. What do you guys recommend?

                            Here is the response I gave on 05-20-2014:

                            To Aermas, on converting a Tremere Lawyer to Requiem.

                            The Tremere seem to be the most difficult to transfer from Mascaraed to Requiem. I will attempt to offer some help, or at least advice for conversion to Requiem, since I do not have Blood and Smoke. I will suggest going into a conversion mechanically first, then going into back story fluff secondly.

                            The first question I would ask is do you want the Tremere to be a Bloodline or a completely separate Clan. Ultimately, I think the Tremere should be whichever is easier for the storyteller to enter into their game.

                            The second question I’d ask is; what does the Tremere bring to the game that is appealing?

                            Considering the Occult power differential between the Tremere and other Clans in Masquerade and Requiems pension for balance, there are several Bloodlines I would suggest working with to build a Franken-Tremere.

                            The Geheim (p. 83-88) and the Corajoso (p. 102-104) Bloodlines from the Ancient Bloodlines Book, and the Architects of the Monolith Bloodline from the Bloodlines: The Hidden Book (p. 30–37), are the most Tremere’y of the game builds I have seen without unbalancing the game.

                            Personally I like the Corajoso Bloodline for entering the enforced close nit familial ties through Linagem without making weakness to Vinculum a Clan weakness. It gives you more room to play with character creation.

                            The Geheim bring in more of a Victorian alchemical occult mad scientist feel to the game.

                            Since most people seem to dislike the Architects, I think their Ritual Blood Magic: The Gilded Cage, should be given to the Tremere with a Clan/Bloodline status restriction.

                            The Corajoso Bloodline Discipline Linagem is so potent that I think is should be used as a Dominate replacement in a Clan/Bloodline build. It also can’t be purchased by Ghouls, so one more thing making the Vampire special.

                            I like Geheim Bloodline Discipline Blut Alchemie as a good Animalism replacement in a Clan/Bloodline build.

                            Using Linagem and Blut Alchemie as replacements for the Common disciplines helps make the Tremere seem separate from the Ventrue, more alien.

                            The weakness should reflect this Alien nature as well. If you are going for the Bloodline build I would suggest the Corajoso Bloodline weakness to be layered on top of the standard Venture weakness. If you are going for the Clan build, I would say that the either using both the Geheim and the Corajoso Bloodline weaknesses as the clan weakness, or either of them in combination with the Derangement Fasination with the Occult.

                            Since this has prattled on, I will go into the possibility of Fluff briefly. The family backgrounds of the Bloodlines work great for building the New Tremere backstory without relying on the Masquerade history so heavily tied to the meta-plot. The Geheim Bloodline history is good for the time frame and founding of the Clan/Bloodline, possibly fitting in that through Alchemy they stole their Vampirism like in the old WoD. The Corajoso Bloodline history work well for the function of the internal politics of the family. Finally the Architects Bloodline history of how they obtained the Gilded Cage Blood Sorcery would work well for secrets and baggage that the family would not like to get out, as well as general mistrust that could build between the Tremere Family and other Covenants.

                            Good luck and I hope this helps.
