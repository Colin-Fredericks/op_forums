            Originally posted by Varsovian View Post

            Well I'm not sure how to put it into words, but I'll try to clarify as best I can. Mekhet embody the aspect of vampire grace and elegance. The Mekhet are perfectionists who devote themselves to their craft. I believe the core book gives some examples of them, stating that a bodyguard is more likely to know an ornate fighting style instead of just being a bruiser with a club or sidearm. A scholar uses his centuries of unlife to acquire a vast wealth of knowledge, most of which he's probably committed to memory.

            When you see a depiction of a vampire dispatching a gang of enemies in the blink of an eye or nimbly weaving their way through heavy machine gun fire, chances are they're imitating a Mekhet. The Mekhet do not like being out in the open unless the have no other options, so when they perform a job, they do so cleanly and efficiently. Leaving behind as little a mess to clean up as possible. Once the job is finished, they retreat into the shadows for which they are named, vanishing without a trace.

                        Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                        Re: Mekhet - see, my problem with them is that I can't find any fictional examples of a vampire like that. So, I'm not sure what they are supposed to be, really...
                        Lords are lordly. Savages are savage. Shadows are shadowy.

                        The Mekhet, more than any other clan, are shadows of what they were in life. They're hollow, faded, in some way incomplete. They're missing something, something hard to define, but whose importance can be seen in its lacking. They don't work right, and they can't always tell what is or isn't off about themselves. The Daeva may be living dead, but the Mekhet are merely walking dead.

                        The Mekhet hide not because they are fearful, but because they're so quiet and still that there's nothing to express. They're the man in the room who's so unremarkable and quiet that you forget he was there the whole time, listening. They watch, because others' lives are fuller than their own. The entire world around them is vastly richer than themselves by contrast. They read omens in the world. They listen. They go looking. They stalk. They obsess. And as they gather information, they become figures of mystique and power to the world around them, made rich and bold by their elusiveness. The rest of the world can't see that the mystique is a facade, skin-deep. There's nothing there as important as what we imagine there to be in the darkness. Ambitious Mekhet recognize that knowledge is a great weapon, but mystery is the greatest.

                        There's an old man in the neighborhood who owns a rich and spooky manor. He hardly ever comes out. Some say he never does, that he hasn't been seen in the light since the fifties. Others claim to have seen him or talked to him, once or twice. Sometimes he writes a letter to the editor in the local paper. All manner of rumors surround the estate. Cats have gone missing. Now a child, too. His power is greater for how little they know about him, how much they wonder without ever seeing him. He's a Shadow.

                        The Mekhet is the apprentice whom you don't recognize as significant until he stabs the villain in the back and usurps his place. He's the faceless authority in dimly-lit video messages who doesn't seem to have any identity outside of his directions, because his everyday life is so innocuous. He's the enforcer whose capabilities are unknown because nobody ever witnesses what is presumably his work. He's the vampire who seems introspective and sympathetic at first, who seems cautious that something has changed for the worse in him, until you realize just how broken he is, and how those he loves suffer worse than those he hates.

                        Originally posted by Varsovian View Post
                        Re: vampires vs other supernaturals - crossover games are one thing, but I was thinking of something a little different: vampires encountering really weird stuff, supernatural mysteries, Lovecraftian monsters / gods etc. There are mentions of such happenings in the Ventrue book - the "Briareos" being, or that doomed expedition to New Orleans that was driven mad by "hags"...
                        That's absolutely something you can do with Requiem, for which the Mekhet, the Ordo Dracul and the Circle of the Crone are especially well suited. It's not necessarily a core focus the way it is in, say, Mage: the Awakening, but it's definitely something described in the world of Requiem, and it's not uncommon for a domain to fail to recognize an outside context problem until it becomes the most pressing issue around which the domain revolves. Wicked Dead, of course, covers some phenomena like that as you saw, and the other clanbooks continue to hint at bizarre mysteries beyond the Kindred. I think you would very much like the end of the Mekhet story and the terrifying uncertainty it engenders.

                                            The general reason for why somebody is embraced again falls upon the sire. Usually the sire believes there is something about the individual that warrants the embrace. Nobody just walks up to a vampire and asks to be turned (unless they're a ghoul), remember that vampires try to make it a point that humans don't know they exist. They might possess a certain set of skills or talents, or perhaps the have valuable contacts and resources the sire wishes to take advantage of. However there are also cases in which the sire embraces a mortal as a form of punishment. Both the Nosferatu and the Lancea Sanctum are notorious for this, with the Nosferatu targeting others for their vanity and the Lancea Sanctum targeting others for their sinful behavior.
