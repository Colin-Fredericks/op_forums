                                  D&D 5th edition is a pretty streamlined system, actually. Some of their big design goals included a very simple baseline with knobs to increase complexity for those that wanted it, character creation that could be done in 10 minutes, and combats that could be done in 10 minutes.

                                  ...Aaaaaanywhoo, I agree with Tsusai, CofD is a rules-medium system by default that can be adjusted for more or less complexity based on which subsystems you chose to employ or not to employ. But it's designed for people who are already familiar with TTRPGs. If I was introducing a new player to tabletop gaming, I would much sooner use D&D 5e than CofD to show them the ropes (though, depending on their age I might even sooner use Pugmire).

                                  And that's not a bad thing, in my opinion! A lot of tabletop RPGs have shifted their focus lately to ease of use for new players, where CofD is one of the few that still caters to those of us who have been involved in the hobby for a while. It's good to have both options out there.
